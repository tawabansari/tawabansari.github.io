Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root Ṣ M M

Deafness, blocked hearing, hardness, closure, and being impenetrable to sound or message

Important Forms from This Root

صَمَّ / يَصَمُّ

ṣamma / yaṣammu; became deaf, did not hear, or had the path of hearing closed.

أَصَمَّ / يُصِمُّ

aṣamma / yuṣimmu; made deaf, closed the path of hearing, made impenetrable to a message.

صَمَم

ṣamam; deafness, blocked hearing, closure of the auditory path.

أَصَمّ / صُمّ

aṣamm / ṣumm; deaf, one whose hearing path is closed; the deaf.

صَمَّاء / صِمَام

ṣammāʾ / ṣimām; hard, closed, without opening; a stopper or seal for an opening.

صَمِيم / تَصْمِيم

ṣamīm / taṣmīm; dense inner core; making firm, resolved intent, structured design.

Central Meaning

The root Ṣ M M means deafness, blocked hearing, hardness, solidity, closure, and being impenetrable to sound. In the material layer, ṣamam means that the ear or the path of hearing is closed and sound does not enter effectively.

This root does not only point to physical deafness. More broadly, it approaches any state of hardness, closure, compactness, and refusal to receive an effect. Ṣamam is a condition in which sound exists, but the entrance is closed; the message exists, but reception does not happen.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of Ṣ M M is the closure of a path of penetration, especially the penetration of sound. In ṣamam, the issue is not merely the absence of sound. Sound may exist, but the path of reception is closed. The ear, heart, or receiving structure has become impenetrable.

From here, the meanings of hardness and solidity become clear. Something ṣamm is not soft, open, and receptive. It is closed, hard, compact, and resistant to penetration. For this reason, the root may apply to hearing, a hard object, a solid tool, a sealed opening, or, semantically, a human being who does not receive truth.

Material Image

The first material image is an ear that sound does not reach or affect. Sound moves through the air, but if the path of the ear is closed, hearing does not occur. The problem is not the absence of sound; it is the closure of reception.

The second image is a hard and compact body into which nothing penetrates: not sound, not impact, not softness. From this image, the root’s sense of hardness and solidity is understood. صَمَّاء may be used for things that are hard and without opening, meaning they have no inner space of receptivity.

The third image is صِمَام, something like a stopper or closure that blocks a passage of entry or exit. This image shows that Ṣ M M is tied to openings being sealed and the flow path being closed.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, ṣamam is used for deafness and lack of hearing. Aṣamm is one who does not hear, or one upon whom surrounding sound has no effect. But the root is not limited to the human ear. Classical Arabic also uses ṣammāʾ for things that are hard, closed, impenetrable, or without openings.

Ṣamīm refers to the dense inner core of something, where it is closest to compression and solidity. Ṣimām is also important in this lexical field because it points to something that closes a mouth, opening, or passage. These uses show that ṣamam is not only not-hearing; it is the closure of the route by which an effect enters or exits.

Qur’anic Usage

Ṣumm with Bukm and ʿUmy

One famous Qur’anic use appears in Surat al-Baqarah: صُمٌّ بُكْمٌ عُمْيٌ فَهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ 2:18. Here, ṣumm means deaf, but the deafness is not necessarily physical. The verse speaks of people in whom the route of receiving guidance has been closed.

The combination ṣumm, bukm, ʿumy forms a complete picture of the cutting off of the tools of communication with truth: they do not hear, they do not articulate a response to truth, and they do not see. The problem is not one body part; it is the whole system of reception, expression, and discernment.

Ṣamam and Not Hearing the Call

The Qur’an says that the Messenger cannot make the dead or the deaf hear when they turn away: إِنَّكَ لَا تُسْمِعُ الْمَوْتَىٰ وَلَا تُسْمِعُ الصُّمَّ الدُّعَاءَ إِذَا وَلَّوْا مُدْبِرِينَ 27:80.

Here, ṣumm are those who have become impenetrable to the call of truth. The issue is not only deafness; they have also turned their backs. The path of hearing is closed, and the direction of body and will has turned away from the message.

Hearing Sound and Hearing Meaning

In the Qur’an, hearing is not only receiving sound. A person may hear the sound but refuse the meaning. Such a person is physically hearing but, in the field of guidance, is in a state of ṣamam. At this level, ṣamam means that the message does not penetrate the structure of understanding and response.

Ṣamam and ʿAmā in Surat al-Māʾidah

Surat al-Māʾidah says: وَحَسِبُوا أَلَّا تَكُونَ فِتْنَةٌ فَعَمُوا وَصَمُّوا ثُمَّ تَابَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ ثُمَّ عَمُوا وَصَمُّوا كَثِيرٌ مِّنْهُمْ 5:71.

Here, blindness and deafness appear together. The root of the matter is ḥasibū: they assumed there would be no trial. This false reckoning prepared the ground for the closure of discernment and reception.

Aṣammahum in Surat Muḥammad

Surat Muḥammad says: أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ لَعَنَهُمُ اللَّهُ فَأَصَمَّهُمْ وَأَعْمَىٰ أَبْصَارَهُمْ 47:23. The previous verses speak of corruption in the earth and cutting family bonds. In this context, aṣammahum means that The God closed the path of hearing truth in them or made them impenetrable to hearing truth.

This use shows that ṣamam may appear as the structural consequence of a corrupt orientation. One who cuts the bonds of truth and kinship also moves toward being cut off from receiving truth.

Ethical and Human Note

The Qur’an sometimes portrays ṣamam as a moral and psychological condition: a person, through choice, arrogance, denial, or habit, makes himself impenetrable to truth. At the same time, physical deafness is a human reality and must not be confused with this moral metaphor.

Moral ṣamam is not an insult toward deaf people. These verses discuss those who have the tools of understanding yet refuse the path of truth. This distinction is important for translation and commentary.

Difference from Nearby Roots

S M AYN (S M ʿ) concerns hearing, receiving sound, and auditory attention. Ṣamam is the opposite of auditory reception, but in the Qur’an it may also oppose accepting the meaning of truth. ʾ DH N is tied to ear, permission, and readiness to hear; ṣamam is tied to the channel being closed.

B K M concerns blocked expression and inability to speak response. AYN M Y (ʿ M Y) concerns the closed path of seeing. KH T M is sealing and closure, and may be upon heart, hearing, or sight. W Q R is closer to heaviness in the ear or heavy burden.

GH L F concerns being covered or wrapped. Q S W is hardness of the heart, while ṣamam is the closing of the path of hearing and resistance to the effect of sound or message. S D D more often points to a barrier or blocking from outside, while ṣamam describes the receiving structure itself as closed and impenetrable.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, ṣumm should not always be taken only in the medical sense of “deaf people,” especially when it appears beside bukm and ʿumy in a context of rejecting truth. In a physical context, “deaf” or “unable to hear” is suitable. In a guidance context, “deaf to truth,” “impenetrable to the call,” or “those whose path of hearing has been closed” may be more precise.

In the context of aṣammahum, “made them deaf” is correct, but for root-level explanation one may add: “closed in them the path of hearing truth” or “made them impenetrable to truth.” In lexical contexts such as ṣamīm, ṣammāʾ, and ṣimām, the meanings of hardness, compactness, and impenetrability should be preserved.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

صَمَم (ṣamam): deafness, blocked hearing, closure of the path of hearing, impenetrability to sound.

أَصَمّ (aṣamm): deaf, unable to hear, closed-eared, impenetrable to sound.

صُمّ (ṣumm): the deaf; in guidance context: deaf to truth or those whose path of hearing is closed.

صَمَّاء (ṣammāʾ): hard, closed, impenetrable, without opening; feminine of aṣamm.

صِمَام (ṣimām): stopper, closure, seal for an opening or passage.

صَمِيم (ṣamīm): dense core, hard inner center, inward essence.

صُمٌّ بُكْمٌ عُمْيٌ: deaf, mute, blind; or impenetrable to sound, blocked in response, blocked in sight.

فَأَصَمَّهُمْ: so He made them deaf; He closed in them the path of hearing truth.

Linguistic Summary

The root Ṣ M M denotes deafness, closure of hearing, hardness, compactness, sealing, and impenetrability. Materially, ṣamam means sound does not enter the ear or the hearing structure. More broadly, something is ṣamm when it is hard, closed, without opening, and unreceptive.

In the Qur’an, this root most often describes those to whom the sound of truth reaches, yet it does not penetrate their understanding, heart, or direction. The sound exists, the call exists, but the path of effect is closed. The central image is this: the message reaches the outside, but the inside is sealed; sound is present, but true hearing does not happen.
