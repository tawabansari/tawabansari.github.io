Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root N Z L

Descent, settling, and the making of truth accessible within human reception and responsibility

Morphological forms of this root

This section is designed for teaching Qur’anic morphology. The field “Used in the Qur’an” shows whether the form or example appears in the Qur’anic text. Forms that may be linguistically possible in Arabic but do not appear as Qur’anic forms of this root are marked “No.”

The root N Z L in the Qur’an

The root ن ز ل (N Z L) is one of the very important roots in the Qur’an. It is used for revelation and the Book, and also for water, angels, clothing, iron, divine command, punishment, lodging, and the prepared reception of the human being.

The basic meaning of this root is not only “coming down.” In Arabic, نزول (nuzūl) can mean descending, entering, taking lodging, residing, and settling in a station. From here, words such as مَنْزِل (manzil), مَنَازِل (manāzil), and نُزُل (nuzul) become understandable.

In Qur’anic vision, نزول (nuzūl) means that something from a higher, hidden, or inaccessible level enters the field of human reception and responsibility. This descent may be mercy, such as water and revelation; it may be blessing and instrument, such as clothing and iron; or it may be warning and punishment when the human being persists in corruption after truth has become clear.

This variety shows that the root ن ز ل (N Z L) is not only about movement from above to below. It is about something entering a place of effect, measure, reception, and responsibility.

Central meaning of the root

The central meaning of ن ز ل (N Z L) may be understood as this: something descends or is brought down toward a station where it becomes receivable, effective, and accountable within human life.

This meaning becomes very clear in 15:21:

وَإِنْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ إِلَّا عِنْدَنَا خَزَائِنُهُ وَمَا نُنَزِّلُهُ إِلَّا بِقَدَرٍ مَعْلُومٍ
wa-in min shay’in illā ʿindanā khazā’inuhu wa-mā nunazziluhu illā bi-qadarin maʿlūm

There is nothing except that its treasuries are with Us, and We do not send it down except by a known measure.

This verse is an important key for understanding descent. The treasuries of everything are with God; that is, the origin and source of things are in a level that the human being does not own. But when something is sent down, it enters measure, time, place, and the capacity of human reception.

So descent means the coming of something from divine treasuries by a known measure. That thing may be revelation, water, iron, clothing, or any blessing that enters human life through God’s order and measure.

Nazala: truth descending to the place of reception

The simple form of this root is نَزَلَ (nazala): it descended, came down, or entered.

In 17:105, the Qur’an has been sent down with truth and has descended with truth. This means that the Qur’an did not come from human imagination, the Prophet’s desire, social pressure, or tribal need. It came from truth and entered the human world with truth.

In 26:193, it is said that the trustworthy Spirit brought it down. Here descent is the transfer of revelation from a level outside human reach into the heart and tongue of the Prophet.

So نَزَلَ (nazala) is not merely a spatial movement. In the case of revelation, it means truth entering the place of reception.

Anzala: bringing down from the divine source

The form أَنْزَلَ (anzala) means He brought down, sent down, or delivered something from a higher level into the field of reception.

In this form, the attention is often on the source. The text makes us understand that this book, blessing, guidance, or command is from God, and that its origin is not earthly or human.

In 2:4, faith in what has been sent down upon the Prophet and what was sent down before him is presented as a mark of those who receive guidance. Revelation here is a divine flow, not the private property of one tribe or one community.

In 57:25, God speaks of sending messengers with clear proofs, sending down the Book and the Balance, and then sending down iron. This verse shows that descent is not only about religious text. The Book, the Balance, and iron each enter human life in a different way: guidance, justice, and power.

In 7:26, the Qur’an speaks of clothing that God has sent down for the human being. Clearly, clothing did not fall from the sky as a ready-made object. So descent here means that the blessing was provided, gifted, and placed within human reach.

Therefore, أَنْزَلَ (anzala) should not always be understood as bodily coming down from the sky. Sometimes it means that God brings something from His treasuries, measure, and command into human life.

Nazzala: descent with process, quality, and care

The form نَزَّلَ (nazzala) also means to send down or bring down. But in many cases, it carries a sense of order, intensity, gradual appearance, quality, or special care.

Still, one should not state with final certainty that أَنْزَلَ (anzala) always means one-time descent and نَزَّلَ (nazzala) always means gradual descent. That distinction may sometimes be useful, but if it becomes a fixed rule, it forces the Qur’anic text.

More carefully, we can say this: أَنْزَلَ (anzala) often emphasizes the act of bringing down and its attribution to the divine source, while نَزَّلَ (nazzala) more often draws attention to the quality, process, ordering, or concrete appearance of descent in human time and situation.

In 15:9, God says that He Himself sent down the Reminder and He Himself is its guardian. Here the descent of the Reminder is not merely handing a text to human beings. It is sending down something that remains under divine preservation.

In 17:106, the Qur’an is recited to people separately and with pause, and then God says that He sent it down as تَنْزِيلًا (tanzīlan). This is one of the clearest signs that the Qur’an’s descent was joined with time, formation, and wisdom.

In 16:102, the Holy Spirit brought it down from your Lord with truth in order to make the believers firm. So the descent of the Qur’an is not only the transfer of information. It is descent for strengthening, guidance, glad tidings, and the shaping of the human being.

Tanzīl: a book sent down from Rabb al-ʿālamīn

The word تَنْزِیل (tanzīl) is one of the most important words of this root. The Qur’an repeatedly calls itself تَنْزِیل (tanzīl).

In 26:192, it says:

وَإِنَّهُ لَتَنْزِيلُ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ
wa-innahu la-tanzīlu rabbi al-ʿālamīn

Indeed, it is the tanzīl of the Lord of the worlds.

This phrase clarifies the source of the Qur’an. The Qur’an is not the product of the Prophet’s mind, Arab culture, tribal history, or a later religious system. It is from Rabb al-ʿālamīn.

In 20:4, the Qur’an is introduced as a tanzīl from the One who created the earth and the high heavens. That is, the same God who created the structure of existence has also sent down the structure of guidance.

In 41:42, the Qur’an is described as a tanzīl from One who is Wise and Praiseworthy. So this Book did not enter the human world without account, purpose, or order.

From here, the method of Forqan becomes clear. Because the Book is the direct تَنْزِیل (tanzīl) of Rabb al-ʿālamīn, no secondary text, whether hadith, fiqh, tafsir, inherited religious custom, or theological system, can rule over it. Every text and tradition must be measured by the Book, not the Book placed under its authority.

Tanazzala: descending by permission and mission

The form تَنَزَّلَ (tanazzala) is often used for angels, the Spirit, devils, or divine command. This form shows that descent may be missioned, repeated, or within the framework of divine permission.

In 97:4, the angels and the Spirit descend in the Night of Qadr, by the permission of their Lord, from every command. Here descent is tied to divine permission. No being from the unseen realm acts independently.

In 41:30, the angels descend upon those who said, “Our Lord is God,” and then remained upright. This descent is the descent of calm, assurance, and glad tidings.

In 65:12, the divine command descends among the heavens and the earth. This shows that descent is not only about the text of revelation. God’s command also runs through the structure of existence.

The descent of water and the descent of revelation

One of the most beautiful Qur’anic connections is the link between the descent of water and the descent of revelation.

Water descends and revives dead earth. Revelation descends and revives dead hearts, societies, and minds.

But descent by itself is not enough. The earth must have the capacity to receive. If rain falls on prepared soil, it brings life; if it falls on hard stone, it passes over it and does not enter.

Likewise, the Qur’an is guidance and mercy for a heart that has humility. But for a heart that sees itself as self-sufficient, the same Qur’an can become proof and exposure.

Manāzil: measured stations and stages

The word مَنَازِل (manāzil) is the plural of مَنْزِل (manzil): stations, lodgings, or stages of placement.

In 10:5 and 36:39, the Qur’an speaks of the stations of the moon. The moon is not disorderly in its movement. Stations and measures have been appointed for it.

This meaning is also important for understanding revelation. The Qur’an did not enter history in disorder or randomness. The descent of the Qur’an came with measure, wisdom, situation, and formation.

Nuzul: prepared reception for arrival

The word نُزُل (nuzul) means hospitality, reception, or something prepared for arrival and residence.

In 3:198, gardens beneath which rivers flow are described as نُزُل (nuzul) from God for those who are mindful.

In 18:107, the gardens of Firdaws are نُزُل (nuzul) for those who believe and do righteous deeds.

But this word is not only used for Paradise. In 56:93, a painful reception with boiling water is mentioned for the people of misguidance. So نُزُل (nuzul) means what is prepared for a human being’s entry into his outcome, whether mercy or the bitter result of his own choices.

Munazzal: sent down from the Lord

The word مُنَزَّل (munazzal) means something that has been sent down, with the color and meaning of Form II.

In 6:114, the People of the Book know that this Book has been sent down from your Lord with truth. Here the Qur’an being مُنَزَّل (munazzal) means that its source is from the Lord, not from the human being.

If the Book is مُنَزَّل (munazzal) from the Lord, no earthly system has the right to place itself above it. The human being may understand, reflect, translate, interpret, and think about it; but he cannot place an earthly source as ruler over a Book sent down from the Lord.

Munzal: a blessed place of settling

The word مُنْزَل (munzal) can mean a place of descent, a lodging, or the act of being brought down into a situation.

In 23:29, it says:

رَبِّ أَنْزِلْنِي مُنْزَلًا مُبَارَكًا وَأَنْتَ خَيْرُ الْمُنْزِلِينَ
rabbi anzilnī munzalan mubārakan wa-anta khayru al-munzilīn

My Lord, settle me in a blessed place of settling, and You are the best of those who settle.

In this verse, مُنْزَل (munzal) may point both to the act of being brought down and to the place in which the human being descends and becomes settled.

This prayer is very deep. The Qur’an does not only teach us to seek blessing. It teaches us to ask God to settle us in a station that is blessed: a place where our residence, stability, and presence are joined with growth, good, security, purity, and right effect.

Descent and literalistic misunderstanding

One common mistake is to restrict every Qur’anic mention of descent immediately to physical movement from the material sky.

But the Qur’an itself limits that understanding. When it speaks of the sending down of clothing, iron, the Book, the Balance, authority, or command, it becomes clear that descent is not merely the falling of a body from above.

15:21 is the key to this understanding: the treasuries of everything are with God, and God does not send it down except by a known measure. In Qur’anic logic, descent means that a reality from the unseen realm and divine treasuries enters a limited form that can be received in the world.

So when we say that the Qur’an descended, we should not understand it in a simple material way. The Qur’an came from God into human language; from the divine station into the field of human understanding and responsibility; from the unseen closer to the witnessed realm, without its source becoming human.

The Qur’an’s descent and the accessibility of God’s speech

In the case of the Qur’an, descent means that God’s speech was placed at a level where the human being could face it. This is an immense mercy.

If divine speech had remained only in its unseen and inaccessible level, how could the human being hear it, recite it, preserve it, understand it, and be guided by it?

God made His speech receivable for the human being. This means that God’s speech entered human language, but its source did not become human. Human understanding came near it, but did not own it. The Prophet conveyed it, but did not produce it. The community recited it, but had no right to place it under the authority of later customs and systems.

The descent of mercy and the descent of punishment

Descent is not always mercy. In the Qur’an, just as the Book, water, and angels descend, punishment and rijz may also descend.

Descent means that something from God enters the field of the human being. But its content differs according to the human condition and divine wisdom.

For a humble human being, the descent of revelation is mercy. For a society that persists in oppression and corruption after truth has become clear, descent may appear as punishment and the result of deeds.

So descent carries both glad tidings and warning. The human being lives under the sky of God’s mercy, but if he lives in oppression and arrogance, the same field can become the place where heavy consequences descend.

Suggested translation in Forqan

For the root ن ز ل (N Z L), one fixed translation is not enough for all cases. The form of the word and the context of the verse must be seen.

نَزَلَ (nazala): descended, came down, entered.

أَنْزَلَ (anzala): brought down, sent down, made accessible from Himself.

نَزَّلَ (nazzala): sent down with ordering, quality, or special care.

تَنْزِیل (tanzīl): revelatory descent, measured sending down, that which is sent down from God.

تَنَزَّلَ (tanazzala): descended, descended by mission or divine permission.

مَنَازِل (manāzil): stations, lodgings, stages, measured positions.

نُزُل (nuzul): prepared reception, hospitality, what is prepared for arrival and residence.

مُنَزَّل (munazzal): sent down, revealed from the Lord.

مُنْزَل (munzal): lodging, place of descent, or being brought into a situation.

مُنْزِل (munzil): one who sends down, brings down, or settles into a station.

Summary

The root ن ز ل (N Z L) shows the relationship between God, revelation, the world, and the human being.

In this root, something from a higher level enters a level where it can be received, settle, and become effective. Sometimes that thing is the Book, sometimes water, sometimes an angel, sometimes iron, sometimes clothing, sometimes command, sometimes mercy, and sometimes punishment.

In the case of the Qur’an, descent means that God’s speech came near to human language and understanding; not that it became human, but that it became receivable. God did not leave the human being in darkness. He sent guidance down toward him.

But descent also brings responsibility. When the Book was sent down, the proof became clear. The human being can no longer place custom, sect, tribe, ancestors, scholars, or power above it.

So ن ز ل (N Z L) is the root of guidance descending into the field of life. God draws near to the human being from above, but the human being must receive what has been sent down with humility, purity, and truthfulness.
