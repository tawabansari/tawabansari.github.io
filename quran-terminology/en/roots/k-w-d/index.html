Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root K W D

To come close to the threshold of occurrence, to stand at the edge of doing or becoming, and to show intense readiness or pressure toward an event

Important Forms from This Root

كَادَ (kāda)

kāda; was close to happening, almost did, reached the threshold of occurrence.

يَكَادُ / تَكَادُ (yakādu / takādu)

yakādu / takādu; is almost, is close to, moves toward the edge of occurrence.

كِدْتُ (kidtu)

kidtu; I almost did, I came close to doing.

لَا يَكَادُ / لَمْ يَكَدْ (lā yakādu / lam yakad)

lā yakādu / lam yakad; hardly, scarcely, almost does not, or does not come near occurrence, depending on context.

Method Note

The word يَكَادُ (yakādu) and its related forms are often translated simply as “almost” or “nearly.” That is basically correct, but it does not show the full force of the root. K W D is not only about closeness in time. It is the language of the threshold: a situation has moved up to the edge of happening, but has not necessarily fully occurred.

For Forqan, the distinction between “close to happening” and “actually happened” is important. Yakādu often shows that the pressures, preparations, or conditions of an event have reached a very high level, so that its occurrence appears extremely near. Yet the word itself does not prove that the event has happened. That narrow gap often carries the meaning of the verse.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of K W D is standing at the final edge of transformation. Something has not fully happened, but the distance between it and happening has become very small. This nearness may come from outside pressure, inner readiness, or the weight and danger of a condition.

When the Qur’an says lightning is close to snatching the eyesight, it shows the intensity of the flash. When it says oil is close to shining, it shows the purity and readiness of that oil. When it says the heavens are close to splitting from a statement, it shows the unbearable weight of that false claim. K W D is therefore not just a simple adverb; it measures how near an event has come to actual occurrence.

Material Image

The material image is a vessel whose water has risen to the rim but has not yet overflowed, or a branch bent under pressure but not yet broken. In both cases, the observer senses that the event is one step away. The condition is not calm or ordinary. Force or readiness has reached a level where change is extremely close.

This image explains why يَكَادُ (yakādu) often carries intensity. The word does not merely say “the time is near.” It says the condition has become so full, pressured, or prepared that one more movement would make the event occur.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic grammar, كَادَ (kāda) is counted among the verbs of nearness, the afʿāl al-muqārabah. These verbs do not simply report the following action as fully completed. They define the relation of that action to occurrence. كَادَ يَفْعَلُ (kāda yafʿalu) means “he was close to doing it,” with only a small distance remaining between the act and its fulfillment.

Negative uses such as لَا يَكَادُ (lā yakādu) and لَمْ يَكَدْ (lam yakad) require care. They do not always mean absolute negation. Sometimes they mean something barely happens, sometimes that it happens with great difficulty, and sometimes that the matter does not come near fulfillment. The context must guide the translation.

Qur’anic Usage

At the Edge of Eyesight Being Snatched in the Parable of Ṣayyib

In يَكَادُ الْبَرْقُ يَخْطَفُ أَبْصَارَهُمْ (yakādu al-barqu yakhṭafu abṣārahum), the lightning is so intense and sudden that it is close to snatching away their eyesight 2:20. The flash is not calm or guiding. It is sudden, sharp, and overwhelming. Yakādu shows the intensity of the light: it has not fully taken their sight, but it has reached the threshold of doing so.

This matters for the parable in al-Baqarah. The hypocrites are in darkness, and they move only when flashes of lightning give momentary visibility. This light is not stable and does not give safety. Even the light itself becomes dangerous, because its intensity nearly disables the instrument of sight.

The Readiness and Purity of the Oil in the Verse of Light

In يَكَادُ زَيْتُهَا يُضِيءُ وَلَوْ لَمْ تَمْسَسْهُ نَارٌ (yakādu zaytuhā yuḍīʾu walaw lam tamsashu nār), the oil of the lamp is so pure, clear, and ready that it is close to giving light even though no fire has touched it 24:35. Here yakādu does not speak of danger, but of inner readiness and clarity.

The verse does not say that the oil has actually lit without fire. It says the oil is so ready and transparent that it has come to the threshold of shining. This gap matters. Yakādu distinguishes between inner capacity and complete outward occurrence.

The Threshold-Reaction of Creation Before a False Claim About The God

In تَكَادُ السَّمَاوَاتُ يَتَفَطَّرْنَ مِنْهُ (takādu al-samāwātu yatafaṭṭarna minhu), the context is the claim that the Most Merciful has taken a child 19:90. The Qur’an shows the weight of this statement through the image of the heavens nearly splitting apart from it. A similar verse says: تَكَادُ السَّمَاوَاتُ يَتَفَطَّرْنَ مِن فَوْقِهِنَّ (takādu al-samāwātu yatafaṭṭarna min fawqihinna) 42:5.

Here takādu shows the severity and weight of that false attribution. The verse is not reporting that the heavens literally split at that moment. It uses threshold-language to show how incompatible the statement is with divine majesty and truth.

Serious Pressure Toward Causing a Slip

In وَإِن كَادُوا لَيَفْتِنُونَكَ عَنِ الَّذِي أَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْكَ (wa-in kādū layaftinūnaka ʿani alladhī awḥaynā ilayka), the opponents were close to pressuring the Messenger away from what had been revealed to him 17:73. This must be read carefully. The verse does not say that the deviation happened. It shows that their effort was serious and near the point of effect.

The following verses show that the Messenger’s firmness depends on The God’s holding and support. So كَادُوا (kādū) here shows the intensity of hostile pressure, not the occurrence of deviation. Their effort reached the threshold of influence, but did not become fulfillment.

Negative Forms and Severe Difficulty in Understanding and Expression

In فَمَالِ هَؤُلَاءِ الْقَوْمِ لَا يَكَادُونَ يَفْقَهُونَ حَدِيثًا (famāli hāʾulāʾi al-qawmi lā yakādūna yafqahūna ḥadīthā), لَا يَكَادُونَ (lā yakādūn) shows that they hardly understand speech, or do not come near proper understanding 4:78. This is not merely absolute denial of understanding; it shows severe weakness and difficulty in reaching understanding.

In أَمْ أَنَا خَيْرٌ مِّنْ هَذَا الَّذِي هُوَ مَهِينٌ وَلَا يَكَادُ يُبِينُ (am anā khayrun min hādhā alladhī huwa mahīnun walā yakādu yubīn), Pharaoh says about Moses that he barely speaks clearly 43:52. This is Pharaoh’s contemptuous speech. Semantically, lā yakādu yubīn means, in Pharaoh’s accusation, that Moses’ clarity of expression scarcely reaches the level of evident speech.

Difference from Nearby Words

Q R B means nearness in a broad sense: near in time, place, rank, or relation. Kāda usually means that an action or condition has come close to occurrence. With K W D, attention is on the threshold of the act, not simply general closeness.

ʿAsā is closer to hope, expectation, or possibility in the future. K W D speaks of pressure or readiness already near fulfillment. Hamma belongs to inner intention and resolve, while K W D can describe an outside phenomenon, a natural event, social pressure, or the inner readiness of a substance.

Translation Method

When translating يَكَادُ (yakādu), “almost” is often useful, but it should not erase the idea of threshold. In many verses, the translation should bring out intensity and nearness to occurrence: “it is so intense that it nearly...,” “it reaches the threshold of...,” or “it is on the verge of...”

In negative verses such as لَا يَكَادُ (lā yakādu), mechanical translation can damage the meaning. Sometimes “hardly” is best, sometimes “does not come near,” and sometimes “almost cannot.” The context must decide.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

يَكَادُ الْبَرْقُ يَخْطَفُ أَبْصَارَهُمْ (yakādu al-barqu yakhṭafu abṣārahum): the lightning is close to snatching their eyesight; the flash is so intense that it reaches the threshold of taking their sight.

يَكَادُ زَيْتُهَا يُضِيءُ (yakādu zaytuhā yuḍīʾu): its oil almost gives light; its oil is so clear and ready that it seems on the verge of shining.

تَكَادُ السَّمَاوَاتُ يَتَفَطَّرْنَ مِنْهُ (takādu al-samāwātu yatafaṭṭarna minhu): the heavens are close to splitting from it; that statement is so weighty that it brings the heavens to the threshold of splitting.

وَإِن كَادُوا لَيَفْتِنُونَكَ (wa-in kādū layaftinūnaka): they nearly caused you to be tried away from it; their effort came to the threshold of influencing and causing fitnah.

لَا يَكَادُونَ يَفْقَهُونَ حَدِيثًا (lā yakādūna yafqahūna ḥadīthā): they hardly understand speech; they do not come near proper understanding of speech.

وَلَا يَكَادُ يُبِينُ (walā yakādu yubīn): he barely speaks clearly; he hardly reaches clear expression.

Summary

The root K W D in the Qur’an is the language of thresholds. It shows that an event has reached the edge of happening, but has not necessarily occurred. Sometimes that threshold comes from danger, as with lightning in the parable of al-Baqarah. Sometimes it comes from purity and readiness, as with the oil in the Verse of Light. Sometimes it comes from the weight of a false statement, as with attributing a child to The God. Sometimes it comes from social and political pressure, as with the opponents’ attempt to influence the Messenger.

In Forqan’s reading, يَكَادُ (yakādu) means the event is standing at the door of occurrence. It has not yet entered, but its nearness is felt. This root helps us distinguish near possibility, intense pressure, complete readiness, and actual fulfillment.
