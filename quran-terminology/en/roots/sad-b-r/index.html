Qur'anic Root Ṣ B R (Sad B R)

Holding, restraining, controlling, and preventing the self or a position from being released, scattered, or broken under pressure.

Core Meaning

The root ص ب ر originally indicates holding, restraining, controlling, and preventing something from being released or scattered. When this root is used about the human being, sabr means that a person holds his nafs in a position and does not allow fear, pain, anger, desire, haste, exhaustion, or pressure to push him out of that position.

Therefore, sabr in the Qur'an is not merely “waiting” or silent, passive endurance. Sabr is often an active act: preserving direction in conditions where a force pulls the person toward escape, hasty reaction, surrender, scattering, or collapse.

Important Forms

صَبَرَ is the past verb of form I: he held himself, remained firm, and did not leave his position under pressure.

فَصَبَرُوا عَلَىٰ مَا كُذِّبُوا وَأُوذُوا

They remained firm before the denial and harm they faced.

Al-An'am 6:34

In this use, sabr does not mean that pain or harm disappeared. It means that harm could not change their direction.

يَصْبِرُ is a present-tense verb: he remains firm, holds himself, and endures pressure.

إِنَّهُ مَنْ يَتَّقِ وَيَصْبِرْ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُضِيعُ أَجْرَ الْمُحْسِنِينَ

Whoever has taqwa and remains firm, The God does not waste the reward of the doers of ihsan.

Yusuf 12:90

اصْبِرْ and اصْبِرُوا are imperative forms: hold yourself, remain firm, or remain firm together.

وَاصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا أَصَابَكَ

Remain firm before what reaches you.

Luqman 31:17

This command is not necessarily a call to silence, immobility, or accepting oppression. Its first meaning is preserving one's position and restraining a reaction that could pull the person out of the right path.

صَبْر is the main verbal noun: holding the nafs, restraining reaction, preserving cohesion, and remaining firm before a force that pulls a person toward escape or collapse.

وَاسْتَعِينُوا بِالصَّبْرِ وَالصَّلَاةِ

Seek help through sabr and salat.

Al-Baqarah 2:45

صَابِر is an active participle: one who holds himself and does not leave his position under pressure. Qur'anic forms include sabir, sabirun, sabirin, sabirat, and sabirah.

وَبَشِّرِ الصَّابِرِينَ

Give glad news to those who remain firm.

Al-Baqarah 2:155

صَبَّار is an intensive form that can indicate intensity, repetition, or firmness in sabr: someone who not only holds himself in one event, but repeatedly has the ability to preserve himself and his direction. In the Qur'an, this form comes with shakur:

إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَاتٍ لِكُلِّ صَبَّارٍ شَكُورٍ

Indeed in that are signs for every deeply steadfast, grateful one.

Ibrahim 14:5

Sabbar may be translated as “deeply steadfast,” “persistently enduring,” or “one who repeatedly holds himself under pressure.”

صَابَرَ and صَابِرُوا are form III. This pattern often indicates confrontation, participation, or facing another side; therefore صابروا is not exactly identical with اصبروا.

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اصْبِرُوا وَصَابِرُوا وَرَابِطُوا

O you who have faith, remain firm, contend in steadfastness, and preserve your bond and readiness.

Al 'Imran 3:200

اصبروا directs attention to the firmness of the person or community itself. صابروا can point to firmness before an opposing force that tries to wear you down or push you out of your position: do not be less enduring than the force against you.

The translation “contend in steadfastness” preserves the difference between this form and the simple imperative اصبروا. “Outlast them in steadfastness” may also approach the meaning in some contexts, but it should not become a competition for moral superiority; the axis is not retreating before the endurance of the opposing force.

اصْطَبَرَ and اصْطَبِرْ are form VIII and can indicate effort, continuity, and actively engaging the self in sabr.

فَاعْبُدْهُ وَاصْطَبِرْ لِعِبَادَتِهِ

So serve Him, and keep yourself firm with endurance in His service.

Maryam 19:65

وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِالصَّلَاةِ وَاصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا

Command your family to salat, and remain firm upon it with sustained endurance.

Ta-Ha 20:132

اصطبر in such contexts points to a sabr that must be preserved and rebuilt continuously. The issue is not merely enduring a sudden incident; it is continuing in an act that exhaustion, occupation, or slackness can interrupt.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of ص ب ر is preventing release and departure. A pressure exists, and something is exposed to that pressure such that it may leave its place, scatter, escape, or react. Sabr is the force that holds it.

In human use, what is restrained is the nafs. The nafs may want to flee, surrender, take revenge, rush, issue a premature judgment, speak beyond the limit, or abandon a difficult responsibility. Sabr means holding the nafs in a direction the person has chosen.

But the root ص ب ر, before context determines it, does not itself mean “being upon truth.” The Qur'an also reports this root in the speech of those who were insisting upon false objects of worship:

إِنْ كَادَ لَيُضِلُّنَا عَنْ آلِهَتِنَا لَوْلَا أَنْ صَبَرْنَا عَلَيْهَا

He almost diverted us from our objects of worship, had we not remained firm upon them.

Al-Furqan 25:42

وَانْطَلَقَ الْمَلَأُ مِنْهُمْ أَنِ امْشُوا وَاصْبِرُوا عَلَىٰ آلِهَتِكُمْ

The chiefs among them went off, saying: go on, and remain firm upon your objects of worship.

Sad 38:6

In these verses, the Qur'an does not approve their behavior. It uses a word that they themselves used for remaining upon their position. They too restrained themselves and their group from leaving those objects of worship. Thus the lexical core of sabr remains on both sides: holding oneself upon a position.

The difference between sabr upon truth and persistence upon falsehood is not in the psychological capacity to remain, but in the object, criterion, and direction of that remaining. Sabr becomes positively Qur'anic when it serves truth, taqwa, reform, justice, and avoidance of transgression. If the same force of staying is used for fanatic attachment, group heat, or defense of falsehood, the capacity for firmness remains, but it has no guidance value.

Therefore, fanatic attachment and sabr are not full synonyms. Fanatic attachment usually points to a person being locked into identity, habit, or group-belonging. Sabr concerns the act of holding and not leaving a position. Fanatic attachment can motivate sabr upon falsehood, but not every sabr is fanatic attachment, and not every fanatic attachment necessarily includes measured self-restraint.

Material Image

The material image of sabr is something held back against the pull to move, flee, scatter, or be released. In older Arabic usage, sabr was used in the field of confinement and restraint. This layer remains clearly visible in the Qur'anic structure below:

وَاصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُمْ

Hold your nafs with those who call upon their Lord.

Al-Kahf 18:28

The presence of نفسك as a direct object reveals the root meaning: hold your own nafs; do not let the eye's desire for the adornment of worldly life pull you away from accompanying this group.

One old image connected with this root is sabir, reported in Arabic lexicons as a white, dense cloud, or a cloud whose parts are stacked upon one another and remain in place for a time. In some lexical reports, it is as if the parts of the cloud are held or confined upon one another. This image helps us feel the root: parts do not scatter under wind pressure; they preserve density and cohesion.

Still, one should not derive every Qur'anic meaning of sabr directly from the word sabir. Sabir is one branch and material image in the word family, not the full definition of every use of sabr. Its value is that it makes the image of preserving cohesion and preventing scattering tangible.

The same image appears in the prayer of those facing external pressure:

رَبَّنَا أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًا وَثَبِّتْ أَقْدَامَنَا

Our Lord, pour sabr upon us and make our feet firm.

Al-Baqarah 2:250

Here sabr is not merely inner calm. It is a force that prevents slipping feet, flight, and collapse of the ranks.

Classical Usage

Classical Arabic lexicons usually place “confinement” at the center of sabr. sabr an-nafs means holding the self and preventing it from doing, without restraint, what it desires. This holding may be before panic, anger, desire, escape, or abandoning responsibility.

In this same field, sabr was also used for detaining and keeping a person or creature under control. Old expressions such as “qatl sabran” refer to someone being killed after having first been held and deprived of the possibility of escape. This usage shows that the meaning of confinement and preventing release is not merely a later ethical expression.

In Arabic lexicons, “the month of sabr” is reported for the month of fasting, and it is explained that fasting is linked to sabr because the human being restrains the self from eating, drinking, and sexual relations. This use makes fasting a concrete example of sabr: active restraint of desire in a defined time.

Even so, one cannot conclude from this usage that whenever the Qur'an uses sabr, its meaning must be fasting. Interpreting sabr in Al-Baqarah 2:45 as fasting is reported in some tafsir sources, but lexically it is better to understand fasting as one clear instance of sabr, not the sole and fixed meaning of the word. Sabr in the Qur'an has a much broader field, including calamity, responsibility, battle, knowledge, waiting, anger, and social firmness.

In Biblical Hebrew, a cognate root close to ص ب ر is used for piling up, stacking, and gathering things into a heap. This cautious comparison can suggest a semantic field of gathering and preventing scattering in Semitic languages. Yet similarity of letters and meaning alone is not enough to prove that all Arabic meanings of sabr arose directly from that Hebrew usage. Therefore, this comparison should remain a cautious comparative witness, not a definitive origin of the Qur'anic meaning.

Firmness Before Fear, Loss, and Pressure

One of the clearest Qur'anic uses of sabr is holding oneself during fear, hunger, decrease of wealth, injury to lives, and loss:

وَلَنَبْلُوَنَّكُمْ بِشَيْءٍ مِنَ الْخَوْفِ وَالْجُوعِ وَنَقْصٍ مِنَ الْأَمْوَالِ وَالْأَنْفُسِ وَالثَّمَرَاتِ وَبَشِّرِ الصَّابِرِينَ

We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger and decrease in wealth, lives, and fruits; and give glad news to those who remain firm.

Al-Baqarah 2:155

Sabr here does not mean denying pain or having no grief. The sabir also feels loss. The difference is that loss cannot sever his connection with his direction and criterion.

Firmness Before Harm and Denial

وَلَنَصْبِرَنَّ عَلَىٰ مَا آذَيْتُمُونَا

We will surely remain firm before the harm you have caused us.

Ibrahim 14:12

Here sabr means that harm cannot push the person out of his responsibility, speech, or position.

فَصَبَرُوا عَلَىٰ مَا كُذِّبُوا وَأُوذُوا حَتَّىٰ أَتَاهُمْ نَصْرُنَا

They remained firm before the denial and harm they faced, until Our help came to them.

Al-An'am 6:34

The preposition على highlights the pressure and weight of the matter. Denial and harm were like a burden upon them, yet under that pressure they did not leave their direction.

Restraining Reaction and Preventing Transgression

In An-Nahl 16:126, the Qur'an separates proportionate response from excessive revenge:

وَإِنْ عَاقَبْتُمْ فَعَاقِبُوا بِمِثْلِ مَا عُوقِبْتُمْ بِهِ وَلَئِنْ صَبَرْتُمْ لَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لِلصَّابِرِينَ

If you respond with punishment, respond with the like of what was done to you; but if you remain firm, that is better for those who remain firm.

An-Nahl 16:126

Sabr in this context is not unconditional acceptance of oppression. The verse does not negate the right of proportionate response. But it presents sabr as the capacity to restrain the desire to expand revenge, break limits, and respond hastily.

The same connection appears with forgiving:

وَلَمَنْ صَبَرَ وَغَفَرَ إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ لَمِنْ عَزْمِ الْأُمُورِ

Whoever remains firm and forgives, that is among the matters of resolve.

Ash-Shura 42:43

Sabr and ghafr are not the same thing. Sabr first holds the person back from an uncontrolled reaction; ghafr may be another stage in which the person gives up punishment or pursuing his right.

Firmness in Action and Responsibility

وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِالصَّلَاةِ وَاصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا

Command your family to salat, and remain firm upon it with sustained endurance.

Ta-Ha 20:132

Here sabr is not a reaction to a sudden calamity. The issue is continuing a responsibility that repetition, fatigue, occupation, or slow results may wear down.

فَاعْبُدْهُ وَاصْطَبِرْ لِعِبَادَتِهِ

Serve Him, and keep firm in His service with sustained endurance.

Maryam 19:65

In this use, sabr means holding direction over time.

Sabr Before the Limits of Knowledge

The story of Musa and the servant to whom The God had taught knowledge reveals a cognitive dimension of sabr:

وَكَيْفَ تَصْبِرُ عَلَىٰ مَا لَمْ تُحِطْ بِهِ خُبْرًا

How can you remain firm before something you have not encompassed in knowledge?

Al-Kahf 18:68

This verse does not reduce inability to have sabr always to moral corruption or weak will. Sometimes a person cannot remain steady because he lacks enough information to understand an event. What he sees conflicts with his present standards, but he does not yet know the background and outcome.

In such a situation, sabr does not mean suspending reason or accepting every claim without question. Cognitive sabr means withholding final judgment, decisive objection, or hasty conclusion until the data becomes complete.

Musa was not indifferent toward what he saw. His objection was formed on the basis of his existing knowledge and ethical criterion. The issue was that he did not yet encompass the matter in khubr. This context shows that sabr sometimes requires awareness of the limits of one's own knowledge.

This sabr differs from gullibility. Gullibility leaves questioning and evaluation aside; cognitive sabr holds judgment until the context becomes clear.

Firmness in Confrontation and Preventing Collective Collapse

وَلَا تَنَازَعُوا فَتَفْشَلُوا وَتَذْهَبَ رِيحُكُمْ وَاصْبِرُوا

Do not dispute with one another, lest you weaken and your force depart; and remain firm.

Al-Anfal 8:46

In this context, internal dispute leads to weakness and loss of force. Sabr is a force that restrains people from hasty reactions and scattering, preserving collective cohesion.

The image of the dense sabir cloud, without determining the verse's meaning, can make this state tangible: parts that do not scatter under pressure and preserve their connection.

Sabr With Taqwa

بَلَىٰ إِنْ تَصْبِرُوا وَتَتَّقُوا

Yes, if you remain firm and have taqwa.

Al 'Imran 3:125

Sabr and taqwa are not synonyms in this structure. Sabr is the strength to remain and not flee; taqwa is guarding the limit, criterion, and direction of that remaining.

A person may be very firm, but firm upon falsehood. The pairing of sabr with taqwa shows that the Qur'an does not praise mere psychological resistance; it values resistance that also preserves the limits of The God, justice, and responsibility.

Therefore, Qur'anic rewards for sabr do not concern every kind of stubbornness and staying. When the Qur'an directly commands sabr or praises the sabirin, its context places that sabr in the direction of truth, taqwa, faith, reform, or avoidance of transgression. The use of the same root about mushrikin describes their ability to remain, not a promise of reward for it.

Sabr With Tawakkul

الَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ

Those who remained firm and place tawakkul upon their Lord.

An-Nahl 16:42

Sabr in this combination does not mean total reliance on personal power. The human being does not abandon responsibility and remains on the path, but he also does not imagine the final outcome as his absolute property or the product of his own ability.

Sabr is the quality of the human being's remaining; tawakkul is the quality of his reliance and trust.

Mutual Counsel to Sabr

وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ

They counsel one another to truth, and counsel one another to sabr.

Al-'Asr 103:3

Sabr in this verse is not a completely individual and isolated matter. Remaining upon truth under pressure needs mutual reminder, support, and counsel. Members of a community help one another so that fatigue, fear, or loneliness does not pull them out of the path.

The placement of truth and sabr together is also decisive: mere firmness is not enough; one must know what this firmness serves.

Beautiful Sabr

فَصَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ

So, a beautiful sabr.

Yusuf 12:18 and 12:83

فَاصْبِرْ صَبْرًا جَمِيلًا

Remain firm with a beautiful sabr.

Al-Ma'arij 70:5

Jamil does not describe the quantity of sabr, but its quality. Beautiful sabr is sabr not polluted by ugliness, transgression, lying, humiliation, limitless revenge, or ethical collapse.

This expression does not mean forcibly hiding grief. Ya'qub expresses his grief and brings his complaint to The God, but his grief does not turn into oppression, lying, or uncontrolled revenge.

Firmness upon Falsehood

وَانْطَلَقَ الْمَلَأُ مِنْهُمْ أَنِ امْشُوا وَاصْبِرُوا عَلَىٰ آلِهَتِكُمْ

The chiefs among them went off, saying: go on, and remain firm upon your objects of worship.

Sad 38:6

This use shows that sabr at the lexical level is a human capacity, not the exclusive name of a religious virtue. Opponents too can call one another to not abandon an inherited position.

But their sabr is not the same as the sabr of the prophets in direction. They use the strength of remaining to defend objects of worship that the Qur'an calls false. Thus sharing a root does not mean sharing a value.

Sabr Before the Words and Behavior of Others

فَاصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا يَقُولُونَ

Remain firm before what they say.

Ta-Ha 20:130

In this context, the pressure comes from speech, false attribution, or verbal harm. Sabr means the person does not allow the speech of others to pull him into abandoning his mission or reacting beyond the limit.

This sabr is not absolute silence before every statement. The issue is preserving the self and direction before verbal pressure.

Asking for Sabr as an Added Force

رَبَّنَا أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًا

Our Lord, pour sabr upon us.

Al-Baqarah 2:250

The verb أفرغ creates an image of pouring or emptying something over a person. Sabr here is not merely a fixed trait already present; it is a force the human being needs to be added and made encompassing in the moment of pressure.

Sabr That No Longer Changes the Outcome

اصْلَوْهَا فَاصْبِرُوا أَوْ لَا تَصْبِرُوا سَوَاءٌ عَلَيْكُمْ

Enter it; then remain firm or do not remain firm, it is the same for you.

At-Tur 52:16

Here sabr no longer means a guidance-oriented choice or correction of path. Endurance or panic does not change the final state. This use shows that not every endurance is saving sabr.

Similar Uses

Sabr in calamity and sabr in responsibility: In Al-Baqarah 2:155, sabr is a response to fear, hunger, and loss. In Ta-Ha 20:132, istabir points to continuity in salat and family responsibility. In the first, an external force tries to break the person; in the second, time, repetition, and exhaustion may interrupt the act.

Sabr before harm and sabr before not knowing: In Ibrahim 14:12, pressure comes from the harm of others. In Al-Kahf 18:68, pressure arises from the human being's inability to understand the full event. One is social sabr; the other is cognitive sabr. In cognitive sabr, the person must hold judgment until knowledge becomes more complete.

Sabr and silence: Sabr is not necessarily silence. A person may have sabr and still speak, object, seek his right, or act for reform. What is restrained is not action itself, but limitless, hasty, or direction-destroying reaction.

Sabr and accepting oppression: Sabr in itself does not mean accepting oppression or abandoning defense. In An-Nahl 16:126, the possibility of proportionate response remains, but transgression is forbidden. Thus sabr can accompany defense, claiming one's right, and measured action.

Sabr upon truth and sabr upon falsehood: The sabr of prophets before denial and the sabr of mushrikin upon their objects of worship are not equal in object and value. The lexical core of both is remaining and not abandoning a position; but the criterion of truth, taqwa, and justice separates their value.

Sabr and obstinacy: Obstinacy usually means continuing a position after error has become clear, out of self-regard or refusal to accept truth. Sabr does not contain that judgment by itself. If a person stays upon error after truth becomes clear, his firmness may be obstinacy. If he remains under pressure without leaving the right criterion, his firmness is praised sabr.

Sabr and fanatic attachment: Fanatic attachment often arises from closed belonging to group, lineage, tradition, or identity. Sabr focuses on the act of holding. Fanatic attachment can make a person sabir upon falsehood, but praised Qur'anic sabr is measured by taqwa and truth, not by intensity of group attachment.

Grammar and Structure

Sabr with 'ala: The structure صبر على is very common in the Qur'an: sabr before harm, before people's speech, before calamity, before responsibility, before something not yet fully understood, and upon objects of worship in the speech of mushrikin.

The preposition على presents the object of sabr as a pressure, burden, or matter placed upon the person. صبر على means that under the weight of that matter, the person does not lose direction or cohesion.

But على by itself does not determine the value of the object. The same structure appears for the harm faced by prophets and for mushrikin remaining upon their objects of worship. Value is determined by the object of sabr and the context.

Sabr with lam: In structures such as:

فَاصْبِرْ لِحُكْمِ رَبِّكَ

Remain firm for the judgment of your Lord.

At-Tur 52:48

وَلِرَبِّكَ فَاصْبِرْ

And for your Lord, remain firm.

Al-Muddaththir 74:7

The lam highlights the direction, attachment, or aim of sabr. In this structure, the issue is not only something that weighs upon the person; sabr is preserved for a purpose and in relation to a reference point.

Thus the general difference is this: صبر على more often shows the matter or pressure before which one holds himself. صبر لِـ more often shows the direction, aim, or reference for which one remains firm. This is not a mechanical rule for every verse, but it is very useful in understanding the angle between the verb, its object, and its aim.

Wa-sbir nafsak: In Al-Kahf 18:28, the nafs is the direct object of the verb:

وَاصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ

This structure reveals the root's core: hold your own nafs. The verse does not merely say “be patient”; it specifies what must be restrained and preserved in a certain companionship.

Sabiru: Form III in صابروا indicates confrontation in steadfastness. The simple translation “be patient” removes its difference from اصبروا. Better equivalents include: contend in steadfastness; do not be less enduring than the opposing force; remain firmer before the firmness of opponents.

Istabir: اصطبر, compared with the simple imperative اصبر, can highlight effort, continuity, and enduring a process. In Ta-Ha 20:132 and Maryam 19:65, this form fits remaining firm in worship and salat over time.

فَمَا أَصْبَرَهُمْ عَلَى النَّارِ:

أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ اشْتَرَوُا الضَّلَالَةَ بِالْهُدَىٰ وَالْعَذَابَ بِالْمَغْفِرَةِ فَمَا أَصْبَرَهُمْ عَلَى النَّارِ

Those are the ones who bought misguidance for guidance and punishment for forgiveness; so what has made them so reckless before the Fire?

Al-Baqarah 2:175

The structure ma af'alahum here is an exclamatory form. The amazement is not at a praised virtue or commendable patience, but at the astonishing behavior of a human being who acts as if he can endure the Fire or has no fear of it.

The ground of the amazement appears in the previous clause: they knowingly chose something whose result is punishment. It is as if their behavior shows that the Fire is bearable to them. Therefore, the literal translation “how patient they are over the Fire” may sound praiseful in English and hide the direction of the amazement.

Translations such as these can preserve the context better: what has made them so reckless before the Fire? How do they dare so before the Fire? How astonishing is this readiness for the Fire. Still, it must remain clear that these translations explain the context of the exclamation; they do not turn the meaning of sabr into a completely separate word such as arrogance.

Nearby Roots

ص ب ر is close to ح ب س. Habs points to detaining, confining, or directly preventing movement and release. Sabr is connected to this field, but in human use it is usually a force the person applies upon his own nafs. Habs can be fully imposed from outside; sabr is usually inward self-restraint or voluntary firmness.

ص ب ر differs from ث ب ت. Thabat points to being firm, becoming established, and not slipping from place. Sabr is the holding force before pressure; thabat is the state or result of remaining firm.

رَبَّنَا أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًا وَثَبِّتْ أَقْدَامَنَا

Our Lord, pour sabr upon us and make our feet firm.

Al-Baqarah 2:250

Sabr and making firm are close, but not complete synonyms.

ص ب ر stands opposite ج ز ع. Jaza' is agitated reaction, panic, and loss of restraint before pain. Sabr, in contrast, is preserving cohesion and preventing collapse.

سَوَاءٌ عَلَيْنَا أَجَزِعْنَا أَمْ صَبَرْنَا

It is the same for us whether we panic or remain firm.

Ibrahim 14:21

ص ب ر differs from ح ل م. Hilm is more about restraining anger, not rushing in reaction, and behaving with deliberation before provocation. Sabr is broader and applies to calamity, responsibility, knowledge, battle, poverty, harm, and waiting.

ص ب ر is not identical with ك ظ م. Kazm points to swallowing down something that has accumulated inside and is ready to come out, especially anger. Sabr can include swallowing anger, but is not limited to anger. A person may have sabr before fear, desire, fatigue, loss, or not knowing.

ص ب ر differs from أ ن ي. Anah relates to slowness, deliberation, and not rushing before a decision. Sabr may include deliberation, but its core is holding oneself before pressure and the urge to leave a position.

ص ب ر is not the same as ت ح م ل. Tahammul focuses on carrying a burden. Sabr emphasizes restraining the nafs and preserving direction under that burden. A person may carry suffering, yet be pulled by it into oppression, hatred, or transgression; therefore, not every endurance is praised Qur'anic sabr.

ص ب ر is close to ر ب ط. Rabt relates to tying, connecting, and strengthening a bond. In Al 'Imran 3:200, اصبروا, صابروا, and رابطوا come one after another. Isbiru: remain firm yourselves. Sabiru: contend in firmness against the opposing force. Rabitu: preserve your bond, readiness, and collective position.

ص ب ر differs from ت و ك ل. Tawakkul relates to reliance, trust, and entrusting the outcome to The God. Sabr relates to holding oneself on the path. One is the quality of trust; the other is the quality of standing firm.

ص ب ر is close to ع ز م. 'Azm indicates firm decision and will that removes scattering and hesitation. Sabr is the force that preserves that decision under pressure. Therefore, the Qur'an counts sabr and ghafr among “the matters of resolve”:

وَلَمَنْ صَبَرَ وَغَفَرَ إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ لَمِنْ عَزْمِ الْأُمُورِ

Ash-Shura 42:43

ص ب ر is not identical with ح م ي. Hamiyyah is close to stirred zeal, fanatic heat, and defense of identity or group. This force may keep a person firm upon falsehood. Sabr focuses on the act of holding; hamiyyah can be one motive for that holding. When sabr is fed by jahili hamiyyah, firmness exists, but its direction is not truth.

ص ب ر differs from ل ج ج. Lajajah is close to continuing opposition or error after the matter has become clear. Sabr is not necessarily opposition to truth. Sabr upon truth means pressure cannot push the person out of the right criterion; obstinacy means truth cannot pull the person out of his wrong position.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For ص ب ر, no single mechanical equivalent works in every verse. “Patience” is suitable in some contexts, but at times it suggests a calm and passive state that does not carry the full field of the root.

صَبَرَ: remained firm; held himself; showed steadfastness; endured; stayed in his position; did not leave the path under pressure.

يَصْبِرُ: remains firm; holds himself; endures; does not collapse under pressure; does not leave his position.

صَبْر: sabr; firmness; steadfastness; holding the nafs; restraining reaction; preserving cohesion; conscious endurance on the path.

صَابِر: steadfast; patient; one who remains firm; one who holds himself under pressure; one who does not leave his position.

صَبَّار: deeply steadfast; very patient; persistently enduring; one who repeatedly holds himself under pressure.

اصْبِرُوا: remain firm; show steadfastness; hold yourselves; do not retreat under pressure.

صَابِرُوا: contend in steadfastness; do not be less enduring than the opposing force; remain firmer before the endurance of opponents.

اصْطَبِرْ: remain firm with continuity; keep yourself upon it continually; endure in it with effort; do not become worn down and broken off in doing it.

وَاصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ: hold your nafs; keep yourself firm with them; do not let your nafs leave their company.

وَاصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا أَصَابَكَ: remain firm before what reaches you; hold yourself under the pressure of what has reached you; do not let what reached you push you out of the path.

صَبْرٌ جَمِيلٌ: beautiful sabr; firmness free of ugly reaction; patience with preservation of limit and dignity; firmness not polluted by transgression or ethical collapse.

وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ: they counsel one another to steadfastness; they help one another in standing firm; they counsel one another to preserve themselves upon the path.

فَمَا أَصْبَرَهُمْ عَلَى النَّارِ: what has made them so reckless before the Fire? How do they dare so before the Fire? How astonishing is this readiness for the Fire.

Summary

The root ص ب ر indicates holding, restraining, controlling, and preventing escape or scattering. Its material image is something that does not break apart under pressure or get released from its place: a restrained nafs, or a dense cloud whose parts remain together.

In the Qur'an, this root extends from firmness in calamity and harm to continuity in responsibility, restraining revenge, preserving collective cohesion, withholding hasty judgment, and remaining on the path. Its use about mushrikin shows that, at the lexical level, sabr is the capacity to remain; whether it is upon truth or falsehood is determined by the object, criterion, and direction of the sabr.

Sabr overlaps with confinement, firmness, hilm, kazm, endurance, deliberation, resolve, tawakkul, hamiyyah, and obstinacy, but is not identical with any of them. Its special core is holding the nafs or position before a force that pulls the human being toward escape, uncontrolled reaction, abandonment of responsibility, or collapse.

Sources Note

Arabic lexicon reports about the original sense of confinement, the use of sabir for a white or dense cloud, and the expression “the month of sabr” for the month of fasting appear in Lisan al-'Arab under the entry for sabr. The same source states that fasting is linked to sabr because it restrains the nafs from eating, drinking, and sexual relations.

The Hebrew use of צבר in the meaning of stacking, piling up, and gathering into a heap is confirmed; nevertheless, it is used here only as a cautious comparison, and no definitive origin of the Arabic meaning is derived from it.

Interpreting sabr in Al-Baqarah 2:45 as fasting is reported in some tafsir sources, but this report is one transmitted interpretation and should not restrict the broad Qur'anic meaning of sabr to fasting.
