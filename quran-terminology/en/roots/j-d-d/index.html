Principal meanings: newness and renewal; cutting and separation; a distinct stripe or band; greatness and majesty; and, in other branches, fortune, earnestness, and ancestry

The Arabic root ج د د forms a family with several semantic branches. جَدِيد, جُدَد, جَدّ, جِدّ, and جَدّ meaning grandfather share consonants but have different uses.

جَدِيد describes something new or contrasted with something worn. In descriptions of mountains, جُدَد denotes distinct stripes or bands of differing colors. جَدّ can express greatness, majesty, a share, or fortune; جِدّ concerns earnestness and effort, contrasting with jest or slackness.

جَدّ also names a father's father or a mother's father.

These branches should not be forced into a single definition. Associations between cutting, separation, distinction, and newness do not by themselves establish that every meaning historically developed from one original image.

The root occurs ten times in the Quran: eight instances of جَدِيد, one of جُدَد, and one of جَدّ. These concern new creation, colored mountain bands, and the Lord's majesty, respectively.

1. Meanings in early Arabic

جَدّ: fortune, a share, and prosperity

Fortune, a share, and prosperity are established early senses of جَدّ.

Calling someone ذُو جَدّ could mean that the person enjoyed good fortune, wealth, or a favorable position.

A verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Imruʾ al-Qays reads:

وَقَاهُمْ جَدُّهُمْ بِبَنِي أَبِيهِمْ
وَبِالْأَشْقَيْنَ مَا كَانَ الْعِقَابُ

waqāhum jadduhum bi-banī abīhim / wa-bi-al-ashqayna mā kāna al-ʿiqābu

“Their fortune protected them at their kinsmen's expense; punishment fell upon the unfortunate ones.”

The line accompanies an account in which one group escaped harm while others suffered instead. الْأَشْقَيْنَ here is plural and should not be translated as “those two unfortunate people.”

جَدُّهُمْ does not mean their grandfather here: it concerns the fortune or favorable share credited with their escape.

The witness illustrates the branch concerning fortune and prosperity.

Early sources attribute the line to Imruʾ al-Qays. As with other poetic witnesses, attribution to a pre-Islamic poet should be distinguished from independently dating the individual line.

جُدَّة: a distinct line or stripe

Another branch describes a line, stripe, or portion of a surface distinguished from its surroundings.

جُدَّة can denote a visible line on a surface, such as a stripe along an animal's back.

Another verse attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays reads:

كَأَنَّ سَرَاتَهُ وَجُدَّةَ مَتْنِهِ
كَنَائِنُ يَجْرِي فَوْقَهُنَّ دَلِيصُ

ka-anna sarātahu wa-juddata matnihi / kanāʾinu yajri fawqahunna dalīṣu

“Its back and the stripe along it seemed like quivers over which a sheen flowed.”

The poet describes a wild ass and the distinct stripe along its back.

جُدَّة is the line or stripe distinguished in appearance and color from the rest of the back.

Its meaning here is neither newness nor greatness, but a distinct surface marking.

Variants include مَتْنِهِ and ظَهْرِهِ, and فَوْقَهُنَّ and بَيْنَهُنَّ. These do not alter the relevant sense of جُدَّة.

This use helps explain جُدَد in Surah Fatir.

جَدِيد: new rather than worn

جَدِيد contrasts with خَلَق, old and worn.

ثَوْبٌ جَدِيدٌ is a newly made garment or one not yet worn out.

Some lexical explanations connect جَدِيد with جَدَّ, to cut: cloth is cut in making a garment, and a freshly cut and made garment is new.

Such an explanation differs from historical proof of origin. A semantic connection alone does not establish that “new” necessarily arose from cutting cloth.

A verse attributed to Labīd ibn Rabīʿah reads:

تَحَمَّلَ أَهْلُهَا وَأَجَدَّ فِيهَا
نِعَاجُ الصَّيْفِ أَخْبِيَةَ الظِّلَالِ

taḥammala ahluhā wa-ajadda fīhā / niʿāju al-ṣayfi akhbiyata al-ẓilāli

“Its people departed, and the wild cows of summer established fresh shelters of shade there.”

أَجَدَّ expresses making or establishing something anew; أَخْبِيَةَ الظِّلَالِ pictures the animals' shaded resting places as tents. The line does not mean that the animals literally built human tents.

Labīd lived across the pre-Islamic and early Islamic periods. Without an independently established date for this line, it cannot confidently be called pre-Quranic; it nevertheless witnesses the vocabulary of newness around that period.

The Quran itself uses جَدِيد for newness, establishing this sense in the Arabic of revelation.

جِدّ: earnestness rather than jest

جِدّ, with a kasrah on jīm, contrasts with هَزْل.

هَزْل concerns joking or lack of seriousness, while جِدّ denotes earnestness, application, and serious attention to a task.

جَدَّ فِي الْأَمْرِ means to apply oneself seriously and diligently to a matter.

Someone may talk about a task without attempting it. A person who works attentively and persistently toward a goal can be called جَادّ or مُجِدّ.

Earnestness alone does not determine an action's moral value: someone can work diligently toward either a good or a bad end.

This branch is established in Arabic, but جِدّ meaning earnestness or effort does not occur in the Quran.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important forms include:

جَدِيد: new or fresh, contrasted with something worn or with an earlier state.

جِدَّة: newness, contrasted with wear.

جَدَّدَ: renewed something or gave it a new state.

تَجَدَّدَ: became new or occurred anew.

تَجْدِيد: renewing or making new.

جَدَّ: in one use, cut or severed, as with cloth or harvesting produce.

جَدَاد and جِدَاد: harvesting or cutting produce, especially dates.

جُدَّة: a distinct line, stripe, or track on a surface.

جُدَد: plural of جُدَّة; distinct lines or bands.

جَادَّة: a clear, recognizable road, especially a main route.

جَدَد: in some lexical uses, level, firm, or even ground.

جَدّ: greatness, majesty, a share, fortune, or prosperity, according to context.

جِدّ: earnestness, application, and effort, contrasted with jest.

جَادّ: an earnest or diligent person.

جَدّ: a paternal or maternal grandfather.

جَدَّة: a paternal or maternal grandmother.

Vowels and word structure matter when distinguishing these forms.

جُدَد in Fatir 35:27 is the plural of جُدَّة, a stripe or band. جُدُد, with a ḍammah on the first dāl, can be the plural of جَدِيد, new.

جَدّ in Al-Jinn 72:3 means majesty or greatness, not grandfather.

Shared consonants do not make all the meanings identical.

3. جَدِيد: new creation

Most Quranic occurrences of the root are instances of جَدِيد.

In all of them, the adjective accompanies خَلْق.

خَلْق belongs to خ ل ق and concerns creation or what is created; جَدِيد describes its newness.

خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ therefore means “a new creation.”

The expression occurs in two different contexts: recreating people after death, and God's power to remove a group and bring a new creation.

New creation after death

Ar-Ra'd 13:5

وَإِنْ تَعْجَبْ فَعَجَبٌ قَوْلُهُمْ أَإِذَا كُنَّا تُرَابًا أَإِنَّا لَفِي خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ ۗ أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا بِرَبِّهِمْ ۖ وَأُولَٰئِكَ الْأَغْلَالُ فِي أَعْنَاقِهِمْ ۖ وَأُولَٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ ۖ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ

wa-in taʿjab fa-ʿajabun qawluhum a-idhā kunnā turāban a-innā la-fī khalqin jadīd ulāʾika alladhīna kafarū bi-rabbihim wa-ulāʾika al-aghlālu fī aʿnāqihim wa-ulāʾika aṣḥābu al-nāri hum fīhā khālidūn

Al-Raʿd 13:5

“If you are astonished, astonishing is their statement: ‘When we have become dust, will we really be in a new creation?’ Those are the ones who disbelieved in their Lord; shackles are upon their necks, and they are the companions of the Fire, remaining therein.”

Those denying resurrection express astonishment at being created again after the body becomes dust.

جَدِيد concerns a fresh state in relation to the earlier one.

Before death a person has a recognizable body and form; afterward the body may decay into dust.

They ask whether, after such dissolution, they can be in a new creation again.

جَدِيد here does not mean another person or a new generation: it describes the new creation in which those same people are raised.

The adjective alone does not specify how recreation occurs, the body's nature, or the mechanism of return.

Those questions require examination of passages about creation, death, resurrection, and life.

Al-Isra 17:49

وَقَالُوا أَإِذَا كُنَّا عِظَامًا وَرُفَاتًا أَإِنَّا لَمَبْعُوثُونَ خَلْقًا جَدِيدًا

wa-qālū a-idhā kunnā ʿiẓāman wa-rufātan a-innā la-mabʿūthūna khalqan jadīdā

Al-Isrāʾ 17:49

“They said: ‘When we have become bones and crumbled remains, will we really be raised as a new creation?’”

The same question appears through a different image.

Ar-Ra'd 13:5 mentions dust; this verse mentions bones and crumbled remains.

In both, جَدِيد qualifies creation.

Dust, bones, and scattered remains are expressed by other words, not by ج د د.

Al-Isra 17:98 repeats the expression concerning being raised after becoming bones and crumbled remains.

In As-Sajdah 32:10, the deniers ask whether, after disappearing into the earth, they will be in a new creation.

In Saba 34:7, the denial accompanies مُزِّقْتُمْ كُلَّ مُمَزَّقٍ: having been thoroughly torn apart and scattered.

In all these cases, newness concerns people's return after bodily dissolution.

The first creation and a new creation

Qaf 50:15

أَفَعَيِينَا بِالْخَلْقِ الْأَوَّلِ ۚ بَلْ هُمْ فِي لَبْسٍ مِنْ خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ

a-fa-ʿayīnā bi-al-khalqi al-awwali bal hum fī labsin min khalqin jadīd

Qāf 50:15

“Were We exhausted by the first creation? Rather, they are in confusion about a new creation.”

The verse presents an important contrast:

الْخَلْقِ الْأَوَّلِ and خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ.

أَوَّل expresses being first, whereas جَدِيد expresses being new.

The verse sets the ability to create initially against doubt about new creation.

جَدِيد need not mean something appearing for the very first time in all existence.

Something may be new relative to an earlier state or creation even when it recreates something that existed before.

For example, someone can rebuild an old house. The building is newly made, without erasing the place's prior existence or its history of having a house.

This example only clarifies the relational sense of “new”; human building does not explain the manner of creation in the Hereafter.

The passage concerns God's creative power and human doubt about recreation.

جَدِيد alone does not settle whether all earlier bodily components return unchanged or are replaced by entirely different ones.

It expresses the newness of the creation.

A new creation replacing another group

Ibrahim 14:19

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بِالْحَقِّ ۚ إِنْ يَشَأْ يُذْهِبْكُمْ وَيَأْتِ بِخَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ

a-lam tara anna allāha khalaqa al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍa bi-al-ḥaqq in yashaʾ yudhhibkum wa-yaʾti bi-khalqin jadīd

Ibrāhīm 14:19

“Have you not seen that The God created the heavens and the earth in truth? If He wills, He will remove you and bring a new creation.”

خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ occurs in a different construction here.

The statement concerns removing the addressees and bringing a new creation.

Unlike the resurrection passages, it does not directly describe those same people being raised after death; it expresses God's power to remove a group and bring new creation.

جَدِيد is therefore not intrinsically a technical term for resurrection.

It is a general adjective of newness; the sentence and context determine the expression's precise application.

Fatir 35:16

إِنْ يَشَأْ يُذْهِبْكُمْ وَيَأْتِ بِخَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ

in yashaʾ yudhhibkum wa-yaʾti bi-khalqin jadīd

Fāṭir 35:16

“If He wills, He will remove you and bring a new creation.”

The same construction occurs here.

Fatir 35:15 describes people's need for God and God's independence.

New creation is thus discussed in relation to God's power and independence and creatures' dependence on Him.

These two verses prevent defining جَدِيد in every Quranic occurrence as exclusively recreating a dead human being.

It expresses newness, whether in return after death or in bringing another creation instead of an earlier group.

4. جُدَد: tracks and colored mountain bands

Fatir 35:27

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ أَنْزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَأَخْرَجْنَا بِهِ ثَمَرَاتٍ مُخْتَلِفًا أَلْوَانُهَا ۚ وَمِنَ الْجِبَالِ جُدَدٌ بِيضٌ وَحُمْرٌ مُخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَانُهَا وَغَرَابِيبُ سُودٌ

a-lam tara anna allāha anzala mina al-samāʾi māʾan fa-akhrajnā bihi thamarātin mukhtalifan alwānuhā wa-mina al-jibāli judadun bīḍun wa-ḥumrun mukhtalifun alwānuhā wa-gharābību sūd

Fāṭir 35:27

“Have you not seen that The God sent water down from the sky, and with it We brought forth fruits of different colors? Among the mountains are white and red bands of differing colors, and intensely black formations.”

جُدَد is the plural of جُدَّة.

جُدَّة denotes a distinct line, track, or band on a surface, particularly when its color differs from its surroundings.

Some mountains display bands of stone in different colors, extending across their surfaces: white, red, dark, or other shades.

This illustrates the sense of جُدَد in the verse.

Why جُدَد does not mean new things here

Its written resemblance to plurals of جَدِيد can cause confusion.

Here it denotes lines or tracks, not newly made things.

Several contextual indications support this reading.

First, it appears with الْجِبَال: mountains are the subject.

Second, white and red immediately follow.

Third, غَرَابِيبُ سُودٌ describes intense blackness.

Fourth, the verse begins with fruits of different colors, while Fatir 35:28 continues with differing colors among people, moving creatures, and livestock.

Color variation in creation is therefore central to this sequence.

جُدَد accordingly refers to distinct bands in the mountains.

Does جُدَد mean roads?

جَادَّة names a clear, recognizable road and belongs to this family.

Not every جُدَّة, however, is necessarily a road used by people.

A stripe on an animal's back can also be called جُدَّة, although no one travels along it.

The colors in the Fatir passage support understanding visible tracks or bands in the mountains. A broader sense of natural paths is also attested; the word does not require a human-built road.

“Colored bands” or “colored tracks” therefore offer clear explanations.

“Roads” may suggest constructed routes for human travel, which the passage's mountain colors do not establish.

The verse does not by itself explain each band's geological origin, mineral composition, or formation date.

Those are geological questions and should not replace the lexical meaning of جُدَد.

5. جَدّ: the Lord's greatness and majesty

Al-Jinn 72:3

وَأَنَّهُ تَعَالَىٰ جَدُّ رَبِّنَا مَا اتَّخَذَ صَاحِبَةً وَلَا وَلَدًا

wa-annahu taʿālā jaddu rabbinā mā ittakhadha ṣāḥibatan wa-lā waladā

Al-Jinn 72:3

“And exalted is the majesty of our Lord; He has taken neither a consort nor a child.”

جَدّ here denotes majesty, greatness, and grandeur.

Arabic dictionaries record greatness, majesty, fortune, wealth, and sufficiency among its senses.

Here the context emphasizes the Lord's greatness and exaltedness.

تَعَالَىٰ جَدُّ رَبِّنَا declares His majesty exalted.

It is followed by denial that He has taken a consort or a child.

The speakers thus declare God's greatness and transcendence of human relationships of consort and offspring.

Does جَدّ mean grandfather here?

No.

Although جَدّ also names a grandfather, that kinship sense does not fit this verse.

تَعَالَىٰ جَدُّ رَبِّنَا concerns the exaltedness of the Lord's majesty.

Identical consonants and vowels do not justify importing the family relationship into this passage.

Is sufficiency another possible interpretation?

جَدّ can also concern prosperity, wealth, or sufficiency.

Some lexical explanations accordingly connect جَدُّ رَبِّنَا with the Lord's independence from need.

That interpretation relates to His having no need for consort or offspring. Forqan's proposed direct translation is “majesty” or “greatness,” fitting تَعَالَىٰ and the passage; this preference does not invalidate the attested interpretation of sufficiency.

Human material wealth should not be projected onto God, nor should divine majesty be reduced to bodily size or rank in a human social structure.

“Majesty” expresses the Lord's greatness and grandeur here.

This particular use does not make every derivative of ج د د refer to divine majesty.

6. Comparison with related roots

جَدِيد and حُدُوث

جَدِيد and حَدِيث can both concern newness, but their ranges of use differ.

جَدِيد expresses newness relative to something worn or an earlier state.

حَدِيث has a lexical use concerning recent occurrence and Quranic uses concerning speech or discourse.

خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ, for example, means a new creation, not a new discourse.

Their overlap in newness does not make them interchangeable everywhere.

جَدِيد and بَدْء

بَدْء concerns beginning and initiating creation.

جَدِيد concerns being new.

Qaf 50:15 makes the distinction clear by contrasting الْخَلْقِ الْأَوَّلِ with خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ.

The first creation is initial in sequence; a new creation can be described relative to an earlier one.

Newness is therefore not always first existence.

جَدِيد and عَوْد

ع و د concerns returning and doing something again.

جَدِيد concerns newness.

In resurrection, both concepts can apply to the same event: a person's return in a new creation.

Return and newness nevertheless remain distinct meanings.

ع و د expresses return; ج د د expresses newness.

خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ should not invariably be translated merely as return: Ibrahim 14:19 and Fatir 35:16 concern new creation replacing another group.

جُدَد and طَرِيق

طَرِيق concerns a road or route.

جُدَّة can also denote a distinct line or track.

A colored mountain band need not be a traversable route.

In Fatir 35:27, the color descriptions support distinct mountain bands.

The relationship between track and road does not make all uses of the two families synonymous.

جَدّ and مَجْد

مَجْد concerns greatness, honor, and majesty.

جَدّ in Al-Jinn 72:3 likewise expresses majesty and greatness.

They overlap here but belong to different roots.

Other senses of جَدّ, such as fortune, a share, or grandfather, should not be transferred to م ج د.

“Majesty” is appropriate here, without erasing the distinctions between the roots.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

جَدِيدٍ / جَدِيدًا — Ar-Ra'd 13:5, Al-Isra 17:49, Al-Isra 17:98, As-Sajdah 32:10, Saba 34:7, and Qaf 50:15

Brief root meaning: newness and renewal; other branches concern cutting, distinct lines or bands, and greatness.

Contextual meaning: the newness of creation relative to an earlier creation or condition, in resurrection after death.

Proposed translation: “a new creation.”

Al-Isra 17:49 and Al-Isra 17:98 use accusative جَدِيدًا, which must be translated within that construction.

“Be raised as a new creation” is natural in these passages.

جَدِيدٍ — Ibrahim 14:19 and Fatir 35:16

Brief root meaning: newness and renewal.

Contextual meaning: a new creation God can bring after removing the addressees.

Proposed translation: “a new creation.”

It is better not to restrict this to “a new generation”: خَلْق is broader, and the expression does not itself specify the new creation's nature or identity.

جُدَدٌ — Fatir 35:27

Brief root meaning: distinct lines or bands; other branches include newness, cutting, and majesty.

Contextual meaning: distinct colored tracks or bands in mountains.

Proposed translation: “bands” or “streaks.”

“Colored mountain bands” is a useful explanation, but since the colors follow in the verse, “colored” need not be added to the independent translation of جُدَد.

جَدُّ — Al-Jinn 72:3

Brief root meaning: greatness, majesty, and prosperity; other branches concern newness, cutting, and earnestness.

Contextual meaning: the Lord's greatness and majesty.

Proposed translation: “the majesty of our Lord.”

The full expression تَعَالَىٰ جَدُّ رَبِّنَا can be rendered:

“Exalted is the majesty of our Lord.”

Here جَدّ means neither grandfather nor effort or earnestness.

8. Conclusion

ج د د has several established semantic branches that should not all be reduced to one meaning.

In the newness branch, جَدِيد contrasts with something worn or an earlier state. The Quran uses it with خَلْق for recreation after death and for bringing new creation in place of another group.

In the branch of lines and tracks, جُدَد describes distinct mountain bands of differing colors.

In Al-Jinn 72:3, جَدّ expresses the exalted majesty of the Lord.

Fortune, a share, earnestness, cutting, and ancestral relationships are also established Arabic meanings, but should not be imposed on Quranic occurrences without evidence.

The three Quranic forms show that sharing a root does not entail complete identity of meaning. Form, vowels, grammatical relationships, and context identify the relevant branch.

Final definition of ج د د:

“An Arabic family with several branches: newness and a fresh state; cutting and separation; a distinct surface line or band; greatness, majesty, and prosperity; and separate uses concerning earnestness and ancestry. In the Quran, جَدِيد describes new creation, جُدَد distinct mountain bands, and جَدّ the Lord's greatness and majesty.”
