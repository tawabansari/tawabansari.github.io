Core Quranic meaning: groups of people identified or moving together; other Arabic branches concern sound and music, and smallness or deficiency in quantity.

1. The root and its meaning in early Arabic

Z M R has several semantic branches, chiefly sound, smallness or deficiency, and human groups.

Zamr and zimār concern pipes and certain bird calls, particularly the female ostrich. زَمَرَ بِالْمِزْمَارِ means to play a pipe or wind instrument.

In another branch, زَمِرَ الشَّيْءُ concerns something becoming scant, such as hair or wool. رَجُلٌ زَمِرُ الْمُرُوءَةِ describes a man lacking in honorable character.

زُمْرَة denotes a group or company of people; its plural is زُمَر.

All three fields are recorded in early Arabic, but historical evidence is needed before reducing them to one origin.

One early lexical analysis distinguishes two foundations: smallness and sound. It connects zumrah with sound because people gathering commonly produce a hubbub.

This offers a semantic link between a crowd’s noise and its name, but does not establish that zumrah historically named the sound before naming the group.

Pre-Quranic meaning and the limits of evidence

Dictionary meanings must be distinguished from evidence demonstrably belonging to the pre-Islamic period.

Early lexicons define zumrah as فَوْجٌ مِنَ النَّاسِ or جَمَاعَةٌ مِنَ النَّاسِ: a group of people.

Another explanation reads:

الْجَمَاعَةُ فِي تَفْرِقَةٍ، بَعْضُهَا عَلَى أَثَرِ بَعْضٍ

al-jamāʿatu fī tafriqatin baʿḍuhā ʿalā athari baʿḍ

Meaning: “Separate groups, some following others.”

This matters because zumar need not describe one large crowd assembled together; it can describe distinct groups coming successively.

The evidence examined here does not establish an independently dated pre-Islamic poetic witness for zumrah as a human group.

It therefore does not establish identical usage across every pre-Islamic region and dialect. The Quranic and early lexical meaning is clear, while the word’s earliest origin requires independent evidence.

The sound branch includes ostrich calls and pipe-playing. Familiarity with wind instruments and animal calls makes these uses intelligible, but cannot by itself date their naming with this root.

A defensible early Arabic definition of zumrah is a group of people distinguishable as a company. Where movement is involved, several groups can proceed separately and successively.

The recorded meaning supports this definition; a certain historical connection with sound or small numbers remains unestablished.

2. Important forms and derivatives

زَمَرَ يَزْمِرُ and يَزْمُرُ

In the sound branch, it means making a sound or playing a pipe or wind instrument.

زَمَرَ بِالْمِزْمَارِ

zamara bi-al-mizmār

Meaning: “He played a pipe or wind instrument.”

Applied to an animal:

زَمَرَتِ النَّعَامَةُ

zamarati al-naʿāmatu

Meaning: “The female ostrich called.”

Zimār is recorded for the female ostrich’s call and ʿurār for the male’s.

Animal-call terminology can be specific; this distinction should not become a rule for all birds.

زَمْر, زَمِير, and زَمَرَان

These are recorded verbal nouns in the sound branch.

Zamr here concerns playing or producing sound through a pipe or similar instrument.

Zamīr is also recorded as a verbal noun for pipe-playing; pleasant sound is a contextual description rather than a necessary meaning of the form.

This sound meaning must be distinguished from زُمَر, the broken plural of zumrah meaning groups.

زَامِر and زَمَّار

These denote someone playing a pipe or wind instrument.

Some early accounts favor zammār for a man and zāmirah for a woman; others also record zāmir for a man.

No one form should therefore be presented as the sole correct form throughout Arabic.

زَمَّرَ يُزَمِّرُ

This Form II verb denotes playing a pipe or wind instrument in its sound use.

زَمَّرَ بِالْمِزْمَارِ

zammara bi-al-mizmār

Meaning: “He played a wind instrument.”

It is also recorded for filling a container, although the historical relationship with sound is unclear.

مِزْمَار

It names an instrument sounded by blowing into it.

Its plural is مَزَامِير.

Arabic uses the name for pipes and certain wind instruments.

Later literary and religious uses also associate mazāmīr with voice and melody. Those uses are not necessary meanings of Quranic zumar.

Nor are mazāmīr and zabūr the same derivation: zabūr belongs to Z B R and denotes writing or a book, while mizmār belongs to Z M R.

زَمِرَ يَزْمَرُ

In the smallness branch, it means becoming scant or diminished.

زَمِرَ الشَّعْرُ

zamira al-shaʿru

Meaning: “The hair became sparse.”

زَمِرَ الصُّوفُ

zamira al-ṣūfu

Meaning: “The wool became scant.”

Some uses concern reduced quantity or quality.

زَمِر and زَمِرَة

Zamir describes something with scant hair, wool, or covering.

شَاةٌ زَمِرَةٌ

shātun zamiratun

Meaning: “A sheep with scant wool.”

Applied to a person, زَمِرُ الْمُرُوءَةِ denotes little honorable character.

عَطِيَّةٌ زَمِرَةٌ denotes a small gift.

These meanings concern scantness, but no derivative from this branch occurs in the Quran.

اِسْتَزْمَرَ

A lexical use denotes shrinking, drawing back, or making oneself small under humiliation.

It can describe a person contracting from an expansive or firm posture.

This does not make zumrah necessarily a small or subordinate group.

زُمْرَة

It denotes a group or company of people.

Lexical explanations emphasize both a recognized company and separate groups positioned or moving successively.

Zumrah itself does not establish a shared ethnicity, family, party, or social class.

The defining feature of the group must come from subject and context.

زُمَر

The plural of zumrah denotes multiple groups or companies.

The expression جَاءُوا زُمَرًا means:

“They came in groups.”

It can indicate separate or successive companies rather than everyone arriving as one undivided crowd.

This is the only form of the family occurring in the Quran.

زَمَرَ بِالْحَدِيثِ

Another use means to spread an account or circulate it among people.

It concerns making speech known to others.

This does not make Quranic zumar mean propaganda, speaking, or spreading news.

زَمَرَ الْقِرْبَةَ

It is recorded for filling a waterskin or container.

This differs from sound and human groups; its historical relationship with the other senses is unclear.

The recorded form does not justify reducing the entire family to filling or gathering for the sake of a uniform definition.

3. Human groups: number and separation

Zumrah names a group, but does not specify its membership count.

It can include a few people or a larger company.

It does not say whether the people assembled voluntarily or were grouped by someone else.

Travelers may move together because of a common destination; another group may assemble for a task; others may be classified by a shared feature or condition.

All are groups, but the reason for grouping is not supplied by zumrah alone.

Must a zumrah be noisy?

Some lexical explanations connect zumrah with zamr as sound.

They suggest that gathering commonly produces a hubbub and that the group’s name may reflect this.

Even if the etymology is plausible, it does not require every group to make noise while moving or assembling.

People can move in silence and remain a zumrah.

Noise is therefore not a necessary component of Quranic zumar.

Must a zumrah be small?

Another branch, zamir, denotes scantness.

Some early definitions explicitly describe zumrah as a small group and connect it with scantness; others simply define a group without that qualification.

Neither explanation determines a precise membership count.

Arabic records zumrah for a human group, with plural zumar, without fixing a number.

Quranic zumaran should therefore not be restricted to a few people or groups of a fixed size, though the small-group definition should be acknowledged as a recorded interpretation.

4. Quranic usage

Z M R occurs twice, both as زُمَرًا in Al-Zumar.

Both verses describe people conveyed toward their destinations in the hereafter.

Their destinations and conditions differ, rather than the word zumar itself.

Al-Zumar 39:71: groups taken toward Hell

وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا إِلَىٰ جَهَنَّمَ زُمَرًا حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا فُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا وَقَالَ لَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَا أَلَمْ يَأْتِكُمْ رُسُلٌ مِنْكُمْ يَتْلُونَ عَلَيْكُمْ آيَاتِ رَبِّكُمْ وَيُنْذِرُونَكُمْ لِقَاءَ يَوْمِكُمْ هَٰذَا قَالُوا بَلَىٰ وَلَٰكِنْ حَقَّتْ كَلِمَةُ الْعَذَابِ عَلَى الْكَافِرِينَ

wa-sīqa alladhīna kafarū ilā jahannama zumaran ḥattā idhā jāʾūhā futiḥat abwābuhā wa-qāla lahum khazanatuhā a-lam yaʾtikum rusulun minkum yatlūna ʿalaykum āyāti rabbikum wa-yundhirūnakum liqāʾa yawmikum hādhā qālū balā wa-lākin ḥaqqat kalimatu al-ʿadhābi ʿalā al-kāfirīn

Al-Zumar 39:71

Translation:

“Those who disbelieved will be driven toward Hell in groups. When they reach it, its gates will open and its keepers will ask: Did messengers from among you not come to recite your Lord’s signs to you and warn you of meeting this day? They will say: Yes, but the word of punishment has come true against the disbelievers.”

Zumaran is the accusative plural of zumrah.

It functions as a circumstantial expression describing the people being conveyed toward Hell.

وَسِيقَ belongs to S W Q and expresses driving or conveying.

الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا identifies the people concerned.

إِلَىٰ جَهَنَّمَ identifies the destination.

زُمَرًا specifies their being conveyed in multiple groups.

Z M R thus expresses grouping, not driving, punishing, or admitting into Hell.

Those notions come from other words and the passage.

Are the groups formed by type of sin?

Interpretations sometimes distinguish them by sin, degree of disbelief, or shared characteristics.

Such proposals can be examined as possible accounts of the grouping.

Zumar itself does not specify the criterion.

The verse does not say that each group consists only of people committing one kind of sin or contains a fixed number.

Their being conveyed in multiple groups is explicit.

Al-Zumar 39:69 describes judgment in truth without injustice.

Al-Zumar 39:70 describes each soul being fully recompensed for what it has done.

Being conveyed in groups in verse 71 therefore does not remove individual responsibility.

People can be gathered collectively while each person is judged by their own deeds.

The keepers and the question about messengers

The keepers ask whether messengers from among them came to recite their Lord’s signs and warn them of this day.

They answer affirmatively.

The passage concerns more than gathering a population: divine invitation, warning, and human response are at issue.

The question and answer should not be added to zumar’s lexical definition.

Zumar still names the groups; responsibility emerges from the complete verse.

Al-Zumar 39:72 commands entry through Hell’s gates and condemns the abode of the arrogant.

This sequence relates the groups’ fate to their conduct and stance toward truth.

5. Al-Zumar 39:73: groups taken toward the Garden

وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا رَبَّهُمْ إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ زُمَرًا حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا وَفُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا وَقَالَ لَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَا سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ طِبْتُمْ فَادْخُلُوهَا خَالِدِينَ

wa-sīqa alladhīna ittaqaw rabbahum ilā al-jannati zumaran ḥattā idhā jāʾūhā wa-futiḥat abwābuhā wa-qāla lahum khazanatuhā salāmun ʿalaykum ṭibtum fa-udkhulūhā khālidīn

Al-Zumar 39:73

Translation:

“Those who were mindful of their Lord will be conveyed toward the Garden in groups, until they reach it and its gates are opened, and its keepers say: Peace be upon you; you have become good and pure, so enter it to remain.”

Zumaran again functions circumstantially.

The core pattern parallels verse 71; the following is an abbreviated grammatical outline, not a continuous Quranic quotation:

وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ ... إِلَى ... زُمَرًا

wa-sīqa alladhīna … ilā … zumarā

And those who … were conveyed toward … in groups; an abbreviated grammatical outline.

There are two principal differences.

First, those conveyed are الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا رَبَّهُمْ, people mindful of their Lord.

Second, their destination is الْجَنَّةِ, the Garden.

The same zumar used for groups taken to Hell now describes groups taken to the Garden.

This shows that a zumrah is not inherently sinful or mindful.

Moral character and destiny follow from the people’s descriptions and destination.

Conveyance toward the Garden and Hell

Both verses use وَسِيقَ.

The verb cannot therefore be defined as driving in one verse but lexically as welcoming or reward in the other.

Its central sense in both is conveying or driving toward a destination.

The condition of those conveyed differs.

In verse 71, Hell’s keepers question them about messengers and warnings.

In verse 73, the Garden’s keepers welcome them with peace.

The destination and keepers’ words clarify the different reception.

It should not be attributed to zumar, or to sīqa alone.

How the opening of the gates is expressed

Al-Zumar 39:71 reads:

فُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا

futiḥat abwābuhā

Al-Zumar 39:71

Its gates were opened.

Al-Zumar 39:73 reads:

وَفُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا

wa-futiḥat abwābuhā

Al-Zumar 39:73

And its gates were opened.

The second expression’s wāw invites grammatical examination.

Some analyses take it circumstantially; others relate it to coordination.

Without further evidence, the difference does not establish a particular contrast in gate numbers or one necessary sequence of opening.

Nor does it alter zumar’s meaning.

Both describe groups arriving at their destinations.

Do all groups in the Garden have the same rank?

Zumaran describes the mindful being conveyed in multiple groups.

It does not say that everyone has one rank or that reward rank precisely determines each group.

Other verses discuss differing degrees, including Al-Anam 6:132, which relates degrees to deeds.

Such verses belong in a study of differing positions in the hereafter.

In Al-Zumar 39:73, zumar itself expresses grouping.

Group criteria and membership count cannot be extracted from the word alone.

6. Related words

Z M R (زُمْرَة) and F W J (فَوْج)

Fawj also denotes a group of people.

Al-Nasr 110:2 describes people entering the religion of The God in afwāj.

Al-Naba 78:18 uses فَتَأْتُونَ أَفْوَاجًا: “Then you come in groups.”

These collective images are close to zumaran in Al-Zumar.

Some lexical definitions directly explain zumrah through fawj.

An absolute boundary between them is therefore unwarranted.

Definitions of zumrah also emphasize distinct groups and possible succession.

That fits the passage’s multiple companies being conveyed.

Z M R (زُمْرَة) and Ḥ Z B (حِزْب)

Quranic ḥizb denotes people identified through affiliation, a shared aim, or a common position.

Al-Mujadilah 58:19 mentions the party of Satan, while Al-Mujadilah 58:22 mentions the party of The God.

Affiliation and orientation matter in these uses.

Zumrah itself does not establish a party, organization, or common program.

Members can share a feature without the word proving they belonged to one worldly organization or doctrinal movement.

Rendering zumaran as “party by party” is therefore unsuitable here.

Z M R (زُمْرَة) and F R Q (فِرْقَة)

Firqah belongs to F R Q, associated with separation and distinction.

Zumrah can likewise denote a company distinct from others.

It does not establish that an original unified body split through disagreement or conflict.

Firqah can describe the result of separation; zumrah need not explain a group’s origin.

The verses should therefore not be restricted to religious sects.

Z M R (زُمَر) and S W Q (سَوْق)

Both occur in Al-Zumar 39:71 and Al-Zumar 39:73 with different roles.

Sawq expresses conveying or driving toward a destination.

Zumar identifies the groups undergoing that movement.

“In groups” renders zumar; “being driven or conveyed” renders the verb of S W Q.

The distinction is necessary for accurate translation.

Z M R (زُمَر) and Z B R (زُبُر)

The forms look similar but belong to different roots.

Zumar, with mīm, is the plural of zumrah and means groups.

Zubur, with bāʾ, is the plural of zabūr and denotes writings or books in familiar Quranic uses.

Zubar, with fatḥah on bāʾ, refers to pieces of iron in Al-Kahf 18:96.

Visual similarity must not blur their meanings.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

The following equivalents suit the Arabic uses. These research proposals do not replace installed verse translations or existing authored word-box explanations.

زَمَرَ يَزْمِرُ: played a pipe or wind instrument; called, when referring to an ostrich.

زَمْر: pipe- or wind-instrument playing; voice or sound in the acoustic branch.

زِمار: a female ostrich’s call in the relevant use.

زَمِير: a verbal noun for pipe-playing; musical sound in that context.

زَامِر and زَمَّار: a pipe or wind-instrument player.

زَامِرَة: a female player or singer, depending on context.

مِزْمَار: a pipe or wind instrument.

مَزَامِير: the plural of mizmār.

زَمِرَ: became scant; became sparse when referring to hair or wool.

زَمِر: scant-haired, scant-woolled, or possessing little of something.

اِسْتَزْمَرَ: shrank or made oneself small under humiliation.

زُمْرَة: a group or company of people.

زُمَر: multiple groups or companies.

Proposed renderings of the Quranic passages

In Al-Zumar 39:71:

وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا إِلَىٰ جَهَنَّمَ زُمَرًا

wa-sīqa alladhīna kafarū ilā jahannama zumarā

Al-Zumar 39:71

“Those who disbelieved will be driven toward Hell in groups.”

In Al-Zumar 39:73:

وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا رَبَّهُمْ إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ زُمَرًا

wa-sīqa alladhīna ittaqaw rabbahum ilā al-jannati zumarā

Al-Zumar 39:73

“Those who were mindful of their Lord will be conveyed toward the Garden in groups.”

Zumaran is rendered “in groups” in both.

This conveys multiple distinct companies without fixing their size or how they formed.

A brief root explanation could read:

“Groups and companies; other branches concern sound and scantness.”

A contextual explanation for both verses:

“In groups, as multiple companies.”

Zumar should not be rendered as music, singing, sinful groups, or mindful groups: sound belongs to another branch, and moral descriptions come from other words.

8. Conclusion

Z M R has several Arabic fields: sound and playing, scantness, and human groups.

Early lexical records define zumrah as a company of people, with plural zumar.

Some connect the name with the noise of gathering people; others with a small number. Neither alone establishes the word’s historical origin.

The evidence examined here does not establish sufficient independently dated pre-Islamic evidence for the human-group sense. Later dictionary definitions should not be projected without qualification onto all pre-Quranic Arabic.

Only zumaran occurs in the Quran: once for those taken toward Hell, once for those taken toward the Garden.

The word carries no inherent positive or negative moral judgment. Groups can consist of disbelievers or people mindful of their Lord.

Both passages describe people in groups while moving. The grouping criteria, membership count, and individual judgment cannot be inferred from zumar alone.

Quranic zumar denotes distinct groups of people; in these verses, they are conveyed toward their destinations in companies.

This preserves the two verses’ meaning without replacing zumar’s direct sense with acoustic meanings, etymological hypotheses, or interpretations of the hereafter.
