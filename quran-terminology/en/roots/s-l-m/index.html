Introduction to the Root

The root S L M moves around soundness, safety, freedom from harm, wholeness, delivery without loss, peace, non-aggression, abandoning resistance, handing something over fully, and surrender.

The base image is not merely a spoken greeting or a later religious label. It begins from the idea that something remains intact, unbroken, unharmed, and free from defect. From there the root branches into greeting, peace, delivery, surrender, and the name Islam.

  • bodily or material soundness;
  • safety and release from harm;
  • being preserved from danger;
  • being whole and free from defect;
  • delivering something completely;
  • ending war and aggression;
  • establishing a relationship without harm;
  • abandoning resistance;
  • handing oneself or something over fully;
  • surrender.

Semantic Branches in Arabic

Soundness and Well-Being

One branch points to being safe, whole, and unharmed. A heart can be salim; a body, object, or situation can be free from damage or defect.

Peace and Non-Aggression

From safety comes a social meaning: a relation in which harm, attack, and aggression are withdrawn. In this field the root can speak of peace, truce, or ceasing hostility.

Salam and Greeting

Salam is not a bare formula. It carries a wish, announcement, or recognition of safety and non-harm between persons.

Delivery and Handing Over

To sallama can mean to deliver something fully, without withholding or loss. This branch keeps the idea of wholeness: the object reaches its recipient complete.

Abandoning Resistance

Aslama can indicate laying down resistance before a reference point. It may be voluntary and aware, outward and social, the result of defeat, or the unavoidable placement of a creature under a command and order.

Exclusive Belonging

Salaman li-rajul in the Qur'an shows a branch of being exclusively and undividedly for one owner, not pulled apart by competing claims.

Ladder

Sullam belongs to the idea of a means of ascent, a constructed path by which one tries to reach upward.

Outside Qur'anic Usage

Arabic has wider uses of the family, but the Qur'anic study should remain governed by the Qur'anic forms and contexts.

Introduction to Qur'anic Usage

The Qur'an uses this family in many fields: soundness of heart and body, an animal free from defect, complete delivery of a financial right, greeting, safety from danger, peace and cessation of hostility, submission before judgment, voluntary surrender to The God, outward surrender without faith entering the heart, forced surrender at the moment of incapacity, the divine name As-Salam, and a ladder or ascent route.

The root appears in several Qur'anic forms: the verb sallama, the verb aslama, salam, salam/silm/salm, sullam, salim, salimun, taslim, musallamah, Islam, Muslim and its plural forms, Muslimah and Muslimat, and mustaslimun.

The variety of forms is important. A single English or Persian equivalent cannot carry all of them. The context decides whether the sense is soundness, greeting, peace, delivery, surrender, or ascent.

Salim, Salimun, and Soundness

Qalbin Salim

إِلَّا مَنْ أَتَى اللَّهَ بِقَلْبٍ سَلِيمٍ

Ash-Shuara 26:89

إِذْ جَاءَ رَبَّهُ بِقَلْبٍ سَلِيمٍ

As-Saffat 37:84

A salim heart is not merely emotionally calm. It is a heart sound, whole, free from inner fracture, shirk, corruption, and false attachment. The root keeps the image of intactness.

Wa Hum Salimun

وَقَدْ كَانُوا يُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى السُّجُودِ وَهُمْ سَالِمُونَ

Al-Qalam 68:43

Here salimun points to a condition in which they had capacity and soundness. The later incapacity exposes the weight of the earlier refusal.

Musallamah: Sound or Delivered

Musallamatun La Shiyata Fiha

مُسَلَّمَةٌ لَا شِيَةَ فِيهَا

Al-Baqarah 2:71

In the cow passage, musallamah means sound and free from defect, with no mark or blemish in it.

Diyatun Musallamah

وَدِيَةٌ مُسَلَّمَةٌ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهِ

An-Nisa 4:92

Here musallamah means delivered fully to the family. In one place the body is sound; in the other, the right is delivered without withholding.

Salam: Safety, Greeting, and Non-Aggression

Greeting

قَالُوا سَلَامًا قَالَ سَلَامٌ

Hud 11:69

وَتُسَلِّمُوا عَلَىٰ أَهْلِهَا

An-Nur 24:27

فَسَلِّمُوا عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِكُمْ تَحِيَّةً مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ مُبَارَكَةً طَيِّبَةً

An-Nur 24:61

Salam as greeting is a speech act of safety, recognition, and non-harm.

Safety from Harm

قُلْنَا يَا نَارُ كُونِي بَرْدًا وَسَلَامًا عَلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ

Al-Anbiya 21:69

The fire is commanded to be cool and salam upon Ibrahim: not merely cool, but harmless.

Bi-Salamin Aminin

ادْخُلُوهَا بِسَلَامٍ آمِنِينَ

Al-Hijr 15:46

Salam stresses the state of non-harm; aminin stresses the absence of fear and security.

Paradisal Speech and Atmosphere

تَحِيَّتُهُمْ فِيهَا سَلَامٌ

Yunus 10:10

لَا يَسْمَعُونَ فِيهَا لَغْوًا إِلَّا سَلَامًا

Maryam 19:62

Withdrawal from Dispute

وَإِذَا خَاطَبَهُمُ الْجَاهِلُونَ قَالُوا سَلَامًا

Al-Furqan 25:63

سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ لَا نَبْتَغِي الْجَاهِلِينَ

Al-Qasas 28:55

Here salam is not only greeting; it can mark withdrawal from ignorant conflict.

Salam upon Messengers

  • سَلَامٌ عَلَىٰ نُوحٍ
  • سَلَامٌ عَلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ
  • سَلَامٌ عَلَىٰ مُوسَىٰ وَهَارُونَ
  • سَلَامٌ عَلَىٰ إِلْ يَاسِينَ
  • وَسَلَامٌ عَلَى الْمُرْسَلِينَ

As-Salam as a Divine Description

هُوَ اللَّهُ الَّذِي لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْمَلِكُ الْقُدُّوسُ السَّلَامُ الْمُؤْمِنُ الْمُهَيْمِنُ

Al-Hashr 59:23

As-Salam as a divine description is tied to perfect soundness, freedom from defect, and the source of safety. The God is beyond deficiency, injury, breakage, and need; safety and non-harm reach creation from Him.

Salam, Silm, and Salm

Alqaw Ilaykum As-Salam

فَإِنِ اعْتَزَلُوكُمْ فَلَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ وَأَلْقَوْا إِلَيْكُمُ السَّلَمَ فَمَا جَعَلَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ عَلَيْهِمْ سَبِيلًا

An-Nisa 4:90

The context combines withdrawal from fighting, non-combat, and casting peace toward the believers.

Forced Surrender

فَأَلْقَوُا السَّلَمَ مَا كُنَّا نَعْمَلُ مِنْ سُوءٍ

An-Nahl 16:28

وَأَلْقَوْا إِلَى اللَّهِ يَوْمَئِذٍ السَّلَمَ

An-Nahl 16:87

This is not the same as voluntary faith; it is surrender when resistance no longer has room.

Salaman Li-Rajul

ضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا رَجُلًا فِيهِ شُرَكَاءُ مُتَشَاكِسُونَ وَرَجُلًا سَلَمًا لِرَجُلٍ هَلْ يَسْتَوِيَانِ مَثَلًا

Az-Zumar 39:29

Salaman here means undivided and exclusively for one man, not split among quarrelling partners.

Enter into Silm Entirely

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا ادْخُلُوا فِي السِّلْمِ كَافَّةً

Al-Baqarah 2:208

The phrase calls for entering the field of peace/surrender wholly, not partially.

Salm in Relation to War

وَإِنْ جَنَحُوا لِلسَّلْمِ فَاجْنَحْ لَهَا

Al-Anfal 8:61

فَلَا تَهِنُوا وَتَدْعُوا إِلَى السَّلْمِ وَأَنْتُمُ الْأَعْلَوْنَ

Muhammad 47:35

Salm here is read in the field of peace and the cessation of fighting, but each passage has its own strategic and moral context.

Sallama and Taslim

Delivery

فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا سَلَّمْتُمْ مَا آتَيْتُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

Al-Baqarah 2:233

Preserving from Harm

وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ سَلَّمَ

Al-Anfal 8:43

Complete Submission

ثُمَّ لَا يَجِدُوا فِي أَنْفُسِهِمْ حَرَجًا مِمَّا قَضَيْتَ وَيُسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا

An-Nisa 4:65

Faith and Taslim

وَمَا زَادَهُمْ إِلَّا إِيمَانًا وَتَسْلِيمًا

Al-Ahzab 33:22

Greeting the Messenger

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا صَلُّوا عَلَيْهِ وَسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا

Al-Ahzab 33:56

Taslim can therefore be delivery, greeting, or acceptance without resistance. The context must decide.

Sullam: Ladder and Ascent Route

أَوْ سُلَّمًا فِي السَّمَاءِ

Al-Anam 6:35

أَمْ لَهُمْ سُلَّمٌ يَسْتَمِعُونَ فِيهِ

At-Tur 52:38

Sullam is a ladder or means of ascent. In these contexts it exposes the claim that someone can reach a higher realm or access hidden knowledge by a path of their own.

Aslama: Handing Over and Abandoning Resistance

Aslama Wajhahu Lillah

بَلَىٰ مَنْ أَسْلَمَ وَجْهَهُ لِلَّهِ وَهُوَ مُحْسِنٌ فَلَهُ أَجْرُهُ عِنْدَ رَبِّهِ

Al-Baqarah 2:112

وَمَنْ أَحْسَنُ دِينًا مِمَّنْ أَسْلَمَ وَجْهَهُ لِلَّهِ وَهُوَ مُحْسِنٌ

An-Nisa 4:125

وَمَنْ يُسْلِمْ وَجْهَهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَهُوَ مُحْسِنٌ

Luqman 31:22

Wajhahu is the object, and lillah or ila The God marks the destination. Ihsan describes the quality that accompanies this surrender.

Aslamtu Li-Rabb Al-Alamin

إِذْ قَالَ لَهُ رَبُّهُ أَسْلِمْ قَالَ أَسْلَمْتُ لِرَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

Al-Baqarah 2:131

Abraham’s answer identifies the destination of surrender: the Lord of the worlds.

Submission of Creatures Willingly or Unwillingly

وَلَهُ أَسْلَمَ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ طَوْعًا وَكَرْهًا

Ali Imran 3:83

Earlier Prophets and Followers

وَأَسْلَمْتُ مَعَ سُلَيْمَانَ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

An-Naml 27:44

Social or Military Context

تُقَاتِلُونَهُمْ أَوْ يُسْلِمُونَ

Al-Fath 48:16

قَالَتِ الْأَعْرَابُ آمَنَّا قُلْ لَمْ تُؤْمِنُوا وَلَٰكِنْ قُولُوا أَسْلَمْنَا وَلَمَّا يَدْخُلِ الْإِيمَانُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ

Al-Hujurat 49:14

Al-Hujurat 49:14 proves that aslamna and amanna are not complete synonyms. It does not define every use of aslama.

Islam: The Masdar of Aslama

Islam appears as the masdar of aslama. Aslama is the verb; Islam is its verbal noun; Muslim is its active participle. Islam is therefore the name of an act or state of surrender.

Islam and Din

إِنَّ الدِّينَ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ الْإِسْلَامُ

Ali Imran 3:19

وَمَنْ يَبْتَغِ غَيْرَ الْإِسْلَامِ دِينًا فَلَنْ يُقْبَلَ مِنْهُ

Ali Imran 3:85

وَرَضِيتُ لَكُمُ الْإِسْلَامَ دِينًا

Al-Maidah 5:3

These verses directly connect al-Islam with din. Yet the word remains, morphologically, a masdar. Later institutional, legal, and sectarian loads should not be forced back into every Qur'anic use without evidence.

Opening the Chest for Islam

فَمَنْ يُرِدِ اللَّهُ أَنْ يَهْدِيَهُ يَشْرَحْ صَدْرَهُ لِلْإِسْلَامِ

Al-Anam 6:125

أَفَمَنْ شَرَحَ اللَّهُ صَدْرَهُ لِلْإِسْلَامِ فَهُوَ عَلَىٰ نُورٍ مِنْ رَبِّهِ

Az-Zumar 39:22

Expansion of the chest for Islam links it to inner openness and readiness, not merely legal membership in a society.

Islamihim and Islamakum

وَكَفَرُوا بَعْدَ إِسْلَامِهِمْ

At-Tawbah 9:74

قُلْ لَا تَمُنُّوا عَلَيَّ إِسْلَامَكُمْ

Al-Hujurat 49:17

Adding Islam to pronouns shows its verbal and state-like function: their Islam, your Islam, something attributable to persons and open to testing for quality and truth.

Muslim: The Active Participle

Muslim is the active participle of aslama: one who submits himself or hands himself over. The destination of submission matters.

  • مُسْلِمَيْنِ لَكَ
  • أُمَّةً مُسْلِمَةً لَكَ
  • وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ
  • فَلَهُ أَسْلِمُوا
  • أَسْلَمْتُ لِرَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

Abraham and Ishmael

رَبَّنَا وَاجْعَلْنَا مُسْلِمَيْنِ لَكَ وَمِنْ ذُرِّيَّتِنَا أُمَّةً مُسْلِمَةً لَكَ

Al-Baqarah 2:128

The repeated laka identifies the destination of surrender.

وَلَٰكِنْ كَانَ حَنِيفًا مُسْلِمًا

Ali Imran 3:67

Because Abraham lived before the historical community of Muhammad, Muslim here describes his orientation, not membership in a later institution.

Children of Jacob and the Disciples

إِلَٰهًا وَاحِدًا وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ

Al-Baqarah 2:133

آمَنَّا بِاللَّهِ وَاشْهَدْ بِأَنَّا مُسْلِمُونَ

Ali Imran 3:52

These uses show that Muslim in the Qur'an is not limited to the later historical followers of Muhammad.

The Collective Title Al-Muslimin

هُوَ سَمَّاكُمُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ مِنْ قَبْلُ وَفِي هَٰذَا

Al-Hajj 22:78

The Qur'an can use al-muslimin as a collective title, while its participial connection with aslama remains visible.

The Ethical Weight of Al-Muslimin

أَفَنَجْعَلُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ كَالْمُجْرِمِينَ

Al-Qalam 68:35

وَأَنَّا مِنَّا الْمُسْلِمُونَ وَمِنَّا الْقَاسِطُونَ

Al-Jinn 72:14

In these contrasts, Muslim carries ethical meaning and is not merely an administrative class.

Muslim and Mu'min

Muslim comes from S L M and stresses submission and handing oneself over. Mu'min comes from A M N and relates to faith, trust, confirmation, and inner security.

Islam Without Faith Entering the Heart

قُلْ لَمْ تُؤْمِنُوا وَلَٰكِنْ قُولُوا أَسْلَمْنَا وَلَمَّا يَدْخُلِ الْإِيمَانُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ

Al-Hujurat 49:14

Faith and Islam Together

آمَنَّا بِاللَّهِ وَاشْهَدْ بِأَنَّا مُسْلِمُونَ

Ali Imran 3:52

إِنْ كُنْتُمْ آمَنْتُمْ بِاللَّهِ فَعَلَيْهِ تَوَكَّلُوا إِنْ كُنْتُمْ مُسْلِمِينَ

Yunus 10:84

الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِآيَاتِنَا وَكَانُوا مُسْلِمِينَ

Az-Zukhruf 43:69

Believers and a House of Muslims

فَأَخْرَجْنَا مَنْ كَانَ فِيهَا مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ ۝ فَمَا وَجَدْنَا فِيهَا غَيْرَ بَيْتٍ مِنَ الْمُسْلِمِينَ

Adh-Dhariyat 51:35-36

Two Descriptions Side by Side

إِنَّ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَالْمُسْلِمَاتِ وَالْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ...

Al-Ahzab 33:35

The Qur'an preserves the lexical distinction while allowing both descriptions to gather in one person. Every claim of Islam does not prove faith in the heart, but Islam and faith can be harmoniously joined.

Mustaslimun: Surrender After Resistance Ends

بَلْ هُمُ الْيَوْمَ مُسْتَسْلِمُونَ

As-Saffat 37:26

The context is incapacity before judgment. This surrender is not the same as Abraham’s voluntary response, “I have submitted to the Lord of the worlds.” Surrender by itself is not always faith or virtue; its meaning is known from destination, choice, and context.

Historical Development of Islam and Muslim

The Qur'an itself uses Islam and Muslim; they are not inventions of later governments. In the Qur'an, Islam is the masdar of aslama, Muslim is the active participle and description of one who submits, al-Islam is linked with din, al-muslimin can be a collective title, and the destination of surrender is repeatedly marked by laka, lahu, lillah, and li-rabb al-alamin.

At the same time, the Qur'an often addresses the community of the Prophet with:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا

A recurring Quranic address

This does not erase al-muslimin as a title; it shows the prominence of faith in Qur'anic address.

With the formation of rule, legal boundaries, and social institutions, Muslim gradually gained official, legal, and identity functions in addition to the Qur'anic descriptive sense. The shift did not happen in one moment and does not mean the word did not exist before. The main development was the dominance of identity and legal function over the verbal and descriptive sense.

  • Islam as the act and state of surrender;
  • al-muslimin as a collective Qur'anic title;
  • Islam and Muslim as later official, legal, and sectarian identities.

Difference from Nearby Roots

S L M and A M N

Salamah stresses absence of harm, defect, and breakage. Amn stresses absence of fear, trust, and felt security.

S L M and S L H

Salah stresses correctness, order, and freedom from corruption. Salamah stresses being intact and unharmed.

S L M and N J W

Najat stresses being brought out of danger. Salamah describes the state of remaining unharmed.

Aslama and Ata'a

Ata'a emphasizes obeying a command. Aslama can express handing over one’s direction, will, or self.

Aslama and Khada'a

Khudu' focuses on humility or subjection. Islam stresses abandoning resistance and handing oneself over.

Islam and Iman

Islam comes from surrender and soundness; iman from security, trust, and confirmation. The Qur'an preserves their distinction while allowing them to gather together.

Conclusion

The root S L M begins from soundness and wholeness and expands into connected branches: soundness, freedom from harm, relation without aggression, peace, complete delivery, abandoning resistance, and surrender.

  • Salam can be greeting, safety, harmlessness, or non-aggression;
  • salam, silm, and salm can express peace, cessation of hostility, exclusive belonging, or surrender;
  • salim indicates soundness and integration;
  • musallam can mean sound or fully delivered;
  • taslim can be delivery, greeting, or acceptance without resistance;
  • aslama expresses handing over and abandoning resistance;
  • Islam is the masdar and name of this act or state;
  • Muslim is one who performs this surrender;
  • mustaslim is one who no longer resists;
  • sullam is a ladder or ascent route.

Islam in the Qur'an has an explicit relation to din, and al-muslimin can function as a collective title, but the verbal and descriptive structure remains active. Muslim is first a description of one who has handed himself over, and the Qur'an repeatedly identifies the destination of that surrender.

Islam and iman are not synonyms. Surrender may occur without faith having entered the heart, and Islam and faith may also be joined harmoniously in one person.

The fixed elements of this root are soundness, wholeness, freedom from harm, absence of aggression, complete delivery, and abandoning resistance.
