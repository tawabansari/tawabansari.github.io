Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root Ẓ L M

Darkness, concealment, lack of light, covering right, diminishing right, and displacing things from their proper place

Important Forms from This Root

ظَلَمَ / يَظْلِمُ

ẓalama / yaẓlimu; wronged, covered a right, or displaced something from its proper place.

ظُلْم / ظُلْمَة

ẓulm / ẓulmah; injustice, wrong, covering right, darkening the place of right; darkness.

ظُلُمَات

ẓulumāt; darknesses, layers of darkness, states of concealment and misguidance.

ظَالِم / ظَالِمُون / ظَالِمِينَ

ẓālim / ẓālimūn / ẓālimīn; wrongdoer, those who cover right or remove things from their proper place.

مَظْلُوم / مَظْلَمَة

maẓlūm / maẓlamah; one wronged; a violated right or case of injustice.

ظَلَّام / ظَلِيم

ẓallām / ẓalīm; one strongly associated with wrong; in some lexical uses, a trace of improper digging or displacement.

Central Meaning

The root Ẓ L M originally points to darkness, concealment, absence of light, and the loss of the ability to see and discern. In the ethical and legal field, it means covering right, diminishing a right, darkening the place of truth, and placing something where it does not belong.

So ẓulm is not only “oppression” in the common sense. Oppression is one result of ẓulm. The deeper center of the root is right becoming dark and the proper place of things being covered. Wherever right is no longer seen, proper placement is covered, natural yield is diminished, or the correct relation between The God, the human being, society, wealth, judgment, and the self is broken, the field of Ẓ L M has appeared.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of Ẓ L M is the absence of light and the covering of right. At the material level, ظُلْمَة means darkness: a place where light is absent and things are not seen properly. In darkness, the path, boundary, danger, face, and place of things become unclear.

From this material image, the ethical and legal meaning of ẓulm emerges. When a right is covered, when something is not in its proper place, when the correct measure is not observed, when the natural timing of a thing is ignored, or when someone is deprived of a right, an ethical darkness has appeared. In this sense, ẓulm means the darkening of the order of right.

Therefore, ẓulm is not only a violent act. It may occur in judgment, measure, language, economy, family, government, religion, or even in a human being’s relation to himself. Wherever the proper place of things is covered or displaced, ẓulm begins.

Material Image

The first material image of this root is a dark space. In darkness, a person does not see things correctly. He may mistake a path for a pit, fail to recognize a face, or lose the boundary between things. Darkness is not only the absence of light; it is the loss of discernment.

Another material image is improper interference with something, such as using something before its proper time or digging into land where one has no place to dig. In some Arabic lexical uses, ẓulm is used for a waterskin or land when something is handled before its proper time or in an improper place. These images show that ẓulm is not only darkness; it also contains displacement, wrong timing, and improper possession.

A third image is diminishing the natural yield or right of something. If something is supposed to give its fruit, share, or output fully, but that output is diminished, this too enters the field of ẓulm. This image matters in the Qur’an because ẓulm may be used for a garden and its produce, not only for a human moral act.

These images are important for understanding the ethical meaning of ẓulm. When a society commits ẓulm, right becomes dark. When someone wrongs another, he removes that person’s place from the light of right. When a human being wrongs himself, he deprives his inner self of sound discernment. Thus ẓulm is ethical darkness: a darkness that covers truth, right, measure, time, yield, and proper place.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, ظُلْمَة refers to darkness: the darkness of night, the darkness of a place, or a darkness that prevents seeing. But the root Ẓ L M did not remain only in the sensory meaning. Classical Arabic also uses ẓulm for wrongdoing, diminishing a right, transgression in judgment, and placing something outside its proper place.

The famous lexical definition of ẓulm is: placing something where it does not belong. This definition shows that ẓulm is not only causing harm; it is a disturbance of the proper place of things. If someone diminishes measure, weight, judgment, share, or human relation, ẓulm has occurred. This use is tied to darkness because right is no longer seen fully and clearly in its place.

Some lexical traces also show ẓulm being used for improper interference with things, such as handling something before its natural time or digging land in a place that is not proper. This layer turns “placing something outside its proper place” from an abstract definition into a material image.

Qur’anic Usage

Darknesses Opposite Light

One important Qur’anic contrast is darknesses and light: يُخْرِجُهُم مِّنَ الظُّلُمَاتِ إِلَى النُّورِ; He brings them out of the darknesses into the light 2:257.

In the Qur’an, darknesses often appear in the plural, while light often appears in the singular. This is meaningful. The darknesses may be many: ignorance, shirk, fear, injustice, misguidance, distortion, desire, tyranny, and self-deception. But the light of right is one: the clear path of discernment and guidance. Here, darknesses are not merely the absence of physical light; they are conditions in which the human being does not see right or recognize the proper path.

Ẓulm and Shirk

The Qur’an calls shirk a great ẓulm: إِنَّ الشِّرْكَ لَظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌ; indeed, shirk is a great wrong 31:13.

Here, shirk is ẓulm because it displaces the place of The God and the created thing. Something that has no right of Lordship is placed in the position of lord, protector, independent intercessor, or final authority. Shirk, then, is not only a doctrinal mistake; it is the darkening of the greatest relation of right. The place of The God is covered, and a created thing is seated where it has no right to sit.

Wronging the Self

The Qur’an often speaks of wronging the self: وَمَا ظَلَمُونَا وَلَٰكِن كَانُوا أَنفُسَهُمْ يَظْلِمُونَ; they did not wrong Us, but they were wronging themselves 2:57.

Wronging the self means removing oneself from the proper place of servanthood, growth, purity, and guidance. One who sins does not merely break an external rule; he also introduces darkness into his own inner structure. In this sense, the self has a right. A human being cannot treat himself in any way and assume that nothing has been affected.

Wronging People and Judgment

Ẓulm is also used in the Qur’an for wronging others. This may occur in wealth, life, honor, right, security, judgment, and dignity. When someone’s right is covered, his share is diminished, his voice is silenced, or his human place is ignored, ẓulm has occurred.

One major field of ẓulm is judgment. If a judge, ruler, family, society, or political order does not place a matter where it belongs, ẓulm occurs. Right should be made clear, but unjust judgment covers it. Here, ẓulm is not a simple mistake. It means that a judgment or action darkens and distorts truth, right, or the human place.

Ẓulm in Economy and Measure

Ẓulm can occur in measure, weight, debt, wage, usury, transaction, and valuation. When someone diminishes another person’s share, manipulates weight, turns debt into an instrument of domination, or covers the real value of labor and property, economic ẓulm has occurred.

This use is connected with roots such as W Z N, K Y L, Q S Ṭ, R B W, KH S R, and B GH Y. But ẓulm is more foundational because it emphasizes the darkening of right and the removal of things from their proper place.

Wronging the Signs

The Qur’an speaks of people who deny or distort The God’s signs. This too belongs to the field of ẓulm because a clear sign is being covered. A sign comes to reveal right. When a person covers it, diminishes it, distorts it, or makes another speech the authority over it, he darkens the light of the sign.

Ẓulm as a Social Structure

The Qur’an does not speak only of individual ẓulm. Sometimes towns, peoples, or settlements are described as being in a state of ẓulm: وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَخْذُ رَبِّكَ إِذَا أَخَذَ الْقُرَىٰ وَهِيَ ظَالِمَةٌ; and such is the taking of your Lord when He takes the settlements while they are wronging 11:102.

Here, ẓulm is not only an individual sin. It is a social and structural condition. A settlement or society may be ẓālimah: its order, judgment, power, economy, and human relations may darken and displace right. Thus, in the Qur’an, ẓulm can reach the level of civilization.

Ẓulm as Diminishing Natural Yield

One important verse for understanding this root appears in the story of the two gardens in Surat al-Kahf: كِلْتَا الْجَنَّتَيْنِ آتَتْ أُكُلَهَا وَلَمْ تَظْلِم مِّنْهُ شَيْئًا; both gardens gave their fruit and did not diminish anything from it 18:33.

This verse is important because translating ẓulm only as moral oppression would make the meaning inadequate. A garden does not “oppress” someone. The more precise meaning is that the garden did not diminish its produce, yield, or natural right. Here, ẓulm means reducing the proper output, withholding the true share, or failing to give the natural right of a thing.

Denying Ẓulm from The God

The Qur’an says: وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِظَلَّامٍ لِّلْعَبِيدِ; and your Lord is not a wrongdoer toward the servants 41:46.

The word ظَلَّام is an intensive form. It should not be understood as meaning that The God is only not “very unjust,” while a small amount of injustice remains possible. In Arabic, such a form can strongly deny the attribution itself: The God is not one associated with ẓulm, one who permits ẓulm, or one who has any share of ẓulm toward the servants. Also, because the servants are many, even the smallest displacement of right toward each of them, if it belonged to the divine order, would become immense in total.

Ẓulm and Ẓulumāt

Ẓulm and ẓulumāt are related, but they are not the same. Ẓulumāt are the state or environment of darkness. Ẓulm is the act or structure that produces darkness or removes right from light.

Simply put: ẓulumāt are the dark condition; ẓulm is the darkening of right or the placement of something where it does not belong. Ẓulm may produce ẓulumāt, and a human being may be in ẓulumāt and commit ẓulm because of it.

Difference from Nearby Roots

N W R is light, making clear, guidance, and the possibility of discernment. Ẓulm, opposite light, is the darkening and covering of right. K F R focuses more on covering truth; ẓulm focuses on disturbing the place of right and darkening the proper relation.

F S D is the corruption of the order and soundness of a thing. Corruption may be the result or structure of ruin; ẓulm can be one root of corruption. AYN D W (ʿ D W) emphasizes crossing a boundary and attacking another’s right; ẓulm emphasizes darkening right and placing things wrongly.

B GH Y carries a movement of excessive seeking and transgression; ẓulm carries the darkening of right and displacement of proper place. J W R is close to deviation from justice; ẓulm is broader and also includes darkness and concealment of right. H Ḍ M is more specific, meaning diminishing a right; ẓulm may include this, but is not limited to it. B KH S means diminishing the value or right of something; ẓulm is broader and includes covering right, displacing proper place, and darkening the order of right. GH Ṣ B is taking by force or coercive possession; ẓulm is broader and includes every displacement of right.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, ظُلْم should not always be translated only as “oppression.” In social and legal contexts, “oppression,” “wrong,” or “violating a right” may fit. In a monotheistic context, such as shirk, ẓulm should be understood as the displacement of the place of right. In the context of the self, “wronging oneself” means darkening and harming one’s own inner structure.

In the context of ẓulumāt, the meanings of darkness, concealment, and lack of light should be preserved. In judgment and economy, “removing something from its proper place,” “diminishing a right,” or “covering a right” may be more precise than “oppression” alone. In social contexts, such as a ẓālimah settlement, ẓulm should be seen as a structural condition, not only an individual fault. In al-Kahf 18:33, ẓulm should be understood as diminishing yield, withholding natural right, or reducing proper output, not moral oppression.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

ظُلْم (ẓulm): injustice, wrong, oppression, darkening of right, placing something where it does not belong, covering a right, diminishing a right, improper possession.

ظَلَمَ (ẓalama): wronged, covered a right, removed from the proper place, diminished a right, oppressed, interfered improperly.

ظَالِم (ẓālim): wrongdoer, oppressor, one who covers right or removes things from their proper place.

مَظْلُوم (maẓlūm): wronged one, one whose right has been covered or violated.

ظُلُمَات (ẓulumāt): darknesses, layers of darkness, states of concealment and misguidance.

مَظْلَمَة (maẓlamah): violated right, case of injustice, darkness caused by wrong.

ظَلَّام (ẓallām): one strongly associated with wrong; in divine negation, having no share at all in ẓulm.

إِنَّ الشِّرْكَ لَظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌ: indeed, shirk is a great wrong; or shirk is a foundational displacement of the place of right.

وَهِيَ ظَالِمَةٌ: while it was wronging; or while its structure had darkened and displaced right.

وَلَمْ تَظْلِم مِّنْهُ شَيْئًا: and it did not diminish anything from it; or it did not withhold anything from its fruit and yield.

وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِظَلَّامٍ لِّلْعَبِيدِ: your Lord is not one associated with wrong toward the servants; or your Lord does not darken or violate any right of the servants.

Linguistic Summary

The root Ẓ L M denotes darkness, concealment, absence of light, and, in the ethical and legal field, covering right, diminishing right, and removing something from its proper place. At the material level, ẓulmah is a place where light is absent and discernment becomes difficult or impossible. Structurally, ẓulm is where right is not seen, the proper place of things is covered, natural yield is diminished, or a human being, judgment, wealth, self, society, or understanding of The God is removed from its proper relation.

Thus ẓulm is not only external oppression. Ẓulm is darkness in the order of right. Wherever right is covered, wherever the correct measure is broken, wherever something withholds its due fruit or share, wherever the created thing is seated in the place of the Creator, wherever a human being denies right to himself or another, wherever society builds its structure on the displacement of right, and wherever the light of discernment is extinguished, the field of Ẓ L M has appeared.
