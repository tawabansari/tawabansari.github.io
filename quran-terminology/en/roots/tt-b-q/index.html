Short Meaning of the Root

The root ط ب ق (ṭ b q) indicates something being placed upon another thing in a covering, equal-sized, or proportionate way. From this core, meanings such as covering, layer, correspondence, harmony, rank, and successive state are formed.

In the Qur'an, this root appears in two main fields: the layered structure of the heavens and the human transition from one state to another.

Important Forms of the Root

طَبَق (ṭabaq) is said of a covering or something that is placed upon another thing and is equal or proportionate to it. The word can indicate a lid, surface, layer, rank, state, or a group of the same standing.

أَطْبَاق (aṭbāq) is the plural of طَبَق (ṭabaq) and is used for multiple coverings, layers, or surfaces.

طِبَاق (ṭibāq) can indicate things placed one upon another or things proportionate and harmonious with one another. In the Qur'an, the form طِبَاقًا (ṭibāqan) occurs twice in the description of the seven heavens.

طَبَقَة (ṭabaqah) means a layer, rank, level, or group of the same standing, and its plural is طَبَقَات (ṭabaqāt). This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

طَبَّقَ (ṭabbaqa) in form II means to cover, place something upon something else, encompass all of something, or layer it.

أَطْبَقَ (aṭbaqa) in form IV can mean to close, cover, place over something, or encompass it completely.

طَابَقَ (ṭābaqa) in form III means to be equal, compatible, or corresponding with something.

تَطَابَقَ (taṭābaqa) means to become mutually harmonious or corresponding.

اِنْطَبَقَ (inṭabaqa) means to close, to be placed upon something, or to fit it completely.

مُطَابَقَة (muṭābaqah) indicates correspondence, harmony, and equality between two things in form, limit, state, or meaning.

Among these forms, the Qur'an only uses the nominal forms طِبَاقًا (ṭibāqan) and طَبَقًا / طَبَقٍ (ṭabaqan / ṭabaq).

Linguistic Note

Several elements are joined together in the semantic core of ط ب ق (ṭ b q):

First, something is placed upon another thing.

Second, what is placed covers the surface or boundary of what lies beneath it.

Third, there is a kind of proportion, equality, or alignment between the two.

Fourth, several أطباق (aṭbāq) can appear as layers, ranks, or successive states.

Therefore, “covering,” “layer,” “correspondence,” and “rank” are not unrelated branches of one word. All of them return to the relation between two surfaces, two limits, two states, or several connected parts.

Nevertheless, the material meaning of the root should not be imposed mechanically on every usage. In one context, covering is prominent; in another, equality and harmony; and in a context such as طَبَقًا عَنْ طَبَقٍ (ṭabaqan ʿan ṭabaq), succession and replacement of states.

Material Image of the Root

The clearest material image of this root is a covering placed upon something and proportionate to its surface. A طَبَق (ṭabaq) is not merely something that accidentally falls above another object; its boundary or surface has a kind of fit with what it covers.

Another image is two surfaces or two parts settling upon one another so that their limits fit together. In lexical sources, طَبَق (ṭabaq) is used both for “the covering of every thing” and for something equal and corresponding to another thing.

The placement of bodily parts or natural components upon one another can also clarify this image, such as vertebrae each positioned in relation to another vertebra. Still, very specific examples, such as a shell or an animal’s hoof, should only be entered into the final version when there is an explicit and reliable lexical witness for that exact usage.

From this material image, the concept of “layer” and then “rank or successive state” emerges: each state takes another position in relation to the previous state and comes after it.

Historical Note and Ancient Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic, الطَّبَق (al-ṭabaq) is used for the covering of every thing, a covering that remains attached to something, and something equal or proportionate to another thing. Expressions such as أطبقه (aṭbaqahu), طبّقه (ṭabbaqahu), and انطبق (inṭabaqa) also indicate covering, closing, or something being placed upon another thing.

Also in Arabic usage, طبق (ṭabaq) can refer to a state, station, or rank. This semantic expansion shows that the move from “surface and covering” to “state and stage” was known in the Arabic lexical field before the interpretive use of the verse of Al-Inshiqaq.

In some lexical sources, the expression بَنَاتُ طَبَقٍ (banātu ṭabaq) is transmitted for hardships, transformations, or heavy events of time. This expression may show that طبق (ṭabaq) in ancient language had the capacity to be used for successive states and changing pressures of time. However, the precise relation of this expression to the pre-Qur'anic period and its poetic witness should be separately examined before definite inclusion in the final version.

Therefore, one cannot claim without independent evidence that every usage recorded in later dictionaries was necessarily common in pre-Islamic poetry. In the final version, if a reliable and dateable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or pre-Qur'anic usage is found, it can be added.

Qur'anic Usage of the Root

Layered Heavens

الَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا

The One who created seven heavens layer-wise.

Al-Mulk 67:3

In this verse, طِبَاقًا (ṭibāqan) describes the structure and relation among the heavens. The basic meaning can be that the heavens are arranged as orderly, proportionate layers, one in relation to another.

طِبَاق (ṭibāq) in itself has the semantic capacity of equality, proportion, and things being placed upon one another. The continuation of the verse does not create this capacity; rather, it highlights and confirms it in this context:

مَا تَرَىٰ فِي خَلْقِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ مِنْ تَفَاوُتٍ

You do not see any disharmony in the creation of The All-Merciful.

Al-Mulk 67:3

Therefore, the translation “layer-wise” preserves the main image; but in linguistic explanation one may add that these layers are not described as disproportionate or disjointed.

One should not extract, from طِبَاقًا (ṭibāqan) alone, a specific scientific model of the physical structure of the cosmos. The word shows a layered and ordered relation, but it does not define the physical shape, distance, or nature of those layers.

Seven Heavens in a Layered Order

أَلَمْ تَرَوْا كَيْفَ خَلَقَ اللَّهُ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا

Have you not seen how The God created seven heavens layer-wise?

Nuh 71:15

In this verse too, طِبَاقًا (ṭibāqan) describes the seven heavens. This form can indicate layeredness, one being positioned in relation to another, and structural harmony.

The translation “one above another” may convey part of the image, but it may also insert a specific spatial direction more strongly than the word itself explicitly states. “Layer-wise” or “in proportionate layers” keeps the semantic range more open.

Passing from One State to Another

لَتَرْكَبُنَّ طَبَقًا عَنْ طَبَقٍ

Surely you will pass from one state to another.

Al-Inshiqaq 84:19

In this verse, طَبَق (ṭabaq) is not translated as a physical covering or vessel. The word indicates a state, stage, or rank that comes after another state.

The connection with the material meaning remains visible: states are like successive surfaces or layers, but the verse does not focus on their stillness; the axis of the expression is the addressee’s transition from one طبق (ṭabaq) to another.

The verb لَتَرْكَبُنَّ (latarkabunna) also shows movement and entry into a state. ركوب (rukūb) here is not merely mounting a riding animal; it conveys entering, traversing, or being enveloped in a condition.

The context of the surah speaks of the transformation of heaven and earth, human striving, meeting the Lord, and different final outcomes. Therefore, the expression can include successive stages of human experience. Nevertheless, the verse does not name these stages as a specific list; so the translation should preserve the open and direct meaning of “one state after another.”

Distinguishing Similar Uses

طِبَاقًا in the Description of the Heavens

In Al-Mulk 67:3 and Nuh 71:15, the word refers to the relation among several heavens. The axis of this usage is layeredness and structural proportion.

طَبَقًا in the Description of Human States

In Al-Inshiqaq 84:19, طبق (ṭabaq) is not a fixed physical surface, but a state or stage that the human being traverses and leaves behind.

Covering in Lexical Usage

In lexical usage, طبق (ṭabaq) can be a lid or covering placed upon something. This image is important for knowing the origin of the root’s meaning, but it should not be translated mechanically as “lid” in the verses.

Correspondence and Harmony

Forms such as طابق (ṭābaqa), تطابق (taṭābaqa), and مطابقة (muṭābaqah) do not occur in the Qur'an, but they show that the concept of two things becoming equal and harmonious exists in the historical field of this root. However, طِبَاقًا (ṭibāqan) should not be reduced only to “harmonious,” because the image of layers and things positioned in relation to one another remains in it.

Syntactic and Structural Notes

The Position of طِبَاقًا

In the expression:

سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا

the word طِبَاقًا (ṭibāqan) is accusative and describes the heavens in terms of how they are structured. In natural English, it can be rendered as “layer-wise” or “in proportionate layers.”

The Emphasis in لَتَرْكَبُنَّ

In the expression:

لَتَرْكَبُنَّ طَبَقًا عَنْ طَبَقٍ

the initial لام (lām) on the verb and the emphatic نون (nūn) at its end indicate certainty. Therefore, the translation should preserve this emphasis as much as possible:

“Surely you will pass”

or

“You will certainly enter one state after another.”

The Role of عَنْ

The particle عن (ʿan) is important in this structure. عن (ʿan) can indicate distancing, passing beyond, and leaving behind. Therefore, the expression does not merely present an image of several static layers upon one another.

If the intended image were simply one layer being upon another, a structure with على (ʿalā) could have made a more direct image of “being on top of something.” But طَبَقًا عَنْ طَبَقٍ (ṭabaqan ʿan ṭabaq) highlights movement away from one state and entry into the next.

Therefore, “stage after stage” or “from one state to another” is more accurate than “layer upon layer.” Nevertheless, عن (ʿan) does not necessarily mean that the previous state is, in every case, completely destroyed or emptied out; its more certain meaning is passing beyond and distancing from the previous condition.

Difference from Nearby Roots

ط ب ق and و ف ق

The root و ف ق (w f q) indicates compatibility, accompaniment, and fitting together. In ط ب ق (ṭ b q), beyond compatibility, the image of two surfaces, limits, or states being placed in relation to one another is also present.

Two opinions may agree with one another without one being طبق (ṭabaq) of the other. مطابقة (muṭābaqah) usually conveys a more precise correspondence of the limits or qualities of two things.

ط ب ق and و ض ع

The root و ض ع (w ḍ ʿ) indicates placing and setting down. Anything may be placed somewhere, but ط ب ق (ṭ b q) usually also includes a kind of covering, proportion, or layered relation.

ط ب ق and س ت ر

The root س ت ر (s t r) focuses on covering and hiding from sight. طبق (ṭabaq) does not necessarily hide something; it may simply be placed upon it, become equal-sized with it, or be one of its layers.

ط ب ق and غ ط ي

The root غ ط ي (gh ṭ y) indicates covering and encompassing. Not every covering necessarily corresponds to what it covers. In ط ب ق (ṭ b q), proportion and surface placement are more prominent.

ط ب ق and ر ت ب

The root ر ت ب (r t b) indicates being ordered in ranks and defined places. Rank more often shows the position of something within an order, while طبق (ṭabaq) preserves the image of a surface, layer, or state placed in relation to another طبق (ṭabaq).

ط ب ق and د ر ج

The root د ر ج (d r j) indicates gradual, step-by-step movement. A درجة (darajah) can be one step of an ascending or descending path. In طبق (ṭabaq), the distinct states or layers themselves and their relation to one another are more prominent.

ط ب ق and ط و ر

The root ط و ر (ṭ w r), in usages such as أَطْوَارًا (aṭwāran), indicates different states and forms of growth or transformation:

وَقَدْ خَلَقَكُمْ أَطْوَارًا

while He created you in varied states and phases.

Nuh 71:14

طور (ṭawr) can be a stage in which the form, feature, or condition of a being changes. طبق (ṭabaq) relies more on the relation between successive states and the passage from one condition to another.

Nevertheless, this difference should not be made absolute. Both roots can be translated into English as “stage.” The main difference lies in their linguistic image: طور (ṭawr) highlights transformation of form or state more strongly; طبق (ṭabaq) highlights the placement and succession of states in relation to one another.

ط ب ق and ص ف ف

The root ص ف ف (ṣ f f) indicates being placed next to one another in a row or line. A row shows more of a side-by-side relation, while طبق (ṭabaq) usually has a layered, covering, or staged relation.

ط ب ق and س و ي

The root س و ي (s w y) indicates equality, evenness, balance, or setting something right. ط ب ق (ṭ b q) focuses on the relation between two or more surfaces, coverings, or states; but س و ي (s w y) can indicate equality and balance without a layered relation.

Linguistic Summary

The root ط ب ق (ṭ b q) indicates something being placed upon another thing with a kind of covering, proportion, or alignment. Its material image is a surface or covering that fits what lies beneath it.

In the Qur'an, طِبَاقًا (ṭibāqan) describes the layered structure of the heavens, while طَبَقًا عَنْ طَبَقٍ (ṭabaqan ʿan ṭabaq) indicates the human passage from one state to another. In the first usage, the relation and harmony of layers is prominent; in the second, succession and transition between states.

This root is connected to covering, equality, rank, and stage, but its distinctive feature is the link between placement, correspondence, and the succession of layers or states.
