Boys, adolescents, young men, growing up, and uses concerning service and youthful vigor

The Arabic root غ ل م concerns childhood, youth, and human growth, with another branch concerning sexual arousal. Its best-known word is غُلَام, a boy, adolescent, or young man. Its plurals include غِلْمَان and غِلْمَة.

In ordinary use, غلام denotes a male during childhood or youth, without specifying an exact age. Lexicons record it for a young boy, an adolescent approaching maturity, and a young man, and sometimes even an adult man.

Another branch has غُلْمَة for sexual arousal and اغْتَلَمَ for its intensification. This branch is associated with growth and puberty, but does not mean everyone called غلام has reached sexual maturity or experiences intense desire.

Arabic also sometimes uses غلام for a male servant or slave. Neither service nor enslavement is a necessary meaning of the word: a boy can be called غلام without being anyone's servant or slave.

The root occurs thirteen times in the Quran, always within the noun family غلام: eleven singular occurrences, one dual غلامين, and one plural غلمان.

It describes a promised son, a boy discovered during a journey, the boy in the story of Moses and his companion, two orphan boys, and youths circulating among the inhabitants of the Garden.

This range shows a designation concerning male youth and life stage. Exact age, social status, task, and moral qualities are established by each verse's context.

1. The root in early Arabic

غلام: a boy growing up

In early Arabic, غلام was a recognized designation for a boy or young man.

Lexicons record different definitions of its age range.

One definition concerns a boy whose mustache is just beginning to grow, pointing mainly to adolescence and approaching young adulthood.

Another definition extends from birth until the hair turns gray. This broad formulation itself shows that usage is not confined to a fixed age.

Arabs are also reported to have called an adult man غلام, much as Persian may address a young or even adult man as “boy” without specifying his age.

The differences show that the designation has a flexible age range.

One person called غلام might still be a child; another might have reached adolescence.

The designation can fit both, but hearing it alone does not establish their exact ages.

The Quran also exhibits this range.

A witness attributed to pre-Quranic poetry: غلام and غلامة in Aws ibn Ghalfa

A poem attributed to Aws ibn Ghalfa al-Hujaymi, identified as a pre-Islamic poet, describes a mare:

وَمُرْكِضَةٌ صَرِيحِيٌّ أَبُوهَا

wa-murkiḍatun ṣarīḥiyyun abūhā

يُهَانُ لَهَا الْغُلَامَةُ وَالْغُلَامُ

yuhānu lahā al-ghulāmatu wa-l-ghulāmu

Translation:

“And a pregnant mare whose foal stirs within her, her sire of pure stock; for her the girl and boy are put to hard service.”

The poet describes a valued mare whose care requires the labor of young people. With the supplied vocalization مُرْكِضَة, the word concerns movement of the foal in the womb. Another transmitted vocalization, مِرْكَضَة, describes a running mare.

Two words occur together:

الْغُلَامَةُ for a girl.

الْغُلَامُ for a boy.

The witness attests the feminine غلامة in early Arabic, although the ordinary and familiar غلام denotes a male.

Here both figures appear in a context of caring for a horse.

This does not establish “slave” as the necessary meaning of غلام. The service is understood from their role in caring for the horse, rather than from their designation alone.

A young person might be employed, belong to a household, or occupy another social position. غلام alone does not determine that relationship.

Early literary sources identify Aws ibn Ghalfa as a pre-Islamic poet. Lexical and literary works transmit this verse with some variations, including بها instead of لها in some versions.

It is useful evidence for usage attributed to the pre-Islamic period, but cannot determine the word's earliest appearance or every social characteristic of those so designated.

غلام used of an adult man

Early Arabic also records its application to adult men.

A man recognized for ability, generosity, or physical readiness might be called غلام in speech or poetry after childhood and adolescence.

Such use can retain associations with youth, vigor, and position without fixing an age.

This breadth does not mean every occurrence should simply be translated as “man.”

The ordinary direct meaning remains boy or young man; an adult referent requires contextual support.

2. Semantic branches and derivatives

A. غُلَام: a boy or young man

غلام is a noun whose principal use denotes a boy or young male.

It can designate a child not yet born whose birth is announced.

Telling a father he will have a غلام concerns the future birth of a son.

It need not describe a presently adolescent child; it identifies the future child's sex.

By contrast, saying someone saw a غلام on the road concerns a boy already present.

The sentence establishes the person's time and situation, not غلام alone.

B. غِلْمَان and غِلْمَة: plurals of غلام

Both are recognized plurals of غلام.

Lexical and morphological descriptions generally identify غلمان as a plural of abundance and, in some analyses, غلمة as a plural of paucity.

This classification must not become an absolute rule specifying a numerical count in every use of غلمان.

The Quran uses غلمان only once, in Tur.

Its appearance in the Garden does not make it a proper name for an independently defined kind of being. Lexically, it is the plural of غلام.

C. غُلُومَة: the period of boyhood

Arabic sources record غُلُومَة, غُلُومِيَّة, and غُلَامِيَّة for the stage or condition of being a غلام.

They concern a period of life in which that designation applies.

Some lexical accounts mention an upper age of around seventeen for this period.

That number is not a definitive universal definition of غلام.

The sources also record its use for differing ages.

A single lexical definition therefore cannot establish that every Quranic غلام was under seventeen.

D. غُلْمَة: sexual arousal

In another branch, غُلْمَة denotes arousal and intensity of sexual desire.

غَلِمَ and اغْتَلَمَ likewise describe such arousal in humans or animals.

For example, the family is used of a male animal experiencing strong mating desire.

Some sources emphasize animal use of اغتلم, while others also record human use.

A semantic connection with youth is understandable, since physical maturation accompanies the development of sexual capacity.

It must not become the definition of every occurrence of غلام.

The Quran calls an unborn future child غلام, so the meaning there plainly does not depend on actual sexual desire.

A shared root likewise does not make غلام a sexual designation in the Quran.

E. اِغْتِلَام: intensification and exceeding a limit

Beyond desire, اغتلم is recorded for intensifying or becoming violently agitated.

اغتلم البحر, for example, describes a sea with fiercely disturbed waves.

It does not attribute human sexual desire to the sea.

The word expresses intense movement and turbulence.

Elsewhere, اغتلام can concern exceeding an ordinary measure or the intensification of a condition.

This branch does not occur in the Quran and should not determine غلام in birth, childhood, or Garden passages.

F. غَيْلَم: other recorded uses

Arabic sources record غَيْلَم and غَيْلَمِيّ for a young man with abundant hair and particular bodily features.

غيلم also names certain animals, including a turtle and a frog.

Their inclusion in lexicons does not establish one proven historical derivation linking all of them to غلام as boy.

The animal names, description of a young man, and arousal sense need separate examination.

For Quranic study, the principal relevant branch is غلام as boy or young man.

3. Quranic forms of غ ل م

All Quranic occurrences belong to one noun family.

The forms include:

  • غُلَامٌ: a boy or son, according to context.
  • غُلَامًا: the accusative form of the same noun.
  • بِغُلَامٍ: with the preposition باء, in announcements of birth.
  • الْغُلَامُ: the particular boy known within the story.
  • لِغُلَامَيْنِ: for two boys.
  • غِلْمَانٌ: boys or young men, the plural of غلام.

The differences concern syntax, case, prepositions, definiteness, and number.

The verbs غلم, اغتلم, and أغلم do not occur in the Quran.

Neither the feminine غلامة nor غلمة as a verbal noun for sexual desire occurs there.

Non-Quranic derivatives must not be counted as Quranic occurrences merely to make a study seem complete.

4. غلام in announcements of a son's birth

Many Quranic occurrences concern announcing the birth of a son.

They refer to a child not yet born.

This is particularly clear evidence for the word's Quranic meaning.

The announcement of Yahya to Zakariyya

Maryam 19:7

يَا زَكَرِيَّا إِنَّا نُبَشِّرُكَ بِغُلَامٍ اسْمُهُ يَحْيَىٰ لَمْ نَجْعَلْ لَهُ مِنْ قَبْلُ سَمِيًّا

yā Zakariyyā innā nubashshiruka bi-ghulāmin ismuhu Yaḥyā lam najʿal lahu min qablu samiyyā

Maryam 19:7

“O Zakariyya, We give you good news of a boy whose name is Yahya; We have not previously given anyone his name.”

غلام is the child whose birth is announced to Zakariyya.

It does not mean a presently adolescent boy or a servant here.

It concerns a son who will be born in the future.

The verse also identifies his name: Yahya.

In Maryam 19:8, Zakariyya asks how he can have a boy when his wife is barren and he has reached old age.

Al Imran 3:40 presents the same question.

Both uses concern Zakariyya becoming a father.

“Son” is therefore a natural, precise translation.

Sonship here follows from Zakariyya's relationship to the expected birth. غلام does not invariably mean “the addressee's son.”

غلام and صبي in Yahya's story

After the birth announcement, Maryam 19:12 reports God's address to Yahya:

يَا يَحْيَىٰ خُذِ الْكِتَابَ بِقُوَّةٍ

yā Yaḥyā khudhi al-kitāba bi-quwwa

Maryam 19:12

“O Yahya, take hold of the Book firmly.”

It then says he was given judgment in childhood.

The word used is صَبِيًّا.

The Quran thus uses different words to describe life stages.

غلام in the birth announcement identifies the future child as male.

صبي in Maryam 19:12 emphasizes childhood at a stage of his life.

The words should not be treated as fully synonymous everywhere.

The announcement of a boy to Maryam

Maryam 19:19

قَالَ إِنَّمَا أَنَا رَسُولُ رَبِّكِ لِأَهَبَ لَكِ غُلَامًا زَكِيًّا

qāla innamā anā rasūlu rabbiki li-ahaba laki ghulāman zakiyyā

Maryam 19:19

“He said: I am only the Messenger of your Lord, to bestow upon you a pure boy.”

غُلَامًا denotes the boy whose birth is announced to Maryam.

زَكِيًّا describes him as pure and worthy.

Purity is not part of the meaning of غلام itself.

Without زكيا, the noun would still mean boy.

In Maryam 19:20, Maryam asks how she can have a boy when no human has touched her and she has not been unchaste.

Again, the word concerns an unborn son.

These uses show that غلام does not depend on a child's present sexual development or capacity.

A knowledgeable boy and a forbearing boy

In Hijr 15:53, divine messengers announce to Abraham a غُلَامٍ عَلِيمٍ, a knowledgeable boy.

Dhariyat 51:28 uses the same expression.

Saffat 37:101 announces a غُلَامٍ حَلِيمٍ, a forbearing boy.

In all three, غلام means boy; عليم or حليم supplies his quality.

The expression need not mean a newborn already possesses all the knowledge or forbearance of a mature person.

An announcement can identify future qualities.

One may say that an unborn child will become knowledgeable and worthy. The future description is attributed to the person without requiring every quality to be fully realized at birth.

In Saffat 37:102, the same child reaches a stage at which he can work alongside his father and participate in an important conversation.

This continuity shows growth toward ability and understanding, but غلام alone determines neither age nor degree of maturity.

5. غلام in Joseph's story

Yusuf 12:19

وَجَاءَتْ سَيَّارَةٌ فَأَرْسَلُوا وَارِدَهُمْ فَأَدْلَىٰ دَلْوَهُ قَالَ يَا بُشْرَىٰ هَٰذَا غُلَامٌ وَأَسَرُّوهُ بِضَاعَةً وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ بِمَا يَعْمَلُونَ

wa-jāʾat sayyāratun fa-arsalū wāridahum fa-adlā dalwahu qāla yā bushrā hādhā ghulāmun wa-asarrūhu biḍāʿatan wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun bi-mā yaʿmalūn

Yūsuf 12:19

“A caravan arrived and sent its water-drawer, who lowered his bucket. He said: Good news! Here is a boy. They concealed him as merchandise; and The God knew what they were doing.”

غلام refers to Joseph.

The caravan finds him in the well, and the water-drawer says on seeing him:

هَٰذَا غُلَامٌ

hādhā ghulām

Yūsuf 12:19

That is, here is a boy.

Here he is neither a child whose future birth is announced nor someone identified as an established servant.

The word identifies Joseph as male and in a youthful stage of life.

Does غلام mean slave here?

No. At the caravan's first encounter with Joseph, it identifies him as a boy.

The verse then mentions concealing him as merchandise.

Yusuf 12:20 describes selling him for a small price.

Trading and appropriating Joseph are understood from conduct and the continuing story, not from غلام itself. The commentators differ over the referents of some plural pronouns in these verses.

This distinction matters.

A free person may be seized and sold as a slave by someone else.

That imposes a social condition upon the person without making enslavement the meaning of the age designation or establishing the legitimacy of the act.

Joseph's being a boy and others' treating him as merchandise are distinct matters.

غلام and فتى in Joseph's story

In Yusuf 12:30, the city's women say that the official's wife is seeking to seduce فَتَاهَا.

From ف ت ي, the expression refers here to the young man in her household.

Joseph is thus called غلام at one point and فتى at another.

The words can describe one person at different stages or in different situations.

They are not identical in meaning.

غلام chiefly emphasizes being a boy and growing up.

فتى can denote youth and, in some social uses, a young companion or servant. Ability may be associated with youth, but is not inherent in every occurrence.

The later فتاها does not make غلام at the well intrinsically mean servant.

6. غلام in the story of Moses and his companion

The boy who was killed

Kahf 18:74

فَانْطَلَقَا حَتَّىٰ إِذَا لَقِيَا غُلَامًا فَقَتَلَهُ قَالَ أَقَتَلْتَ نَفْسًا زَكِيَّةً بِغَيْرِ نَفْسٍ لَقَدْ جِئْتَ شَيْئًا نُكْرًا

fa-nṭalaqā ḥattā idhā laqiyā ghulāman fa-qatalahu qāla a-qatalta nafsan zakiyyatan bi-ghayri nafsin la-qad jiʾta shayʾan nukrā

Al-Kahf 18:74

“They set out until, when they met a boy, he killed him. He said: Have you killed a pure soul without its having killed another? You have indeed done a dreadful thing.”

غُلَامًا denotes the boy Moses and his companion encounter.

The word itself does not fix his age.

He may have been a child or adolescent; the verse gives no age in years.

Conversely, one cannot restrict غلام to a mature young man and assert that he was necessarily an adult.

The Quranic range of the family does not support that restriction.

نَفْسًا زَكِيَّةً and the boy's condition

Moses uses نَفْسًا زَكِيَّةً in his objection.

That description concerns the soul's purity, not the lexical meaning of غلام.

Moses adds that the person was killed without having killed another.

His objection concerns killing a human being without an evident justification.

غلام itself establishes neither innocence nor guilt.

Those questions belong to the dialogue and the story's later explanation.

The companion's explanation

Kahf 18:80

وَأَمَّا الْغُلَامُ فَكَانَ أَبَوَاهُ مُؤْمِنَيْنِ فَخَشِينَا أَنْ يُرْهِقَهُمَا طُغْيَانًا وَكُفْرًا

wa-ammā al-ghulāmu fa-kāna abawāhu muʾminayni fa-khashīnā an yurhiqahumā ṭughyānan wa-kufrā

Al-Kahf 18:80

“As for the boy, his parents were believers, and we feared that he would burden them with rebellion and disbelief.”

الْغُلَامُ has the definite article and refers back to the boy already known in the story.

It still means boy.

His parents' faith, the fear of rebellion and disbelief, and the outcome are conveyed by other parts of the sentence.

The verse does not make غلام mean a rebellious child or sinful adolescent.

Nor does فَخَشِينَا أَنْ يُرْهِقَهُمَا provide a lexical definition of puberty or every adolescent's responsibility.

This part of the story explains a particular event; lexically, غلام remains the designation of that boy.

Two orphan boys and reaching maturity

Kahf 18:82

وَأَمَّا الْجِدَارُ فَكَانَ لِغُلَامَيْنِ يَتِيمَيْنِ فِي الْمَدِينَةِ وَكَانَ تَحْتَهُ كَنْزٌ لَهُمَا وَكَانَ أَبُوهُمَا صَالِحًا فَأَرَادَ رَبُّكَ أَنْ يَبْلُغَا أَشُدَّهُمَا وَيَسْتَخْرِجَا كَنْزَهُمَا رَحْمَةً مِنْ رَبِّكَ وَمَا فَعَلْتُهُ عَنْ أَمْرِي ذَٰلِكَ تَأْوِيلُ مَا لَمْ تَسْطِعْ عَلَيْهِ صَبْرًا

wa-ammā al-jidāru fa-kāna li-ghulāmayni yatīmayni fī al-madīnati wa-kāna taḥtahu kanzun lahumā wa-kāna abūhumā ṣāliḥan fa-arāda rabbuka an yablughā ashuddahumā wa-yastakhrijā kanzahumā raḥmatan min rabbika wa-mā faʿaltuhu ʿan amrī dhālika taʾwīlu mā lam tasṭiʿ ʿalayhi ṣabrā

Al-Kahf 18:82

“As for the wall, it belonged to two orphan boys in the city. Beneath it was a treasure for them, and their father was righteous. Your Lord therefore wished them to reach maturity and extract their treasure, as a mercy from your Lord. I did not do it on my own command. That is the interpretation of what you could not bear patiently.”

لِغُلَامَيْنِ is the dual form of غلام.

يَتِيمَيْنِ describes those same two boys.

The words identify distinct characteristics.

غلامين identifies two boys.

يتيمين identifies their family circumstances.

Being orphaned is not part of the meaning of غلام.

غلام and reaching full strength

The verse continues:

أَنْ يَبْلُغَا أَشُدَّهُمَا

an yablughā ashuddahumā

Al-Kahf 18:82

That is, for the two to reach their maturity and strength.

The expression indicates they had not yet reached the relevant stage of development.

غلام here fits a period in which a person is still growing.

The expression does not establish their precise ages.

Nor should reaching أَشُدّ automatically be equated with sexual puberty: physical development, ability, and readiness to manage affairs can be relevant in different Quranic contexts.

Here their maturation is connected with extracting their own treasure.

That comes from the whole verse, not غلام alone.

7. غِلْمَان in the Garden

Tur 52:24

وَيَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ غِلْمَانٌ لَهُمْ كَأَنَّهُمْ لُؤْلُؤٌ مَكْنُونٌ

wa-yaṭūfu ʿalayhim ghilmānun lahum ka-annahum luʾluʾun maknūn

Al-Ṭūr 52:24

“Youths assigned to them circulate among them, as though they were sheltered pearls.”

غِلْمَانٌ is the plural of غلام.

Its basic meaning is boys or young men.

Other parts of the sentence describe their situation in the Garden.

وَيَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ

يَطُوفُ belongs to ط و ف and concerns circulating or moving around something or someone.

The youths circulate among the Garden's inhabitants.

The nearby context of food, drink, and enjoyment supports understanding this activity as attendance and service.

Service follows from the action in context, not from the independent meaning of غلمان.

Explaining the phrase as “young attendants” should therefore make clear that attendance is understood from the whole sentence.

The noun itself remains boys or young men.

Does غلمان mean slaves?

The word does not necessarily mean that.

In some Arabic social contexts, غلام could denote a male servant or slave. Such use does not erase the principal boy or young-man sense.

Tur 52:24 says nothing about their purchase, captivity, coercion, or legal enslavement.

They should not be identified as slaves solely from غلمان.

لَهُمْ means “for them” and expresses a relation to the Garden's inhabitants.

Depending on context, لام can express assignment, association, benefit, or possession.

لهم alone cannot definitively establish the kind of ownership or service involved.

The verse clearly presents boys or young men circulating among the Garden's inhabitants.

كَأَنَّهُمْ لُؤْلُؤٌ مَكْنُونٌ

The verse compares them to sheltered pearls.

لؤلؤ means pearls.

مكنون belongs to ك ن ن and concerns being covered or protected.

The comparison evokes beauty, purity, and preciousness.

It does not imply that the youths are literally made of pearls or have bodies constructed like gemstones.

كَأَنَّهُمْ marks a comparison.

Nor does the comparison establish their exact age, ethnicity, skin color, or other bodily characteristics.

غلمان and ولدان مخلدون

Waqiah 56:17 and Insan 76:19 speak of وِلْدَانٌ مُخَلَّدُونَ.

ولدان belongs to و ل د, not غ ل م.

مخلدون belongs to خ ل د.

The subject resembles the youths of Tur: both occur amid the Garden's blessings and attendance.

Different words should nevertheless not be treated as completely identical without examination.

غلمان is the plural of غلام.

ولدان is the plural of ولد.

مخلدون qualifies ولدان in those two verses, but does not directly accompany غلمان in Tur 52:24.

Features understood from مخلدون should not be incorporated into the lexical meaning of غلام itself.

In discussing the Hereafter, the Quran's imagery should remain distinct from details it does not state.

8. Comparison with related roots

غ ل م and و ل د: a youthful male and a child

ولد concerns a child and the relationship of birth.

It is not intrinsically restricted to males and can denote a daughter or son.

غلام ordinarily identifies a boy.

Zakariyya's announcement of a غلام concerns a son's birth.

In Yusuf 12:19, however, the caravan calls Joseph غلام without his being their son.

غلام therefore need not mean the speaker's son.

ولد can also describe an adult offspring, since the parent-child relation is not confined to childhood.

غلام chiefly concerns sex and life stage, while ولد emphasizes birth and offspring.

غ ل م and ص ب و: a boy and a child

صبي denotes a child, especially at an early age.

Maryam 19:12 describes Yahya in childhood.

Maryam 19:29 speaks of a child in the cradle.

غلام can concern a promised unborn child, a present child, or an adolescent growing up.

Its field extends beyond one narrow phase of childhood.

Their ranges can overlap without being identical. صبي principally denotes a male child and has the feminine صبية, while غلام ordinarily denotes a boy or young man.

غ ل م and ف ت ي: a boy and a youth

فتى concerns youth and a stage of growth; vigor can be associated with it in context.

The Quran uses فتى and فتية for young people, including the youths of the Cave.

It also uses فتى in Joseph's story.

The two words can therefore apply to the same person.

In some social uses, فتى also identifies a young companion or servant.

غلام may occur in a similar context, though its principal meaning concerns being a boy and growing up.

No precise age divisions should be invented that the Quran does not establish, such as an absolute cutoff between غلام and فتى.

غ ل م and ط ف ل: a boy and a young child

طفل concerns a child, especially a young child.

Hajj 22:5 describes human emergence as a child and progression through later life stages.

غلام can describe a child without being limited to early childhood.

The two orphan boys in Kahf 18:82 are still to reach maturity.

The designation fits ongoing growth without specifying a newborn, a young child, or an adolescent approaching puberty.

غ ل م and ع ب د: a boy and servitude

عبد concerns service, submission, and the relationship of servitude.

غلام denotes a boy or young man.

A boy might be enslaved, and غلام may then be used for him in that social setting.

The designation alone does not establish enslavement.

عبد in the Quran also denotes God's servants and must not invariably be restricted to human slavery.

Expressions such as مَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ require their own examination in discussions of human ownership and social status.

غلام should therefore not be equated with عبد or terms concerning human ownership.

غ ل م and ج ر ي: a boy and a young girl

جارية in some Arabic uses denotes a young girl.

غلام ordinarily denotes a boy.

The designations can thus contrast in sex and life stage.

جارية also denotes a female servant or slave in some uses.

As with غلام for a servant, the social use does not erase the age and sex meaning.

Early poetry also records the feminine غلامة, so جارية is not Arabic's only way to designate a girl alongside غلام.

غ ل م and ش ب ب: growth and youth

شباب concerns youth and the development of strength.

غلام can cover part of that period, but Quranic use also designates an unborn child.

It must not be confined to post-pubertal youth.

Although youth has associations with vigor and growth, such qualities are not necessary meanings of every غلام.

9. Questions of understanding and translation

Does غلام mean slave in the Quran?

No: enslavement is not its necessary meaning.

In the stories of Zakariyya and Maryam, it denotes a promised son.

In Moses' story, it denotes the boy encountered by Moses and his companion.

In Joseph's story, it identifies the boy found by the caravan; trading and appropriating him are separate matters in the narrative.

Translating every occurrence as slave or servant is therefore incorrect.

Is a غلام always a prepubescent child?

No. Lexicons record the word for young boys, adolescents, and young men.

The Quran likewise uses it across different contexts of birth and growth, without giving an exact age for every referent.

This does not mean every occurrence may be interpreted as an adult man.

Approximate age requires support from context, conduct, family circumstances, and expressions of development.

Does غلام mean son?

In many birth-announcement verses, yes.

But it does not necessarily express sonship to the speaker.

In Yusuf 12:19, the caravan finds a boy and calls him غلام.

“Boy” fits there, not “our son.”

Does غلام lexically concern sexual desire?

The family has a recorded branch concerning intense sexual desire.

That branch must not be imposed on every use of غلام.

The Quran particularly uses غلام for birth announcements and boys growing up.

Those uses do not establish a sexual meaning for the designation itself.

Are the Garden's غلمان slaves?

The verse describes youths circulating among the Garden's inhabitants.

This occurs in a context of blessing and attendance.

غلمان and لهم alone do not establish a legal relation of enslavement.

The verse says nothing of their being bought, sold, or coerced.

Are the Garden's youths sexual partners?

No such meaning follows from the root or Tur 52:24.

The verse mentions youths, their circulating, and their comparison to sheltered pearls.

Assigning them a sexual role adds something the verse does not state.

The non-Quranic غلمة for sexual desire does not establish that conclusion either.

Does a knowledgeable or forbearing boy possess those qualities fully at birth?

Not necessarily.

The announcements attribute qualities to a future child.

They may become manifest later in life.

عليم and حليم are separate descriptions; their meanings should not enter the definition of غلام.

10. Translation proposals for Forqan

In most Quranic uses, the basic equivalent of غلام is boy; birth announcements can naturally use son.

This is concise, natural, and suited to the Quranic contexts.

Birth announcements can use “a boy” or “a son”:

غُلَامٌ: a boy.

بِغُلَامٍ: of a boy, as in “We give you good news of a boy.”

غُلَامًا زَكِيًّا: a pure boy.

غُلَامٍ عَلِيمٍ: a knowledgeable boy.

غُلَامٍ حَلِيمٍ: a forbearing boy.

In Joseph's story:

هَٰذَا غُلَامٌ: “Here is a boy.”

In the story of Moses and his companion:

لَقِيَا غُلَامًا: “They encountered a boy.”

الْغُلَامُ: “the boy,” according to sentence structure.

In Kahf 18:82:

لِغُلَامَيْنِ يَتِيمَيْنِ: “belonging to two orphan boys.”

In Tur 52:24:

غِلْمَانٌ: “boys” or “youths,” according to the wording chosen.

In the complete sentence, “Youths assigned to them circulate among them” retains the lexical and contextual sense.

An explanatory “young attendants” should make clear that attendance follows from the action and context, not from غلمان alone.

For non-Quranic derivatives:

غُلَام: a boy, adolescent, or young man.

غِلْمَان: boys or young men.

غِلْمَة: a plural of غلام; the differently vocalized غُلْمَة means sexual desire.

غُلُومَة: boyhood or adolescence.

غُلَامَة: a young girl, in an uncommon early usage.

غَلِمَ: became sexually aroused.

اغْتَلَمَ: became intensely aroused; in another use, became violently agitated.

غِلِّيم: a person or animal with intense sexual desire.

غَيْلَم: in the relevant human use, a young man; in other uses, a name for certain animals.

Translation should not replace an age designation with a social or moral judgment. غلام can be a boy, adolescent, or young man; enslavement, service, purity, knowledge, and forbearance follow from circumstances or separate descriptions.

Conclusion

The Arabic root غ ل م concerns childhood and youth, with another branch concerning sexual arousal.

Its best-known derivative, غلام, denotes a boy, adolescent, or young man. It has no fixed exact age: sources record it from birth through youth and sometimes even for an adult man.

The poem attributed to Aws ibn Ghalfa attests غلام for a boy and غلامة for a girl. They care for a horse, but service is understood contextually rather than being the necessary meaning of غلام.

The Quran uses only derivatives of the noun family غلام.

In announcements, it denotes the son promised to Zakariyya, Maryam, or Abraham.

In Joseph's story, it denotes the boy found by the caravan. Trading and appropriating him do not make slave the meaning of غلام itself.

In Moses' story, it denotes the boy who is killed; the passage concerning two orphan boys expressly says they are to reach maturity.

In Tur 52:24, غلمان is the plural of غلام, amid circulation and attendance in the Garden. Service follows from يطوف in context, not from the noun alone. No sexual role or legal enslavement follows from it.

Other branches have غلمة for sexual desire and اغتلام for its intensification or violent agitation elsewhere. These senses do not occur in the Quran and should not be imposed on غلام.

غلام differs from ولد, صبي, طفل, فتى, and عبد.

ولد expresses offspring and the relation of birth.

صبي and طفل mainly concern childhood.

فتى emphasizes youth and, in some uses, a young person's social position.

عبد concerns servitude.

غلام principally identifies a boy or young male.

The Quranic core of غ ل م concerns male childhood and growth toward youth: a designation that does not alone establish exact age, social status, or moral qualities.

For most occurrences, “boy” is a precise, natural equivalent, with “son” fitting birth announcements. Where the context supports a clearer age or position, “adolescent” or “young man” may also fit.
