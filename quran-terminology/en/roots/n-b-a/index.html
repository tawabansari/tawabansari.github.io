Foundational Meaning

The root ن ب أ is connected with news and awareness, but not every light or ordinary report. نَبَأ (naba') is news that has weight, produces effect, places the listener in a new state, or reveals an important truth.

For this reason, in the Qur'an, naba' usually appears in relation to great news, unseen reports, the fate of peoples, Resurrection, revelation, or forms of awareness toward which the human being cannot remain indifferent.

The central axis of this root is “significant making-known.” One who يُنبِئ does not merely speak; he gives news that must be known. And نَبِي (nabī) belongs to this same field: one connected with divine naba', with weighty divine knowledge and news.

Important Forms

The nominal form نَبَأ in the Qur'an means important news, a great report, or consequential awareness.

Form II, نَبَّأَ (nabba'a), means to inform or make known, as in أُنَبِّئُكُم or يُنَبِّئُكُم.

Form IV, أَنبَأَ (anba'a), also means to inform or make known. In some contexts, it comes close to precise disclosure or informing about names and hidden matters.

The form اسْتَنبَأَ (istanba'a) means to seek news or ask for information. It appears only once in the Qur'an.

نُبُوَّة (nubuwwah) points to prophethood as a station or condition of relation to divine news and revelatory awareness.

نَبِي (nabī) is the most important derivative of this root. A nabī is one connected with divine naba': the receiver and speaker of a knowledge that is beyond ordinary news.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of ن ب أ is important news. The difference between naba' and ordinary khabar is the weight and effect of the report. Every naba' is news, but not every news item is naba'.

Naba' is usually a report of great standing, or a report behind which there is a hidden truth that must be revealed. For this reason, the Qur'an speaks of النَّبَإِ الْعَظِيمِ:

عَمَّ يَتَسَاءَلُونَ ۝ عَنِ النَّبَإِ الْعَظِيمِ

About what are they asking one another? About the great news. An-Naba' 78:1-2

Here naba' comes with عظيم (ʿaẓīm). This combination shows that the root does not belong to weightless passing news, but to heavy and destiny-shaping news.

The same context then mentions disagreement about that news: الَّذِي هُمْ فِيهِ مُخْتَلِفُونَ, the one about which they differ. An-Naba' 78:3

This shows that naba' can be news that produces positions, raises questions, and places people before an important truth.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root can be seen as “news arriving from somewhere distant or hidden.” News is like something emerging from behind a veil and entering the field of awareness. Before the naba', the listener either does not know, only guesses, or remains in ambiguity. After the naba', something becomes clear and the state of knowledge changes.

In this image, news is not merely sound. It carries a burden of meaning. This is why naba' is tied to important reports, destinies, signs, Resurrection, revelation, and accounts of earlier peoples. It is as if something from beyond the horizon of human knowing has emerged and stood before the listener.

Older Arabic Usage

In older Arabic, نَبَأ was used for important and noteworthy news. Arabs distinguished ordinary information from a report that carried status and effect. Naba' is more often used for news worthy of attention, transmission, and response.

Lexical sources sometimes connect this root with appearing, emerging, or news arriving from elsewhere. For a final expanded version, if a strong pre-Qur'anic poetic witness for نبأ, أنباء, or the use of نبي is found, it can be added. Until a precise witness is available, it is better to remain with the general lexical field and Qur'anic usage.

Qur'anic Usage

The first field of usage is great and consequential news:

عَنِ النَّبَإِ الْعَظِيمِ

About the great news. An-Naba' 78:2

Here naba' is news about which people ask and differ. A suitable translation is “great news” or “great report.” This use shows that naba' is connected with something important and weighty.

Another use is reports of earlier peoples:

تِلْكَ مِنْ أَنبَاءِ الْغَيْبِ نُوحِيهَا إِلَيْكَ

That is from the reports of the unseen which We reveal to you. Hud 11:49

Here أَنبَاء is the plural of naba', and it refers to reports outside the ordinary reach of the listener. These reports are disclosed through revelation. Therefore, naba' can be an account of the past that is unseen to the present audience.

Another example:

نَحْنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ نَبَأَهُم بِالْحَقِّ

We recount their news to you with truth. Al-Kahf 18:13

Here نَبَأَهُم is the important report of a group, an account that must be told with truth, not with conjecture or legend. Naba' is joined with بِالْحَقِّ, showing that the weight of the report is tied to its truthfulness.

Informing and Making Known

Another field of usage is informing and making known:

قُلْ أَؤُنَبِّئُكُم بِخَيْرٍ مِّن ذَٰلِكُمْ

Say: Shall I inform you of something better than that? Āl ʿImrān 3:15

Here أُنَبِّئُكُم means: shall I give you important news, or make you aware? “Inform you” is accurate, but “make you aware” often captures the force better, because the verb is not merely transferring sound; it is delivering awareness.

Another example:

وَأُنَبِّئُكُم بِمَا تَأْكُلُونَ وَمَا تَدَّخِرُونَ فِي بُيُوتِكُمْ

And I inform you of what you eat and what you store in your houses. Āl ʿImrān 3:49

Here informing concerns something hidden from the listener or seemingly beyond the speaker’s access. Therefore, naba' is also connected with disclosing a hidden matter.

Another important usage is God informing human beings about their deeds:

ثُمَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُم مَّرْجِعُكُمْ فَيُنَبِّئُكُم بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ

Then your return is to your Lord, and He will make you aware of what you used to do. Az-Zumar 39:7

Here يُنَبِّئُكُم means that God will make human beings aware of the reality of their deeds. This is not merely external reporting; it is the disclosure of the truth of what was done. In translation, “He will make you aware” can carry more weight than simply “He will tell you.”

Distinguishing Nabī and Rasūl

نَبِي comes from the root ن ب أ and is connected with naba', important news, divine awareness, and informing about a matter of weight. A nabī is one connected with divine news: he receives awareness, carries revelatory knowledge, and makes people aware of an important truth.

رَسُول comes from the root ر س ل and is connected with sending, mission, and being sent. A rasūl is one who has been sent and who delivers a message or mission. Therefore, the axis of nabī is news and awareness; the axis of rasūl is sending and mission.

Linguistically, one person may be both nabī and rasūl. The Qur'an brings the two together:

وَكَانَ رَسُولًا نَّبِيًّا

And he was a messenger, a prophet. Maryam 19:51

But this pairing does not mean the two words are identical. The same person may be nabī from the angle of receiving and expressing divine naba', and rasūl from the angle of being sent with a mission.

Rasūl is not always human. The Qur'an uses rasūl for angels as well:

جَاعِلِ الْمَلَائِكَةِ رُسُلًا

The One who makes the angels messengers. Fāṭir 35:1

This verse shows that rasūl is a title of mission, not the name of a species. An angel can be rasūl because it is sent and entrusted with delivering or executing a command. A human being can also be rasūl. Rasūl is therefore understood from the function of sending.

By contrast, the Qur'an does not call angels nabī. This matters for the distinction: nabī in the Qur'an is known through receiving divine news in a human and revelatory station, while rasūl can be used for angels and human beings.

Regarding Jibrīl, the Qur'an says:

إِنَّهُ لَقَوْلُ رَسُولٍ كَرِيمٍ

Indeed, it is the speech of a noble messenger. At-Takwīr 81:19

Here رَسُولٍ كَرِيمٍ appears in the context of revelation’s descent. This expression shows that a rasūl can carry revelation. But the identity of Jibrīl, Rūḥ al-Qudus, and Rūḥ al-Amīn needs its own article. For this entry, the linguistic point is enough: rasūl means a sent agent; nabī means one connected with naba' and divine awareness.

Istinbā' and Seeking News

The form اسْتَنبَأَ, meaning to seek news or ask for information, appears once in the Qur'an:

وَيَسْتَنبِئُونَكَ أَحَقٌّ هُوَ

And they seek news from you: is it true? Yūnus 10:53

Here يَسْتَنبِئُونَكَ means that they ask you for consequential news, or ask about the truth of the matter. This form shows that the root can also move toward seeking news and awareness.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In أُنَبِّئُكُم بِخَيْرٍ, the preposition بـ after the verb introduces the content of the news: to make you aware of something.

In يُنَبِّئُكُم بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ, ما marks the content of the awareness: what you used to do.

In نَبَأَهُم بِالْحَقِّ, the بـ in بِالْحَقِّ shows that the report is joined to truth: it is an account based on reality, not legend or conjecture.

In يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ النَّبَإِ الْعَظِيمِ, the preposition عن marks the direction of questioning: the question is about the great news.

In رَسُولًا نَبِيًّا, two descriptions appear together. This structure matters for semantic distinction: being rasūl and being nabī are two different aspects gathered in one person.

Difference From Nearby Roots

ن ب أ is close to خ ب ر, but they are not identical. Khabar can be any report or information. Naba' is heavier and more consequential. Every naba' is khabar, but not every khabar is naba'.

ن ب أ differs from ر س ل. Naba' is centered on awareness and important news; R S L is centered on sending and mission. Nabī is tied to divine news; rasūl is tied to carrying and delivering a message.

ن ب أ also differs from و ح ي. Waḥy is the mode or process of delivering divine message; naba' is the content of important news. A naba' may come through waḥy, but waḥy and naba' are not the same word.

ن ب أ differs from ق ص ص. Qaṣaṣ is following and recounting events, usually with sequence and tracking. Naba' is important news, whether it comes as a narrative or a short report.

ن ب أ is also not identical to ع ل م. ʿIlm is knowing and knowledge; naba' is news or awareness that is delivered. Naba' can cause knowledge, but it is not knowledge itself.

ن ب أ differs from ب ش ر in the sense of good news. Bushrā is glad tidings and hopeful news; naba' may be good, warning-bearing, heavy, or judgment-bearing.

ن ب أ is also separate from ن ب ط. استنبط means to draw out or extract a conclusion from data; استنبأ means to seek news or ask for information. Sound similarity should not cause these roots to be merged.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For نَبَأ: “important news,” “great news,” “weighty report,” or, depending on context, “significant awareness.”

For النَّبَإِ الْعَظِيمِ: “the great news” or “the tremendous news.”

For أَنبَاءِ الْغَيْبِ: “reports of the unseen” or “unseen reports.”

For نَبَأَهُم بِالْحَقِّ: “We recount their news with truth” or “We report their account with truth.”

For أُنَبِّئُكُم and يُنَبِّئُكُم: “I make you aware,” “I inform you,” “He makes you aware,” or “He informs you.” In many contexts, “make aware” carries the force more clearly than a flat “tell.”

For نَبِي: the traditional “prophet” is common, but Forqan’s explanation should note that, by root, nabī is tied to naba' and divine news. If an explanatory rendering is needed, “bearer of divine news” or “one made aware by divine news” can show the root layer, though in smooth translation “prophet” or “nabī” will usually remain.

For نُبُوَّة: “prophethood,” with the explanation that nubuwwah is the station or condition of relation to divine news and revelatory awareness.

For رَسُول: “messenger,” “sent one,” or “agent of delivery,” depending on context. In explanation, note that rasūl is a title of mission, not necessarily the name of a species.

For يَسْتَنبِئُونَكَ: “they seek news from you,” or “they ask you whether it is true.”

Linguistic Summary

The root ن ب أ indicates important news and consequential awareness. Naba' is not ordinary news. It is news that reveals an important truth and places the listener before knowledge that has effect.

In the Qur'an, this root is used for the great news, reports of the unseen, accounts of peoples, awareness of deeds, and the station of prophethood. Nabī comes from this root and is tied to naba' and divine awareness.

Rasūl comes from another root and is connected with being sent and entrusted with delivering a message. One person may be both nabī and rasūl, but the two titles come from different linguistic axes: nabī from the axis of divine news, and rasūl from the axis of sending and mission.
