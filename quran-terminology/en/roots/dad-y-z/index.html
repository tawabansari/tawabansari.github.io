Diminishing another's right, unjust judgment, and unfair division

In early Arabic, ض ي ز concerns injustice in judgment, diminishing another person's entitlement, and withholding what they deserve. In social dealings and judgments, ضَازَ describes departing from fairness and reducing or denying another's right.

ضَيْز denotes this wrongful reduction or injustice, while ضِيزَى describes an unjust division.

For example, if several people have shares in property and the distributor wrongfully gives one person less than their entitlement, that distribution can be called قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَى.

The word does not merely describe unequal quantities. Two people can have different shares on legitimate grounds and the division remain fair. What matters is departure from right and fairness in judgment or allocation.

The root occurs only once in the Quran, as ضِيزَىٰ in An-Najm 53:22. In قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَىٰ, it describes a division condemned as unjust.

Understanding it requires distinguishing division itself, its unfairness, and the particular subject under discussion in the Quran.

1. The root in early Arabic

Early dictionaries record three closely related uses of ضَازَ: judging unjustly, diminishing someone's right, and withholding their entitlement.

These uses concern more than a reduction in quantity. The significant point is deprivation of a right or share that should be received.

For example, the root can describe someone responsible for distributing spoils who wrongfully reduces another participant's share.

Conversely, differing shares are not in themselves ḍayz when property is divided according to rights and legitimate agreement.

A poetic witness concerning diminished rights

Early dictionaries transmit the following verse:

إِذَا ضَازَ عَنَّا حَقَّنَا فِي غَنِيمَةٍ
تَقَنَّعَ جَارَانَا فَلَمْ يَتَرَمْرَمَا

idhā ḍāza ʿannā ḥaqqanā fī ghanīmatin / taqannaʿa jārānā fa-lam yataramramā

Translation: “If he withholds our rightful share of spoils, our two neighbours cover their heads and utter not a word.”

The important expression here is ضَازَ عَنَّا حَقَّنَا.

حَقَّنَا names what the speaker regards as their right; ضَازَ describes wrongfully diminishing or withholding it.

The verse concerns spoils, but spoils are not part of the root's meaning. The verb can describe violations of rights in other matters too.

Here, ضَازَ does not name the act of distribution. It describes wrongful conduct occurring during distribution.

Early lexical sources transmit this line through Abū Zayd, but its poet and date have not been securely established in this study. It cannot therefore be presented as a definitively pre-Islamic poetic witness.

Its preservation in the early lexical tradition nevertheless shows that the connection between ضَازَ and diminishing someone's right was recognized.

A witness attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays

Some literary and exegetical sources attribute the following line to the pre-Islamic poet Imruʾ al-Qays:

ضَازَتْ بَنُو أَسَدٍ بِحُكْمِهِمْ
إِذْ يَجْعَلُونَ الرَّأْسَ كَالذَّنَبِ

ḍāzat banū asadin bi-ḥukmihim / idh yajʿalūna al-raʾsa ka-l-dhanabi

Translation: “Banū Asad were unjust in their judgment, making the head like the tail.”

The image sets “head” and “tail” against each other. The poet criticizes a judgment that improperly equates things he considers different in standing.

This usage indicates that ḍayz is not restricted to reducing a numerical share. It can also describe a judgment that disregards what the speaker considers proper proportion or standing.

The attribution requires caution. The wording varies across transmissions, and this study has not independently authenticated the line through a critical edition of Imruʾ al-Qays' collected poetry.

It should therefore not be presented without qualification as his securely established wording.

Its evidential value is that the lexical and literary tradition uses ضَازَ for wrongful judgment and departure from fairness.

What the historical witnesses establish

Taken together, the recorded uses connect this family with three ideas:

First, reducing someone's rightful share or entitlement.

Second, acting unjustly or departing from fairness in judgment.

Third, the wrongfulness of a division or judgment that fails to respect rights and proper proportion.

The evidence does not establish which use came first historically.

Nor does the connection with deviation in judgment prove that physical bending was the original source of every meaning in the family.

The social use concerning rights is clear; any proposed original physical image needs further historical evidence.

2. Forms and derivatives

ض ي ز is a root with a weak middle radical. In ضَازَ يَضِيزُ, the underlying yāʾ undergoes the expected changes in weak verbs. The wāw form ضَازَ يَضُوزُ is separately recorded and should not be treated merely as an inflection of the yāʾ form.

Arabic sources record the following forms:

ضَازَ: he judged unjustly or departed from fairness.

يَضِيزُ: he acts unjustly, diminishes an entitlement, or disregards someone's right.

ضَيْز: wrongful diminution, injustice, or departure from fairness.

ضِزْتُ: I diminished someone's right or treated them unjustly.

أَضِيزُ: I diminish someone's right or act unjustly toward them.

ضِيزَى: a feminine description of an unjust division or allocation.

Related forms are also recorded:

ضَازَ يَضُوزُ: a variant verbal paradigm used for diminution and injustice.

ضُوزَى: another form describing an unjust division.

ضَأَزَ: to diminish someone's right or act unjustly, with hamzah in the word's structure.

ضِئْزَى and ضُؤْزَى: recorded hamzated forms describing an unjust division.

These forms are close in meaning, but they should not all be declared certain derivatives of a single original form without historical investigation.

Some sources discuss ض ي ز, ض و ز, and ض أ ز together. This grouping indicates a recognized linguistic relationship among forms containing yāʾ, wāw, and hamzah.

The existence of related forms does not by itself establish which was original or which developed from another.

For this study, ضِيزَىٰ is the base form because it occurs in the Quranic reading used by Forqan; another recognized reading is discussed below.

The meaning of ضِيزَى

In the Quranic phrase, ضِيزَى modifies قِسْمَة.

قِسْمَة is feminine, and ضِيزَى occupies the corresponding descriptive position.

Early grammarians debated the word's morphological analysis.

One analysis assigns it an underlying فُعْلَى pattern, explaining the initial kasrah as a change that preserves yāʾ.

In simpler terms, the surface form ضِيزَى begins with an i-vowel, but this analysis relates its structure to the فُعْلَى pattern found in feminine adjectives.

The reasoning reflects the rarity and disputed status of initial-kasrah فِعْلَى as an adjectival pattern in Arabic.

Another analysis treats it as a verbal noun, like ذِكْرَى, used descriptively. This has been discussed especially for the hamzated reading, and also for the form without hamzah.

The morphological disagreement matters, but it does not change the passage's basic meaning.

Across these analyses, قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَى describes an unjust division.

A rare morphological pattern does not make the word mean “strange,” “astonishing,” or “unusual division.”

Injustice is the word's meaning; morphological rarity is a property of its linguistic form.

3. Variation among Quranic readings

In the reading of Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim, the word in An-Najm 53:22 is read:

ضِيزَىٰ

ḍīzā

Al-Najm 53:22

That is, with yāʾ and without hamzah.

The reading of Ibn Kathīr al-Makkī has the hamzated form:

ضِئْزَىٰ

ḍiʾzā

Here, a quiescent hamzah follows ḍād.

Works on Quranic readings record this as an established variation in reading the same word.

Both forms denote an unjust division.

For root analysis, the difference matters: one is read with yāʾ and the other with hamzah.

Arabic dictionaries also record forms such as ضُوزَى and ضُؤْزَى. A dictionary's inclusion of a form does not automatically make it an established Quranic reading.

Three matters must be kept distinct: recorded Arabic forms, recognized reading variants, and the Quranic text used by Forqan.

This study takes ضِيزَىٰ as its base form. Other forms help explain the linguistic range and morphological discussion.

4. Quranic usage: an unjust division

An-Najm 53:22

تِلْكَ إِذًا قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَىٰ

tilka idhan qismatun ḍīzā

Al-Najm 53:22

Translation: “That, then, is an unjust division.”

This is the only Quranic occurrence assigned to ض ي ز in the reading used here.

The verse is brief, but the preceding verses identify the division being discussed.

An-Najm 53:19 and An-Najm 53:20 name al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, and Manāt, deities known among the polytheists addressed.

An-Najm 53:21 then challenges them: is the male for themselves, while they assign the female to God?

Following that question, An-Najm 53:22 states:

تِلْكَ إِذًا قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَىٰ

tilka idhan qismatun ḍīzā

Al-Najm 53:22

In other words, on their proposed assignment, this is a wrongful and unjust division.

Sentence structure

تِلْكَ is a feminine demonstrative referring to the division or assignment raised in the preceding verse.

إِذًا connects the conclusion to the preceding statement: “then” or “in that case.”

قِسْمَةٌ means division or allocation.

ضِيزَىٰ describes the division as unjust.

The sentence can thus be explained as follows:

The allocation or assignment you propose is unjust.

ضِيزَى does not itself mean dividing; that comes from قِسْمَة.

ضِيزَى characterizes the quality of the division.

This distinction matters: dividing can be fair or unfair. Adding ضِيزَى identifies the division as unjust.

5. Why is this division called unjust?

The verse is not about actually distributing property among several people.

It concerns claims the polytheists made about God and their deities.

In the social and religious setting of the original audience, some preferred sons for themselves while reacting to daughters with displeasure or shame.

An-Naḥl 16:58 and An-Naḥl 16:59 criticize that reaction to the news of a daughter's birth.

Az-Zukhruf 43:16 and Az-Zukhruf 43:17 likewise place the attribution of daughters to God beside the speakers' own negative response to having a daughter.

Against that background, the question in An-Najm 53:21 exposes a contradiction:

They assign to themselves what their own standards regard as preferable, while assigning to God what they themselves value less.

قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَىٰ exposes that inconsistency and wrongfulness.

Does the verse affirm that boys are inherently superior to girls?

No such conclusion follows from the meaning of ضِيزَى.

The verse does not endorse their evaluation of male and female. It uses their discriminatory standard to reveal the contradiction in their claims.

For example, suppose someone consistently considers one thing more valuable for themselves, but assigns to another person what they themselves regard as inferior.

Their inconsistent treatment is visible even before their original ranking is assessed.

Criticizing that treatment does not endorse the underlying ranking.

Similarly, calling this division unjust does not mean that God declares males inherently more valuable than females.

The issue is inconsistency and wrongfulness in the claims made about God.

An-Najm 53:45 also speaks of God creating male and female. Across the Quran, human worth is not reduced to sex.

Criticism of a polytheistic attribution should therefore not be turned into a rule of inherent superiority for either sex.

Does the Quran accept dividing offspring between God and humans?

No.

The notion of division here is part of a critical argument against the polytheists' claim.

An-Najm 53:23 explains that the deities in question are names that they and their ancestors have assigned, for which God has sent down no authority.

An-Najm 53:27 and An-Najm 53:28 also address their feminine designation of angels and their lack of knowledge concerning it.

The issue is therefore not simply a mistaken division that could be repaired by changing God's allotted share.

The attribution of offspring or family status to God is itself unsupported.

In context, قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَىٰ exposes their inconsistency in their own terms; it does not recognize a valid division of offspring between God and humans.

This prevents reducing the passage to a merely numerical or financial discussion.

6. Comparison with related roots

ض ي ز (ḍīzā) and ق س م (qismah)

ق س م concerns dividing and separating shares.

Its Quranic forms concern division, portions, and oaths.

قِسْمَة is not inherently just or unjust. It names the act or result of dividing.

ضِيزَى identifies the division as unfair.

For example, property distributed among heirs according to their established entitlements may produce different shares. Difference in quantity alone does not make the division unjust.

But knowingly assigning someone less than their entitlement makes the distribution wrongful.

In An-Najm 53:22, قِسْمَة is what is described, and ضِيزَى describes it.

ض ي ز (ḍīzā) and ب خ س (bakhs)

بَخْس concerns diminishing another's right, value, or share.

Hūd 11:85 forbids depriving people of what is due to them in their possessions and affairs.

Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:183 likewise prohibits diminishing what belongs to people.

This is close to the early Arabic expression ضَازَهُ حَقَّهُ.

In both, someone is deprived of a right or receives less than their entitlement.

However, not every use of ض ي ز can be reduced to lowering a quantity.

In قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَى, the family can describe the wrongfulness of an allocation or judgment even without a specified numerical share.

Bakhs and ḍayz therefore overlap in violations of rights, but they are not interchangeable in every construction.

ض ي ز (ḍīzā) and ظ ل م (ẓulm)

ظُلْم has a broad Quranic range involving injustice, violation of rights, and wrongful conduct.

The social uses of ḍayz also concern injustice.

But ضِيزَى in the verse under study specifically describes a division.

Ẓulm can concern many actions, from denying others their rights to wronging oneself.

The Quranic occurrence of ض ي ز, by contrast, evaluates a particular allocation or attribution.

“An unjust division” is therefore an appropriate explanation of قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَى, but the entire semantic field of ẓulm should not be imported into this root's definition.

ض ي ز (ḍīzā) and ج و ر (jawr)

جَوْر in Arabic concerns departure from justice and acting wrongfully.

Dictionaries explain ضَازَ فِي الْحُكْمِ through جَارَ: he departed from fairness in judgment.

The two families are very close in this use.

That proximity does not establish that ض ي ز derives from ج و ر, or the reverse.

“Turning aside from justice” may illustrate both, but it does not prove a shared original physical image.

For An-Najm 53:22, the Persian equivalent meaning “an unjust division” is natural and accessible; the more Arabic-derived expression for “a wrongful division” is also defensible.

ض ي ز (ḍīzā) and ع د ل (ʿadl)

عَدْل concerns fairness and respect for rights.

An-Nisāʾ 4:58 commands judging justly between people.

In its Quranic use, ضِيزَى describes a division that departs from such fairness.

Justice does not always mean numerically equal shares.

A just allocation considers the parties' rights and relevant circumstances. Sometimes equal shares are fair; sometimes differing shares reflect established entitlements.

ضِيزَى should therefore not be translated simply as “unequal.”

A quantitative difference is wrongful when it violates rights and fairness.

ض ي ز (ḍīzā), ض أ ز (ḍaʾaza), and ض و ز (ḍāza)

Arabic sources record related hamzated and wāw forms with similar meanings.

ضَأَزَ is used for injustice and diminishing a right.

ضَازَ يَضُوزُ also carries this meaning in recorded uses.

Descriptions such as ضِئْزَى, ضُؤْزَى, and ضُوزَى are reported alongside them.

Their differences primarily concern structure and vocalization, rather than three independent kinds of injustice or division.

Historical investigation must nevertheless leave open questions about root classification and the development of these forms.

Here, ضِيزَىٰ remains the base form; other uses are considered insofar as they clarify it.

7. Broader relevance in human life

Although ض ي ز occurs only once in the Quran, unjust allocation is a broader issue in human relationships.

Fair distribution of property, resources, opportunities, and responsibilities requires regard for relevant rights and circumstances.

For example, participants in a shared undertaking may be treated unfairly when someone uses power or influence to disregard their shares.

Fairness is also at stake when public resources are allocated through kinship, group affiliation, or personal advantage instead of legitimate criteria.

In such examples, ḍayz can help explain wrongful judgment and allocation.

These are contemporary applications of the concept. The Quranic word itself does not directly discuss public budgets, administration, or economic systems.

The important social point is that unfair judgment does not always mean giving a smaller numerical quantity; sometimes the criterion of allocation itself is wrong.

For example, formally equal treatment of two groups may produce an unfair result when their relevant rights and responsibilities differ.

Conversely, differing allocations can be fair in light of people's actual circumstances and entitlements.

The governing consideration is respect for rights and fairness, not simply whether shares match.

This is compatible with قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَى, but it must not replace the verse's actual subject.

An-Najm 53:22 addresses the inconsistency and wrongfulness of the polytheists' attribution to God, rather than property distribution.

8. Translation guidance for Forqan

For the Quranic ضِيزَى, equivalents include “unjust,” “unfair,” “wrongful,” and “iniquitous.”

“Unjust” is clear and natural, conveying a division or judgment that departs from fairness.

“Wrongful” also works, encompassing the impropriety of the attribution as well as its unfairness.

“Iniquitous” conveys the condemnation but is less familiar to general readers than “unjust.”

“Crooked” may suggest deviation, but in a base translation it can obscure the ethical meaning of the division.

The existing Persian Forqan translation of An-Najm 53:22 preserves the following meaning:

تِلْكَ إِذًا قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَىٰ

tilka idhan qismatun ḍīzā

Al-Najm 53:22

“That, then, is an unjust division.”

This rendering retains the word's basic meaning and does not require a change in meaning.

Suggested word-box wording can distinguish two levels:

ضِيزَىٰ

ḍīzā

Al-Najm 53:22

Brief root meaning: “Diminishing a right and departing from fairness in judgment or allocation.”

Meaning in this verse: “Unjust and wrongful; describing the polytheists' allocation concerning God.”

The brief root meaning identifies rights and fairness; the contextual meaning explains this particular description in the sentence.

ضِيزَى should not be rendered merely “unequal,” since inequality and injustice are not always identical.

Nor does the word itself mean “dividing daughters and sons.” Male and female come from the preceding verse, not from ض ي ز.

For other Arabic forms, ضَازَ حَقَّهُ can be rendered “he diminished his entitlement” or “he violated his right.”

ضَازَ فِي الْحُكْمِ means “he judged unjustly” or “he departed from fairness.”

Equivalents should be selected for their context; one translation cannot capture every construction's distinctions.

9. Summary

In early Arabic, ض ي ز concerns diminishing rights, injustice in judgment, and unfair division.

Lexical and literary witnesses apply the family to shares of spoils and social judgment. Some poetic attributions remain uncertain, however, and do not establish the root's precise date of origin.

Arabic sources record related yāʾ, wāw, and hamzah forms. The morphology and Quranic readings of ضِيزَى have been debated, without changing the central meaning.

In the Quranic reading used here, ض ي ز occurs only as ضِيزَىٰ in An-Najm 53:22.

The verse calls the polytheists' allocation unjust: they claim for themselves what they value more and assign to God what they themselves value less.

This argument does not endorse inherent male superiority. It exposes their inconsistency, and the following verses also reject the unsupported attribution itself.

Lexically, ضِيزَى does not describe every unequal division. It applies when division or judgment departs from rights and fairness.

The final definition is:

ض ي ز: diminishing another's right, withholding their rightful share, or departing from fairness in judgment or allocation, producing a wrongful and unjust result.

In its Quranic use, قِسْمَةٌ ضِيزَىٰ describes an allocation incompatible with right and fairness.

The expression distinguishes dividing from dividing justly. Distribution alone does not ensure justice; justice depends on sound criteria, respect for rights, and the absence of wrongful treatment in the outcome.
