1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root م ن ن in the Qur'an has several connected but distinct branches: granting a marked favor, reminding someone of a favor and holding it over them, releasing or giving freely, the food called mann, cutting off or coming to an end, and manun as death or the wearing events of time.

In positive use, manna ʿalayhi means that a great favor was given to someone, an act of grace reached them, or a situation was opened for them that they did not possess by themselves. The Qur'an presents the raising of a messenger, guidance to faith, rescue from punishment, returning Musa to his mother, and raising the oppressed as examples of this divine mann.

In negative human use, a person holds their gift or deed over the receiver and turns it into a means of superiority, debt, or humiliation. The Qur'an joins this behavior with adha, harm, and says that charity is ruined by mann and harm.

In another branch, mannan baʿd refers to releasing captives without taking compensation. In ghayru mamnun, the word points to something that is not cut off, diminished, or ended. Manun in Arabic is connected with death or the wearing events of time, because death or time cuts down strength, lifespan, and the line of worldly life.

So this root cannot be defined only as “giving a favor” or only as “putting someone under obligation.” Context determines whether the discussion is about grace, hurtful reminding, release, food, or cutting off and ending.

Short meaning:

Granting a marked favor or act of grace; holding that favor over someone; releasing without compensation; and in another branch, cutting off, diminishing, or ending.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

The root م ن ن occurs twenty-seven times in the Qur'an in four morphological forms: sixteen times as the simple verb مَنَّ, six times as the noun مَنّ, once as the noun مَنون, and four times as the adjective or passive participle مَمْنون.

مَنَّ / يَمُنُّ

The simple verb. In the Qur'an it has three main uses.

First, showing grace and giving a marked favor:

لَقَدْ مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ

The God has certainly shown a marked favor to the believers.

Second, holding a deed or favor over someone:

يَمُنُّونَ عَلَيْكَ أَنْ أَسْلَمُوا

They hold their submission over you as a favor.

Third, giving or releasing:

فَامْنُنْ أَوْ أَمْسِكْ

So give freely, or hold back.

The exact meaning of the verb is determined by its object, preposition, and context.

مَنّ

The noun mann appears in three fields in the Qur'an: the food sent down with salwa for the Children of Israel; hurtful reminding of charity and favor; and releasing a captive without taking ransom.

These three uses should not be forced into one English rendering.

مَنون

This form occurs only once in the Qur'an:

رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ

In Arabic, manun may refer to death, death-bringing fate, or the wearing events of time, because death or time diminishes and cuts off the continuity of life. Classical sources report both “death” and “time” as possibilities.

مَمْنون

This form occurs four times in the phrase:

أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ

Mamnun in this structure can mean cut off, ended, or diminished. Some sources also mention the possibility of “held over someone” or “reckoned,” but the phrase with ghayr and the branch of cutting strengthen the meaning “unbroken and unending” in these verses.

Non-Qur'anic Forms

Forms such as mannan are known in Arabic, but they do not occur in the Qur'anic text. Therefore they should not be placed in the Qur'anic derivative cards or treated as a Qur'anic divine attribute in this article.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The uses of this root cannot be reduced carelessly to one fully unified definition. Lexical sources record for م ن ن favor and blessing, holding a favor over someone, cutting, diminishing, exhaustion and weakness, death and time, and the food called mann.

In the branch of favor, the core is:

Bringing a significant good or possibility to another, so that their situation is changed through it.

Divine mann in the Qur'an is usually not a small ordinary gift. The raising of a messenger, guidance to faith, rescue from punishment, preservation of Musa, rescue of Yusuf and his brother, and the will to raise the oppressed are expressed with this verb.

But the same gift, when used by a human being for display and superiority, becomes negative. The giver counts the favor against the receiver, repeats it, and expects submission, praise, or repayment.

Thus the negative meaning of mann is not imposed from outside the root. The relation between giver and receiver can take two forms: a gift given for the good of the other; or a gift that, after being given, becomes a tool of control and harm.

In the other branch, the root connects with cutting and diminishing. Habl manin is used for a rope that is worn or cut, and manun is used for death or wearing time. From this field, ghayru mamnun can mean a reward that is not cut off or diminished.

One should not confidently claim that all these branches arose historically from a single proven image. The proposed links between great favor and “making the scale heavy,” or between gift and the unit of weight mann, should not be made the certain core of the root without clear evidence.

5. The Material Image of the Root

For the branch of cutting and diminishing, the material image is clear: a rope weakened by use and wear until it finally breaks. Lexical sources record habl manin for a weak, worn, or cut rope. The verb is also used for weakening or exhausting a human or animal during travel.

This image helps us understand manun and ghayru mamnun: manun is what wears down and cuts off life and strength; ghayru mamnun is what is not broken, severed, or diminished.

For the branch of favor, the better image is a person placing a good or possibility in the hands of someone who did not have it. The receiver leaves a prior condition because of that gift. But this result should not be turned into expressions such as “injecting energy” or “saving a system from collapse.”

In negative use, the image changes: after giving, the giver keeps a thread of claim and pressure between themselves and the receiver. Each reminder keeps the receiver from becoming free of the psychological and social burden of the gift.

This is an ethical and relational image, not a new lexical definition. The Qur'an itself names the result of such behavior as harm and the invalidation of charity.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, manna ʿalayhi is used both for showing grace and giving blessing, and for holding that grace over someone. Lexical sources clearly distinguish the two uses.

In the first use, a person brings good and favor to another without turning it into boast or humiliation.

In the second use, a person magnifies their favor, repeatedly reminds the receiver of it, and blames or burdens them because of it.

Persian preserves part of this duality. Classical “منّت نهادن” can mean showing favor, while modern “منت گذاشتن” is usually negative.

In another branch, mannahu is connected with cutting, diminishing, or weakening. Manun is used for death or time because it diminishes living things and finally cuts them off. Mamnun can also describe something cut or diminished.

Mann as food was also known in Arabic for a sweet substance or something available without normal planting and labor. Some lexicographers likened it to honey or taranjabin. The Qur'an, however, does not explain its botanical or chemical nature.

For a Qur'anic article, the cautious statement is enough: al-mann was a known provision or food made available with salwa for the Children of Israel. Identifying it with taranjabin, resin, mushroom, or a specific substance cannot be established from the Qur'an itself.

7. History of Understanding and Restricting the Meaning

One error is to present the negative meaning of “putting someone under obligation” as a later corruption and to claim that the root originally meant only support and distribution of resources. This does not fit the Qur'an itself.

لَا تُبْطِلُوا صَدَقَاتِكُمْ بِالْمَنِّ وَالْأَذَىٰ

Do not invalidate your charities by mann and harm.

The Qur'an also says:

يَمُنُّونَ عَلَيْكَ أَنْ أَسْلَمُوا

They hold their submission over you as a favor.

So blameworthy favor-reminding is not an invention of later culture. It is an explicit Qur'anic use.

Another error is to understand divine mann and human mann in the same way. When The God reminds people of His favor, He is not seeking praise, repayment, or psychological control over servants. The reminder of divine favor discloses truth, responsibility, and the right relation of the human being to blessing.

By contrast, a human being may remind someone of a favor in order to humiliate them or demand advantage. This difference is clear in Al-Hujurat 49:17: they hold their submission over the messenger, while guidance to faith, if they are truthful, is favor from The God upon them.

Another narrowing is translating every form of the root as “blessing.” That will fail in rayba al-manun, ghayru mamnun, and fa-imma mannan baʿd.

Likewise, al-mann, the food of the Children of Israel, should not be translated as a “strategic rescue package.” That provision may have preserved them, but the word in the verse is simply mann, and its exact nature requires caution.

8. Methodological Note

Several levels must remain distinct when studying this root.

Direct meaning in the branch of favor: showing grace and giving a marked blessing.

Negative human use: holding a favor over someone and turning it into a tool of claim or harm.

The branch of cutting and diminishment: cutting, wearing away, diminishing, and ending.

Contextual result: rescue, guidance, empowerment, responsibility, or the continuity of reward.

The result of a gift should not be placed inside the lexical meaning of the verb. For example, in Al-Qasas 28:5, divine mann leads to raising the oppressed, making them leaders, and making them inheritors. But “making leaders” or “changing the balance of power” is not the direct meaning of namunna; those are the continuation and result stated in the verse.

Nor should one build a theory of exact mathematical weighing of every gift from the weight unit mann or from uses connected with strength and weakness. A homonymous or related lexical field alone does not prove such a theory.

In ghayru mamnun, several lexical possibilities should be distinguished: unending, unbroken, undiminished, unreckoned, or not held over someone. The context of lasting reward and the root's cutting branch strengthen “unbroken and unending,” while other possibilities may be mentioned as lexical notes.

In wa-la tamnun tastakthir, the brief structure allows more than one reading. It may forbid giving in order to receive more, magnifying one's deed, or holding it over others. The base translation should remain concise, with the possibilities explained separately.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

Divine mann through raising a messenger

The Qur'an says:

لَقَدْ مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِذْ بَعَثَ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًا مِنْ أَنْفُسِهِمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِهِ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ

The God has certainly shown a marked favor to the believers when He raised among them a messenger from themselves, reciting His signs to them, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom.

Ali 'Imran 3:164

The divine mann in this verse is the raising of the messenger itself. The rest of the verse explains why that raising is a great favor: recitation of the signs, purification, and teaching the Book and wisdom.

The verb should not be translated with technological expressions. Such language neither preserves the lexical meaning nor the dignity of natural English prose.

Divine mann through guidance to faith

يَمُنُّونَ عَلَيْكَ أَنْ أَسْلَمُوا قُلْ لَا تَمُنُّوا عَلَيَّ إِسْلَامَكُمْ بَلِ اللَّهُ يَمُنُّ عَلَيْكُمْ أَنْ هَدَاكُمْ لِلْإِيمَانِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ صَادِقِينَ

They hold their submission over you as a favor. Say: Do not hold your submission over me as a favor. Rather, The God shows favor to you by guiding you to faith, if you are truthful.

Al-Hujurat 49:17

This verse shows the difference between human and divine mann in one structure. They treat what should benefit themselves as a credit against the messenger. The Qur'an reverses the relation: guidance, if truthful, is favor from The God upon them.

The verse also leaves a meaningful distance between entering Islam and being guided to faith, but this root article should not replace the analysis of م ن ن with a full discussion of Islam and faith.

Divine mann through bringing out of a former condition

كَذَٰلِكَ كُنْتُمْ مِنْ قَبْلُ فَمَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْكُمْ فَتَبَيَّنُوا

You were like that before, then The God showed favor to you, so verify carefully.

An-Nisa 4:94

The favor here concerns a change in the addressees' condition. They should not forget their own past and judge others hastily.

Favor upon those whom others look down upon

أَهَٰؤُلَاءِ مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنْ بَيْنِنَا

Are these the ones upon whom The God has shown favor from among us?

Al-An'am 6:53

Their objection shows that they measure divine favor by their own social rankings. The verse criticizes the arrogance of those who want the gift from The God to follow their hierarchy.

The God favoring Yusuf and his brother

أَنَا يُوسُفُ وَهَٰذَا أَخِي قَدْ مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْنَا

I am Yusuf, and this is my brother. The God has certainly shown favor to us.

Yusuf 12:90

The context includes rescue from the well, slavery, prison, separation, the disclosure of truth, and reunion with his brother. The word is not a “substance” or “energy source,” but divine grace and governance through a long path.

Divine favor in choosing servants for message

وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يَمُنُّ عَلَىٰ مَنْ يَشَاءُ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ

But The God shows favor to whomever He wills from His servants.

Ibrahim 14:11

The messengers do not deny being human, but they understand messengerhood as a gift from The God. The verb names a divine grant, not an engineered allocation.

The God favoring Musa in childhood

وَلَقَدْ مَنَنَّا عَلَيْكَ مَرَّةً أُخْرَىٰ

And We certainly showed favor to you another time.

Ta-Ha 20:37

The following verses explain the favor: inspiration to Musa's mother, placing him in the chest, the chest reaching the shore, love being cast upon him, and his return to his mother. The verb itself does not carry all these details; the later verses unfold its instance.

Pharaoh's favor-claim over Musa

وَتِلْكَ نِعْمَةٌ تَمُنُّهَا عَلَيَّ أَنْ عَبَّدْتَ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ

And is that a blessing you hold over me, while you enslaved the Children of Israel?

Ash-Shuara 26:22

Pharaoh counts Musa's upbringing in the palace as a favor that should keep him indebted and silent. Musa places that claim against Pharaoh's wider oppression.

This is one of the clearest examples of human, dominating mann: a limited benefit is held over someone to cover a greater injustice.

The God favoring the oppressed

وَنُرِيدُ أَنْ نَمُنَّ عَلَى الَّذِينَ اسْتُضْعِفُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ وَنَجْعَلَهُمْ أَئِمَّةً وَنَجْعَلَهُمُ الْوَارِثِينَ

And We willed to show favor to those who had been made weak in the earth, and to make them leaders, and to make them inheritors.

Al-Qasas 28:5

Namunna is the main verb, while making them leaders and inheritors are the following results. Translating the verb itself as “change the balance of power” imports the rest of the verse into the verb.

Divine favor in rescue from Qarun's fate

لَوْلَا أَنْ مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْنَا لَخَسَفَ بِنَا

Had The God not shown favor to us, He would have caused the earth to swallow us too.

Al-Qasas 28:82

Favor here is rescue from the outcome and from the delusion that had attracted them. Sometimes mann is not the addition of wealth, but the prevention of harm and awakening from a false desire.

The God favoring Musa and Harun

وَلَقَدْ مَنَنَّا عَلَىٰ مُوسَىٰ وَهَارُونَ وَنَجَّيْنَاهُمَا وَقَوْمَهُمَا مِنَ الْكَرْبِ الْعَظِيمِ

And We certainly showed favor to Musa and Harun, and We rescued them and their people from the great distress.

As-Saffat 37:114

Rescue from great distress, support, and victory in the following verses are manifestations of this favor.

Favor from The God in rescue from punishment

فَمَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْنَا وَوَقَانَا عَذَابَ السَّمُومِ

So The God showed favor to us and protected us from the punishment of the scorching wind.

At-Tur 52:27

The favor is explained by protection from punishment. Divine gift is not always adding a possession; it may also be withholding harm and preserving the person.

Giving or withholding the gift

هَٰذَا عَطَاؤُنَا فَامْنُنْ أَوْ أَمْسِكْ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ

This is Our gift; so give freely, or hold back, without reckoning.

Sad 38:39

Here fa-mnun does not mean “hold it over someone.” Its contrast with amsik shows that the fitting sense is “give,” “release,” or “bestow.”

Do not hold submission over the messenger

يَمُنُّونَ عَلَيْكَ أَنْ أَسْلَمُوا قُلْ لَا تَمُنُّوا عَلَيَّ إِسْلَامَكُمْ

They hold their submission over you as a favor. Say: Do not hold your submission over me as a favor.

Al-Hujurat 49:17

They imagine their act as a favor to the messenger or community. The verse restores the real relation: accepting the path of truth, if truthful, benefits them.

وَلَا تَمْنُنْ تَسْتَكْثِرُ

وَلَا تَمْنُنْ تَسْتَكْثِرُ

And do not give or hold favor in order to seek increase.

Al-Muddaththir 74:6

The structure is brief. It may forbid giving with the aim of receiving more, or magnifying one's deed and holding it over others. A base translation should preserve the compact structure, with the possibilities explained in a note.

Mann and harm in giving

الَّذِينَ يُنْفِقُونَ أَمْوَالَهُمْ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ لَا يُتْبِعُونَ مَا أَنْفَقُوا مَنًّا وَلَا أَذًى

Those who spend their wealth in the path of The God and then do not follow what they spent with mann or harm...

Al-Baqarah 2:262

The phrase la yutbiʿun matters. The problem is not only the moment of giving; after giving, the giver may follow the gift with reminder, demand, and harm.

لَا تُبْطِلُوا صَدَقَاتِكُمْ بِالْمَنِّ وَالْأَذَىٰ

Do not invalidate your charities by mann and harm.

Al-Baqarah 2:264

In this context, mann is a verbal or practical behavior that injures the dignity of the receiver and turns charity from sincere good into display and control.

Releasing the captive without ransom

فَإِمَّا مَنًّا بَعْدُ وَإِمَّا فِدَاءً

Then either release freely afterward, or ransom.

Muhammad 47:4

The contrast with fida clarifies the meaning: after battle and restraint of captives, either release without compensation or release in exchange for ransom.

Mann and salwa

وَأَنْزَلْنَا عَلَيْكُمُ الْمَنَّ وَالسَّلْوَىٰ

And We sent down upon you the mann and the salwa.

Al-Baqarah 2:57

The same expression also appears in Al-Aʿraf 7:160 and Ta-Ha 20:80.

Al-mann here is the name of a provision or food. The Qur'an counts it among the wholesome provisions made available for the Children of Israel but does not explain its precise substance.

For Forqan, it is more cautious to keep mann and explain it briefly as “a provision or food made available for the Children of Israel.”

رَيْبُ الْمَنُون

أَمْ يَقُولُونَ شَاعِرٌ نَتَرَبَّصُ بِهِ رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ

Or do they say: A poet; we await for him the death-bringing turns of time.

At-Tur 52:30

Manun may refer to death or to time. The phrase can point to harmful turns of time, the arrival of death, or events that finally remove him. They hope that with time and the messenger's death, the call will end.

Unbroken reward

لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ

For them is a reward that is not cut off.

Fussilat 41:8

Al-Inshiqaq 84:25

At-Tin 95:6

وَإِنَّ لَكَ لَأَجْرًا غَيْرَ مَمْنُونٍ

And surely for you is a reward that is not cut off.

Al-Qalam 68:3

In this structure, ghayru mamnun points to reward that is not cut, ended, or diminished. “Unbroken” and “unending” are therefore strong translations.

10. Examination of Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

مَنَّ with ʿala

The common structure in the positive use is:

مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ

The God showed favor to him.

The preposition ʿala expresses the relation between giver and receiver. It does not by itself determine whether the verb is positive or negative. The same structure appears in human favor-claiming:

تَمُنُّهَا عَلَيَّ

مَنَّ with a verbal clause

In Al-Hujurat 49:17:

يَمُنُّونَ عَلَيْكَ أَنْ أَسْلَمُوا

The clause an aslamu states what they present as a favor: their submission.

يَمُنُّ عَلَيْكُمْ أَنْ هَدَاكُمْ لِلْإِيمَانِ

The second clause states the subject of divine favor: guidance to faith. Comparing the two structures clarifies the ethical contrast of the verse.

مَنًّا as a verbal noun

In Al-Baqarah 2:262, mannan expresses following charity with favor-reminding. In Muhammad 47:4, the same verbal noun expresses a different act: release without compensation. The form alone is not enough to determine the translation.

مَمْنون with ghayr

In all four Qur'anic occurrences, mamnun follows ghayr:

غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ

The Qur'an does not use the positive phrase ajr mamnun. The meaning is therefore understood through negation and the field of cutting and diminishing.

مَنون in construct with rayb

رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ

Rayb here concerns insecure events, turns, or occurrences. Its attachment to manun strengthens the sense of death-bringing events of time.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

Two directions of مَنَّ عَلَيْهِ

The structure manna ʿala can be used both for real favor and for a favor held over someone. Manna The God ʿalayna is positive, while tamunnuha ʿalayya in Musa's speech criticizes Pharaoh's claim. The structure is stable; the ethical direction comes from context.

لَا يُتْبِعُونَ and continuing favor-reminding

In Al-Baqarah 2:262, mann is something attached after spending:

ثُمَّ لَا يُتْبِعُونَ مَا أَنْفَقُوا مَنًّا وَلَا أَذًى

This shows that charity may be given at first, but later behavior by the giver can ruin its value.

بِالْمَنِّ وَالْأَذَى

The preposition bi in la tubtilu sadaqatikum bil-manni wal-adha expresses the means or cause of invalidation: charity is ruined by favor-reminding and harm.

فَامْنُنْ أَوْ أَمْسِكْ

The contrast between the two verbs in Sad 38:39 limits the meaning: give and release, or hold back. “Hold it over someone” is not correct here.

فَإِمَّا مَنًّا بَعْدُ وَإِمَّا فِدَاءً

The paired construction places release without compensation against release by ransom. It is one of the strongest signs for the meaning of mannan in that verse.

وَلَا تَمْنُنْ تَسْتَكْثِرُ

The second verb may describe state, result, or purpose: do not hold favor while magnifying your deed, or do not give in order to gain more. Because the structure allows more than one reading, translation should not force a long interpretation into the base text.

12. M N N (MANN) Compared with AYN T W / AYN T Y (AʿTA)

The root ع ط و / ع ط ي is connected with giving and transferring something to another. It emphasizes the act of giving itself.

Manna, in positive use, highlights a gift whose value, effect, or importance for the receiver is special. Every divine mann is a kind of giving, but not every giving is expressed with manna.

In Sad 38:39, both fields appear together:

هَٰذَا عَطَاؤُنَا فَامْنُنْ أَوْ أَمْسِكْ

ʿAta names the given gift; fa-mnun in this context is the act of giving or releasing from it.

13. M N N (MANN) Compared with W H B (WAHABA)

W H B is close to giving and granting without exchange. A hibah is often something a giver transfers without transaction or reciprocal claim.

Manna can also be a gift without compensation, but it adds the importance and effect of the gift in the receiver's condition. It can also become negative when the giver holds the gift over someone; this branch is not central to wahaba.

The difference is directional, not mechanical. Context decides each verse.

14. M N N (MANN) Compared with F D L (FADL)

F D L concerns excess, superiority, and good beyond measure or entitlement. Fadl may name the abundant good itself.

Mann is the act of bringing or reminding of a gift. Thus fadl highlights the nature of abundant good, while mann highlights the relation between giver, gift, and receiver. A blessing may be both fadl and mann, but the two words view it from different angles.

15. M N N (MANN) Compared with N AYN M (NIʿMAH)

Niʿmah is something that brings comfort, good, and improvement of condition. Mann may be the act of delivering a marked blessing, or in negative use, holding that blessing over someone.

In Ash-Shuara 26:22 both words occur together:

وَتِلْكَ نِعْمَةٌ تَمُنُّهَا عَلَيَّ

The alleged niʿmah is the benefit Pharaoh names; mann is the way he counts it against Musa and reminds him of it.

16. M N N (MANN) Compared with A DH Y (ADHA)

In Al-Baqarah, mann and adha appear together but are not identical.

Mann concerns reminding of the gift and holding it over the receiver. Adha is any speech or act that hurts and injures the receiver.

Favor-reminding can itself be a kind of harm, but the Qur'an names both so that the field of behavior that ruins charity is broader: someone may hold the gift over the receiver, or may harm them in another way.

17. M N N (MANN) Compared with Q T AYN (QATAʿA)

Q T AYN points to cutting and separating in a direct and visible way.

One branch of م ن ن is also connected with cutting, wearing down, and diminishing, but in the Qur'an this appears especially in manun and mamnun.

As a tendency, qataʿa highlights the act of cutting or separation itself; mann in this branch may include wearing away, diminishing, and finally breaking.

This comparison should never lead us to translate manna in verses of favor as “cut.” The branch is always determined by context.

18. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ

  • The God showed favor to him.
  • The God granted him a marked favor.
  • The God brought him a great blessing.

“Showed favor” preserves the positive divine use. If “mann” is retained, it needs explanation, because in modern Persian and English discussion it can sound negative.

يَمُنُّونَ عَلَيْكَ

  • They hold it over you as a favor.
  • They hold their deed over you.
  • They count it as a favor upon you.

لَا تَمُنُّوا عَلَيَّ إِسْلَامَكُمْ

Do not hold your submission over me as a favor.

تَمُنُّهَا عَلَيَّ

You hold it over me as a favor.

Or: You count it as a favor to me and throw it back at me.

فَامْنُنْ أَوْ أَمْسِكْ

So give freely, or hold back.

Or: Release it, or keep it.

مَنًّا وَلَا أَذًى

favor-reminding and harm.

In explanation: holding the gift over the receiver and hurting them.

فَإِمَّا مَنًّا بَعْدُ

Then either release freely afterward.

Or: then release from grace without compensation.

الْمَنَّ وَالسَّلْوَىٰ

the mann and the salwa.

In explanation: two kinds of provision or food made available for the Children of Israel.

رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ

  • the death-bringing turns of time
  • the events of death
  • the wearing harms of time
  • the turn of time until death arrives

أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ

  • a reward that is not cut off
  • an unending reward
  • an undiminished reward
  • a reward whose line is never severed

وَلَا تَمْنُنْ تَسْتَكْثِرُ

And do not hold favor in order to seek increase.

Another explanatory possibility: do not magnify your deed and make it a means of seeking more.

19. Conclusion

The root م ن ن in the Qur'an is layered. Its most important branch is showing grace and bringing a marked blessing, but the same root can also refer to hurtful reminding of a gift, release without compensation, the food called mann, death and wearing time, and cutting off or ending.

The proposed definition:

M N N: bringing a marked gift or act of grace; holding that gift over another; and in another branch, cutting, diminishing, or ending.

Divine mann in the Qur'an is connected with raising a messenger, guidance to faith, preserving Musa, rescuing Yusuf, freeing the oppressed, and protecting people from punishment. These gifts change the receiver's condition, but “changing structure” or “distributing resources” is not the direct meaning of the root.

Human mann can be blameworthy when a person turns their gift into a tool of claim and harm. The Qur'an explicitly criticizes this behavior in charity and in the claim of submission. Therefore the negative sense of favor-reminding is part of Qur'anic usage, not a later corruption.

In fa-imma mannan baʿd, mann means releasing a captive without compensation. In al-mann wa-al-salwa, mann is a provision or food given to the Children of Israel. In rayb al-manun, the phrase refers to death or the wearing events of time. In ajr ghayru mamnun, the promised reward is not cut off or diminished.

The root approaches ʿata in giving, wahb in free grant, fadl in abundant good, niʿmah in received blessing, and qataʿa in the branch of cutting and ending. None of these roots covers its whole field.

The article should translate each occurrence in its own context. Reducing all forms to “blessing,” “favor-reminding,” “support resource,” or “cutting” erases part of Qur'anic usage. The language of the Qur'an is clearer: The God shows grace, a human being may hold a gift over someone, a captive may be released without ransom, a food may be called mann, time may wear down the thread of life, and divine reward may never be cut off.
