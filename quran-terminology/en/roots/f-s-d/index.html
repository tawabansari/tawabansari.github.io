Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root F S D

Corruption, ruin, the breakdown of salāh, and departure from health, balance, and beneficial order

Morphological forms of this root

The root ف س د (F S D) stands opposite the field of salāh: rightness, health, sound order, benefit, and balance. Fasād is not merely an individual bad action. It is the departure of a thing, relationship, society, covenant, land, or life-system from its sound and beneficial state.

General meaning of the root F S D

The root ف س د (F S D) points to a state in which something leaves salāh, health, balance, and beneficial function. If salāh means being right, sound, ordered, and beneficial for life, then fasād means separation from that state.

So fasād is not only a small mistake or a private sin. Food can become corrupted, water can become spoiled, the body can leave health, judgment can become unsound, a covenant can be ruined, a relationship can lose trust, and a society can lose its just and beneficial order.

In the Qur’an, this root enters a wide field of moral, social, earthly, religious, and structural ruin. Fasād is when something that should serve life, justice, trust, and reform becomes a source of breakdown, disorder, oppression, destruction, and collapse.

Usage of the root in Arabic

In Arabic, الفَسَاد (al-fasād) stands opposite الصَّلَاح (al-ṣalāḥ). When one says فَسَدَ الشيءُ (fasada al-shayʾ), it means that the thing became spoiled, left its correct state, or no longer has its sound and beneficial function. When one says أَفْسَدَهُ (afsadahu), it means that someone or something corrupted it.

This root is broad in Arabic usage. فَسَدَ الطعامُ (fasada al-ṭaʿām) means the food spoiled. فَسَدَ الماءُ (fasada al-māʾ) means the water became polluted, bitter, or bad. فَسَدَ الرأيُ (fasada al-raʾy) means an opinion or judgment became unsound. فَسَدَ العهدُ (fasada al-ʿahd) means a covenant lost its soundness and force.

This shows that fasād is not only an abstract moral word. It comes from real life: food, water, land, body, contract, government, city, religion, transaction, relationship, and social order.

Examples outside the Qur’an

In ordinary Arabic usage, when people say فَسَدَ الطعامُ (fasada al-ṭaʿām), they mean that the food is no longer edible or beneficial. Its outer form may still look like food, but its quality has left soundness.

When they say فَسَدَ الماءُ (fasada al-māʾ), they mean that water has left purity and healthy usability. Corruption here is not only moral badness; it is a real change in state and function.

In legal language, one may also speak of عقد فاسد (ʿaqd fāsid), a corrupt or invalid contract, meaning a contract whose structure or condition lacks the soundness required for it to function properly. In later philosophical and theological language, the phrase كون و فساد (kawn wa fasād) was also used for generation and corruption, the coming-to-be and passing-away of forms and compositions.

Morphological and semantic forms

فَسَدَ / يَفْسُدُ (fasada / yafsudu) emphasizes the occurrence of corruption in the thing itself: something became spoiled, ruined, or left salāh. The focus here is the event or state of corruption.

أَفْسَدَ / يُفْسِدُ (afsada / yufsidu) emphasizes the agent of corruption: someone or something disrupts the sound order. This is why the command لَا تُفْسِدُوا (lā tufsidū) restrains human beings from producing ruin in the earth and society.

فَسَاد (fasād) names the state and result: corruption, ruin, and the breakdown of salāh. مُفْسِد (mufsid) is the one who produces or spreads this corruption.

Corruption, water, crops, and progeny in lived Arabic

For the Arabic world close to the Qur’an, fasād was a concrete reality. If water became corrupt, life was threatened. If crops were destroyed, livelihood collapsed. If progeny, herd, or children were harmed, the future of the tribe and society was endangered.

This is why the Qur’an does not separate fasād from the earth, crops, progeny, security, and the order of life. In 2:205, the Qur’an says: وَيُهْلِكَ الْحَرْثَ وَالنَّسْلَ وَاللَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ الْفَسَادَ. The verse connects corruption with the destruction of crop and progeny; fasād eats away the field of life and burns the future.

Usage of the root in the Qur’an

In the Qur’an, the root ف س د (F S D) is sometimes used for the order of creation itself. In 21:22, if there had been gods in the heavens and the earth other than God, لَفَسَدَتَا (lafasadatā): both would have been corrupted, and their order would have collapsed. Here fasād means the breakdown of the order of existence.

But in many verses, fasād relates to human conduct: war, oppression, bloodshed, insecurity, breaking covenants, cutting what God commanded to be joined, destroying crops and progeny, and claiming reform while producing ruin.

So the Qur’an does not see fasād only inside the private self. Corruption can appear in the earth, society, government, religion, relationships, economy, family, covenant, and human understanding. Wherever truth, justice, trust, mercy, and salāh become tools of power, deception, discrimination, destruction, or ego, the field of fasād has opened.

The difference between fasada and afsada

فَسَدَ (fasada) mostly points to the thing itself becoming spoiled or ruined. Water became corrupted, food became spoiled, or the order of the heavens and the earth would have collapsed.

أَفْسَدَ (afsada) adds another layer: someone caused corruption. This form makes responsibility clearer. When the Qur’an says لَا تُفْسِدُوا فِي الأَرْضِ (lā tufsidū fī al-arḍ), it speaks to human beings whose actions bring the earth and society out of salāh.

Therefore, translation should preserve the difference between “became corrupt” and “caused corruption.” One shows the state or event; the other shows action, responsibility, and the agent of fasād.

Fasād against salāh

To understand fasād, it must be seen against salāh. Salāh means that something is in its right place, functions soundly, serves life and truth, and is far from crookedness, destruction, and disorder.

Fasād is the opposite of this state. It is when health becomes disease, trust becomes betrayal, justice becomes oppression, the earth becomes a field of destruction, religion becomes a tool of domination, speech becomes deception, and reform becomes a cover for ruin.

This is why fasād can sometimes appear with religious, political, or reformist language. The Qur’an is not satisfied with names. It examines the result. If the result of an action is the destruction of salāh and the spread of ruin, that action is fasād, even if it is called reform.

Corruption in the earth

The expression الفَسَاد فِي الأَرْض (al-fasād fī al-arḍ) is very important in the Qur’an. The earth is not only soil. It is the field of life, trust, provision, generations, security, justice, relationships, and human responsibility.

Corruption in the earth means that this field of life leaves the state of trust and salāh. War, oppression, bloodshed, insecurity, destruction of resources, breaking human bonds, religious deception, abuse of power, and disregard for future generations can all be forms of fasād in the earth.

In the Qur’anic view, the human being is not merely a consumer of the earth. He is responsible not to turn the earth into a field of corruption. When power, wealth, speech, and religion are used to destroy life, fasād in the earth has occurred.

Corruption and breaking the covenant

The Qur’an also connects fasād with breaking the covenant. In 2:27, it speaks of those who break the covenant of God after it has been made firm, cut what God commanded to be joined, and cause corruption in the earth.

This connection is very important. Corruption is not only external destruction. Sometimes it begins by cutting the right bonds: the bond of the human being with God, with truth, with trust, with family, with society, with the poor, with covenant, and with responsibility.

When the bonds that should preserve life are cut, fasād grows. Therefore, reform is not only outward building. Preserving the bonds of truth, justice, trust, and mercy is also part of iṣlāḥ.

Corruption in the name of reform

One of the most precise warnings in the Qur’an is that corruption may come under the name of reform. In 2:11, the warning is given: لَا تُفْسِدُوا فِي الأَرْضِ (lā tufsidū fī al-arḍ): do not cause corruption in the earth. Their response is: إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُصْلِحُونَ (innamā naḥnu muṣliḥūn): we are only reformers.

But the Qur’an then says that they are the corrupters, yet they do not perceive. This means that a person may consider himself a reformer while the result of his action is fasād. Claiming reform is not enough; one must see whether the action produces salāh or ruin.

This point matters for religion, politics, society, and ethics. Anyone can speak the language of reform. But the Qur’an does not place the standard in the slogan. The standard is the reality of the action and its effect on the earth, the human being, justice, and trust.

Translation suggestions for Forqan

فَسَدَ / فَسَدَتْ (fasada / fasadat): became corrupt; was ruined; departed from salāh and soundness.

لَفَسَدَتَا (lafasadatā): both would have become corrupted; the order of both would have collapsed; both would have fallen apart.

أَفْسَدَ / يُفْسِدُ (afsada / yufsidu): caused corruption; ruined; disrupted the sound structure.

لَا تُفْسِدُوا (lā tufsidū): do not cause corruption; do not produce ruin; do not spoil the sound order.

فَسَاد (fasād): corruption; ruin; breakdown; loss of salāh.

الفَسَاد فِي الأَرْض (al-fasād fī al-arḍ): corruption in the earth; ruin within the field of life, society, justice, and earthly order.

مُفْسِد / مُفْسِدُون / مُفْسِدِينَ (mufsid / mufsidūn / mufsidīn): corrupter; spoiler; those who disrupt salāh and produce ruin in the earth or society.

مُصْلِحُون (muṣliḥūn) against مُفْسِدُون (mufsidūn): reformers or rectifiers against corrupters; but the Qur’an warns that a claim of reform is not always real salāh.

Summary

The root ف س د (F S D) in the Qur’an points to departure from salāh, soundness, balance, and beneficial order. Fasād can occur in food, water, the body, the earth, society, covenant, religion, government, judgment, relationship, and human action.

In the Qur’an, corruption is not merely a private sin. Anything that drives truth, justice, trust, the earth, crops, progeny, relationships, religion, and beneficial order toward ruin enters the field of fasād.

For this reason, the root ف س د (F S D) is one of the key roots for understanding the responsibility of the human being in the earth. If the human being produces ruin instead of reform, even if he names his work reform, he stands in the Qur’anic path of fasād.
