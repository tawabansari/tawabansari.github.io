Introduction to the Root

The root W Sh Y in Arabic points to adorning a surface with different colors, lines, or patterns, producing a design within something, and creating a visible mark that stands out from the main background. From this field, washy is used for patterned or colorful cloth, and shiyah for a mark, spot, or color that differs from the dominant color of an animal’s body.

In its lexical uses, this root also developed other branches: adorning and coloring speech, shaping or embellishing a report, carrying speech to someone else, and tale-bearing. In early lexical reports, a separate branch meaning increase, growth, and abundance is also recorded.

Therefore, the meanings of this root may be recognized in three major fields:

  • patterning, color-adornment, and creating visual distinction;
  • adorning or coloring speech and transmitting it;
  • growth, increase, and abundance in some early usages.

The Qur'anic use of this root belongs to the first field.

Material Meaning and Basic Image

The most tangible image of W Sh Y is a cloth whose background carries other colors, lines, or patterns. In such cloth, one uniform color has not covered the whole surface; rather, another color or design interrupts the main background and makes a distinct sign upon it.

Washā ath-thawb means he patterned, colored, or adorned the cloth. Thawb mawshiyy or muwashshā is a cloth adorned with different colors or patterns.

From this same image, the word is also used for the natural covering of an animal. If the dominant color of an animal’s body is interrupted by a spot, stripe, patch, or differently colored part, that distinct mark is called shiyah. In this usage, shiyah is not necessarily a defect, disease, or impurity; before anything else, it is a visual difference within the dominant color.

Semantic Core

In the main branch of this root, three elements appear together:

1. the existence of a background or surface; 2. the addition of another color, line, pattern, or mark; 3. the distinction of that addition from the dominant background.

On this basis, the semantic core of W Sh Y may be expressed as:

Bringing about, or the existence of, a distinct pattern or mark upon a background.

In the verb washā, the act of patterning and adorning is prominent; but in the noun shiyah, the distinct mark itself or the visible result of that act comes into focus.

Lexical Branches of the Root

Adorning and Patterning

Washy is used for adorning cloth with various colors and patterns. This adornment may occur through weaving, stitching, dyeing, or adding design. The shared element in all these uses is that the surface leaves plain uniformity and recognizable patterns appear upon it.

A Color Mark on the Body of an Animal

Shiyah is a color or mark that differs from the dominant color of an animal’s body, such as whiteness within a black background, blackness within a white background, or any colored spot or stripe that breaks the uniformity of the body covering.

For this reason, shiyah is not simply “color”; it is a color that, in comparison with the dominant background, appears as a distinct mark.

Adorning Speech

This root moved from the field of color and pattern into the field of speech. Just as cloth may be adorned with colors and designs, speech may also be adorned or “colored” through phrasing, additions, or changes in the manner of narration.

Washā al-kalām can indicate constructing, adorning, or embellishing speech. If this adornment is joined with adding things contrary to reality, the meaning comes close to fabricating a lie or distorting a report.

Tale-Bearing and Carrying Reports

Washā bihi ilā fulān means to carry someone’s words to another, report against him, or engage in tale-bearing. The masdar wishāyah and the active participle wāshin are formed from this usage.

The connection between this meaning and pattern-adornment is probably that the tale-bearer does not merely transmit speech; he may arrange, color, or present it in a way meant to produce a particular effect on the listener. However, this use does not appear in the Qur'an and should not be imposed on shiyah in the Qur'anic verse.

Growth and Increase

In early lexical reports, W Sh Y is also used for increase in number, abundance of offspring, and the appearance of growth. Forms of this root are reported for a group whose number has increased or for a woman who has many children. Some early usages are also connected with the beginning of growth or the appearing of fruit.

Lexicographers sometimes treated this field as a branch separate from the meaning of pattern and adornment. Therefore, there is no need to force all these usages back through interpretation to color-adornment.

Important Derivatives

Among the most important forms of this family are the following:

  • وَشَى يَشِي: to pattern, adorn, color; and in another usage, to carry speech or report.
  • وَشْي: patterning, adorning, weaving, or colorful design.
  • شِیَة: a mark or color different from the dominant color.
  • وِشایة: carrying speech, reporting against someone, or tale-bearing.
  • واشٍ: a tale-bearer or reporter; and depending on context, someone who adorns something.
  • وُشاة: plural of wāshin, meaning tale-bearers or informers.
  • مَوْشِيّ and مُوَشًّى: patterned, color-adorned, or carrying different designs.
  • تَوْشِیَة: adorning, patterning, and making colorful.

These derivatives show that the root is connected on one side with pattern and visual distinction, and on the other with arranging and transmitting speech.

The Morphological Structure of Shiyah

Shiyah appears to begin with the letter shin, but its root is W Sh Y. Its historical structure is traced back to a form such as wishyah, in which the initial waw was dropped and the final ta was understood as replacing it in the structure of the word. This change is comparable to words such as idah from W Ayn D and zinah from W Z N.

Therefore, the absence of waw in the visible form shiyah does not mean that it belongs to the root Sh Y Y. The root waw fell away in the process of word formation.

Its plural is shiyāt.

Qur'anic Usage

Reference Cards for Qur'anic Derivatives

Qur'anic formشِیَة
شِیَة

A mark, spot, or color-pattern distinct from the dominant color of a surface or body.

The root W Sh Y appears in the Qur'an only once and only in the form shiyah:

مُسَلَّمَةٌ لَا شِيَةَ فِيهَا

Al-Baqarah 2:71

This phrase is part of the description of the cow about whose features the people had asked. Before this phrase, the cow’s color is described as:

بَقَرَةٌ صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَوْنُهَا

Al-Baqarah 2:69

By placing these two descriptions beside each other, it becomes clear that shiyah in this context means a mark or color that differs from the dominant yellowness of the cow’s body.

The Meaning of La Shiyata Fiha

The phrase la shiyata fiha does not mean that the cow has no color, because two verses earlier its color is explicitly identified as yellow and intense. The meaning is that within the yellow background of its body, there is no patch, spot, stripe, or differently colored pattern.

The semantic structure of the phrase is therefore as follows:

  • the cow has a specified color;
  • that color dominates the body covering;
  • no other color interrupts it;
  • the body covering is uniform in color.

Shiyah here is not the name of one specific color. Any color that differs from the cow’s main color could be called shiyah.

Difference Between Shiyah and Lawn

Lawn is the general name for color and the color-quality of something. An object can have only one lawn and be completely uniform.

Shiyah appears when, inside one color-background, a sign with a different color or appearance is seen. Therefore:

  • lawn states the color itself;
  • shiyah states the visual difference or break within the dominant color.

Every shiyah is connected with color, but not every color is shiyah. Shiyah requires comparison between a dominant background and a distinct sign.

In the Qur'anic description of the cow, fāqi'un lawnuhā states the intensity and clarity of the color, while la shiyata fiha states the absence of a different color or mark. The two phrases complete each other.

Difference Between Shiyah and Musallamah

In the same verse, before la shiyata fiha, the word musallamah appears:

مُسَلَّمَةٌ لَا شِيَةَ فِيهَا

Al-Baqarah 2:71

Musallamah indicates soundness, wholeness, and the absence of defect or injury. La shiyata fiha adds another feature: the absence of a different color mark.

Therefore, if shiyah is translated only as “defect,” its boundary with musallamah disappears and two independent descriptions are reduced into one meaning. Shiyah may be viewed negatively in some contexts, but in its lexical core it is not necessarily a defect; it is a visual and differently colored mark.

The order of the phrase shows two separate qualities:

  • musallamah: sound and unharmed;
  • la shiyata fiha: without a spot, patch, or different color-pattern.

Grammatical Note

In la shiyata fiha, la is used for negation of genus. Shiyata is its singular indefinite noun, and fiha is its predicate.

This structure does not negate only one specific shiyah; it negates every kind of different mark from the cow. The meaning of the phrase is:

There is no shiyah of any kind in it.

The indefinite shiyah inside the scope of negation makes the expression general: no white spot, no black part, no stripe, and no other color that interrupts the uniform yellowness of the body.

Difference from Nearby Words

Shiyah and Wasm

Wasm is a mark placed on something for recognition or identification, and it can be deliberate, such as a brand or identifying sign.

Shiyah, however, may be a natural part of the animal’s body color and may not have been produced for the purpose of marking. The main element in shiyah is visual difference from the dominant color, not the act of marking.

Shiyah and Alamah

Alamah is anything that points to another matter, whether visual or audible, colored or not colored.

Shiyah is narrower and refers to a visible mark, especially a color difference or pattern on a surface.

Shiyah and Buqah

Buqah mainly indicates a patch, area, or limited part of a surface. That part may have a different color, but color difference is not necessary to the meaning of buqah.

In shiyah, the color distinction or different pattern itself is prominent.

Shiyah and Shayb

Shiyah and shayb do not share the same root. Shayb is from Sh Y B and means the whitening of hair because of age; shiyah is from W Sh Y and means a mark or color different from the main background. The outward resemblance between the two forms should not cause the roots to be mixed.

Care in Translation

Translating la shiyata fiha as “there is no defect in it” is not precise, because it repeats the meaning of musallamah and removes the element of color and visual distinction.

“It has no spot” is closer to the root meaning, but “spot” in English, like its Persian equivalent, can suggest impurity, dirt, or skin disease, whereas shiyah may be a natural and healthy mark.

More precise renderings may be:

“It is sound, and there is no different color mark in it.”

“It is sound, with no patch or differently colored pattern in it.”

“It is sound and uniform in color, with no different color mark visible in it.”

Conclusion

In its main branch, the root W Sh Y points to patterning, color-adornment, and the appearance of a distinct mark upon a background. From this material image came meanings such as adorning speech, shaping and embellishing a report, and carrying speech. In early lexical reports, a separate branch concerning growth, increase, and abundance is also recorded.

The only Qur'anic derivative of this root is shiyah. In the phrase la shiyata fiha, the word points to a spot, patch, stripe, or any color mark that differs from the dominant color of the cow. Its pairing with fāqi'un lawnuhā and musallamah clarifies its boundary: the cow is both sound and uniform in color; no differently colored pattern or mark interrupts the dominant yellowness of its body.

The basic meaning of the Qur'anic form may therefore be compressed as:

Shiyah: a mark, spot, or color-pattern distinct from the dominant color of a surface or body.
