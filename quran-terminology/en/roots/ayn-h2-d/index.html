Foundational Meaning

The root ʿ H D in the Qur'an does not mean only covenant. It holds a wider field: knowing, remembering, entrusting, charging, covenanting, preserving obligation, and remaining upon a clear knowledge or responsibility.

At its foundation, ʿahd is something a person has come to know and has become responsible for. It is not merely an external contract. It is binding knowledge. When something is made an ʿahd with a person, it has been entrusted to that person, made clear to them, and they are expected not to forget it, abandon it, or betray it.

In the language behind the root, ʿahd is also connected with prior recognition, remembrance, care, and an effect that remains. In Arabic usage, ʿahd can point to knowing something from before or being familiar with it in a particular state. It can also point to early rain whose effect remains in the land, and to repeated care or maintenance. These layers help explain why Qur'anic ʿahd is not a loose spoken promise. It must remain alive in memory, will, action, and care.

This is why ʿahd in the Qur'an becomes connected with wafā', mīthāq, forgetfulness, ʿazm, naqḍ, nabdh, accountability, taqwa, and ẓulm. ʿAhd is not knowledge without consequence. It is knowledge that brings the human being into responsibility.

Forms and Qur'anic Examples

The root ʿ H D appears in several main forms. In the simple verb, as in عَهِدْنَا (ʿahidnā) and أَعْهَدْ (aʿhad), the meaning comes close to entrusting, charging, instructing, or placing a person before a responsibility. In form III, as in عَاهَدُوا (ʿāhadū) and عَاهَدْتُمْ (ʿāhadtum), ʿahd takes the form of a mutual pact or social commitment. As the noun عَهْد (ʿahd), it names the covenant, responsibility, divine charge, safe-conduct, or ethical obligation itself.

عَهِدْنَا / أَعْهَدْ (ʿahidnā / aʿhad)

These forms show ʿahd as entrusting a clear matter to a person. The responsibility is not merely announced; it is placed upon the addressee.

عَاهَدُوا / عَاهَدْتُمْ (ʿāhadū / ʿāhadtum)

In this form, ʿahd comes close to mutual covenant and social commitment. The parties enter a relation that calls for trust, loyalty, and accountability.

عَهْد (ʿahd)

The noun ʿahd can name the covenant, responsibility, divine charge, safe-conduct, or ethical obligation itself. The exact shade is determined by context.

In Al-Baqarah 2:27: الَّذِينَ يَنْقُضُونَ عَهْدَ اللَّهِ مِنْ بَعْدِ مِيثَاقِهِ (alladhīna yanquḍūna ʿahda Allāhi min baʿdi mīthāqih), those who break the covenant of The God after its firm binding.

In Al-Baqarah 2:40: وَأَوْفُوا بِعَهْدِي أُوفِ بِعَهْدِكُمْ (wa-awfū bi-ʿahdī ūfi bi-ʿahdikum), fulfill My covenant, and I will fulfill your covenant.

In Al-Baqarah 2:124: لَا يَنَالُ عَهْدِي الظَّالِمِينَ (lā yanālu ʿahdī al-ẓālimīn), My covenant does not reach the unjust.

In Al-Baqarah 2:125: وَعَهِدْنَا إِلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْمَاعِيلَ (wa-ʿahidnā ilā Ibrāhīma wa-Ismāʿīl), We entrusted a charge to Abraham and Ishmael.

In Ali 'Imran 3:76: بَلَىٰ مَنْ أَوْفَىٰ بِعَهْدِهِ وَاتَّقَىٰ (balā man awfā bi-ʿahdihi wa-ittaqā), yes, whoever fulfills his covenant and has taqwa.

In Al-Baqarah 2:100: أَوَكُلَّمَا عَاهَدُوا عَهْدًا نَبَذَهُ فَرِيقٌ مِّنْهُمْ (awa-kullamā ʿāhadū ʿahdan nabadhahu farīqun minhum), whenever they made a covenant, a group of them cast it aside.

In Al-Isra 17:34: وَأَوْفُوا بِالْعَهْدِ ۖ إِنَّ الْعَهْدَ كَانَ مَسْئُولًا (wa-awfū bi-l-ʿahdi inna al-ʿahda kāna mas'ūlā), fulfill the covenant; surely the covenant is something asked about.

In Ta-Ha 20:115: وَلَقَدْ عَهِدْنَا إِلَىٰ آدَمَ مِنْ قَبْلُ فَنَسِيَ وَلَمْ نَجِدْ لَهُ عَزْمًا (wa-laqad ʿahidnā ilā Ādama min qablu fa-nasiya wa-lam najid lahu ʿazmā), We had entrusted a charge to Adam before; then he forgot, and We did not find in him firm resolve.

In Ya-Sin 36:60: أَلَمْ أَعْهَدْ إِلَيْكُمْ يَا بَنِي آدَمَ (alam aʿhad ilaykum yā banī Ādam), did I not entrust a charge to you, children of Adam?

In Maryam 19:87: إِلَّا مَنِ اتَّخَذَ عِندَ الرَّحْمَٰنِ عَهْدًا (illā mani ittakhadha ʿinda al-Raḥmāni ʿahdā), except one who has taken a covenant with The Merciful.

ʿAhd as Binding Knowledge

One key to this root is the connection between ʿahd and knowledge. ʿAhd does not properly concern something of which a person has no awareness at all. It appears when a truth has reached a person, something has become clear to them, or a responsibility has been entrusted to them.

In 20:115, ʿahd stands across from forgetting: فَنَسِيَ (fa-nasiya). This means that ʿahd is something that must be held in memory, will, and action. Adam's problem was not simple ignorance. A charge had been entrusted to him, but that charge was forgotten, and the resolve needed to carry it was not found.

So in the Qur'an, ʿahd is tied to remembrance and inward preservation. It means that a person knows something, does not let it disappear from memory, and remains firm before it.

ʿAhd and ʿAzm

Verse 20:115 places ʿahd beside forgetfulness and resolve: فَنَسِيَ وَلَمْ نَجِدْ لَهُ عَزْمًا (fa-nasiya wa-lam najid lahu ʿazmā). This pairing matters. ʿAhd does not need memory alone. It also needs ʿazm, firm resolve.

A person may know a truth but fail to preserve it. A responsibility may be clear to them, yet they may not have the will to carry it. In this sense, Qur'anic ʿahd is living and practical. It must remain in memory, but memory alone is not enough. Resolve must accompany it.

ʿAhd and Wafā'

The most important companion of ʿahd in the Qur'an is wafā', fulfillment. In 2:40 it says: وَأَوْفُوا بِعَهْدِي أُوفِ بِعَهْدِكُمْ (wa-awfū bi-ʿahdī ūfi bi-ʿahdikum). In 17:34 it says: وَأَوْفُوا بِالْعَهْدِ (wa-awfū bi-l-ʿahd). This shows that ʿahd is not completed by acceptance alone. It needs fulfillment.

Wafā' means bringing an obligation to its fullness. A person does not merely accept the covenant; they preserve it, carry it to completion, and do not betray it in action.

For this reason, ʿahd is tied to time. A covenant may be made in a moment, but fulfillment appears through time. The truth of a covenant becomes clear when interest, pressure, forgetfulness, fear, or power enters the field.

ʿAhd and Continuing Care

In the broader field of ʿ H D, commitment can also include repeated attention, maintenance, and care for something. This matters for Qur'anic ʿahd. A covenant is not a statement spoken and finished. It must be cared for.

If a covenant is not cared for, forgetfulness enters. If forgetfulness continues, resolve weakens. If resolve weakens, naqḍ or nabdh becomes easier. That is why fulfilling a covenant is not only a momentary act. It is a continuing guardianship over responsibility.

From this angle, ʿahd resembles a trust that must be kept alive. A person must return to it, examine it, protect it from erosion and forgetfulness, and not allow it to become a lifeless phrase.

ʿAhd and Mīthāq

In some verses, ʿahd appears with mīthāq, as in Al-Baqarah 2:27: عَهْدَ اللَّهِ مِنْ بَعْدِ مِيثَاقِهِ (ʿahda Allāhi min baʿdi mīthāqih). This pairing shows that ʿahd can reach a state of confirmation and firmness. Mīthāq is a covenant that has been bound, made firm, and made clear.

Every ʿahd carries responsibility, but an ʿahd that has become mīthāq carries a heavier responsibility. In that state, breaking the covenant is not abandoning something vague. It is breaking something toward which a person had knowledge and commitment.

Here the relation between ʿ H D and N Q Ḍ becomes clear. The covenant is entrusted or made. Mīthāq makes it firm. Wafā' preserves it. Continuing care keeps it alive. Naqḍ unravels it.

ʿAhd, Naqḍ, and Nabdh

Breaking covenant in the Qur'an is a sign of collapsing responsibility. In 2:27, those are criticized who break the covenant of The God after its mīthāq. Naqḍ of ʿahd means emptying from within the knowledge and responsibility one had accepted. The outer form of the covenant may remain, but loyalty has disappeared.

In 2:100, the phrase نَبَذَهُ فَرِيقٌ مِّنْهُمْ (nabadhahu farīqun minhum) appears. Nabdh means casting something behind and abandoning it. This image is close to naqḍ but not identical. Naqḍ is unraveling the structure of a covenant. Nabdh is pushing aside and ignoring a responsibility that should have stayed before the person.

This verse shows that covenant-breaking does not always begin with open denial. Sometimes a person does not formally reject the covenant, but casts it behind them. They no longer allow the covenant to guide conduct and decision.

ʿAhd and Taqwa

In 3:76, fulfilling covenant appears beside taqwa: بَلَىٰ مَنْ أَوْفَىٰ بِعَهْدِهِ وَاتَّقَىٰ (balā man awfā bi-ʿahdihi wa-ittaqā). This pairing matters. Taqwa here is not merely a feeling of fear. It is conscious self-guarding against breaking what is right.

One who has taqwa does not treat covenant lightly. Such a person guards against interest, power, anger, or forgetfulness pulling them into betrayal. In this sense, fulfilling covenant is one practical sign of taqwa.

ʿAhd and Ẓulm

In 2:124, after Abraham is tested with words, The God says: لَا يَنَالُ عَهْدِي الظَّالِمِينَ (lā yanālu ʿahdī al-ẓālimīn). This is one of the most important verses about ʿahd. The divine covenant does not reach the unjust.

This shows that the covenant of The God is not an ethnic, bloodline, or inherited privilege. Divine covenant is tied to justice, qualification, and freedom from injustice. Even when the discussion concerns the descendants of Abraham, the final measure is not lineage; it is justice and injustice.

So divine covenant cannot be turned into a tool that legitimizes wrongdoers. Anyone who commits ẓulm falls outside the fitness to carry divine covenant, even if they attach themselves to a family, people, prophet, or sacred history.

ʿAhd and Accountability

In 17:34, the Qur'an says: إِنَّ الْعَهْدَ كَانَ مَسْئُولًا (inna al-ʿahda kāna mas'ūlā). The covenant is something asked about. This expression shows that ʿahd is not merely a soft ethical recommendation. It creates accountability.

A person will be questioned about covenants: What covenant did they make? With what intention? Did they understand it? Did they preserve it? Did they break it? Did they use it to deceive others?

In the Qur'an, ʿahd binds speech, intention, knowledge, and action. That is why breaking a covenant is not merely breaking a sentence. It is breaking responsibility.

Covenant with The God and Covenants with People

The Qur'an speaks about the covenant of The God and also about covenants among human beings. These two are not severed from each other. One who breaks covenant before The God does not remain trustworthy before people. And one who treats human covenants lightly has violated the sanctity of responsibility that The God has placed upon human speech and action.

Covenant with The God connects the human being to truth, justice, worship, trust, and ethical responsibility. Covenants with people preserve social order, trust, security, and justice.

That is why the Qur'an places fulfillment of covenant beside faith and taqwa. A society without covenant becomes a society without trust. Faith without fulfillment of covenant becomes incomplete and ineffective.

ʿAhd and Prophets

In the Qur'an, ʿahd sometimes appears in relation to prophets and their missions. The God entrusted Abraham and Ishmael with the charge of purifying the House for those who circle, remain devoted, bow, and prostrate. This shows that ʿahd can appear as a divine and practical mission.

In the story of Adam, divine ʿahd is also tied to responsibility and remembrance. Adam forgets the ʿahd, and the Qur'an connects that forgetfulness with the absence of firm resolve. From this angle, ʿahd is not only knowing a command. It requires ʿazm. The human being must have the will to preserve it.

ʿAhd with The Merciful

In Sūrat Maryam, the phrase عِندَ الرَّحْمَٰنِ عَهْدًا (ʿinda al-Raḥmāni ʿahdā) appears. This expression shows that ʿahd can also have spiritual and final weight. It is not only a social contract in worldly life. It is a relation that has standing with The Merciful.

But the Qur'an does not reduce this ʿahd to verbal claim, lineage, or group attachment. An ʿahd with The Merciful must be aligned with faith, sound action, truthfulness, and loyalty. A covenant joined to injustice, shirk, deception, and breach cannot be a valid covenant with The Merciful.

Difference from Nearby Words

ʿAhd is not the same as waʿd. Waʿd mostly concerns giving notice of something to be done in the future. ʿAhd is heavier: it carries knowledge, commitment, and responsibility.

ʿAhd is not identical to mīthāq. Mīthāq is a covenant made more firm, bound, and emphatic. Every mīthāq is a kind of ʿahd, but not every ʿahd is necessarily at the same degree of confirmation and firmness as mīthāq.

ʿAhd is close to ʿaqd, but they are not the same. ʿAqd focuses more on tying, knotting, and forming a bond or contract. ʿAhd, besides binding, carries knowledge, remembrance, preservation, and loyalty.

ʿAhd is not the same as faith, but it should not be separated from faith. Faith without ʿahd can become a mental claim. When faith appears in covenant and fulfillment, it becomes practical responsibility.

Translation Suggestions

عَهْد (ʿahd): covenant, entrusted responsibility, binding commitment, depending on context.

عَهْدَ اللَّهِ (ʿahda Allāh): the covenant of The God; or the responsibility The God has made clear and binding upon the human being.

عَهِدْنَا إِلَىٰ (ʿahidnā ilā): We entrusted a charge to; We gave a clear responsibility to.

عَاهَدُوا عَهْدًا (ʿāhadū ʿahdā): they made a covenant; they entered a conscious commitment.

أَوْفُوا بِالْعَهْدِ (awfū bi-l-ʿahd): fulfill the covenant; preserve the commitment until its fullness.

إِنَّ الْعَهْدَ كَانَ مَسْئُولًا (inna al-ʿahda kāna mas'ūlā): the covenant is something asked about; or the human being is accountable for covenant.

لَا يَنَالُ عَهْدِي الظَّالِمِينَ (lā yanālu ʿahdī al-ẓālimīn): My covenant does not reach the unjust; divine covenant does not accept the qualification of injustice.

Summary

The root ʿ H D in the Qur'an points to knowledge that becomes responsibility; a covenant that must be preserved; a charge that must not be forgotten; and an obligation about which the human being will be questioned.

ʿAhd means knowledge joined with responsibility, entrusting an important matter to a person, making a covenant on the basis of awareness, preserving commitment through time, remaining loyal to truth after accepting it, continuing care over responsibility, not forgetting what has been made clear, having resolve to preserve the obligation, and remaining upon a bond that The God or human beings have established upon truth.

In the Qur'an, ʿahd remains alive through wafā', is guarded by taqwa, is strengthened by mīthāq, is carried by ʿazm, is weakened by forgetfulness, is pushed aside by nabdh, is collapsed by naqḍ, and loses divine fitness through ẓulm.

The root may be summarized this way: ʿ H D means knowledge that brings the human being into commitment, preservation, and accountability. In Qur'anic usage, ʿahd is not merely a verbal covenant; it is a responsibility placed upon a person after awareness, and it must be preserved through wafā', taqwa, ʿazm, care, and justice.
