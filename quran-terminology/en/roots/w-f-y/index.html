Foundational Meaning

The root W F Y fundamentally indicates completing, bringing to full completion, fully rendering the right of something, and not falling short in covenant, measure, accounting, promise, or recompense. The main axis of this root is "fullness" and "not reducing." Something that must reach its full limit must be fulfilled. A right that must be rendered must be given without deficiency. A covenant that has been made must be kept completely. And a self that is taken is taken in its entirety.

Important Qur'anic Forms

Important forms of this root in the Qur'an include wafā, awfā, yūfūn, awfū, wafaw, tawaffā, yatawaffā, tawaffāhum, tawaffanī, tawaffanā, wafāh, mustawfā, and wafāʾ. Wafā and awfā point to completing, fulfilling, and rendering fully. Tawaffā in Form V indicates taking something in its entirety; from here it is used for taking the self in death or sleep. Wafāʾ means fully rendering a covenant or right.

Linguistic Core

The linguistic core of W F Y is bringing the right of something to completion. If something is rendered deficiently, it has not been fulfilled. If a covenant is outwardly accepted but then broken in action, fulfillment has not occurred. If wage, recompense, measure, weight, or accounting is given less than its right, it conflicts with the root W F Y. So fulfillment is not only a gentle ethical virtue; it is a principle of justice in rendering a right completely.

Material Image

The material image of this root is a measure that must be filled completely, a debt that must be paid fully, or a road that must be traveled to the end. Falling short, leaving something half-done, or giving a right deficiently is the opposite of fulfillment.

Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic usage, W F Y is connected with being complete, becoming complete, faithfulness to covenant, rendering a right, paying fully, and taking something fully. In the Qur'an, this meaning expands to covenant, pledge, measure and weight, recompense, wage, the self, death, sleep, and divine promise.

Fulfilling Covenant

One foundational field of this root is fulfilling covenant:

وَأَوْفُوا بِالْعَهْدِ ۖ إِنَّ الْعَهْدَ كَانَ مَسْئُولًا

And fulfill the covenant; indeed, the covenant is subject to questioning.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:34

In this verse, awfū bi-al-ʿahd means fulfill the covenant completely, not merely accept it with the tongue. Covenant in the Qur'an is weighty because it ties the human relation with The God, people, and society to responsibility. Fulfillment means not falling short in that responsibility.

Another verse says:

وَأَوْفُوا بِعَهْدِ اللَّهِ إِذَا عَاهَدتُّمْ

And fulfill the covenant of The God when you have made a covenant.

Al-Naḥl 16:91

Here fulfilling covenant is not only individual ethics. It is the boundary between truthfulness and betrayal. If the human being makes a covenant, they must bring it to completion. Breaking covenant is not merely breaking a statement; it is the collapse of trust and justice.

Two-Sided Fulfillment in Divine Covenant

The second field is two-sided fulfillment in divine covenant:

وَأَوْفُوا بِعَهْدِي أُوفِ بِعَهْدِكُمْ

And fulfill My covenant, and I will fulfill your covenant.

Al-Baqarah 2:40

This verse is addressed to the Children of Israel, but its principle is very broad. Fulfillment with The God is a serious and responsible relation. The human being cannot treat the divine covenant lightly and then expect the fulfillment of divine promises. The fulfillment of The God is complete, but the human being must render the covenant in their own place.

This verse shows that fulfillment in the Qur'an is not only emotional loyalty; it is the structure of covenant, responsibility, and realization of promise.

Full Measure and Weight

The third field is full measure and weight:

وَأَوْفُوا الْكَيْلَ وَالْمِيزَانَ بِالْقِسْطِ

And render measure and balance fully with qisṭ.

Al-Anʿām 6:152

In this verse, awfū al-kayl wa-al-mīzān means give full measure and weight and do not reduce. Fulfillment here is entirely economic and social. People's right in transaction must not be reduced. So fulfillment is not only in great covenants; it also runs through scales, measures, markets, and exchange.

In Surat Hūd, Shuʿayb also says:

يَا قَوْمِ أَوْفُوا الْمِكْيَالَ وَالْمِيزَانَ بِالْقِسْطِ

O my people, render the measure and the balance fully with qisṭ.

Hūd 11:85

The call of Shuʿayb shows that economic corruption, shortchanging, and reducing people's rights oppose fulfillment and oppose qisṭ. A society that falls short in measure and weight has in reality broken its relation with justice.

Fulfillment Against Shortchanging and Taṭfīf

The fourth field is fulfillment against shortchanging and taṭfīf:

وَيْلٌ لِّلْمُطَفِّفِينَ

Woe to the shortchangers.

Al-Muṭaffifīn 83:1

Although the root W F Y does not appear in this verse, its semantic opposite is clear. A muṭaffif is one who falls short in the rights of others. Fulfillment means giving the right fully. Taṭfīf means reducing from the right. This contrast is important for understanding W F Y: fulfillment is not only speech; it is justice in measure and right.

Fulfilling a Vow

The fifth field is fulfilling a vow:

يُوفُونَ بِالنَّذْرِ وَيَخَافُونَ يَوْمًا كَانَ شَرُّهُ مُسْتَطِيرًا

They fulfill the vow, and they fear a day whose evil is widespread.

Al-Insān 76:7

Here yūfūn bi-al-nadhr means they fully render what they made binding upon themselves. Fulfilling a vow is a sign of the seriousness of faith. The faithful human being does not treat their commitment lightly, because they know their speech and covenant before The God are not without effect and accounting.

Fulfillment and Full Recompense of Deeds

The sixth field is fulfillment and full recompense of deeds:

وَإِنَّمَا تُوَفَّوْنَ أُجُورَكُمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ

And you will only be given your wages in full on the Day of Standing.

Āl ʿImrān 3:185

Tuwaffawna ujūrakum means the wages are given fully. On the Day of Standing, the right of action is not reduced, ignored, or paid deficiently. This use shows that W F Y is connected to final justice: each person receives the result of their action in full.

Another verse says:

وَوُفِّيَتْ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ مَّا عَمِلَتْ

And every self was fully given what it had done.

Āl ʿImrān 3:25

Here fulfillment comes in relation to action and recompense. Every self meets what it has done, without its right being reduced or anything being overlooked. This is one of the foundations of Qur'anic justice: recompense is complete, not arbitrary and not deficient.

Surat al-Zumar also says:

وَوُفِّيَتْ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ مَّا عَمِلَتْ وَهُوَ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا يَفْعَلُونَ

And every self was fully given what it had done, and He knows best what they do.

Al-Zumar 39:70

Here too, fulfillment is tied to complete accounting. No action, intention, wrongdoing, reform, concealment, or real effort is outside the knowledge of The God. Fulfillment on the Day of Standing means an accounting that is not deficient and does not reduce any right.

Fulfillment and Divine Promise

The seventh field is fulfillment and divine promise:

وَمَنْ أَوْفَىٰ بِعَهْدِهِ مِنَ اللَّهِ

And who is more fulfilling of his covenant than The God?

Al-Tawbah 9:111

In this verse, the fulfillment of The God in covenant is presented. Divine fulfillment is complete and without deficiency. The God does not treat His promise lightly, does not forget, and does not render it deficiently. This is an important difference: the human being may break covenant, but The God does not fall short in His promise and covenant.

Another verse says:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُخْلِفُ الْمِيعَادَ

Indeed, The God does not fail the appointment.

Āl ʿImrān 3:9

The root W F Y does not appear here, but the meaning is close. Fulfillment is connected with not breaking the promise. Divine promise is complete and firm in its realization.

Taking the Self Fully in Death and Sleep

The eighth field is taking the self fully in death and sleep:

اللَّهُ يَتَوَفَّى الْأَنفُسَ حِينَ مَوْتِهَا وَالَّتِي لَمْ تَمُتْ فِي مَنَامِهَا

The God takes the selves fully at the time of their death, and the one that has not died in its sleep.

Al-Zumar 39:42

This verse is very important for understanding tawaffā. Tawaffā does not mean killing; it means taking something in its entirety. Death is one field of tawaffī, but the root itself does not mean death. The God takes the self fully at the time of death, and in sleep a kind of taking also occurs, but returning it or holding it is by the will of The God.

So wherever tawaffā appears, it should not be immediately translated as "to cause death." One must see what its object is and what the context says. Tawaffī of the self means taking the self in its entirety, and death is one of its outcomes or fields.

Another verse says:

حَتَّىٰ تَتَوَفَّاهُمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ

Until the angels take them fully.

Al-Naḥl 16:28

Here too, tawaffī by the angels occurs at the time of death. Yet again the root meaning is taking fully, not simply killing. The angels take the self from the worldly state, and the human being enters another stage.

Tawaffanī in Joseph's Supplication

The ninth field is tawaffanī in Joseph's supplication:

تَوَفَّنِي مُسْلِمًا وَأَلْحِقْنِي بِالصَّالِحِينَ

Take me fully in a state of submission, and join me with the righteous.

Yūsuf 12:101

In common translation, one may say "make me die as a Muslim." That can be understandable from the side of outcome, but from the side of the root, the more precise meaning is: "Take me fully in a state of submission." Joseph asks The God that his ending, his being taken fully, and his passing from dunya be in a state of submission. Here tawaffī is connected with the faithful outcome of the human being.

The Tawaffī of Jesus

The tenth field is the tawaffī of Jesus:

إِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَىٰ إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ

When The God said: O Jesus, I am taking you fully and raising you to Myself.

Āl ʿImrān 3:55

This verse is sensitive. Mutawaffīka is from the root W F Y, and its foundational meaning is taking fully. It should not be restricted to "causing death" without evidence, and it should not be used to build another definite conclusion without evidence either. The verse says that The God takes Jesus fully and raises him to Himself. The details of how this occurs belong to the field of the unseen and must be understood with caution.

But from the perspective of root study, the point is clear: tawaffī means taking fully, not necessarily death in the ordinary sense. Death can be one instance of tawaffī, but not every tawaffī can be reduced to death.

This precision is important for understanding Jesus. The Qur'an does not say that the enemies of Jesus overpowered him and destroyed his truth. Rather, it says The God took him fully and raised him to Himself. So the axis of the verse is the power of The God to take fully and preserve Jesus from their plotting, not a simple and rushed proof of an ordinary death.

Abraham Who Fulfilled

The eleventh field is the fulfillment of Abraham:

وَإِبْرَاهِيمَ الَّذِي وَفَّىٰ

And Abraham, the one who fulfilled.

Al-Najm 53:37

This expression is short but very deep. The Qur'an presents Abraham with wafā: one who completed, rendered, and did not fall short. This may point to covenant, trials, commands, responsibilities, and his path of tawḥīd. Abraham is the human example of one who did not remain half-done before The God.

Here fulfillment is not only one separate act; it is a description of Abraham's formed character. In his path, he rendered the right of servanthood and covenant as far as was asked of him.

Difference Between Wafā, Awfā, and Tawaffā

A distinction must be made between wafā, awfā, and tawaffā. Wafā and awfā mostly point to fulfilling, rendering fully, and not falling short in covenant, measure, promise, or right. Tawaffā points to taking something in its entirety; it is used regarding the self, death, sleep, and the end of worldly life. So tawaffī shares a root with fulfillment, but its direction differs: in fulfillment, a human being or agent renders something fully; in tawaffī, something is taken fully.

Structural Notes

Structurally, particles and objects are important. Awfū bi-al-ʿahd means fulfill the covenant completely. Awfū al-kayl wa-al-mīzān means give full measure and balance. Yūfūn bi-al-nadhr means they fulfill the vow completely. Tawaffā al-anfus means He takes the selves fully. Tuwaffawna ujūrakum means your wages are given fully. In every case, the central meaning of not falling short and bringing to completion remains.

Nearby Roots

W F Y differs from several nearby roots. T M M is close to completion from the angle of parts being finished; wafā emphasizes fully rendering a right or commitment. K M L points to perfection and being complete; wafā concerns bringing a right to practical completion. ʿAhd is the commitment itself; wafā is fully rendering that commitment. ʿAqd is tying and binding a covenant; wafā is keeping and rendering it. Ṣidq is harmony with truth; wafā shows ṣidq in the field of covenant and action. Qisṭ is justice in measure and share; wafā is one practical form of qisṭ because it does not reduce a right. Bakhs is reducing and giving less than the right; it is the opposite of fulfillment in transaction and people's rights. Ṭ F F is shortchanging and falling short in measure and weight; wafā is giving that right fully.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

In Forqan translation, W F Y must be translated according to context. Awfū bi-al-ʿahd is best rendered "fulfill the covenant completely" or "render the covenant fully." Awfū al-kayl wa-al-mīzān means "render the measure and balance fully." Yūfūn bi-al-nadhr means "they fulfill the vow completely." Tuwaffawna ujūrakum means "your rewards will be given fully." Tawaffā al-anfus means "He takes the selves fully." Tawaffanī musliman means "Take me fully in a state of submission." Mutawaffīka about Jesus is best translated "I am taking you fully," not rushed into "I am causing you to die," unless it is explained contextually and with caution.

Linguistic Summary

The root W F Y indicates completing, bringing to full completion, fully rendering a right, and not falling short. In the Qur'an, this root expands from fulfilling covenant and vow to giving full measure and weight, from receiving full recompense to taking the self fully in death and sleep, and from the fulfillment of Abraham to the fulfillment of The God in covenant. Fulfillment means not leaving something half-done, deficient, or deprived of its right. Tawaffī means taking something in its entirety, not necessarily killing. In ethics, fulfillment is a pillar of trust; in economics, a pillar of justice; in covenant, a pillar of truthfulness; in death and sleep, a sign of taking the self fully; and in the Hereafter, a sign of complete accounting and recompense.
