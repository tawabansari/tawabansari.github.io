Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root Ṣ B AYN (Ṣ B ʿ)

Fingers; the slender branching parts of hand or foot used for touch, grasping, pointing, measuring, and sometimes closing an opening

Important Forms from This Root

إِصْبَع / أُصْبُع (iṣbaʿ / uṣbuʿ)

iṣbaʿ / uṣbuʿ; a finger, or sometimes a toe.

أَصَابِع (aṣābiʿ)

aṣābiʿ; fingers, the plural form used in the Qur’an.

إِصْبَعِيّ (iṣbaʿī)

iṣbaʿī; finger-like, related to a finger, in lexical usage.

بَنَان (banān)

banān; fingertips, a nearby word from another root.

Method note: the Qur’anic word appears in the plural form أَصَابِع (aṣābiʿ). The singular is إِصْبَع (iṣbaʿ) or أُصْبُع (uṣbuʿ). This root does not have a wide Qur’anic field, but its two Qur’anic uses are physically precise.

Central Meaning

The core meaning of this word is a slender, separate member branching from the hand or foot, making precise contact with the outside world possible.

A finger is not the same as the palm. The palm is the open surface of the hand; a finger is a narrow, mobile, precise branch of the hand. A finger can grasp, point, touch, count, press, or close the entrance of something.

Material Image

The material image is a hand branching from one palm into several narrow members. These branches work together, but each has its own movement and role. This is why fingers are instruments of precision: with them we touch, hold, point, count, press, and sometimes close an opening.

In the two Qur’anic uses, the finger appears exactly in that final role: an instrument used to close the way of hearing.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, إِصْبَع (iṣbaʿ) is used for a finger of the hand and sometimes for a toe. In practical life, the finger was an instrument of touching, grasping, pointing, counting, and rough measurement. The width of a finger could serve as a simple measure, and fingers were also central in bodily gestures and immediate reactions.

In some Arabic usage, having a “finger” in a matter can suggest direct involvement in it. That is a secondary figurative use. The core meaning remains the slender branching member of the body.

Qur’anic Usage

Fingers in Ears in the Parable of Ṣayyib

Surat al-Baqarah says: يَجْعَلُونَ أَصَابِعَهُمْ فِي آذَانِهِم مِّنَ الصَّوَاعِقِ حَذَرَ الْمَوْتِ (yajʿalūna aṣābiʿahum fī ādhānihim mina al-ṣawāʿiqi ḥadhara al-mawti); they put their fingers in their ears because of the thunderbolts, fearing death 2:19.

Physically, a person does not put the whole finger into the ear. Usually the fingertip, or part of the finger, enters or presses against the ear. But the Qur’an says أَصَابِعَهُمْ (aṣābiʿahum), their fingers. The expression makes the reaction concrete and intense: fear is so strong that the whole capacity of the hand seems to be used to shut the ears.

Here, the finger is not an instrument of hearing. It is an instrument for blocking the way of hearing.

Fingers in Ears in the Call of Noah

Surat Nūḥ says: جَعَلُوا أَصَابِعَهُمْ فِي آذَانِهِمْ وَاسْتَغْشَوْا ثِيَابَهُمْ (jaʿalū aṣābiʿahum fī ādhānihim wastaghshaw thiyābahum); they put their fingers in their ears and covered themselves with their garments 71:7.

In al-Baqarah, the background is fear of thunderbolts and death. In Surat Nūḥ, the background is refusal to hear the call. So in one context, the finger marks a frightened bodily reaction; in the other, it marks deliberate refusal to listen.

In both cases, the human body reacts to sound. But instead of receiving, the body turns toward blockage.

Structural Note

In both verses, the structure is stable: أَصَابِع + آذَان (aṣābiʿ + ādhān), fingers with ears. This pairing shows that the Qur’an is emphasizing a bodily member and a physical action, not only an inner mental state.

The refusal in these verses is not only inward. The body participates in it. The human being closes the route of hearing with his own hand.

Difference from Nearby Words

Y D is the whole hand and can indicate power, action, and control. إِصْبَع (iṣbaʿ) is the narrow, precise part of the hand. K F F is the palm, the open surface of the hand; the finger is the separate mobile branch.

بَنَان (banān) is closer to fingertips and the delicate end of the finger. أَصَابِع (aṣābiʿ) names the fingers as complete members. U DH N is the ear, the organ of receiving sound. In these two verses, the finger becomes the tool used to block the ear.

Ṣ M M means closedness or impenetrability toward hearing. But جَعْلُ الأَصَابِعِ فِي الآذَان (jaʿlu al-aṣābiʿi fī al-ādhān) is the physical image of producing that closure: the human being closes the route of hearing himself.

Modern Human Note

The finger is still one of the human being’s main tools of direct contact with the world. With fingers we touch, sign, point, press a button, open a screen, and sometimes push sound away from the ear.

In this sense, the finger can be an instrument of reception, but it can also become an instrument of blocking reception.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

إِصْبَع / أُصْبُع (iṣbaʿ / uṣbuʿ): finger.

أَصَابِع (aṣābiʿ): fingers.

يَجْعَلُونَ أَصَابِعَهُمْ فِي آذَانِهِمْ (yajʿalūna aṣābiʿahum fī ādhānihim): they put their fingers in their ears; or they press their fingers into their ears out of fear.

مِنَ الصَّوَاعِقِ حَذَرَ الْمَوْتِ (mina al-ṣawāʿiqi ḥadhara al-mawti): because of the thunderbolts, fearing death.

جَعَلُوا أَصَابِعَهُمْ فِي آذَانِهِمْ (jaʿalū aṣābiʿahum fī ādhānihim): they put their fingers in their ears; or they pressed their fingers into their ears so they would not hear.

Summary

The root Ṣ B AYN (Ṣ B ʿ) appears in the Qur’an through a fully bodily and concrete image. Fingers are tools of precise contact with the world. But in the two Qur’anic uses, that tool of contact becomes a tool of blockage.

The human being closes the path of hearing with his own fingers. So أَصَابِع (aṣābiʿ) in this entry is not merely the name of a body part. It is an image of human precision and agency: the same capacity that can help reception may, in fear or refusal, close reception off.
