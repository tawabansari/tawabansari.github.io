Qur’anic Root Ḥ Ṣ D

Qur’anic and lexical forms

حَصَدْتُمْ
(ḥaṣadttum)
Yūsuf 12:47; second-person plural past, form I

حَصَادِهِ
(ḥaṣādihi)
Al-Anʿām 6:141; genitive verbal noun with a pronoun

الْحَصِيدِ
(al-ḥaṣīdi)
Qāf 50:9; genitive in ḥabb al-ḥaṣīd

حَصِيدًا
(ḥaṣīdan)
Yūnus 10:24; accusative in the image of land laid low

وَحَصِيدٌ
(wa-ḥaṣīdun)
Hūd 11:100; nominative, contrasting with qāʾim

حَصِيدًا
(ḥaṣīdan)
Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:15; accusative in an image of human destruction

مُسْتَحْصَد
(mustaḥṣad)

From reaping a field to the removal of flourishing life and settlements

The Arabic root ح ص د is best known for cutting and reaping plants. حَصَدَ is used when wheat stalks or other plants are cut and harvested from the ground. حَصَاد refers to harvesting or its time, and حَصِيد to what has been reaped or is a crop for reaping.

This family is not confined to fields. Just as standing stalks fall when reaped, حَصِيد also supplies an image for people’s destruction, settlements’ devastation, and the end of a flourishing condition.

A separate branch concerns firmness and compactness, as in a tightly twisted rope. Dictionaries record it, but it does not occur in the Qur’an.

The Qur’anic forms are حَصَدْتُمْ in Yūsuf 12:47, حَصَادِهِ in Al-Anʿām 6:141, and حَصِيد in Yūnus 10:24, Hūd 11:100, Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:15, and Qāf 50:9.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: six occurrences in three morphological categories

1. Semantic bases and early Arabic usage

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris gives ح ص د two distinct bases: cutting something and making it firm. He regards them as different rather than forcing both into one image.

Ibn Fāris, Maqāyīs al-Lughah: cutting and making firm

Cutting and reaping

حَصَدَ الزَّرْعَ means reaping a crop. In agricultural language, this usually involves cutting plant stalks, especially grain crops, at harvest.

حَصْد is the principal verbal noun, expressing reaping. حَصَاد and حِصَاد also denote harvesting, harvest time, and sometimes the harvested crop.

Recorded derivatives include حَاصِد, a reaper; مِحْصَد, a sickle; حَصِيد, reaped; and حَصِيدَة, a harvested field.

أَحْصَدَ and اسْتَحْصَدَ also describe a crop whose time for reaping has arrived. Ripening, the act of reaping, and the resulting condition are therefore distinguishable.

In broader uses, حَصَاد can name the harvested produce. Some lexical accounts also apply it to tree produce and seeds scattered after plants dry out.

From cutting plants to destroying people

An early use describes killing people through an image drawn from reaping.

A line attributed to Al-Aʿshā reads:

قَالُوا الْبَقِيَّةَ وَالْهِنْدِيُّ يَحْصُدُهُمْ
وَلَا بَقِيَّةَ إِلَّا الثَّأْرُ وَانْكَشَفُوا

qālū al-baqiyyata wa-l-hindiyyu yaḥṣuduhum / wa-lā baqiyyata illā al-thaʾru wa-nkashafū

Its meaning is: they pleaded for mercy and for their lives to be spared while the Indian sword reaped them; but there was no sparing, only vengeance, and they scattered.

يَحْصُدُهُمْ is not literal plant harvesting here. The sword does to people what a sickle does to a field’s stalks. Cutting and felling in succession form the image.

This shows that extending reaping from plants to people’s destruction was familiar in Arabic. The Qur’anic image of people or settlements becoming حَصِيد is thus understandable within the language.

Lisān al-ʿArab: Al-Aʿshā’s witness and people being reaped

Firmness and compactness

The second branch concerns structural firmness rather than cutting.

حَبْلٌ مُحْصَدٌ describes a tightly twisted rope. دِرْعٌ حَصْدَاء is recorded for strong, firmly made armor, and رَأْيٌ مُسْتَحْصَدٌ for sound, firmly established judgment.

A line attributed to Labīd reads:

وَخَصْمٍ كَنَادِي الْجِنِّ أَسْقَطْتُ شَأْوَهُمْ
بِمُسْتَحْصَدٍ ذِي مِرَّةٍ وَضُرُوعِ

wa-khaṣmin ka-nādī al-jinni asqaṭtu shaʾwahum / bi-mustaḥṣadin dhī mirratin wa-ḍurūʿi

That is: I overcame opponents like an assembly of jinn through firm and powerful judgment.

مُسْتَحْصَد describes firm, steadfast thought here, rather than something cut down.

This branch should neither be ignored nor forcibly derived from reaping. The Arabic family ح ص د extends beyond its Qur’anic uses. All six Qur’anic occurrences, however, belong to reaping and its result.

Lisān al-ʿArab: Labīd’s witness and firm judgment

2. Agricultural uses: reaping, harvesting, and produce

Yūsuf 12:47: reaping and storing the crop

قَالَ تَزْرَعُونَ سَبْعَ سِنِينَ دَأَبًا فَمَا حَصَدْتُمْ فَذَرُوهُ فِي سُنْبُلِهِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِمَّا تَأْكُلُونَ

qāla tazraʿūna sabʿa sinīna daʾaban fa-mā ḥaṣadttum fa-dharūhu fī sunbulihi illā qalīlan mimmā taʾkulūna

Suggested translation: “He said: You will cultivate for seven consecutive years; leave what you reap in its ears, except a little from which you eat.”

This is the clearest verbal occurrence. After interpreting the king’s dream, Joseph gives guidance about the years of cultivation and how to preserve the crop.

Three stages are distinguished: تَزْرَعُونَ concerns cultivation; حَصَدْتُمْ reaping the crop; and فَذَرُوهُ فِي سُنْبُلِهِ keeping most of it in its ears.

حصد does not mean removing grain from its ear here. The following instruction shows that the crop can remain in its ears after reaping. Cutting the crop from the ground and extracting and preparing the grain are not one action.

حَصَدْتُمْ is a second-person plural past verb, but فَمَا حَصَدْتُمْ occurs within guidance about future years. A natural Persian rendering can therefore use “what you reap.”

Here the basic meaning is a particular agricultural task, rather than obtaining produce or the results of effort in general.

Al-Anʿām 6:141: harvest time and the crop’s due

وَهُوَ الَّذِي أَنْشَأَ جَنَّاتٍ مَعْرُوشَاتٍ وَغَيْرَ مَعْرُوشَاتٍ وَالنَّخْلَ وَالزَّرْعَ مُخْتَلِفًا أُكُلُهُ وَالزَّيْتُونَ وَالرُّمَّانَ مُتَشَابِهًا وَغَيْرَ مُتَشَابِهٍ كُلُوا مِنْ ثَمَرِهِ إِذَا أَثْمَرَ وَآتُوا حَقَّهُ يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ وَلَا تُسْرِفُوا إِنَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ الْمُسْرِفِينَ

wa-huwa alladhī anshaʾa jannātin maʿrūshātin wa-ghayra maʿrūshātin wa-l-nakhla wa-l-zarʿa mukhtalifan ukuluhu wa-l-zaytūna wa-l-rummāna mutashābihan wa-ghayra mutashābihin kulū min thamarihi idhā athmara wa-ātū ḥaqqahu yawma ḥaṣādihi wa-lā tusrifū innahu lā yuḥibbu al-musrifīna

Suggested translation: “He it is who brought forth gardens, trellised and untrellised, date palms and crops with differing food, olives and pomegranates, alike and unlike. Eat of its fruit when it bears, give its due on the day of its harvest, and do not be excessive; He does not love the excessive.”

حَصَاد occurs in يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ, emphasizing the time of harvesting.

The verse names gardens, dates, crops, olives, and pomegranates. Although reaping grain is the root’s familiar concrete image, ḥaṣād here relates to harvesting a broader range of agricultural produce.

Lexical meaning and the accompanying command should be distinguished. حَصَاد itself does not mean charity, zakāt, taxation, or a fixed percentage of produce. وَآتُوا حَقَّهُ states a separate command, with يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ specifying its time.

وَلَا تُسْرِفُوا is also part of the verse. Understanding the complete command requires considering it alongside giving the crop’s due; excess is nevertheless not part of the root ح ص د itself.

Qāf 50:9: حَبَّ الْحَصِيدِ

وَنَزَّلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً مُبَارَكًا فَأَنْبَتْنَا بِهِ جَنَّاتٍ وَحَبَّ الْحَصِيدِ

wa-nazzalnā mina al-samāʾi māʾan mubārakan fa-anbatnā bihi jannātin wa-ḥabba al-ḥaṣīdi

Suggested translation: “We sent down blessed water from heaven, and through it grew gardens and the grain of crops for reaping.”

الْحَصِيد accompanies حَبّ. Unlike Yūsuf 12:47, which concerns the act of reaping, this verse concerns plants growing through water.

حَصِيد, on the pattern فَعِيل, can have a passive sense: what has been reaped. In حَبَّ الْحَصِيدِ, however, it need not mean grain already harvested, since the verse concerns growing gardens and grain. Reaped explains the passive pattern, but naming a crop or grain for reaping does not necessarily report a completed past harvest.

Al-Farrāʾ treats the construction as adding something to another expression for the same concept. Al-Zajjāj explains it as the grain of plants for reaping, encompassing suitable grain crops generally.

حَبّ الحصيد thus concerns grain from crops grown for reaping and harvesting. In context, it means grain of harvestable crops rather than merely produce already reaped.

Compared with Yūsuf 12:47, this shows that حَصِيد can name a kind of crop through its harvestable character, rather than describing only a condition after reaping.

Tāj al-ʿArūs: Al-Farrāʾ and Al-Zajjāj on ḥabb al-ḥaṣīd

3. Ḥaṣīd as an image of destruction and removal

The other three occurrences do not concern ordinary harvesting. A reaped field supplies an image of destruction, collapse, and the end of a previous condition.

Yūnus 10:24: flourishing life suddenly swept away

إِنَّمَا مَثَلُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا كَمَاءٍ أَنْزَلْنَاهُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ فَاخْتَلَطَ بِهِ نَبَاتُ الْأَرْضِ مِمَّا يَأْكُلُ النَّاسُ وَالْأَنْعَامُ حَتَّى إِذَا أَخَذَتِ الْأَرْضُ زُخْرُفَهَا وَازَّيَّنَتْ وَظَنَّ أَهْلُهَا أَنَّهُمْ قَادِرُونَ عَلَيْهَا أَتَاهَا أَمْرُنَا لَيْلًا أَوْ نَهَارًا فَجَعَلْنَاهَا حَصِيدًا كَأَنْ لَمْ تَغْنَ بِالْأَمْسِ كَذَلِكَ نُفَصِّلُ الْآيَاتِ لِقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ

innamā mathalu al-ḥayāti al-dunyā ka-māʾin anzalnāhu mina al-samāʾi fa-khtalaṭa bihi nabātu al-arḍi mimmā yaʾkulu al-nāsu wa-l-anʿāmu ḥattā idhā akhadhati al-arḍu zukhrufahā wa-zzayyanat wa-ẓanna ahluhā annahum qādirūna ʿalayhā atāhā amrunā laylan aw nahāran fa-jaʿalnāhā ḥaṣīdan ka-an lam taghna bi-l-amsi kadhālika nufaṣṣilu al-āyāti li-qawmin yatafakkarūna

Suggested translation: “The likeness of worldly life is water We sent down from heaven, with which the earth’s vegetation mingled, of what people and livestock eat. When the earth took on its adornment and became beautiful, and its inhabitants thought they had power over it, Our command came by night or day, and We made it like a reaped field, as though it had not flourished yesterday. Thus We explain the signs for people who reflect.”

The earth appears at the height of greenery and beauty. Plants have grown, the earth is adorned, and its inhabitants imagine they can enjoy its produce.

When the divine command arrives, that same scene becomes حَصِيدًا.

Reaping is not a beneficial, planned harvest here. The image centers on the sudden disappearance of greenery and flourishing life. The field is cleared as though it had never flourished.

Rendering فَجَعَلْنَاهَا حَصِيدًا simply as “We harvested it” would therefore misrepresent the verse. حَصِيد describes land deprived of its vegetation and flourishing appearance.

This does not make destruction the word’s meaning everywhere. Here destruction follows from combining the reaping image with the command’s arrival and the loss of flourishing life.

Hūd 11:100: settlements standing and settlements laid low

ذَلِكَ مِنْ أَنْبَاءِ الْقُرَى نَقُصُّهُ عَلَيْكَ مِنْهَا قَائِمٌ وَحَصِيدٌ

dhālika min anbāʾi al-qurā naquṣṣuhu ʿalayka minhā qāʾimun wa-ḥaṣīdun

Suggested translation: “These are some of the accounts of the settlements that We relate to you; some remain standing and some have been laid low.”

حَصِيد directly contrasts with قَائِم.

قائم is still standing, while حصيد evokes a reaped field to describe something no longer possessing its former upright condition.

The verse concerns earlier settlements whose stories have been related and whose remains may still stand or have disappeared.

The main feature is no longer cutting plant stalks, but the removal of something formerly standing and flourishing.

Laid low, devastated, or destroyed suit this context. حَصِيد evokes reaping, but a natural Persian translation need not impose a literal field upon settlements.

The word alone does not establish that no physical trace survives. The verse contrasts standing with being laid low without specifying every settlement’s exact degree of destruction.

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:15: people like a reaped field

فَمَا زَالَتْ تِلْكَ دَعْوَاهُمْ حَتَّى جَعَلْنَاهُمْ حَصِيدًا خَامِدِينَ

fa-mā zālat tilka daʿwāhum ḥattā jaʿalnāhum ḥaṣīdan khāmidīna

Suggested translation: “That remained their cry until We made them like a reaped field, extinguished.”

The preceding verses concern people acknowledging their wrongdoing when confronted by punishment, after the consequences of their actions have arrived.

حَصِيدًا compares them to a reaped field. As reaping fells standing stalks, they lose their former active, upright condition.

خَامِدِينَ adds another image: extinguishing and subsiding. It belongs to خ م د, whose meaning should not be transferred into حصد itself.

The verse places two images together: being laid low like a reaped crop, and being extinguished afterward.

حَصِيد here is an image of collective human destruction, rather than simply harvested produce. That does not make slaughter the root’s meaning everywhere: Yūsuf 12:47 uses it for beneficial agriculture.

4. Qur’anic forms and structural differences

The three Qur’anic forms belong to one family but perform different functions.

حَصَدْتُمْ is a form I past verb. In Yūsuf 12:47, its subjects are people reaping their crop. It expresses a direct, particular act of reaping.

حَصَادِهِ combines the verbal noun حَصَاد with an attached pronoun. In يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ, حَصَادِ is the genitive complement of يَوْمَ and itself governs the attached pronoun ـهِ. In Al-Anʿām 6:141, the whole phrase identifies harvest time, presenting the act as an event with a specified time.

Al-Mughrib, Al-Ṣiḥāḥ, and Lisān al-ʿArab: ḥaṣād as verbal noun and harvest time

حَصِيد, on the pattern فَعِيل with passive meaning, describes what has been reaped. The Qur’an uses it both for agricultural produce and for a condition in which something loses its former upright state.

In Qāf 50:9 it accompanies حَبّ, referring to crops for reaping. In Yūnus 10:24 it describes land after its greenery is cleared. In Hūd 11:100 it contrasts with قائم, and in Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:15 it describes destroyed people.

The shared image is something leaving a standing or growing condition, like a crop. The outcome differs: harvested produce, the removal of flourishing life, or human destruction.

The root’s second branch, firmness and tight twisting, is absent from these Qur’anic forms. Translating any of the six with firmness or becoming firm therefore lacks contextual support.

5. Distinctions from related roots

Ḥ Ṣ D (حَصَد) and Z R ʿ (زَرَع)

Zarʿ concerns planting and cultivation, while ḥaṣd concerns cutting and harvesting the crop.

Both roots occur in Yūsuf 12:47: تَزْرَعُونَ names cultivation, and حَصَدْتُمْ reaping.

This distinction prevents ḥaṣd from becoming a general term for all agriculture. Reaping is one task within cultivation, rather than the entire process.

Study of Z R ʿ

Ḥ Ṣ D (حَصَد) and Ḥ R TH (حَرْث)

Ḥarth concerns working, preparing, or cultivating land. Qur’anic uses also relate it to cultivated land, produce, and even a field of action and the yield of effort.

Ḥaṣd more specifically emphasizes harvesting by cutting and the reaped condition.

The families are close within agriculture, but equating them erases the distinction between cultivation and reaping.

Study of Ḥ R TH

Ḥ Ṣ D (حَصَد) and Q Ṭ ʿ (قَطَع)

Qaṭʿ has a broader range, concerning cutting, separating, and severing many kinds of things.

Within its cutting branch, ḥaṣd is identified with reaping plants. That particular image makes its uses for killing people or laying settlements low understandable.

Every agricultural ḥaṣd is a kind of cutting, but not every act of cutting is ḥaṣd.

Study of Q Ṭ ʿ

Ḥ Ṣ D (حَصَد) and Ḥ S D (حَسَد)

The roots differ only in their middle consonant: ṣād in ح ص د, sīn in ح س د.

ح س د concerns envy and has no derivational connection with reaping or tight twisting. Their written resemblance should not transfer one root’s meanings to the other.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

For حَصَدْتُمْ in Yūsuf 12:47, “you reaped” is a direct rendering. The construction’s future setting also allows a natural Persian rendering as “what you reap.”

For حَصَادِهِ in Al-Anʿām 6:141, “its harvest” or “reaping it” fits. The gardens and different produce make “the day of its harvest” broader and more natural in Persian than a wording confined to reaping grain.

For الْحَصِيدِ in Qāf 50:9, “crops for reaping” suits the context: growing grain, rather than destruction or necessarily an already completed harvest.

In Yūnus 10:24, “like a reaped field” preserves the central image and the clearing of greenery.

In Hūd 11:100, “laid low” or “devastated” suits the contrast with قائم.

In Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:15, “like a reaped field” preserves the image applied to people, complemented by خامدين.

Each verse’s context must guide translation. حَصِيد cannot invariably be rendered as produce, destruction, or slaughter alone.

Conclusion

Brief root meaning: Arabic records two branches of ح ص د: cutting and reaping, and making firm or strong. The first is familiar in cutting stalks and harvesting crops; the second concerns tightly made ropes, armor, and similar structures.

All six Qur’anic occurrences belong to reaping: actual harvesting in Yūsuf 12:47, harvest time in Al-Anʿām 6:141, harvestable grain in Qāf 50:9, and images of removal, devastation, and destruction in Yūnus 10:24, Hūd 11:100, and Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:15.

The central meaning of the Qur’anic branch is cutting and reaping a crop and its resulting condition. This image extends to laying low something formerly standing, growing, or flourishing. Destruction and slaughter are not fixed meanings of every derivative; they arise when the reaping image is used in a destructive context.
