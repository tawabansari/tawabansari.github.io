Qur’anic Root Q R SH

Gathering, earning a livelihood, and the historical name Quraysh

Qur’anic forms

قُرَيْشٍ

Qurayshin

Proper name of the people; 106:1

In classical Arabic, Q R SH denotes gathering scattered things, acquiring property from different sources, and earning a livelihood. Its derivatives also describe people assembling and dispersed groups coming together.

These meanings are recorded in classical Arabic lexicons. This study does not rely on an independently dated pre-Islamic witness to establish their precise antiquity or the origin of the name. The Qur’an uses the family only in the proper name Quraysh, a people known in Mecca at the time of revelation.

In verse attributed to Ruʾba ibn al-ʿAjjāj, a poet of the Umayyad and early Abbasid periods, we read:

قَدْ كَانَ يُغْنِيهِمْ عَنِ الشَّغُوشِ / وَالْخَشْلِ مِنْ تَسَاقُطِ الْقُرُوشِ / شَحْمٌ وَمَحْضٌ لَيْسَ بِالْمَغْشُوشِ

Fat and pure, unadulterated milk could have sufficed them instead of shaghūsh wheat and small ornaments obtained through earnings.

قُرُوش (qurūsh) here is explained as trade and acquisition, not modern coins. This post-Qur’anic witness illustrates the economic branch of the root in the Arabic of that period.

Forms examined: قَرَشَ (qarasha), قَرْش (qarsh), قُرُوش (qurūsh), تَقَرَّشَ (taqarrasha), تَقَرُّش (taqarrush), اِقْتَرَشَ (iqtarasha), تَقَارَشَ (taqārasha), قِرْش (qirsh), قُرَيْش (Quraysh), and قُرَشِيّ (Qurashī).

Qur’anic form: قُرَيْش (Quraysh), occurring once, in 106:1.

1. Principal meaning and lexical uses

قَرَشَ (qarasha) can mean gathering things from different places and adding them together.

قَرَشَ لِعِيَالِهِ (qarasha li-ʿiyālihi) means earning a livelihood and providing for one’s dependants. اِقْتَرَشَ (iqtarasha) and تَقَرَّشَ (taqarrasha) likewise occur for acquiring and gathering property.

Socially, تَقَرَّشَ الْقَوْمُ (taqarrasha l-qawmu) describes a people assembling.

The family also describes things intermingling: تَقَارَشَتِ الرِّمَاحُ (taqārashati l-rimāḥu), for example, describes spears crossing or meeting in battle.

قِرْش (qirsh) is also recorded as the name of a marine animal; in modern Arabic it denotes a shark.

Lexicons also record tale-bearing and stirring discord. The historical relationship between all these branches is not established.

2. Why were Quraysh called Quraysh?

Morphologically, قُرَيْش (Quraysh) has the fuʿayl pattern, one of the Arabic diminutive patterns. Its form alone does not establish the historical reason for the name.

Historical and lexical sources offer several accounts of its origin.

Gathering after dispersion

One account connects Quraysh with تَقَرُّش (taqarrush), “gathering together.”

Historical reports credit Quṣayy ibn Kilāb with assembling dispersed groups of this people in Mecca and associate him with the epithet Mujammiʿ, “gatherer.” This is a transmitted account, not independent proof of the name’s origin.

This account connects the name with the gathering of dispersed groups and the formation of a community around the Kaaba.

Livelihood and trade

Another account connects the name with acquiring property, trading, and providing a livelihood.

On this account, commercial activity and journeys to obtain necessities explain the people’s designation by a name from Q R SH.

The Qur’anic reference to their winter and summer journeys is compatible with this historical setting, but the journeys alone do not prove the name’s derivation.

A marine animal

Another account derives Quraysh from the name of a marine animal described in Arabic sources as powerful and dominant over other sea creatures.

This explanation associates the people’s name with their power and influence among other tribes.

Other reports connect the name with an ancestor or a person responsible for their caravans.

These differing accounts distinguish two questions: the lexical meanings of the family and the historical origin of the name. Gathering and earning a livelihood illuminate the word family, but establishing the naming event requires further historical evidence.

3. Quraysh in the Qur’an

The family’s sole Qur’anic occurrence is at the opening of Surah Quraysh:

لِإِيلَافِ قُرَيْشٍ

Li-īlāfi Quraysh.

For the accustomed association of Quraysh,

Quraysh 106:1

إِيلَافِهِمْ رِحْلَةَ الشِّتَاءِ وَالصَّيْفِ

Īlāfihim riḥlata l-shitāʾi wa-l-ṣayf.

Their accustomed association with the journey of winter and summer,

Quraysh 106:2

فَلْيَعْبُدُوا رَبَّ هَٰذَا الْبَيْتِ

Fa-l-yaʿbudū rabba hādhā l-bayt.

So let them serve the Lord of this House,

Quraysh 106:3

الَّذِي أَطْعَمَهُمْ مِنْ جُوعٍ وَآمَنَهُمْ مِنْ خَوْفٍ

Alladhī aṭʿamahum min jūʿin wa-āmanahum min khawf.

Who fed them against hunger and secured them against fear.

Quraysh 106:4

In the first verse, قُرَيْشٍ (Qurayshin) is a proper name in the genitive construction with إِيلَاف (īlāf). The plural pronouns in the following verses refer to the people of Quraysh.

The surah places three aspects of their life together: accustomed seasonal journeys, access to food, and security.

Historical and exegetical reports commonly identify Yemen as the winter destination and Syria as the summer destination. The Qur’an itself names neither destination; these details should remain distinct from the verse translation.

Where agricultural resources are limited, travel and access to outside supplies can play an important role in sustaining a population.

In Ibrāhīm 14:37, Abraham speaks in prayer of settling some of his descendants in an uncultivated valley near The God’s sacred House and asks for provision for them.

In al-Baqara 2:126, the land’s security and its inhabitants’ provision are also subjects of Abraham’s prayer.

Surah Quraysh recalls these two blessings together: relief from hunger and security against fear.

The relationship with Surah al-Fīl

Grammatical explanations differ over what the initial lām in لِإِيلَافِ (li-īlāfi) relates to.

One analysis connects it to the command in 106:3: because of this association and provision, they should serve the Lord of this House.

Another connects the opening to the end of Surah al-Fīl, relating the repulsion of the Companions of the Elephant to preserving Quraysh’s circumstances and association.

An analysis expressing wonder at Quraysh’s association is also recorded.

These are grammatical interpretations, not necessarily differences in recitation. In each, Quraysh remains the same people’s proper name; none by itself establishes its historical derivation.

4. Quraysh: blessings or hereditary privilege?

The surah’s subject is not their superiority by descent or ethnicity. It recalls their blessings and calls them to serve the Lord of the House in response.

Living near The God’s House, enjoying security, and possessing economic resources do not remove ethical responsibility.

Elsewhere the Qur’an says:

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ إِنَّا خَلَقْنَاكُمْ مِنْ ذَكَرٍ وَأُنْثَىٰ وَجَعَلْنَاكُمْ شُعُوبًا وَقَبَائِلَ لِتَعَارَفُوا ۚ إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ أَتْقَاكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ خَبِيرٌ

Yā ayyuhā l-nāsu innā khalaqnākum min dhakarin wa-unthā wa-jaʿalnākum shuʿūban wa-qabāʾila li-taʿārafū. Inna akramakum ʿinda llāhi atqākum. Inna llāha ʿalīmun khabīr.

O people, We created you from a male and a female and made you peoples and tribes so that you might know one another. Indeed, the most honored of you before The God is the one most mindful in taqwa. Indeed, The God is Knowing, Aware.

Al-Hujurat 49:13

Quraysh is one of these human tribes. Descent from it alone does not demonstrate virtue or an exclusive right to rule others.

In Surah Quraysh, blessing is accompanied by responsibility, not hereditary privilege.

5. Comparison with neighboring roots

Q R SH (qarsh) and J M ʿ (jamʿ)

Jamʿ means gathering people or things and ending their dispersion.

Qarsh overlaps with this field, while its lexical uses highlight acquiring things from different sources and gathering property for a livelihood.

Since the Qur’an does not use qarsh as a verb or verbal noun, its Qur’anic occurrence cannot establish an independent semantic boundary between the two roots.

Q R SH (qarsh) and K S B (kasb)

Kasb concerns acquiring something and obtaining a result from human action.

The economic branch of qarsh concerns earning a livelihood and gathering property.

K S B has a much broader Qur’anic range, encompassing material gains, good deeds, wrongdoing, and responsibility. Q R SH does not have that range of Qur’anic uses.

Q R SH (taqarrush) and A L F (īlāf)

Social taqarrush concerns assembling people, whereas īlāf concerns familiarity, habituation, and establishing association or connection.

People can gather in one place without establishing harmony or familiarity.

In Surah Quraysh, īlāf expresses their relation to the winter and summer journeys; Quraysh names the people who enjoyed that condition.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

قُرَيْش (Quraysh) is a proper name and should remain “Quraysh” in translation.

Rendering it “traders,” “those gathered together,” or “sharks” would substitute one proposed derivation for the Qur’anic proper name.

Brief root meaning: Gathering scattered things, acquiring property and earning a livelihood, and people assembling; in the Qur’an, the family occurs only as the proper name Quraysh.

Meaning in Quraysh 106:1: The name of the people known in Mecca whose accustomed winter and summer journeys, provision, and security the surah recalls.

Conclusion

Arabic Q R SH concerns gathering, assembly, and earning a livelihood. The precise origin of the name Quraysh remains uncertain, with several transmitted explanations.

The Qur’an uses the family only as this people’s proper name. Lexical meanings should therefore not replace their historical name in translation.

Surah Quraysh connects livelihood and security with responsibility before the Lord. Its emphasis is on recognizing blessings and serving their source, rather than one people’s superiority over others.
