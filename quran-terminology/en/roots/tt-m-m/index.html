Core meaning: rising, filling, encompassing, and overwhelming something until it is covered or overshadowed.

1. The root in early Arabic

The triliteral root ط م م is a doubled Arabic root: its second and third consonants are identical and appear with a shaddah in طَمَّ.

Early uses reveal two related meanings: filling or becoming full until a surface is covered or levelled; and rising or increasing until something prevails over other things.

Abundant water and flooding illustrate this relationship. Water that rises beyond its usual bounds covers ground or depressions, submerging what was visible. Filling a well or pit with earth presents a related image: an empty space is filled until its surface is level with its surroundings.

A verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet ʿAlqamah ibn ʿAbadah, also cited in Arabic lexicography, reads:

تَسْقِي مَذَانِبَ قَدْ زَالَتْ عَصِيفَتُهَا

tasqī madhāniba qad zālat ʿaṣīfatuhā

حَدُورُهَا مِنْ أَتِيِّ الْمَاءِ مَطْمُومُ

ḥadūruhā min atiyyi al-māʾi maṭmūm

It waters channels whose crop foliage has gone; their lower ground is covered by the incoming water.

The verse depicts water channels and ground covered by flowing water, with the lower slopes filled or submerged. مَطْمُوم is the root's passive participle and describes this state of being filled or covered.

Some transmitted versions differ in their wording. The quoted wording is attested in lexicographical citation under عصف. Its attribution to an early poet supports the material use of the root, although the date of composition of this particular verse is not independently established.

Before considering Quranic usage, we can therefore identify being filled, covered, or engulfed by water or another substance in the early language of this family. Dictionaries also record the broader sense of a great matter prevailing over others; the antiquity of every branch cannot, however, be inferred from this one verse.

2. The word family and extensions of meaning

Recorded forms include طَمَّ, يَطِمُّ, يَطُمُّ, طَمّ, طُمُوم, طَامّ, طَامَّة, and مَطْمُوم.

Their principal uses fall into several branches.

Filling and covering: طَمَّ البِئْرَ means filling a well with earth and levelling it. طَمَّ الإِنَاءَ means filling a vessel to its utmost capacity or above its rim. The latter is transitive: it describes filling the vessel, rather than the vessel itself overflowing. These uses concern filling a defined space.

Rising and encompassing: in طَمَّ المَاءُ or طَمَّ البَحْرُ, water becomes abundant, rises, and engulfs what lies in its course. It may thus overrun its former bounds.

Increase and predominance: طَمَّ الأَمْرُ describes a matter that has become so great or pervasive that it prevails over other matters. Neither water nor a physical covering is necessary here; increase and predominance are central.

From this branch, الطَّامَّة denotes a great event or calamity whose severity and magnitude surpass other events.

The recorded expression مَا مِنْ طَامَّةٍ إِلَّا وَفَوْقَهَا طَامَّةٌ conveys a similar idea: above every great calamity there may be a greater one. It shows the word's broader use in classical Arabic, though the expression's precise date of origin is uncertain.

Dictionaries record other uses, such as طَمَّ الشَّعْرَ for cutting or shaving hair and طَمَّ الفَرَسُ for a horse moving swiftly. The first relates to shortening and levelling, while the historical connection between speed and covering is less evident. These senses should neither be forced into one image nor transferred to the Quranic use without evidence.

3. Quranic usage

The root occurs once in the Quran, in the form الطَّامَّةُ. The verb طَمَّ, its verbal nouns, and the other lexical derivatives do not occur in the Quranic text.

الطَّامَّةُ الْكُبْرَىٰ: the overwhelming great event

فَإِذَا جَاءَتِ الطَّامَّةُ الْكُبْرَىٰ

fa-idhā jāʾati al-ṭāmmatu al-kubrā

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:34

Then, when the greatest calamity comes.

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:34

Morphologically, الطَّامَّة is the feminine of طَامّ, on the pattern فَاعِلَة. Used as a noun, it denotes an event or calamity that overwhelms other events and overshadows them in magnitude.

The adjective الْكُبْرَىٰ belongs to a different root, ك ب ر. Here it conveys the superlative degree of greatness. The two words contribute differently: الطامة conveys overwhelming magnitude, while الكبرى intensifies its rank as the greatest.

In the sentence, الطَّامَّةُ is the subject of جَاءَتْ and الْكُبْرَىٰ qualifies it. The past-tense verb جاءت refers to a future event in the construction introduced by فإذا.

The identity of this overwhelming event becomes clear in context.

The preceding passage speaks of the creation of the sky, earth, water, pasture, and mountains, and their benefit to humans and livestock. Verse 34 then introduces an event that brings the present condition of life to an end.

The next verse explains:

يَوْمَ يَتَذَكَّرُ الْإِنْسَانُ مَا سَعَىٰ

yawma yatadhakkaru al-insānu mā saʿā

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:35

The day when the human being remembers what they have done.

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:35

The passage continues:

وَبُرِّزَتِ الْجَحِيمُ لِمَنْ يَرَىٰ

wa-burrizati al-jaḥīmu li-man yarā

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:36

And Hell is brought into view for whoever sees.

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:36

The following verses contrast those who transgressed and preferred worldly life with those who feared standing before their Lord and restrained themselves from desire. Al-Nāziʿāt 79:42 also raises the question of when the Hour will arrive.

This sequence identifies الطامة الكبرى with the great event of Resurrection and the disclosure of the outcomes of human deeds.

A flood or a filled pit is not the direct meaning here. Such images help explain the root's semantic range, but the verse concerns an event whose magnitude and consequences outweigh the affairs of present life.

The word الطامة alone does not reveal the physical process of Resurrection, the number of trumpet blasts, or the mechanism of the world's transformation. Those questions require examination of the passages that address them.

4. Related Quranic expressions

The Quran uses several expressions for events of the Hereafter. They may refer to the same event or related stages, but each highlights a different aspect linguistically.

Ṭ M M and Ṣ KH KH: overwhelming magnitude and a deafening sound

فَإِذَا جَاءَتِ الصَّاخَّةُ

fa-idhā jāʾati al-ṣākhkhah

ʿAbasa 80:33

Then, when the deafening blast comes.

ʿAbasa 80:33

الصاخة belongs to ص خ خ and concerns an intensely loud, deafening sound. الطامة, from ط م م, emphasizes an event's overwhelming magnitude and predominance.

The similar construction and continuation of the two passages place both in the context of the Hereafter. This does not make their lexical meanings identical. A deafening sound is not the direct meaning of طامة, just as an overwhelming calamity does not fully convey the auditory sense of صاخة.

Ṭ M M and GH SH W: predominance and covering

هَلْ أَتَاكَ حَدِيثُ الْغَاشِيَةِ

hal atāka ḥadīthu al-ghāshiyah

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:1

Has the account of the Overwhelming reached you?

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:1

الغاشية belongs to the word family of غ ش و and concerns covering, encompassing, and enveloping. This overlaps with part of the semantic range of ط م م.

In غاشية, the act of covering is prominent. In طامة, especially when it denotes an immense calamity, the event's excess and predominance over other matters also play a central role.

Thus “the Overwhelming” for غاشية and “an overwhelming, immense calamity” for طامة can help bring out their different emphases.

Ṭ M M and Ṭ M S: covering and effacing

The root ط م س may also involve concealment in some uses, but its characteristic sense is effacing, removing distinguishing marks, or eliminating something's visible appearance, as with the stars in Al-Mursalāt 77:8.

In ط م م, covering results from filling, encompassing, or overwhelming. In ط م س, the disappearance of a trace or appearance is more prominent. “Effacement” should therefore not be inserted into the basic meaning of الطامة without evidence.

Nor is ط م م identical to ت م م (تَمَّ). The latter concerns completion and coming to an end. Phonetic similarity does not justify merging their meanings.

5. Translation proposals for Forqan

For ط م م, distinguish the basic meanings of the verb, the derivative طامة, and its Quranic referent.

In material uses, appropriate equivalents include “fill,” “cover,” “engulf,” “overflow,” and “level,” depending on the object and context.

In nonmaterial uses involving a great matter or calamity, “increase,” “prevail,” and “overwhelm everything” better convey its meaning.

For الطَّامَّة, “overwhelming calamity,” “immense disaster,” and “a great, prevailing event” each convey part of its range. The Persian equivalent meaning “an overwhelming and immense calamity” naturally preserves the word's two principal aspects.

In الطَّامَّةُ الْكُبْرَىٰ, “the greatest calamity” preserves the superlative force of الكبرى. “The greatest overwhelming calamity” additionally brings out the predominance conveyed by طامة. The context identifies it as Resurrection; the word طامة need not always be translated directly as “Resurrection.”

Brief root meaning: filling, encompassing, and overwhelming to the point of covering or overshadowing.

Meaning in Al-Nāziʿāt 79:34: an immense event or calamity that overwhelms other events; in context, the great event of Resurrection.

6. Conclusion

In its principal branch, ط م م describes something filling, rising, or spreading until it encompasses and prevails over something else. Material uses involve abundant water, a full vessel, or a levelled pit. Nonmaterial uses concern the magnitude and severity of a matter that overshadows other affairs.

The verse attributed to ʿAlqamah ibn ʿAbadah illustrates the material use in early poetry. Dictionaries also record predominance, magnitude, and calamity. Cutting hair and swift movement belong to the broader lexical family but should not be inserted into the Quranic meaning of الطامة without justification.

The sole Quranic derivative, الطَّامَّةُ in Al-Nāziʿāt 79:34, denotes an event whose magnitude and reach overwhelm present life and its concerns. The following verses connect it with the recollection of deeds, accountability, and human destiny.

Final definition: in the principal branch relevant to its Quranic use, ط م م concerns something becoming full or spreading until it covers, encompasses, or overwhelms something else. الطَّامَّة names or describes an event whose intensity and magnitude prevail over other events; الطَّامَّةُ الْكُبْرَىٰ in the Quran refers to the great event of Resurrection.
