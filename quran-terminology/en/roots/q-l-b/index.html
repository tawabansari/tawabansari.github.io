The central meaning of the root

The root ق ل ب (Q L B) is one of the very important roots of the Qur’an, but it is not easy to understand. The basic harm in understanding this root is to translate it only as “heart” and reduce its meaning to human feelings.

In the Qur’an, Q L B is connected, before anything else, with movement, return, turning, overturning, change of direction, and transformation of state.

From here we can understand why the Qur’an calls the human inner center qalb. The qalb in the Qur’an is not a solid and static thing. It is a field of transformation. In his qalb, the human being understands or fails to understand, believes or denies, becomes calm or sick, softens or hardens, turns toward truth or turns back from it.

The central meaning of this root can be understood this way: Q L B is the root of turning, transformation, and change of direction; and the human qalb is the inner center of this moral, faithful, and cognitive transformation.

Qalb and reasoning

In the Qur’an, the qalb is not only a place of emotion. The Qur’an speaks about people who have hearts, yet do not reason with them. In Al-Ḥajj 22:46, it says:

أَفَلَمْ يَسِيرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ فَتَكُونَ لَهُمْ قُلُوبٌ يَعْقِلُونَ بِهَا

Have they not traveled through the earth so that they may have hearts with which they reason?

This verse shows that the qalb in Qur’anic language is connected with understanding, discernment, and responsibility. Therefore the simple translation “heart” is not always enough. Sometimes we must say: inner center, center of understanding, or center of human orientation.

The Qur’anic image of the root

In the sensory image of this root, Q L B means that something turns from one state to another. Something is overturned. A direction changes. A hidden layer becomes exposed.

This image can be seen in the turning of night and day. In Al-Nūr 24:44, it says:

يُقَلِّبُ اللَّهُ اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ

God turns the night and the day.

Here taqlīb is not aimless rotation. It is an ordered passage from one state to another. Night reaches day, and day reaches night. The condition changes, but within this transformation there is order and sign.

When this same root reaches the human being, it becomes moral. The human qalb can also turn. It can become bright or dark. It can accept truth or turn away from it. It can move in a path of growth or suddenly turn back.

Inqalaba ʿalā ʿaqibayh: turning back from truth

One of the clearest images in this branch is the expression انقلب على عقبيه (inqalaba ʿalā ʿaqibayh): someone turns back upon his heels. In Āl ʿImrān 3:144, it says:

أَفَإِن مَّاتَ أَوْ قُتِلَ انقَلَبْتُمْ عَلَىٰ أَعْقَابِكُمْ

If he dies or is killed, will you turn back upon your heels?

This is not an ordinary return. It is the image of a human being who changes his path and turns back from the direction toward which he should have been moving. In Qur’anic language, turning away from truth is an inner transformation that becomes visible in the human being’s outward movement.

The qalb: a field of testing, not a guarantee of purity

The Qur’an does not present the qalb as inherently pure and always trustworthy. The qalb can be the place of faith, but it can also be the place of hypocrisy, sickness, hardness, fear, heedlessness, and denial. Therefore the qalb should not be treated only as the luminous and reliable center of the human being. The Qur’an is more precise: the qalb is a field of testing.

The human being turns toward truth or turns back from truth with his qalb. This inner turning is what builds the human path. For this reason, there is a deep relationship between qalb and munqalab.

The final return of the human being is not separate from his inner orientation. What was hidden in the qalb will one day become manifest in the munqalab.

Main layers of the root

This root can be understood through three layers.

  • The layer of movement and transformation: turning, returning, overturning, changing state, and changing direction.
  • The inner layer of the human being: qalb as the center of understanding, faith, denial, hypocrisy, fear, calm, sickness, and responsibility.
  • The layer of final outcome: inqalaba and munqalab show that every inner orientation leads the human being toward a return and final result.

Suggested rendering in Forqan

This root should not always be translated with one fixed word. The context of the verse must be considered.

  • qalb: heart, inner center, center of understanding, center of human orientation.
  • qulūb: hearts, inner centers of human beings.
  • taqallub: movement, circulation, turning, transformation of state, change of direction.
  • yuqallibu: He turns, overturns, transforms.
  • inqalaba: turned back, reversed, returned upon himself, returned to his outcome.
  • yanqalibūn: they return, they turn back, they return to their outcome.
  • munqalab: final return, place of return, final state of reversal.

Summary

ق ل ب (Q L B) in the Qur’an is not merely the root of an emotional “heart.” It is the root of turning, transformation, overturning, and change of direction.

The human qalb is named from this field because the human being turns inwardly, changes, accepts, denies, becomes calm, becomes sick, and eventually returns toward the result of that same orientation.

In the Qur’an, the qalb is not only something that feels. It is a center by which the human being takes direction. And this orientation reveals the human being’s responsibility before truth.
