Linguistic Note

The semantic core of F W Q is being above in relation to a reference point. That reference may be spatial, as something above one’s head; it may be rank and power; it may be measure and amount; or it may be an example, as in بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (baʿūḍatan fa-mā fawqahā).

Therefore, فَوْق (fawq) must always be translated relationally. Something is fawq because it is above, beyond, greater than, higher than, or dominant over something else. If the reference is ignored, the meaning becomes vague and may be falsely turned into moral superiority.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of F W Q is being above or beyond a reference point. This above-ness is not always higher moral value. Sometimes it is only spatial direction; sometimes numerical increase; sometimes social degree; and sometimes power, dominance, and encompassing authority.

The key question is always: above what, beyond what, or dominant over what? Without that reference, the translation is incomplete.

Material Image

The material image is something located over another thing: a roof above a house, a mountain over a people, clouds over the earth, or a hand positioned above something in power. From this spatial image, meanings of rank, quantity, and authority develop.

In the Qur’an, this image can be calm and natural, as with the sky above people, or terrifying, as with punishment from above. Fawq is neither positive nor negative by itself; it names direction and relation, while context gives value.

Historical and Arabic Usage Note

In classical Arabic, فَوْق (fawq) is used for spatial above-ness and for higher rank. As “above” in English can refer to place, rank, authority, or increase, Arabic fawq also expands from place into degree, intensity, and superiority.

Verbs such as فَاقَ (fāqa) can mean surpassing or rising beyond peers. Even so, this root should not be equated with every kind of moral superiority. Sometimes it only gives direction; sometimes power; sometimes quantity.

Spatial Above: Overhead and Aloft

In Qaf 50:6, the Qur’an says:

أَفَلَمْ يَنظُرُوا إِلَى السَّمَاءِ فَوْقَهُمْ كَيْفَ بَنَيْنَاهَا وَزَيَّنَّاهَا وَمَا لَهَا مِن فُرُوجٍ (afa-lam yanẓurū ilā al-samā'i fawqahum kayfa banaynāhā wa-zayyannāhā wa-mā lahā min furūj)

Here فَوْقَهُمْ (fawqahum) means above them, overhead. The sky is presented as a vast adorned structure above the human being. It is not dominance or moral superiority; it is spatial and visible location.

In An-Naba 78:12, it says: وَجَعَلْنَا فَوْقَكُمْ سَبْعًا شِدَادًا (wa-jaʿalnā fawqakum sabʿan shidādā): We placed above you seven firm ones.

Punishment from Above

In Al-An'am 6:65, the Qur’an says:

قُلْ هُوَ الْقَادِرُ عَلَىٰ أَن يَبْعَثَ عَلَيْكُمْ عَذَابًا مِّن فَوْقِكُمْ أَوْ مِن تَحْتِ أَرْجُلِكُمْ (qul huwa al-qādiru ʿalā an yabʿatha ʿalaykum ʿadhāban min fawqikum aw min taḥti arjulikum)

مِّن فَوْقِكُمْ (min fawqikum) marks the direction from which punishment may come. Its contrast with مِن تَحْتِ أَرْجُلِكُمْ (min taḥti arjulikum) builds an image of encompassing from above and below.

In Az-Zumar 39:16, it says: لَهُم مِّن فَوْقِهِمْ ظُلَلٌ مِّنَ النَّارِ وَمِن تَحْتِهِمْ ظُلَلٌ (lahum min fawqihim ẓulalun mina al-nāri wa-min taḥtihim ẓulal). Fawq here belongs to the space of punishment and enclosure.

Fawq as Dominion and Power

In Al-An'am 6:18, the Qur’an says:

وَهُوَ الْقَاهِرُ فَوْقَ عِبَادِهِ ۚ وَهُوَ الْحَكِيمُ الْخَبِيرُ (wa-huwa al-qāhiru fawqa ʿibādihi wa-huwa al-ḥakīmu al-khabīr)

Here فَوْقَ عِبَادِهِ (fawqa ʿibādih) is not bodily location. The God is not being placed in a physical location above servants. The phrase expresses qahr, dominance, governance, and encompassing power. The word الْقَاهِرُ (al-qāhir) makes the meaning clear.

The same structure appears in Al-An'am 6:61. For The God, fawq must be understood in a transcendent way: dominion, power, and encompassing authority, not geography.

Fawq in Social Rank or Degree

In Az-Zukhruf 43:32, the Qur’an says:

نَحْنُ قَسَمْنَا بَيْنَهُم مَّعِيشَتَهُمْ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا ۚ وَرَفَعْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ دَرَجَاتٍ لِّيَتَّخِذَ بَعْضُهُم بَعْضًا سُخْرِيًّا (naḥnu qasamnā baynahum maʿīshatahum fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā wa-rafaʿnā baʿḍahum fawqa baʿḍin darajātin li-yattakhidha baʿḍuhum baʿḍan sukhriyyā)

فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ دَرَجَاتٍ (fawqa baʿḍin darajāt) refers to degrees in worldly livelihood and social position. The verse does not turn those differences into inherent human worth. It speaks of distributed livelihood, social degrees, and mutual need.

Raising and Above: The Mountain

In Al-Baqarah 2:63, the Qur’an says:

وَإِذْ رَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ خُذُوا مَا آتَيْنَاكُم بِقُوَّةٍ (wa-idh rafaʿnā fawqakumu al-ṭūra khudhū mā ātaynākum bi-quwwah)

فَوْقَكُمُ (fawqakum) is spatial, but with رَفَعْنَا (rafaʿnā) it also carries the force and seriousness of covenant. The mountain is raised over them, placing them before the weight of the pledge.

In An-Nisa 4:154, it says: وَرَفَعْنَا فَوْقَهُمُ الطُّورَ بِمِيثَاقِهِمْ (wa-rafaʿnā fawqahumu al-ṭūra bi-mīthāqihim).

Fawq and the Layered Heavens

In Fussilat 41:12, the word fawq itself does not appear, but the layered image of the heavens matters: فَقَضَاهُنَّ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ فِي يَوْمَيْنِ (fa-qaḍāhunna sabʿa samāwātin fī yawmayn). The heavens have ordered levels, and the nearest heaven is distinct.

When verses speak of فَوْقَكُمْ (fawqakum), this image of elevation and ordered upperness is active in the background.

Fa-Mā Fawqahā: Beyond the Mosquito

In Al-Baqarah 2:26, the Qur’an says:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَسْتَحْيِي أَن يَضْرِبَ مَثَلًا مَّا بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (inna Allāha lā yastaḥyī an yaḍriba mathalan mā baʿūḍatan fa-mā fawqahā)

فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (fa-mā fawqahā) means beyond it. If the reference is outward size, it may point to something larger than a mosquito. If the reference is smallness and insignificance in human eyes, it may point to something even beyond the mosquito in smallness. Either way, the truth of the example is not bound to outward size.

“A mosquito or what is beyond it” preserves more flexibility than a flat “above it.”

More Than Two: Number and Threshold

In An-Nisa 4:11, the Qur’an says:

فَإِن كُنَّ نِسَاءً فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ فَلَهُنَّ ثُلُثَا مَا تَرَكَ (fa-in kunna nisā'an fawqa ithnatayni fa-lahunna thuluthā mā tarak)

فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ (fawqa ithnatayn) means more than two. This is not spatial height and not moral superiority; it is numerical increase past a threshold.

Beyond a Limit

In Al-Baqarah 2:286, the Qur’an says: لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا (lā yukallifu Allāhu nafsan illā wusʿahā). The word fawq does not appear here, but the verse helps frame “beyond a limit.” When something goes beyond a measure, it is understood in relation to that measure.

Fawāq: Brief Respite or Return

In Ṣād 38:15, the Qur’an says:

مَا يَنظُرُونَ إِلَّا صَيْحَةً وَاحِدَةً مَّا لَهَا مِن فَوَاقٍ (mā yanẓurūna illā ṣayḥatan wāḥidatan mā lahā min fawāq)

فَوَاق (fawāq) should not be confused with spatial فَوْق (fawq). Lexically, fawāq is connected with a short interval, brief return, or small respite. In this verse, the cry has no pause, return, or second chance.

Distinguishing Qur’anic Uses

In Qaf 50:6 and An-Naba 78:12, fawq is spatial. In Al-An'am 6:65 and Az-Zumar 39:16, it marks punishment and enclosure from above. In Al-An'am 6:18 and 6:61, fawq concerning The God means dominance and qahr, not bodily location. In Az-Zukhruf 43:32, fawq marks worldly rank and degrees.

In Al-Baqarah 2:63 and An-Nisa 4:154, fawq is spatial but carries covenantal seriousness. In Al-Baqarah 2:26, فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (fa-mā fawqahā) means beyond the mosquito or beyond the small example. In An-Nisa 4:11, fawq is numerical increase. In Ṣād 38:15, fawāq means no brief respite or return.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

فَوْق (fawq) is usually an adverbial noun and needs a reference: فَوْقَهُمْ (fawqahum), فَوْقَكُمْ (fawqakum), فَوْقَهَا (fawqahā). In each case, one must ask: above what, relative to what, and in which field?

In مِن فَوْقِكُمْ (min fawqikum), مِن (min) marks direction from above. In فَوْقَ عِبَادِهِ (fawqa ʿibādih), when it refers to The God, the context of الْقَاهِرُ (al-qāhir) shows dominance, not location.

In فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ (fawqa ithnatayn), fawq attaches to a number and means “more than.” In فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (fa-mā fawqahā), fawq returns to بَعُوضَة (baʿūḍah) and means “beyond it” in the field of example.

Difference from Nearby Roots

F W Q is close to ʿ L W, but not identical. ʿuluw is height, elevation, or superiority in rank, and it can carry the negative sense of arrogance or seeking superiority. Fawq usually marks direction or relation above a reference.

F W Q differs from R F ʿ. Rafʿ means raising or lifting; fawq names the position above a reference. In رَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ (rafaʿnā fawqakumu al-ṭūr), rafʿ is the act of raising, and fawq is the resulting position.

F W Q should also be distinguished from D R J, K B R, S M W, and T Ḥ T. Darajah is degree; kibr is largeness; sumuw is elevation or rising; taḥt is below. Fawq names above-ness in relation to a reference.

Forqan Translation Method

In Forqan translation, فَوْق (fawq) should not always be translated “superior.” Depending on context, “above,” “over,” “from above,” “more than,” “beyond,” “dominant over,” or “higher in rank” may be more accurate.

For The God, فَوْقَ عِبَادِهِ (fawqa ʿibādih) should not imply bodily location. “Dominant over His servants” or “qāhir over His servants,” with a transcendent explanation, is safer.

In Al-Baqarah 2:26, فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (fa-mā fawqahā) is better rendered “or what is beyond it,” because the verse concerns an example and outward size should not restrict the meaning.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For فَوْق (fawq): above; over; beyond; more than; dominant over, in power contexts.

For السَّمَاءِ فَوْقَهُمْ (al-samā'i fawqahum): the sky above them; the sky overhead.

For وَجَعَلْنَا فَوْقَكُمْ سَبْعًا شِدَادًا (wa-jaʿalnā fawqakum sabʿan shidādā): and We placed above you seven firm ones.

For عَذَابًا مِّن فَوْقِكُمْ (ʿadhāban min fawqikum): punishment from above you; punishment from over you.

For الْقَاهِرُ فَوْقَ عِبَادِهِ (al-qāhiru fawqa ʿibādih): dominant over His servants; qāhir over His servants, with a transcendent note.

For رَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ (rafaʿnā fawqakumu al-ṭūr): We raised the Mount above you.

For فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (fa-mā fawqahā): or what is beyond it; or something above it, in explanation.

For فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ (fawqa ithnatayn): more than two; beyond two persons.

For مَا لَهَا مِن فَوَاقٍ (mā lahā min fawāq): it has no respite or return; it has no brief interval or return.

Summary

The root F W Q in the Qur’an points to being above in relation to a reference point. This may be spatial, as with the sky overhead; directional, as with punishment from above; theological, as with divine dominance; social, as with worldly degrees; numerical, as with more than two; or interpretive, as with what is beyond an example.

The main precision is relationality. No fawq is understood without a reference. One must ask: above what, beyond what, or dominant over what? This prevents every fawq from being turned into inherent or moral superiority.
