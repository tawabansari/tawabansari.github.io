Foundational Meaning

The root ح ك م is connected at its core with restraint, prevention of disorder, making firm, and sound judgment. The common meaning “to rule” or “to judge” is only one branch of the root.

At a deeper level, ḥukm means restraining something from disorder, rebellion, corruption, or displacement from its proper position, and placing it where it belongs. From this field come meanings such as judgment, command, wisdom, firmness, firm verses, and judge or ruler.

In this root, judgment is not merely announcing an opinion. A true ḥukm must restrain disorder, separate truth from falsehood, and return a matter to its proper place. Likewise, ḥikmah is not merely theoretical knowledge; it is the ability to recognize the proper place of each thing and the right action in the right situation.

Important Forms

The verb حَكَمَ appears in the Qur'an with the meaning of judging, ruling, or settling disputes among people.

Form II appears in expressions such as يُحَكِّمُوكَ, meaning to appoint someone as judge or make someone the reference of judgment.

Form IV, أَحْكَمَ, appears in relation to verses being made firm or firmly structured. This branch relates to firmness, precision, and structural soundness.

Form VI, يَتَحَاكَمُوا, means to seek judgment or go toward a reference of judgment.

حُكْم refers to judgment, command, ruling, legal decision, or binding order. حِكْمَة means restraining and ordering knowledge: understanding that prevents error, excess, disorder, and misplaced action.

حَكِيم, when used of God, means the One whose action, command, creation, and judgment are by knowledge, precision, measure, and proper placement. When used of the Qur'an, as in الْقُرْآنِ الْحَكِيمِ or كِتَابٌ حَكِيمٌ, it points to a Book whose structure is firm, whose direction is sound, and whose speech restrains disorder.

مُحْكَمَات and مُحْكَمَة are passive participles from Form IV: made firm, firmly established, and possessing a clear and stable structure.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of ح ك م can be described as “restraining disorder in order to reach the proper place.” The root has both a restraining side and an ordering side.

It restrains because it prevents corruption, rebellion, disorder, and injustice. It orders because through judgment, measure, wisdom, and firmness, it places each thing in its proper position.

From here, the relation between حُكْم and حِكْمَة becomes clear. Ḥukm without ḥikmah may become mere power or command. But Qur'anic ḥukm must be connected with truth, justice, and knowledge. Ḥikmah, if it is merely information and does not restrain action or place matters correctly, moves away from the root's core.

Material Image of the Root

The well-known material image of this root is the restraint of an animal. In classical Arabic, حَكَمَة اللِّجَام refers to the part of a bridle that controls the animal’s mouth or jaw and prevents it from bolting without restraint.

From this image, the meaning of ḥukm becomes clear: a force that restrains raw, dangerous, or directionless motion and brings it into a controllable path.

Ḥukm is the bridle of disorder. Ḥikmah is the bridle of ignorance and haste. A muḥkam verse is speech whose structure is not loose or unstable. Wise judgment is judgment that restrains desire, anger, tribalism, and confusion.

Older Arabic Usage

In older Arabic, this root was connected with preventing, restraining, controlling, judging, and making firm. Its use for the bridle of an animal shows that the original field is not merely issuing law or political government, but preventing corrupt or unrestrained movement and bringing it back to a proper path.

For a final expanded version, if a reliable pre-Qur'anic poetic witness for حَكَمَة اللِّجَام, حَكَمَ الفَرَس, or ḥukm in the sense of judgment and restraint is found, it can be added. Until a precise witness is available, the classical lexical field is sufficient for the material image of the root.

Judgment Between Disputes

The first Qur'anic field is judgment between disputes:

فَاللَّهُ يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَهُمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ فِيمَا كَانُوا فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ

So God will judge between them on the Day of Resurrection concerning that over which they used to differ. Al-Baqarah 2:113

Here يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَهُمْ means that He judges and settles between them. In this use, ḥukm ends dispute and clarifies the place of truth and falsehood.

Another example:

وَأَنزَلَ مَعَهُمُ الْكِتَابَ بِالْحَقِّ لِيَحْكُمَ بَيْنَ النَّاسِ فِيمَا اخْتَلَفُوا فِيهِ

And He sent down with them the Book with truth, so that it might judge between people concerning that over which they differed. Al-Baqarah 2:213

Here the Book is tied to judgment. The Book is not merely a text to be read; it is the criterion of judgment in dispute. From the root perspective, the Book restrains the disorder of disagreement and becomes the reference for discernment.

Judgment must be joined with justice:

وَإِذَا حَكَمْتُم بَيْنَ النَّاسِ أَن تَحْكُمُوا بِالْعَدْلِ

And when you judge between people, judge with justice. An-Nisa' 4:58

This pairing shows that Qur'anic ḥukm is not merely issuing a decision. Judgment must put each thing in its proper place and prevent the displacement of truth and falsehood.

God's Judgment and the Final Criterion

إِنِ الْحُكْمُ إِلَّا لِلَّهِ

Judgment belongs only to God. Yusuf 12:40

Here ḥukm returns to the final right of judgment, legislation, and determination of the criterion. Linguistically, judgment belongs to God because He is the final reference of measure, judgment, and placing things correctly.

This phrase must not be turned into a slogan for human religious power-seeking. The root itself shows that ḥukm means restraining corruption and judging by truth, not human ownership over people's consciences.

Another example:

أَفَغَيْرَ اللَّهِ أَبْتَغِي حَكَمًا

Shall I seek a judge other than God? Al-An'am 6:114

Here حَكَمًا points to a judge or reference of judgment. The text asks: from where is final judgment taken?

Seeking Judgment and Choosing a Reference

يُرِيدُونَ أَن يَتَحَاكَمُوا إِلَى الطَّاغُوتِ

They want to seek judgment from the taghut. An-Nisa' 4:60

يَتَحَاكَمُوا means to go toward a reference for judgment. This use matters because it shows that ḥukm is not only issuing a verdict; people choose a reference of judgment, and that choice reveals their moral and epistemic direction.

Another example:

حَتَّىٰ يُحَكِّمُوكَ فِيمَا شَجَرَ بَيْنَهُمْ

Until they make you judge concerning what has become entangled and disputed among them. An-Nisa' 4:65

Here يُحَكِّمُوكَ means that they appoint you as the reference of judgment. The root idea is entrusting a dispute to judgment.

Hikmah

يُؤْتِي الْحِكْمَةَ مَن يَشَاءُ

He gives wisdom to whom He wills. Al-Baqarah 2:269

Ḥikmah here is knowledge that carries abundant good. It is not mere information or transmitted material; it is understanding that places action, discernment, speech, and judgment correctly.

ادْعُ إِلَىٰ سَبِيلِ رَبِّكَ بِالْحِكْمَةِ

Call to the way of your Lord with wisdom. An-Nahl 16:125

Calling must be with ḥikmah: with understanding of the situation, measured speech, knowledge of the listener, and words free of misplacement, harshness, haste, and disorder.

وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا لُقْمَانَ الْحِكْمَةَ

And We certainly gave Luqman wisdom. Luqman 31:12

This verse shows that ḥikmah is not limited to one people or one later historical corpus. In the Qur'an, ḥikmah is broader and more rooted.

The pairing of Book and Wisdom is also important:

وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ

And he teaches them the Book and wisdom. Al-Baqarah 2:129

And about Jesus:

وَيُعَلِّمُهُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ

And He teaches him the Book and wisdom. Āl ʿImrān 3:48

If ḥikmah is attributed to Jesus and others in these contexts, it cannot be reduced to a later specific body of reports after the Prophet Muhammad. Its root meaning must remain: firm, restraining understanding that places speech and action properly.

Hakim

وَهُوَ الْعَلِيمُ الْحَكِيمُ

And He is the Knowing, the Wise. At-Tahrim 66:2

حَكِيم alongside عَلِيم shows that wisdom is not merely knowing. ʿIlm is knowledge; ḥikmah is knowledge with measure, proper placement, and firm action. When God is called Ḥakīm, His action and judgment are not directionless, disordered, misplaced, or unjust.

About the Qur'an:

يس ۝ وَالْقُرْآنِ الْحَكِيمِ

Ya Sin. By the Wise Qur'an. Ya Sin 36:1-2

The Wise Qur'an is a Qur'an whose structure, direction, expression, and judgment are wise and firm. This does not merely mean that it contains wise ideas; the text itself, in its order and function, restrains disorder and stands firm.

Firm Verses

كِتَابٌ أُحْكِمَتْ آيَاتُهُ

A Book whose verses have been made firm. Hud 11:1

Here أُحْكِمَتْ is from Form IV. The meaning is close to verses being made firm, precise, and structurally sound. A muḥkam verse is not loose, disordered, or disconnected. Being muḥkam means the path of disorder, contradiction, and slippage has been closed in its structure.

مِنْهُ آيَاتٌ مُّحْكَمَاتٌ هُنَّ أُمُّ الْكِتَابِ

Part of it is firm verses; they are the mother of the Book. Āl ʿImrān 3:7

مُحْكَمَات means firm, foundational, and clear verses in the position of reference. In root analysis, being muḥkam relates to firmness and prevention of semantic disorder. These verses are the reference and pillars of the Book.

Within the same field, God's superiority in judgment appears:

أَلَيْسَ اللَّهُ بِأَحْكَمِ الْحَاكِمِينَ

Is God not the most judging of judges? At-Tin 95:8

وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ الْحَاكِمِينَ

And You are the best of judges. Al-A'raf 7:89

No judgment is firmer, more just, or more properly placed than God's judgment.

But human judgment can be exposed to corruption:

وَتُدْلُوا بِهَا إِلَى الْحُكَّامِ

And you cast it toward the judges/rulers. Al-Baqarah 2:188

When ḥukm leaves its proper place, it no longer restrains corruption; it becomes an instrument of corruption.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَهُمْ, the word بين matters. Judgment in this structure takes place between two or more sides and settles dispute.

In يَحْكُم بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ, the بـ in بما marks the criterion of judgment: judging by what God has sent down.

In أَن تَحْكُمُوا بِالْعَدْلِ, the بـ in بالعدل clarifies the quality and criterion of judgment. Judgment must be with justice.

In يَتَحَاكَمُوا إِلَى الطَّاغُوتِ, the preposition إلى shows movement toward a reference of judgment. The issue is not only a verdict; it is orientation and choice of authority.

In كِتَابٌ أُحْكِمَتْ آيَاتُهُ, the passive verb shows that the verses have been made firm; firmness has been placed within the structure of the text.

In آيَاتٌ مُّحْكَمَاتٌ, the feminine plural adjective is attached to the verses themselves, attributing firmness to the verses.

Difference From Nearby Roots

ح ك م is close to ق ض ي, but they are not identical. Qaḍā more often points to completion, final settlement, carrying something out, or a decisive decree. Ḥukm focuses on judgment, restraining disorder, and placing matters correctly.

ح ك م differs from ف ص ل. Faṣl is separating and drawing boundaries between two things. Ḥukm may create separation, but it is not merely separation; it must determine the proper place according to truth and measure.

ح ك م differs from أ م ر. Amr is command or matter. Ḥukm is judgment and criterion-setting. Every ḥukm may contain binding command, but not every command is ḥukm in the sense of judgment.

ح ك م differs from ش ر ع. Sharʿ is the opening of a way and the structure of legislation; ḥukm is judgment and determining the proper place in a matter.

ح ك م also differs from م ل ك. Mulk is ownership, dominion, and having authority over something. Ḥukm is judgment, restraint, and firmness. A king may judge, but ḥukm is not rooted in ownership.

ح ك م differs from س ل ط. Sulṭān is power, proof, or authority. Ḥukm is judgment and firm ordering. Ḥukm without truth and wisdom may become domination, but at its root ḥukm should restrain corruption.

Ḥikmah is not identical to ʿilm. ʿIlm is knowing; ḥikmah is measured knowing that places things correctly. A person may have knowledge without wisdom.

Ḥikmah is also not identical to fiqh. Fiqh is precise understanding and grasping layers of meaning. Ḥikmah is the ability to place understanding and action correctly. Fiqh may help ḥikmah, but ḥikmah is broader than issue-based understanding.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For حَكَمَ in the sense of judgment: “judged,” “made judgment,” or “settled.” Depending on context, “judged” is often more precise and less politically loaded than “ruled.”

For الْحُكْم: “judgment,” “ruling,” “reference of judgment,” or “right of judgment.” In verses such as إِنِ الْحُكْمُ إِلَّا لِلَّهِ, it should be explained as the final reference of judgment and criterion of truth.

For حِكْمَة: “wisdom,” with explanation that ḥikmah means firm, measured, properly placing understanding, not mere knowledge and not a specific later report-corpus.

For حَكِيم: “Wise.” In explanation: the One who acts with firm knowledge, places each thing correctly, and whose action is neither aimless nor disordered.

For كِتَاب حَكِيم or قُرْآن حَكِيم: “Wise Book” or “Wise Qur'an,” with explanation that the wisdom of the text relates to its firmness, measure, and disorder-restraining function.

For أُحْكِمَتْ آيَاتُهُ: “its verses have been made firm” or “its verses have been firmly structured.”

For آيَات مُحْكَمَات: “firm verses,” “firmly established verses,” or “foundational and clear verses.”

For يَتَحَاكَمُوا: “to seek judgment,” “to take for judgment,” or “to refer for judgment.”

For حُكَّام: depending on context, “judges,” “judicial authorities,” or “references of judgment.”

Linguistic Summary

The root ح ك م indicates restraint, prevention of corruption, making firm, and sound judgment. Its material image is the bridle of an animal: something that restrains uncontrolled movement and returns it to a manageable path.

In the Qur'an, this root appears in judgment between people, God's judgment, wisdom, the Wise Qur'an, firm verses, seeking judgment, and references of judgment.

The difference between ḥukm and qaḍā is that qaḍā points more to completion and final settlement, while ḥukm focuses on judgment and restraining disorder. The difference between ḥikmah and ʿilm is that ʿilm is knowing, while ḥikmah is measured knowing that places things correctly and restrains the human being from misplacement, excess, and corruption.
