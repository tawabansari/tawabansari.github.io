Core Meaning

The root أ و ل fundamentally points to being before, being first, beginning an order, and returning or being returned to an origin, reference point, or final outcome. It is not only about being first numerically or chronologically. It can also point to precedence in rank, precedence in position, reference, the beginning of a path, and a matter reaching its truth or final consequence.

Two directions come close together in this root. On one side, something stands at the beginning or at the front of an order. On the other side, a statement, dream, judgment, or event is brought back to a point where its truth, reference, or outcome becomes clear. From this field come both the meaning of أول and the meaning of تأويل.

Important Forms

أول means first, firstmost, initial, or foremost in an order. This precedence can be temporal, rank-based, positional, or connected with leading the way in a movement.

الأول, when it comes about The God, moves beyond ordinary usage and enters the field of divine names and theology of transcendence. In this usage, الأول does not mean “first” in a time-line. The God is not inside created time, created beginning and ending, or limited sequence. الأول about The God means the absolute origin upon whom every beginning, every sequence, every emergence, and every relation of existence depends.

الأولى is the feminine form and can point to the first, the former, a previous stage, or the first realm in contrast with the Hereafter.

الأولون / الأولين points to those who came before, past generations, or those earlier in a historical sequence.

أولو / أولي / أولات means possessors, bearers, or people of a quality. In Qur'anic usage, these forms are not limited to simple material ownership. They show that a group is linked with a quality so strongly that the quality becomes how they are known: أولو الألباب, أولي الأبصار, أولي النهى, and أولات الأحمال.

تأويل is a verbal noun from form II. In the Qur'an it means returning, or the arrival of a statement, dream, judgment, sign, or event at its truth, reference, outcome, or final realization. Ta'wil in the Qur'an is not always verbal interpretation. Sometimes it is the interpretation of a dream, sometimes the objective realization of a sign, sometimes the outcome of a judgment or action, and sometimes the final truth of a matter that has not yet been fully opened to the human being.

آل in Qur'anic usage points to a circle attached to an axis: the al of Pharaoh, the al of Abraham, the al of Imran. It should not always be reduced to blood family. Sometimes it does mean household, but in many contexts it can include the apparatus, followers, attached network, or movement related to a central figure.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of أ و ل turns around precedence and reference. Something that is أول stands at the beginning or at the front of an order. But this beginning is not always numerical or surface-level. Something may be “first” because of rank, role, direction-giving force, or foundational quality.

At the same time, تأويل shows that this root does not look only toward the beginning. It also relates to return and outcome. When a dream receives ta'wil, its hidden or initial image reaches its clear truth or objective realization. When a judgment or action has أحسن تأويلا, it has a sounder return and final result.

So أ و ل can be understood as a root that connects beginning and outcome, origin and reference, precedence and return. أول shows the prior or foremost point; تأويل shows the arrival or return of a matter to its truth and outcome.

Material Image

The material image of this root can be seen in a road, a line, a source, or a flow. In a line, someone or something stands at the front: that one is أول. On a road, there is a point from which the path begins; that point begins and directs the path. In a river, the source is where the flow comes out, but understanding the whole river does not end with seeing the source. The path and the final reach must also be known.

تأويل is like following a flow until one reaches its truth. A dream is first an image; when it is realized in reality, its ta'wil becomes manifest. A judgment first appears as a statement or command; when it is lived out, its outcome and ta'wil become known. A statement may seem clear or unclear on the surface, but its ta'wil is understood when it is returned to its reference, outcome, or final truth.

In the usage of آل, the material image can be a circle attached to an axis. The al of Pharaoh means those who stood in Pharaoh's orbit and whose social and behavioral direction returned to him. The al of Abraham means a circle whose relation and direction are known through Abraham. The type of this relation must be understood from context, not from a fixed translation.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, أول is used for precedence in time, rank, order, position, or initiative. A person can be first in arriving, speaking, accepting, denying, or beginning an action; that is, before others or at the front of that movement.

تأويل in lexical usage is close to returning a matter to its origin, outcome, or reference. For that reason, ta'wil is different from simple verbal explanation. A verbal explanation can clarify the meaning of an expression, but ta'wil usually points to a deeper point: what this statement, dream, judgment, or event finally returns to, or in what outcome it becomes manifest.

Regarding آل, caution is needed. In lexical sources, the precise relation of al to ahl or to the field of أ و ل is debated. The surest path is to center Qur'anic usage: al means a circle attached to a person or axis, whether a household, followers, a social and political apparatus, or a spiritual and historical line.

Awwal as First, Initial, or Foremost

One of the clearest Qur'anic usages of this root is أول as first or foremost in an order:

إِنَّ أَوَّلَ بَيْتٍ وُضِعَ لِلنَّاسِ لَلَّذِي بِبَكَّةَ مُبَارَكًا وَهُدًى لِلْعَالَمِينَ

Indeed, the first House placed for people is the one in Bakkah, blessed and a guidance for all worlds.

Ali 'Imran 3:96

In this verse, أول بيت is not only a dry numerical statement. The context shows that this House has an initial and foundational role in place, blessing, and guidance. Its precedence is connected both with order and with rank.

وَلَا تَكُونُوا أَوَّلَ كَافِرٍ بِهِ

And do not be the first to cover truth regarding it.

Al-Baqarah 2:41

Here أول كافر means becoming a forerunner in covering truth. This firstness is not only chronological precedence; it is also opening the way for a negative stance. One who leads in denial is not merely the first person in a line; he may begin or strengthen a movement.

Al-Awwal as a Divine Name and Transcendent Theology

One of the most important derivatives of this root is الأول, which appears in the Qur'an about The God and belongs to the divine names:

هُوَ الْأَوَّلُ وَالْآخِرُ وَالظَّاهِرُ وَالْبَاطِنُ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

He is al-Awwal and al-Akhir, al-Zahir and al-Batin; and He knows every thing.

Al-Hadid 57:3

When the Qur'an calls The God الأول, this word should not be understood like the firstness of one being among other beings. The firstness of created things is always inside time, sequence, comparison, and limitation. Something is first because something else came after it, or because it stands earlier in a line, a history, or a set. But The God is not inside such a line. He is not a part of the chain of time so that one could say He stands at its first point.

Here we enter the theology of transcendence. This means understanding The God as beyond likeness to created things and beyond the limits of time, place, need, beginning, ending, and measurable scale. Therefore الأول about The God does not mean “first in time.” It means the absolute origin upon whom every beginning depends, while He Himself is not subject to beginning.

The definite article in الأول is very important. أول without the article can be used for any foremost or first thing: أول بيت, أول كافر, أول المؤمنين. But الأول with the article, in a divine context, is no longer an ordinary comparable adjective. It carries the word into the station of what is known, singular, absolute, and without equal. English has no single permanent equivalent for this article in this usage. Phrases such as “the absolute First,” “the origin before every beginning,” or “the One upon whom every beginning depends” can help in explanation, but none of them carries the whole capacity of Arabic الأول in one word.

In the same verse, الأول appears beside الآخر. This pairing is very important. If الأول is reduced to first in time, then الآخر is also reduced to last in time. But the verse speaks about the encompassing of The God over every origin and every end. The God is al-Awwal, meaning before every beginning and the foundation of every emergence; and He is al-Akhir, meaning every outcome, every return, and every end comes to Him. So al-Awwal and al-Akhir are not two points on a time-line. They express the encompassing of The God over all origins and all destinations.

For this reason, translating الأول as “the First” is not enough unless explanation makes clear that this firstness is not creature-like, temporal, numerical, or comparative.

Awwal as Precedence in Faith, Submission, or Stance

In several verses, أول means someone who leads in an existential or faithful stance:

قُلْ إِنِّي أُمِرْتُ أَنْ أَكُونَ أَوَّلَ مَنْ أَسْلَمَ

Say: I have been commanded to be the first of those who submit.

Al-An'am 6:14

Here أول من أسلم does not mean the first human being in history. The context shows that it speaks of the Prophet's precedence in submission to the divine command before his addressees. The firstness is relative and contextual.

سُبْحَانَكَ تُبْتُ إِلَيْكَ وَأَنَا أَوَّلُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ

Glory be to You; I turned back to You, and I am the first of the believers.

Al-A'raf 7:143

Here too, أول المؤمنين must be understood in the context of that experience. Moses is not saying he is the first believer in all human history. After that tremendous event, in humility, return, and renewed faith, he says he is the first of the believers.

This matters for translation. Wherever أول appears, it should not immediately be made absolute and historical. Sometimes أول is first within a people, a mission, a moment, or a particular stance.

Al-Ula and Al-Awwalin as Former, First, or Past Generations

The Qur'an uses الأولى and الأولين for a former stage, earlier writings, or past generations:

إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَفِي الصُّحُفِ الْأُولَىٰ

Indeed, this is in the earlier scrolls.

Al-A'la 87:18

Here الصحف الأولى points to earlier scrolls: writings that came before this message.

فَلِلَّهِ الْآخِرَةُ وَالْأُولَىٰ

So to The God belong the Hereafter and the first/former realm.

Al-Najm 53:25

In this verse, الأولى stands opposite الآخرة. It therefore points to the first stage or former realm: the realm experienced before the Hereafter.

أَفَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَيِّتِينَ ۝ إِلَّا مَوْتَتَنَا الْأُولَىٰ

Are we then not to die, except our first death?

Al-Saffat 37:58-59

موتتنا الأولى means our first death: the death that occurred in the worldly stage. Here أول is about the order of experiencing death, not rank or station.

Ulu / Uli / Ulat as Possessors and Bearers

The forms أولو, أولي, and أولات appear in the Qur'an as possessors, bearers, or people of a quality. This usage should not be confused with أول as first, though it can be said that these forms show a stable relation with a quality.

إِنَّ فِي خَلْقِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَاخْتِلَافِ اللَّيْلِ وَالنَّهَارِ لَآيَاتٍ لِأُولِي الْأَلْبَابِ

Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for possessors of deep understanding.

Ali 'Imran 3:190

أولي الألباب means those who possess lubb. Lubb in Arabic points to the core, kernel, and pure inner part of something. So أولي الألباب are not merely “reasonable people” in a general sense; they are those who pass beyond the surface of things and come close to the core of understanding.

فَاعْتَبِرُوا يَا أُولِي الْأَبْصَارِ

So take lesson, O possessors of sight.

Al-Hashr 59:2

أولي الأبصار does not only refer to people with physical eyes. All sighted people have eyes, but the Qur'an addresses those able to take lesson. In this context, basar is close to seeing with discernment and passing beyond outward appearance.

وَأُولَاتُ الْأَحْمَالِ أَجَلُهُنَّ أَنْ يَضَعْنَ حَمْلَهُنَّ

And those carrying pregnancies, their term is that they lay down their burden.

Al-Talaq 65:4

أولات الأحمال means bearers of pregnancy, women who are pregnant. This example shows that this form can also be concrete and direct, not always epistemic or moral. Each phrase must be translated according to its context.

Ta'wil as Return of Meaning, Outcome, or Final Realization

تأويل is one of the most important forms of this root in the Qur'an. It should not be reduced to “interpretation.” Tafsir is closer to making the meaning of a word or expression clear. Ta'wil in the Qur'an often concerns the return of a matter to its truth, outcome, realization, or final reference.

وَكَذَٰلِكَ يَجْتَبِيكَ رَبُّكَ وَيُعَلِّمُكَ مِنْ تَأْوِيلِ الْأَحَادِيثِ

And thus your Lord chooses you and teaches you from the ta'wil of accounts and events.

Yusuf 12:6

تأويل الأحاديث here is not merely explaining words. Joseph is given the ability to understand the relation between dream, speech, event, and future realization.

نَبِّئْنَا بِتَأْوِيلِهِ

Inform us of its ta'wil.

Yusuf 12:36

Here ta'wil means returning the dream to its final meaning or future realization.

يَا أَبَتِ هَٰذَا تَأْوِيلُ رُؤْيَايَ مِنْ قَبْلُ

O my father, this is the ta'wil of my earlier vision.

Yusuf 12:100

In this verse, ta'wil is not only a mental interpretation of a dream. It is its objective realization. The dream was first an image; now it has reached reality and its truth has become manifest.

Ta'wil in Judgment, Dispute, and Outcome of Action

In Surat al-Nisa, ta'wil comes at the end of a verse about returning dispute to The God and the Messenger:

فَإِنْ تَنَازَعْتُمْ فِي شَيْءٍ فَرُدُّوهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَالرَّسُولِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ وَأَحْسَنُ تَأْوِيلًا

Then if you dispute in anything, return it to The God and the Messenger, if you have faith in The God and the Last Day; that is better and more excellent in ta'wil.

Al-Nisa 4:59

Here أحسن تأويلا does not mean “best interpretation.” The context is resolving dispute, returning to the reference of judgment, faith in the Last Day, and the right result. Ta'wil here is close to outcome, practical return, and a sounder final consequence.

وَأَوْفُوا الْكَيْلَ إِذَا كِلْتُمْ وَزِنُوا بِالْقِسْطَاسِ الْمُسْتَقِيمِ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ وَأَحْسَنُ تَأْوِيلًا

And fulfill the measure when you measure, and weigh with the straight balance; that is better and more excellent in ta'wil.

Al-Isra 17:35

Here too, ta'wil concerns the practical result and social outcome of justice in measuring and weighing. Correct weighing is not only a technical act; its outcome appears in trust, order, and justice.

Ta'wil in Clear and Ambiguous Verses

In Ali 'Imran, ta'wil appears in the discussion of clear and ambiguous verses:

فَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ زَيْغٌ فَيَتَّبِعُونَ مَا تَشَابَهَ مِنْهُ ابْتِغَاءَ الْفِتْنَةِ وَابْتِغَاءَ تَأْوِيلِهِ

As for those in whose hearts is deviation, they follow what is ambiguous from it, seeking fitnah and seeking its ta'wil.

Ali 'Imran 3:7

Here ta'wil relates to the final truth or hidden reference of ambiguous verses. The verse warns that ambiguous passages can become a field of fitnah when a human being, instead of scholarly humility, tries to own final ta'wil and use it according to desire.

The continuation of the verse is discussed in relation to pause and reading. For Forqan's text, the firm point is that the Qur'an distinguishes clear verses from ambiguous verses and criticizes a fitnah-seeking following of the ambiguous. Therefore ta'wil in this context must be approached with caution, humility, and return to the clear verses, not with a claim of absolute ownership over hidden truth.

Al as the Circle Attached to an Axis

In the Qur'an, آل is used for a circle attached to a person or central axis:

وَإِذْ نَجَّيْنَاكُمْ مِنْ آلِ فِرْعَوْنَ يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ

And when We rescued you from the al of Pharaoh; they were subjecting you to severe punishment.

Al-Baqarah 2:49

The al of Pharaoh here is not only Pharaoh's private family. The context concerns an oppressive force, a structure of domination, and a group acting within the Pharaonic order. Therefore “family of Pharaoh” may be too narrow in some places. Depending on context, “al of Pharaoh,” “apparatus of Pharaoh,” or “the circle attached to Pharaoh” may be more precise.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ اصْطَفَىٰ آدَمَ وَنُوحًا وَآلَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَآلَ عِمْرَانَ عَلَى الْعَالَمِينَ

Indeed, The God chose Adam, Noah, the al of Abraham, and the al of Imran over all worlds.

Ali 'Imran 3:33

The al of Abraham and the al of Imran in this verse are not merely simple blood relations. They show a line of attachment, responsibility, purity, and relation to a divine axis. At the same time, no complete theory of blood privilege or religious inheritance should be built from the word al alone without contextual evidence. Al shows attachment to an axis; the type and value of that attachment are determined by Qur'anic context and criteria.

Similar Uses

أول is not the same in every place. In أول بيت, precedence is connected with the House's place and guidance. In أول كافر به, precedence points to taking the lead in covering truth. In أول المؤمنين, firstness is relative and contextual. In هو الأول, firstness is about The God and must be understood through transcendence, not through the time-measure of created things.

الأولى also does not always mean one thing. In الصحف الأولى it points to earlier writings. In الآخرة والأولى it is close to the former realm in contrast with the Hereafter. In موتتنا الأولى it points to the first death.

تأويل is also contextual. In Surat Yusuf, ta'wil relates to the realization of a dream and understanding the outcome of events. In Al-Nisa 4:59 and Al-Isra 17:35, ta'wil is close to the right outcome and a better consequence. In Ali 'Imran 3:7, ta'wil relates to the final truth or hidden reference of ambiguous verses.

آل also should not always be limited to blood family. The al of Pharaoh often includes the Pharaonic apparatus and followers. In some contexts, household may be part of the meaning. Translation must follow context.

Grammar and Structure

In أول من أسلم, the particle من defines the circle of comparison: the first among those who submit. This structure should usually be translated within its context, not as an absolute statement about all history.

In أحسن تأويلا, ta'wil explains the angle of superiority: better in ta'wil, outcome, return, or final result. So “best interpretation” is not precise in these cases, because the context is action, judgment, measuring, justice, and outcome.

In ابتغاء تأويله, the verbal noun ابتغاء shows an active seeking of ta'wil. The problem is not merely understanding; seeking ta'wil is placed beside the intention of fitnah. The structure shows danger in a possessive, fitnah-seeking pursuit of ta'wil.

In آل فرعون, the construct relation creates attachment to an axis. This attachment can be familial, political, social, or structural following. Its precise type must be understood from context.

In الأول, the definite article plays an important role. In a divine context, it moves the word from a general adjective to a known, singular, and absolute name. Therefore الأول about The God should not be translated like أول in ordinary phrases. The English “first” may lead the mind to counting and time, while الأول in Al-Hadid points to the absolute origin beyond time.

Nearby Roots

ب د أ is close to beginning and bringing forth from the start. بدأ الخلق means beginning creation. But أول more often marks the first place or precedence in an order. Bada'a is the act of beginning; awwal is the initial place or rank-based precedence.

ق د م is close to being ahead, being in front, or sending forward. Qadam focuses more on frontness or sending ahead, while أول points to the beginning of an order or the first position.

س ب ق points to preceding or outstripping in movement or competition. In sabaq, the field of race or active getting ahead is stronger. In أول, there does not have to be competition; it may simply mark first place or precedence.

آ خ ر is the important opposite of this root. Akhir means later, last, final, or the next/final stage. Awwal and akhir together form origin and destination. About The God, al-Awwal and al-Akhir must be understood transcendentally, not like created beginnings and endings.

ر ج ع means returning or making return. It is close to ta'wil, but ruju' is the return itself, while ta'wil is returning meaning, a dream, a judgment, or an event to its origin, truth, or outcome.

ع و د is close to coming again or repeated return. Ta'wil, however, is not necessarily repetition; it is more about the disclosure of reference or outcome.

ف س ر is close to making meaning clear and explaining. Tafsir usually unveils the meaning of expression, but ta'wil in the Qur'an can go beyond verbal explanation and reach realization, outcome, or final truth.

ع ق ب / عاقبة points to the end and consequence of an action. Ta'wil sometimes comes close to outcome, but it is not only consequence; it is the return of a matter to its truth or reference.

أهل is close to al. Ahl usually refers to the people of a house, city, religion, or those belonging to something. Al usually highlights a circle attached to a specific person or axis. In some places they come close, but they are not identical.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

أول: first, firstmost, initial, foremost, forerunner; depending on context.

الأول about The God: the absolute First, the origin before every beginning, the One upon whom every beginning depends. In the main translation one may keep al-Awwal or say “the First,” but the explanation must make clear that this firstness is not temporal, numerical, creature-like, or comparative.

الأولى: first, former, first stage, first realm, worldly realm; depending on its contrast with الآخرة and the context.

الأولون / الأولين: those before, past generations, the first ones, earlier communities.

أول من أسلم: the first to submit; the forerunner in submission; the first in this stance and context.

أول المؤمنين: the first of the believers in this station/context; the forerunner in faith; not necessarily the first believer in all history.

أولي الألباب: possessors of deep understanding, bearers of pure understanding, people of the core of understanding.

أولي الأبصار: possessors of insight, bearers of deep sight, people of lesson-taking and discerning vision.

أولي النهى: possessors of restraining understanding, bearers of reason that holds one back from slipping.

أولات الأحمال: pregnant women, bearers of pregnancy.

تأويل: return of meaning, outcome, consequence, final realization, returning truth, dream interpretation; depending on context.

أحسن تأويلا: better in final outcome, better in final return, sounder in result and consequence.

تأويل الرؤيا: realization of the vision, interpretation of the vision, the vision returning to its final reality.

آل فرعون: the al of Pharaoh, the apparatus of Pharaoh, the circle attached to Pharaoh, the Pharaonic order; depending on context.

آل إبراهيم / آل عمران: the al of Abraham, the al of Imran, the household and line attached to Abraham/Imran; with contextual explanation, not merely blood family.

Summary

The root أ و ل points to precedence, being first, reference, and the return of a matter to its origin or outcome. At the material level, this root can be understood through the image of the beginning of a road, the point at the front, the source of a flow, and the return of a matter to its truth or final consequence. In the Qur'an, this root extends from أول as first and forerunner, to الأول as a divine name, to الأولى for a former stage, to أولو and أولي for possessors of a quality, to تأويل for the return of meaning and final realization, and to آل for a circle attached to an axis.

Its difference from بدأ is that bada'a is more about beginning an act, while awwal marks first place or precedence. Its difference from فسر is that tafsir mostly makes the meaning of expression clear, while ta'wil can return a statement, dream, judgment, or event to its truth and outcome. In divine usage, الأول must be understood through transcendence: not first in time, but the absolute origin upon whom every beginning and every order depends.
