Half and division into two equal parts: financial shares, rights, and measuring the night; distinguishing niṣf from fairness in Arabic

1. Introducing the root and its semantic branches

Arabic N Ṣ F has two important branches. The first concerns a half, division into two equal parts, reaching the midpoint, and, by extension, fairness and respecting another’s due. Some older Arabic forms in the second branch concern serving and being a servant.

In the first branch, niṣf is one of two equal parts of a whole. If something is divided into two equal portions, each portion is half of it.

The concept applies to quantities, property, time, distance, and other measurable things.

Niṣfu al-māli means half the property; niṣfu al-layli concerns half the night.

The verb naṣafa al-shayʾa can concern taking half of something or reaching its midpoint.

Naṣṣafa al-shayʾa describes dividing something into two halves; intaṣafa al-nahāru describes the day reaching the midpoint of its duration.

Another use of anṣafa means acting justly and respecting another’s due.

That meaning relates to equality and respecting shares, but is not identical with mathematical division into halves.

Respecting rights may require equal division in one situation, while people’s entitlements differ in another. In that case, fairness means giving each person their due, rather than necessarily dividing everything equally.

Another Arabic branch records naṣafahu and tanaṣṣafahu as serving someone. Their historical connection with “half” is uncertain and should not be derived from equal division without independent evidence.

Thus one of two equal parts of a whole is the core meaning of every Qur’anic N Ṣ F occurrence; fairness, reaching a midpoint, and serving are other uses that require separate examination.

Qur’anic forms

N Ṣ F occurs seven times, in seven verses of three surahs.

All seven instances are forms of the noun niṣf.

VerseQur’anic formSubject
Al-Baqarah 2:237فَنِصْفُHalf the financial due stipulated in marriage
Al-Nisāʾ 4:11النِّصْفُOne daughter’s share in the stated situation
Al-Nisāʾ 4:12نِصْفُA husband’s share of his wife’s estate under a stated condition
Al-Nisāʾ 4:25نِصْفُHalf the punishment in the stated situation
Al-Nisāʾ 4:176نِصْفُA sister’s share in kalālah
Al-Muzzammil 73:3نِصْفَهُHalf the night
Al-Muzzammil 73:20وَنِصْفَهُHalf the night

Despite written differences such as fa-niṣfu, al-niṣfu, and niṣfahu, all belong to the same noun.

Attached fa and wa, the definite article al, and the pronoun hu do not create new derivatives of the root.

No verb of this family, such as naṣafa, anṣafa, naṣṣafa, nāṣafa, or intaṣafa, occurs in the Qur’an.

The familiar words inṣāf, naṣafah, naṣīf, muntaṣaf, munāṣafah, and tanṣīf likewise do not occur in its text.

This matters because in contemporary Persian, inṣāf is especially familiar, and its ethical meaning may dominate people’s understanding of the root. Yet all seven Qur’anic instances have the quantitative, proportional meaning “half”, rather than directly meaning justice or fairness.

2. Niṣf in early Arabic: one of two equal parts

A half: dividing a whole into two portions

One lexical definition states:

النِّصْفُ: أَحَدُ شِقَّيِ الشَّيْءِ

al-niṣfu: aḥadu shiqqayi al-shayʾi

Meaning:

“A half is one of the two portions of a thing.”

This concise definition identifies the basic meaning.

When a complete quantity is divided into two equal portions, each is half of it.

Arabic records niṣf, nuṣf, naṣf, and naṣīf in uses concerning a half, although their frequency and forms differ.

Ḥafṣ’s Qur’anic reading has niṣf with kasrah on nūn.

Mathematically:

Half of a quantity = the complete quantity ÷ 2

This relationship holds in all quantitative uses.

What counts as the complete quantity must nevertheless be identified in each sentence.

Inheritance verses concern a share of property left behind.

The verse about the marital financial due concerns half the stipulated amount.

The punishment verse concerns half the punishment identified as the reference quantity.

In Al-Muzzammil, the reference quantity is the night’s duration.

A half is therefore a definite proportion, while the sentence and context identify what that proportion measures.

Naṣafa: reaching the midpoint or taking half

Arabic uses:

نَصَفَ الشَّيْءَ

naṣafa al-shayʾa

Meaning:

“He reached its halfway point or took half of it.”

Also:

نَصَّفَ الشَّيْءَ

naṣṣafa al-shayʾa

Meaning:

“He divided it into two halves.”

Nāṣafahu al-māla means sharing property with someone in two equal portions.

“Half” is evident throughout, but the verbs and constructions remain distinct.

Taking half the property and dividing it into two shares may approach each other in outcome, without being the same action.

Someone may divide property into two portions without taking either portion personally.

Personal ownership of one portion therefore does not follow automatically from the idea of a half.

Ownership, entitlement, and obligation depend on the sentence or a further ruling.

Day and night reaching their midpoint

Intaṣafa al-nahāru describes the day reaching the midpoint of its duration.

Muntaṣafu al-layli likewise refers to the middle of the night.

Naṣafa al-izāru al-sāqa describes a garment reaching halfway down the lower leg.

These uses show the family’s capacity to identify either a quantity or a midpoint.

Half a duration nevertheless differs from the instant at its midpoint.

Half the night is a duration, whereas midnight can identify the point separating its two halves.

That distinction matters when translating Al-Muzzammil.

Niṣfahu there expresses a duration, rather than necessarily a single instant in the middle of the night.

Pre-Islamic poetry: halfway through the day

The pre-Islamic poet Al-Musayyab ibn ʿAlas describes a diver who entered the sea to obtain a pearl:

نَصَفَ النَّهَارُ الْمَاءُ غَامِرُهُ
وَرَفِيقُهُ بِالْغَيْبِ لَا يَدْرِي

naṣafa al-nahāru al-māʾu ghāmiruhu / wa-rafīquhu bi-al-ghaybi lā yadrī

Translation:

“The day reached its midpoint while water covered him, and his companion, unable to see him, did not know.”

Naṣafa al-nahāru here means the day reached its midpoint.

The example attests a temporal use of the verb.

The poet describes a moment in the middle of the day, rather than dividing property or justice between people.

Lexical explanations attribute the line to Al-Musayyab ibn ʿAlas and cite it for intaṣafa al-nahāru.

The temporal sense of reaching halfway therefore has clear support in early Arabic.

It does not make niṣf invariably the midpoint of time in the Qur’an; most Qur’anic instances concern financial rights and quantities.

3. Fairness: giving what is due without necessarily dividing equally

From a half to fairness

Arabic inṣāf concerns justice, respecting entitlements, and treating people fairly.

A lexical explanation states:

النَّصَفُ وَالنَّصَفَةُ وَالإِنْصَافُ: إِعْطَاءُ الْحَقِّ

al-naṣafu wa-al-naṣafatu wa-al-inṣāfu: iʿṭāʾu al-ḥaqqi

Meaning:

“Naṣaf, naṣafah, and inṣāf in this use concern giving what is due.”

Also:

أَنْصَفَ الرَّجُلُ صَاحِبَهُ

anṣafa al-rajulu ṣāḥibahu

Meaning:

“The man treated his companion or counterpart fairly.”

Fairness here concerns respecting the other person’s due.

Another explanation connects anṣafa with receiving or giving what is due.

The important question is how that ethical meaning relates to a half.

One proposed derivational analysis connects fairness in transactions with accepting half or placing the two parties on an equal footing.

This is semantically intelligible: fairness can involve abandoning excessive demands and respecting reciprocal rights.

The proposal alone does not establish the actual history of the word.

Nor does fairness always mean paying exactly fifty percent to each party.

Fairness and quantitative equality

Suppose two people have equal ownership shares in common property. Dividing it into equal halves can be fair.

If their ownership shares differ from the outset, equal division may violate one person’s actual entitlement.

Fairness therefore depends on recognizing and respecting rights, rather than simply equal amounts received.

Qur’anic legal contexts likewise make shares dependent on relationships and conditions.

For example, Al-Nisāʾ 4:12 assigns a husband half in one situation and a quarter in another.

Fairness therefore does not mean that everyone’s correct share must always be half.

A half is a definite numerical proportion; fairness concerns whether rights are respected.

The two can relate without being synonymous.

Poetry about fairness

Al-Farazdaq, a poet of the Islamic period, says:

وَلَكِنَّ نِصْفًا لَوْ سَبَبْتُ وَسَبَّنِي
بَنُو عَبْدِ شَمْسٍ مِنْ مَنَافٍ وَهَاشِمِ

wa-lākinna niṣfan law sababtu wa-sabbanī / banū ʿabdi shamsin min manāfin wa-hāshimi

Approximate sense:

“It would be fair if, when I exchange insults with the families named in the verse, the two sides of the encounter were of equal standing.”

Niṣfan here concerns fairness and treatment proportionate to the counterpart’s standing.

The subject is not dividing a specified quantity into halves.

The poet speaks of equality or comparable social standing in a confrontation.

The example shows an established Arabic use for fairness independent of numerical division into two portions.

Al-Farazdaq lived after the Qur’an’s revelation, so his poetry attests classical Arabic rather than directly attesting pre-Islamic usage.

His value judgment about families’ standing belongs to the poem’s setting; it should not become a definitive ethical meaning of fairness.

The lexical point is the use of niṣf for proportionate, fair treatment.

4. Other Arabic branches of the root

Naṣaf: middle age

Lexicons record naṣaf, with fatḥah on nūn and ṣād, for someone beyond early youth who has reached middle age.

It is especially recorded for a woman between youth and old age.

Some explanations mention approximately forty-five or fifty years, but these figures are not uniform across the sources.

“Middle-aged” is therefore preferable to assigning a fixed, exact age.

The connection with a half is intelligible: the person has reached a stage beyond early youth.

This does not establish that early Arabs supposed everyone’s lifespan was exactly twice the specified age.

An Arabic poetic example

A verse of uncertain attribution says:

وَإِنْ أَتَوْكَ فَقَالُوا إِنَّهَا نَصَفٌ
فَإِنَّ أَطْيَبَ نِصْفَيْهَا الَّذِي غَبَرَا

wa-in atawka fa-qālū innahā naṣafun / fa-inna aṭyaba niṣfayhā alladhī ghabarā

Translation:

“If they come to you saying she is middle-aged, the better of her two halves is the one that has passed.”

Two uses of the N Ṣ F family stand together here.

Naṣaf describes a middle-aged woman.

Niṣfayhā concerns the two halves of her life.

The semantic example is clear, but the consulted transmission does not identify its poet or composition date.

It cannot therefore be presented unconditionally as securely pre-Islamic poetry.

The poet’s judgment about the value of a woman’s years is also his own view, rather than part of the lexical meaning of naṣaf.

Naṣīf: a covering or head covering

Arabic records naṣīf for a head covering or garment a woman uses to cover part of her body.

Some explanations identify it with khimār; others describe a broader covering worn over clothing.

One early explanation describes it as screening the woman from other people’s gaze.

That explanation does not prove that the name originated from dividing the body into two halves.

Its precise relationship with the quantitative branch requires further historical evidence.

An example from Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī

The pre-Islamic poet Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī says in a well-known line:

سَقَطَ النَّصِيفُ وَلَمْ تُرِدْ إِسْقَاطَهُ
فَتَنَاوَلَتْهُ وَاتَّقَتْنَا بِالْيَدِ

saqaṭa al-naṣīfu wa-lam turid isqāṭahu / fa-tanāwalathu wa-ittaqatnā bi-al-yadi

Translation:

“Her covering fell without her intending to drop it; she reached for it and screened herself from our gaze with her hand.”

Al-naṣīf names a covering that fell from the woman’s body or head.

The line is attributed to Al-Nābighah and is widely transmitted in early literary and lexical works.

The exact kind of covering is disputed: khimār, miʿjar, or a larger garment.

Its precise shape and dimensions cannot therefore be determined securely from the verse.

Nor does naṣīf here mean half of someone’s property.

Forms of the same family can have different established applications.

Naṣīf: a half or a measure

Arabic also records naṣīf for half of a quantity.

In some uses it names a measure.

This concerns quantity and measurement.

A named measure nevertheless differs from half of any quantity.

When naṣīf names a measure, its amount depends on the measuring system in use.

It should not become half a specified modern unit without contextual evidence.

Serving: a distinct N Ṣ F branch

One early analysis identifies two distinct main branches for this family:

أَحَدُهُمَا يَدُلُّ عَلَى شَطْرِ الشَّيْءِ، وَالْأُخْرَى عَلَى جِنْسٍ مِنَ الْخِدْمَةِ وَالِاسْتِعْمَالِ

aḥaduhumā yadullu ʿalā shaṭri al-shayʾi wa-al-ukhrā ʿalā jinsin mina al-khidmati wa-al-istiʿmāli

Meaning:

“One concerns half of something, and the other a kind of service and being employed.”

Arabic records tanaṣṣafahu as serving someone.

Minṣaf and nāṣif also describe a servant in some uses.

This branch differs semantically from a half and division into two equal portions.

Whereas some roots can be explained through a relatively clear shared image, this analysis identifies two separate principles for N Ṣ F.

Serving should therefore not be forcibly derived from division into halves.

A verse attributed to Ḥuraqah, daughter of Al-Nuʿmān

A poem attributed to Ḥuraqah, daughter of Al-Nuʿmān ibn Al-Mundhir, says:

فَبَيْنَا نَسُوسُ النَّاسَ وَالْأَمْرُ أَمْرُنَا
إِذَا نَحْنُ فِيهِمْ سُوقَةٌ نَتَنَصَّفُ

fa-baynā nasūsu al-nāsa wa-al-amru amrunā / idhā naḥnu fīhim sūqatun natanaṣṣafu

Translation:

“While we governed people and commanded affairs, suddenly we became ordinary people among them, serving others.”

Natanaṣṣafu concerns serving or being in a servant’s position.

The poem describes a change of social standing and the loss of rule.

Early lexicons record it, but the historical attribution and exact composition date require caution.

It usefully attests a recorded Arabic application rather than securely dating the emergence of this branch.

None of the seven Qur’anic N Ṣ F occurrences has this meaning.

Other spatial uses

Arabic records manṣaf, with fatḥah on mīm, for the midpoint of a route.

In some uses, nāṣifah identifies an open, broad area of ground or part of a valley’s watercourse.

Sources explain these spatial uses differently.

Some relate more clearly to a midpoint; others are local names or particular geographical terms.

They should not all be defined simply as half a distance.

These forms illustrate the family’s breadth but do not occur in the Qur’an.

5. A half in Al-Baqarah 2:237: the financial due in separation before marital contact

وَإِنْ طَلَّقْتُمُوهُنَّ مِنْ قَبْلِ أَنْ تَمَسُّوهُنَّ وَقَدْ فَرَضْتُمْ لَهُنَّ فَرِيضَةً فَنِصْفُ مَا فَرَضْتُمْ إِلَّا أَنْ يَعْفُونَ أَوْ يَعْفُوَ الَّذِي بِيَدِهِ عُقْدَةُ النِّكَاحِ

wa-in ṭallaqtumūhunna min qabli an tamassūhunna wa-qad faraḍtum lahunna farīḍatan fa-niṣfu mā faraḍtum illā an yaʿfūna aw yaʿfuwa alladhī bi-yadihi ʿuqdatu al-nikāḥi

Translation:

“If you divorce them before marital contact, having stipulated an amount for them, then half of what you stipulated [is due], unless they waive it or the person in whose hand is the marriage bond waives it.”

Al-Baqarah 2:237, excerpt

Fa-niṣfu refers to half the stipulated financial amount.

The context is separation after stipulating the marital financial due but before marital contact.

Half of what?

The expression is:

فَنِصْفُ مَا فَرَضْتُمْ

fa-niṣfu mā faraḍtum

Meaning:

“Then half of what you stipulated.”

The reference quantity is the stipulated amount.

If an amount was stipulated in marriage, half identifies one of two equal parts of that amount.

The word does not itself determine whether the stipulated amount takes the form of money, property, or something else.

That subject comes from farīḍah and the financial setting of marriage.

It does not mean half of either person’s entire lifetime property, but half of what was stipulated in that relationship.

The stipulated entitlement and the possibility of waiver

The continuation states:

إِلَّا أَنْ يَعْفُونَ أَوْ يَعْفُوَ الَّذِي بِيَدِهِ عُقْدَةُ النِّكَاحِ

illā an yaʿfūna aw yaʿfuwa alladhī bi-yadihi ʿuqdatu al-nikāḥi

“Unless they waive it, or the one in whose hand is the marriage bond waives it.”

This introduces the possibility of waiving a right or part of it.

Commentarial explanations differ over the person in whose hand is the marriage bond: some identify the husband, others the marriage guardian.

That dispute concerns authority to waive, rather than the meaning of a half.

The verse also continues:

وَأَنْ تَعْفُوا أَقْرَبُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ وَلَا تَنْسَوُا الْفَضْلَ بَيْنَكُمْ

wa-an taʿfū aqrabu li-al-taqwā wa-lā tansawū al-faḍla baynakum

Meaning:

“Your waiving is nearer to mindfulness; do not forget generosity between you.”

Alongside determining a financial amount, the verse therefore addresses waiver and generosity.

Neither generosity nor waiver is the lexical meaning of niṣf.

A half identifies the stipulated amount, while waiver introduces the possibility of relinquishing an entitlement.

“A fair portion” would therefore lose the exact one-half proportion.

6. A half in inheritance verses: a quantitative share rather than a judgment of human worth

Three N Ṣ F occurrences in Al-Nisāʾ concern inheritance shares.

A half is a definite proportion of property left by someone who has died.

The conditions determining the share nevertheless differ in each instance.

A. Al-Nisāʾ 4:11: the share of one daughter

فَإِنْ كُنَّ نِسَاءً فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ فَلَهُنَّ ثُلُثَا مَا تَرَكَ وَإِنْ كَانَتْ وَاحِدَةً فَلَهَا النِّصْفُ

fa-in kunna nisāʾan fawqa ithnatayni fa-lahunna thuluthā mā taraka wa-in kānat wāḥidatan fa-lahā al-niṣfu

Translation:

“If the daughters are more than two, theirs is two-thirds of what was left; if there is one, hers is half.”

Al-Nisāʾ 4:11, excerpt

Al-niṣfu identifies one daughter’s share in the situation described.

Fa-lahā indicates that the half is assigned to her.

This identifies a quantitative share without independently explaining every inheritance rule.

The remaining estate and other heirs’ rights require considering the rest of this verse and related verses.

Fa-lahā al-niṣfu does not establish that a daughter’s human worth is half a son’s.

The subject is the financial division of an inheritance.

A financial share is different from judging people’s dignity, ability, or moral worth.

The verse also begins by addressing sons’ and daughters’ shares in a specified situation. This cannot become a simple rule that every woman always inherits half of every man in all situations.

The inheritance verses themselves assign different, conditional shares to different heirs.

B. Al-Nisāʾ 4:12: a husband’s share of his wife’s estate

وَلَكُمْ نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ أَزْوَاجُكُمْ إِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُنَّ وَلَدٌ

wa-lakum niṣfu mā taraka azwājukum in lam yakun lahunna waladun

Translation:

“Yours is half of what your wives leave, if they have no child.”

Al-Nisāʾ 4:12, opening excerpt

A half identifies a husband’s share of his deceased wife’s estate.

That share is conditional:

إِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُنَّ وَلَدٌ

in lam yakun lahunna waladun

If the wife has no child.

The continuation assigns the husband a quarter if there is a child.

A half is therefore not the same entitlement in every situation.

This change does not alter the lexical meaning of niṣf.

A half always means one-half; what changes is the condition under which that share applies.

The verse also mentions bequests and debts:

مِنْ بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِينَ بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ

min baʿdi waṣiyyatin yūṣīna bihā aw daynin

After a bequest and debt.

The division of shares therefore belongs within a broader legal framework.

The order and details of carrying out bequests and paying debts do not come from N Ṣ F itself.

C. Al-Nisāʾ 4:176: a sister’s share in kalālah

يَسْتَفْتُونَكَ قُلِ اللَّهُ يُفْتِيكُمْ فِي الْكَلَالَةِ إِنِ امْرُؤٌ هَلَكَ لَيْسَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَلَهُ أُخْتٌ فَلَهَا نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ

yastaftūnaka quli allāhu yuftīkum fī al-kalālati ini imruʾun halaka laysa lahu waladun wa-lahu ukhtun fa-lahā niṣfu mā taraka

Translation:

“They ask you for a ruling. Say: The God gives you a ruling about kalālah. If a person dies having no child but having a sister, hers is half of what he leaves.”

Al-Nisāʾ 4:176, opening excerpt

Niṣfu concerns a sister’s share in a specified situation.

The subject is kalālah, whose precise identification requires independent examination of that word and other inheritance verses.

A half alone cannot define kalālah.

Nor should mentioning “he has no child” establish all legal conditions without examining the Qur’an’s other relevant passages.

The verse goes on to discuss two sisters and a group of brothers and sisters.

A half is therefore one quantitative proportion within the verse’s provisions.

A half and an entitlement

In all three inheritance verses, a half is a definite quantity.

Who receives it depends on the kinship situation and the conditions stated in the Qur’an.

Niṣf should therefore not be translated directly as justice, superiority, or lesser worth in inheritance verses.

Those are separate judgments and questions.

A half here identifies a one-half share of the quantity being divided.

7. Al-Nisāʾ 4:25: half the punishment and the limits of numerical meaning

فَإِذَا أُحْصِنَّ فَإِنْ أَتَيْنَ بِفَاحِشَةٍ فَعَلَيْهِنَّ نِصْفُ مَا عَلَى الْمُحْصَنَاتِ مِنَ الْعَذَابِ

fa-idhā uḥṣinna fa-in atayna bi-fāḥishatin fa-ʿalayhinna niṣfu mā ʿalā al-muḥṣanāti mina al-ʿadhābi

Translation:

“When they enter the state of iḥṣān, if they commit an indecency, theirs is half the punishment assigned to the muḥṣanāt.”

Al-Nisāʾ 4:25, excerpt

This is particularly important for understanding the word’s precision.

The reference quantity is punishment, rather than property or time.

The reference quantity

The expression is:

نِصْفُ مَا عَلَى الْمُحْصَنَاتِ مِنَ الْعَذَابِ

niṣfu mā ʿalā al-muḥṣanāti mina al-ʿadhābi

Meaning:

“Half of what is assigned to the muḥṣanāt as punishment.”

The verse itself specifies a one-half proportion.

Determining the final amount requires identifying the punishment meant by al-ʿadhāb and the people meant by al-muḥṣanāt in this passage.

Those questions involve other roots and the verse’s context.

The verse begins with fatayātikumu al-muʾmināti in the setting of mā malakat aymānukum and marrying them.

It then introduces iḥṣān and committing an indecency as conditions.

A half is therefore part of a conditional ruling, rather than a freely generalizable proportion for every punishment.

Can every kind of punishment be halved?

Logically, halving requires a reference quantity that permits quantitative division.

For example, halving is numerically conceivable if a specified ruling sets a particular number of strokes.

A punishment whose nature does not permit division into two quantitative portions raises a different interpretive and legal question in relation to “half.”

Early commentaries discuss that point when examining a punishment capable of being halved.

Identifying the punishment and its conditions nevertheless requires independent study of the verses concerning indecency, iḥṣān, and punishment.

Its kind or manner of execution should not be extracted from N Ṣ F alone.

The secure lexical point is that a half means one-half of the reference quantity, rather than simply an unspecified lighter punishment.

“They receive a lesser punishment” loses the numerical proportion and is therefore imprecise.

Does a half here mean fairness?

No.

The rationale and justice of differing punishments can be discussed, but niṣf itself has a quantitative meaning here.

This proportion shares a root with ethical fairness without being synonymous with it.

“A fair punishment” is therefore also an unsuitable replacement for the word.

8. Half the night in Al-Muzzammil

The final two Qur’anic N Ṣ F occurrences concern measuring nighttime.

They are especially useful because the half measures a duration rather than a financial or legal share.

A. Al-Muzzammil 73:1, Al-Muzzammil 73:2, Al-Muzzammil 73:3, and Al-Muzzammil 73:4: half the night

يَا أَيُّهَا الْمُزَّمِّلُ

yā ayyuhā al-muzzammilu

“You who are wrapped in a garment.”

Al-Muzzammil 73:1

قُمِ اللَّيْلَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

qumi al-layla illā qalīlan

“Stand during the night, except a little.”

Al-Muzzammil 73:2

نِصْفَهُ أَوِ انْقُصْ مِنْهُ قَلِيلًا

niṣfahu awi unquṣ minhu qalīlan

“Half of it, or reduce it a little.”

Al-Muzzammil 73:3

أَوْ زِدْ عَلَيْهِ وَرَتِّلِ الْقُرْآنَ تَرْتِيلًا

aw zid ʿalayhi wa-rattili al-qurʾāna tartīlan

“Or add to it, and recite the Qur’an distinctly and in measured order.”

Al-Muzzammil 73:4

Niṣfahu here refers to half the night.

The pronoun hu relates to the night, and the quantity measured is its duration.

Early explanations differ over the grammatical relationship between niṣfahu and illā qalīlan.

Differing analyses of niṣfahu

One analysis treats niṣfahu as clarification or apposition to an amount mentioned in the preceding sentence, with the verse describing standing for about half the night.

Another makes half the measuring baseline, with awi unquṣ minhu qalīlan and aw zid ʿalayhi indicating slightly less or more than half.

Early grammatical analyses offer different explanations of how niṣfahu relates to the preceding verse.

They affect analysis of the commanded duration, without changing the meaning of a half itself.

In all these readings, a half means one-half.

Qalīlan means a little, and zid concerns adding; these are independent words from other roots.

Reduction and addition should therefore not become part of N Ṣ F’s definition.

Half the night and clock time

Half the night is a proportion of its duration.

The night’s length nevertheless varies across seasons and regions.

Niṣfa al-layli alone therefore cannot establish a fixed worldwide clock time.

If a night lasts ten hours, half its duration is five hours; if it lasts fourteen, half is seven hours.

The numerical meaning of a half remains unchanged.

Careful study must also distinguish half the duration from the midpoint instant.

These verses concern the duration of standing and recitation, rather than simply a clock reaching midnight.

B. Al-Muzzammil 73:20: half and a third of the night

إِنَّ رَبَّكَ يَعْلَمُ أَنَّكَ تَقُومُ أَدْنَىٰ مِنْ ثُلُثَيِ اللَّيْلِ وَنِصْفَهُ وَثُلُثَهُ وَطَائِفَةٌ مِنَ الَّذِينَ مَعَكَ

inna rabbaka yaʿlamu annaka taqūmu adnā min thuluthayi al-layli wa-niṣfahu wa-thuluthahu wa-ṭāʾifatun mina alladhīna maʿaka

Translation following Ḥafṣ’s reading:

“Your Lord knows that you and a group of those with you stand for less than two-thirds of the night, and [at times] half of it, and [at times] a third of it.”

Al-Muzzammil 73:20, opening excerpt

Three temporal proportions are mentioned:

Two-thirds of the night, half the night, and a third of it.

Niṣfahu again means half the night’s duration.

An important reading difference nevertheless affects the syntax.

Wa-niṣfahu and wa-niṣfihi: differing readings

Ḥafṣ and some other readings have wa-niṣfahu and wa-thuluthahu in the accusative.

On this reading, the sentence can describe standing at times for less than two-thirds of the night and at other times for half or a third. In this analysis, “less than” qualifies only two-thirds.

Other established readings have the genitive wa-niṣfihi and wa-thuluthihi.

Both then connect grammatically to thuluthayi al-layli, and the expression can refer to less than two-thirds, less than half, or less than a third of the night.

Both readings are recognized in the reading tradition, and early explanations examine their grammatical difference.

The difference matters when analyzing the nighttime standing precisely.

A half nevertheless means one-half in either form.

Accusative versus genitive changes its grammatical relationship with the other sentence elements, rather than the root meaning.

The easing in the verse’s continuation

Al-Muzzammil 73:20 continues:

وَاللَّهُ يُقَدِّرُ اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ عَلِمَ أَنْ لَنْ تُحْصُوهُ فَتَابَ عَلَيْكُمْ فَاقْرَءُوا مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنَ الْقُرْآنِ

wa-allāhu yuqaddiru al-layla wa-al-nahāra ʿalima an lan tuḥṣūhu fa-tāba ʿalaykum fa-iqraʾū mā tayassara mina al-qurʾāni

Translation:

“The God measures the night and day. He knew that you would not be able to reckon it fully, so He turned toward you; therefore recite what is practicable of the Qur’an.”

This addresses difficulty in measuring and fulfilling the durations.

It then commands reciting what is practicable of the Qur’an.

The same verse goes on to mention illness, traveling in search of divine bounty, and fighting in The God’s path.

The subject is therefore not merely an abstract number, but a duration and people’s actual capacity to fulfill it.

Niṣf alone cannot establish a final ruling for every nighttime practice or the number of prayers.

Those questions require independent examination of the surah, related verses, and the relationship between their commands.

N Ṣ F’s role here is simply to express one-half.

9. Comparison with related roots

N Ṣ F and Q S Ṭ: a half and justice

Different Qur’anic forms of Q S Ṭ concern justice or departing from it.

Qisṭ in a positive use means respecting justice.

A half is a definite quantitative proportion.

Dividing equally in halves may exemplify justice in one setting, while justice requires different shares in another.

Justice should therefore not be reduced to halving, nor a half to justice.

N Ṣ F and ʿ D L: a half and justice

Qur’anic ʿadl concerns justice and equality, and in other uses also setting up an equivalent.

Ethical inṣāf is close to justice.

Qur’anic niṣf, however, concerns quantitative division.

Justice and a half should therefore not be treated as synonymous.

Inheritance verses specify amounts, while judging entitlements and the justice of division is broader than the word niṣf itself.

Study of ʿ D L

N Ṣ F and W S Ṭ: a half and a middle

W S Ṭ concerns the middle or an intermediate point, and can also concern moderation or an intermediate position.

Some N Ṣ F constructions likewise identify the midpoint of a duration or route.

There is nevertheless a distinction.

A half identifies one of two equal portions.

A middle can identify a point or position between two ends.

Muntaṣaf al-ṭarīq identifies the halfway point of a route, whereas niṣf al-ṭarīq can identify one of its two portions.

They overlap in some sentences without being completely identical.

Study of W S Ṭ

N Ṣ F and TH L TH: a half and a third

Niṣf means one-half of a quantity.

Thuluth means one-third of it.

Both occur in inheritance verses and Al-Muzzammil, with different numerical proportions.

Appearing together in a verse does not make them synonyms or interchangeable.

Their different proportions matter for understanding duration in Al-Muzzammil 73:20.

Study of TH L TH

N Ṣ F and R B ʿ: a half and a quarter

Rubuʿ means one-quarter.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:12 gives a husband half in one situation and a quarter in another.

The two proportions differ numerically and each applies in its specified situation.

Niṣf alone does not determine the conditions under which the share changes.

Study of R B ʿ

N Ṣ F and J Z ʾ: a half and a part

J Z ʾ concerns a part or portion.

Every half is part of a whole, but not every part is half.

Something may be divided into three, four, or ten parts, each called a portion.

“Part” is therefore too general for niṣf.

Translations should retain “half” to preserve the precise one-half proportion.

Study of J Z ʾ

N Ṣ F and N Q Ṣ: a half and reduction

N Q Ṣ concerns a decrease in quantity.

Al-Muzzammil 73:3 says:

أَوِ انْقُصْ مِنْهُ قَلِيلًا

awi unquṣ minhu qalīlan

“Or reduce it a little.”

Niṣf and naqṣ are different concepts.

A half is a one-half quantity.

Naqṣ describes the action or state of decreasing.

Subtracting from half gives less than one-half, but that reduction comes from unquṣ, rather than niṣf itself.

Study of N Q Ṣ

10. Translation questions and proposals for Forqan

Does Qur’anic niṣf mean anything other than a half?

In Ḥafṣ’s reading, no.

All seven instances belong to the noun niṣf and mean one-half.

None means serving, a head covering, middle age, or ethical fairness.

Unlike many other roots, its basic Qur’anic translation is therefore straightforward.

Identifying the reference quantity and applicable conditions still depends on each verse’s context.

Do the Persian words nīmah and niṣf differ?

Persian nīmah and niṣf commonly identify one of two equal parts of a thing.

Niṣf is clear and precise in legal, financial, and numerical sentences.

Nīmah can sound more natural in Persian, especially concerning time.

For example:

“Half the property.”

And:

“Half the night.”

Both are natural renderings.

The sentence’s construction must also be respected in verse translation.

Is a half always a share given to someone?

No.

In verses such as Al-Nisāʾ 4:11 and Al-Nisāʾ 4:12, a half is a financial share.

In Al-Baqarah 2:237, the quantity concerns the marital financial obligation.

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:25, a half concerns punishment.

In Al-Muzzammil, it identifies a duration.

The root therefore does not necessarily concern ownership or payment.

“Half” preserves the quantitative precision of inheritance and financial-entitlement passages.

For Al-Muzzammil, the Persian “half the night” is more natural, provided it denotes a duration rather than simply the instant at the night’s midpoint.

The accusative versus genitive reading of niṣfahu in Al-Muzzammil 73:20 should also remain clear in grammatical explanation.

Distinguishing the brief root meaning from the meaning in the verse

A suitable approach for Forqan’s word boxes is:

Brief root meaning: half; one of two equal parts of a whole.

Meaning in the verse: depending on context, half the stipulated amount, half the estate, half the punishment, or half the night’s duration.

This preserves the fixed numerical meaning while identifying the reference quantity in each verse.

The boxes should preferably leave inheritance-law questions, punishment details, and interpretation of the nighttime command to the reflection on the relevant verses.

11. Conclusion

Arabic N Ṣ F has two important branches.

The first concerns a half, a midpoint, dividing into two equal parts, and, by extension, fairness and respecting rights.

Some older Arabic derivatives of the second branch concern serving and being a servant.

The early analysis mentioned above identifies them independently; neither should become the certain origin of the other without historical evidence.

In the quantitative branch, niṣf is one of two equal parts of a whole.

It applies to amounts of property, durations, routes, and other measurable things.

Early Arabic naṣafa al-nahāru concerns the day reaching its midpoint, while anṣafa concerns respecting rights and acting fairly.

Fairness nevertheless does not mean dividing everything equally; justice may require different shares in a particular setting.

Naṣīf also names a woman’s covering, and naṣaf a stage of middle age.

Poetry from Al-Musayyab ibn ʿAlas, Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī, and Al-Farazdaq illustrates quantitative, temporal, clothing-related, and ethical applications in early and classical Arabic.

In the Qur’an, however, the family occurs only seven times, always as the noun niṣf.

Al-Baqarah 2:237 concerns the stipulated financial amount in a particular situation of separation.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:11, Al-Nisāʾ 4:12, and Al-Nisāʾ 4:176 concern inheritance shares.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:25 concerns the quantity of punishment in a specified situation.

Al-Muzzammil 73:3 and Al-Muzzammil 73:20 concern the night’s duration.

The numerical one-half meaning remains constant throughout.

What is halved and the conditions under which it applies come from the context and other words.

The case-ending difference in the readings of niṣfahu in Al-Muzzammil 73:20 particularly affects its grammatical relationship with the other durations, without changing its numerical meaning.

“A portion,” “a lesser amount,” “a fair share,” and “an approximate middle” can therefore lose the verses’ numerical precision.

Proposed final definition for Forqan

N Ṣ F’s main branch concerns a half, one of two equal parts of a whole. Related uses include reaching a midpoint, dividing into halves, and, by extension, fairness and respecting entitlements.

Some derivatives in a separate early Arabic branch concern serving. Its certain historical relationship with “half” has not been established.

All seven Qur’anic N Ṣ F instances belong to niṣf, meaning one-half.

The proportion sometimes concerns property and inheritance, sometimes punishment, and sometimes the night’s duration.

Thus one of two equal parts of a whole is a concise, precise definition shared by every Qur’anic instance.

Fairness, serving, and other linguistic branches belong in the lexical study, but should never replace niṣf’s clear quantitative meaning in Qur’anic verses.
