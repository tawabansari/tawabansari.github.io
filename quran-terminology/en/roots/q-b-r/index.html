The Qur’anic Root Q B R

Graves, cemeteries, and providing a burial place

Qur’anic forms

Providing burial

In early Arabic, Q B R concerns burying the dead, placing a body in the earth, and the place provided for burial. This family distinguishes the act of burial, making burial possible, an individual grave, and a site containing a collection of graves.

A verse attributed to al-Aʿshā, a poet whose life extended from the late pre-Islamic period into the time of Qur’anic revelation, describes the beloved’s beauty:

لَوْ أَسْنَدَتْ مَيْتًا إِلَى نَحْرِهَا
عَاشَ وَلَمْ يُنْقَلْ إِلَى قَابِرِ

Law asnadat maytan ilā naḥrihā / ʿāsha wa-lam yunqal ilā qābiri.

If she rested a dead man against her bosom, he would live and would not be carried to a gravedigger.

Qābir (قَابِر) here means someone who buries the dead. Although the precise date of the verse is not independently established, its attribution places it within the transmitted Arabic poetry of a poet close to the period of revelation.

Arabic dictionaries also associate this root with depression and concealment. For example, arḍun qabūrun (أَرْضٌ قَبُورٌ) denotes low-lying ground. The core of the Qur’anic word family, however, concerns burial and its location, rather than every kind of depression or concealment.

Forms examined: qabara (قَبَرَ), qābir (قَابِر), maqbūr (مَقْبُور), qabr (قَبْر), qubūr (قُبُور), aqbara (أَقْبَرَ), maqbarah (مَقْبَرَة), and maqābir (مَقَابِر).

Qur’anic forms: qabr, qubūr, aqbara, and maqābir. Together, these occur eight times.

1. Burying and providing a burial place

Arabic makes a significant distinction between qabara and aqbara.

Qabara means to bury a dead person. Someone placing a body in a grave performs the act of burial.

Aqbara, a Form IV verb, means to provide a burial place, make burial possible, or cause someone to be buried.

Describing the stages of human life, the Qur’an says:

ثُمَّ أَمَاتَهُ فَأَقْبَرَهُ

Thumma amātahu fa-aqbarah.

Then He caused him to die and provided a burial place for him.

'Abasa 80:21

The verb here is aqbara, not qabara. The former expresses providing the possibility and place of burial; the latter denotes performing the burial directly.

This meaning also relates to the account of Adam’s two sons in Al-Māʾidah 5:31. After one brother is killed, The God sends a crow to scratch the ground and show the other brother how to cover his brother’s body.

In this narrative, the human learns about burying the dead through observing nature.

In Sūrat ʿAbasa, providing a burial place is not the end of the account of the human:

ثُمَّ إِذَا شَاءَ أَنْشَرَهُ

Thumma idhā shāʾa ansharah.

Then, whenever He wills, He will raise him again.

'Abasa 80:22

Death, burial, and resurrection are three distinct stages. The meaning of one should not be merged into another.

2. Qabr and qubūr: the burial place of the dead

Qabr is a singular noun; qubūr is its broken plural on the pattern fuʿūl. It names a burial place without itself specifying a grave’s construction, depth, covering material, or the condition of the person after burial.

A grave as an individual’s burial place

وَلَا تُصَلِّ عَلَىٰ أَحَدٍ مِنْهُمْ مَاتَ أَبَدًا وَلَا تَقُمْ عَلَىٰ قَبْرِهِ ۖ إِنَّهُمْ كَفَرُوا بِاللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ وَمَاتُوا وَهُمْ فَاسِقُونَ

Wa-lā tuṣalli ʿalā aḥadin minhum māta abadan wa-lā taqum ʿalā qabrihi. Innahum kafarū bi-llāhi wa-rasūlihi wa-mātū wa-hum fāsiqūn.

Never perform ṣalāt over any of them who has died, nor stand at his grave. They disbelieved in The God and His Messenger, and died while defiantly disobedient.

At-Tawbah 9:84

Qabrihi (قَبْرِهِ) refers here to a particular person’s grave. The attached pronoun identifies the relationship between that place and the deceased.

Graves and the human inability to make others hear

وَمَا يَسْتَوِي الْأَحْيَاءُ وَلَا الْأَمْوَاتُ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُسْمِعُ مَنْ يَشَاءُ ۖ وَمَا أَنْتَ بِمُسْمِعٍ مَنْ فِي الْقُبُورِ

Wa-mā yastawī l-aḥyāʾu wa-lā l-amwātu. Inna llāha yusmiʿu man yashāʾu wa-mā anta bi-musmiʿin man fī l-qubūr.

The living and the dead are not equal. The God makes whomever He wills hear, and you cannot make those in the graves hear.

Fatir 35:22

The preceding verses contrast the blind and the seeing, darkness and light, shade and scorching heat. The contrast between the living and the dead continues this imagery.

Those in the graves provide a concrete image of Muhammad’s inability to make those unreceptive to his message hear.

The expression concerns Muhammad’s ability to make others hear. Human perception after death is a separate question from the lexical meaning of “grave” and the main subject of this verse.

The inhabitants of graves

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَتَوَلَّوْا قَوْمًا غَضِبَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ قَدْ يَئِسُوا مِنَ الْآخِرَةِ كَمَا يَئِسَ الْكُفَّارُ مِنْ أَصْحَابِ الْقُبُورِ

Yā ayyuhā lladhīna āmanū lā tatawallaw qawman ghaḍiba llāhu ʿalayhim qad yaʾisū mina l-ākhirati kamā yaʾisa l-kuffāru min aṣḥābi l-qubūr.

You who have believed, do not take as protectors a people with whom The God is angry. They have despaired of the Hereafter, just as the disbelievers have despaired of the inhabitants of graves.

Al-Mumtahanah 60:13

The final phrase permits two grammatical analyses; these concern the attachment of min, not different recitations of the verse.

In the first analysis, min is connected to yaʾisa, “despaired”: the disbelievers despair of the return or resurrection of those in the graves.

In the second, min specifies the group meant by “the disbelievers”: disbelievers among the inhabitants of graves who have despaired of the good of the Hereafter.

In both analyses, aṣḥāb al-qubūr denotes the dead identified through their graves. The difference concerns the preposition’s attachment and who is despairing, not the meaning of “grave.”

3. Graves and resurrection

The Qur’an does not present graves as the end of human existence. In resurrection passages, they are places from which those within are raised or brought out again.

وَأَنَّ السَّاعَةَ آتِيَةٌ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهَا وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ يَبْعَثُ مَنْ فِي الْقُبُورِ

Wa-anna l-sāʿata ātiyatun lā rayba fīhā wa-anna llāha yabʿathu man fī l-qubūr.

And the Hour is coming, with no doubt about it, and The God will raise those in the graves.

Al-Hajj 22:7

Qubūr names the place of the dead, while yabʿathu expresses their being raised.

Sūrat al-Infiṭār says:

وَإِذَا الْقُبُورُ بُعْثِرَتْ

Wa-idhā l-qubūru buʿthirat.

And when the graves are overturned.

Al-Infitar 82:4

Sūrat al-ʿĀdiyāt presents the same image with attention to the contents of graves:

أَفَلَا يَعْلَمُ إِذَا بُعْثِرَ مَا فِي الْقُبُورِ

A-fa-lā yaʿlamu idhā buʿthira mā fī l-qubūr.

Does he not know, when what is in the graves is brought out,

Al-'Adiyat 100:9

وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ

Wa-ḥuṣṣila mā fī l-ṣudūr.

and what is in the breasts is disclosed,

Al-'Adiyat 100:10

In al-Infiṭār the graves themselves are overturned; in al-ʿĀdiyāt what is within them is brought out. The passage then speaks of what is within the breasts being disclosed.

What has been hidden in the earth and what people have concealed within themselves will become manifest on the day of resurrection.

Human resurrection is not conditional upon having a constructed grave in the earth. Yā Sīn 36:53 speaks of everyone being brought before The God. Those who drowned, whose bodies burned, or who were not buried for any reason are not excluded from the universal resurrection.

4. Maqābir: cemeteries

Maqābir is the broken plural of maqbarah, a place for burying the dead and a site containing graves.

Maqbarah need not mean a mausoleum or a building erected over an individual grave. The Arabic word’s scope is wider than such a structure.

In Sūrat al-Takāthur, the Qur’an says:

أَلْهَاكُمُ التَّكَاثُرُ

Alhākumu l-takāthur.

Rivalry in accumulation distracted you,

At-Takathur 102:1

حَتَّىٰ زُرْتُمُ الْمَقَابِرَ

Ḥattā zurtumu l-maqābir.

until you visited the cemeteries.

At-Takathur 102:2

Here, going to the cemeteries is connected with the continuation of rivalry in accumulation.

People may become so absorbed in increasing their possessions, power, followers, or superiority over others that the distraction continues until death.

In this setting, zurtum al-maqābir can figuratively express reaching the grave and the end of worldly life. Another interpretation relates it to actual visits to cemeteries to boast about the number of one’s ancestors.

The choice of “visiting” is noteworthy in the first interpretation. A visitor does not necessarily regard a place as a final home. The image of the grave as somewhere a person visits accords with the verses about being raised again.

The temporary character of this stay is understood by relating the visit to resurrection passages; it does not come from the lexical meaning of maqābir alone.

5. Comparison with related roots

Q B R (qabr) and J D TH (jadath)

Both words denote a grave or burial place in Arabic. Their Qur’anic uses, however, differ.

All three occurrences of ajdāth—Yā Sīn 36:51, Al-Qamar 54:7, and Al-Maʿārij 70:43—appear in scenes of people emerging and moving on the day of resurrection.

The Q B R family has a wider range of contexts: burial, the place of the dead, cemeteries, and the raising of the dead.

This does not establish two intrinsically different kinds of grave. Qabr and jadath overlap in basic meaning but appear in different Qur’anic settings.

Q B R (qabr) and R Q D (ruqūd)

R Q D concerns sleeping and resting. Marqad is a place of sleep or repose.

قَالُوا يَا وَيْلَنَا مَنْ بَعَثَنَا مِنْ مَرْقَدِنَا ۜ هَٰذَا مَا وَعَدَ الرَّحْمَٰنُ وَصَدَقَ الْمُرْسَلُونَ

Qālū yā waylanā man baʿathanā min marqadinā. Hādhā mā waʿada l-raḥmānu wa-ṣadaqa l-mursalūn.

They said, “Woe to us! Who has raised us from our resting place? This is what the Entirely Merciful promised, and the Messengers spoke the truth.”

Ya-Sin 36:52

Qabr names the burial place, whereas marqad describes a place of repose. Here the dead call the place from which they have been raised their marqad.

This presents another aspect of the Qur’anic imagery of death and resurrection. By itself, however, it does not determine the nature or condition of human awareness between death and resurrection.

Q B R (qabr) and W R Y (muwārāh)

Muwārāh means covering and concealing from view; its use is not restricted to burying the dead.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:31, it concerns covering a dead body. In Al-Aʿrāf 7:26, the same word family concerns clothing that covers human private parts.

Burial conceals the body from view, but not every act of covering or concealment is burial.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

Render qabr as “grave,” qubūr as “graves,” maqābir as “cemeteries,” and aqbara as “providing a burial place” or “making burial possible.”

In translating fa-aqbarahu, preserve the distinction between directly performing burial and making it possible.

Brief root meaning: Burying the dead, providing a burial place, and the place where the bodies of the dead are laid.

Conclusion

Q B R comprises a family of words concerning burial. Qabr is an individual grave, qubūr its plural, maqābir burial grounds, and aqbara providing the possibility or place of burial.

The Qur’an uses this family in connection with human death, burial, the end of worldly distractions, and resurrection.

In the Qur’an, the grave is not the end of the human journey. It is a place associated with death, from which, in the imagery of resurrection, what was hidden becomes manifest again.
