Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root A R Ḍ

Earth, land, settled ground, the field of life, growth, movement, responsibility, and human action

Important Forms from This Root

أَرْض (arḍ)

arḍ; earth, land, ground, or a spread-out field where life stands and moves.

الأَرْض (al-arḍ)

al-arḍ; the earth as a general field of life, or in context a particular land.

أَرَاضٍ / أَرَضُون (arāḍin / araḍūn)

lands, regions, or multiple stretches of earth.

أَرْضِيّ (arḍī)

earthly, terrestrial, belonging to the lower field of life.

أَرْضُ اللَّهِ (arḍu allāh)

The God’s earth; land whose true ownership belongs to The God.

Method Note

The root A R Ḍ is not merely the name of a physical planet opposite the sky. In the Qur’an, أَرْض (arḍ) can mean the material earth, land, settled ground, the field of life, the place of human movement, and the arena of corruption, reform, provision, migration, and return. For translation, it is important to distinguish earth as soil, land as territory, and earth as the field of human action.

The heavens and the earth often appear together in the Qur’an. This pairing places the earth inside the larger order of creation. If samāʾ, the sky or heaven, suggests height, covering, descent of rain, and the coming of command, then arḍ, earth, suggests receiving, growing, settling, and acting. The sky sends; the earth receives and brings forth. The human being chooses, builds, corrupts, reforms, migrates, and leaves the trace of action in this lower and spread-out field.

The singular form الأرض (al-arḍ) is highly dominant in the Qur’an. This should not be overburdened, but it is meaningful. The Qur’an often treats the earth as one shared field for human life and trial, even though in some contexts arḍ means a specific land or region.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of A R Ḍ is the lower spread-out ground on which life can settle. It is the place where a living being stands, moves, grows, builds, and acts. If samāʾ points upward to covering and height, arḍ points downward to the ground underfoot and the wide field beneath the sky.

From this core, arḍ can mean soil, land, territory, living environment, or the social and political field. Context decides the precise sense. Sometimes الأرض (al-arḍ) means the whole earth opposite the heavens. Sometimes it means a particular land, such as Egypt or the sacred land. Sometimes it means the general arena of human life.

The earth is attributed to The God, yet it is also made a place of dwelling and benefit for the human being. These two relations do not conflict. The earth belongs to The God, while the human being settles in it and benefits from it. So the human relation to the earth is not absolute ownership without responsibility. It is trust, benefit, responsibility, and trial.

Material Image

The material image is a spread-out surface under the human being’s feet. A person stands on it, walks on it, builds a house, cultivates crops, receives water into it, sees seeds split within it, and buries the dead in it. The earth is both a ground and a carrier; it receives and it grows.

This image does not reduce the earth to an inert object. In the Qur’an, the earth becomes living and dead, shakes, splits, brings out plants, carries human burdens, and on the Last Day brings out its reports. Arḍ is not only a surface underfoot. It is the field where the traces of life and action appear.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, أَرْض (arḍ) can refer to the earth opposite the sky, soil, land, region, territory, or a wide field in which a person or people settle. It may mean the whole earth or a specific land, depending on context.

The human relation to land in Arabic is not limited to residence. Land is the place of grazing, cultivation, travel, war, trade, migration, and ownership. This helps explain why the Qur’an connects arḍ with provision, corruption, reform, oppression, migration, inheritance, settlement, and political power.

Qur’anic Usage

The Earth Opposite the Heavens

In many verses, the earth appears with the heavens: خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بِالْحَقِّ (khalaqa al-samāwāti wal-arḍa bil-ḥaqq) 64:3. This pairing shows that the earth is part of the total order of creation. The heavens are the field of height, covering, command, and descent; the earth is the field of receiving, growth, life, and action.

In this usage, الأرض (al-arḍ) is not merely soil. It is the whole lower and habitable field opposite the higher structure. The God created the heavens and the earth with truth, meaning that the upper and lower realms are both placed within order and purpose.

The Earth as the Human Place of Settlement

In وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ (walakum fī al-arḍi mustaqarrun wamatāʿun ilā ḥīn), the earth is presented as the human place of settlement and temporary benefit 2:36. The human being is placed on earth and benefits from it, but only until a fixed time.

This verse is a key to the root. The earth is not the final home. It is a temporary settlement, a field of trial, and a place of use for a measured time. The human being should not treat the earth as absolute and everlasting ownership. He is settled in it, but he is not eternally confined to it.

The Earth Belongs to The God

The Qur’an says: إِنَّ الْأَرْضَ لِلَّهِ (inna al-arḍa lillāh), the earth belongs to The God 7:128. At the same time, the human being settles in this earth, eats from its provision, moves along its ways, and may inherit part of it.

This means human benefit from the earth is not absolute ownership. The human being carries responsibility in the earth, not unlimited entitlement. He may cultivate or ruin, reform or corrupt, live with justice or rise like Pharaoh in oppressive superiority. Arḍ is not merely human property for consumption. It is a trust in which the quality of human action becomes visible.

The Earth as the Field of Corruption and Reform

The Qur’an often joins the earth with corruption and reform: وَلَا تُفْسِدُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ بَعْدَ إِصْلَاحِهَا (walā tufsidū fī al-arḍi baʿda iṣlāḥihā) 7:56. Here the earth is not only physical soil. It is the field of social, moral, economic, and natural order.

Corruption in the earth means damaging the structure of life, justice, security, balance, and the right relation between people and their environment. Reform means returning affairs to right order, preventing injustice, preserving balance, and making life sound. In such verses, فِي الْأَرْضِ (fī al-arḍ) means more than physical location. It is the arena of human responsibility.

The Earth and the Growth of Life

Many verses speak of dead earth being revived by water: فَأَحْيَيْنَا بِهِ الْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا (fa-aḥyaynā bihi al-arḍa baʿda mawtihā) 35:9. This usage shows the earth as the field of life. Dry and silent earth moves again when water descends and plants come out.

This image also matters for death and life. The earth had not disappeared; its growing life was inactive. When water came, the earth became active again. Arḍ is the field of possible life, where seed, water, time, and The God’s command meet in growth.

The Earth and Human Movement

In فَامْشُوا فِي مَنَاكِبِهَا وَكُلُوا مِن رِّزْقِهِ (famshū fī manākibihā wakulū min rizqih), the human being is told to walk in the shoulders and pathways of the earth and eat from The God’s provision 67:15. The earth is a wide field prepared for movement, effort, travel, work, provision, and experience.

This verse shows that the earth is not merely a place to sit. It is the field of movement and seeking provision. The human being walks, searches, works, and benefits from what is provided, while knowing that provision belongs to The God.

The God’s Earth and the Possibility of Migration

In أَلَمْ تَكُنْ أَرْضُ اللَّهِ وَاسِعَةً فَتُهَاجِرُوا فِيهَا (alam takun arḍu allāhi wāsiʿatan fatuhājirū fīhā), The God’s earth is described as wide 4:97. The verse speaks to people who remained in a condition of oppression and weakness without migrating. Here, earth means a wide field of possibility, not only a limited piece of soil.

أَرْضُ اللَّهِ وَاسِعَةً (arḍu allāhi wāsiʿah) means that a person should not justify oppression by treating one land as absolute, if there is a path to leave. The earth belongs to The God, and its width can become a way out of pressure, compulsion, and corruption.

The Earth as the Inheritance of the Righteous

In أَنَّ الْأَرْضَ يَرِثُهَا عِبَادِيَ الصَّالِحُونَ (anna al-arḍa yarithuhā ʿibādiya al-ṣāliḥūn), the earth is described as the inheritance of The God’s righteous servants 21:105. This shows that the earth is a field of trust and inheritance. Righteousness is not only inward; it must become fitness to carry and govern the earth.

This usage connects the earth to social and civil responsibility. The inheritors of the earth are not merely those who make claims. They are righteous servants who can carry the earth with truth, justice, and reform.

The Earth as the Field of Pharaoh-Like Power

In إِنَّ فِرْعَوْنَ عَلَا فِي الْأَرْضِ (inna firʿawna ʿalā fī al-arḍ), Pharaoh rose high in the earth 28:4. Here the earth is the field of political and social power. Rising high in the earth means lifting oneself above people, dividing society into classes, weakening a group, and establishing oppressive domination.

So فِي الْأَرْضِ (fī al-arḍ) in this verse is not merely a location. It is the field of power and political behavior. The earth can become a place of justice, or a field of superiority, arrogance, and corruption.

The Earth Bringing Out Its Burdens

Surat al-Zalzalah says: إِذَا زُلْزِلَتِ الْأَرْضُ زِلْزَالَهَا وَأَخْرَجَتِ الْأَرْضُ أَثْقَالَهَا (idhā zulzilati al-arḍu zilzālahā wa-akhrajati al-arḍu athqālahā) 99:1 to 99:2. The earth brings out its burdens. The image shows that the earth is not a silent stage unrelated to human action. What has passed over it is not separate from it.

Then the surah says: يَوْمَئِذٍ تُحَدِّثُ أَخْبَارَهَا (yawmaʾidhin tuḥaddithu akhbārahā) 99:4. On that day, the earth tells its reports. In this usage, arḍ becomes a witness to human action. The human being acts on the earth, but the earth is not unaware or unrelated.

The Earth and the Prostration of Created Beings

In the verses of prostration, whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth stands in relation to The God. The earth is not a lower realm cut off from The God; everything in it belongs to a system of praise, prostration, and command.

This use reminds the reader that the human being is not the only inhabitant of the earth, and the earth is not his private property. The earth is part of the wider order of servanthood and obedience among created beings.

Difference from Nearby Words

Turāb is dust or soft soil, while arḍ is the wider ground or field. Ṭīn is clay, matter mixed with water, often appearing in relation to the creation of the human being or the shaping of material. Ṣaʿīd is the visible surface of earth or soil, especially in the context of dry ablution. Qāʿ is flat, empty ground.

Balad is a land, city, or region with identity, sacredness, settlement, or social boundary. Qaryah is a settled town or community, a human gathering on the earth. These are social and human structures formed upon arḍ. Arḍ is broader and more primary. Before a city is built or a town forms, the earth is there; after settlements fall, the earth remains.

Dār is a house, abode, or domain of dwelling. Arḍ is wider than all of these. It can be soil, but it is not only soil. It can be land, but it is not only a political border. It can mean the whole earth or a particular region, depending on the verse.

Translation Method

In translating arḍ, it is not always enough to write “earth” and move on. Sometimes “earth” is correct, especially when it appears opposite the heavens. But in other verses, “land,” “the spread of the earth,” “the field of life,” “the living ground,” “territory,” or “the field of action” may be more precise.

In verses about corruption and reform, earth must be heard with social and moral weight. In verses about provision and movement, earth is the field of effort and benefit. In verses about political power, earth is the arena of domination or reform. The translator must also avoid assuming that الأرض (al-arḍ) always means the whole planet. Sometimes it means a particular land, a specific community, or the local field of an event.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بِالْحَقِّ (khalaqa al-samāwāti wal-arḍa bil-ḥaqq): He created the heavens and the earth with truth; He brought the higher expanse and the earth into being upon truth.

وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ (walakum fī al-arḍi mustaqarr): for you there is a place of settlement in the earth; for you on earth is a temporary place of settlement.

إِنَّ الْأَرْضَ لِلَّهِ (inna al-arḍa lillāh): surely the earth belongs to The God; the true ownership of the earth belongs to The God.

وَلَا تُفْسِدُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ (walā tufsidū fī al-arḍ): do not cause corruption in the earth; do not spread corruption in the field of life and society.

فَأَحْيَيْنَا بِهِ الْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا (fa-aḥyaynā bihi al-arḍa baʿda mawtihā): We revived the earth with it after its death; We brought the earth back into growth after its life had gone silent.

فَامْشُوا فِي مَنَاكِبِهَا (famshū fī manākibihā): walk through its broad paths; move along the shoulders of the earth and seek.

أَرْضُ اللَّهِ وَاسِعَةً (arḍu allāhi wāsiʿah): The God’s earth is wide; The God’s land is spacious.

عَلَا فِي الْأَرْضِ (ʿalā fī al-arḍ): he rose high in the earth; he exalted himself in the land and society.

أَخْرَجَتِ الْأَرْضُ أَثْقَالَهَا (akhrajati al-arḍu athqālahā): the earth brought out its heavy burdens.

Summary

The root A R Ḍ in the Qur’an is not only the name of soil under the feet. Arḍ is the field of life, settlement, movement, growth, provision, corruption, reform, power, responsibility, and witness. The human being lives on the earth, but the earth is not merely a place of consumption. It is a trust, a field of trial, and the arena where human action becomes visible.

In Forqan’s reading, arḍ is the lower and spread-out field in which the human being settles and reveals his responsibility. From it he receives provision, within it he corrupts or reforms, through it he may migrate, and from it one day its reports will be brought out.
