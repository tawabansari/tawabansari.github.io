Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root ʿ R Ḍ (Ayn R Dad)

Presenting, setting before, or placing in view for assessment; width, fleeting matters, or turning away.

Important Forms from This Root

Simple Verb
Used in the Qur’an: Yes
عَرَضَ (ʿarada)

To present, display, or set something before others for inspection.
Form IV
Used in the Qur’an: Yes
أَعْرَضَ (aʿrada)

To turn away, avoid, or withdraw from a message or sign.
Nouns
Used in the Qur’an: Yes
عَرَض / عَرْض (ʿarad / ʿard)

ʿarad: Fleeting gain. ʿard: Width or vastness.

Method Note

The root ʿ-R-Ḍ is fundamentally tied to placing something in front of sight, attention, or measurement. Anything that is "presented" (ʿarda) is brought from the background to the foreground for judgment, choice, or testing. It is a multi-dimensional root: if translated only as "display," its precise Qur’anic weight is lost. It can mean presenting a case, the width of a space, fleeting material gain, or the act of turning away from an presented truth.

Linguistic Logic

The semantic core is "positioning in front of." If you put something before someone's eyes, you have presented it. If someone turns their face from what is set before them, aʿrada (turning away) occurs. Even "width" belongs to this field: it is the surface that expands in front of the viewer, as opposed to "length," which is the extension of the path.

Material Image

The primary image is placing goods, news, or a scene before someone's gaze. A market is a perfect material analogy: goods are presented, and the buyer inspects, weighs, chooses, or leaves them. In judgment, a witness is presented to be assessed. Another image is the "horizon" (ʿarid), where a cloud appears and fills the field of vision. Finally, "turning away" is the physical act of changing direction to avoid a confrontation with what is right in front of you.

Older Arabic Usage

In ancient Arabic, the root was used in fields of vision, trade, and tribal presence. Armies were "presented" for review, and goods were displayed for sale. Turning away (iʿrad) was understood as a deliberate change of direction away from a path or person.

Qur’anic Usage

1. Presenting and Setting Before

ثُمَّ عَرَضَهُمْ عَلَى الْمَلَائِكَةِ (thumma ʿaradahum ʿalā al-malāʾikati)

Then He presented them before the angels.

2:31

وَعُرِضُوا عَلَىٰ رَبِّكَ صَفًّا (wa-ʿuriḍū ʿalā rabbika ṣaffan)

And they were presented before your Lord in ranks.

18:48

2. Turning Away (Iʿrad)

ثُمَّ أَعرَضَ عَنها (thumma aʿraḍa ʿanhā)

...then turned away from them.

18:57

3. Fleeting Gains (ʿArad)

تُرِيدُونَ عَرَضَ الدُّنْيَا (turīdūna ʿaraḍa al-dunyā)

You desire the fleeting gain of this world.

8:67

4. Vastness and Width (ʿArd)

وَجَنَّةٍ عَرْضُهَا السَّمَاوَاتُ وَالْأَرْضُ (wa-jannatin ʿarḍuhā al-samāwātu wa-al-arḍu)

...and a garden whose width is the heavens and the earth.

3:133

Difference from Nearby Words

  • ʿ-R-Ḍ vs. Zh-H2-R: Zhuhur is to simply appear; ʿArd is a deliberate presentation for assessment.
  • ʿ-R-Ḍ vs. B-D-W: Budu is emerging from hiding; ʿArd implies a structure of confrontation or display.
  • Aʿrada vs. Tawalla: Tawalli is a complete turning of the back; Aʿrad is a targeted withdrawal from something specifically presented before you.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

For ʿArada: "Presented," "set before," "placed in front of."

For Aʿrada ʿan: "Turned away from," "withdrew from," "shunned."

For ʿArad al-dunya: "Fleeting gain of the world," "transient benefit."

For ʿArd (width): "Vastness," "width," "breadth."

Linguistic Summary

The root ʿ-R-Ḍ signifies placing something in a position of encounter, sight, or measurement. It covers scenes ranging from the presentation of Adam's knowledge to the angels, to the vast breadth of paradise, to the psychological act of turning away from signs. Its core material image remains the deliberate act of display and the subsequent reaction of the viewer.
