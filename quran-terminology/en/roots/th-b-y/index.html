The Root TH B Y in the Qur’an

Companies, grouping, and gathering: from human groups to setting out in separate bands

Qur’anic and lexical forms

ثُبَاتٍ
(thubātin)
Plural, accusative with kasrah; the sole Qur’anic form, Al-Nisāʾ 4:71

ثُبَة
(thubah)
Singular: company or band; this singular is not Qur’anic

ثُبُون / ثُبِين
(thubūn / thubīn)
Nominative and accusative/genitive of one plural; not Qur’anic

ثُبًى
(thuban)
A recorded plural with a rare use; not Qur’anic

ثُبَيَّة
(thubayyah)
Diminutive; not conclusive by itself about the third consonant; not Qur’anic

أُثْبِيَّة / أَثَابِيّ / أَثَابِيَة
(uthbiyyah / athābiyy / athābiyah)
Company and its plural forms; not Qur’anic

ثَبَّى / تَثْبِيَة
(thabbā / tathbiyah)
Collecting, persevering, or praising according to context; not Qur’anic

ثُبَةُ الْحَوْضِ
(thubatu al-ḥawḍ)
Middle of a basin; disputed derivation, sometimes under TH W B; not Qur’anic

ثُبَة (thubah) in Arabic denotes a company or band of people; its plural is ثُبَات (thubāt). It can describe a group of people or horsemen. In its Qur’anic use, ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) describes several separate groups setting out from a larger body.

The word occurs only once in the Qur’an, at Al-Nisāʾ 4:71. فَانْفِرُوا ثُبَاتٍ (fa-infirū thubātin) stands alongside أَوِ انْفِرُوا جَمِيعًا (awi infirū jamīʿan). The first describes setting out in separate bands; the second, setting out collectively, all together.

The etymology of ثُبَة (thubah) is noteworthy because lexicographers disagree about its third root consonant: some assign it to ث ب ي (TH B Y), others to ث ب و (TH B W). Arabic also records an identical form for the middle of a watering basin, which some derive from ث و ب (TH W B). Some lexical reports also connect the human-company sense with ثَابَ (thāba); this possibility should therefore not be categorically restricted to the basin sense.

Although Qur’anic morphological indexing places ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) under ث ب ي (TH B Y), careful study must distinguish the word’s established meaning from competing proposals about its historical derivation.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: one occurrence at Al-Nisāʾ 4:71

1. The semantic core and root forms

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris records remaining or persevering in something as the basis of ث ب ي (TH B Y).

Quoting Al-Khalīl ibn Aḥmad, he gives التَّثْبِيَة (al-tathbiyah) both for perseverance in an activity and for praising someone during their lifetime.

When discussing ثُبَة (thubah), however, he describes a company of horsemen and records the plurals ثُبَات (thubāt) and ثُبُون (thubūn).

Maqāyīs al-Lughah: perseverance, companies, and Ibn Fāris’s derivational preference

The early sources thus discuss two semantic fields within this family: remaining and perseverance on one side, and people coming together as a company on the other.

Their possible relationship deserves examination. It should not simply be assumed, however, that a company originally meant people who remained in one place for a long time.

In clear uses of ثُبَة (thubah), the defining feature is the existence of a group, rather than necessarily the length of its stay.

ثُبَة (thubah): a company or band

ثُبَة (thubah), with ḍammah on thāʾ, denotes a group of people. Lisān al-ʿArab explains it through عُصْبَة (ʿuṣbah): people recognized as a company.

The same meaning appears in uses concerning horsemen. A group of horsemen may form one ثُبَة (thubah); the plural applies when they constitute several separate bands.

A military setting is not an invariable requirement. Horsemen are a familiar example, but the poetic witness concerning companions also shows the word naming a human company outside warfare.

The principal definitions examined in Maqāyīs, Al-Ṣiḥāḥ, Al-Mufradāt, and Lisān explain the word as a company or band. Nevertheless, a report attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās in Masāʾil Nāfiʿ ibn al-Azraq gives “ten or more.” It is therefore inaccurate to claim that no source offers a numerical explanation. That report alone, however, does not establish a fixed, binding number for the bands in Al-Nisāʾ 4:71.

Masāʾil Nāfiʿ ibn al-Azraq: report attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās, question 238, page 177

ثُبَات (thubāt): separate bands

ثُبَات (thubāt) is the plural of ثُبَة (thubah).

In expressions such as جَاءَتِ الْخَيْلُ ثُبَاتٍ (jāʾati al-khaylu thubātin), the horses or horsemen came in separate bands.

Depending on context, this may describe groups moving at different times or moving as distinct units.

ثُبَات (thubāt) thus names several companies; in an appropriate context their separation from one another is also relevant.

The word itself, however, means neither enemy, war, attack, nor victory. Those ideas come from the situation in which the groups appear.

ثُبُون, ثُبِين, and ثُبًى (thubūn, thubīn, and thuban)

Alongside ثُبَات (thubāt), an irregular plural inflected like a sound masculine plural appears as ثُبُون (thubūn) in the nominative and ثُبِين (thubīn) in the accusative and genitive. These are case forms of one plural. ثِبُون (thibūn), with kasrah on thāʾ, is also recorded. Some sources also treat ثُبًى (thuban) as a plural for companies, although its expression for noblemen’s assemblies in Al-Find’s poetry is described as rare.

ثُبَات (thubāt) is the plural form found in the Qur’an. ثُبُون (thubūn) and ثُبِين (thubīn) should not be treated as two independent plural formations: their endings differ according to syntactic case.

Al-Zawzanī’s commentary: thubūn in the nominative, thubīn in the accusative and genitive

In certain Arabic patterns, a noun that has lost an original consonant may take a plural in wāw and nūn or yāʾ and nūn even when the noun itself is neither a personal name nor a human adjective.

ثُبَة (thubah) is another noun whose earlier formation is debated.

أُثْبِيَّة (uthbiyyah) is likewise recorded for a company, with the plurals أَثَابِيّ (athābiyy) and أَثَابِيَة (athābiyah).

In a precise morphological account, however, أَثَابِيّ (athābiyy) should not be presented without qualification as the direct plural of ثُبَة (thubah): Ibn Barrī identifies it as the plural of أُثْبِيَّة (uthbiyyah).

Lisān al-ʿArab: the plural of uthbiyyah and Ibn Barrī’s qualification

ثَبَّى and تَثْبِيَة (thabbā and tathbiyah): persevering, collecting, and praising

Arabic records several uses of ثَبَّى (thabbā) and its verbal noun تَثْبِيَة (tathbiyah).

ثَبَّيْتُ عَلَى الشَّيْءِ (thabbaytu ʿalā al-shayʾ) is reported for continuing or persevering in something.

Another use, ثَبَّيْتُ الرَّجُلَ (thabbaytu al-rajul), concerns praising a person during his lifetime and recounting his good qualities.

ثَبَّيْتُ الشَّيْءَ (thabbaytu al-shayʾ) is also explained as collecting something and adding more to it.

These uses may relate semantically to remaining, continuing, or collecting. Some lexicographers explain praise through the act of collecting and recounting a person’s good qualities.

That explanation does not by itself prove that the noun ثُبَة (thubah) was necessarily derived from the verb of praising. Morphological evidence and early uses must also be weighed when investigating historical derivation.

2. Disagreement about the third root consonant

One of the central questions is the morphological origin of ثُبَة (thubah).

Its present form visibly contains only ث (th) and ب (b), followed by ة (tāʾ marbūṭah).

Early analyses hold that an original consonant was lost and that the final tāʾ replaced it.

The question is whether the lost consonant was yāʾ or wāw.

The first analysis: ث ب ي (TH B Y)

One analysis assigns the word to ث ب ي (TH B Y).

On this account, ثُبَة (thubah) relates to verbs such as ثَبَّى (thabbā), for which collecting, persevering, and praising are recorded. Nevertheless, ثَبَّيْتُ (thabbaytu) alone does not prove a final yāʾ: as Ibn Jinnī and Ibn Barrī explain, Form II verbs with an original final wāw can have the same appearance.

Ibn Jinnī, Sirr Ṣināʿat al-Iʿrāb: thabbaytu alone does not establish yāʾ

The collecting sense offers a possible connection with a company: separate individuals gather to form a group.

The diminutive ثُبَيَّة (thubayyah) also figures in the discussion.

After reporting different views, Ibn Fāris favors connecting ثُبَة (thubah) with the yāʾ family.

The diminutive alone does not conclusively establish the third consonant, however: where a word’s original formation has changed, its diminutive may also follow the word’s current shape.

The second analysis: ث ب و (TH B W)

Another analysis identifies the lost final consonant as wāw and reconstructs ثُبْوَة (thubwah).

Ibn Jinnī points to the prevalence of nouns that have lost an original final wāw.

Ibn Barrī likewise prefers this analysis, appealing to comparable morphological patterns and uses of the ث ب و (TH B W) family.

On this account, ثُبَة (thubah) may originally belong to a family with wāw as its third consonant.

This is more than a spelling difference. Identifying an original consonant affects which lexical family a word is assigned to.

A Qur’anic index’s classification therefore cannot by itself invalidate the other view.

ثُبَة الحوض (thubat al-ḥawḍ) and ث و ب (TH W B)

Study of TH W B

The dictionaries record ثُبَةُ الْحَوْضِ (thubatu al-ḥawḍ) for the middle of a watering basin, where water collects.

Some lexicographers derive this from ثَابَ يَثُوبُ (thāba yathūbu), meaning returning.

On this analysis, water returns or gathers at a point from different parts of the basin.

This differs from the two preceding analyses in the position of wāw.

Under ث ب و (TH B W), wāw is the third root consonant; under ث و ب (TH W B), it is the second.

The proposals must therefore not be conflated simply because both involve wāw.

Ibn Fāris favors one derivation for the basin and company senses, explaining both through ثَبَّى (thabbā) and perseverance. This is his own derivational preference, rather than a vague suggestion of semantic resemblance. Al-Rāghib and some other lexicographers distinguish the formation of the two senses; other reports also connect the company sense with ثَابَ (thāba). Each view’s proponent and the proposed position of the lost consonant should be made explicit.

Lisān and Tahdhīb: some reports connect the company sense with thāba

The defensible conclusion is that Qur’anic ثُبَات (thubāt) has a clear meaning, while the derivational origin of ثُبَة (thubah) is genuinely disputed.

Al-Rāghib and Lisān: basin versus company and the disputed lost consonant

That disagreement should not be hidden through invented roots or an unwarrantedly certain historical narrative.

3. Witnesses from early Arabic

Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā: a company of companions

The well-known pre-Islamic poet Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā says:

وَقَدْ أَغْدُو عَلَى ثُبَةٍ كِرَامٍ
نَشَاوَى وَاجِدِينَ لِمَا نَشَاءُ

wa-qad aghdū ʿalā thubatin kirāmin / nashāwā wājidīna li-mā nashāʾu

Translation:

“I may go in the morning to a noble company, drunk and having access to what we desire.”

ثُبَة (thubah) here names a company of people.

The context concerns companionship and convivial drinking, rather than military organization or a campaign. It therefore shows that ثُبَة (thubah) could name a group outside the battlefield. نَشَاوَى (nashāwā) here refers to intoxication with wine, rather than mere happiness: the subsequent lines speak of wine and the cup’s effect.

The word itself does not establish the company’s exact membership.

The line is recorded in Zuhayr’s Dīwān and is an important witness to the word’s use in early poetry.

Zuhayr’s Dīwān: thubah, nashāwā, and the drinking context

ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm: bands of horsemen

The Muʿallaqah of ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm contains the following:

فَأَمَّا يَوْمَ خَشْيَتِنَا عَلَيْهِمْ
فَتُصْبِحُ خَيْلُنَا عُصَبًا ثُبِينَا

fa-ammā yawma khashyatina ʿalayhim / fa-tuṣbiḥu khaylunā ʿuṣaban thubīnā

Translation:

“On the day we fear for them, our horsemen form companies and separate bands.”

Here ثُبِينَا (thubīnā) stands alongside عُصَبًا (ʿuṣaban).

The pairing concerns grouping horsemen. The poet describes the mounted body in distinct bands rather than one undivided unit. In relation to the preceding line, Al-Zawzanī takes عَلَيْهِمْ (ʿalayhim) to refer to the children and families whom the horsemen defend against enemies. The phrase does not mean being afraid of those enemies.

Al-Zawzanī’s commentary: defending children and families

This witness matters for Qur’anic ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin), because it illustrates the same field of grouping and collective movement in a military setting.

It does not, however, establish that ثُبَة (thubah) originally meant a military unit of fixed membership.

ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm is a pre-Islamic poet, and this line is recorded in the Muʿallaqah attributed to him.

Labīd: تَثْبِيَة (tathbiyah) as praise

Labīd ibn Rabīʿah says:

يُثَبِّي ثَنَاءً مِنْ كَرِيمٍ وَقَوْلُهُ
أَلَا انْعَمْ عَلَى حُسْنِ التَّحِيَّةِ وَاشْرَبِ

yuthabbī thanāʾan min karīmin wa-qawluhu / alā inʿam ʿalā ḥusni al-taḥiyyati wa-ishrabi

Translation:

“He recounts praise of a noble man; and his saying is: Be well with this fair greeting, and drink.”

يُثَبِّي (yuthabbī) here concerns praise and the recounting of good qualities.

Lexical sources cite this use to explain تَثْبِيَة (tathbiyah). انْعَمْ (inʿam) in this greeting concerns being well or happy; rendering it as benefiting from hospitality adds an explanation beyond the greeting itself.

Sirr Ṣināʿat al-Iʿrāb: Labīd’s witness and collecting praise

Labīd lived through both the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods. The precise date of this line is unclear: it is a valuable witness to early usage, but should not be described as certainly pre-Qur’anic without establishing its date.

Labīd’s Dīwān: the line at page 20 of the source edition

Semantically, the witness belongs to the praise branch. Connecting it to ثُبَة (thubah) as a company requires a separate derivational argument.

Shared letters alone do not make Qur’anic ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) mean praise or perseverance.

4. The Qur’anic use: فَانْفِرُوا ثُبَاتٍ (fa-infirū thubātin)

Al-Nisāʾ 4:71

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا خُذُوا حِذْرَكُمْ فَانفِرُوا ثُبَاتٍ أَوِ انفِرُوا جَمِيعًا

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū khudhū ḥidhrakum fa-infirū thubātin awi infirū jamīʿan

English rendering of Forqan’s current Persian translation:

“You who have believed, take your precautionary readiness; then go out in bands, or go out all together.”

The verse first commands taking حِذْر (ḥidhr), then presents two ways of setting out.

ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) stands alongside جَمِيعًا (jamīʿan). This contrast clarifies the word’s meaning within the verse.

The syntax of ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin)

ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) is the plural of ثُبَة (thubah), in the accusative case here.

It functions as a circumstantial expression, describing how the action فَانْفِرُوا (fa-infirū) is performed.

The verse does not merely say that groups exist: it instructs the addressees to set out as separate bands.

Its accusative marker is kasrah, since this form is a sound feminine plural.

جَمِيعًا (jamīʿan) in the second clause likewise describes the manner of collective movement.

The two expressions thus present two ways of performing one action: setting out in distinct bands or all together.

Al-Rāghib’s Al-Mufradāt: a distinct company and the contrast with jamīʿan

Does ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) mean small groups?

Exegetical explanations sometimes render ثُبَات (thubāt) as small groups or military detachments.

This fits many practical instances: when a large body divides into several separate groups, each group can be smaller than the whole.

The verse’s construction, however, prescribes no particular number for each band. The report of “ten or more” likewise does not establish a fixed membership in this command.

“Small groups” may therefore be a contextual explanation, while “in bands” is a more direct translation that does not assign their size without evidence.

The difference between ثُبَاتٍ and جَمِيعًا (thubātin and jamīʿan)

This difference is central to the root’s Qur’anic use.

ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) names several distinct groups that may move separately.

جَمِيعًا (jamīʿan) in this construction describes collective movement, all together.

أَوِ (awi) stands between them, presenting two possibilities for carrying out the command.

The difference does not imply that one is invariably superior to the other.

The verse assigns neither method absolute superiority. It first commands caution and readiness, then presents two forms of movement.

One may infer that different situations allow different ways of setting out. The details of military decisions, the number of detachments, and their deployment sequence do not come from ثُبَات (thubāt) itself.

Warfare and the need for readiness

Al-Nisāʾ 4:71 belongs to a passage concerning conflict, danger, and individual responsibility in confrontation.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:72 mentions those who hang back and, when misfortune occurs, regard their absence from the others as a favor to themselves.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:73 discusses their response when favor and victory come instead.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:74 concerns fighting in God’s path. Al-Nisāʾ 4:75 speaks of men, women, and children living under oppression and seeking deliverance.

This setting connects فَانْفِرُوا (fa-infirū) with readiness and setting out in conditions of conflict.

ثُبَات (thubāt) should therefore not be detached from its actual setting here and converted solely into intellectual, religious, or social groups.

At the same time, the lexical meaning of ثُبَة (thubah) is not restricted to warfare, as Zuhayr’s poetic witness shows.

The word’s general meaning must thus be distinguished from its military application in Al-Nisāʾ 4:71.

Does the verse command permanent division of believers into groups?

ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) itself yields no such conclusion.

Grouping here describes how انْفِرُوا (infirū) is to be carried out: the subject is the manner of setting out in the situation addressed.

The word does not discuss establishing religious sects, permanent identities, or doctrinal divisions within society.

The verse also places setting out all together alongside setting out in bands.

The root’s use therefore neither establishes that group separation is always valuable nor prohibits grouping.

Sentence structure and the subject of movement guide its interpretation.

5. Comparison with related roots

ث ب ي and ث ب ت (TH B Y and TH B T): grouping and firmness

Study of TH B T

This comparison is especially important because unvocalized ثُبَات (thubāt) can be confused with ثَبَات (thabāt).

ثَبَات (thabāt), from ث ب ت (TH B T), means firmness and steadfastness.

ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) in Al-Nisāʾ 4:71, by contrast, is the plural of ثُبَة (thubah), a company.

The difference is more than a short vowel: two distinct root analyses and meanings are involved.

Al-Anfāl 8:45

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا لَقِيتُمْ فِئَةً فَاثْبُتُوا وَاذْكُرُوا اللَّهَ كَثِيرًا لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū idhā laqītum fiʾatan fa-uthbutū wa-udhkurū Allāha kathīran laʿallakum tufliḥūna

Translation:

“You who have believed, when you meet a company, stand firm and remember The God much, so that you may prosper.”

فَاثْبُتُوا (fa-uthbutū) here comes from ث ب ت (TH B T), expressing firmness and steadfastness in confrontation.

ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) in Al-Nisāʾ 4:71, on the other hand, concerns separate bands.

Standing firm in confrontation and forming bands are distinct ideas, although both may arise in warfare.

ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) must not be translated as “steadfastly” merely because of its spelling resemblance.

ث ب ي and ج م ع (TH B Y and J M ʿ): separate bands and gathering

Study of J M ʿ

ج م ع (J M ʿ) denotes gathering and bringing together.

ثُبَة (thubah) also names a company, but its plural in Al-Nisāʾ 4:71 refers to several separate groups.

In that same verse, جَمِيعًا (jamīʿan) stands alongside ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin).

Being a group and setting out all together are not identical. Several groups may move separately, or individuals may set out as one collective body.

ثُبَات (thubāt) thus emphasizes distinct bands, while jamīʿan emphasizes collective movement.

ث ب ي and ف ر ق (TH B Y and F R Q): a company and separation

Study of F R Q

ف ر ق (F R Q) concerns things becoming separate and distinct from one another.

ثُبَة (thubah) names a company; in ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin), several distinct companies are present.

The families consequently come close in the idea of grouping.

فِرْقَة (firqah), however, can emphasize separation and distinction, whereas ثُبَة (thubah) identifies the group itself.

This distinction means that ثُبَات (thubāt) in the verse does not mean creating disagreement or conflict among believers. Separation of bands in deployment is not necessarily doctrinal separation or hostility.

ث ب ي and ف و ج (TH B Y and F W J): a group and a moving company

Study of F W J

فَوْج (fawj) also names a group of people and may accompany movement or entry in groups.

The Qur’an uses أَفْوَاجًا (afwājan) at Al-Nabaʾ 78:18 for people coming in companies.

ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) in Al-Nisāʾ 4:71 describes setting out in separate bands.

Both words concern human groups, but their uses in these two verses differ.

أَفْوَاجًا (afwājan) there concerns people coming on the Day of Resurrection, while ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) concerns movement in conditions of readiness and confrontation.

Neither verse’s particular subject should therefore be converted into the root’s lexical meaning itself.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

For the general use of ثُبَة (thubah), “group,” “company,” and “band” are natural equivalents.

In a military setting, “a troop of horsemen” or “a band of combatants” may fit. These particular applications should not be imposed on the word’s general definition.

For ثُبَات (thubāt), “groups” and “bands” are suitable; in a construction such as Al-Nisāʾ 4:71, “in groups” or “in separate bands” works naturally.

For non-Qur’anic تَثْبِيَة (tathbiyah), context determines the equivalent: persevering, collecting, adding, or praising. None of these meanings should replace the company sense in Qur’anic ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) without evidence.

The Qur’anic form: ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin)

Brief root meaning: A company or band; people gathered in a group. Its derivation from TH B Y or TH B W is disputed; some reports also propose TH W B.

Meaning in this verse: In groups and separate bands, describing the manner of setting out.

English rendering of Forqan’s existing Persian translation:

“You who have believed, take your precautionary readiness; then go out in bands, or go out all together.”

This translation preserves the contrast between ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) and جَمِيعًا (jamīʿan).

“In bands” is suitable because it naturally describes the manner of movement without assigning a number of members to each group.

Lexical research therefore supplies no reason to change this part of Forqan’s current provisional translation.

7. Conclusion

ثُبَة (thubah) in Arabic denotes a company or band of people; ثُبَات (thubāt) is its plural. Early poetry and lexical sources clearly attest this meaning.

Its use for companions and horsemen shows that the company sense is not inherently restricted to warfare, although Al-Nisāʾ 4:71 uses it in a setting of readiness and deployment for confrontation.

In that verse, ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) stands alongside جَمِيعًا (jamīʿan). The distinction is between setting out in separate bands and setting out all together. It clarifies the word’s Qur’anic meaning without prescribing a fixed number, size, or structure for the groups.

The third consonant in ثُبَة (thubah) is disputed: some lexicographers identify yāʾ, others wāw. The basin sense may also belong to the different family ث و ب (TH W B). These disagreements must remain visible rather than being concealed by an invented semantic picture. Some reports also connect the company sense with TH W B; the choice between final yāʾ and final wāw therefore does not exhaust all recorded views.

ثُبَاتٍ (thubātin) must not be confused with ثَبَات (thabāt), firmness or steadfastness. The Qur’anic word is the plural of ثُبَة (thubah); within the sentence, it means being in groups.

“Go out in bands” is therefore an accurate and natural rendering in Al-Nisāʾ 4:71. It preserves distinct companies and their contrast with setting out collectively.
