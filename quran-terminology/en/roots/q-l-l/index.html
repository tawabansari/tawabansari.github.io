Core Meaning

The root ق ل ل points, at its base, to being little, being few, decreasing, becoming light, or becoming slight in relation to something else. This root is not only about number and quantity. At times it speaks about few people; at times about shortness of time; at times about the slightness of worldly enjoyment before the Hereafter; at times about the little amount of gratitude, faith, remembrance, or understanding; and at times about one group being made to appear few in the eyes of another group.

In the Qur'an, qalil is often a relational word. Something is judged qalil when compared with something greater, more lasting, heavier, or more important. The world appears large in the limited gaze of the human being, but before the Hereafter it is qalil. Human knowledge matters in human life, but before the command of the Lord it is qalil. Human gratitude before the favors of The God is qalil.

So qalil is not only “little.” Sometimes it means something that has little weight, range, or permanence in the scale of truth.

Important Forms

قَلَّ is a past-tense verb and points to being little or becoming little:

مِمَّا قَلَّ مِنْهُ أَوْ كَثُرَ

From what of it is little or much.

Al-Nisa 4:7

In this verse, قَلَّ comes opposite كَثُرَ. The base meaning is clear: being little in contrast with being much.

يُقَلِّلُ is from form II and points to making something appear few, making it small in perception, or making it look smaller in another's eyes:

وَيُقَلِّلُكُمْ فِي أَعْيُنِهِمْ

And He was making you appear few in their eyes.

Al-Anfal 8:44

Here Q L L is not only actual numerical fewness; it is also being seen as few in the perception of the other side.

أَقَلَّتْ is from form IV and appears in the Qur'an in the context of carrying clouds:

حَتَّىٰ إِذَا أَقَلَّتْ سَحَابًا ثِقَالًا

Until, when it lifted/carried heavy clouds.

Al-A'raf 7:57

This form should not be translated as “made fewer.” Here the meaning is connected with lifting, raising, and carrying. This use is not unrelated to the field of lightness and capacity to be carried, but because the verse speaks of سحابا ثقالا, heavy clouds, it should not be simplified. It is better to say: the winds lift and carry the heavy clouds; that is, the force of movement overcomes their weight and stillness.

أقلّ is a comparative form and points to being less:

إِنْ تَرَنِ أَنَا أَقَلَّ مِنْكَ مَالًا وَوَلَدًا

If you see me as less than you in wealth and children.

Al-Kahf 18:39

فَسَيَعْلَمُونَ مَنْ أَضْعَفُ نَاصِرًا وَأَقَلُّ عَدَدًا

Then they will know who is weaker in helper and fewer in number.

Al-Jinn 72:24

قليل is the most important form of this root in the Qur'an. It can be used for the number of people, the amount of time, worldly enjoyment, the measure of gratitude, faith, reminder, understanding, or human knowledge.

قَلِيلًا مَا تَشْكُرُونَ

How little you give thanks.

Al-A'raf 7:10

قليلة is the feminine form:

كَمْ مِنْ فِئَةٍ قَلِيلَةٍ غَلَبَتْ فِئَةً كَثِيرَةً بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ

How many a small group has overcome a large group by the permission of The God.

Al-Baqarah 2:249

In this verse, qalilah stands opposite kathirah. But the verse is not only about number. The Qur'an shows that numerical fewness does not always mean real weakness.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of Q L L turns around littleness, reduction, lightening, limited range, and becoming slight in relation to something else. The root is used in counting, in evaluation, in perception, in time, and in human action and response.

Qalil is not always absolute. The question must be asked: little in relation to what? Few compared with what? The world may seem vast and attractive in itself, but in relation to the Hereafter it is qalil. Human knowledge may be important and useful in human life, but in relation to the command of the Lord it is qalil. Human gratitude before gifts such as hearing, sights, and hearts is qalil; that is, there is no proportion between the great gift and the small human response.

So qalil in the Qur'an can be numerical, temporal, evaluative, perceptual, or ethical. It is one of the Qur'an's tools for breaking the illusion that small things are great.

Material Image

The material image of Q L L can be seen in a scale, a measure, counting, or the gaze of the eye. When something is lighter in the pan of a scale, it has less weight in relation to something else. When a container has only a little water, it is qalil beside a full container. When a group stands before a large army, it appears few by sight and count.

But the Qur'an takes this simple image into deeper fields. Worldly life is like an enjoyment that appears large in the human hand, but when placed before the Hereafter it becomes qalil. A human being may think he knows much, but when his knowledge is measured before the command of The God, the Qur'an says:

وَمَا أُوتِيتُمْ مِنَ الْعِلْمِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

And you have not been given of knowledge except a little.

Al-Isra 17:85

Another material image appears in the verse of the clouds:

حَتَّىٰ إِذَا أَقَلَّتْ سَحَابًا ثِقَالًا

Until, when it lifted/carried heavy clouds.

Al-A'raf 7:57

Here the winds carry heavy clouds. This use shows that the root Q L L, in some branches, also connects with lifting and carrying. Something carried is placed under a force that lifts it from its place and moves it along a path. But because the verse itself calls the clouds ثقالا, heavy, oversimplification must be avoided. The issue here is not that the clouds are “few”; it is that they are carried in the field of the winds' movement.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, qalil stands opposite kathir and points to fewness or littleness in number, amount, time, wealth, force, or people. From this field, aqall means less, and qillah means fewness or scarcity.

The branch أقلّت, meaning carried or lifted, in Al-A'raf 7:57, must be seen carefully. It shows that one root can produce close but non-identical semantic branches in different forms and patterns. Forqan should therefore not force every derivative under one flat and simple translation.

Here the main reliance is on Qur'anic usage, because the Qur'an uses this root across many fields: inheritance, battle, worldly enjoyment, gratitude, faith, reminder, knowledge, time, number of people, carrying clouds, and the enemy's perception.

Qalil Against Kathir: Numerical or Quantitative Fewness

The base use of the root is being little in contrast with being much:

مِمَّا قَلَّ مِنْهُ أَوْ كَثُرَ

From what of it is little or much.

Al-Nisa 4:7

In this verse, qalla and kathura stand opposite each other. The subject is inheritance: whether what remains is little or much. This use shows the base meaning of the root: little quantity in contrast with much.

كَمْ مِنْ فِئَةٍ قَلِيلَةٍ غَلَبَتْ فِئَةً كَثِيرَةً بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ

How many a small group has overcome a large group by the permission of The God.

Al-Baqarah 2:249

Here qalilah stands opposite kathirah. But the verse is not merely a numerical comparison. The Qur'an shows that numerical fewness, when joined with permission from The God, patience, and firmness, does not prevent victory. Therefore being few in number does not always mean defeat or worthlessness.

Qalil About the Number of Believers or the Saved

In several verses, qalil speaks about the small number of those who believed, remained loyal, or restrained corruption:

وَمَا آمَنَ مَعَهُ إِلَّا قَلِيلٌ

And none believed with him except a few.

Hud 11:40

Here qalil points to the small number of believers with Nuh. But the verse does not lower their value. On the contrary, it shows that truth is not always measured by numerical majority.

فَلَمَّا كُتِبَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْقِتَالُ تَوَلَّوْا إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِنْهُمْ

Then when fighting was written upon them, they turned away, except a few of them.

Al-Baqarah 2:246

Here qalil points to a minority that remained in a hard test. In this context, the few are few in number, but weighty in loyalty and steadfastness.

فَلَوْلَا كَانَ مِنَ الْقُرُونِ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ أُولُو بَقِيَّةٍ يَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْفَسَادِ فِي الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِمَّنْ أَنْجَيْنَا مِنْهُمْ

Why were there not, among the generations before you, people of remaining virtue who restrained corruption in the earth, except a few of those We saved from among them?

Hud 11:116

Here qalil shows the fewness of those who stood against corruption. This fewness is not worthless fewness; it is a rare and weighty fewness.

Qalil About Worldly Enjoyment and Short Time

One important use of qalil is to make the value of the world small before the Hereafter:

قُلْ مَتَاعُ الدُّنْيَا قَلِيلٌ وَالْآخِرَةُ خَيْرٌ لِمَنِ اتَّقَىٰ

Say: the enjoyment of the world is little, and the Hereafter is better for whoever has taqwa.

Al-Nisa 4:77

Here qalil does not only mean that worldly blessings are small in quantity. The world may seem large to the human being, but when measured against the Hereafter it is qalil. Therefore its qalil-ness is relational: the world becomes small before the Hereafter.

فَمَا مَتَاعُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا فِي الْآخِرَةِ إِلَّا قَلِيلٌ

The enjoyment of worldly life, in relation to the Hereafter, is nothing but little.

Al-Tawbah 9:38

In this verse, the phrase في الآخرة clarifies the scale of comparison. The world may be dazzling in itself, but in relation to the Hereafter it is qalil. Here qalil is not numerical fewness; it is the lightening of an enjoyment when placed in comparison with something greater.

Qalil is also used for a short time:

قَالَ عَمَّا قَلِيلٍ لَيُصْبِحُنَّ نَادِمِينَ

He said: after a little while, they will surely become regretful.

Al-Mu'minun 23:40

وَذَرْنِي وَالْمُكَذِّبِينَ أُولِي النَّعْمَةِ وَمَهِّلْهُمْ قَلِيلًا

Leave Me with the deniers, those of comfort, and give them a little respite.

Al-Muzzammil 73:11

In these uses, qalil points to shortness of time or the limited nature of respite.

Qalil About Gratitude, Faith, Reminder, and Understanding

One very important field of this root is the smallness of the human being's proper response before the favors and signs of The God:

وَجَعَلْنَا لَكُمْ فِيهَا مَعَايِشَ ۗ قَلِيلًا مَا تَشْكُرُونَ

And We placed for you in it means of livelihood; how little you give thanks.

Al-A'raf 7:10

Here qalil points to the smallness of gratitude before the abundance of favor. The favor is vast, but the human being's gratitude is little. The same structure appears in other verses:

وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَالْأَفْئِدَةَ ۚ قَلِيلًا مَا تَشْكُرُونَ

And He made for you hearing, sights, and hearts; how little you give thanks.

Al-Sajdah 32:9

Here the smallness of gratitude stands before the foundational tools of knowing: hearing, sights, and hearts. The divine input and gift are great, but the human output in gratitude and right response is little. Qalil here is not just the small amount of an action; it is the lack of proportion between a great gift and a small human response.

About faith, it says:

بَلْ لَعَنَهُمُ اللَّهُ بِكُفْرِهِمْ فَقَلِيلًا مَا يُؤْمِنُونَ

Rather, The God distanced them because of their kufr, so how little they believe.

Al-Baqarah 2:88

About reminder:

قَلِيلًا مَا تَذَكَّرُونَ

How little you remember.

Al-A'raf 7:3

And about understanding:

بَلْ كَانُوا لَا يَفْقَهُونَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

Rather, they did not understand except a little.

Al-Fath 48:15

These uses show that qalil in the Qur'an is not only external number or quantity. It can also show the quality and depth of human response. A human being may have tools of understanding, but his understanding may be qalil; he may have favor, but his gratitude may be qalil; he may hear signs, but his reminder may be qalil.

Qalil About Human Knowledge

One of the deepest uses appears in the verse of the spirit:

وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الرُّوحِ ۖ قُلِ الرُّوحُ مِنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّي وَمَا أُوتِيتُمْ مِنَ الْعِلْمِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

And they ask you about the spirit. Say: the spirit is from the command of my Lord, and you have not been given of knowledge except a little.

Al-Isra 17:85

Here qalil places the human being in the right epistemic position. The human being has knowledge, but that knowledge is limited and little before the command of The God. The verse does not deny knowledge, nor does it forbid questioning; it shows the measure of human knowledge before divine command.

This use matters for the method of Forqan. The qalil-ness of human knowledge means that one must approach mutashabihat, the unseen, the spirit, and realities beyond experience with humility. Claims of complete enclosure do not fit this verse.

Qalil in Battle Perception and Seeing the Enemy as Few

In Surat Al-Anfal, qalil enters the field of battle perception:

إِذْ يُرِيكَهُمُ اللَّهُ فِي مَنَامِكَ قَلِيلًا

When The God showed them to you in your sleep as few.

Al-Anfal 8:43

وَإِذْ يُرِيكُمُوهُمْ إِذِ الْتَقَيْتُمْ فِي أَعْيُنِكُمْ قَلِيلًا وَيُقَلِّلُكُمْ فِي أَعْيُنِهِمْ

And when, at the time you met, He showed them to you as few in your eyes, and made you appear few in their eyes.

Al-Anfal 8:44

In these verses, qalil is not only a numerical report. It is tied to perception and sight. One group is made to appear few in the eyes of another. In the context of the verse, this being made to appear few is part of divine arrangement for an affair that had to be accomplished.

This use shows that Q L L sometimes enters the field of perception. Something may have one measure in reality, but appear little or much in the human gaze. The Qur'an shows this difference between reality and perception with precision. Seeing as few here is not the actual shrinking of body or number; it is a change in the field of perception and calculation on both sides.

Qalil Against Kathir: The Defeated Few and the Victorious Few

The Qur'an does not always treat qalil negatively. Sometimes being qalil indicates weakness, insufficient response, or poor action, as in little gratitude or little faith. But sometimes being qalil points to a steadfast and valuable minority:

كَمْ مِنْ فِئَةٍ قَلِيلَةٍ غَلَبَتْ فِئَةً كَثِيرَةً بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ

How many a small group has overcome a large group by the permission of The God.

Al-Baqarah 2:249

In this verse, being few in number is not equal to defeat. The final measure is not merely number. If there is permission from The God, patience, faith, and steadfastness, numerical fewness can overcome numerical abundance.

By contrast, in the verses of gratitude and faith, qalil-ness shows deficiency in human response. Therefore qalil must be understood through context: sometimes it is numerical fewness, sometimes evaluative fewness, sometimes ethical smallness, and sometimes an apparent smallness that becomes less important before a greater quality.

Aqallat: Carrying Heavy Clouds

The form أقلّت appears in the verse of Al-A'raf:

حَتَّىٰ إِذَا أَقَلَّتْ سَحَابًا ثِقَالًا سُقْنَاهُ لِبَلَدٍ مَيِّتٍ

Until, when it lifted/carried heavy clouds, We drove it to a dead land.

Al-A'raf 7:57

This use must be seen separately and precisely. أقلّت here does not mean “made fewer”; it points to lifting and carrying heavy clouds. The phrase سحابا ثقالا shows that the topic is not the fewness of clouds, but the carrying of heavy clouds along the path of the winds.

One may say that this use has a connection with the root's main field from the side of relative lightness and the ability to be carried: something is lifted and carried when the carrying force overcomes its weight and resistance. But this connection should not be turned into a forced, one-line claim. In the verse itself, the clouds are heavy, yet they are carried by the movement of the winds.

This verse shows that root study should not translate all derivatives with one simple and direct meaning. Form, pattern, and context can activate a particular branch of meaning.

Similar Uses

Qalil does not mean the same thing everywhere. In Al-Nisa 4:7, qalla comes opposite kathura and means little in amount. In Al-Baqarah 2:249, fi'ah qalilah means a group few in number. In Al-Nisa 4:77, mata' al-dunya qalil means the world is limited and low in worth in relation to the Hereafter. In Al-Isra 17:85, human knowledge is qalil before the command of The God. In Al-Anfal 8:44, qalil is tied to appearing few in the eyes of the other side.

Qalil is sometimes negative, as in little gratitude, faith, understanding, or reminder. But it is not always negative, as in a small group overcoming a large group by the permission of The God. Therefore qalil-ness should not always be understood as contempt. Sometimes numerical fewness comes with faithful value and steadfastness.

Qalil is not the same as saghir. Saghir points more to smallness of size, age, or outward rank. Qalil points more to amount, number, time, enjoyment, or relational value.

Qalil is also not the same as bakhs. Bakhs means wrongfully diminishing or withholding due value. Qalil may be natural and real; bakhs usually carries the weight of injustice and wrongful reduction.

أقلّت must also be separated from qalil in the sense of “little.” In Al-A'raf 7:57, aqallat means carried, lifted, and moved along the path; not that it made the clouds few.

Grammar and Structure

In قليلًا ما تشكرون, the structure قليلا ما intensifies the smallness: how little you give thanks. This is not only a statistical report; it criticizes the relation between abundant favor and the small human response.

In إلا قليلا, qalil marks exception. Sometimes it means that only a few remained, believed, or accompanied:

تَوَلَّوْا إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِنْهُمْ

They turned away except a few of them.

Al-Baqarah 2:246

In متاع الدنيا قليل, qalil is the predicate for the enjoyment of the world and states the world's relational value. The continuation of the verse clarifies the scale: the Hereafter is better for the people of taqwa.

In فما متاع الحياة الدنيا في الآخرة إلا قليل, the letter في clarifies the comparative field. When the enjoyment of the world is measured in relation to the Hereafter, it is nothing but little.

In ما أوتيتم من العلم إلا قليلا, the structure of negation and exception shows the limit of human knowledge. Knowledge has been given, but in relation to the command of the Lord, it is little.

In أقل منك مالا وولدا, aqall is comparative and shows comparison between two people: less than you in wealth and children.

In يقللكم في أعينهم, the letter في shows the place of perception: in their eyes. The issue is not actual decreasing, but being seen as few in the other side's gaze.

In أقلت سحابا ثقالا, the object سحابا ثقالا shows that the verb is about carrying heavy clouds. Therefore “made few” is incorrect here.

Nearby Roots

ك ث ر is the clear opposite of Q L L. K TH R points to being many, abundant, and numerous. Qalil and kathir in the Qur'an are sometimes numerical, but final value is not always with abundance.

ص غ ر points to smallness, youth, or low outward rank and size. Qalil is more tied to smallness of amount, number, time, or benefit. Something can be not small but still qalil; or qalil but not small in value.

ن ق ص points to being reduced from a quantity, being diminished, or becoming deficient. Qalil is not necessarily deficiency; something may be little from the start. Naqs more often points to reduction from a complete or necessary measure.

ب خ س points to wrongful reduction of right or value. Bakhs carries the weight of injustice and undergiving; qalil simply conveys little-ness and is not necessarily injustice.

خ ف ف points to lightness in weight or heaviness. Qalil is tied to amount, number, range, and relational value; khafif is more about being light, less heavy, or easier to move and carry.

ح ق ر is close to considering something contemptible or low. Qalil may be treated as contemptible in a human gaze, but qalil itself does not mean contempt. Contempt is a lowering value judgment; qalil is being little or being counted as little.

ض ع ف points to weakness and inability. Numerical fewness may accompany weakness, but they are not the same. A فئة قليلة in Al-Baqarah 2:249 is numerically few, but not necessarily weak.

ق ص ر points to shortness, limitation, or not reaching full extension. Qalil can be used for short time, but Q S R is more tied to shortness or restriction of extension.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

قَلَّ: was little, was few, decreased.

قليل: little, few, slight, limited, small in scope; according to context.

قليلا ما تشكرون: how little you give thanks; you give very little thanks; your gratitude before the favors is little; your response to this great gift is slight.

متاع الدنيا قليل: the enjoyment of the world is little; worldly enjoyment is slight before the Hereafter; the enjoyment of the world is limited and small in scope.

فما متاع الحياة الدنيا في الآخرة إلا قليل: the enjoyment of worldly life in relation to the Hereafter is nothing but little; when worldly enjoyment is measured in the field of the Hereafter, it is slight.

إلا قليلا: except a few; except a small group; except a little amount.

قليل من عبادي الشكور: few of My servants are deeply grateful; truly grateful ones among My servants are few.

فئة قليلة: a small group; a few-numbered group.

أقل منك مالا وولدا: less than you in wealth and children.

أقل عددا: fewer in number; more few-numbered.

يقللكم في أعينهم: He was making you appear few in their eyes; He was making you look few-numbered in their gaze.

أقلت سحابا ثقالا: it lifted/carried heavy clouds; it took up the burden of heavy clouds; it brought heavy clouds into movement and carrying.

Summary

The root Q L L points to being little, becoming few, becoming light or slight in relation to a greater matter, and sometimes appearing few in human perception. On the material level, its image can be understood through a scale, counting, a container with little content, a short time, a few-numbered group, or something carried before a greater force. In the Qur'an, this root expands from little quantity and few people to little gratitude, faith, reminder, understanding, knowledge, worldly enjoyment, time, and appearing few in the field of perception.

Qalil in the Qur'an is not always contempt. Sometimes fewness shows weakness of human response, as in little gratitude and little faith. Sometimes fewness is simply numerical, as in a small group. And sometimes that same small group, by the permission of The God, overcomes a large group. Qalil must therefore always be understood in relation to context.

The difference between qalil and saghir is that saghir more directly shows smallness, while qalil shows smallness of amount, number, range, or relation. The difference with naqs is that naqs is reduction from a needed measure, while qalil may be natural or relational fewness. The difference with bakhs is that bakhs is wrongful reduction of right, while qalil is not necessarily injustice or undergiving.

In Forqan translation, Q L L should not always be translated only as “little.” At times “few,” “slight,” “limited,” “few-numbered,” “short-lived,” “small in scope,” “slight in relation to a greater matter,” or “made to appear few in the gaze” will be more precise.
