Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root B R Q

Flashing, lightning, sudden brightness, and a sharp momentary disclosure through intense light

Important Forms from This Root

بَرَقَ / يَبْرُقُ (baraqa / yabruqu)

baraqa / yabruqu; flashed, shone suddenly, produced a sharp brightness.

بَرْق (barq)

barq; lightning, sudden flash, brief intense brightness.

بَرِيق (barīq)

barīq; brightness, gleam, sharp shining appearance.

بَارِق (bāriq)

bāriq; flashing, shining, gleaming.

أَبْرَقَ (abraqa)

abraqa; flashed, made something flash, entered a state of brightness.

بَرَّاق / بَرْقَاء (barrāq / barqāʾ)

barrāq; very bright or flashing. Barqāʾ; piebald or marked by light and dark patches, in lexical usage.

Central Meaning

The root B R Q means flashing, lightning, sudden brightness, a brief sharp gleam, and momentary disclosure through intense light.

In the Qur’an, بَرْق (barq) appears in al-Baqarah 2:19 and 2:20 beside ṣayyib, darknesses, and thunder. In that scene, lightning is not calm, steady light. It is a sudden flash that tears through darkness for a moment, then disappears.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of B R Q is sudden, sharp, eye-catching brightness. Lightning is not the same as light in general. Light may be steady, guiding, inward, or calm. Lightning is usually brief, intense, dazzling, and unstable.

Lightning also differs from ḍiyāʾ. Ḍiyāʾ is active radiance and illuminating brightness. Barq emphasizes a sudden leap of light, a flash that appears and vanishes.

In material usage, this root is not limited to lightning in the sky. The gleam of a sword, the flash of an eye, the shine of metal, a polished surface, or a bright patch against a darker field can all approach this root’s semantic field. What joins these uses is a sharp brightness that catches the eye.

So this root gathers several elements: intensity, suddenness, short duration, unstable disclosure, the breaking of darkness by brightness, and surface shine or reflection.

Material Image

The first material image is lightning in a dark sky. Clouds have gathered. The sky is heavy. Suddenly a line of light leaps through the darkness. For a moment, the earth, the path, objects, and the horizon become visible. Then darkness returns.

This image is essential for reading the Qur’anic use. Lightning gives light, but it does not remain. It can show the way for a moment, but it cannot be trusted as a permanent lamp. A person may move during its flash, but he cannot build a stable path upon it.

The second image is surface gleam: the flash of a sword, the sparkle of an eye, the shine of metal, or the brightness of something polished. This shows that B R Q is not only meteorological. Any sudden, sharp brightness may enter its field.

The third image is a bright patch against a darker background. In lexical usage, بَرْقَاء (barqāʾ) can describe something piebald or marked by light and dark patches. This image fits the sky: bright whiteness appearing suddenly within a dark field.

The fourth image is polish and reflected shine. This layer should be supported by precise lexical evidence before being used as a final proof, but semantically it belongs near gleam, shine, and brightness that catches the eye.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, بَرْق (barq) refers to lightning, especially in the context of clouds and rain. It is also used for the shining of the eye, sword, metal, and other bright or gleaming things.

For desert life, lightning carried two feelings at once: fear and hope. It could signal rain and mercy, but it could also accompany thunder, storm, and thunderbolt. Lightning was therefore not merely beautiful light; it was a sign that the sky had changed and that either water or danger might be near.

The gleam of a sword or eye can also be described through this root, because both involve sudden, sharp, effective brightness. The use of بَرْقَاء (barqāʾ) for a piebald animal or a surface with light and dark patches also shows the root’s connection with brightness appearing within darkness.

If later we add verified examples from early Arabic poetry or lexical sources, this section can become more fully documented. Until then, it is better not to present any unverified example as if it were a fixed proof.

Qur’anic Usage

Lightning in the Parable of al-Baqarah

Surat al-Baqarah says: أَوْ كَصَيِّبٍ مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ فِيهِ ظُلُمَاتٌ وَرَعْدٌ وَبَرْقٌ (aw kaṣayyibin min al-samāʾi fīhi ẓulumātun waraʿdun wabarqun); or like a pouring rain from the sky, within it are darknesses, thunder, and lightning 2:19.

Here, lightning is one part of a full scene. Ṣayyib is pouring rain. Darknesses close the field of sight. Thunder shakes the field of hearing. Lightning suddenly lights the scene.

Lightning here is not steady light. It is a flash that appears inside darkness and disappears again.

Lightning and Momentary Movement

The next verse says: يَكَادُ الْبَرْقُ يَخْطَفُ أَبْصَارَهُمْ كُلَّمَا أَضَاءَ لَهُم مَّشَوْا فِيهِ وَإِذَا أَظْلَمَ عَلَيْهِمْ قَامُوا (yakādu al-barqu yakhṭafu abṣārahum kullamā aḍāʾa lahum mashaw fīhi wa-idhā aẓlama ʿalayhim qāmū); lightning almost snatches away their sight. Whenever it gives light for them, they walk in it; and when darkness falls over them, they stand still 2:20.

This verse is central for understanding the root. Lightning gives light, but not in a stable way. They move only when the flash gives them a momentary path. When darkness returns, they stop.

The phrase يَخْطَفُ أَبْصَارَهُمْ (yakhṭafu abṣārahum) shows the severity of the flash. Khaṭf means sudden snatching. The lightning is so intense that the eye has no time to adjust; sight is almost seized by the flash.

This is different from the light of guidance. Guiding light is steady, path-making, and continuous. In this parable, lightning is sharp, sudden, and unstable.

Lightning Between Fear and Hope

Surat al-Raʿd says: هُوَ الَّذِي يُرِيكُمُ الْبَرْقَ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا وَيُنْشِئُ السَّحَابَ الثِّقَالَ (huwa alladhī yurīkumu al-barqa khawfan waṭamaʿan wayunshiʾu al-saḥāba al-thiqāla); He is the One who shows you the lightning for fear and hope, and He brings forth the heavy clouds 13:12.

This verse shows the double human experience of lightning: fear and hope. Fear, because lightning may come with thunder, thunderbolt, storm, and danger. Hope, because it may signal rain, water, and mercy.

So in the Qur’an, lightning is not only a sudden brightness. It is a sign that places the human being between fear and hope.

The Flashing Eye on the Day of Rising

Surat al-Qiyāmah says: فَإِذَا بَرِقَ الْبَصَرُ (fa-idhā bariqa al-baṣaru); then, when the sight is dazzled and struck into a flash-like stare 75:7.

Here, the root moves from the sky to the human eye. بَرِقَ الْبَصَرُ (bariqa al-baṣaru) means the eye becomes dazzled, stunned, or flash-struck before something overwhelming.

This is not the cheerful English expression “his eyes lit up.” The context of al-Qiyāmah is fear, collapse of ordinary order, and confrontation with a tremendous reality. The eye is brightened or fixed by shock, not by delight.

Lightning and Light

Lightning and light are not the same. Light can be stable, guiding, calm, and path-making. In the Qur’an, light has a wide semantic field: The God, the Book, faith, guidance, the moon, and clarification.

Lightning is momentary, sudden, intense, and sometimes dazzling. In al-Baqarah 2:20, lightning lights the way for a moment, but it does not replace steady light. They move by the flash, then stop when darkness returns.

So lightning illuminates, but it does not necessarily give lasting guidance.

Lightning and Ḍiyāʾ

Ḍiyāʾ is active radiance, brightness that shines and makes things visible. Lightning is a sudden flash. It has intensity, but not duration. Ḍiyāʾ is closer to active, broader illumination; barq is closer to the leap of a momentary flash.

Lightning and Thunder

Lightning and thunder often appear together in the sky, but they belong to different fields. Lightning is visual; it seizes the eye. Thunder is auditory; it shakes the ear, space, and body.

In al-Baqarah 2:19 and 2:20, lightning is tied to أَضَاءَ لَهُمْ (aḍāʾa lahum), giving light for them, and خَطْفِ أَبْصَارِهِمْ (khaṭfi abṣārihim), the snatching of their sight. Thunder is tied to fear, sound, and thunderbolts.

Difference from Nearby Roots

N W R is light, guidance, manifestation, and the ability to discern. Light is broader and more stable; lightning is momentary and leaping.

Ḍ W ʾ is shining, radiating, and illuminating. Ḍiyāʾ may illuminate a field; barq is sudden and unstable.

L M ʿ is flashing or gleaming, close to lightning and shine. But B R Q is more strongly tied to heavenly lightning and sudden intensity. L W Ḥ may approach appearing, becoming visible, or flashing from afar; barq emphasizes the leap of light.

R AYN D (R ʿ D) is thunder, the roar of the sky. Thunder belongs to hearing; lightning belongs to seeing. Ṣ AYN Q (Ṣ ʿ Q) is thunderbolt, stunning, or destructive heavenly strike. Thunderbolt may accompany lightning, but thunderbolt is the striking danger; lightning is the flash.

KH Ṭ F is sudden snatching. In al-Baqarah 2:20, the lightning almost snatches their sight. Khaṭf is the act of snatching; lightning is the intense light that causes the danger.

B Y Ḍ is whiteness. Barqāʾ can relate to light and dark patches, but B R Q emphasizes gleam and sudden bright appearance.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, بَرْق (barq) can usually be translated as “lightning,” because the English word is clear. But the root explanation should clarify that barq is a sudden and brief brightness, not stable guiding light.

In al-Baqarah 2:19, lightning is part of a scene of heavy rain, darkness, and thunder. In al-Baqarah 2:20, lightning is tied to momentary light, the near-snatching of sight, and unstable movement. In al-Raʿd 13:12, lightning must be read through the double experience of fear and hope. In al-Qiyāmah 75:7, بَرِقَ الْبَصَرُ (bariqa al-baṣaru) should not be rendered as happy brightness of the eyes; the context points to stunned, dazzled sight before overwhelming reality.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

بَرْق (barq): lightning, sudden flash, brief sharp brightness.

بَرَقَ / يَبْرُقُ (baraqa / yabruqu): flashed, shone suddenly, became dazzled or flash-struck, depending on context.

بَرِيق (barīq): gleam, brightness, sharp shining appearance.

بَارِق (bāriq): flashing, shining, gleaming.

بَرَّاق (barrāq): very bright, intensely gleaming.

بَرْقَاء (barqāʾ): marked by light and dark patches, piebald, in lexical usage.

فِيهِ ظُلُمَاتٌ وَرَعْدٌ وَبَرْقٌ (fīhi ẓulumātun waraʿdun wabarqun): within it are darknesses, thunder, and lightning; or within it are darknesses, the roar of the sky, and the flash of lightning.

يَكَادُ الْبَرْقُ يَخْطَفُ أَبْصَارَهُمْ (yakādu al-barqu yakhṭafu abṣārahum): lightning almost snatches away their sight; or the sudden sharp flash nearly seizes their vision.

يُرِيكُمُ الْبَرْقَ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا (yurīkumu al-barqa khawfan waṭamaʿan): He shows you the lightning for fear and hope.

فَإِذَا بَرِقَ الْبَصَرُ (fa-idhā bariqa al-baṣaru): when the sight is dazzled, stunned, or flash-struck.

Linguistic Summary

The root B R Q denotes flashing, lightning, sudden brightness, brief sharp disclosure, bright appearance against darkness, and the eye becoming dazzled or stunned before a powerful sight.

Materially, lightning is sudden brightness in a dark sky. It is intense, but it does not remain. It may show the path for a moment, but it does not replace steady light and lasting guidance.

In the Qur’an, lightning shapes the visual field of the parable in al-Baqarah. Alongside thunder, pouring rain, and darknesses, it completes a scene of fear and instability. In Surat al-Raʿd, lightning stands between fear and hope. In Surat al-Qiyāmah, the root moves to the human eye: sight becomes stunned before an overwhelming reality.

Its difference from light is that light may be stable and guiding, while lightning is momentary and leaping. Its difference from thunder is that lightning belongs to sight, while thunder belongs to hearing and bodily trembling. The central image is this: darkness is present; suddenly, sharp light leaps out, seizes the eye, reveals something for a moment, and then darkness returns.
