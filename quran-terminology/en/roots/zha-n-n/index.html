Qur'anic Root Ẓ N N (Zha N N)

Supposition, dominant perception, serious expectation, and a mental calculation in which one possibility becomes weightier and affects judgment or action.

Core Meaning

The root ظ ن ن indicates supposition, dominant perception, serious expectation, and a mental calculation in which one possibility becomes weightier in the mind and affects judgment or action.

Ẓann is not always weak doubt or baseless guessing, and it is not always complete certainty either. It can range from an unproven possibility to a very strong perception close to certainty. Its exact meaning must be understood from the clues, subject, practical result, and context of the verse.

Important Forms

ظَنَّ is the past verb of form I: he supposed, considered, estimated, expected, or reached a perception from clues.

وَظَنَّ دَاوُودُ أَنَّمَا فَتَنَّاهُ

Dawud realized that We had tested him.

Sad 38:24

Here “he doubted” is not suitable. Dawud draws a conclusion from the event: he had been placed in a test.

يَظُنُّ is the present verb: he supposes, gives a possibility dominant weight, expects, or understands something in that way.

الَّذِينَ يَظُنُّونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُلَاقُو رَبِّهِمْ

Those who consider the meeting with their Lord a serious and determining expectation.

Al-Baqarah 2:46

تَظُنُّونَ is the present verb for plural addressees:

وَتَظُنُّونَ بِاللَّهِ الظُّنُونَا

You were making many assumptions about The God.

Al-Ahzab 33:10

In a crisis, fear and pressure can expose hidden mental scenarios and calculations.

ظَنّ is the main noun and verbal noun: assumption, perception, dominant judgment, expectation, mental estimate, serious likelihood.

إِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ إِثْمٌ

Indeed, some ẓann is sin.

Al-Hujurat 49:12

The presence of “some” shows that not every ẓann has the same kind or value.

ظُنُون is the plural of ẓann. Its plural form can show scattered assumptions and different calculations under pressure. ظَنُّ السَّوْءِ is a Qur'anic expression for evil assumption, corrupt expectation, or bad calculation.

الظَّانِّينَ بِاللَّهِ ظَنَّ السَّوْءِ

Those who assume an evil assumption about The God.

Al-Fath 48:6

This is not merely a passing thought. It is a perception that affects trust, decision, and stance. Maẓnun, as a lexical passive participle, means something about which supposition is held; this form does not occur in the Qur'an. In lexical Arabic, ẓanun can also describe something not fully reliable, such as a well whose water is uncertain or a debt whose payment is uncertain.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of ظ ن ن is one possibility becoming weightier in the mind. A person does not always have complete reality, direct observation, or certain knowledge. Sometimes he sees part of the information, gathers signs together, and finds one of several possibilities stronger. When this dominant possibility affects decision or behavior, we have entered the field of ẓann.

Ẓann has no single fixed degree. It may be a weak baseless guess, an initial perception, a dominant likelihood, an estimate based on clues, a serious expectation, practical certainty, or a perception very close to certainty. The Qur'an does not assign separate words for all these degrees; intensity and validity are understood from context.

In verses such as Al-Baqarah 2:46 and Al-Haqqah 69:20, ẓann is so strong that it shapes behavior around accountability. In verses such as An-Najm 53:28, ẓann lacks knowledge and proof and illegitimately replaces truth.

The problem, then, is not mental estimation itself. The main questions are: what is this estimation based on? Has a mental possibility been placed where certain truth belongs? Does a person attribute his ẓann to others without evidence? Does he build judgment, accusation, or creed upon it? And when clear evidence appears, does he still remain upon his ẓann?

Material Image

One old and tangible use from this word family is bi'r ẓanun: a well with little or uncertain water, where it is not known whether enough water exists. A traveler sees signs of water and lowers the bucket into the well, but before drawing it out, he does not know whether it will return full or empty.

This image clarifies several features of ẓann: there is a sign, but not complete knowledge; different possibilities remain, but one is taken seriously; the person acts on an estimate; and the result may confirm or overturn the perception.

Thus ẓann is not mere fantasy. Sometimes it is a rational action under incomplete information. But if there is no sign, or if desire and fear replace evidence, the same mental mechanism can become baseless assumption.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, ẓann did not only mean doubt. It was used for estimation, expectation, knowledge derived from clues, and sometimes a perception close to certainty.

The expression bi'r ẓanun, a well whose water is uncertain, and dayn ẓanun, a debt whose repayment is uncertain, show that the root was used in practical situations of incomplete certainty. The issue was not a total absence of signs, but lack of certain access to the result.

In Arabic usage, ẓann is also connected with practical reason: the human being must sometimes decide before possessing the whole reality. This historical use matches the Qur'anic field of the root, but it does not allow us to reduce all Qur'anic ẓann to one degree of validity.

Ẓann as Serious Expectation of Meeting and Return

الَّذِينَ يَظُنُّونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُلَاقُو رَبِّهِمْ وَأَنَّهُمْ إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ

Those who consider the meeting with their Lord and the return to Him a serious, determining expectation.

Al-Baqarah 2:46

This ẓann is not weak and ineffective. The verse describes people whose awareness of meeting and return shapes their khushuʿ and behavior. In modern English, “suppose” can sound too weak. In this context, “are certain,” “expect with certainty,” or “hold the meeting before them” may better convey the practical force.

Still, retaining the word ẓann has a subtle point: the meeting has not yet occurred in sensory experience. They have taken it from revelation, signs, and reflection so seriously that it acts upon life as a reality placed before them.

Ẓann as Responsible Expectation of Account

إِنِّي ظَنَنْتُ أَنِّي مُلَاقٍ حِسَابِيَهْ

I expected that I would meet my account.

Al-Haqqah 69:20

The speaker did not treat account as a remote and ineffective possibility. The expectation of account oriented his behavior in worldly life. Here ẓann is practical certainty: the person had not yet seen the account with his own eyes, but he treated it as real enough to live by it.

Ẓann at the Nearness of Separation

وَظَنَّ أَنَّهُ الْفِرَاقُ

He realized that this was the separation.

Al-Qiyamah 75:28

The human being has not fully separated from the body, but the signs of death surround him so strongly that separation becomes the dominant and unavoidable conclusion. The word ẓann preserves a subtle boundary: separation is not complete yet, but all signs point to its arrival.

Even so, one should not conclude from this verse that there is real doubt about death. Many classical explanations understand ẓann here as close to certainty and decisive realization.

Ẓann After Seeing the Fire

وَرَأَى الْمُجْرِمُونَ النَّارَ فَظَنُّوا أَنَّهُمْ مُوَاقِعُوهَا وَلَمْ يَجِدُوا عَنْهَا مَصْرِفًا

The criminals saw the Fire and realized that they were going to fall into it, and they found no way to turn away from it.

Al-Kahf 18:53

They have seen the Fire and find no escape. Thus ẓann here is not a vague guess. It is a very strong perception of the result standing before them. “They supposed” may leave open the possibility that their perception was wrong. “They realized” or “they became certain” better conveys the context.

Ẓann as Inference From an Event

وَظَنَّ دَاوُودُ أَنَّمَا فَتَنَّاهُ

Dawud realized that We had tested him.

Sad 38:24

Dawud concludes from the event that he was facing a test. Ẓann here is a perception based on clues, not fantasy or suspicion. Suitable renderings include “realized,” “noticed,” and “concluded.”

Ẓann About a Future Outcome

وَقَالَ لِلَّذِي ظَنَّ أَنَّهُ نَاجٍ مِنْهُمَا

He said to the one of the two whom he considered likely to be saved.

Yusuf 12:42

The matter had not yet occurred outwardly, but Yusuf, based on the interpretation of the dream, concludes that one of the two prisoners would be saved. Here ẓann preserves the distance between knowledge of the interpretation and the future occurrence of the event.

A Mistaken Ẓann About the Consequence of Action

وَذَا النُّونِ إِذْ ذَهَبَ مُغَاضِبًا فَظَنَّ أَنْ لَنْ نَقْدِرَ عَلَيْهِ

And the Companion of the Fish, when he went away angry and thought that We would not constrain him.

Al-Anbiya 21:87

نقدر عليه in this context is not about The God being unable. It is closer to constraining, tightening, and placing in a narrow situation. Yunus made a calculation about the consequence of leaving. Later events showed that this estimate was not correct. The verse is a clear example of ẓann shaping behavior: a person's view of the consequence of a decision shapes the decision itself.

Good Ẓann About Others

لَوْلَا إِذْ سَمِعْتُمُوهُ ظَنَّ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتُ بِأَنْفُسِهِمْ خَيْرًا

Why, when you heard it, did the believing men and believing women not assume good about one another?

An-Nur 24:12

This verse shows that ẓann is not inherently negative. When an accusation spreads without evidence, lack of proof and prior knowledge of others should make the possibility of innocence weightier. Good ẓann here is not gullibility. The verse does not say every claim should be rejected without examination; it says an unproven report should not immediately become the dominant possibility and basis of condemnation.

Avoiding Much Ẓann

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اجْتَنِبُوا كَثِيرًا مِنَ الظَّنِّ إِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ إِثْمٌ

O you who have faith, avoid much ẓann. Indeed, some ẓann is sin.

Al-Hujurat 49:12

Two expressions are decisive: كثيرًا من الظن and بعض الظن. The Qur'an does not call all ẓann sin. If every ẓann were inherently forbidden, “much” and “some” would not be needed. The verse shows that the human mind constantly estimates, but not all estimations are equal morally or epistemically.

In the context of the verse, the dangerous ẓann is one that forms about another person without proof, makes the bad possibility outweigh the good, leads to spying, and then prepares the ground for backbiting and destroying human dignity. The verse shows a chain: unproven assumption, spying, finding or constructing fault, then backbiting and harm.

So the problem is not merely the involuntary arrival of a possibility in the mind. The danger becomes serious when a person feeds it, follows it, and enters the private space of others on its basis.

Bad Ẓann About The God

الظَّانِّينَ بِاللَّهِ ظَنَّ السَّوْءِ

Those who assume an evil assumption about The God.

Al-Fath 48:6

Bad ẓann here may include expecting the defeat of truth, distrust toward the promise, or a corrupt perception of the governance and justice of The God. This is not an abstract thought. A person's calculation about the outcome determines whether he joins, withdraws, accepts responsibility, or chooses another path in the moment of testing.

Many Assumptions in Crisis

وَإِذْ زَاغَتِ الْأَبْصَارُ وَبَلَغَتِ الْقُلُوبُ الْحَنَاجِرَ وَتَظُنُّونَ بِاللَّهِ الظُّنُونَا

When the eyes swerved, the hearts reached the throats, and you were making many assumptions about The God.

Al-Ahzab 33:10

Severe pressure exposes hidden perceptions. In crisis, the mind creates scenarios about promise, future, defeat, rescue, and the governance of The God. Here ẓann is not merely a mental concept; it is part of the human test. Crisis reveals what real trust was built upon.

Wrong Ẓann About the Return of the Messenger and Believers

بَلْ ظَنَنْتُمْ أَنْ لَنْ يَنْقَلِبَ الرَّسُولُ وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِيهِمْ أَبَدًا

Rather, you assumed that the Messenger and the believers would never return to their families.

Al-Fath 48:12

This ẓann did not remain a mere prediction. They acted on it by refusing to accompany. The continuation exposes the source of that calculation:

وَزُيِّنَ ذَٰلِكَ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ وَظَنَنْتُمْ ظَنَّ السَّوْءِ

That was made beautiful in your hearts, and you assumed an evil assumption.

Al-Fath 48:12

Sometimes a person treats a desired or fear-driven perception as realism. When one possibility is beautified in the heart, it may feel weightier than the real evidence.

Following Ẓann Instead of Truth

وَمَا يَتَّبِعُ أَكْثَرُهُمْ إِلَّا ظَنًّا إِنَّ الظَّنَّ لَا يُغْنِي مِنَ الْحَقِّ شَيْئًا

Most of them follow nothing but ẓann; indeed ẓann does not avail anything against truth.

Yunus 10:36

The key issue here is following ẓann: a person makes a possibility or perception take the place of truth and proof, and then follows it. لا يغني من الحق شيئا can be understood as: assumption does not replace truth; mental estimation does not free a person from truth; and a possibility, however strong in the mind, does not create reality by itself.

The phrase “mental estimation does not replace objective reality” may help explain the meaning, but Qur'anic haqq is broader than objective reality alone. It also includes truth, rightness, due claim, and the correct criterion.

Ẓann Against Knowledge

وَمَا لَهُمْ بِهِ مِنْ عِلْمٍ إِنْ يَتَّبِعُونَ إِلَّا الظَّنَّ

They have no knowledge of it; they follow nothing but ẓann.

An-Najm 53:28

Here ẓann is placed against knowledge. The criticism concerns a person who has no knowledge-based support for his claim but still makes ẓann the basis of belief. This verse does not say that every probable decision in life is invalid. The issue is making a truth-claim without knowledge and placing probability where firm proof is required.

Ẓann About Denying the Hereafter

وَقَالُوا مَا هِيَ إِلَّا حَيَاتُنَا الدُّنْيَا نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا وَمَا يُهْلِكُنَا إِلَّا الدَّهْرُ وَمَا لَهُمْ بِذَٰلِكَ مِنْ عِلْمٍ إِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ

They said: it is nothing but our worldly life; we die and live, and nothing destroys us except time. They have no knowledge of that; they do nothing but assume.

Al-Jathiyah 45:24

The speakers state their claim with certainty, but confident language is not the same as epistemic firmness. The Qur'an distinguishes between a confident tone and knowledge-based support. Denial of the Hereafter is not called knowledge here, because they do not possess access or encompassing knowledge of its nonexistence. From the limits of their worldly experience, they construct a universal conclusion about all reality.

This verse shows that a worldview may be expressed in decisive language and still rest upon ẓann, because it has moved beyond available evidence and issued a final judgment about what it does not know. The verse should not be confined to the name of any contemporary school. Its critique applies to any claim that turns lack of access or observation into certain proof of nonexistence.

Ẓann and Claimed Uncertainty About the Hour

وَإِذَا قِيلَ إِنَّ وَعْدَ اللَّهِ حَقٌّ وَالسَّاعَةُ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهَا قُلْتُمْ مَا نَدْرِي مَا السَّاعَةُ إِنْ نَظُنُّ إِلَّا ظَنًّا وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمُسْتَيْقِنِينَ

When it was said: the promise of The God is true and the Hour has no rayb in it, you said: we do not know what the Hour is; we have nothing but ẓann, and we are not among those who reach certainty.

Al-Jathiyah 45:32

Ẓann here is not practical certainty. It is uncertainty maintained against the announcement of truth and absence of rayb. The issue is not only that the Hereafter has not yet been seen. The Qur'an presents promise and signs for it. From the verse's perspective, the speakers stop at “we have only ẓann, not certainty” instead of engaging the signs.

Still, one should not create an absolute rule that every use of ẓann about the Hereafter is positive and every use about tawhid or revelation is negative. The verses themselves show both positive and negative ẓann about the Hereafter: Al-Baqarah 2:46 is responsible expectation of meeting, Al-Haqqah 69:20 is expectation of account, and Al-Jathiyah 45:32 is doubt that has not reached certainty. The difference is not only the topic; it is the human relationship to signs, proof, and the practical effect of perception.

Ẓann That There Is No Escape

وَظَنُّوا مَا لَهُمْ مِنْ مَحِيصٍ

They realized that they had no escape.

Fussilat 41:48

Here ẓann is not a weak guess. The situation has become manifest, leaving little room for the opposite possibility. “They realized” or “they became certain” is clearer than “they supposed.”

Similar Uses

Ẓann as possibility and ẓann close to certainty: In some verses, the opposite possibility is still conceivable and ẓann is an estimate before fulfillment, as in Yusuf 12:42. In other verses, signs are so strong that ẓann approaches practical certainty, as in Al-Qiyamah 75:28 and Al-Kahf 18:53.

Positive and negative ẓann: Positive ẓann may rest on valid clues, measured trust, and avoidance of accusation, as in An-Nur 24:12. Negative ẓann may be fed by fear, desire, group attachment, or lack of knowledge, as in Al-Fath 48:12. The positive or negative content of an assumption alone does not determine its validity. Optimistic assumption may be wrong if it contradicts clear evidence; an unpleasant possibility may not be sinful if it rests on valid evidence.

The arrival of ẓann and following ẓann: The emergence of a possibility in the mind is not always voluntary. But following ẓann, feeding it, and turning it into judgment or behavior is another stage. The Qur'an focuses especially on its consequences: following ẓann, evil assumption, spying after assumption, and making claims without knowledge.

Everyday ẓann and foundational claims: In daily life, deciding on the basis of dominant likelihood is sometimes unavoidable. People decide about travel, treatment, trade, and danger with limited information. But the heavier the claim, the stronger the support required. Attributing speech to The God, denying the Hereafter, condemning a human being, or making binding law cannot be built on baseless ẓann.

Ẓann and observation: Ẓann often occurs before complete encompassing knowledge, but it may also remain after seeing part of reality. The criminals see the Fire, but falling into it has not yet fully occurred. The human being experiences signs of death, but separation has not yet been completed. The boundary between ẓann and knowledge is not always the boundary between seeing and not seeing; sometimes it is the boundary between seeing signs and the complete fulfillment of the result.

Grammar and Structure

Ẓann with an and anna: The verb ẓanna often comes before a clause that states the content of the perception: يَظُنُّونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُلَاقُو رَبِّهِمْ, they hold the meeting with their Lord before them; and وَظَنَّ أَنَّهُ الْفِرَاقُ, he realized that it was separation. The clause after أن or أنّ shows which possibility has become dominant in the person's mind.

Ẓann with bi: In الظَّانِّينَ بِاللَّهِ ظَنَّ السَّوْءِ, the bi marks the object or topic of the assumption. In English, “about The God” is often more natural than a mechanical “with The God.”

Ẓann as a verb of the heart: Ẓann is among the verbs that connect a subject and a judgment in the mind. A person considers something to have a certain state or issues a conclusion about it. Translation can reconstruct this naturally: he considered him likely to be saved; they assumed they would not return; he realized this was the separation.

Nearby Roots

Ẓ N N and SH K K: Shakk is hesitation between two possibilities without one clearly becoming dominant. Ẓann is usually when one possibility becomes weightier in the mind. Approximately: shakk is being suspended between two sides; ẓann is the dominance of one possibility.

Ẓ N N and R Y B: Rayb can include unease, suspicion, and distrust in addition to doubt. Ẓann may be a calm practical estimate, while rayb often emphasizes disturbance and distrust toward the matter.

Ẓ N N and Ḥ S B: Hasb is close to calculating, estimating, and considering something to be a certain way. Hasb emphasizes the act of calculation or mental assumption; ẓann is the dominant result that emerges from calculation or clues. The distinction is not absolute.

Ẓ N N and Z ʿ M: Zaʿm is more about claiming and expressing a statement, especially one whose support is questionable. Ẓann is the mental state or judgment; zaʿm may be the outward expression of that judgment.

Ẓ N N and ʿ L M: ʿIlm indicates established knowledge with support, awareness, or evidence. Ẓann indicates estimation before complete encompassing knowledge or before final fulfillment. It may be valid and clue-based, or baseless and put in the place of knowledge. Thus ʿilm is established supported knowledge; ẓann is an estimate in which possibility, expectation, or distance from complete fulfillment remains. Yet in some contexts, ẓann approaches certainty in practical effect.

Ẓ N N and Y Q N: Yaqin indicates settled knowledge and the removal of doubt. Ẓann has a wider range. The opposite possibility may remain, or clues may become so strong that behavior differs little from certainty.

Ẓ N N and W H M: Wahm is usually closer to a weaker possibility, mistaken imagination, or a perception with little support. Approximately: wahm is a light possibility or mistaken impression; shakk is hesitation between possibilities; ẓann is dominant likelihood; yaqin is settled knowledge. This division helps explanation but should not be treated as a fixed boundary for every historical use.

Ẓ N N and KH R Ṣ: Khars is close to estimation and approximation, such as estimating produce before harvest. In many Qur'anic uses it approaches guessing and speaking without knowledge. Ẓann has a wider field and may rest on valid clues.

Ẓ N N and T H M: Tuhmah is attributing a crime, fault, or bad intention to another. Evil ẓann may be the mental stage before accusation. When a person states his possibility as a definite attribution without evidence, he moves from assumption into accusation.

Ẓ N N and R J W: Raja' is hope and expectation for something to happen. Ẓann is the person's assessment of the likelihood of that happening. A person may hope for something while considering its occurrence unlikely; or dislike something while considering it likely.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

Ẓann should not be translated mechanically as “assumption” in every verse. The fitting equivalent must be understood from the degree of evidence, the subject, and the practical effect.

For ظَنَّ: supposed, considered, estimated, perceived, concluded, expected, became certain.

For يَظُنُّ: supposes, gives dominant likelihood, expects, considers, perceives.

For ظَنّ: assumption, perception, estimate, dominant perception, serious likelihood, expectation, practical certainty.

For ظَنُّ السَّوْءِ: evil assumption, bad suspicion, bad expectation, corrupt perception, pessimistic and wrong calculation.

For يَظُنُّونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُلَاقُو رَبِّهِمْ: they are certain they will meet their Lord; they hold the meeting with their Lord before them; they have a firm expectation of meeting their Lord; they move toward meeting their Lord with responsible expectation. The last option is useful in explanation, but somewhat more interpretive for direct translation.

For إِنِّي ظَنَنْتُ أَنِّي مُلَاقٍ حِسَابِيَهْ: I was certain I would meet my account; I expected this account; I held my account before myself.

For وَظَنَّ أَنَّهُ الْفِرَاقُ: he realized that it was separation; he became certain that the time of separation had arrived; he knew this was the separation.

For فَظَنُّوا أَنَّهُمْ مُوَاقِعُوهَا: they realized they would fall into it; they became certain they would face it; they knew they would encounter it.

For وَظَنَّ دَاوُودُ أَنَّمَا فَتَنَّاهُ: Dawud realized We had tested him; Dawud concluded that he had been placed in a test; Dawud noticed this was his test.

For فَظَنَّ أَنْ لَنْ نَقْدِرَ عَلَيْهِ: he thought We would not constrain him; he calculated that he would not be placed in a narrow situation; he assumed We would not restrict him.

For ظَنَّ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتُ بِأَنْفُسِهِمْ خَيْرًا: the believing men and women assumed good about one another; they gave the possibility of good and innocence greater weight; they held a good perception about their community.

For اجْتَنِبُوا كَثِيرًا مِنَ الظَّنِّ: avoid much assumption; stay away from much suspicion; do not make unproven perceptions the basis of judgment.

For إِنَّ الظَّنَّ لَا يُغْنِي مِنَ الْحَقِّ شَيْئًا: assumption never replaces truth; mental estimation does not fill the place of truth; assumption does not make one independent of truth; baseless possibility cannot stand in for truth.

For bi'r ẓanun: uncertain well; low-water uncertain well; a well whose water is not known.

Summary

The root ظ ن ن indicates supposition, dominant perception, serious expectation, and a mental calculation in which one possibility becomes weightier in the mind. Its material image can be seen in bi'r ẓanun: a well where signs of water exist, but the result is not certain before testing.

In the Qur'an, ẓann is sometimes a baseless possibility placed where knowledge and truth belong; sometimes an estimate not yet fulfilled outwardly; and sometimes so strong that it approaches practical certainty. Thus ẓann is not always condemned and not always valid.

The validity of ẓann depends on subject, evidence, direction, and the behavior produced by it. A ẓann that leads a person toward readiness for account, preservation of others' dignity, or responsible decision is not the same as a ẓann fed by fear, desire, and lack of evidence that leads to accusation, spying, or denial of truth.

Ẓann overlaps with shakk, rayb, hasb, zaʿm, ʿilm, yaqin, wahm, khars, tuhmah, and raja', but is identical with none. Its distinctive core is the dominance of one possibility in the mind, becoming an expectation or calculation that affects judgment and action.
