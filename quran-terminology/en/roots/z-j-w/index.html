Core meaning: driving something forward and setting it in motion; advancing it gradually, and, in other uses, making do with limited resources or describing low-value goods that are difficult to accept in exchange.

The Arabic root z j w concerns moving something and driving it forward. زَجَا and أَزْجَى occur in uses involving driving livestock, moving clouds, and setting things in motion.

Many lexical uses associate this movement with gentle or gradual driving: someone urging an animal onward, for example, or wind moving clouds across the sky.

The family also extends beyond physical movement. It can describe advancing a task, getting through life on limited resources, and goods whose value or quantity is so slight that they are difficult to accept in trade.

The root occurs three times in the Quran: twice as the verb يُزْجِي in Al-Isra 17:66 and Al-Nur 24:43, and once as مُزْجَاةٍ in Yusuf 12:88.

These occurrences illustrate two important aspects: driving and moving ships and clouds, and the inadequacy or difficult acceptance of goods offered in exchange.

1. The root in early Arabic

Driving forward and moving

Early Arabic uses زَجَا الشَّيْءَ and أَزْجَاهُ for driving or moving something.

Lexicons record أَزْجَيْتُ الْإِبِلَ for driving camels.

Imagine a shepherd driving a herd from pasture toward water. The animals move themselves, while the shepherd urges them to continue along the route.

In this situation, إزجاء can describe driving the herd forward.

This differs from carrying: the shepherd does not bear the animals on his shoulders but prompts them to move and continue their journey.

Lexicons frequently explain إزجاء as gentle driving. This nuance matters, but it does not specify a measurable speed in every use; the precise manner of movement depends on context.

A witness from pre-Islamic poetry

A poem attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Al-Nabighah al-Dhubyani says:

سَرَتْ عَلَيْهِ مِنَ الْجَوْزَاءِ سَارِيَةٌ
تُزْجِي الشَّمَالُ عَلَيْهِ جَامِدَ الْبَرَدِ

sarat ʿalayhi mina al-jawzāʾi sāriyatun / tuzjī al-shamālu ʿalayhi jāmida al-barad

“A night raincloud associated with the rains of al-Jawza passed over him; the north wind drove solid hail upon him.” Here al-Jawza belongs to the traditional association of stars with seasonal rains, rather than necessarily naming a geographical direction.

Here تُزْجِي describes the wind driving hail.

The wind carries the hailstones along and drives them onward.

In this witness, إزجاء therefore refers to actual movement in nature.

This is semantically close to Al-Nur 24:43, which likewise describes moving clouds.

The verse occurs in Al-Nabighah al-Dhubyani's well-known ode beginning يَا دَارَ مَيَّةَ بِالْعَلْيَاءِ فَالسَّنَدِ. Its attribution to the pre-Islamic poet is established in the literary tradition, although this particular verse has no independently determined date of composition.

The witness shows that تُزْجِي was used in early poetry to describe driving natural phenomena forward.

Getting through life on little

Arabic lexicons also record أَزْجَيْتُ أَيَّامِي and زَجَّيْتُ أَيَّامِي.

These expressions describe getting through one's days with limited means.

Someone might earn little but spend that amount in a way that keeps life going.

The person enjoys no great prosperity and cannot meet every need, yet carries on with what is available.

Arabic describes this as getting through one's days, or tazjiyah of one's time.

The related expression تَزَجَّيْتُ بِكَذَا also means making do with something.

The idea of advancing remains, but its object is now human life and work rather than camels, ships, or clouds.

This extension is well attested in classical Arabic sources. Without datable evidence, however, one cannot claim that every form already had precisely these uses before Islam.

زَجَا: progressing and finding acceptance

Another branch records زَجَا الْأَمْرُ for a matter proceeding, going well, or getting underway.

Similarly, زَجَا الْخَرَاجُ describes tax revenue becoming easy to collect.

In commerce, something may be زاجٍ: circulating successfully and finding acceptance.

Conversely, goods may be so poor or undesirable that buyers do not readily accept them.

This connects physical movement with the progress of a transaction: something advances in either case, spatially in one and through successful business or acceptance in the other.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important forms in this Arabic family include:

زَجَا: proceeded, flowed, went well, or gained currency; transitively, drove something forward.

يَزْجُو: the imperfect of زَجَا; proceeds, flows, or gains currency.

زَجْو and زَجَاء: verbal nouns associated with proceeding, flowing, and going well.

أَزْجَى: drove something forward or set it in motion.

يُزْجِي: drives forward, propels, or sets in motion.

إِزْجَاء: driving something forward or setting it in motion.

زَجَّى: drove something forward or moved it gently.

تَزْجِيَة: driving forward, moving, or getting by with limited means.

تَزَجَّى: made do with a little or managed with it.

زَاجٍ: something that flows, proceeds, or finds acceptance in trade.

مُزْجٍ: the active participle of أَزْجَى; one who advances or moves something.

مُزْجًى: the passive participle of أَزْجَى; something driven forward, and, concerning goods, something meager or low in value that is difficult to accept.

مُزْجَاة: the feminine of مُزْجًى.

Only يُزْجِي and مُزْجَاة occur in the Quran.

يُزْجِي is a form IV imperfect verb; مُزْجَاة is the feminine passive participle of that form.

Both Quranic forms therefore belong to أَزْجَى. One expresses driving forward; the other describes something presented as meager or of little value in a commercial setting.

3. Quranic uses

Al-Isra 17:66: propelling ships across the sea

رَبُّكُمُ الَّذِي يُزْجِي لَكُمُ الْفُلْكَ فِي الْبَحْرِ لِتَبْتَغُوا مِنْ فَضْلِهِ ۚ إِنَّهُ كَانَ بِكُمْ رَحِيمًا

rabbukumu alladhī yuzjī lakumu al-fulka fī al-baḥri li-tabtaghū min faḍlihi innahu kāna bikum raḥīmā

Al-Isrāʾ 17:66

“Your Lord is the One who propels ships for you across the sea so that you may seek of His bounty. Indeed, He is merciful to you.”

Al-Isra 17:66

Here يُزْجِي concerns propelling and driving ships across the sea.

The Lord is the subject of the verb, and الْفُلْكَ is its object.

Ships allow people to travel over water from one place to another.

Their movement depends on more than their construction. Water, wind, natural forces, and suitable conditions also make sea travel possible.

The verse presents this possibility in relation to the Lord's governance and mercy.

Does يُزْجِي mean slow movement?

Lexicons sometimes explain إزجاء as driving gently.

In this verse, however, يُزْجِي does not specify a slow speed for the ship.

Ships can travel in calm waters or encounter strong winds and waves; these possibilities do not, by themselves, determine the lexical meaning of the verb.

The relevant point is that the verse describes propelling the ship without specifying its precise speed or the state of the sea.

“Propels” is thus a natural rendering: it conveys the ship being moved forward without imposing a particular speed on the verse.

The ship's movement and the purpose of travel

The verse continues:

لِتَبْتَغُوا مِنْ فَضْلِهِ

li-tabtaghū min faḍlihi

Al-Isrāʾ 17:66

That is, so that you may seek of God's bounty.

This clause expresses a purpose of the movement and the opportunity it gives people to benefit.

يُزْجِي itself therefore does not mean trading, obtaining sustenance, or seeking bounty; those ideas arise from other parts of the verse.

For example, a ship may transport goods or passengers or provide access to various resources.

In all these cases, the verb still means setting in motion or driving forward.

The following verse, Al-Isra 17:67, describes distress at sea and turning to God in danger.

Together, these verses place maritime travel and its benefits alongside dangers and the limits of human ability.

Nevertheless, the maritime context clarifies يُزْجِي; the ethical messages of the next verse should not all be included in its lexical definition.

Al-Nur 24:43: moving and joining clouds

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يُزْجِي سَحَابًا ثُمَّ يُؤَلِّفُ بَيْنَهُ ثُمَّ يَجْعَلُهُ رُكَامًا فَتَرَى الْوَدْقَ يَخْرُجُ مِنْ خِلَالِهِ وَيُنَزِّلُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مِنْ جِبَالٍ فِيهَا مِنْ بَرَدٍ فَيُصِيبُ بِهِ مَنْ يَشَاءُ وَيَصْرِفُهُ عَنْ مَنْ يَشَاءُ ۖ يَكَادُ سَنَا بَرْقِهِ يَذْهَبُ بِالْأَبْصَارِ

a-lam tara anna allāha yuzjī saḥāban thumma yuʾallifu baynahu thumma yajʿaluhu rukāman fa-tarā al-wadqa yakhruju min khilālihi wa-yunazzilu mina al-samāʾi min jibālin fīhā min baradin fa-yuṣību bihi man yashāʾu wa-yaṣrifuhu ʿan man yashāʾu yakādu sanā barqihi yadhhabu bi-l-abṣār

Al-Nūr 24:43

“Have you not seen that The God drives a cloud, then joins its parts together, then makes it a heap, and you see rain emerging through it? He sends down from the sky, from mountains within it, hail, striking with it whom He wills and turning it away from whom He wills. The flash of its lightning nearly takes away sight.”

Al-Nur 24:43

Here يُزْجِي describes moving clouds.

The verb used for ships in Al-Isra 17:66 is used here for a cloud.

The object differs:

In Al-Isra, it is الْفُلْكَ: ships.

In Al-Nur, it is سَحَابًا: a cloud.

In each case, something moves forward within its natural setting.

يُزْجِي, يُؤَلِّفُ, and يَجْعَلُهُ رُكَامًا

The verse presents three successive expressions:

First, يُزْجِي سَحَابًا: He drives a cloud.

Then, يُؤَلِّفُ بَيْنَهُ: He joins its parts together.

Next, يَجْعَلُهُ رُكَامًا: He makes it an accumulated heap.

This sequence matters.

يُزْجِي concerns movement, يُؤَلِّفُ joining, and رُكَام accumulation.

إزجاء should therefore not be defined as gathering, condensing, or producing rain.

The verse expresses those subsequent developments with separate words and constructions.

Wind, for example, can move clouds across the sky. Under suitable conditions, cloud masses may also join, expand, or accumulate.

Moving a cloud and accumulating it are different concepts.

The verse describes these phenomena in sequence, with يُزْجِي expressing movement.

The verb alone does not specify a detailed meteorological mechanism, cloud speed, or particular cloud type. Its lexical sense is driving forward; scientific details require separate investigation.

Yusuf 12:88: goods insufficient in value or quantity

فَلَمَّا دَخَلُوا عَلَيْهِ قَالُوا يَا أَيُّهَا الْعَزِيزُ مَسَّنَا وَأَهْلَنَا الضُّرُّ وَجِئْنَا بِبِضَاعَةٍ مُزْجَاةٍ فَأَوْفِ لَنَا الْكَيْلَ وَتَصَدَّقْ عَلَيْنَا ۖ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَجْزِي الْمُتَصَدِّقِينَ

fa-lammā dakhalū ʿalayhi qālū yā ayyuhā al-ʿazīzu massanā wa-ahlanā al-ḍurru wa-jiʾnā bi-biḍāʿatin muzjātin fa-awfi lanā al-kayla wa-taṣaddaq ʿalaynā inna allāha yajzī al-mutaṣaddiqīn

Yūsuf 12:88

“When they entered his presence, they said: O mighty one, hardship has touched us and our family, and we have brought meager goods. Give us full measure and be charitable to us; indeed, The God rewards those who give charity.”

Yusuf 12:88

مُزْجَاةٍ qualifies بِضَاعَةٍ in this verse.

بِضَاعَة refers to goods or property used in exchange.

مُزْجَاة here describes meager or low-value goods: something insufficient in quantity or quality for a satisfactory exchange, or difficult to accept.

Why are meager goods called مُزْجَاة?

In the basic use of إزجاء, something is driven forward so that it moves or passes onward.

In commerce, goods may likewise be so poor that their seller struggles to offer them, or the buyer accepts them only by overlooking their inadequate value.

Such goods make their way through a transaction with difficulty.

Lexicons accordingly record بِضَاعَة مُزْجَاة for scanty, inferior, or low-value goods that do not readily find acceptance.

Imagine a farmer with little produce to sell after a drought. The quantity is inadequate, and the quality falls short of harvests from wetter years.

To meet the family's essential needs with that produce, the farmer may have to offer it at a lower value or ask the buyer for generosity.

The goods may thus be meager in quantity, quality, or exchange value.

This illustrates how مُزْجَاة can concern low value and difficult acceptance in trade.

Does it mean counterfeit money or invalid coins?

Historical and exegetical discussions offer different accounts of the goods Joseph's brothers brought.

Some mention inferior or low-value coins, others a small quantity of goods, and others wool or similar exchangeable products.

The verse itself, however, speaks only of بِضَاعَةٍ مُزْجَاةٍ.

The phrase does not establish that these were coins, identify their material, or specify their exact value.

Translations should therefore not restrict مُزْجَاة to counterfeit money, invalid coins, or a named product.

Nor is small quantity the only possibility: poor quality or low exchange value can also inform the expression.

“Meager goods” or “goods of little worth” preserve the sense without selecting a particular historical identification.

The role of فَأَوْفِ لَنَا الْكَيْلَ وَتَصَدَّقْ عَلَيْنَا

Joseph's brothers then ask for full measure and charity.

This request directly relates to their description of their goods.

They describe their own and their family's hardship and recognize that what they have brought is insufficient or inadequate in value for the desired exchange.

They therefore request full measure and generosity.

This connection shows that مُزْجَاة here does not merely describe goods carried from one place to another.

If it meant only transportation, the adjective would not itself explain their need or appeal for leniency.

In this context, inadequacy and insufficient value matter.

The verse nevertheless leaves open whether the shortfall concerns quantity, quality, or both.

4. Moving something and goods of little value

At first, يُزْجِي سَحَابًا and بِضَاعَةٍ مُزْجَاةٍ might seem entirely unrelated.

One concerns a cloud moving through the sky; the other concerns meager goods.

Lexical usage nevertheless suggests a connection.

In its physical sense, إزجاء drives something onward so that it continues along its way.

Applied to life, it describes getting through one's days with the little one has.

In commerce, low-value goods may be accepted only with difficulty or generosity, allowing the exchange to proceed.

Advancing or getting something underway thus helps explain the relation between these uses.

This does not establish a single, certain historical route by which every sense developed.

Nor does every derivative mean “of little value.”

A ship described with يُزْجِي need not be small or of little worth.

A cloud described with the same verb need not be small or weak.

Inadequacy belongs to مُزْجَاة as a description of goods in this context, rather than to every use of the root.

5. Comparison with related roots

z j w and s w q

Both roots can describe driving something forward.

سَاقَ describes driving and guiding livestock or moving something toward a destination.

أَزْجَى can express a similar action, with gradual advance and gentle driving also present in its lexical uses.

A shepherd, for example, may يسوق or يزجي a herd.

This overlap does not establish a fixed difference in speed. The precise distinction depends on actual usage in each sentence.

In Al-Isra 17:66 and Al-Nur 24:43, يُزْجِي moves ships and clouds. Elsewhere in the Quran, s w q can describe driving people, livestock, and other things.

z j w and j r y

جَرَى concerns flowing or moving.

The transitive أَزْجَى focuses on causing movement or driving something forward.

A ship's movement across the sea can be expressed with جَرَى.

To describe an agent propelling it, أَزْجَى is appropriate.

This matters in Al-Isra 17:66: God is the subject of يُزْجِي and the ship its object.

The verse thus relates the ship's movement to the Lord's governance, rather than merely reporting that it moves.

z j w and d f ʿ

دَفَعَ concerns pushing, repelling, or driving something away.

أَزْجَى can also describe driving forward.

Some uses of دفع emphasize removing an obstacle or displacing something, whereas familiar uses of إزجاء concern advancing and continuing movement.

This is not an absolute boundary: lexicons themselves explain إزجاء with دفع.

The families overlap in some contexts, but they are not interchangeable in every sentence.

z j w and z j r

زَجَرَ concerns restraining, driving with a shout, or prohibiting.

أَزْجَى describes moving and driving forward without inherently meaning shouting, rebuking, or restraining.

Someone might shout an animal away, while another person gently drives it toward a destination.

Both actions can cause movement, but their manner and direction differ.

The root z j w should therefore not be restricted to rebuking or driving with a reprimand.

z j w and q l l

قِلَّة concerns a small quantity.

In Yusuf 12:88, مُزْجَاة can concern low value and difficult acceptance as well as small quantity.

A large quantity of poor goods, for example, may have little exchange value.

Conversely, a small quantity of highly valuable goods may suffice for a major transaction.

Small quantity and low value are therefore distinct concepts.

In بِضَاعَةٍ مُزْجَاةٍ, need and the appeal for generosity show commercial inadequacy, without specifying exact quantity or quality.

z j w and m z j

These roots resemble one another in writing but are independent.

مَزَجَ, from m z j, means mixing or blending.

Mixing water with another drink, for example, can be called مَزْج.

أَزْجَى, from z j w, means driving forward or setting in motion.

Thus مُزْجَاة in Yusuf 12:88 does not mean “mixed goods.”

This distinction is especially important when identifying the word's root.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

يُزْجِي — Al-Isra 17:66

Brief root meaning: driving forward, moving, and setting something in motion.

Meaning in this verse: propelling ships across the sea.

Suggested rendering: “propels.”

The phrase may be translated:

“Your Lord is the One who propels ships for you across the sea.”

يُزْجِي — Al-Nur 24:43

Brief root meaning: driving forward and setting in motion.

Meaning in this verse: moving and driving clouds.

Suggested rendering: “drives” or “drives forward.”

The phrase may be translated:

“Have you not seen that The God drives a cloud?”

“Drives” is natural here, with the joining and accumulation of the cloud expressed later in the verse.

مُزْجَاةٍ — Yusuf 12:88

Brief root meaning: driving and advancing; concerning goods, inadequacy and difficult acceptance.

Meaning in this verse: scanty or low-value goods insufficient for the desired exchange or difficult to accept.

Suggested rendering: “meager” or “of little worth.”

The phrase may be translated:

“And we have brought meager goods.”

This accommodates low value and possibly small quantity without specifying the goods or their exact worth.

7. Conclusion

The Arabic root z j w concerns driving something forward and moving it.

أَزْجَى can describe driving livestock, propelling ships, and moving clouds.

Its wider family also concerns advancing a task and getting through life on limited resources.

In this semantic field, مُزْجَاة describes goods that are scanty, inadequate, or low in value and difficult to accept in trade.

In the Quran, يُزْجِي propels ships in Al-Isra 17:66 and moves clouds in Al-Nur 24:43.

مُزْجَاة in Yusuf 12:88 instead describes goods Joseph's brothers bring in need and hardship.

This shows why a derivative's meaning cannot be extracted from the general root sense without attention to context.

Gentle driving is a recognized nuance of إزجاء, but it does not specify an exact speed in every use. Nor should مُزْجَاة be restricted to counterfeit money or poor-quality goods alone.

Final definition of z j w:

“Driving something forward and setting it in motion; in other uses, advancing or getting a matter underway. The family also supplies expressions for getting through life on little and describing meager or low-value things that are difficult to accept. In the Quran, يُزْجِي moves ships and clouds, while مُزْجَاة describes meager goods offered in exchange.”
