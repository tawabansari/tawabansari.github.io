1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ق ت ل in the Qur'an points above all to causing the death of a living being. In this root, death is attributed to an act, an agent, or a responsibility; someone or something causes the life of another to come to an end.

This feature separates Q T L from M W T. Mawt is death itself, or dying, while qatl points to causing death. A person may die without being killed by a human being; but when it is said that someone was killed, his death is attributed to an agent.

From this same root, forms related to battle are also built:

  • قَتَلَ: he killed;
  • قاتَلَ: he fought or entered battle;
  • اقتَتَلَ: they fought one another or became mutually engaged;
  • قِتال: fighting and battle;
  • قَتْلَىٰ: those killed.

The most important principle in understanding this root is to distinguish between one-sided killing and qital as fighting and battle. Not every battle necessarily results in someone being killed, and not every participant in qital is necessarily a killer.

The shortest expression for this root is:

Causing the death of a living being; and in the reciprocal forms, entering a war or conflict in which killing is possible.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

قَتَلَ / يَقْتُلُ

He killed, or he kills; he caused the end of another's life.

قُتِلَ / يُقْتَلُ

He was killed, or he is killed.

The passive form highlights the event of being killed, and the agent may be removed or left unspecified.

قَتَّلَ / يُقَتِّلُ

He killed intensely or repeatedly; he slaughtered or carried out killing.

The precise intensity, repetition, or multiplicity must be taken from the context, and more than the form can carry should not be extracted only from the verbal pattern.

قاتَلَ / يُقاتِلُ

He fought and participated in battle.

This form emphasizes confrontation and engagement, not the certain occurrence of killing.

قِتال

War, battle, and armed conflict.

اقتَتَلَ / يَقْتَتِلُ

They fought or became engaged with one another.

قاتِل

Killer; the one who has killed another.

مُقاتِل

Fighter, or one who participates in battle.

A muqatil is not necessarily a qatil.

قَتِيل

One who has been killed.

قَتْلَىٰ

Those killed.

مَقْتَل

A killing place or fatal point; a place where impact can lead to death.

This form is known in Arabic, but it is not among the central Qur'anic derivatives.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The main core of Q T L is:

Ending the life of a living being by means of an act or agent.

This definition contains several points.

First, qatl concerns a living being.

Second, qatl does not merely express the state of death; it attributes death to a cause.

Third, qatl may be intentional or unintentional. The Qur'an also speaks of accidental killing:

وَمَنْ قَتَلَ مُؤْمِنًا خَطَأً

So the existence of an agent does not always mean prior intent.

Fourth, qital is not identical with qatl. Qital is entering battle; qatl may be one of its results, but the certain occurrence of death is not embedded in the meaning of qital itself.

Fifth, the ethical value of killing is not obtained from the root alone. The Qur'an distinguishes between:

  • killing without right;
  • accidental killing;
  • qisas;
  • and killing in a restricted battlefield context.

So the root identifies the type of act; the ethical and legal judgment comes from context, intent, right, aggression, and the conditions of the act.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The clearest material image of this root is an act that stops the vital functions of the body and moves the person from the state of living to the state of being dead.

This stopping can occur through different means:

  • a fatal wound;
  • cutting off breath;
  • severe bleeding;
  • poisoning;
  • or any other agent that ends life.

The root Q T L itself is not restricted to one specific method. For this reason, it is broader than dhabh, because dhabh points to a specific manner of killing, while qatl can include any kind of causing death.

The word maqtal is also used in Arabic for a point where a blow can be fatal. This image shows that qatl is connected with the ending of life, but no theory of a "network bottleneck" or "shutting down a system" should be built from it.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, قَتَلَهُ means he killed him.

قُتِلَ الرجل means the man was killed.

قاتَلَهُ means he fought him.

اقتتل القوم means the group fought among themselves.

The relevant vocalization and context determine whether the word points to the one killed or the one who performs the act of killing.

In figurative Arabic uses, qatl may sometimes be used for removing intensity, effect, or ambiguity. But such uses should not dominate a Qur'anic article over the main meaning, because the dominant and direct Qur'anic use of the root concerns killing human beings, being killed, and battle.

Likewise, with expressions such as قَتَلَهُ عِلْمًا or other non-bodily uses, one should avoid making definite historical claims without clear evidence. Such expressions, even if recorded in some linguistic sources, do not determine the basic meaning of Qur'anic verses.

7. Semantic Restrictions and Misuses

One of the most important misuses is translating قاتِلُوا as "kill." Qatilu is from Form III and means fight, while:

اقْتُلُوا

means kill.

Confusing these two can turn a verse about battle into a command for one-sided killing.

Another misuse is separating phrases such as:

وَاقْتُلُوهُمْ حَيْثُ ثَقِفْتُمُوهُمْ

from the context before and after them. Al-Baqarah 2:190 and the verses that follow speak about those who have entered war, driven Muslims out of their homes, and begun aggression. The opening of this passage says:

وَقَاتِلُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ وَلَا تَعْتَدُوا

So the command to kill in this context cannot be generalized to every person of another belief or every non-Muslim.

The opposite error is to erase completely the possibility of defensive fighting from the Qur'an. The Qur'an recognizes qital under specific conditions, but it joins it with the prohibition of aggression, stopping when the enemy stops, and observing limits.

8. Methodological Note: Qatl, Qital, and Iqtital

Qatl

Qatl is a one-sided act that causes the death of another:

قَتَلَ نَفْسًا

Qital

Qital is entering war and confrontation:

قَاتِلُوا الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ

Form III often highlights participation and confrontation, but one should not conclude from it that in every use both sides are equal ethically, in power, or in initiating the conflict.

One side may have initiated aggression and the other may enter qital in defense.

Iqtital

Iqtital is mutual conflict between two or more groups:

اقْتَتَلُوا

In this structure, the sides have become engaged with one another, but the verb itself does not say whether anyone was actually killed.

Therefore:

Qatl: killing; qital: fighting; iqtital: becoming mutually engaged in fighting.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

a. The Prohibition of Killing a Soul Without Right

وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا النَّفْسَ الَّتِي حَرَّمَ اللَّهُ إِلَّا بِالْحَقِّ

And do not kill the soul that The God has made inviolable, except by right.

Al-Isra 17:33

The principle of the verse is the inviolability of life.

The phrase except by right is a limited and responsibility-bearing exception, not an open license that every individual or group can execute on the basis of personal judgment.

The concept of "right" itself must be understood through the collection of verses about justice, qisas, prohibition of aggression, and legal responsibility.

b. Killing One Human Being and the Inviolability of All Human Life

مَنْ قَتَلَ نَفْسًا بِغَيْرِ نَفْسٍ أَوْ فَسَادٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَكَأَنَّمَا قَتَلَ النَّاسَ جَمِيعًا وَمَنْ أَحْيَاهَا فَكَأَنَّمَا أَحْيَا النَّاسَ جَمِيعًا

Whoever kills a soul without it being for a soul or for corruption in the earth, it is as though he killed all people; and whoever gives it life, it is as though he gave life to all people.

Al-Maidah 5:32

The verse presents the unlawful killing of one human being as an assault on the principle of the inviolability of life.

This comparison does not need to become a theory about nodes in a network. When the inviolability of one human life is broken, the very principle on which the security of all human life rests has been violated.

c. Qisas Concerning Those Killed

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الْقِصَاصُ فِي الْقَتْلَىٰ

O you who have faith, qisas has been prescribed for you concerning those killed.

Al-Baqarah 2:178

Al-qatla is the plural of qatil and means those who were killed.

The verse places qisas within the framework of justice and the prevention of unlimited blood vengeance. The continuation of the same verse also leaves open the possibility of pardon and payment in a proper manner.

So qisas does not mean an absolute obligation of revenge; it is one path of justice in which pardon can also have a role.

d. Life in Qisas

وَلَكُمْ فِي الْقِصَاصِ حَيَاةٌ يَا أُولِي الْأَلْبَابِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ

And for you there is life in qisas, O possessors of understanding, so that you may have taqwa.

Al-Baqarah 2:179

Qisas here is a tool for protecting life, not a praise of killing.

The aim is to restrain murder, prevent chains of vengeance, and protect society from the spread of bloodshed.

e. Accidental Killing

وَمَا كَانَ لِمُؤْمِنٍ أَنْ يَقْتُلَ مُؤْمِنًا إِلَّا خَطَأً

It is not for a believer to kill a believer, except by mistake.

An-Nisa 4:92

This verse shows that killing is not always intentional.

A person can cause the death of another without intending to kill. Nevertheless, absence of intent does not remove all responsibility; the verse assigns compensation and responsibility for accidental killing.

So the core of qatl is causing death, not necessarily conscious intent to kill.

f. Intentional Killing of a Believer

وَمَنْ يَقْتُلْ مُؤْمِنًا مُتَعَمِّدًا فَجَزَاؤُهُ جَهَنَّمُ

And whoever kills a believer intentionally, his recompense is Hell.

An-Nisa 4:93

The qualifier intentionally specifies conscious intent.

This qualifier proves that the verb qatl alone does not distinguish between intent and mistake; the type of responsibility must be understood from the context and qualifiers of the sentence.

g. Killing Children Because of Poverty

وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ مِنْ إِمْلَاقٍ نَحْنُ نَرْزُقُكُمْ وَإِيَّاهُمْ

And do not kill your children because of poverty; We provide for you and for them.

Al-Anam 6:151

Here killing has no connection with the battlefield. The subject is killing children because of existing poverty.

In another verse it says:

وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ خَشْيَةَ إِمْلَاقٍ نَحْنُ نَرْزُقُهُمْ وَإِيَّاكُمْ

And do not kill your children out of fear of poverty; We provide for them and for you.

Al-Isra 17:31

One verse speaks of present poverty and the other of fear of future poverty. In both, the life of the child is sacrificed to human pressure and economic calculation.

h. The Girl Buried Alive

وَإِذَا الْمَوْءُودَةُ سُئِلَتْ
بِأَيِّ ذَنْبٍ قُتِلَتْ

And when the girl buried alive is asked for what sin she was killed.

At-Takwir 81:8-9

The victim is questioned not because she is accountable for a crime, but to reveal her innocence and condemn the crime of the killer.

The resurrection context of the verse shows final justice. This meaning does not need terms such as "hardware audit of the body."

i. Killing the Prophets

فَلِمَ تَقْتُلُونَ أَنْبِيَاءَ اللَّهِ مِنْ قَبْلُ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ مُؤْمِنِينَ

Then why did you kill the prophets of The God before, if you were believers?

Al-Baqarah 2:91

Killing the prophets in the Qur'an is a sign of contradiction between the claim of faith and actual behavior.

People may take pride in their religious identity, yet rise in enmity against messengers of justice and truth.

j. "Died or Was Killed"

وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِنْ قَبْلِهِ الرُّسُلُ أَفَإِنْ مَاتَ أَوْ قُتِلَ انْقَلَبْتُمْ عَلَىٰ أَعْقَابِكُمْ

Muhammad is only a messenger; messengers passed before him. So if he dies or is killed, will you turn back on your heels?

Ali 'Imran 3:144

This verse is one of the clearest witnesses for the difference between mawt and qatl.

Mata expresses dying itself. Qutila expresses death that occurs through another agent or act.

But mawt should not be understood only as gradual natural wearing down. A person can die suddenly because of illness and mawt remains correct about him.

k. Qital Restricted by Response and Prohibition of Aggression

وَقَاتِلُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ وَلَا تَعْتَدُوا إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ الْمُعْتَدِينَ

And fight in the way of The God those who fight you, and do not transgress; indeed, The God does not love transgressors.

Al-Baqarah 2:190

This verse joins qital with two clear restrictions: the other side is fighting, and aggression is forbidden.

So Qur'anic qital in this context is not a command for unlimited war against all others.

l. "Kill Them Wherever You Find Them" in Its Full Context

وَاقْتُلُوهُمْ حَيْثُ ثَقِفْتُمُوهُمْ وَأَخْرِجُوهُمْ مِنْ حَيْثُ أَخْرَجُوكُمْ

And kill them wherever you find them in this conflict, and expel them from where they expelled you.

Al-Baqarah 2:191

This sentence must be read as a continuation of the previous verse and the rest of the same verse.

The subject is groups who fight the believers, expelled them from their place of living, and are in a state of battle.

The continuation of the verse also says not to fight them at the Sacred Mosque unless they fight you there. So the verse is not a license to kill people freely on the basis of religious identity; it is part of an instruction for battle in the context of aggression, expulsion, and reciprocal fighting.

m. Stopping Qital When the Enemy Stops

فَإِنِ انْتَهَوْا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ

Then if they stop, then The God is Forgiving, Merciful.

Al-Baqarah 2:192

Qital is not an independent and endless goal. When aggression stops, the ground for continuing war also disappears.

n. Conflict Between Two Believing Groups

وَإِنْ طَائِفَتَانِ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ اقْتَتَلُوا فَأَصْلِحُوا بَيْنَهُمَا

And if two groups of believers fight one another, then make peace between them.

Al-Hujurat 49:9

Iqtatalu points to mutual conflict.

The first command of the Qur'an before such a dispute is reconciliation. If one side transgresses against the other, the community must oppose the transgressing side until it returns to the command of The God; then justice and reconciliation are established.

o. "You Did Not Kill Them"

فَلَمْ تَقْتُلُوهُمْ وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ قَتَلَهُمْ وَمَا رَمَيْتَ إِذْ رَمَيْتَ وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ رَمَىٰ

You did not kill them, but The God killed them; and you did not throw when you threw, but The God threw.

Al-Anfal 8:17

This verse does not deny the human role; the phrase when you threw itself attributes the act of throwing to the Prophet.

But it does not regard the final result and victory as independent of divine will and aid.

Attributing killing to The God should not be translated into a bodily quality or direct physical presence of Him in the field. The verse speaks of His authority, aid, and decisive role in the result.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

قَتَلَ takes an object

In قَتَلَ نَفْسًا, nafs is the object of the verb and the one whose life has ended.

قُتِلَ is passive

In بِأَيِّ ذَنْبٍ قُتِلَتْ, the agent is not mentioned and attention is placed on the innocence of the victim and the event of her being killed.

قاتَلَ comes with an object or in a mutual structure

In يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ, the other side fights you.

The Form III structure shows confrontation, but does not necessarily prove moral equality between the sides.

بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ

This structure expresses the absence of right: يَقْتُلُونَ النَّبِيِّينَ بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ.

إِلَّا بِالْحَقِّ

It is a limited exception from the principle of the inviolability of life: وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا النَّفْسَ الَّتِي حَرَّمَ اللَّهُ إِلَّا بِالْحَقِّ.

فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ

In verses of qital, it specifies the claimed direction and aim of battle. But merely claiming "in the way of The God" does not make aggression legitimate; the same verse explicitly says: وَلَا تَعْتَدُوا.

حَيْثُ ثَقِفْتُمُوهُمْ

This construction, in the context of battle, refers to reaching or finding the fighting party. It must not be separated from the limits of the surrounding verses.

12. Q T L Compared with M W T

Mawt is the end of life itself.

Qatl is causing death through an agent or act.

So: mata: he died; qutila: he was killed.

This difference does not mean that mawt is always gradual, natural, or without a bodily agent. A person may die because of illness, an accident, or a sudden event.

The main difference is this: mawt expresses death from the perspective of the person and the ending of his life; qatl expresses death from the perspective of its agent and cause.

Ali 'Imran 3:144 places this difference side by side:

أَفَإِنْ مَاتَ أَوْ قُتِلَ

13. Q T L Compared with DH B H

Dhabh is a specific manner of killing connected with cutting the throat or vital passage of the neck.

Qatl is more general and is not restricted to a specific method.

So every dhabh that leads to death is a kind of qatl, but not every qatl is dhabh.

Dhabh in the Qur'an is used both about animals and about the killing of human beings:

يُذَبِّحُونَ أَبْنَاءَكُمْ

Here the particular method and violence of the act are highlighted.

14. Q T L Compared with H L K

Halak indicates destruction, loss, or coming to an end, and it may be used about a person, group, wealth, or power.

Qatl specifically points to causing the death of a living being.

A person may perish without being killed by a human being. Likewise, being killed may be the cause of his destruction.

So: qatl: the act of causing death; halak: the result of destruction or loss.

15. Qital Compared with H R B

Qital is the act of fighting and armed confrontation.

Harb more often indicates the state of war, hostility, or broader conflict.

One may cautiously say: qital: the act of battle and fighting; harb: the wider condition or field of war and hostility.

But qital should not be defined only as "hardware defense," nor harb only as "soft and economic war." The Qur'an does not present such a technical division.

Also, all cases of qital in the Qur'an are not identical in ruling and context. The subject, initiator, aim, limits, and stopping point of battle must be understood from the context of each verse.

16. Specialized Note: Is Qital Always Defensive?

The Qur'anic verses present qital in different contexts. In Al-Baqarah 2:190, the confrontation is with those who fight the believers, and aggression is also prohibited.

In other verses, contexts such as expulsion from homes, breaking covenants, persecution and fitnah, or continuation of war are mentioned.

From this collection, one can say that Qur'anic qital is not a free command for expanding violence. War is connected to the actual behavior and condition of the other party, justice, covenant, aggression, and the security of the community.

But a root article should not extract the whole Qur'anic theory of war only from the morphology of qatala.

Form III shows that the subject is confrontation; whether it is defensive or offensive, legitimate or illegitimate, and what its limits are must be understood from the broader context of the verses.

The clear principle in Al-Baqarah 2:190 is:

قَاتِلُوا الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ وَلَا تَعْتَدُوا

Therefore, any reading that removes these restrictions has separated the meaning of qital from Qur'anic ethics.

17. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

قَتَلَ

"Disabling an active flow" is not the translation of the word.

قُتِلَ

Was killed.

قَتَّلَ

Carried out killing. Killed intensely or repeatedly, according to context.

قاتَلَ

Fought. Entered battle with him.

It must not be translated as "killed."

قِتال

War. Battle. Armed conflict.

اقتَتَلَ

They became mutually engaged. They fought one another.

The translation "they killed one another" is correct only when the context also establishes actual killing.

قاتِل

Killer. One who kills.

مُقاتِل

Fighter. Combatant. One who participates in battle.

قَتْلَىٰ

Those killed.

قَتِيل

One killed.

18. Conclusion

The root Q T L in the Qur'an points above all to causing the death of a living being.

Qatl is not identical with mawt. Mawt is death itself, but qatl attributes death to an agent or act. This difference does not mean that mawt is always natural and gradual.

Qatl is also not always intentional. The Qur'an explicitly speaks of accidental killing and intentional killing. Therefore, intent and degree of responsibility are gained from qualifiers and context, not from the verb qatl alone.

A clear boundary must be kept between قَتَلَ and قاتَلَ: قَتَلَ means he killed; قاتَلَ means he fought; and اقتتل means they became mutually engaged.

A muqatil is not necessarily a qatil, and not every qital necessarily results in qatl.

The Qur'an presents human life as inviolable and regards the unlawful killing of one human being as an assault on the principle of the inviolability of all human life.

Qisas is prescribed for the protection of life and to prevent unlimited blood vengeance, and pardon also has a place within the same legal structure.

Killing children because of poverty or fear of poverty, killing prophets, intentional killing, accidental killing, and the killing of an innocent girl all show that this root is not limited to the battlefield.

Qital in the Qur'an must be read in its own context. Al-Baqarah 2:190 connects it with those who fight the believers and at the same time forbids aggression. The following sentences cannot be separated from this restriction.

The comparison between qital and harb must also not be turned into modern technical divisions. Qital is the act of fighting, and harb is the broader state of war and hostility, but the precise meaning of each use is determined by context.

The shortest expression for the core of this root is:

Causing the death of a living being; and in the reciprocal forms of this root, entering war or conflict in which killing is possible.
