Linguistic Note

The semantic core of R W D is the turning of will toward an aim. Irādah is not a raw impulse. It is an inner or practical orientation toward something wanted. When a person wills something, intent, decision, or action begins to face that aim.

In the Qur'an, will may be attributed to The God or to the human being. The will of The God is joined with knowledge, wisdom, justice, and encompassing authority. Human will is limited and mixed with desire, assumption, and weak capacity. Therefore, يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ (yurīdu Allāh) must not be understood like human wanting. The God wills, but not through uncertainty, need, trial and error, or bodily passion.

The branch of murāwadah belongs to the same field of wanting, but it is a wanting that approaches another person, circles around the aim, pulls, or tries to make the other person yield. In the story of Yusuf, this branch is especially precise and sensitive.

Central Meaning

R W D in the Qur'an points to wanting, willing, intending, turning toward an aim, and in some forms seeking with effort, insistence, or gradual approach in order to obtain something.

أَرَادَ (arāda) and يُرِيدُ (yurīdu) indicate wanting and willing. رَاوَدَ (rāwada) and murāwadah are closer to seeking something from another person with insistence, attraction, circling around the aim, and attempting to soften the other party. رُوَيْدًا (ruwaydan) points to calmness, a little delay, and proceeding gently.

Material Image

The material image is a person moving toward a destination, seeking it, testing ways of approaching it, and moving calmly or steadily toward it. In older Arabic usage, a rā'id is someone who goes ahead of the people to find water and pasture. This image helps explain the root: will is turning toward an aim; murāwadah is circling and working to obtain the desired thing.

This also clarifies the difference between will and simple inclination. Inclination may be only attraction, but will is orientation toward an intended object. Murāwadah becomes visible when that orientation turns into insistence and seeking from another person.

Lexical reports mention the mirwad, a fine tool that moves in and out of the kohl container. The image is useful for murāwadah: repeated movement, circling around a center, and soft but persistent effort toward an aim. Still, Qur'anic usage remains the main criterion here.

Historical and Arabic Usage Note

In classical Arabic, R W D is tied to seeking, wanting, gentle movement, searching, and effort toward an aim. Rāwada usually conveys seeking something from someone with back-and-forth approach, softening, deception, or insistence. This meaning is especially vivid in the Qur'anic story of Yusuf.

The well-known saying al-rā'idu lā yakdhibu ahlah, the scout does not lie to his people, also fits this field. The one who goes ahead to find water and pasture must report truthfully, because the desire and survival of the group depend on his direction-finding. The image shows that will is not merely inward feeling; it is turning, assessing, and taking direction toward a goal.

In Qur'anic usage, irādah is wider than a simple psychological wish. It may show a plan, command, direction, aim, legislative will, or creational will. For that reason, translating it only as "want" is often too thin. Depending on context, "willed," "intended," "wants," "seeks," "is after," or "is oriented toward" may be more precise.

The Will of The God: Wisdom and Transcendence

One of the most important uses of this root is attributing will to The God. In Al-Baqarah 2:185, the Qur'an says:

يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ بِكُمُ الْيُسْرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ الْعُسْرَ (yurīdu Allāhu bikumu al-yusra wa-lā yurīdu bikumu al-ʿusr)

The God wants ease for you and does not want hardship for you.

In this verse, divine will is tied to ease and the removal of hardship. This is not an unwise wish or a human impulse; it shows the direction of divine legislation. The ruling of fasting in this context includes travel and illness so that it becomes clear: The God does not want purposeless hardship.

In Al-Ma'idah 5:6, it says: مَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيَجْعَلَ عَلَيْكُم مِّنْ حَرَجٍ وَلَٰكِن يُرِيدُ لِيُطَهِّرَكُمْ وَلِيُتِمَّ نِعْمَتَهُ عَلَيْكُمْ (mā yurīdu Allāhu li-yajʿala ʿalaykum min ḥarajin wa-lākin yurīdu li-yuṭahhirakum wa-li-yutimma niʿmatahu ʿalaykum). The God does not want to place constriction upon you; rather, He wants to purify you and complete His blessing upon you. The rulings of wuḍū', ghusl, and tayammum are not meant to crush the human being. They serve purification, order, and completion of blessing.

Clarification, Easing, and Guidance

In Surat An-Nisa, several nearby verses show the direction of divine will. In An-Nisa 4:26, the Qur'an says:

يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُبَيِّنَ لَكُمْ وَيَهْدِيَكُمْ سُنَنَ الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ وَيَتُوبَ عَلَيْكُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ (yurīdu Allāhu li-yubayyina lakum wa-yahdiyakum sunana alladhīna min qablikum wa-yatūba ʿalaykum wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm)

The God wants to make things clear for you, guide you to the ways of those before you, and turn back to you; The God is knowing and wise.

In An-Nisa 4:28, it says: يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ أَن يُخَفِّفَ عَنكُمْ ۚ وَخُلِقَ الْإِنسَانُ ضَعِيفًا (yurīdu Allāhu an yukhaffifa ʿankum wa-khuliqa al-insānu ḍaʿīfā). The God wants to lighten the burden from you, and the human being was created weak.

In these verses, the will of The God moves toward clarification, guidance, acceptance of return, and easing. This matters for translation. يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ (yurīdu Allāh) is not merely a report that The God wants something; it reveals the aim and direction of divine legislation.

Irādah and Mashī'ah

Irādah and mashī'ah are close, but not identical. Mashī'ah is nearer to enabling possibility, the force of power, and occurrence within the field of creation. Irādah more often shows the direction of will, aim, purpose, and wisdom of a matter.

In simple terms, mashī'ah is closer to whether something occurs, or can occur, within divine power and permission. Irādah is closer to the direction The God wants for guidance, legislation, purification, easing, or completion of blessing. The God may allow the field of choice, and even the possibility of falsehood, in creation; but in legislation, He wills ease, purity, guidance, and return for the human being.

This distinction should not be made rigid or absolute, but it helps translation and interpretation. Wherever the Qur'an says يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ (yurīdu Allāh), the aim and direction of that will must be examined.

The Will of The God Regarding Ahl Al-Bayt in Al-Ahzab 33:33

In Al-Ahzab 33:33, the Qur'an says:

وَقَرْنَ فِي بُيُوتِكُنَّ وَلَا تَبَرَّجْنَ تَبَرُّجَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ الْأُولَىٰ وَأَقِمْنَ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتِينَ الزَّكَاةَ وَأَطِعْنَ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ ۚ إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ الرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًا (wa-qarna fī buyūtikunna wa-lā tabarrajna tabarruja al-jāhiliyyati al-ūlā wa-aqimna al-ṣalāta wa-ātīna al-zakāta wa-aṭiʿna Allāha wa-rasūlahu innamā yurīdu Allāhu li-yudhhiba ʿankumu al-rijsa ahla al-bayti wa-yuṭahhirakum taṭhīrā)

Here يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ (yurīdu Allāh) shows the will to remove impurity and purify. The verse appears in the same context where the wives of the Prophet are directly addressed. Therefore, the first reference of أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ (ahla al-bayt) is the household of the Prophet in this context. The phrase may, in ordinary usage, extend to close family, but the verse itself does not establish a sacred, exclusive group detached from the wives of the Prophet.

For R W D, the main point is that divine will here concerns purification and moral responsibility in the house of the Prophet, not the creation of a sectarian privilege outside the context of the verse.

Human Will: Limited and Directional

The Qur'an also speaks of human will. In Ash-Shuraa 42:20, it says:

مَن كَانَ يُرِيدُ حَرْثَ الْآخِرَةِ نَزِدْ لَهُ فِي حَرْثِهِ ۖ وَمَن كَانَ يُرِيدُ حَرْثَ الدُّنْيَا نُؤْتِهِ مِنْهَا وَمَا لَهُ فِي الْآخِرَةِ مِن نَّصِيبٍ (man kāna yurīdu ḥartha al-ākhirati nazid lahu fī ḥarthihi wa-man kāna yurīdu ḥartha al-dunyā nu'tihi minhā wa-mā lahu fī al-ākhirati min naṣīb)

Whoever wants the harvest of the next life, We increase for him in his harvest; and whoever wants the harvest of this world, We give him from it, and he has no share in the next life.

Here human will shows the direction of life. One wants the harvest of the next life and arranges the path toward it. Another wants the harvest of this world and remains within that horizon. Will here is not mere wishing; it is practical orientation and life-aim.

In Al-Isra 17:18, it says: مَن كَانَ يُرِيدُ الْعَاجِلَةَ عَجَّلْنَا لَهُ فِيهَا مَا نَشَاءُ لِمَن نُّرِيدُ (man kāna yurīdu al-ʿājilata ʿajjalnā lahu fīhā mā nashā'u liman nurīd). يُرِيدُ الْعَاجِلَةَ (yurīdu al-ʿājilah) means the person whose aim is the immediate, rushing life. Will determines the horizon of the human being.

The Wall That Wanted to Collapse

In Al-Kahf 18:77, will is attributed to something that is not human:

فَوَجَدَا فِيهَا جِدَارًا يُرِيدُ أَن يَنقَضَّ فَأَقَامَهُ (fa-wajadā fīhā jidāran yurīdu an yanqaḍḍa fa-aqāmah)

They found in it a wall that wanted to collapse, so he set it upright.

جِدَارًا يُرِيدُ أَن يَنقَضَّ (jidāran yurīdu an yanqaḍḍa) does not mean the wall had a psychological will. The wall was in a condition directed toward collapse; its weight, angle, and structure were moving toward falling. This verse shows that irādah in Qur'anic language can express the direction of a condition, not only conscious human decision.

Conflicting Wills: This World and the Next Life

In some verses, human will and divine will appear in ethical contrast. In Al-Anfal 8:67, the Qur'an says:

تُرِيدُونَ عَرَضَ الدُّنْيَا وَاللَّهُ يُرِيدُ الْآخِرَةَ ۗ وَاللَّهُ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ (turīdūna ʿaraḍa al-dunyā wa-Allāhu yurīdu al-ākhirah wa-Allāhu ʿazīzun ḥakīm)

You want the passing goods of this world, while The God wants the next life; The God is mighty and wise.

Here تُرِيدُونَ (turīdūn) and يُرِيدُ (yurīdu) set two directions against one another: human want moves toward immediate gain, while the will of The God points toward the more enduring and correct aim. Will is not merely mental wishing; it is a path of valuation and decision.

In Ali 'Imran 3:145, it says: وَمَن يُرِدْ ثَوَابَ الدُّنْيَا نُؤْتِهِ مِنْهَا وَمَن يُرِدْ ثَوَابَ الْآخِرَةِ نُؤْتِهِ مِنْهَا (wa-man yurid thawāba al-dunyā nu'tihi minhā wa-man yurid thawāba al-ākhirati nu'tihi minhā). A person may perform an outward act, while the destination of that person's will is this world or the next life.

Hidden Intent and Corrupted Desire

Sometimes the Qur'an exposes a will hidden behind speech or action. In At-Tawbah 9:32, it says:

يُرِيدُونَ أَن يُطْفِئُوا نُورَ اللَّهِ بِأَفْوَاهِهِمْ وَيَأْبَى اللَّهُ إِلَّا أَن يُتِمَّ نُورَهُ (yurīdūna an yuṭfi'ū nūra Allāhi bi-afwāhihim wa-ya'bā Allāhu illā an yutimma nūrah)

They want to extinguish the light of The God with their mouths, but The God refuses except to complete His light.

Here will is destructive intent. They are not merely speaking; behind their speech is an aim: to extinguish the light of The God. The Qur'an exposes the hidden will.

In As-Saff 61:8, it says: يُرِيدُونَ لِيُطْفِئُوا نُورَ اللَّهِ بِأَفْوَاهِهِمْ وَاللَّهُ مُتِمُّ نُورِهِ وَلَوْ كَرِهَ الْكَافِرُونَ (yurīdūna li-yuṭfi'ū nūra Allāhi bi-afwāhihim wa-Allāhu mutimmu nūrihi wa-law kariha al-kāfirūn). Human will is limited and breakable; it cannot extinguish truth before divine completion.

Murāwadah in the Story of Yusuf

Another important branch of this root is murāwadah. In Yusuf 12:23, the Qur'an says:

وَرَاوَدَتْهُ الَّتِي هُوَ فِي بَيْتِهَا عَن نَّفْسِهِ وَغَلَّقَتِ الْأَبْوَابَ وَقَالَتْ هَيْتَ لَكَ (wa-rāwadathu allatī huwa fī baytihā ʿan nafsihi wa-ghallaqati al-abwāba wa-qālat hayta lak)

The woman in whose house he was sought him from himself, shut the doors, and said, "Come, for you."

رَاوَدَتْهُ عَن نَّفْسِهِ (rāwadathu ʿan nafsih) means she sought him from himself with insistence and attraction. She tried to pull him out of his own boundary and make him yield to her desire. This is not merely love, nor a simple request. Murāwadah is seeking with approach, pressure, preparation, and insistence.

The particle عن (ʿan) in عَن نَّفْسِهِ (ʿan nafsih) is important. The wife of the Aziz does not merely invite Yusuf; she seeks him away from his self, away from self-restraint and his moral sovereignty. The structure carries the ethical intensity of the scene.

In Yusuf 12:30, it says: امْرَأَتُ الْعَزِيزِ تُرَاوِدُ فَتَاهَا عَن نَّفْسِهِ (imra'atu al-ʿazīzi turāwidu fatāhā ʿan nafsih). تُرَاوِدُ (turāwidu) shows the continuation and public awareness of this effort. Murāwadah is not a one-time silent wish; it is a continuing and insistent seeking.

In Yusuf 12:61, Yusuf's brothers say: سَنُرَاوِدُ عَنْهُ أَبَاهُ وَإِنَّا لَفَاعِلُونَ (sanurāwidu ʿanhu abāhu wa-innā la-fāʿilūn). Here the meaning is not sexual. It means they will speak with his father, insist, and work to persuade him. This shows that murāwadah is not always sexual; its basic meaning is seeking with effort and softening the other party.

Ruwaydan: Gentle and Brief Delay

In At-Tariq 86:17, the Qur'an says:

فَمَهِّلِ الْكَافِرِينَ أَمْهِلْهُمْ رُوَيْدًا (fa-mahhili al-kāfirīna amhilhum ruwaydā)

So give the coverers of truth time; give them a little calm delay.

رُوَيْدًا (ruwaydan) points to gentleness, a small delay, and calmness in the matter. This form is not about willing in the sense of wanting, but it is still not far from the root's field of gentle and gradual movement.

Distinguishing Qur'anic Uses

In Al-Baqarah 2:185, the will of The God concerns ease and the removal of hardship. In Al-Ma'idah 5:6, it concerns purification and completion of blessing. In An-Nisa 4:26 and 4:28, it concerns clarification, guidance, turning back, and easing. In Al-Ahzab 33:33, it concerns removing impurity and purifying Ahl al-Bayt in the context of the house of the Prophet.

In Al-Kahf 18:77, will attributed to the wall means being about to collapse. In Ash-Shuraa 42:20 and Al-Isra 17:18, human will shows the direction of this world or the next life. In Al-Anfal 8:67, human desire for worldly goods stands opposite the will of The God for the next life. In At-Tawbah 9:32 and As-Saff 61:8, will appears as the intent to extinguish the light of The God. In Yusuf 12:23 and 12:30, murāwadah is seeking with insistence and ethically corrupt attraction. In Yusuf 12:61, murāwadah is effort to persuade the father. In At-Tariq 86:17, ruwaydan means a little gentle delay.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ بِكُمُ الْيُسْرَ (yurīdu Allāhu bikumu al-yusr), the باء (bā') in بِكُم (bikum) shows that the will of The God is directed toward ease for you. The structure carries mercy and legislative wisdom.

In يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُبَيِّنَ لَكُمْ (yurīdu Allāhu li-yubayyina lakum), the lām shows the direction and aim of the will: clarifying for you. The same lām appears in phrases such as لِيُطَهِّرَكُمْ (li-yuṭahhirakum) and لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ الرِّجْسَ (li-yudhhiba ʿankumu al-rijs).

In مَن كَانَ يُرِيدُ (man kāna yurīdu), will is expressed as an ongoing state. It shows the direction of desire and life-aim, not a passing thought.

In جِدَارًا يُرِيدُ أَن يَنقَضَّ (jidāran yurīdu an yanqaḍḍa), will is attributed to a wall. This does not mean psychological decision; it conveys the objective direction of the condition: the wall was about to fall.

In رَاوَدَتْهُ عَن نَّفْسِهِ (rāwadathu ʿan nafsih), the particle عن (ʿan) matters. She sought him from himself, trying to draw him out of his moral boundary. In سَنُرَاوِدُ عَنْهُ أَبَاهُ (sanurāwidu ʿanhu abāh), the same particle shows that the matter sought is taking Binyamin from his father.

Difference From Nearby Roots

R W D is close to Sh Y ', but not identical. Mashī'ah in the Qur'an is closer to effective willing, permission, decree, and occurrence within the field of power. Irādah more often shows the direction of will, aim, and purpose.

R W D differs from ʿ Z M. ʿAzm is firm, bound decision. Irādah may come before ʿazm or be wider than it. A person may will something without yet having firm resolve.

R W D differs from H M M. Hamm is initial intent, concern, or pull, sometimes before action and sometimes on its edge. Irādah shows the direction of will more clearly and steadily.

R W D is close to Q Ṣ D. Qaṣd is directing oneself straight toward an aim and taking a measured path. Irādah is wanting the aim; qaṣd is direct orientation toward it.

R W D differs from B Gh Y. Baghy can mean seeking with transgression or crossing a limit. Irādah is not positive or negative by itself; context determines whether the will is sound or corrupted.

R W D differs from W D D. Wudd is love, affection, and a wish for nearness. Irādah can exist without love; someone may want something for benefit or control, not out of affection.

R W D is close to B Gh Y in the form ibtighā'. Ibtighā' is seeking and pursuing something, usually with effort. Irādah is the direction of will; ibtighā' is the movement of seeking after that will.

R W D differs from Ḥ B B. Ḥubb is love and affectionate attraction. Irādah may be based on love or not. The God wills from wisdom; a human being may love something without practically willing it.

Forqan Translation Method

In Forqan translation, R W D should not always be translated simply as "want." In many verses, "will," "intend," "seek," "be after," "want for you," "direct the ruling toward," or "take as one's aim" is more precise.

Regarding The God, يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ (yurīdu Allāh) must be understood with transcendence. The God does not experience lack, uncertainty, passion, or bodily desire. Divine will means the wise direction of command, creation, guidance, or purification.

In the story of Yusuf, راود (rāwada) must be translated carefully. رَاوَدَتْهُ عَن نَّفْسِهِ (rāwadathu ʿan nafsih) is not merely "she wanted him." It is "she sought him from himself," meaning she insistently tried to pull him out of his own moral boundary. But in سَنُرَاوِدُ عَنْهُ أَبَاهُ (sanurāwidu ʿanhu abāh), the meaning is not sexual; it means they will insist and work with his father to let him go with them.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

أَرَادَ (arāda): wanted; willed; intended.

يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ بِكُمُ الْيُسْرَ (yurīdu Allāhu bikumu al-yusr): The God wants ease for you; The God wills ease for you.

وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ الْعُسْرَ (wa-lā yurīdu bikumu al-ʿusr): and He does not want hardship for you.

يُرِيدُ لِيُطَهِّرَكُمْ (yurīdu li-yuṭahhirakum): He wants to purify you; He wills to make you clean.

يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ أَن يُخَفِّفَ عَنكُمْ (yurīdu Allāhu an yukhaffifa ʿankum): The God wants to lighten the burden from you.

جِدَارًا يُرِيدُ أَن يَنقَضَّ (jidāran yurīdu an yanqaḍḍa): a wall that wanted to collapse; a wall that was about to fall; a wall leaning toward collapse.

مَن كَانَ يُرِيدُ حَرْثَ الْآخِرَةِ (man kāna yurīdu ḥartha al-ākhirah): whoever wants the harvest of the next life; whoever sets the harvest of the next life as the horizon of effort.

تُرِيدُونَ عَرَضَ الدُّنْيَا (turīdūna ʿaraḍa al-dunyā): you want the passing goods of this world.

يُرِيدُونَ أَن يُطْفِئُوا نُورَ اللَّهِ (yurīdūna an yuṭfi'ū nūra Allāh): they want to extinguish the light of The God; they are seeking to extinguish the light of The God.

رَاوَدَتْهُ عَن نَّفْسِهِ (rāwadathu ʿan nafsih): she sought him from himself; she tried to pull him out of his self-restraint; she insistently tried to draw him out of his own boundary.

تُرَاوِدُ فَتَاهَا عَن نَّفْسِهِ (turāwidu fatāhā ʿan nafsih): she seeks her young man from himself; she persistently draws him toward her desire.

سَنُرَاوِدُ عَنْهُ أَبَاهُ (sanurāwidu ʿanhu abāh): we will seek him from his father; we will insist and speak with his father.

رُوَيْدًا (ruwaydan): a little calmly; with a short delay; gently and briefly.

Summary

R W D in the Qur'an points to wanting, willing, intending, and turning toward an aim. This will may be divine, moving toward wisdom, purification, ease, guidance, and lightening burdens. It may also be human, revealing the person's aim: this world, the next life, extinguishing truth, or seeking something from another person.

The branch of murāwadah is not simple wanting. It is seeking with insistence, approach, softening, or drawing someone toward an aim. In the story of Yusuf, this branch appears both in an ethically and sexually charged context and in the context of persuading a father to release Binyamin. Context determines the direction of meaning.

The distinction between this root and mashī'ah, ʿazm, qaṣd, hamm, ibtighā', and wudd should be preserved. Irādah shows the direction of will and aim. Mashī'ah is closer to power and occurrence. ʿAzm is firm resolve. Qaṣd is direct orientation. Hamm is a pull or initial intent. Ibtighā' is seeking and pursuit. Wudd is affection and loving desire.

In summary: R W D in the Qur'an points to directed will and turning toward an intended aim. It begins with divine and human willing, extends to murāwadah as seeking with insistence, and in ruwaydan shows the sense of gentle and gradual delay.
