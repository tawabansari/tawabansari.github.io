Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root N F Q

Infāq as giving out from provision and setting blessing into motion, and nifāq as hidden doubleness, departure from truthfulness, and the concealed passage between outward appearance and inward reality

Important note: this root has two major Qur’anic branches. The branch of infāq concerns giving out, spending, and setting provision into motion. The branch of nifāq concerns hidden doubleness, concealment, and rupture between inward reality and outward appearance. These meanings should be connected through the root image of exit and passage, but they should not be collapsed into one meaning.

General meaning of the root N F Q

The root ن ف ق (N F Q) is one of the Qur’anic roots that points both to one of the purest social actions of the human being and to one of the most dangerous moral fractures within him. In the branch of infāq, it means giving out, spending, and setting something from provision into motion. In the branch of nifāq, it means doubleness, concealment, and the separation of inward reality from outward appearance.

At first glance, infāq and nifāq seem very far apart. One is a sign of faith, generosity, and responsibility; the other is a sign of falsehood, fear, concealment, and inner corruption. Yet the shared root shows that in both there is a kind of exit or way out.

In infāq, the human being takes something he possesses and brings it out of confinement within himself. Wealth, ability, time, knowledge, food, opportunity, or any provision that has reached him does not remain trapped inside the circle of the ego and private ownership. It comes out of him and flows into the path of life, mercy, justice, and reform.

But in nifāq, exit takes a dark form. The human being exits truthfulness, but he does not admit it openly. He builds for himself a hidden passage. Outwardly he is with the people of faith, but inwardly he has another path. His tongue says one thing, while his heart or action pursues another.

So this root is very deep in the Qur’anic view. A human being either brings out what The God has given him and sends it into the path of good, or he himself exits truthfulness and moves through the hidden passage of nifāq.

The sensory layer of the root: path, burrow, exit, and hidden passage

To understand this root more clearly, we need to notice its sensory layer. Nafaq can refer to a hidden passage, a burrow, or a tunnel-like way that leads from one place to another. In this image, something comes out of a closed place, or there is a concealed route for passage.

The same image appears in both branches of the root. In infāq, provision comes out of a closed and confined state. Something that could have remained only in a person’s hand leaves the circle of self-centeredness and enters the flow of other people’s lives.

But in nifāq, the human being hides himself. His outward appearance has one entrance, while his inward reality has another exit. He is like someone who enters through an open way, but has built another route for hidden escape. Outwardly he stands with the community of faith, but inwardly he is not aligned with truth.

This sensory layer helps us understand why the Qur’an uses one root for these two branches. The main issue is not merely spending or hypocrisy; the issue is what the human being does with what is inside him. Does he bring it out in the path of truth, or does he conceal it and build a double path?

The image of the yarbūʿ and the root of nifāq

In old Arabic usage, nafaq was also connected with the image of the burrow of an animal such as the yarbūʿ, a desert jerboa. It is said that this animal would make for its shelter both an open path and a hidden path. It would enter through one burrow, but keep another exit prepared; an exit that was not fully visible from outside and was covered by a thin layer of earth. If danger came through the main entrance, it would escape through that hidden passage.

This image makes the meaning of munāfiq clearer. The munāfiq also has an open path and a hidden path. He enters through the door of faith, speaks with the language of the people of faith, and appears among them, but he keeps an exit route for himself. If faith demands a cost, if truthfulness becomes risky, if truth requires sacrifice, he leaves through that hidden passage.

So nifāq is not merely a simple lie. Nifāq is the construction of an escape route inside the personality. The munāfiq wants to benefit from the honor of faith, but does not want to bear the cost of faith. He wants to appear outwardly aligned with truth, but on the day of testing, he wants to keep his exit.

This image also deepens the root connection between infāq and nifāq. In infāq, provision exits the human being and flows into the path of good. In nifāq, the human being himself exits truthfulness and escapes through a hidden path.

Infāq: giving out from what The God has delivered

In the Qur’an, infāq means giving out from what the human being has in his possession. But in the Qur’anic view, this giving does not begin from the human being’s absolute ownership. The human being does not own the source of provision. He is a receiver. What he has, before being his private possession, is provision that The God has delivered to him.

That is why the Qur’an speaks with expressions such as mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn: from what We have provided them, they give out. This expression is extremely important. The Qur’an does not merely say that they give from their own wealth; it says that they give from what We have delivered to them.

So infāq is the movement of provision from the self toward need. The human being does not imprison within himself what The God has given him. He sets it into motion in the right path. This may be wealth, but it is not only wealth. It may be time, knowledge, skill, bodily ability, social power, food, security, attention, or any other possibility that answers the need of a creature.

From this perspective, infāq is the opposite of imprisoning provision. When provision freezes in the hand of the human being, it can become an idol of ownership. But when it flows in the right path, it becomes mercy, reform, and purification.

Yunfiqūn: a feature of the people of faith

The form yunfiqūn is one of the important signs of the people of faith and the people of taqwā in the Qur’an. It points to those whose faith is not merely a claim, but appears in their social, financial, and moral behavior.

The one who gives out admits through action that what he has is from The God and is not only for himself. He sees provision as a trust, not as a tool of superiority and control. For this reason, infāq is not only an economic act; it is a declaration of tawḥīd. Through infāq, the human being says: I am not the source of provision. I am only a receiver and a responsible being.

Yunfiqūn are those who do not stop provision. When they see a need, when they see a path of good, when they see an opening for reform, they give from what they have. This giving may be public or hidden, much or little, financial or non-financial; but its spirit is one: blessing must not be imprisoned in service of the self.

For this reason, infāq in the Qur’an is connected with faith, taqwā, gratitude, ṣalāt, zakāh, and social reform. Whoever truly sees The God as present and witnessing cannot remain completely indifferent to the need of The God’s creation.

Infāq is not only giving money

One common error is to limit infāq to paying money. Undoubtedly, money is one of the clearest examples of infāq, but it is not the whole meaning.

The Qur’an says that they give out from what The God has provided them. Provision is not only wealth. Provision may be knowledge, power, opportunity, health, skill, experience, language, the ability to write, the ability to build, the ability to heal, or even patience, attention, and kindness.

If someone gives food to a person in need, he has made infāq. If someone teaches another human being the right path, he has given from his knowledge. If someone spends his time for reform, teaching, healing, care, or helping another, he has given from his time. If someone uses his power or position to defend the oppressed, he has given from his social capacity.

So infāq means giving out from provision, not only giving out money. Every blessing that The God has delivered to the human being can become the subject of infāq. The main question is: does the human being keep the blessing only for himself, or does he set it into motion in a pure path?

Infāq and rizq: from what has been provided

The connection between infāq and rizq is one of the keys to understanding this root. With the expression mimmā razaqnāhum, the Qur’an turns the human being’s attention toward the source of blessing. Before the human being gives, he himself has received.

This view heals two diseases. First, it heals miserliness, because the human being realizes that what he has is not absolutely from himself. Second, it heals the arrogance of the giver, because the one who gives out must not imagine himself to be the creator of provision. He gives from something that was first given to him.

For this reason, true infāq is not compatible with reminding, humiliation, or moral domination. If a person helps someone in need but breaks his dignity through that help, he has not yet understood the meaning of infāq. He did not create something from his own independent kingdom; he was only placed in the chain of delivering provision.

When infāq is joined with the understanding of rizq, it saves the receiver from humiliation before human beings and saves the giver from arrogance. The receiver knows that the true Provider is The God. The giver knows that he is only a means and a responsible being.

Infāq and coming out of miserliness

Infāq does not only answer the need of the poor; it also exposes and treats the narrowness within the human being. One of the greatest constrictions of the self is miserliness. Miserliness means that the human being imprisons blessing within himself, fears its being spent, and sees his security in accumulation.

Infāq breaks this confinement. When the human being gives out from something he loves, he teaches his self that provision is from The God, true security is with The God, and wealth is not god. For this reason, infāq is a practice of tawḥīd.

Miserliness is not only a financial problem. A person may be miserly with knowledge, with affection, with time, with attention, with forgiveness. Infāq is a coming out of this narrowness. The human being comes out of self-centeredness and enters the realm of responsibility.

But Qur’anic infāq is not blind or careless. Infāq must be joined with wisdom, justice, and a pure aim. A person should not strengthen oppression through giving, create unhealthy dependence, or neglect his nearest responsibilities. Correct infāq sets provision into motion in the path of life and reform.

Infāq, ṣadaqah, and zakāh

Infāq, ṣadaqah, and zakāh are close to one another in the Qur’an, but they are not the same. All three are connected with giving, the purification of the human being, and the reform of the human relationship with society; yet each has its own angle of meaning.

Infāq comes from the root N F Q and emphasizes giving out, spending, and setting provision into motion. The human being sends something from what The God has delivered to him beyond the circle of himself. So infāq focuses especially on the movement of provision and its exit from private confinement.

Ṣadaqah comes from the root Ṣ D Q and is connected with truthfulness, sincerity, and being true. Ṣadaqah is a giving that reveals the truthfulness of faith, intention, and the human being’s relationship with The God and with people. An act may outwardly be infāq, but if it is joined with showing off, reminding, humiliation, or domination, it becomes distant from the spirit of ṣadaqah, because ṣadaqah must be a sign of truthfulness.

Zakāh comes from the root Z K W / Z K Y and is connected with purity, growth, pure increase, and inward reform. In the Qur’anic view, zakāh is not merely a dry financial payment; it is an act that purifies the human being, reforms society, and changes provision from a stored and self-centered state into a flowing and beneficial one.

To clarify the difference between these three words, we can say: infāq is the form of the movement of possession and blessing; ṣadaqah is the intention behind this movement; and zakāh is the result and effect of this movement.

So infāq is the act of giving out and setting provision into motion; ṣadaqah shows the truthfulness of intention and giving; and zakāh is the purifying and growth-giving effect of this act in the human being and society.

These three can gather in one act. If someone helps a person in need from his wealth, then from the angle of giving wealth out, it is infāq; if it is done from truthfulness and without display, it is ṣadaqah; and if it purifies the self, reduces miserliness, and contributes to social reform, it comes close to zakāh in the broad Qur’anic sense.

But these words should not be forced into one meaning. The Qur’an uses each word in its own place. Infāq has a wide range. Ṣadaqah emphasizes truthfulness of intention and action. Zakāh emphasizes purification and growth.

Nafaqah: expense that answers need

Nafaqah and nafaqāt are noun forms from this root and refer to an expense, maintenance, or something spent to answer a need. In this sense, nafaqah is not merely the exit of wealth; it is the exit of wealth in response to responsibility.

A human being lives within relationships: family, relatives, people in need, society, and sometimes covenants and responsibilities placed upon him. Nafaqah means that part of provision is spent in the path of preservation, support, and answering need.

This meaning shows that spending is not always wrong. The problem is not spending itself; the problem is the direction of spending. Spending in the path of corruption, excess, domination, appetite, or oppression lowers the human being. But spending that answers real need and fulfills responsibility can belong to the path of infāq and reform.

From this angle, nafaqah is close to infāq, but it is not identical to it. Nafaqah points more to a specific expense or maintenance for a need or responsibility. Infāq has a wider range and includes every correct giving out from provision.

Nafaq: the hidden passage and the root-image of nifāq

Nafaq refers to a hidden path, burrow, or passageway that has a route for crossing or exit. This form is very important for understanding nifāq. The munāfiq is not one-pathed. He has an outward face and a hidden passage for exiting truthfulness.

In the life of faith, truthfulness means that the outward appearance, tongue, heart, and action of the human being are, as much as possible, moving in one direction. But nifāq breaks this connection. A person says one thing, but wants something else. He presents himself as aligned with a group, but inwardly or practically follows another path.

The image of nafaq shows that nifāq is not only a simple lie. Nifāq is the building of a hidden passage inside the personality; a passage through which the human being escapes responsibility, truthfulness, and loyalty to truth without openly saying, “I have left.”

For this reason, nifāq may be more dangerous than open disbelief. Open disbelief does not hide its opposition; but nifāq wears the clothing of faith and erodes trust, society, and truth from within.

Nifāq: the rupture between inward reality and outward appearance

In the Qur’an, nifāq is the rupture between inward reality and outward appearance. The munāfiq says something with his tongue, but pursues something else with his heart and action. Outwardly he may speak of faith, reform, or good, but inwardly he may be seeking the security of the self, personal benefit, power, escape from responsibility, or alignment with falsehood.

Nifāq is not merely a dry theological issue; it is an ethical corruption in the connection between a person’s inside and outside. When the human being fears truthfulness, when he does not want to pay the cost of truth, when he wants to benefit from both sides, he moves close to nifāq.

The munāfiq wants to appear outwardly with the people of faith in order to gain safety and honor, but inwardly he keeps a path of exit so that if truth has a cost, he can escape. This is the spirit of nifāq: the refusal of complete truthfulness.

For this reason, nifāq is connected with fear, falsehood, display, breaking covenants, excuses, and two-facedness. The munāfiq may possess religious language, but his religious language becomes a covering for the absence of truthfulness.

The munāfiq: a human being with two paths

The munāfiq is a human being whose inward reality and outward appearance are not one. He builds two paths for himself: one open path for being seen, and one hidden path for his real interests. This two-pathed condition distances him from truthfulness.

Outwardly, he may present himself as aligned with faith. He may use correct words. He may be present among the people of faith. But the Qur’anic measure is not claim and outward presence alone. The measure is truthfulness, action, loyalty, and the real direction of the human being.

Unlike the truthful believer, the munāfiq fears the clarification of truth, because truth exposes his hidden passage. He prefers ambiguity so that he can gain honor from faith and benefit from falsehood at the same time.

This danger is not limited to one historical group. Every human being and every religious society can fall into degrees of nifāq whenever its language becomes separated from its action, its appearance becomes distant from its inward reality, or religion is used to preserve reputation, power, tribe, sect, or benefit.

The difference between infāq and nifāq

Infāq and nifāq come from one root, but ethically they stand in opposite directions.

Infāq is the exit of provision from the prison of the self. Nifāq is the exit of the human being from truthfulness into a hidden passage. Infāq makes the inner self cleaner; nifāq splits the inner self. Infāq sets blessing, openly or secretly, into the path of good; nifāq conceals the inward reality and turns the outward appearance into a tool of deception.

In infāq, the human being brings something out from himself so that life and reform may increase. In nifāq, the human being takes himself out of truth in order to escape the cost of truthfulness. Infāq is a sign of trust in The God; nifāq is a sign of fear of truth and reliance on the calculations of the self.

So this root places the human being between two exits: either provision exits the self and flows in the path of The God, or the human being himself exits truthfulness and becomes lost in the hidden passage of nifāq.

The danger of turning infāq into display

If infāq is separated from truthfulness, it can turn into display. A person may give wealth, but his aim may not be The God, the person in need, or reform; his aim may be being seen, gaining fame, influence, humiliating others, or buying respect.

In such a case, the outward act resembles infāq, but its spirit has been damaged. Provision has come out, but the self is still at the center. The hand has given, but the heart may have been seeking superiority.

This danger is serious, because the human being can deceive himself through a good deed. He may think that because he has given something, he has become purified; while if that giving is joined with reminding, display, and humiliation, the same act may be a sign of inward corruption.

Qur’anic infāq must be joined with truthfulness, mercy, humility, and the understanding of trust. The giver must not imagine himself to be the owner of the receiver’s life and dignity. He is only a means for provision that The God has placed in his hand.

Infāq and social responsibility

Infāq in the Qur’an is not only an individual act. Infāq gives life to society. When provision is imprisoned in the hands of a limited group and the real needs of people remain unanswered, corruption, resentment, anger, poverty, humiliation, and injustice grow.

Infāq is a way for blessing to flow through society. This flow does not only feed the hungry; it also reforms the heart of the giver, strengthens social bonds, and protects society from moral collapse.

But infāq must be wise. Its purpose is not to make people dependent, to create a permanent class of the needy, or to turn the poor into tools of publicity. True infāq must preserve human dignity and, as much as possible, move the person toward ability, independence, tranquility, and growth.

From this view, teaching, healing, creating work, supporting harmed families, helping orphans, defending the oppressed, and building healthy paths for social growth can all belong to the realm of infāq.

Infāq and tawḥīd

Infāq is directly connected with tawḥīd. Deep within himself, the miserly person may see wealth as the source of security. But the person of tawḥīd knows that true security is with The God and that true provision comes from The God.

Infāq is practical worship of tawḥīd.

When the human being gives out, if he is aware, he says through his action: this wealth belongs to The God, this ability belongs to The God, this knowledge belongs to The God, this opportunity belongs to The God, and I am only a trustee. So infāq is not merely social ethics; it is faith in action.

Through infāq, the human being moves away from the hidden shirk of absolute ownership. He realizes that wealth is not god, the employer is not god, the state is not god, the market is not god, and human power is not god. All of these are causes, not the source.

Correct infāq frees the human being from servitude to wealth. Instead of becoming the servant of possession, he places possession in the service of truth. This is the tawḥīd-oriented direction of infāq.

Suggested translation in Forqan

For the root N F Q, one fixed translation is not enough for all cases. The semantic branch and the context of each verse must be seen.

For the branch of infāq: anfaqa may be translated as “he gave out,” “spent,” or “gave from his provision”; yunfiqu and yunfiqūn as “he gives out” and “they give out”; anfiqū as “give out”; infāq as “giving out from provision” or “setting blessing into motion”; munfiq as “one who gives out”; and nafaqah as “expense,” “maintenance,” or “spending that answers need or responsibility.”

For the branch of nifāq: nafaq may be translated as “hidden passage,” “concealed route,” or “exit-burrow”; nāfaqa as “he practiced nifāq” or “he took a double and hidden path”; yunāfiqu and yunāfiqūn as “he practices nifāq” and “they practice nifāq”; munāfiq as “one whose outward appearance and inward reality are not one in faith and truthfulness”; munāfiqūn and munāfiqīn as “male hypocrites”; and munāfiqāt as “female hypocrites.”

In Forqan translation, infāq should not be limited to financial help only, and nifāq should not be reduced to simple hypocrisy. Infāq is the flowing of provision. Nifāq is the construction of a hidden exit from truthfulness.

Summary

The root N F Q places the human being before two paths. In the first path, the human being brings out what The God has delivered to him from the prison of the self and sets it into motion in the path of good, mercy, justice, and reform. This is the path of infāq.

In the second path, the human being himself exits truthfulness, but hides his exit. His outward appearance and inward reality no longer remain one. He builds for himself a hidden route between faith and benefit, between claim and action, between the community of faith and the desire of the self. This is the path of nifāq.

So this root is, on one side, the root of generosity and responsibility; and on the other side, the root of warning against concealment and doubleness. Infāq frees provision. Nifāq traps the inner self in doubleness.

Yunfiqūn in the Qur’an are not only those who give money. They are those who give, in the right path, from what The God has delivered to them. They do not worship provision, they do not imprison blessing, and they do not turn ownership into an idol.

And the munāfiqūn are not merely people who have told a simple lie. They are those who have built a hidden passage between outward appearance and inward reality. Their tongue moves in one direction, while their heart or action moves in another.

This root teaches the human being: either bring blessing out from yourself so that you may be purified, or if you bring truthfulness out of yourself, you will become lost in the hidden passage of nifāq.
