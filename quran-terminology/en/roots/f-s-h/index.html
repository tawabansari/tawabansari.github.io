The root f-s-ḥ — Spaciousness, openness, and making room for others

Qur’anic and lexical forms

(tafassahu)

(fa-fsahu)
Then make room

يَفْسَحِ
(yafsahi)

The three-consonant Arabic root f-s-ḥ concerns spaciousness, breadth, and openness. فَسِيح describes a place with sufficient room for occupancy, movement, or use. In the same family, فَسَحَ لَهُ means making room for someone and providing more space for them.

This spaciousness may be a natural feature of a place, such as an extensive plain or a large house. It may also result from human conduct, as when people move over in a gathering so that a newcomer can sit down.

In broader Arabic usage, fasḥ can also concern providing opportunity, scope for action, and relief in practical affairs.

F-s-ḥ occurs three times in the Qur’an, all in one verse: al-Mujādilah 58:11. Its Qur’anic forms are تَفَسَّحُوا, فَافْسَحُوا, and يَفْسَحِ.

All three concern making room, but their agents and direction differ. In the first two, people are commanded to make room for one another; in the third, God promises to provide room for them.

The root’s semantic core is the presence or provision of sufficient space, removing constriction that prevents occupancy, movement, or benefit.

1. The root in early Arabic

Spaciousness and breadth of place

In established Arabic usage, فَسُحَ الْمَكَانُ means that a place was or became spacious.

مَكَانٌ فَسِيحٌ describes a place with ample space.

بَلَدٌ فَسِيحٌ is an extensive territory.

مَفَازَةٌ فَسِيحَةٌ denotes a desert or broad expanse with abundant open space.

مَنْزِلٌ فَسِيحٌ likewise describes a spacious house or dwelling.

In these uses, spaciousness is a feature of the place itself.

For example, two houses may differ in size. One has small rooms and narrow corridors; the other provides broad, open areas for movement and occupancy. The second house can be described as فسيح.

Spaciousness does not itself mean beauty, cleanliness, economic value, or safety.

A place may be very spacious yet unsuitable for living. Conversely, a small place can be clean, safe, and peaceful.

The root therefore concerns breadth and openness; other qualities must be identified separately.

Making room for another person

فَسَحَ لَهُ فِي الْمَجْلِسِ means that someone made room for another person in a gathering.

This use moves from a place’s natural condition to human conduct in using shared space.

A gathering place may remain the same size while its occupants sit in ways that use the available space poorly.

When they move slightly, reduce unnecessary gaps, or provide a suitable place for a newcomer, they have performed fasḥ for that person.

The walls have not moved, nor has the building’s floor area increased.

The additional room results from changing how people use the existing space.

This matters for understanding the Qur’anic usage: fasḥ does not always mean physically enlarging a place. It can mean providing more room within the same space.

Evidence from early Arabic poetry

Mālik ibn al-Ḥārith al-Hudhalī says in a poem:

فَلُومُوا مَا بَدَا لَكُمْ فَإِنِّي
سَأُعْتِبُكُمْ إِذَا انْفَسَحَ الْمُرَاحُ

Fa-lumu ma bada lakum fa-inni / sa-u'tibukum idha nfasaha l-murahu.

Translation: “Blame me as much as you wish; I shall satisfy you when the place where my livestock return and are kept becomes spacious.”

الْمُرَاح is a place where livestock, especially camels, gather and spend the night.

اِنْفَسَحَ الْمُرَاحُ here describes the livestock enclosure becoming spacious. In the poem’s context, it may suggest an increase in the poet’s herds and resources.

Some lexical transmissions give the second hemistich as سَأُغْنِيكُمْ إِذَا انْفَسَحَ الْمُرَاحُ. Others, including early literary accounts of the poem, record سَأُعْتِبُكُمْ.

This variation does not change the root’s relevant meaning.

In both versions, انفسح المراح expresses openness and spaciousness of the livestock enclosure.

Sources sometimes identify Mālik ibn al-Ḥārith as a pre-Islamic poet and sometimes as one whose lifetime spanned the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods. His poetry survives in collections of Hudhayl’s verse and early literary accounts, but this particular verse’s date of composition is not independently established.

It therefore illustrates early Arabic usage, but should not be treated without further examination as conclusive evidence from a particular period before the Qur’an’s revelation.

Scope in nonmaterial affairs

In Arabic, فُسْحَة is not confined to physical space.

فِي الْأَمْرِ فُسْحَةٌ means that there is latitude or room in a matter.

For example, someone with sufficient time to decide has room and opportunity in that matter.

The figurative expression اِنْفَسَحَ صَدْرُهُ also concerns the breast becoming expansive, suggesting greater ease or receptiveness.

Lexical sources also report a speaker from Banū ʿUqayl telling a waterskin maker to leave sufficient space between stitches so that the seam does not tear.

Here, fasḥ concerns leaving space between two points.

These examples show the root’s range extending from a territory’s broad expanse to the distance between stitches and the latitude available for a task.

Their recording in later lexicons does not, by itself, establish that all these expressions were already current in the same forms before Islam.

What the evidence more securely establishes is the root’s enduring association with breadth, spaciousness, and providing room.

2. Linguistic forms and semantic core

The main recorded forms include:

  • فَسُحَ: was or became spacious.
  • فَسَحَ: made room or provided scope for another.
  • فَسْح: making room or creating space.
  • فُسْحَة: spaciousness, latitude, or opportunity.
  • فَسَاحَة: breadth and spaciousness.
  • فَسِيح: spacious and open.
  • فُسَاح: in some uses, a description of a spacious place.
  • فَسَّحَ: expanded or provided more space.
  • تَفَسَّحَ: made room or opened up space where one was.
  • تَفَاسَحَ: people made room for one another.
  • اِنْفَسَحَ: became open or spacious.
  • مُنْفَسِح: spacious or open.
  • مُتَفَسَّح: a place or opportunity for making room.

Not all these forms occur in the Qur’an.

Only two verbal structures appear there: Form I and Form V.

Form I occurs in فَافْسَحُوا and يَفْسَحِ.

Form V appears in تَفَسَّحُوا.

No noun or adjective such as فسحة, فسيح, or فساحة occurs in the Qur’an.

This family’s semantic range can be explained on three levels.

Existing spaciousness: a place or situation that is open and expansive.

Space provided: an action that reduces spatial constraints and creates room or scope for another person.

Relief in practical affairs: opportunity, possibility, and ease arising in a previously constrained situation.

These levels are related, but should not be collapsed into an entirely identical meaning.

The second is most prominent in the Qur’anic uses; the third also merits consideration in the divine promise.

3. Qur’anic usage: making room in gatherings

Al-Mujādilah 58:11

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا قِيلَ لَكُمْ تَفَسَّحُوا فِي الْمَجَالِسِ فَافْسَحُوا يَفْسَحِ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ ۖ وَإِذَا قِيلَ انْشُزُوا فَانْشُزُوا يَرْفَعِ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنْكُمْ وَالَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْعِلْمَ دَرَجَاتٍ ۚ وَاللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ خَبِيرٌ

Ya ayyuha lladhina amanu idha qila lakum tafassahu fi l-majalisi fa-fsahu yafsahi llahu lakum; wa-idha qila nshuzu fa-nshuzu yarfa'i llahu lladhina amanu minkum wa-lladhina utu l-'ilma darajatin; wa-llahu bi-ma ta'maluna khabir.

Translation: “You who have believed, when you are told to make room in gatherings, then make room; The God will provide ample room for you. And when you are told to rise, then rise. The God will raise those among you who have believed and those given knowledge by degrees; and The God is aware of what you do.”

This is the only verse containing f-s-ḥ in the Qur’an.

Its opening contains three successive forms from this family:

«تَفَسَّحُوا»

Tafassahu.

«فَافْسَحُوا»

Fa-fsahu.

«يَفْسَحِ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ»

Yafsahi llahu lakum.

This repetition connects human conduct with the divine promise.

First, believers are asked to make room for one another. Next comes the command to carry out that action. Divine provision of room is then presented in response.

تَفَسَّحُوا فِي الْمَجَالِسِ

تفسحوا is Form V.

In this construction, the addressees are called to open up room in the seating area.

فِي الْمَجَالِسِ specifies where this action takes place.

مجالس is the plural of مجلس, from j-l-s, and refers to a place of sitting or assembly.

The command directly concerns how people occupy shared space.

For example, several people may sit with ample gaps between them while newcomers have nowhere to sit. Appropriate movement can provide room for others.

This can happen without displacing someone from their seat.

If the gathering space is genuinely limited and no further seating is possible, however, the command to make room does not mean creating unlimited space from nothing. The action must remain within what is actually possible.

The central meaning here is providing room and reducing constriction for others.

Does تفسحوا mean making room for one another?

As a plural command, it can encompass cooperation in creating shared space.

Arabic also has تَفَاسَحُوا, in which the reciprocal aspect of making room is more explicit.

A reading تفاسحوا is also reported from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, whereas the familiar Qur’anic text has تفسحوا.

The two structures are close in general meaning.

Form V alone should not be taken as evidence that each person must move by an equal amount.

Someone seated beside a wall may have little room to move, while another can provide more room through a slight adjustment.

Cooperation and consideration are therefore not a mechanical rule determining how much each person must move.

فَافْسَحُوا: carrying out the command

After mentioning the request to make room, the Qur’an says:

«فَافْسَحُوا»

Fa-fsahu.

That is, then make room.

This is Form I and expresses the act of providing space.

Its distinction from تفسحوا within this sentence is subtle.

تفسحوا is the content of the request addressed to people.

فافسحوا directly commands them to carry it out.

The repeated root is therefore more than verbal ornament: it connects hearing a request with practical action.

A person may hear discussion of respecting others’ rights and even agree with it, yet refuse to shift their own position slightly when action is required.

The verse emphasizes actually carrying out the conduct.

This command should not, however, be taken to mean that anyone in authority may demand that people arbitrarily surrender their places.

The text concerns making room in gatherings. Turning it into permission for humiliation, discrimination, or arbitrary displacement requires independent evidence.

4. يَفْسَحِ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ: divine spaciousness in response to making room for others

After the command to make room comes:

«يَفْسَحِ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ»

Yafsahi llahu lakum.

That is, God will provide ample room for you.

God is the subject of this sentence.

لَكُمْ identifies those who receive the divine provision of room.

This usage differs significantly from the preceding two forms.

In تفسحوا and فافسحوا, people make room for others in shared space.

In يفسح الله لكم, however, God promises to provide room.

Is this room confined to the same gathering?

The verse does not separately specify the kind or location of the divine spaciousness.

It may concern a person’s life, opportunities, share in divine mercy, or place in the hereafter.

Some exegetical explanations relate the promise to a spacious place in Paradise; others envisage a broader sense of divine relief.

The verse itself does not say that God only creates additional physical room for the person in that same gathering.

Nor does it restrict the promise to a fixed amount of wealth, lifespan, land, or rank.

It is therefore preferable to preserve the direct meaning “provide ample room,” explaining possible applications only where appropriate contextual evidence exists.

Is divine spaciousness a material transaction?

The verse does not establish that everyone who makes room for another in a gathering will necessarily acquire more wealth, a larger house, or a particular success that same day.

Such detailed promises are not stated in the verse.

The underlying relationship is clear, however: God attends to human conduct in making room for others and speaks of His own provision of room in response.

This gives a simple social act significance beyond outward etiquette.

In shared space, a person can consider only their own comfort and position, or also respect others’ needs.

Making room for another means arranging some of the available resources so that the other person can benefit too.

The verse does not present such conduct as worthless or unrewarded.

Making room should nevertheless not be understood as depriving oneself or harming someone else. The central concern is mutual consideration and proper use of shared resources.

5. Making room, rising, and the standing of knowledge

The same verse continues:

«وَإِذَا قِيلَ انْشُزُوا فَانْشُزُوا»

Wa-idha qila nshuzu fa-nshuzu.

That is, when you are told to rise, rise.

انشزوا comes from n-sh-z and concerns rising or getting up from one’s place.

This command follows the instruction to make room.

In social situations, the two actions can be related.

Sometimes making room for someone is sufficient.

At other times, a person needs to rise so that others can use the space, or to undertake another task themselves.

The commands are nevertheless not identical.

فسح means providing room and space.

نشوز in this construction expresses rising from one’s position.

تفسحوا should therefore not be translated as leaving the gathering, nor انشزوا merely as shifting slightly in the same place.

Does the verse tell people of lower status to rise for those of higher status?

The verse itself states no such classification.

The command addresses those who have believed.

The sentence mentions neither a privileged social class nor any group’s exclusive entitlement to other people’s places.

A gathering may provide room for an elderly person, someone with limited mobility, or a newcomer. This may reflect consideration for their actual needs.

Turning the command into a rule that people with wealth, power, or religious rank are entitled to displace others does not follow from f-s-ḥ itself.

يَرْفَعِ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا ...

The verse’s ending speaks of raising believers and those given knowledge by degrees.

يَرْفَعِ comes from r-f-ʿ.

الْعِلْمَ comes from ʿ-l-m.

This part therefore concerns elevation and the standing of faith and knowledge, not the lexical meaning of fasḥ.

Their placement together in one verse nevertheless invites reflection.

The opening asks people to look beyond their own place in shared space and provide room for others too.

The ending speaks of the value of faith and knowledge before God.

This connection shows that social conduct is not separate from a person’s life of faith and learning.

Someone may possess extensive knowledge yet disregard others’ rights in everyday conduct.

Conversely, someone who believes in truth and takes knowledge seriously should be able to express this through responsible conduct.

This reflection should not be added to the lexical meaning of fasḥ. The root concerns spaciousness and providing room; the value of faith, knowledge, and degrees is conveyed by other parts of the verse.

6. The surah’s context: from private conversations to consideration in shared space

The verses preceding al-Mujādilah 58:11 discuss private conversations and conduct that can cause anxiety, grief, or harm in human relationships.

In al-Mujādilah 58:9, believers are prohibited from private conversation about sin, aggression, and disobedience, and directed to converse about goodness and taqwa.

Al-Mujādilah 58:10 speaks of private conversation associated with Satan that can grieve believers.

Al-Mujādilah 58:11 then turns to how people behave in gatherings.

This connection matters.

Social conduct is not limited to what is said. Seating arrangements, accommodating others, and treating newcomers can also affect people’s sense of belonging or exclusion.

For example, a group may sit together in ways that make others feel there is no room for them.

Conversely, making room can acknowledge a newcomer’s place in the gathering and opportunity to benefit from it.

The word fasḥ itself does not mean love, legal equality, or acceptance of everyone else’s views.

These may be social implications of the conduct, but the root’s lexical meaning remains providing space and scope.

Al-Mujādilah 58:12 also discusses private consultation with the Messenger and its accompanying responsibilities.

Together, these verses attend to human interaction in conversation, social presence, and the observance of responsibilities.

Details from a particular historical gathering should not be imposed on the general meaning of fasḥ without independent evidence.

7. Morphological and syntactic observations

The root’s three Qur’anic occurrences stand within one sentence but differ in structure.

تَفَسَّحُوا: a Form V imperative

تَفَسَّحُوا is a second-person masculine plural imperative in Form V.

Its singular is تَفَسَّحْ and its perfect verb is تَفَسَّحَ.

In the verse, تَفَسَّحُوا فِي الْمَجَالِسِ is what the addressees are told.

Form V here concerns making room and providing sufficient space.

The form alone does not establish that everyone must perform exactly the same movement or an equal action.

فَافْسَحُوا: the command to act

فَافْسَحُوا consists of the connective فَـ and the imperative افْسَحُوا.

افسحوا comes from the basic verb فَسَحَ يَفْسَحُ, not Form IV.

This distinction is especially important because of the initial hamzah. The hamzah in افسحوا is the connecting hamzah of a Form I imperative; it does not identify the verb as Form IV.

The initial fāʾ connects the clause to the preceding condition:

If you are told to make room, then make room.

The verse therefore does not merely describe conduct; it commands its performance.

يَفْسَحِ: a jussive imperfect in response to a command

يَفْسَحِ is a third-person masculine singular imperfect verb in Form I.

Its subject is اللَّهُ.

It follows the command فَافْسَحُوا as its consequence and is syntactically jussive.

The final kasrah in يَفْسَحِ prevents two unvowelled consonants from meeting in connected speech; it does not mark the verb as genitive.

The construction means: make room, and God will provide ample room for you.

No specific object such as “gathering place,” “house,” or “wealth” follows the verb here.

This leaves the divine promise more broadly expressed than a particular spatial application.

The absence of a specific object does not, however, permit every desired reward to be presented as the verse’s definite promise.

The three verbs at a glance

تفسحوا conveys the request to make room in the gathering.

فافسحوا commands that request’s implementation.

يفسح الله لكم promises divine spaciousness to those who act accordingly.

All three share a semantic core, while differing in agent, addressee, and result.

8. Comparison with related roots

F-s-ḥ (فَسَحَ) and w-s-ʿ (وَسِعَ)

W-s-ʿ concerns breadth, capacity, and comprehensiveness.

F-s-ḥ also belongs to the field of spaciousness and openness.

In material contexts, both can describe a spacious place.

In al-Mujādilah 58:11, however, fasḥ concerns making room and providing more space for others.

W-s-ʿ has a broader semantic range and can concern knowledge, mercy, power, and ability.

In al-Baqarah 2:286, وُسْعَهَا concerns a person’s ability and capacity.

This is not the same as making room in a gathering.

Nor should an absolute distinction be invented whereby w-s-ʿ means only existing capacity and f-s-ḥ only its increase. In some constructions, both can concern expansion and the provision of space.

F-s-ḥ (فَسَحَ) and r-ḥ-b (رَحُبَ)

R-ḥ-b likewise concerns spaciousness and breadth.

In al-Tawbah 9:118, رَحُبَتْ describes the earth’s spaciousness.

The same verse speaks of the earth becoming constricted for people facing a difficult situation.

R-ḥ-b expresses spaciousness in that usage.

F-s-ḥ in its Qur’anic usage emphasizes providing room for others.

The two thus belong to neighboring semantic fields, but the sentence’s structure and subject determine their application.

F-s-ḥ (فَسَحَ) and f-t-ḥ (فَتَحَ)

Fatḥ concerns opening something previously closed, such as a door.

Fasḥ, by comparison, more often concerns expanding space or making room.

A room’s door can be open while the interior is very cramped.

A room can also be spacious while its door is closed.

Opening access and providing sufficient space are therefore different situations.

The two roots can nevertheless approach one another in broader senses of opening and relief.

Translation should not turn every fasḥ into “opening a door,” nor every fatḥ into “making room in a gathering.”

F-s-ḥ (فَسَحَ) and f-r-j (فَرَجَ)

F-r-j concerns a gap, interval, or opening between things.

Fasḥ also concerns the availability of open space.

A gap and spaciousness are nevertheless not one concept.

There may be a narrow gap between two walls that is insufficient for anyone to pass through or sit in.

In the gathering context, fasḥ emphasizes creating sufficient, usable room.

Not every interval therefore provides suitable room for another person.

F-s-ḥ (فَسَحَ) and f-j-w (فَجْوَة)

F-j-w in al-Kahf 18:17 concerns a broad, open part of the cave.

فجوة names such a space.

Fasḥ in al-Mujādilah 58:11, however, expresses the act of providing sufficient room for people.

A cave or room may already have a spacious area; in that case, openness is a feature of the place.

By contrast, people can make room for another through appropriate movement within a limited area.

This distinguishes available space from the act of providing room.

F-s-ḥ (فَسَحَ) and n-sh-z (نَشَزَ)

These roots occur together in al-Mujādilah 58:11.

Fasḥ concerns making room.

N-sh-z in انشزوا concerns rising or getting up from one’s place.

Sometimes a slight adjustment makes room for another without requiring anyone to stand up fully.

At other times, rising becomes necessary.

The two commands in one verse may thus complement each other without being identical in meaning.

F-s-ḥ (فَسَحَ) and f-ṣ-ḥ (فَصُحَ)

F-ṣ-ḥ concerns clarity and intelligibility of speech.

F-s-ḥ concerns spaciousness and providing room.

These roots differ in writing by only one letter, but their Qur’anic meanings are distinct.

Eloquent speech does not mean a spacious gathering, just as making room does not itself make someone’s speech clear and eloquent.

Similarity in sound or spelling does not establish that roots are identical.

9. Challenges in understanding and translation

One major error is restricting fasḥ to an increase in a place’s physical dimensions.

In al-Mujādilah 58:11, people are commanded to make room in gatherings, usually through movement and appropriate use of existing space.

A second error equates fasḥ with expelling others.

Making room may involve rising, but the root itself does not mean expulsion or depriving someone of their place.

Nor does the verse authorize anyone in power to humiliate others or disregard their rights in the name of etiquette.

A third error restricts يفسح الله لكم to a particular material reward.

The verse speaks of divine spaciousness without separately specifying its type or amount.

Promises of greater wealth, a larger house, or a particular station in the hereafter should therefore not be extracted from the verb alone.

A fourth error turns making room in a gathering into an unlimited theory of surrendering all personal rights.

Consideration for others and providing room do not mean disregarding one’s own rights or accepting injustice.

In shared space, making room must take account of actual possibilities and people’s rights.

A fifth error identifies a place’s spaciousness with its moral worth.

A spacious gathering may contain misconduct. Conversely, a group in a small place may practice justice, learning, and mutual respect.

The moral value of a space therefore cannot be determined by its physical extent alone.

A sixth error places the entire verse’s meaning within f-s-ḥ itself.

Alongside making room, the verse speaks of rising, faith, knowledge, degrees, and God’s awareness of human conduct.

These themes are related, but each derives meaning from its own words and constructions.

Finally, social accommodation should not be restricted to sitting in one particular gathering.

The conduct can apply to educational sessions, public assemblies, family discussions, and other shared settings.

This is a contemporary application of the verse’s principle; the root’s direct meaning remains making room and providing space.

10. Suggested translations for Forqan

For فَسُحَ in general usage, “became spacious,” “was extensive,” and “opened out” are suitable equivalents.

For فَسَحَ لَهُ, “made room for him,” “gave him scope,” or “provided space” may be used.

فُسْحَة may mean “opportunity,” “latitude,” “sufficient room,” or “openness.”

فَسِيح can be translated as “spacious,” “open,” or “extensive.”

The three Qur’anic forms in al-Mujādilah 58:11 can be rendered as follows:

تَفَسَّحُوا فِي الْمَجَالِسِ

“Make room in gatherings.”

This is brief and natural, retaining the collective setting.

فَافْسَحُوا

“Then make room.”

Repeating the same equivalent is appropriate, since the Qur’an itself repeats the root to emphasize carrying out the request.

يَفْسَحِ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ

“The God will provide ample room for you.”

Here “ample room” should convey broader scope than a seating place, since the divine promise is not confined to one particular location.

Brief root meaning and meaning in the verse

A short explanation can distinguish the root’s general meaning from each form’s meaning in the verse as follows.

تَفَسَّحُوا

Brief root meaning: spaciousness, openness, and providing sufficient room.

Meaning in this verse: make room for one another in gatherings; a plural imperative in Form V.

فَافْسَحُوا

Brief root meaning: making room and providing space.

Meaning in this verse: actually carry out the request to make room; a plural imperative in Form I.

يَفْسَحِ

Brief root meaning: providing room and spaciousness.

Meaning in this verse: God will provide ample room for you in response to your making room for others; a jussive imperfect verb.

These explanations keep the root’s meaning distinct from the particular derivative’s role in the sentence.

11. Conclusion

F-s-ḥ concerns spaciousness, breadth, and openness in Arabic. It describes natural places and buildings as well as the act of making room for another person.

The early verse attributed to Mālik ibn al-Ḥārith al-Hudhalī uses انفسح for the spaciousness of a livestock enclosure. Lexicons also record spacious houses, plains, gatherings, and intervals between things as established uses of this family.

In the Qur’an, the root occurs only in al-Mujādilah 58:11, in three forms.

تفسحوا conveys the request to make room in gatherings.

فافسحوا commands that request’s actual implementation.

يفسح الله لكم promises divine spaciousness in response to this conduct.

These three uses connect making room for others with divine provision of room.

The verse then addresses rising and the standing of faith and knowledge, showing that believers’ conduct in shared space is not separate from their other responsibilities.

The semantic core is the presence or provision of sufficient room and openness: as a place’s feature, as the result of movement and consideration for others, or, in broader usage, as latitude and relief in practical affairs.

Fasḥ does not mean depriving another of their place, guaranteeing a particular material benefit, or naming every social virtue.

Its Qur’anic usage does, however, highlight a clear principle of conduct: someone who looks beyond themselves in shared space and provides room for others performs an action that the Qur’an associates with a promise of divine spaciousness.

Sometimes this takes only a few moments of moving over in a gathering. Even that simple action shows a person refusing to ignore others’ presence and needs for the sake of their own comfort.
