The Qur’anic Root Q Ḥ M

Bold entry, plunging into difficulty, and overcoming hard obstacles

Qur’anic forms

اقْتَحَمَ

iqtaḥama

Tackling the difficult pass
مُقْتَحِم

muqtaḥim

Plunging in

In classical Arabic, Q Ḥ M denotes entering a difficult situation, advancing against obstacles, or plunging into a demanding or dangerous undertaking.

Arabic lexicons use iqtiḥām for entering a river, a depression in the ground, or a difficult passage. The sense is securely recorded in classical Arabic, and the Qur’anic occurrences attest it in the period of revelation. Lexical registration alone, however, does not establish the date of an independent pre-Islamic witness; no securely dated witness is used here to make that claim.

Quḥamu l-ṭarīq denotes the difficulties and hard stretches of a road; quḥmah can denote a formidable, demanding undertaking that not everyone can confront.

In nonphysical usage, derivatives can describe either someone entering a dangerous undertaking without sufficient thought or someone boldly confronting difficulty.

Forms examined: qaḥama, aqḥama, qaḥḥama, taqaḥḥama, iqtaḥama, iqtiḥām, muqtaḥim, miqḥām, quḥmah, and quḥam.

Qur’anic forms: iqtaḥama and muqtaḥim, each occurring once: two occurrences in total.

1. Core meaning and physical illustration

To illustrate the root, imagine two travelers. One enters a settlement along a level road; the other must cross a difficult mountain pass or a rushing river to reach the destination.

Both enter a place, but the second person also confronts difficulty and advances against an obstacle. This aspect of movement is associated with iqtiḥām.

Iqtaḥama means to enter a demanding undertaking, plunge into something, or advance to overcome a difficult obstacle.

Aqḥama means to cause someone or something to enter such a situation; muqtaḥim describes the one making that entry.

Miqḥām describes someone bold and forward in confronting demanding matters.

Some Arabic usages associate iqtiḥām with haste and recklessness, but thoughtlessness is not an inseparable part of its meaning. Its use in Al-Balad shows that it can describe a deliberate, worthwhile, commendable action.

2. Tackling the steep pass: the difficulty of doing good

فَلَا اقْتَحَمَ الْعَقَبَةَ

Fa-lā qtaḥama l-ʿaqabah.

Yet he did not cross that difficult pass.

Al-Balad 90:11

Iqtaḥama is a form VIII perfect verb; al-ʿaqabah is its object.

ʿAqabah denotes a steep, difficult mountain passage. Iqtiḥām expresses a person’s movement into such a route and through its difficulty.

Earlier verses describe someone boasting of spending abundant wealth:

يَقُولُ أَهْلَكْتُ مَالًا لُبَدًا

Yaqūlu ahlaktu mālan lubadā.

He says, “I have spent heaps of wealth.”

Al-Balad 90:6

The passage then mentions human faculties of understanding and being shown the two paths. Despite these resources, the person has not crossed the difficult pass.

What, then, is this pass?

وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الْعَقَبَةُ

Wa-mā adrāka mā l-ʿaqabah.

And what has made you know what the pass is?

Al-Balad 90:12

فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ

Fakku raqabah.

Freeing a person from bondage.

Al-Balad 90:13

أَوْ إِطْعَامٌ فِي يَوْمٍ ذِي مَسْغَبَةٍ

Aw iṭʿāmun fī yawmin dhī masghabah.

Or feeding, on a day of severe hunger,

Al-Balad 90:14

يَتِيمًا ذَا مَقْرَبَةٍ

Yatīman dhā maqrabah.

an orphan of near kin,

Al-Balad 90:15

أَوْ مِسْكِينًا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍ

Aw miskīnan dhā matrabah.

or a destitute person in the dust.

Al-Balad 90:16

ثُمَّ كَانَ مِنَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْمَرْحَمَةِ

Thumma kāna mina lladhīna āmanū wa-tawāṣaw bi-l-ṣabri wa-tawāṣaw bi-l-marḥamah.

Then being among those who have believed and urged one another to patience and urged one another to mercy.

Al-Balad 90:17

The Qur’an itself identifies what crossing this pass involves: freeing a person from bondage, feeding those in need in a time of hunger, faith, and mutual encouragement to patience and mercy.

Feeding others in abundance differs from feeding them during widespread hunger. In hardship, people may themselves need what they possess. Setting aside personal advantage and attending to another’s need under these conditions requires greater resolve and perseverance.

A similar quality appears in Al-Ḥashr 59:9, which describes people who prefer others over themselves despite their own need.

A person may endure many difficulties to obtain wealth, power, or fame, yet be unwilling to surrender some resources when freeing someone in bondage or helping those in need is at issue.

The pass described in Al-Balad does not acquire its value from the amount of suffering endured. Its value lies in the purpose and actions involved in crossing it.

The construction fa-lā iqtaḥama

When Arabic lā negates a past-tense verb, it is often repeated in a following clause. The Qur’an provides this example:

فَلَا صَدَّقَ وَلَا صَلَّىٰ

Fa-lā ṣaddaqa wa-lā ṣallā.

So he neither affirmed the truth nor performed ṣalāt.

Al-Qiyamah 75:31

In Al-Balad 90:11, however, lā appears only once. Two established grammatical and interpretive explanations of this construction are discussed here; these are not different recitations of the verse.

The first understands a negative statement: “Yet he did not cross that difficult pass.” The actions described afterward explain what the person has failed to do.

The second understands reproach and exhortation: “Why, then, did he not cross that difficult pass?”

Both interpretations preserve the meaning of iqtiḥām. They differ in the sentence’s manner of expression and function, not in the root’s meaning.

3. Muqtaḥim: a group entering the Fire

هَٰذَا فَوْجٌ مُقْتَحِمٌ مَعَكُمْ ۖ لَا مَرْحَبًا بِهِمْ ۚ إِنَّهُمْ صَالُو النَّارِ

Hādhā fawjun muqtaḥimun maʿakum, lā marḥaban bihim, innahum ṣālū l-nār.

This is a group plunging in with you. No welcome to them! They will indeed enter and burn in the Fire.

Sad 38:59

Muqtaḥim is a form VIII active participle qualifying fawj. Fawj denotes a group of people; muqtaḥim describes how that group enters.

Here, iqtiḥām occurs in the context of entry into a harsh and terrifying situation. Unlike Al-Balad, the passage does not describe a commendable effort to overcome an obstacle, but another group plunging into the Fire.

Muqtaḥim alone does not establish that they enter willingly. It describes the manner of entry, not the entrants’ consent.

The following verse clarifies the relationship between these groups:

قَالُوا بَلْ أَنْتُمْ لَا مَرْحَبًا بِكُمْ ۖ أَنْتُمْ قَدَّمْتُمُوهُ لَنَا ۖ فَبِئْسَ الْقَرَارُ

Qālū bal antum lā marḥaban bikum, antum qaddamtumūhu lanā, fa-biʾsa l-qarār.

They said, “Rather, no welcome to you! You brought this upon us beforehand. What an evil resting place!”

Sad 38:60

The arriving group holds those already there responsible for its outcome. Worldly association and following become mutual blame and disavowal on the Day of Reckoning.

Al-Baqarah 2:166 and Al-Baqarah 2:167 also express this theme: those followed disown their followers, while the followers wish to return.

The root’s two Qur’anic occurrences show that iqtiḥām is not inherently morally positive or negative. Al-Balad urges crossing the difficult pass of doing good; Ṣād describes a group plunging into a harsh situation of punishment.

4. Comparison with related roots

Q Ḥ M (iqtiḥām) and D KH L (dukhūl)

Dukhūl denotes entering a place, group, or situation. Difficulty, danger, haste, and obstacles are not necessary components of its meaning.

Iqtiḥām is entry or advancement associated with greater intensity, difficulty, or boldness.

Someone entering a house through an open gate has entered it. If a person reaches the inside despite obstacles and difficulty, the action can be described as iqtiḥām.

Q Ḥ M (iqtiḥām) and W L J (wulūj)

Wulūj emphasizes entering the inside of something or gaining access to its interior.

The Qur’an uses W L J derivatives for what enters the earth in Sabaʾ 34:2, and for night entering day and day entering night in Al-Ḥadīd 57:6.

Iqtiḥām brings difficulty, intensity, or danger into greater focus; wulūj emphasizes passage from outside to inside.

Q Ḥ M (iqtiḥām) and ʿ B R (ʿubūr)

ʿUbūr concerns crossing something and reaching its other side. That passage may be easy or difficult.

Iqtiḥām emphasizes entering and advancing amid difficulty; not every usage necessarily signifies successful arrival on the other side.

In iqtaḥama l-ʿaqabah, the difficult pass makes “crossed the difficult pass” an appropriate contextual rendering.

Q Ḥ M (iqtiḥām) and J H D (jahd)

J H D concerns exerting capacity and making an effort, whereas iqtiḥām denotes entering and advancing amid difficulty.

Crossing an obstacle may require considerable effort. In such a case, jahd describes the exertion, while iqtiḥām describes entry into the demanding route.

5. Translation proposals for Forqan

Depending on context, iqtaḥama can be rendered “entered into difficulty,” “ventured onto a hard path,” “plunged into,” or “crossed a difficult obstacle.”

In Al-Balad 90:11, “Yet he did not cross that difficult pass” preserves both the image of ʿaqabah and the sense of iqtiḥām.

In Ṣād 38:59, muqtaḥim can be rendered “plunging in” or “entering,” since the verse concerns a group entering the Fire.

Brief root meaning: Entering and advancing amid difficulty, intensity, or danger; venturing into a demanding undertaking and overcoming its obstacles.

Conclusion

Q Ḥ M denotes entry and advancement into a demanding or intense situation. Iqtiḥām differs from ordinary entry and may involve boldness, confronting obstacles, or plunging into a terrifying situation.

The Qur’an uses the root in two contrasting contexts: crossing the difficult pass of doing good and a group entering the Fire.

Difficulty is not a virtue in itself. Freeing people from bondage, caring for the hungry and needy, faith, patience, and mercy give the pass in Al-Balad its value.
