Suckling, breastfeeding, and the relationship arising from a child’s nourishment

The Arabic root R Ḍ ʿ concerns drawing milk from a mother’s breast or a milk-producing animal’s udder. From this basic sense come usages concerning breastfeeding a child, a nursing woman, a suckling child, requesting nursing from another person, and family relationships established through breastfeeding.

The distinction between رَضَعَ and أَرْضَعَ is important. In its basic usage, رَضَعَ concerns the child or creature sucking milk; أَرْضَعَ concerns the person giving milk and nourishing another.

رَضَاعَة and رِضَاع concern suckling and breastfeeding during infancy. رَضِيع is a suckling child, while مُرْضِع and مُرْضِعَة refer to a nursing woman.

The Qur’an uses this root in descriptions of a child’s nourishment and upbringing, parental rights and responsibilities, Moses’s story, kinship and marriage, and the terror of the Day of Resurrection.

This range matters because breastfeeding has different roles in these verses. One discusses its duration and parental responsibility; another concerns kinship established through nursing; in Al-Ḥajj, the relationship between a nursing woman and child illustrates the severity of an event.

The basic meaning nevertheless remains connected with nourishment through giving and sucking milk. Financial rights, marriage prohibitions, weaning, and responsibility for care arise from the verses’ wording and context, rather than from the root alone.

The root occurs eleven times in six Qur’anic verses.

Qur’anic forms: يُرْضِعْنَ, الرَّضَاعَةَ, and تَسْتَرْضِعُوا (Al-Baqarah 2:233); أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ and الرَّضَاعَةِ (Al-Nisāʾ 4:23); مُرْضِعَةٍ and أَرْضَعَتْ (Al-Ḥajj 22:2); أَرْضِعِيهِ (Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:7); الْمَرَاضِعَ (Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:12); أَرْضَعْنَ and فَسَتُرْضِعُ (Al-Ṭalāq 65:6).

Arabic also records forms including رَضَعَ, رَضِعَ, يَرْضَعُ, رَضْع, رِضَاع, رَضَاعَة, رِضَاعَة, رَاضِع, رَضِيع, رُضَّع, رُضَعَاء, مُرْضِع, مُرْضِعَة, مَرَاضِع, مَرَاضِيع, أَرْضَعَ, إِرْضَاع, اِرْتَضَعَ, اِسْتَرْضَعَ, and اِسْتِرْضَاع.

These forms differ in structure and usage. Only forms actually attested in the Qur’an should be counted as Qur’anic derivatives.

1. Core meaning and early Arabic usage

Sucking milk from the breast or udder

A lexical explanation states the root’s core meaning as follows:

الرَّاءُ وَالضَّادُ وَالْعَيْنُ أَصْلٌ وَاحِدٌ، وَهُوَ شُرْبُ اللَّبَنِ مِنَ الضَّرْعِ أَوِ الثَّدْيِ

al-rāʾu wa-al-ḍādu wa-al-ʿaynu aṣlun wāḥidun wa-huwa shurbu al-labani mina al-ḍarʿi awi al-thadyi

“Rāʾ, ḍād, and ʿayn have one basic sense: drinking milk from an udder or a human breast.”

This definition clarifies the root’s physical meaning.

Arabic ثَدْي concerns the human breast, while ضَرْع more commonly concerns a milk-producing animal’s udder.

In its basic usage, the root therefore refers to sucking milk from its source.

رَضَعَ الطِّفْلُ means “the child suckled.”

رَضَعَ مِنْ أُمِّهِ means “he suckled from his mother.”

Early usage records the same verb for sucking milk from an animal’s udder.

Suckling must nevertheless be distinguished from every kind of milk drinking.

A person may drink milk from a vessel, but ordinary Arabic usage does not necessarily call this رَضَاعَة.

Conversely, the root’s physical definition does not establish that every Qur’anic ruling concerning nursing can be determined solely by examining direct contact between a child’s mouth and the breast.

Defining an action lexically and determining a relationship’s legal conditions are different tasks.

رَضَعَ: the child’s action

The basic verb’s usage is explained as follows:

رَضَعَ الصَّبِيُّ وَغَيْرُهُ يَرْضَعُ

raḍaʿa al-ṣabiyyu wa-ghayruhu yarḍaʿu

“The child, or another, suckled.”

Early sources record differently vocalized forms of the basic verb, including رَضَعَ يَرْضَعُ and رَضِعَ يَرْضَعُ.

These differences concern Arabic dialects and pronunciation patterns; they do not themselves create two entirely separate concepts.

In the basic verb’s principal usage, the subject is the one sucking milk.

Sometimes verbs or derivatives in the family also concern a nursing woman; sentence structure clarifies their meaning.

The presence of the three consonants R Ḍ ʿ alone therefore does not identify who gives milk and who receives it. The verb’s structure and the roles of subject and object must be considered.

أَرْضَعَ: breastfeeding another

أَرْضَعَ is a Form IV verb.

In its principal usage, أَرْضَعَتِ الْأُمُّ وَلَدَهَا means “the mother breastfed her child.”

Here the subject gives milk and the object receives it.

Its distinction from رَضَعَ can be illustrated as follows:

رَضَعَ الطِّفْلُ: the child suckled.

أَرْضَعَتِ الْمَرْأَةُ الطِّفْلَ: the woman breastfed the child.

This distinction also matters in the Qur’an: Form IV verbs express breastfeeding, whereas Form X تَسْتَرْضِعُوا expresses requesting or arranging nursing by another, rather than the subject directly performing it.

For example, Moses’s mother is told in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:7:

أَنْ أَرْضِعِيهِ

an arḍiʿīhi

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:7

“That you nurse him.”

The action here concerns the mother, and the pronoun ـه refers to Moses.

Form IV does not imply that breastfeeding must be ordered by another person or that it is always contractual or paid work.

A mother may breastfeed her own child; another woman may nurse a child; in another situation, nursing may be provided for payment.

These differences arise from circumstances and context, rather than from Form IV itself.

رَضَاعَة and رِضَاع

Arabic records رَضَاع, رِضَاع, رَضَاعَة, and رِضَاعَة for suckling and breastfeeding.

Differences in the vowels of some forms are also reported in dictionaries and Arabic readings.

The noun الرَّضَاعَة occurs twice in the Qur’an: once concerning completion of the nursing period, and once concerning kinship arising through nursing.

These usages are clearly related, since both rest on nourishment through milk.

The first concerns the period and process of nursing; the second concerns a resulting family relationship.

رَضَاعَة alone therefore does not mean kinship.

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:23, kinship is understood through its association with أُمَّهَاتُكُم and أَخَوَاتُكُم, “your mothers” and “your sisters.”

2. مُرْضِع, مُرْضِعَة, and مَرَاضِع: morphological distinctions

The distinction between مُرْضِع and مُرْضِعَة is an important feature of this family.

مُرْضِع: a woman with a suckling child

Early Arabic uses امْرَأَةٌ مُرْضِعٌ for a woman who has a suckling child or is in a nursing condition.

The adjective can describe her state or characteristic without requiring that she be breastfeeding at that very moment.

This construction can be compared with امْرَأَةٌ حَامِلٌ and امْرَأَةٌ حَائِضٌ, a pregnant or menstruating woman.

In such usages, the adjective can lack a feminine ending because it denotes a state conventionally associated with women.

This practice is nevertheless not without exceptions across every period and usage of Arabic.

مُرْضِعَة: emphasizing the act of breastfeeding

A lexical explanation distinguishes the adjectives as follows:

وَيُقَالُ امْرَأَةٌ مُرْضِعٌ، إِذَا كَانَ لَهَا وَلَدٌ تُرْضِعُهُ. فَإِنْ وَصَفْتَهَا بِإِرْضَاعِهَا الْوَلَدَ قُلْتَ مُرْضِعَةٌ

wa-yuqālu imraʾatun murḍiʿun idhā kāna lahā waladun turḍiʿuhu fa-in waṣaftahā bi-irḍāʿihā al-walada qulta murḍiʿatun

“A woman who has a child to nurse is called murḍiʿ; when describing her act of nursing the child, one says murḍiʿah.”

This distinction is particularly relevant to Al-Ḥajj 22:2.

That verse says:

تَذْهَلُ كُلُّ مُرْضِعَةٍ عَمَّا أَرْضَعَتْ

tadhhalu kullu murḍiʿatin ʿammā arḍaʿat

Al-Ḥajj 22:2

“Every nursing woman becomes distracted from what she nurses.”

مُرْضِعَة here can emphasize the ongoing act of breastfeeding.

Differences among lexical explanations must also be considered.

The precise distinction between مُرْضِع and مُرْضِعَة has received similar, but not entirely identical, lexical explanations.

One therefore cannot claim that مُرْضِع never describes a woman nursing at that moment, or that مُرْضِعَة can never describe a nursing woman generally.

The familiar distinction is a tendency in usage: مُرْضِع can describe a woman’s state of having a suckling child, while مُرْضِعَة can emphasize her action of breastfeeding.

مَرَاضِع: nursing women

In the familiar reading of this verse, مَرَاضِع is the broken plural of مُرْضِع and means nursing women. Another analysis treats it as the plural of مَرْضَع, a place of suckling, namely the breast. This article uses the nursing-women reading as the basis for translation.

This plural can refer to women who breastfeed children.

In Moses’s story, “nursing women” rests on that adjectival analysis.

The plural form alone does not establish that all these women are the children’s biological mothers.

A woman can nurse her own child or another child as a wet nurse.

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:12 concerns the infant Moses and other nursing women considered for feeding him before his return to his mother.

“Nursing women” or “wet nurses” is therefore a clear rendering in context; the alternative morphological analysis should also be retained in a detailed study.

رَضِيع: a suckling child

Arabic رَضِيع describes someone who suckles.

رُضَّع and رُضَعَاء are familiar plurals.

In kinship usage, رَضِيعِي can also denote someone related to the speaker through a recognized nursing relationship.

These forms do not occur as such in the Qur’an.

The important distinction is that رَضِيع principally describes the child receiving milk, while مُرْضِعَة describes the woman providing it.

Translations must therefore not reverse the nursing woman and the suckling child without regard to sentence structure.

3. Pre-Islamic poetry and early Arabic witnesses

فَمِثْلِكِ حُبْلَى قَدْ طَرَقْتُ وَمُرْضِعٍ

The Muʿallaqah attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Imruʾ al-Qays contains this verse:

فَمِثْلِكِ حُبْلَى قَدْ طَرَقْتُ وَمُرْضِعٍ
فَأَلْهَيْتُهَا عَنْ ذِي تَمَائِمَ مُحْوِلِ

fa-mithliki ḥublā qad ṭaraqtu wa-murḍiʿin / fa-alhaytuhā ʿan dhī tamāʾima muḥwili

“Many a pregnant or nursing woman like you have I visited by night, distracting her from her year-old child wearing amulets.”

The poet describes a pregnant woman and a woman with a suckling child within his romantic narrative.

حُبْلَى refers to a pregnant woman.

مُرْضِع concerns a nursing woman or a woman with a suckling child.

ذِي تَمَائِمَ refers to a child wearing protective amulets.

In explanations of this reading, مُحْوِل is a child who has completed a year. The variant مُغْيِل is also transmitted and has a different explanation; it should not be conflated with this form.

The principal lexical point in the verse is مُرْضِع.

The poet describes a woman with a suckling child while using an adjective without the feminine tāʾ.

The lexical commentary explains مُرْضِع as ذات رضيع, a woman with a suckling child.

This witness helps clarify the distinction between مُرْضِع and مُرْضِعَة.

The verse’s romantic subject should not enter the root’s definition.

Lexically, مُرْضِع denotes a nursing woman rather than the behavior or moral judgment expressed in the poet’s story.

مَرَاضِيع in the poetry of Abū Dhuʾayb al-Hudhalī

A poem by Abū Dhuʾayb al-Hudhalī reads:

تَظَلُّ عَلَى الثَّمْرَاءِ مِنْهَا جَوَارِسُ
مَرَاضِيعُ صُهْبُ الرِّيشِ زُغْبُ رِقَابِهَا

taẓallu ʿalā al-thamrāʾi minhā jawārisu / marāḍīʿu ṣuhbu al-rīshi zughbu riqābihā

“Among those bees, sucking foragers remain upon al-Thamrāʾ, with tawny wings and down-covered necks.”

Here مَرَاضِيع describes sucking bees. Explanations identify الثَّمْرَاء both as a mountain name and as a fruit-bearing tree; the descriptions of wings and necks also belong to the poem’s imagery.

This usage transfers the image of suckling to drawing sap or nourishment from plants.

It does not extend the root’s primary meaning to every creature feeding upon every substance.

The poet employs a similarity in the manner of feeding.

Abū Dhuʾayb lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods; this verse’s precise composition date is not clear enough to assign it confidently to the pre-Islamic period.

The witness attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays places مُرْضِع within the pre-Islamic poetic tradition, although this verse’s precise date has not been independently determined here. Abū Dhuʾayb’s witness also illustrates the family’s literary range.

لَئِيمٌ رَاضِعٌ: a derogatory usage

Lexical records also contain the expression لَئِيمٌ رَاضِعٌ.

It described a miser or mean person who, in one explanation, sucked milk directly from an animal’s udder so that a guest would not hear milk falling into a vessel and ask to share it.

Another explanation describes someone as though he had absorbed meanness from the beginning of life.

This was a derogatory expression within Arabic language and social culture.

It does not make suckling or infancy objectionable in itself.

Nor should ordinary رَضِيع referring to a child be translated as “a miser.”

The derogatory meaning is a specific usage dependent on its cultural setting.

None of the root’s eleven Qur’anic occurrences has this derogatory meaning.

4. وَالْوَالِدَاتُ يُرْضِعْنَ أَوْلَادَهُنَّ: breastfeeding in Al-Baqarah

One of the root’s most comprehensive usages appears in Al-Baqarah 2:233:

وَالْوَالِدَاتُ يُرْضِعْنَ أَوْلَادَهُنَّ حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ

wa-al-wālidātu yurḍiʿna awlādahunna ḥawlayni kāmilayni li-man arāda an yutimma al-raḍāʿata

Al-Baqarah 2:233

“And mothers shall breastfeed their children for two complete years, for whoever wishes to complete the nursing.”

The passage has three principal elements: mothers, the act of breastfeeding, and the complete nursing period.

The morphology of يُرْضِعْنَ

يُرْضِعْنَ is a third-person feminine plural imperfect verb.

The Qur’anic vocalization has ḍammah on yāʾ and kasrah on ḍād, identifying it as Form IV أَرْضَعَ يُرْضِعُ.

The final نَ is the feminine plural subject pronoun.

أَوْلَادَهُنَّ is its object.

The mothers therefore perform the breastfeeding, and their children receive the milk.

The sentence is declarative in form, but within family provisions it also guides and regulates nursing responsibility.

Lexical and exegetical explanations differ over whether this is a declarative construction carrying a command, or a statement about ordinary nursing practice regulated by the verse’s other provisions.

The verb’s form alone should not settle this disagreement.

The responsibility’s scope requires consideration of the whole verse: لِمَنْ أَرَادَ, the possibility of weaning by mutual agreement and consultation, the prohibition of harm, and permission to arrange nursing by another woman.

حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ

حَوْلَيْنِ means two years, and كَامِلَيْنِ specifies their completeness.

This phrase identifies the complete nursing period in the verse’s context.

رَضَاعَة itself does not mean two years.

The duration comes from حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ, while the action comes from R Ḍ ʿ.

This matters because Arabic can discuss nursing elsewhere without specifying a two-year duration.

Two years should therefore not be included in the lexical definition of رَضَاعَة.

لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ

This phrase connects two complete years with completing the nursing period.

أَرَادَ expresses willing or wishing.

يُتِمَّ concerns completing.

الرَّضَاعَةَ is what is completed.

The verse itself thus distinguishes nursing from completion of its period.

This does not imply that every instance of nursing before two years is lexically deficient or without value.

The verse identifies the complete period, then also describes weaning by mutual agreement and consultation.

Completion of the period and the possibility of ending it under other circumstances thus appear within the same verse.

فَإِنْ أَرَادَا فِصَالًا

Al-Baqarah 2:233 continues:

فَإِنْ أَرَادَا فِصَالًا عَنْ تَرَاضٍ مِنْهُمَا وَتَشَاوُرٍ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا

fa-in arādā fiṣālan ʿan tarāḍin minhumā wa-tashāwurin fa-lā junāḥa ʿalayhimā

Al-Baqarah 2:233

“If the two wish to wean by mutual agreement and consultation, there is no blame upon them.”

فِصَال belongs to F Ṣ L and here concerns separating the child from the nursing period: weaning.

It does not belong to R Ḍ ʿ.

Weaning should therefore not be included in the meaning of رَضَاعَة itself.

Nursing and weaning are related but distinct stages.

تَرَاضٍ belongs to R Ḍ Y and means mutual agreement.

The shared first two consonants of R Ḍ ʿ and R Ḍ Y should not cause these families to be conflated.

In this very verse, breastfeeding and consent occur together while retaining separate lexical meanings.

Preventing harm and respecting capacity

Al-Baqarah 2:233 says:

لَا تُكَلَّفُ نَفْسٌ إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا لَا تُضَارَّ وَالِدَةٌ بِوَلَدِهَا وَلَا مَوْلُودٌ لَهُ بِوَلَدِهِ

lā tukallafu nafsun illā wusʿahā lā tuḍārra wālidatun bi-waladihā wa-lā mawlūdun lahu bi-waladihi

Al-Baqarah 2:233

“No person is burdened beyond their capacity. No mother should suffer harm because of her child, nor any father because of his child.”

This passage indicates that responsibilities concerning the child should not become a means of harming either parent.

ضَرَر and وُسْع nevertheless belong to other roots.

R Ḍ ʿ should therefore not be translated directly as preventing harm or respecting capacity.

These principles form the Qur’anic framework for relationships involving nursing.

أَنْ تَسْتَرْضِعُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ

The closing discussion of nursing in Al-Baqarah 2:233 says:

وَإِنْ أَرَدْتُمْ أَنْ تَسْتَرْضِعُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا سَلَّمْتُمْ مَا آتَيْتُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

wa-in aradtum an tastarḍiʿū awlādakum fa-lā junāḥa ʿalaykum idhā sallamtum mā ātaytum bi-al-maʿrūfi

Al-Baqarah 2:233

“And if you wish to arrange wet nursing for your children, there is no blame upon you, provided you pay what you give in an appropriate manner.”

تَسْتَرْضِعُوا is Form X.

Here it concerns requesting or arranging nursing for a child.

This construction differs from يُرْضِعْنَ.

يُرْضِعْنَ expresses the mothers’ own act of breastfeeding.

تَسْتَرْضِعُوا concerns requesting or arranging nursing by another person.

The context permits entrusting the nursing to another woman.

Istirḍāʿ itself, however, does not mean formal employment or a specified contract with every legal detail.

Financial conditions and appropriate conduct come from other phrases in the verse.

Lexically, istirḍāʿ should therefore be understood as requesting or arranging nursing for a child; payment arrangements and the parties’ responsibilities require separate examination.

5. أُمَّهَاتُكُمُ اللَّاتِي أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ: nursing kinship in Al-Nisāʾ

Al-Nisāʾ 4:23 says:

وَأُمَّهَاتُكُمُ اللَّاتِي أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ وَأَخَوَاتُكُمْ مِنَ الرَّضَاعَةِ

wa-ummahātukumu allātī arḍaʿnakum wa-akhawātukum mina al-raḍāʿati

Al-Nisāʾ 4:23

“And your mothers who breastfed you, and your sisters through nursing.”

The phrase occurs in a list of people whom one is prohibited from marrying.

أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ: an action establishing a relationship

أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ is a third-person feminine plural Form IV past verb.

كُمْ is its object.

It refers to women who breastfed those addressed.

The verse does more than state that these women provided nourishment: it identifies them through أُمَّهَاتُكُمُ اللَّاتِي أَرْضَعْنَكُمْ, “your mothers who breastfed you.”

A maternal relationship through nursing is therefore at issue here.

This differs from motherhood through childbirth, but the Qur’an gives it consequences concerning marriage.

أَخَوَاتُكُمْ مِنَ الرَّضَاعَةِ

مِنَ الرَّضَاعَةِ identifies the source of the sisterly relationship.

أَخَوَاتُكُمْ here therefore concerns sisters related through nursing rather than merely sisters by birth.

This shows that breastfeeding could establish a family bond within the linguistic and legal culture addressed by the Qur’an.

رَضَاعَة itself does not mean sister.

The sisterly relationship comes from the complete phrase أَخَوَاتُكُمْ مِنَ الرَّضَاعَةِ.

What the verse explicitly establishes

Al-Nisāʾ 4:23 explicitly names two nursing-related categories:

Mothers who breastfed, and sisters whose relationship arises through nursing.

The phrase itself does not specify the number of feedings, the quantity of milk, or every detail required to establish the relationship.

Nor does it provide a separate list of everyone who might be connected through nursing kinship.

Juristic traditions contain extensive discussions of feeding frequency, the child’s age, and the extension of kinship relationships through nursing.

Those discussions should not automatically be presented as the root’s own meaning or this verse’s explicit wording.

Assessing any ruling requires distinguishing the Qur’anic wording, conclusions argued from its verses together, and provisions discussed in other sources.

The two-year nursing period in Al-Baqarah 2:233 should likewise not be presented, solely through topical proximity and without independent argument, as specifying every condition of nursing kinship in Al-Nisāʾ 4:23.

One passage describes the complete nursing period; the other addresses the relationship’s consequences for marriage.

They are related, but do not constitute a single ruling containing every detail.

6. مُرْضِعَة in Al-Ḥajj: the terror of the Day of Resurrection

Al-Ḥajj 22:2 says:

يَوْمَ تَرَوْنَهَا تَذْهَلُ كُلُّ مُرْضِعَةٍ عَمَّا أَرْضَعَتْ وَتَضَعُ كُلُّ ذَاتِ حَمْلٍ حَمْلَهَا وَتَرَى النَّاسَ سُكَارَىٰ وَمَا هُمْ بِسُكَارَىٰ وَلَٰكِنَّ عَذَابَ اللَّهِ شَدِيدٌ

yawma tarawnahā tadhhalu kullu murḍiʿatin ʿammā arḍaʿat wa-taḍaʿu kullu dhāti ḥamlin ḥamlahā wa-tarā al-nāsa sukārā wa-mā hum bi-sukārā wa-lākinna ʿadhāba allāhi shadīdun

Al-Ḥajj 22:2

“On the day you see it, every nursing woman will be distracted from what she nurses, and every pregnant one will lay down her burden. You will see people as intoxicated, although they are not intoxicated; rather, The God’s punishment is severe.”

R Ḍ ʿ occurs twice here, in مُرْضِعَةٍ and أَرْضَعَتْ.

كُلُّ مُرْضِعَةٍ: a woman engaged in nursing

As discussed in the lexical section, مُرْضِعَة can emphasize a woman engaged in breastfeeding.

That sense is particularly relevant here because the nursing woman becomes distracted from the very being she is feeding.

تَذْهَلُ belongs to DH H L and concerns becoming distracted or forgetting under the impact of an overwhelming event.

The verse thus depicts fear severe enough to affect even the close, immediate relationship between a nursing woman and her suckling child.

مُرْضِعَة itself does not mean being afraid or distracted.

Nursing comes from R Ḍ ʿ; distraction comes from تَذْهَلُ.

عَمَّا أَرْضَعَتْ: what she has nursed

مَا here refers to the being whom the woman has nursed.

Qur’anic use of مَا rather than مَنْ does not necessarily deny the child’s humanity.

The sentence focuses on the being who receives the nursing action.

A natural rendering in Persian would mean:

“She becomes distracted from her suckling child.”

A lexical study must nevertheless clarify that the Arabic does not directly say وَلَدَهَا, “her child,” but مَا أَرْضَعَتْ, “what she has nursed.”

If “child” is used in translation, that identification comes from context.

An image of terror, not a judgment about mothers

The verse depicts the terror of the Day of Resurrection through a profoundly unsettling situation.

A mother or nursing woman has a close, caring relationship with the child; distraction from the child in this situation therefore illustrates the event’s severity.

This image does not imply that nursing women ordinarily neglect their children or that the Qur’an accuses them of moral weakness.

The verse’s subject is the severity of an event.

Nor should specific medical details about breastfeeding, milk production, or the body’s response to fear be derived from this phrase.

The verse describes overwhelming fear affecting a person’s attention to the closest concerns of life.

Study of DH H L

7. أَرْضِعِيهِ and الْمَرَاضِع: nursing in Moses’s story

Two further occurrences appear in Moses’s story.

أَنْ أَرْضِعِيهِ: the instruction to Moses’s mother

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:7 says:

وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَىٰ أُمِّ مُوسَىٰ أَنْ أَرْضِعِيهِ فَإِذَا خِفْتِ عَلَيْهِ فَأَلْقِيهِ فِي الْيَمِّ وَلَا تَخَافِي وَلَا تَحْزَنِي

wa-awḥaynā ilā ummi mūsā an arḍiʿīhi fa-idhā khifti ʿalayhi fa-alqīhi fī al-yammi wa-lā takhāfī wa-lā taḥzanī

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:7

“And We inspired Moses’s mother: nurse him; then, when you fear for him, cast him into the water, and do not fear or grieve.”

أَرْضِعِيهِ is a second-person feminine singular Form IV imperative.

Moses’s mother is being addressed.

The pronoun ـه refers to Moses.

This usage describes the mother’s actual breastfeeding of the child.

It does not make protection from danger or placing a child in water the meaning of R Ḍ ʿ.

Those subjects arise from the verse’s other instructions and Moses’s story.

وَحَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِ الْمَرَاضِعَ

The story continues in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:12:

وَحَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِ الْمَرَاضِعَ مِنْ قَبْلُ فَقَالَتْ هَلْ أَدُلُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ أَهْلِ بَيْتٍ يَكْفُلُونَهُ لَكُمْ وَهُمْ لَهُ نَاصِحُونَ

wa-ḥarramnā ʿalayhi al-marāḍiʿa min qablu fa-qālat hal adullukum ʿalā ahli baytin yakfulūnahu lakum wa-hum lahu nāṣiḥūna

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:12

“And We had previously barred nursing women from him. Then she said: shall I direct you to a household that will take responsibility for him for you and care sincerely for him?”

In this article’s chosen rendering, الْمَرَاضِع denotes nursing women; its alternative analysis as places of suckling was also noted in the morphological section.

The contextual meaning of حَرَّمْنَا needs particular attention.

حَرَّمْنَا here does not establish a general prohibition

In Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:11, Moses’s mother tells his sister to follow him, and the sister observes him from a distance. Pharaoh’s household searching for a wet nurse is a narrative explanation of the setting, not something explicitly stated in the preceding verse.

The text then says that The God had barred الْمَرَاضِع from him.

In the story, Moses would not accept other women’s nursing, or his nourishment from them was prevented.

His sister then proposes a household, preparing the way for his return to his mother.

حَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِ الْمَرَاضِعَ thus describes an event in Moses’s story, rather than a general prohibition on other women nursing children.

This distinction matters.

Al-Baqarah 2:233 permits arranging nursing by another woman.

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:12 therefore does not establish that engaging a wet nurse is inherently forbidden.

Nor should حَرَّمْنَا here be made into general legislation for every child without examining H R M and the story’s context.

Study of Ḥ R M

فَرَدَدْنَاهُ إِلَىٰ أُمِّهِ

The following verse, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:13, says:

فَرَدَدْنَاهُ إِلَىٰ أُمِّهِ كَيْ تَقَرَّ عَيْنُهَا وَلَا تَحْزَنَ

fa-radadnāhu ilā ummihi kay taqarra ʿaynuhā wa-lā taḥzana

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:13

“So We returned him to his mother, that her eyes might find comfort and she might not grieve.”

This continuation places the rejection of other nursing women within the sequence leading to Moses’s return to his mother.

رَدَدْنَاهُ belongs to R D D and differs from R Ḍ ʿ.

The child’s return to his mother is therefore an outcome of the narrative’s events, rather than the lexical meaning of breastfeeding.

8. فَإِنْ أَرْضَعْنَ لَكُمْ: nursing and financial rights in Al-Ṭalāq

Al-Ṭalāq 65:6 says:

فَإِنْ أَرْضَعْنَ لَكُمْ فَآتُوهُنَّ أُجُورَهُنَّ وَأْتَمِرُوا بَيْنَكُمْ بِمَعْرُوفٍ وَإِنْ تَعَاسَرْتُمْ فَسَتُرْضِعُ لَهُ أُخْرَىٰ

fa-in arḍaʿna lakum fa-ātūhunna ujūrahunna wa-ʾtamirū baynakum bi-maʿrūfin wa-in taʿāsartum fa-sa-turḍiʿu lahu ukhrā

Al-Ṭalāq 65:6

“If they nurse for you, give them their wages, and consult together in an appropriate manner. If you encounter difficulty with one another, another woman will nurse for him.”

The root occurs twice in this verse.

أَرْضَعْنَ: women nursing

أَرْضَعْنَ is a third-person feminine plural Form IV past verb.

The women are discussed within the context of divorce and responsibilities concerning a child.

The verse says that if they provide nursing, their remuneration must be paid.

Breastfeeding is thus connected with the nursing woman’s financial right.

أَرْضَعْنَ itself does not mean receiving remuneration.

Remuneration comes from فَآتُوهُنَّ أُجُورَهُنَّ, “give them their wages.”

The act of nursing must therefore be distinguished from its associated agreement or financial responsibility.

وَأْتَمِرُوا بَيْنَكُمْ بِمَعْرُوفٍ

The passage continues with consultation and appropriate conduct.

This indicates that nursing arrangements after parental separation should not become a matter of coercion or one party imposing its wishes.

The opening of Al-Ṭalāq 65:6 also says:

وَلَا تُضَارُّوهُنَّ لِتُضَيِّقُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ

wa-lā tuḍārrūhunna li-tuḍayyiqū ʿalayhinna

Al-Ṭalāq 65:6

“And do not harm them in order to constrain them.”

Nursing is therefore discussed in a context emphasizing rights, prevention of harm, and consultation.

These principles accord with Al-Baqarah 2:233.

R Ḍ ʿ itself should nevertheless not be translated as consultation or prevention of harm.

These are provisions expressed alongside the subject of nursing.

فَسَتُرْضِعُ لَهُ أُخْرَىٰ

This phrase is morphologically important.

فَسَتُرْضِعُ contains a Form IV imperfect verb with the future marker سَـ.

Its subject is أُخْرَىٰ, “another woman.”

The meaning is therefore that another woman will nurse.

This differs from تَسْتَرْضِعُوا in Al-Baqarah 2:233.

تَسْتَرْضِعُوا is Form X, expressing the request or arrangement for another person to provide nursing.

سَتُرْضِعُ here is Form IV and expresses the other woman’s own act of nursing.

This is a clear example of why morphological precision matters.

In context, the verse offers a way to continue nourishing the child if agreement becomes difficult.

The phrase alone does not establish every detail of decisions about guardianship, the child’s residence, or any family contract.

Those subjects require an independent examination of the verses concerning family and children’s rights.

9. Nursing, weaning, and the period of a child’s upbringing

Several related concepts accompany nursing in the Qur’an.

One of the most important is فِصَال.

وَفِصَالُهُ فِي عَامَيْنِ

Luqmān 31:14 says:

وَوَصَّيْنَا الْإِنْسَانَ بِوَالِدَيْهِ حَمَلَتْهُ أُمُّهُ وَهْنًا عَلَىٰ وَهْنٍ وَفِصَالُهُ فِي عَامَيْنِ

wa-waṣṣaynā al-insāna bi-wālidayhi ḥamalat-hu ummuhu wahnan ʿalā wahnin wa-fiṣāluhu fī ʿāmayni

Luqmān 31:14

“And We enjoined the human being concerning his parents: his mother carried him in weakness upon weakness, and his weaning is in two years.”

This verse uses فِصَال rather than directly employing R Ḍ ʿ.

Its subject nevertheless relates to nursing.

Here weaning is connected with the period of the child’s upbringing.

Al-Baqarah 2:233 identifies two years as the complete nursing period.

The verses are closely related in subject.

Breastfeeding must nevertheless be distinguished from cessation of nursing.

Weaning ends or changes a stage of feeding; nursing is the preceding action.

وَحَمْلُهُ وَفِصَالُهُ ثَلَاثُونَ شَهْرًا

Al-Aḥqāf 46:15 says:

وَحَمْلُهُ وَفِصَالُهُ ثَلَاثُونَ شَهْرًا

wa-ḥamluhu wa-fiṣāluhu thalāthūna shahran

Al-Aḥqāf 46:15

“And carrying him and weaning him are thirty months.”

This phrase expresses gestation and weaning within a combined period.

حَمْل and فِصَال belong to other roots and should not be counted as occurrences of R Ḍ ʿ.

The verse helps establish the context of a child’s upbringing but does not replace separate examination of each word.

Nor should combining the verses produce a completely uniform biological schedule for every human being without regard to differences in pregnancy and children’s nourishment.

The Qur’an describes periods and responsibilities; individual circumstances and actual possibilities also require consideration in their own place.

10. Distinguishing R Ḍ ʿ from related roots

R Ḍ ʿ (raḍāʿah) and R Ḍ Y (riḍā)

These roots look similar in writing, but their third consonants differ.

R Ḍ ʿ concerns suckling and breastfeeding.

R Ḍ Y concerns consent and approval.

Both families appear in Al-Baqarah 2:233:

الرَّضَاعَةَ

al-raḍāʿata

Al-Baqarah 2:233

“Nursing.”

And:

عَنْ تَرَاضٍ مِنْهُمَا

ʿan tarāḍin minhumā

Al-Baqarah 2:233

“By the mutual agreement of the two.”

Their occurrence together shows a topical relationship, but they do not share one root.

Similarity in sound therefore does not imply that breastfeeding lexically means consent, or that consent developed from the meaning of suckling.

Each root requires independent study.

R Ḍ ʿ (raḍāʿah) and F Ṣ L (fiṣāl)

In verses concerning a child, فِصَال refers to weaning.

رَضَاعَة concerns the period and action of suckling and breastfeeding.

They are connected within one life process but are not identical.

Nursing is the provision of nourishment through milk.

Weaning separates the child from this manner of feeding or ends its period.

Al-Baqarah 2:233 clearly illustrates the distinction: it first discusses nursing and its completion, then the possibility of فِصَال through mutual agreement and consultation.

Study of F Ṣ L

R Ḍ ʿ (raḍāʿah) and GH DH W (ghidhāʾ)

غِذَاء concerns nourishment and the provision of food for a living being.

رَضَاعَة is one specific manner of feeding.

Every instance of breastfeeding is nourishment, but not every form of nourishment is breastfeeding.

A child may feed on milk during one stage of life and eat different foods during another.

R Ḍ ʿ should therefore not be defined as upbringing in general or feeding on any substance whatsoever.

The poetic metaphor of مَرَاضِيع for bees should not erase this lexical distinction.

R Ḍ ʿ (raḍāʿah) and W L D (wilādah)

وِلَادَة concerns giving birth and a child being born from its mother.

رَضَاعَة concerns breastfeeding and suckling.

In Al-Baqarah 2:233, وَالْوَالِدَاتُ occurs with يُرْضِعْنَ.

They nevertheless convey different concepts.

The same woman can give birth to a child and nurse it, but this does not happen in every situation.

The Qur’an also discusses nursing by another woman.

The biological mother-child relationship should therefore not be equated with the relationship established through nursing.

This distinction matters in Al-Nisāʾ 4:23, where nursing mothers appear alongside relatives by birth in the list of marriage prohibitions.

Study of W L D

R Ḍ ʿ (raḍāʿah) and K F L (kafālah)

Moses’s story says in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:12:

هَلْ أَدُلُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ أَهْلِ بَيْتٍ يَكْفُلُونَهُ لَكُمْ

hal adullukum ʿalā ahli baytin yakfulūnahu lakum

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:12

“Shall I direct you to a household that will take responsibility for his care for you?”

كَفَالَة concerns undertaking responsibility and providing care.

رَضَاعَة concerns breastfeeding and suckling.

A person or household may undertake both responsibilities, but the words are not identical.

الْمَرَاضِع in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:12 should therefore not be translated merely as “guardians.”

Nursing women are at issue in this reading; responsibility for care is expressed separately in the following clause.

11. Translation proposals for Forqan

Translation must distinguish the recipient of milk, its provider, and the relationship arising through nursing.

رَضَعَ / رَضِعَ: suckled; drew milk from the breast.

رَضْع: suckling; the act of drawing milk.

رِضَاع / رَضَاعَة: nursing or the nursing period; suckling and breastfeeding, according to context.

رَاضِع: a suckling; one who sucks milk. In certain early derogatory usages, a description of a miser.

رَضِيع: a suckling child; one who receives milk.

رُضَّع / رُضَعَاء: suckling children.

أَرْضَعَ: breastfed another.

إِرْضَاع: breastfeeding; providing nursing.

مُرْضِع: a woman with a suckling child or in a nursing condition.

مُرْضِعَة: a nursing woman, especially where her act of breastfeeding is emphasized.

مَرَاضِع: nursing women; wet nurses, according to context.

مَرَاضِيع: a plural associated with nursing women in lexical usage; in the poetic witness examined here, a metaphorical description of sucking bees.

اِرْتَضَعَ: suckled; drew milk.

اِسْتَرْضَعَ: requested that someone nurse a child; sought nursing from another.

اِسْتِرْضَاع: requesting or arranging nursing by another.

In Al-Baqarah 2:233, the Persian Forqan rendering about engaging a wet nurse fits the context: the verse permits entrusting nursing to another and specifies appropriate payment.

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:23, الرَّضَاعَة in أَخَوَاتُكُمْ مِنَ الرَّضَاعَةِ identifies the source of the sisterly relationship, rather than itself meaning sister.

In Al-Ḥajj 22:2, a rendering can preserve the nursing woman and what she nurses, with the identification of the child explained contextually.

12. Conclusion

The Arabic root R Ḍ ʿ concerns sucking milk from a human breast or a milk-producing animal’s udder.

From this core come usages concerning breastfeeding, the nursing period, a suckling child, a nursing woman, requesting nursing, and family relationships arising through it.

Early Arabic distinguishes رَضَعَ, suckling, from أَرْضَعَ, breastfeeding.

مُرْضِع can describe a woman with a suckling child, while مُرْضِعَة in some usages emphasizes her act of breastfeeding.

The witness attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays illustrates مُرْضِع for a woman with a suckling child within the pre-Islamic poetic tradition.

Abū Dhuʾayb’s literary usage metaphorically applies مَرَاضِيع to creatures drawing nourishment by sucking.

Derogatory usages of رَاضِع are also recorded in certain early expressions, but these secondary meanings should not be imposed upon ordinary nursing or Qur’anic usage.

The root occurs eleven times in six Qur’anic verses.

Al-Baqarah 2:233 discusses nursing in relation to a complete two-year period, provision of necessities, parental capacity, prevention of harm, weaning through agreement and consultation, and permission to arrange nursing by another woman.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:23 recognizes two nursing-related kinship categories concerning marriage: mothers who breastfed and sisters through nursing.

The verse itself does not give every juristic detail concerning feeding frequency, milk quantity, or the extension of all kinship relationships.

Al-Ḥajj 22:2 places a nursing woman and her child within an image of the Day of Resurrection’s overwhelming terror.

In Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:7, Moses’s mother is instructed to nurse him; in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:12, his rejection of other nursing women prepares the way for his return to his mother.

Al-Ṭalāq 65:6 associates nursing with remuneration, consultation, and the possibility of another nursing woman when agreement becomes difficult.

These usages show that breastfeeding can be a natural physical action while also carrying financial rights, family responsibilities, and legal consequences in different situations.

All these consequences should not be included in the root’s independent meaning.

Breastfeeding and weaning, nursing and consent, and motherhood through childbirth and through nursing must also be distinguished.

Final definition: R Ḍ ʿ primarily concerns sucking milk from the breast and its associated process. رَضَعَ principally expresses the recipient’s suckling; أَرْضَعَ concerns breastfeeding another; رَضَاعَة names the action or period of suckling and nursing; مُرْضِعَة describes a nursing woman, and مَرَاضِع is the plural for nursing women in the reading adopted here; اِسْتَرْضَعَ concerns requesting or arranging nursing by another. Qur’anic provisions about nursing duration, parental rights, kinship, and remuneration come from the verses’ wording and context, rather than from the root alone.
