Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root S F H

Lightness of judgment, immaturity in discernment, weakness of ḥilm, and speech or conduct that lacks weight and steadiness

Morphological forms of this root

The root س ف ه (S F H) does not mean simple ignorance only. Its core is closer to lightness: lightness in reason, judgment, ḥilm, decision, or behavior. Opposite fields include ḥilm, rushd, gravity, and steadiness.

General meaning of the root S F H

The root س ف ه (S F H) points to lightness, immaturity, and lack of weight in reason, judgment, and behavior. Something that should have gravity, restraint, discernment, and steadiness enters this root’s field when it becomes weightless, rushed, immature, or lacking ḥilm.

In Arabic, safah is treated as the opposite of ḥilm. Ḥilm is not only calmness; it is gravity, self-restraint, judgment, and maturity in dealing with matters. So when someone is called safīh, the problem is not merely that he does not know. His judgment and behavior lack weight.

For this reason, safah can appear in religion, wealth, speech, relationships, judgment, and social behavior. A person may speak, claim knowledge, and even consider himself wise; but if his judgment is weightless, rushed, immature, or against truth and proper growth, he stands in the field of safah.

Usage of the root in Arabic

In classical Arabic one says سَفِهَ الرجلُ (safiha al-rjlu): the man became light-minded, or his judgment left maturity and steadiness. One may also say سَفِهَ رأيَهُ (safiha rʾyahu) or سَفِهَ رأيُهُ (safiha rʾyuhu): his opinion became light, unsound, and unsettled.

السَّفَه (al-safah) and السَّفَاهَة (al-safāhah) refer to lightness of intellect, the opposite of ḥilm, and immaturity in conduct. Arabic distinguishes between jahl and safah. Jahl may be not knowing; safah is more about the lightness of discernment and behavior.

A person may know something and still act from safah, because knowledge has not become ḥilm, rushd, and measured judgment within him.

The material and linguistic image of safah

One important point in understanding this root is that Arabic does not use safah only for human intellect. Lexical usage includes ثوب سفيه (thwb sfyh), a badly woven, weak, or poor-quality cloth. This example matters because it shows that س ف ه (S F H) is also near structural weakness and lightness.

If a cloth is not woven properly, its threads are not firm and ordered, and it does not perform its function well, it can be described in this field. The image then moves to the human being: a safīh opinion is an opinion whose inner weave is not firm.

Some usages also connect this field with wind that shifts, blows lightly, or lacks a firm direction. This image helps clarify safah: the person or his opinion lacks sufficient steadiness and weight before truth and proper growth.

Examples outside the Qur’an

Lexical texts mention ثوب سفيه (thwb sfyh), a badly woven or weak cloth. This non-human usage shows that safah is not always merely an ethical insult. It can describe a weak, light, or poor-quality structure.

Arabic also says سَفِهَ رأيُهُ (safiha rʾyuhu): his opinion became light and immature. The opinion left steadiness and soundness, even if its owner considers himself knowledgeable.

In legal and social language, safīh is used for someone who cannot properly manage his wealth. Here safah does not mean insanity; it means immaturity in managing wealth and lack of sufficient rushd for proper disposal.

Morphological and semantic forms

The verbal form is سَفِهَ / يَسْفَهُ (safiha / yasfahu): he became light-minded, left rushd and ḥilm, or became immature in discernment.

The noun سَفَه (safah) points to the state of safah: light-mindedness, immaturity, or a weightless and unmeasured action. سَفَاهَة (safāhah) shows a more visible or established state of safah.

سَفِيه (safīh) describes a person or thing that carries this lightness, immaturity, or lack of steadiness. Its plural is سُفَهَاء (sufahāʾ). In the Qur’an, السُّفَهَاء (al-sufahāʾ) are those who imagine themselves to possess discernment, while their view of faith, wealth, responsibility, or speech about God lacks proper growth and weight.

Safah against ḥilm and rushd

To understand this root carefully, it should be placed against two concepts: ḥilm and rushd. Ḥilm is gravity, restraint, judgment, and maturity in conduct. Safah is the opposite: a light, rushed, immature, or weightless reaction.

Rushd is the ability to discern the right path and use choice, wealth, and responsibility properly. Safah is also the opposite of rushd, because the safīh may have freedom, but he does not have the ability to use it properly.

For this reason, safah in the Qur’an sometimes stands opposite sound faith, sometimes proper management of wealth, and sometimes measured speech about God.

Usage of the root in the Qur’an

In the Qur’an, the root س ف ه (S F H) appears in several major fields: religion, faith, wealth, speech about God, and judgment about believers.

In 2:130, the one who turns away from the way of Abraham is described as one who سَفِهَ نَفْسَهُ (safiha nafsahu): he has thrown himself into safah or made the truth of his own being light.

In other verses, the peoples of prophets call the call of truth safāhah. But the prophet replies that there is no safāhah in him; rather, he is a messenger from the Lord of the worlds. This shows that sometimes the foolish call the people of truth foolish, because their standards have been inverted.

In 6:140, those who kill their children from سَفَهًا بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ (safahanā bighayri ʿilmin) are called losers. Here safah joins lack of knowledge and reaches a destructive decision concerning family and progeny.

Al-sufahāʾ in the Qur’an

The word السُّفَهَاء (al-sufahāʾ) appears several times in the Qur’an, each time opening an important field.

In 2:13, the hypocrites say about the believers: shall we believe as the السُّفَهَاء (al-sufahāʾ) have believed? The Qur’an answers that they themselves are the foolish ones, but they do not know.

In the verse of the change of qiblah, 2:142, it says: سَيَقُولُ السُّفَهَاءُ مِنَ النَّاسِ (sayaqūlu al-sufahāʾu mina al-nāsi). Here safah is a surface-level, weightless reaction to a divine change of direction.

In the verse of wealth, 4:5, it says: وَلَا تُؤْتُوا السُّفَهَاءَ أَمْوَالَكُمُ (walā tuʾtūā al-sufahāʾa amwālakumu). Here safah relates to lack of sufficient rushd in managing wealth.

In the story of Moses, the expression السُّفَهَاءُ مِنَّا (al-sufahāʾu minnā) appears, and in Sūrah al-Jinn, سَفِيهُنَا (safīhunā) refers to one who spoke about God in a false and excessive way.

Safah and the wasting of capacity

One important field of safah in the Qur’an is its relation to capacity and responsibility. In 4:5, wealth is described as a means of qiyām: something by which life, family, society, and the human ability to stand are maintained.

The contrast in this verse is sharp: wealth is a means of standing, but the safīh is light and immature in discernment and management. Giving the means of stability to someone unable to keep it stable can harm the structure of livelihood.

So safah is not only a mental or ethical weakness. It can waste capacities. Wealth, time, religion, relationships, progeny, knowledge, and responsibility can become waste, damage, and lost opportunity when placed under immature and weightless judgment.

Safiha nafsahu and making one’s own truth light

In 2:130, the deep expression سَفِهَ نَفْسَهُ (safiha nafsahu) appears. This does not merely mean “he became ignorant.” When safah is attached to the self, the meaning becomes deeper: the human being makes his own truth light and fails to recognize the value and station of his own being.

The way of Abraham is the path of tawḥīd, purified direction, and returning the human being to his proper place. Turning away from this path is not presented in the Qur’an as simple ignorance. It is the lightening of the human being’s own self.

Cognitive safah and stopping at the surface

In the qiblah verse, السُّفَهَاءُ مِنَ النَّاسِ (al-sufahāʾu mina al-nāsi) are those who react superficially to a visible change of direction. They ask: what turned them away from the qiblah they were upon?

The problem is not merely asking. A question can open understanding. The safah is that their sight stops at the outer form and physical direction, while they fail to see the divine command and formative meaning behind the qiblah.

This may be called cognitive safah: clinging to the surface, failing to understand meaning, stopping at the form, and neglecting the truth behind the form.

Safah in wealth, religion, and speech about God

The Qur’anic uses show that safah is not merely a simple personal weakness. It becomes dangerous when it reaches wealth, religion, society, or speech about God.

In wealth, safah means that a person cannot discern benefit and preserve the capital of life. In religion, safah means that a person belittles the call of truth, calls the faith of believers foolishness, or abandons the path of tawḥīd.

In speech about God, safah becomes more severe, because a person, from weightlessness and immaturity, attributes to God a statement that is not true. In 72:4, the wording is: سَفِيهُنَا عَلَى اللَّهِ شَطَطًا (safīhunā ʿalā allāh shaṭaṭan).

Translation suggestions for Forqan

سَفِهَ (safiha): became light-minded; threw himself into immaturity; left rushd and ḥilm.

سَفِهَ نَفْسَهُ (safiha nafsahu): made his own self light; lowered the truth of himself; deprived himself of true growth.

سَفَهًا (safahanā): from light-mindedness; from immaturity; without measured judgment or knowledge.

سَفَهًا بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ (safahanā bighayri ʿilmin): from immaturity and low judgment, without knowledge as support.

سَفَاهَة (safāhah): safah; light-mindedness; immaturity in discernment; weakness of ḥilm.

سَفِيه (safīh): light-minded; immature in discernment; lacking ḥilm; lacking growth in responsibility.

السُّفَهَاء (al-sufahāʾ): the light-minded; those immature in discernment; those whose opinion and behavior lack the weight and growth required.

وَلَا تُؤْتُوا السُّفَهَاءَ أَمْوَالَكُمُ (walā tuʾtūā al-sufahāʾa amwālakumu): do not give your wealth to the safīh ones; that is, to those who lack the growth and ability to preserve wealth properly.

سَفِيهُنَا عَلَى اللَّهِ شَطَطًا (safīhunā ʿalā allāh shaṭaṭan): our light-minded one used to say about God something excessive and far from truth.

Summary

The root س ف ه (S F H) begins from the meaning of lightness, immaturity, and weakness in structure or discernment. This lightness can appear in cloth, opinion, behavior, wealth management, judgment about faith, or speech about God.

In the Qur’an, a safīh is not simply someone who does not know. He is someone who lacks the weight required for discernment, ḥilm, rushd, and responsibility, or someone who makes truth light and whose standards have become inverted.

Safah in the Qur’an is therefore deeper than simple “foolishness.” It is the lightening of reason and discernment, the lack of ḥilm and rushd, and the inability to place faith, wealth, speech, and responsibility in their proper position.
