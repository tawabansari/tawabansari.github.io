Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root A L K

Message, mission, carrying a command, being sent, and delivering an entrusted matter

Important Related Forms

  • مَلَك (malak) — an envoy, bearer of a command, or divine agent, in the analysis that traces the word to A L K.
  • مَلَائِكَة (malāʾikah) — divine envoys or agents who carry and execute The God’s command.

Brief Explanation

The root of مَلَائِكَة (malāʾikah) is not settled without disagreement, but two major analyses are found.

The stronger and more common view is that مَلَائِكَة comes from مَلَك (malak), and many lexicographers trace malak back to the root A L K. The basic meaning of this root is message, mission, and sending a message. On this reading, malak means an envoy, bearer of a message, or one appointed to deliver a command. Malāʾikah are therefore divine envoys or agents who carry and execute The God’s command.

Another view connects the word with the root M L K, whose field includes power, dominion, authority, sovereignty, and the capacity to execute command. On that reading, the angels are understood through force, authority, and execution within the divine order.

In Forqan, until a separate article is written for the word malāʾikah itself, references for the word angels are directed to this page, the root A L K, not to the root M L K.
