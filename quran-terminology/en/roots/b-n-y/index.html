Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root B N Y

Building, erecting, founding, forming an ordered structure, and joining parts into a stable whole

Important Forms from This Root

بَنَى / يَبْنِي (banā / yabnī)

built, erected, raised a structure part by part.

بِنَاء (bināʾ)

building, structure, or erected form.

بُنْيَان (bunyān)

a firm, joined, foundational structure.

مَبْنِيّ (mabnī)

built, constructed, or based upon a structure.

ابن / بنون / بني (ibn / banūn / banī)

son, children, descendants, or a lineage built upon an origin.

Method Note

The root B N Y should not be translated only as the general word “to make.” This root carries the sense of building with stability, joining parts, raising layers, and forming an ordered structure. What is built moves from scattered parts into a reliable whole.

In the Qur’an, B N Y appears for material structures such as buildings, walls, houses, and the sky. It also appears in human structures such as بَنِي آدَمَ (banī ādam), the children of Adam, and بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ (banī isrāʾīl), the children of Israel. This range shows that the root is not only about architecture. A bināʾ is something whose parts are joined into a lasting identity.

The architectural sense and the lineage sense should not be collapsed into one flat meaning. Yet the link is clear: a building is a structure, and a child or descendant is an extension of a family and generational structure.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of B N Y is forming a whole from joined parts. This usually involves raising, strengthening, placing part upon part, and producing an ordered body that can stand and function. Building is not merely collecting materials. It is arranging materials into a structure.

From this core, بني (banī) also carries the meaning of human relation. بني آدم (banī ādam) means the children of Adam, but it is not merely a dry biological label. It points to a generation and continuation built upon one origin. A building rests on a foundation; a lineage rests on an origin and family relation. The image must be handled carefully, because human beings are not bricks, but the root shows that lineage has a structural extension.

Material Image

The material image is the building of a house or wall. Stones, clay, wood, and bricks are not yet a building by themselves. When they are placed in the right order and joined with proper measure, a building appears. A built thing is a set of parts taken out of scattering and made to stand as one body.

The foundation matters. If the foundation is weak, the building collapses. If the parts are not joined well, the structure does not endure. From this image, the Qur’an can speak of بُنْيَان (bunyān) in relation to faith, piety, community, and hypocrisy. Moral and social structures also have foundations and joined parts.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, بَنَى الْبَيْتَ (banā al-bayt) means “he built the house.” بُنْيَان (bunyān) refers to a firm building or structure whose parts and layers have been placed together into a visible and lasting body.

In family relation, ابن (ibn), son, and بنون (banūn), sons or children, belong to the same field. A child is an extension of family and generation. The connection between building and lineage is natural in language: a family is also a structure; generations are built upon one another.

Qur’anic Usage

The Sky as a Building

In الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ الْأَرْضَ فِرَاشًا وَالسَّمَاءَ بِنَاءً (alladhī jaʿala lakumu al-arḍa firāshan wal-samāʾa bināʾā), the sky is described as a building 2:22. The earth is the spread-out bed under the human being; the sky is the raised structure above, completing the living world.

Here بِنَاء (bināʾ) is not merely a visible roof. In the Qur’an, the sky is an ordered, raised structure that stands in relation to the earth. The expression shows that the world is not loose or structureless. Its above and below are joined in a meaningful order.

The Sky Built with Power

In وَالسَّمَاءَ بَنَيْنَاهَا بِأَيْدٍ وَإِنَّا لَمُوسِعُونَ (wal-samāʾa banaynāhā bi-aydin wa-innā lamūsiʿūn), the sky is described with the verb “We built it” 51:47. The sky has an ordered and firm structure. The phrase بِأَيْدٍ (bi-aydin) points to power and capacity in this building.

Here B N Y appears at the level of creation. The sky is not accidental or unrelated. It is built and raised. Its structure is a sign of power, order, and expansion.

A Structure Founded on Taqwā or on a Collapsing Edge

In أَفَمَنْ أَسَّسَ بُنْيَانَهُ عَلَىٰ تَقْوَىٰ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَرِضْوَانٍ خَيْرٌ أَم مَّنْ أَسَّسَ بُنْيَانَهُ عَلَىٰ شَفَا جُرُفٍ هَارٍ (afaman assasa bunyānahu ʿalā taqwā mina allāhi wariḍwān khayrun am man assasa bunyānahu ʿalā shafā jurufin hār), the Qur’an speaks of a structure founded either on taqwā and The God’s approval, or on the edge of a crumbling cliff 9:109. This moves B N Y from material architecture into moral and religious architecture.

A religious or social structure is also like a building. If its foundation is taqwā, The God’s approval, and truth, it stands. But if it is built on hypocrisy, harm, and division, it is like a building placed on a collapsing edge. Bunyān is not only a wall; any system with foundation and structure has a bunyān.

Bunyān Marṣūṣ: A Joined Collective Structure

In إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَ فِي سَبِيلِهِ صَفًّا كَأَنَّهُم بُنْيَانٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ (inna allāha yuḥibbu alladhīna yuqātilūna fī sabīlihi ṣaffan ka-annahum bunyānun marṣūṣ), those who stand in rows in The God’s way are described as a compact structure 61:4. Marṣūṣ means firm, closely joined, without gaps.

Here B N Y is connected to collective order and social cohesion. Individuals separated from one another are like scattered stones. When they stand in the cause of truth with the right joining, they become a firm structure. A community too can be built, or it can fall apart.

The Buildings of Arrogance and the Futility of False Structures

In أَتَبْنُونَ بِكُلِّ رِيعٍ آيَةً تَعْبَثُونَ (atabnūna bikulli rīʿin āyatan taʿbathūn), the people of ʿĀd are rebuked: do you build a sign on every high place, while behaving vainly? 26:128.

This verse shows that building is not valuable by itself. A person may build structures whose purpose is arrogance, display, vanity, or domination. So B N Y in the Qur’an also raises the question of purpose: what are you building, upon what foundation, and for what end?

Banī Ādam: The Generational Structure of Humanity

The expression يَا بَنِي آدَمَ (yā banī ādam), O children of Adam, appears several times in the Qur’an. It returns human beings to their origin. The children of Adam are descendants of one root, not unrelated groups cut off from one another.

In this use, بني (banī) shows lineage, human connection, and shared memory. Human beings are built upon a common origin and extend from one human root.

Banī Isrāʾīl: A Historical and Covenant Structure

The expression بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ (banī isrāʾīl) is very frequent in the Qur’an. It is not only an ethnic label. It points to a generation and continuation built on the root of Israel, standing in a history of revelation, covenant, blessing, trial, and responsibility.

Here B N Y shows that a human group may be built on a historical and covenantal relation. But that relation does not become an unrestricted privilege if it is separated from responsibility. The Qur’an repeatedly reminds the Children of Israel of blessing, covenant, and accountability.

Banūn and the Illusion of Lineage as Security

In the Qur’an, بَنُون (banūn) means sons, children, or a supporting lineage. A person may become attached to wealth and children, as in الْمَالُ وَالْبَنُونَ زِينَةُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا (al-mālu wal-banūna zīnatu al-ḥayāti al-dunyā) 18:46. Wealth and children are adornment of worldly life, but they are not the most enduring value.

This use shows B N Y entering the field of lineage and human continuation. A person may feel extension and support through children, but the Qur’an reminds him that such support does not bring final rescue if it is separated from truth.

Foundation and Layers in Bunyān

In Qur’anic use, bunyān depends on two things: foundation and joined parts. If the foundation is right but the parts remain scattered, the building is incomplete. If many parts are present but the foundation is weak, the structure does not last. This is why the Qur’an speaks both of أَسَّسَ بُنْيَانَهُ (assasa bunyānahu), he founded his structure, and بُنْيَانٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ (bunyānun marṣūṣ), a tightly joined structure.

This matters for religion and society. Faith, community, covenant, and family are not built by claim alone. The foundation must be right, and the parts must be joined rightly. A structure built on taqwā is not the same as one built on the unstable edge of hypocrisy, even if both appear to have a building.

Difference from Nearby Words

Khalq is bringing something into being in form and measure. B N Y is more about arranging parts, raising them, and erecting a structure upon a foundation. Jaʿl is placing something in a role, station, or condition. Ṣunʿ is making with craft, workmanship, and skill. ʿUmrān is inhabiting, cultivating, and filling a place with life and activity.

Assasa means to found or lay a foundation. A building may rise upon that foundation. Shayyada points to strengthening and raising a building, especially a prominent or fortified structure. Rafaʿa means raising; it may be part of building, but it is not identical to building. B N Y is closest to a joined, erected structure with a foundation.

Translation Method

“Build” is often correct for B N Y, but it is not always enough. In verses about the sky, “built” or “raised as a structure” may be clearer. In the verse about bunyān upon taqwā, the moral and religious structure must be seen, not only a physical building. In بني آدم (banī ādam) and بني إسرائيل (banī isrāʾīl), “children” is correct, but it also carries generational extension and structural relation.

For بُنْيَان (bunyān), “building” is sometimes too narrow. “Structure,” “foundation,” “firm construction,” or “joined structure” may be more accurate. In بُنْيَانٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ (bunyānun marṣūṣ), the meaning of cohesion and absence of gaps should be preserved.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

وَالسَّمَاءَ بِنَاءً (wal-samāʾa bināʾā): and the sky as a raised building; and the sky as an upper structure.

وَالسَّمَاءَ بَنَيْنَاهَا بِأَيْدٍ (wal-samāʾa banaynāhā bi-aydin): and We built the sky with power; and We raised the sky with strength.

بُنْيَانَهُ عَلَىٰ تَقْوَىٰ (bunyānahu ʿalā taqwā): he built his structure upon taqwā; he founded his structure upon God-conscious guarding.

بُنْيَانٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ (bunyānun marṣūṣ): a firm joined building; a compact structure without gaps.

أَتَبْنُونَ بِكُلِّ رِيعٍ آيَةً (atabnūna bikulli rīʿin āyah): do you build a sign on every high place? do you erect display-structures on every height?

يَا بَنِي آدَمَ (yā banī ādam): O children of Adam; O descendants of Adam.

بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ (banī isrāʾīl): Children of Israel; descendants of Israel.

الْمَالُ وَالْبَنُونَ (al-mālu wal-banūn): wealth and children; property and lineage.

Summary

The root B N Y begins from building and erecting, but it is not limited to material construction. A built thing has parts, foundation, cohesion, and a reliable structure. The sky is a building. A community may become a compact structure. Religion may be built on taqwā or on a collapsing edge. Human lineage appears in the expressions Children of Adam and Children of Israel.

In Forqan’s reading, B N Y means building a structure. That structure may be heavenly, earthly, social, familial, moral, or religious. The value of a building is not in its outward appearance alone. It is in its foundation, purpose, joined parts, and relation to truth.
