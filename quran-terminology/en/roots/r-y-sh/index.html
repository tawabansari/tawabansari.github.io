Central meanings: covering, adornment, material provision, and improving one’s circumstances and livelihood

1. Introducing the root

The Arabic root R Y Sh includes feathers and a bird’s covering among its concrete meanings. Its wider uses concern clothing, adornment, property, household goods, and resources that help sustain human life. These connections do not by themselves establish a precise historical sequence of meanings.

Rīsh in its concrete sense means a bird’s feathers: a covering that clothes its body, shapes and beautifies its appearance, and, in the case of some feathers, contributes to flight. Arabic also uses the word for clothing and things with which people adorn their lives.

This semantic range extends beyond clothing. Rīsh and riyāsh can refer to wealth, furnishings, livelihood, and material comfort. The root therefore cannot be defined solely as “feathers” or “adornment.”

2. Semantic core and principal branches

Two aspects stand together in lexical analysis: visible covering and adornment, and resources that improve a person’s circumstances.

One early lexical analysis identifies good circumstances and benefits obtained as the root’s organizing principle. Another begins with a bird’s feathers and explains the word’s application to human clothing.

These explanations address different parts of the semantic field. One emphasizes its range in human life; the other connects concrete and figurative uses.

The principal branches are:

  • Natural covering: feathers covering a bird’s body.
  • Outward adornment: clothing and objects that beautify a person’s appearance.
  • Resources for living: wealth, furnishings, and means used to sustain a livelihood.
  • Improved circumstances: assisting someone, providing resources, and relieving poverty or hardship.

These meanings are not equally present in every usage. A passage may concern a physical covering, wealth and comfort, or assistance that improves someone’s condition.

3. Lexical forms and derivatives

رِيش — rīsh

Bird feathers; also clothing, adornment, wealth, and household goods, depending on context.

رِيشَة — rīshah

One feather. Rīsh is a collective designation whose individual unit is rīshah; aryāsh is also recorded as a plural form in this lexical family.

رِياش — riyāsh

Clothing, furnishings, equipment, and property. In some uses it closely resembles rīsh; some lexical accounts emphasize clothing or material resources. A fixed boundary cannot be imposed on every occurrence of the two words.

رَاشَ الطَّائِرُ — rāsha al-ṭāʾiru

The bird developed feathers or down.

رَاشَ السَّهْمَ — rāsha al-sahma

He fitted an arrow with feathers. Traditional arrows carry feathers at the rear to help guide and stabilize their flight.

رَاشَ فُلَانًا يَرِيشُهُ — rāsha fulānan yarīshuhu

He benefited or assisted someone, provided the means of livelihood, or improved that person’s circumstances.

اِرْتَاشَ — irtāsha

He acquired wealth or benefit and his circumstances improved, with the effect becoming apparent in his life.

تَرَيَّشَ — tarayyasha

He gained property and resources, or his financial circumstances improved.

مَرِيش — marīsh

In reference to an arrow, fitted with feathers or fletched.

Of these forms, only rīshan occurs in the Ḥafṣ reading used on this site. The other derivatives are recorded Arabic forms and should not be presented as separate Qur’anic occurrences.

4. An Arabic poetic witness

The following line is transmitted in lexical accounts:

فَرِشْنِي بِخَيْرٍ طَالَمَا قَدْ بَرَيْتَنِي
وَخَيْرُ الْمَوَالِي مَنْ يَرِيشُ وَلَا يَبْرِي

fa-rishnī bi-khayrin ṭālamā qad baraytanī / wa-khayru al-mawālī man yarīshu wa-lā yabrī

Its sense is:

“Give me some good, after so long wearing me down; the best patrons and allies are those who strengthen a person rather than diminish him.”

Yarīshu here does not mean giving feathers to a bird; it means assisting, strengthening, and improving someone’s circumstances.

By contrast, yabrī concerns shaving away or diminishing. The opposition clarifies the line: one action adds to a person’s means and well-being, while the other takes away.

The witness shows that the root extends beyond clothing and appearance to livelihood and material benefit. Its presence in a dictionary does not, by itself, establish a pre-Islamic date.

5. Qur’anic usage

The root occurs once in the Qur’an, as rīshan in Al-Aʿrāf 7:26.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:26

يَا بَنِي آدَمَ قَدْ أَنْزَلْنَا عَلَيْكُمْ لِبَاسًا يُوَارِي سَوْآتِكُمْ وَرِيشًا وَلِبَاسُ التَّقْوَىٰ ذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ ذَٰلِكَ مِنْ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَذَّكَّرُونَ

yā banī Ādama qad anzalnā ʿalaykum libāsan yuwārī sawʾātikum wa-rīshan wa-libāsu al-taqwā dhālika khayrun dhālika min āyāti Allāhi laʿallahum yadhdhakkarūna

Al-Aʿrāf 7:26

Translation:

“Children of Adam, We have indeed sent down to you clothing that covers your private parts, and means of adornment. But the clothing of mindfulness—that is better. These are among the signs of The God, so that they may take heed.”

Rīshan follows libāsan yuwārī sawʾātikum and is coordinated with libāsan.

The first expression presents clothing as a covering for the body and nakedness. Rīshan introduces another aspect of material gifts: adornment, beauty, and clothing or resources beyond the minimum necessary covering.

Reducing both expressions to clothing that merely covers the body weakens the distinction suggested by their juxtaposition.

Since Arabic also uses rīsh for property and furnishings, its range cannot necessarily be confined to decorative clothing. It may encompass means of adornment and material provision more broadly.

In this verse, adornment and fine clothing are especially prominent because the immediate context concerns clothing, covering the body, and the clothing of mindfulness.

6. Rīsh, clothing, and human dignity

The preceding verses relate the story of Adam and his spouse. Al-Aʿrāf 7:22 describes their private parts becoming visible and their attempt to cover themselves with leaves from the Garden.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:27 again mentions their clothing and the exposure of their private parts.

Clothing in this context serves more than protection from heat and cold. It also relates to bodily privacy, adornment, and human social life.

Rīsh alongside clothing presents access to clothing and beauty as a blessing. Human needs are not limited to essential covering; people also have means to organize and adorn their lives.

Nevertheless, rīsh does not itself mean dignity. Human dignity and worth must be understood from the wider Qur’anic context rather than substituted for the word’s lexical meaning.

7. Rīsh, libās, and zīnah

Rīsh, libās, and zīnah are related Qur’anic words, but they are not exact synonyms.

Libās concerns wearing and covering. In Al-Aʿrāf 7:26 it names clothing that covers the private parts.

Rīsh can refer not only to clothing and adornment but also to resources, property, and material comfort.

Zīnah covers beauty and adornment more broadly, whether in clothing or in other things that beautify a person’s appearance or life.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:31 tells the children of Adam to take their adornment at every place of worship, to eat and drink, and not to be excessive.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:32 asks who has forbidden the adornment of The God brought forth for His servants and the good things of provision.

These verses illuminate Al-Aʿrāf 7:26. Adornment and material benefits are not inherently condemned. An ethical standard cannot be extracted from rīsh alone: how people use blessings, mindfulness, and avoidance of excess must also be considered.

8. Rīsh and the clothing of mindfulness

Al-Aʿrāf 7:26 continues:

وَلِبَاسُ التَّقْوَىٰ ذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ

wa-libāsu al-taqwā dhālika khayrun

Al-Aʿrāf 7:26

“But the clothing of mindfulness—that is better.” After recalling material covering and adornment, the Qur’an identifies the clothing of mindfulness as better.

On this reading, the clothing of mindfulness is not simply decorative clothing. Just as clothes cover the body and keep private what should remain covered, mindfulness has a protective role in moral life.

The analogy does not make the two coverings identical. One is material and perceptible; the other concerns conduct, conscientious restraint, and human orientation.

Preferring the clothing of mindfulness does not mean that beautiful clothing or material provision is worthless or forbidden. The verse recalls these gifts before showing the higher standing of mindfulness.

A person may possess the finest clothing and resources yet lack mindfulness. Another may own little and stand higher in mindfulness.

Material provision and moral worth are thus different. Rīsh may indicate comfort and outward adornment, but does not inherently establish its possessor’s moral superiority.

9. Rīsh and riyāsh: one meaning or two?

Lexical accounts give varying explanations of rīsh and riyāsh.

Some treat them as nearly synonymous; others associate rīsh more with adornment or property, and riyāsh with clothing and furnishings.

Riyāshan is also reported in some transmitted readings of Al-Aʿrāf 7:26. The Ḥafṣ reading followed here has rīshan; the reported alternative is not counted as another occurrence of the root.

These forms and explanations help reveal the word’s range but do not justify two completely separate, unchanging definitions.

Translation should first consider the actual form and context, then use lexical evidence to clarify its range.

10. Translation proposals for Forqan

The following equivalents fit different forms and usages:

Rīsh: feathers, covering, adornment, fine clothing, or material comfort and provision, depending on context.

Riyāsh: clothing, furnishings, equipment, and resources for living.

Rāsha fulānan: he assisted someone, improved his livelihood, or provided him with resources.

Irtāsha: he obtained resources; his circumstances improved.

Rāsha al-sahma: he fitted the arrow with feathers.

For wa-rīshan in Al-Aʿrāf 7:26, “and means of adornment” is a suitable concise rendering. An explanation may add that the Arabic word also encompasses material comfort and resources. This proposal does not replace the verse’s existing translation or authored word-box explanations.

“And feathers,” though valid for a concrete lexical sense, does not fit this context of human clothing.

“And luxurious clothing” expresses one possible instance but does not encompass the word’s full range.

11. Conclusion

The semantic range of R Y Sh links feathers and a bird’s covering with clothing, adornment, furnishings, property, and improved human livelihood, without requiring a definite historical sequence for these senses.

Arabic uses the root both for what covers and adorns a living being and for benefits and resources that improve living conditions.

Its sole Qur’anic occurrence is Al-Aʿrāf 7:26. Rīshan stands alongside essential bodily covering and highlights adornment and material provision.

The verse does not reject beauty or resources. It presents these benefits as blessings without reducing human worth to them, and identifies the clothing of mindfulness as better.

Understanding and translating rīshan therefore requires attention both to adornment and clothing and to the wider lexical range of comfort and resources, without forcing every root meaning into one word in the verse.
