Separating, setting aside, withdrawing, and remaining apart from someone or something

In Arabic, ʿAYN Z L concerns separating something from a group or position, setting it aside, keeping it away, and withdrawing. The separation may be spatial, social, behavioral, or limited to a particular relationship or activity.

The Qur’an uses the root in different settings: Noah’s son stands apart from those aboard the ship; Abraham withdraws from his people and their objects of worship; the companions of the cave withdraw from their community and its worship; groups withdraw from conflict; menstruation involves abstention within marital relations; and the devils are excluded from the hearing discussed in the passage.

These uses show that ʿazl is not confined to seclusion or abandoning society altogether. Someone may withdraw voluntarily, be set aside by another, or be separated only in relation to a particular action or relationship.

The root occurs ten times in ten Qur’anic verses. Its forms are عَزَلْتَ, فَاعْتَزِلُوا, اعْتَزَلُوكُمْ, يَعْتَزِلُوكُمْ, اعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ, أَعْتَزِلُكُمْ, اعْتَزَلَهُمْ, فَاعْتَزِلُونِ, مَعْزِلٍ, and مَعْزُولُونَ.

They fall into four main formations: the Form I verb عَزَلَ, Form VIII اِعْتَزَلَ, the noun مَعْزِل, and the passive participle مَعْزُول.

1. Core meaning and early Arabic uses

Separating and setting aside

Two explanations of the root’s core are removal to one side and causing something to incline: setting aside or moving aside.

Arabic uses the following expressions:

عَزَلَ الشَّيْءَ: he separated or set something aside.

عَزَلَهُ عَنْ عَمَلِهِ: he removed someone from a job or responsibility.

اِعْتَزَلَ الْقَوْمَ: he withdrew from the group.

In the first, someone separates a thing; in the second, a person is removed from a responsibility; in the third, someone withdraws from a group.

The differences matter. A single root can describe separating another person through a transitive verb and the subject’s own withdrawal through another formation.

عُزْلَة and اِعْتِزَال belong to the same family. The former concerns a state of separation or seclusion, and the latter the act of withdrawing or separating. They are not precisely identical in every Arabic use.

Spatially, بِمَعْزِلٍ عَنْهُمْ means being somewhere apart from them. The expression alone does not specify the distance, duration, or reason for separation.

Evidence from early poetry

A verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Taʾabbaṭa Sharran reads:

وَلَسْتُ بِجَلْبٍ جَلْبَ رِيحٍ وَقُرَّةٍ
وَلَا بِصَفَا صَلْدٍ عَنِ الْخَيْرِ مَعْزِلِ

wa-lastu bi-jalbin jalba rīḥin wa-qurratin / wa-lā bi-ṣafā ṣaldin ʿani al-khayri maʿzil

“I am neither a rainless cloud bringing wind and cold, nor a smooth, hard stone kept apart from goodness and generosity.”

The poet disclaims two images: an unbeneficial cloud and a hard stone from which no good comes.

مَعْزِل in عَنِ الْخَيْرِ مَعْزِلِ concerns being apart from good. It illustrates nonspatial separation: the issue is not a stone’s geometrical distance from something called goodness, but its lack of generosity or benefit.

The verse is transmitted with attribution to Taʾabbaṭa Sharran, although versions differ slightly in the first half-line.

Another witness by al-Aʿshā reads:

تُذْهِلُ الشَّيْخَ عَنْ بَنِيهِ وَتَلْوِي
بِلَبُونِ الْمِعْزَابَةِ الْمِعْزَالِ

tudh-hilu al-shaykha ʿan banīhi wa-talwī / bi-labūni al-miʿzābati al-miʿzāl

“It distracts the old man from his sons and brings hardship upon the milk camels of the solitary, withdrawn herdsman.”

مِعْزَال describes someone living apart or pasturing a herd away from the group. The opening تُخْرِجُ الشَّيْخَ مِنْ بَنِيهِ is also transmitted; this variation does not change the descriptor’s relevant sense.

The use shows that withdrawal could form part of a livelihood, such as a herdsman moving away from a settlement to seek better pasture.

The descriptor alone does not make the person cowardly, antisocial, or blameworthy. Early explanations even describe such solitary living, in some settings, as a sign of independence and capability.

Being apart from a group is therefore not necessarily blameworthy; its value depends on the reason, purpose, and consequences.

Other branches of the family

أَعْزَل also describes someone without weapons or a spear. The person is characterized as lacking the equipment of war.

Traditional star names contrast السِّمَاك الأَعْزَل with السِّمَاك الرَّامِح: in the traditional image, one had a nearby star pictured as its spear, while the other lacked such a companion.

عَزْلَاء also names a waterskin’s outlet. Its lexical explanation connects it with an opening at the vessel’s side.

One derivational examination finds this use difficult to fit into ʿazl’s central sense and considers it possibly exceptional. The caution matters: words recorded in one family do not necessarily share an equally certain connection with its principal image.

Arabic also records عَزْل for a man’s withdrawal during intercourse with the intention of preventing pregnancy. This sexual use should not be equated with every Qur’anic expression about withdrawing from women; each expression must be read through its own structure and context.

2. Spatial separation and withdrawal from a community and its worship

فِي مَعْزِلٍ: Noah’s son in a place apart

Hūd 11:42 reads:

وَهِيَ تَجْرِي بِهِمْ فِي مَوْجٍ كَالْجِبَالِ وَنَادَىٰ نُوحٌ ابْنَهُ وَكَانَ فِي مَعْزِلٍ يَا بُنَيَّ ارْكَبْ مَعَنَا وَلَا تَكُنْ مَعَ الْكَافِرِينَ

wa-hiya tajrī bihim fī mawjin ka-al-jibāli wa-nādā nūḥun ibnahu wa-kāna fī maʿzilin yā bunayya irkab maʿanā wa-lā takun maʿa al-kāfirīn

“It carried them through waves like mountains. Noah called to his son, who was in a place apart: My son, board with us, and do not be with the disbelievers.”

مَعْزِل describes a place or condition of separation.

فِي مَعْزِلٍ, alongside Noah’s call and ارْكَبْ مَعَنَا, establishes spatial separation in the scene. His son is not aboard with the others, and Noah calls him to join them.

The continuation shows that the difference was more than physical distance. His son declines to board and says in Hūd 11:43 that he will seek refuge on a mountain protecting him from the water.

The difference in belief and conduct should nevertheless not replace مَعْزِل’s direct meaning. The word describes separation; its reasons and consequences emerge from the story.

Abraham: withdrawing from his people and their objects of worship

In Maryam 19:48, Abraham says:

وَأَعْتَزِلُكُمْ وَمَا تَدْعُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ وَأَدْعُو رَبِّي عَسَىٰ أَلَّا أَكُونَ بِدُعَاءِ رَبِّي شَقِيًّا

wa-aʿtazilukum wa-mā tadʿūna min dūni allāhi wa-adʿū rabbī ʿasā allā akūna bi-duʿāʾi rabbī shaqiyyā

“I withdraw from you and what you invoke apart from The God, and I invoke my Lord. I hope not to be disappointed in invoking my Lord.”

أَعْتَزِلُكُمْ is a Form VIII imperfect verb with Abraham as its subject.

He declares his decision to withdraw from two related objects: his people and what they invoke apart from The God.

Before this, Maryam 19:46 has Abraham’s father threaten him and command him to stay away for a long time. Abraham answers peaceably in Maryam 19:47, then announces his withdrawal in Maryam 19:48.

The separation develops amid threats and disagreement over worship. It should therefore not be reduced to a liking for seclusion or inability to converse.

Maryam 19:49 reads:

فَلَمَّا اعْتَزَلَهُمْ وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ وَهَبْنَا لَهُ إِسْحَاقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ وَكُلًّا جَعَلْنَا نَبِيًّا

fa-lammā iʿtazalahum wa-mā yaʿbudūna min dūni allāhi wahabnā lahu isḥāqa wa-yaʿqūba wa-kullan jaʿalnā nabiyyā

“When he withdrew from them and what they worshipped apart from The God, We granted him Isaac and Jacob, and made each a prophet.”

The first verse voices Abraham’s decision; the second reports its realization.

Together they distinguish deciding to withdraw from actually doing so.

Abraham’s withdrawal does not mean abandoning every human relationship. He leaves a community and its worship; the Qur’an then describes his continuing life and divine gifts.

Withdrawal here concerns preserving faith and leaving an incompatible environment, rather than rejecting social life itself.

The companions of the cave: withdrawing and seeking shelter

Al-Kahf 18:16 reads:

وَإِذِ اعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ إِلَّا اللَّهَ فَأْوُوا إِلَى الْكَهْفِ يَنْشُرْ لَكُمْ رَبُّكُمْ مِنْ رَحْمَتِهِ وَيُهَيِّئْ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَمْرِكُمْ مِرْفَقًا

wa-idhi iʿtazaltumūhum wa-mā yaʿbudūna illā allāha fa-ʾwū ilā al-kahfi yanshur lakum rabbukum min raḥmatihi wa-yuhayyiʾ lakum min amrikum mirfaqā

“When you have withdrawn from them and what they worship apart from The God, take shelter in the cave. Your Lord will spread some of His mercy over you and prepare ease for you in your affair.”

اعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ concerns the young people’s withdrawal from their community.

Here too, two subjects are connected: separation from the people and their worship, and sheltering in the cave.

They are expressed separately: اعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ describes withdrawal, and فَأْوُوا إِلَى الْكَهْفِ going to a refuge.

Going to a cave is therefore not part of اِعْتِزَال’s lexical meaning; it is an action proposed after withdrawal.

Al-Kahf 18:13, Al-Kahf 18:14, and Al-Kahf 18:15 describe the young people’s faith in their Lord and opposition to assigning other gods alongside The God. Their withdrawal concerns preserving faith and leaving the worship they reject.

Abraham and the cave companions illustrate withdrawal amid religious conflict. Neither story establishes a universal rule requiring broken relations and physical departure for every religious or social disagreement.

Each story’s actual conditions, and the possibility of preserving faith and safety, matter.

3. Withdrawal amid hostility, war, and peace

Withdrawal from fighting: Al-Nisāʾ 4:90

Al-Nisāʾ 4:90 reads:

إِلَّا الَّذِينَ يَصِلُونَ إِلَىٰ قَوْمٍ بَيْنَكُمْ وَبَيْنَهُمْ مِيثَاقٌ أَوْ جَاءُوكُمْ حَصِرَتْ صُدُورُهُمْ أَنْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ أَوْ يُقَاتِلُوا قَوْمَهُمْ وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَسَلَّطَهُمْ عَلَيْكُمْ فَلَقَاتَلُوكُمْ فَإِنِ اعْتَزَلُوكُمْ فَلَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ وَأَلْقَوْا إِلَيْكُمُ السَّلَمَ فَمَا جَعَلَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ عَلَيْهِمْ سَبِيلًا

illā alladhīna yaṣilūna ilā qawmin baynakum wa-baynahum mīthāqun aw jāʾūkum ḥaṣirat ṣudūruhum an yuqātilūkum aw yuqātilū qawmahum wa-law shāʾa allāhu la-sallaṭahum ʿalaykum fa-la-qātalūkum fa-ini iʿtazalūkum fa-lam yuqātilūkum wa-alqaw ilaykumu al-salama fa-mā jaʿala allāhu lakum ʿalayhim sabīlā

“Except those who join a people bound to you by a treaty, or come to you with hearts constrained from fighting you or their own people. Had The God willed, He could have empowered them against you so that they fought you. If they withdraw from you, do not fight you, and offer you peace, The God has given you no way against them.”

اعْتَزَلُوكُمْ concerns withdrawing from hostile confrontation.

The following phrases clarify the withdrawal: فَلَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ and وَأَلْقَوْا إِلَيْكُمُ السَّلَمَ.

It accompanies not fighting and offering peace. The subject is leaving conflict and refraining from fighting, rather than abandoning every human relationship or being forced to change belief.

The result is explicit: فَمَا جَعَلَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ عَلَيْهِمْ سَبِيلًا. The God has given no way to act against them.

Withdrawal can thus be peaceful and effective in preventing war from spreading.

Here it is not presented as cowardice, unbelief, or evasion of responsibility. Refraining from fighting and offering peace instead grounds a limit on military action against the group.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:91: when withdrawal does not occur

Al-Nisāʾ 4:91 reads:

سَتَجِدُونَ آخَرِينَ يُرِيدُونَ أَنْ يَأْمَنُوكُمْ وَيَأْمَنُوا قَوْمَهُمْ كُلَّ مَا رُدُّوا إِلَى الْفِتْنَةِ أُرْكِسُوا فِيهَا فَإِنْ لَمْ يَعْتَزِلُوكُمْ وَيُلْقُوا إِلَيْكُمُ السَّلَمَ وَيَكُفُّوا أَيْدِيَهُمْ فَخُذُوهُمْ وَاقْتُلُوهُمْ حَيْثُ ثَقِفْتُمُوهُمْ وَأُولَٰئِكُمْ جَعَلْنَا لَكُمْ عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَانًا مُبِينًا

sa-tajidūna ākharīna yurīdūna an yaʾmanūkum wa-yaʾmanū qawmahum kulla mā ruddū ilā al-fitnati urkisū fīhā fa-in lam yaʿtazilūkum wa-yulqū ilaykumu al-salama wa-yakuffū aydiyahum fa-khudhūhum wa-uqtulūhum ḥaythu thaqiftumūhum wa-ulāʾikum jaʿalnā lakum ʿalayhim sulṭānan mubīnā

“You will find others who wish to be safe from you and from their people. Whenever returned to turmoil, they are plunged back into it. If they do not withdraw from you, offer you peace, and restrain their hands, seize them and kill them wherever you encounter them. Against these We have given you clear authority.”

Withdrawal again occurs in a context of war, but this time the group returns to turmoil and conflict.

يَعْتَزِلُوكُمْ accompanies offering peace and restraining their hands. The subject is the group’s actual conduct toward the other side.

Comparing Al-Nisāʾ 4:90 and Al-Nisāʾ 4:91 matters because withdrawal from fighting and restraint from aggression receive explicit consideration. A different ruling concerns those returning to hostility and refusing to restrain their warlike conduct.

Both verses must be read together within their wartime setting. The latter command cannot be detached from its qualifications and applied to every opponent or person of different faith.

Lexically, withdrawal means stepping aside in both verses. Its legal consequence depends on the war situation, the parties’ conduct, and the verses’ combined qualifications.

Moses: فَاعْتَزِلُونِ

Al-Dukhān 44:21 reads:

وَإِنْ لَمْ تُؤْمِنُوا لِي فَاعْتَزِلُونِ

wa-in lam tuʾminū lī fa-iʿtazilūn

“If you do not believe me, then leave me alone.”

The words follow Moses’ request to release The God’s servants and refrain from oppression and threats.

In Al-Dukhān 44:20, Moses seeks his Lord’s protection from being stoned. In Al-Dukhān 44:21, he then asks that, if they do not accept him, they at least leave him alone.

In a context of threats, فَاعْتَزِلُونِ involves more than physical distance: it includes desisting from aggression and leaving him unharmed.

The situation supplies this meaning; every use of اِعْتِزَال does not inherently mean abandoning violence.

Withdrawal can thus be proposed instead of continued hostility. Not accepting a prophet’s words is not itself permission to harm him.

4. Withdrawal within particular relationships: menstruation and setting aside

فَاعْتَزِلُوا النِّسَاءَ فِي الْمَحِيضِ

Al-Baqarah 2:222 reads:

وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْمَحِيضِ قُلْ هُوَ أَذًى فَاعْتَزِلُوا النِّسَاءَ فِي الْمَحِيضِ وَلَا تَقْرَبُوهُنَّ حَتَّىٰ يَطْهُرْنَ فَإِذَا تَطَهَّرْنَ فَأْتُوهُنَّ مِنْ حَيْثُ أَمَرَكُمُ اللَّهُ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ التَّوَّابِينَ وَيُحِبُّ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ

wa-yasʾalūnaka ʿani al-maḥīḍi qul huwa adhan fa-iʿtazilū al-nisāʾa fī al-maḥīḍi wa-lā taqrabūhunna ḥattā yaṭhurna fa-idhā taṭahharna fa-ʾtūhunna min ḥaythu amarakumu allāhu inna allāha yuḥibbu al-tawwābīna wa-yuḥibbu al-mutaṭahhirīn

“They ask you about menstruation. Say: It is a hurt. Withdraw from women during menstruation and do not approach them until they become clean. When they have cleansed themselves, approach them from where The God has commanded you. The God loves those who repeatedly turn back and those who seek purification.”

فَاعْتَزِلُوا is a Form VIII imperative.

Its basic sense is withdrawal, but فِي الْمَحِيضِ and the continuation specify the scope here.

The opening asks about menstruation; the reply calls it أَذًى, then describes withdrawal in that condition.

The restriction on approaching until they become clean, followed by the direction to approach from where commanded, shows that the main subject concerns sexual intimacy within marriage.

اِعْتِزَال alone therefore does not establish that menstruating women must be excluded from their home, family, meals, conversation, or social life.

That extension requires independent evidence and does not follow from this verse’s structure.

At the same time, the direct translation need not replace withdrawal with a narrow interpretive term without explanation. It can retain the expression and separately explain how the context limits its scope.

The verse clearly shows that ʿazl need not mean complete separation between people. It can concern abstention from a particular relationship or action in specified conditions.

مِمَّنْ عَزَلْتَ: setting aside within an arrangement

Al-Aḥzāb 33:51 reads:

تُرْجِي مَنْ تَشَاءُ مِنْهُنَّ وَتُؤْوِي إِلَيْكَ مَنْ تَشَاءُ وَمَنِ ابْتَغَيْتَ مِمَّنْ عَزَلْتَ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكَ ذَٰلِكَ أَدْنَىٰ أَنْ تَقَرَّ أَعْيُنُهُنَّ وَلَا يَحْزَنَّ وَيَرْضَيْنَ بِمَا آتَيْتَهُنَّ كُلُّهُنَّ وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ مَا فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ وَكَانَ اللَّهُ عَلِيمًا حَلِيمًا

turjī man tashāʾu minhunna wa-tuʾwī ilayka man tashāʾu wa-mani ibtaghayta mimman ʿazalta fa-lā junāḥa ʿalayka dhālika adnā an taqarra aʿyunuhunna wa-lā yaḥzanna wa-yarḍayna bi-mā ātaytahunna kulluhunna wa-allāhu yaʿlamu mā fī qulūbikum wa-kāna allāhu ʿalīman ḥalīmā

“You defer whichever of them you wish and receive near you whichever you wish. There is no blame on you if you again seek someone you had set aside. That is likelier to comfort their eyes, prevent their grief, and make them all content with what you have given them. The God knows what is in your hearts; The God is Knowing, Forbearing.”

عَزَلْتَ is a Form I past verb from ʿAYN Z L.

It stands beside تُرْجِي, تُؤْوِي, and ابْتَغَيْتَ. The subject is the arrangement and manner of relationship described concerning these women.

عَزَلْتَ conveys setting someone aside from a particular position. The verb alone does not mean divorce, ending marriage, or permanently severing a relationship.

وَمَنِ ابْتَغَيْتَ مِمَّنْ عَزَلْتَ allows again seeking someone previously set aside. The Persian expression here means seeking again, rather than calling someone to account.

ʿAzl therefore need not be irreversible: someone or something can be set aside from an arrangement and subsequently restored.

The verse’s family and legal details require its wider context, rather than the isolated meaning of عَزَلْتَ.

5. مَعْزُولُونَ: excluded from access

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:212 reads:

إِنَّهُمْ عَنِ السَّمْعِ لَمَعْزُولُونَ

innahum ʿani al-samʿi la-maʿzūlūn

“They are indeed excluded from hearing.”

مَعْزُولُونَ is the sound masculine plural of the passive participle مَعْزُول.

Its difference from اِعْتَزَلَ matters. Many uses of اِعْتِزَال describe someone withdrawing, while مَعْزُول describes someone or something in a condition of being set aside.

عَنِ السَّمْعِ specifies what they are excluded from: hearing.

The preceding verses clarify the expression. Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:210 reads:

وَمَا تَنَزَّلَتْ بِهِ الشَّيَاطِينُ

wa-mā tanazzalat bihi al-shayāṭīn

“The devils did not bring it down.”

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:211 says that this is neither fitting for them nor within their ability.

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:212 then adds that they are excluded from hearing.

مَعْزُولُونَ here concerns the devils’ lack of the auditory access in question. The subject is more than physical distance; it is exclusion from a kind of access.

The word alone nevertheless cannot establish a theory of that separation’s physical mechanism, exact location, or the manner of revelation’s transmission.

The lexical point is clear: ʿazl can describe exclusion from a particular opportunity, relationship, or access without necessarily describing departure from a place.

6. Related roots and translation proposals

ʿAYN Z L (ʿazala) and F R Q (faraqa): separation and distinction

F R Q has a broad Arabic field including separation, distinguishing, and making differences evident.

ʿAzl more particularly concerns setting something aside or separating it from a group, position, or relationship.

Two things can be distinguished by their features without spatial separation. By contrast, ʿazl can remove a person or thing practically from an activity or group.

This is a semantic tendency, rather than an absolute boundary between the families.

Study of F R Q

ʿAYN Z L (ʿazala) and J N B (janaba): withdrawal and avoidance

Avoidance, اِجْتِنَاب, belongs to J N B and concerns keeping away from something.

Qur’anic uses often involve avoiding a particular behavior or matter, such as impurity or false speech.

اِعْتِزَال can also involve avoidance, but the Qur’an additionally uses it for separation from people and groups, withdrawing from conflict, and abstention within a particular relationship.

The roots overlap, but their morphological and practical differences should remain clear.

Study of J N B

ʿAYN Z L (ʿazala) and H J R (hajara): withdrawal and leaving

H J R can concern leaving, separation, and, in some uses, migration.

اِعْتِزَال can also involve leaving an environment or group, as with Abraham and the companions of the cave.

Not every withdrawal is migration. In Al-Nisāʾ 4:90, withdrawing from fighting need not mean leaving the land. In Al-Baqarah 2:222, abstention concerns a particular relationship for a limited time.

Nor does every migration necessarily mean religious withdrawal from a people. Travel and relocation can have different causes.

Study of H J R

ʿAYN Z L (ʿazala) and B ʿAYN D (baʿuda): separation and distance

بُعْد concerns being far away or at a distance.

ʿAzl more often emphasizes the relation of separating, setting aside, or withdrawing from something.

Someone may be far away without being excluded or choosing withdrawal. Conversely, someone may lose a role or authority while remaining physically in the same place.

The difference is illustrated by مَعْزِل in Noah’s story and مَعْزُولُونَ in Al-Shuʿarāʾ.

Study of B ʿAYN D

Translation proposals for Forqan

Translations of this family’s Qur’anic derivatives should follow the subject of separation.

عَزَلْتَ in Al-Aḥzāb 33:51 means “you set aside,” without necessarily adding divorce or permanent rupture.

اِعْتَزَلَ and its forms can, according to context, be rendered “withdrew,” “stepped aside,” “kept away,” or “separated from them.”

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:90 and Al-Nisāʾ 4:91, withdrawing suits the context of war and peace. In Maryam 19:48, Maryam 19:49, and Al-Kahf 18:16, withdrawing or separating conveys the relevant sense.

In Al-Dukhān 44:21, “leave me alone” or “withdraw from me” follows the wording. A contextual explanation can add desisting from aggression.

In Al-Baqarah 2:222, “withdraw” preserves the verb’s basic meaning, while a separate explanation should clarify its scope during menstruation.

مَعْزِل in Hūd 11:42 is best rendered “a place apart” or “a place away from the others,” since the scene’s spatial condition matters.

مَعْزُولُونَ in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:212 means “excluded” or “barred from access to hearing.” The latter is more explanatory but clarifies عَنِ السَّمْعِ.

7. Concluding findings

ʿAYN Z L concerns separating, setting aside, or keeping someone or something apart from a particular group, position, or relationship. اِعْتَزَلَ can describe the subject’s own withdrawal; مَعْزُول describes being set aside.

Its main Qur’anic settings include spatial separation in Noah’s story, Abraham’s and the cave companions’ religious and social withdrawal, withdrawing from conflict, abstention from sexual relations during menstruation, setting aside within a family arrangement, and exclusion from hearing in the description of the devils.

None of these uses makes ʿazl invariably good or invariably blameworthy.

Abraham’s withdrawal concerns preserving faith and leaving rejected worship. Al-Nisāʾ 4:90 joins withdrawal with refraining from war and offering peace. By contrast, Noah’s son remaining apart from the ship leads, in the story’s continuation, to his not being saved.

ʿAzl also need not be permanent or completely sever communication. It can concern only a particular act, relationship, responsibility, or access, ending when conditions change.

Final definition: ʿAYN Z L concerns separating or setting someone or something apart from a particular group, position, relationship, or activity. اِعْتِزَال forms describe withdrawing from a person, group, or action; forms of being مَعْزُول describe being kept apart or excluded from the access or communication in question.

Each derivative therefore requires asking who separates or is separated, from what, and under which conditions. The root alone does not specify moral value, duration, cause, or consequence; the sentence and context supply these features.
