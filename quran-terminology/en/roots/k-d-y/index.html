The Qur’anic Root K D Y (ك د ي)

Meeting an obstacle, ceasing an effort, and cutting off giving after beginning it

Qur’anic forms

وَأَكْدَىٰ

1. Meaning in early Arabic

ك د ي concerns hard ground, encountering an obstacle, inability to continue, frustrated effort, and discontinuing giving.

A physical example is كُدْيَة: hard, stony ground that is difficult to dig and may stop the digger’s progress.

Classical Arabic records:

حَفَرَ فَأَكْدَى

He dug, then reached hard ground and could dig no farther.

The same verb is used for someone who begins giving, gives a little, and then refuses to continue.

A verse attributed to Al-Khansa’, a poet spanning the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods, laments her brother Sakhr:

فَتَى الْفِتْيَانِ مَا بَلَغُوا مَدَاهُ وَلَا يُكْدِي إِذَا بَلَغَتْ كُدَاهَا

Foremost among noble youths, whose stature they could not reach; he would not cease giving when they reached their limits.

يُكْدِي concerns ceasing to give; كُدَاهَا evokes the obstacles and limits that stop others. She praises her brother’s generosity in difficult circumstances.

Another line is transmitted in lexical and exegetical sources, without a secure attribution to a particular poet:

فَأَعْطَى قَلِيلًا ثُمَّ أَكْدَى عَطَاءَهُ وَمَنْ يَبْذُلِ الْمَعْرُوفَ فِي النَّاسِ يُحْمَدِ

He gave a little, then cut off his giving; whoever offers kindness among people is praised.

أَكْدَى عَطَاءَهُ clearly expresses discontinuing a gift already begun.

The precise dates of these lines are unknown. The second has variant wording and a conspicuous verbal resemblance to the Qur’anic verse. They can illustrate literary usage, but cannot independently establish a pre-Qur’anic date for the meaning.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important Arabic forms include:

  • كُدْيَة: hard, stony ground.
  • كُدًى: plural of kudyah; hard, stony portions of ground.
  • كَدَايَة: another form concerning hard ground.
  • أَكْدَى ـ يُكْدِي: encountered a hard obstacle, could not continue, or stopped giving.
  • إِكْدَاء: cessation of an effort or of giving.
  • مُكْدٍ: someone who stops or cannot continue working or giving.
  • كَدِيَتْ أَصَابِعُهُ: his fingers grew tired and weak from work.
  • أَكْدَى الْمَطَرُ: rainfall became scant.
  • أَكْدَى الْعَامُ: the year was afflicted by drought.

Form examined in the Qur’an:

وَأَكْدَىٰ

The family’s sole Qur’anic occurrence is the Form IV perfect أَكْدَىٰ in Al-Najm 53:34.

3. Semantic core and physical image

Ibn Faris locates the core in hardness and solidity and explains other uses through a digger encountering hard ground.

Imagine digging for water. Soft soil permits progress; a hard, rocky layer may prevent further digging and bring the work to a halt.

Kudyah names the hard obstacle; ikdāʾ describes reaching it and ceasing to advance.

Other uses apply to someone who begins a task without completing it, or starts giving and then stops.

The semantic range therefore includes not just a hard obstacle, but cessation and failure to continue.

The cause varies. Digging may be stopped by an external barrier; giving may cease through stinginess or deliberate refusal.

An external obstacle or genuine inability is therefore not a necessary component of every use of أَكْدَى.

4. Qur’anic use

أَكْدَىٰ: giving a little and then stopping

These verses place three actions together: turning away, giving a little, and stopping the giving.

أَعْطَىٰ, from ع ط و, expresses giving; قَلِيلًا specifies its small amount; أَكْدَىٰ states that it did not continue.

If smallness alone were intended, أَعْطَىٰ قَلِيلًا would suffice. أَكْدَىٰ adds another feature: the person began giving but did not continue.

This matters because giving little is not inherently blameworthy. Someone with few possessions may give a small amount proportionate to their means.

Al-Tawbah 9:79 mentions believers who find nothing to contribute beyond their own limited effort and condemns those who mock them.

The criticism in Al-Najm 53:34 should therefore be read in the whole pattern of turning away, giving little, and stopping, rather than in the amount alone.

Ikdāʾ and individual responsibility

The surah continues:

The context extends beyond an isolated financial action to turning away from truth and human responsibility for deeds.

People cannot evade accountability through assumptions about the unseen or by transferring responsibility for their actions to someone else.

The context also presents a notable contrast between أَكْدَىٰ and وَفَّىٰ.

First comes someone who gave little and stopped; a few verses later, Abraham is described as الَّذِي وَفَّىٰ, one who fulfilled completely.

Ikdāʾ and wafāʾ are not direct opposites in every lexical use. Here, however, they offer different images: abandoning continuation of an action, and fulfilling what has been undertaken.

These verses do not name the person, the amount, the recipient, or the precise reason for stopping. Understanding أَكْدَىٰ does not depend on identifying those historical particulars.

5. Morphology and syntax

أَكْدَىٰ is a third-person masculine singular perfect in Form IV. The final root letter, ya’, appears as alif maqṣūrah in this form.

Its implicit subject refers to الَّذِي in the preceding verse.

تَوَلَّىٰ, أَعْطَىٰ, and أَكْدَىٰ all refer to the same person.

أَكْدَىٰ has no explicit object here. Its combination with أَعْطَىٰ قَلِيلًا indicates that the cessation concerns the giving already begun.

وَ alone does not establish temporal sequence. The verbs’ meanings and relationship, however, indicate giving followed by stopping. “Then” can make that semantic relationship explicit in translation.

6. Comparison with related roots

ك د ي (إِكْدَاء) and ب خ ل (بُخْل)

Bukhl means withholding or refusing to give something one could make available to another.

Ikdāʾ in Al-Najm 53:34 means stopping a gift already begun.

Someone may be miserly and refuse from the start. Here the person gives a little and subsequently stops.

The meanings overlap, but translating أَكْدَىٰ merely as “was miserly” obscures discontinuation.

ك د ي (إِكْدَاء) and م ن ع (مَنْع)

Manʿ concerns restraint, obstruction, and preventing something from reaching another.

This verse concerns withholding something that could be made available. يَمْنَعُونَ alone does not show that giving or assistance had previously begun.

In Al-Najm 53:34, the combination of giving and ikdāʾ shows that a gift began but did not continue.

ك د ي (إِكْدَاء) and ق ط ع (قَطْع)

Qaṭʿ concerns cutting, separating, and ending continuity.

It can apply to a flow of water, relationships, a road, speech, or other activity.

Ikdāʾ shares the notion of interruption, but its lexical background also includes obstacles, inability to continue, and failure.

“Stopping giving” suits the Qur’anic use, but not every lexical use of ikdāʾ can be reduced to cutting off.

ك د ي (إِكْدَاء) and م س ك (إِمْسَاك)

Imsāk concerns retaining, restraining, and not releasing something.

Someone may retain money for personal and family needs; this is not necessarily blameworthy.

In Al-Najm 53:34, ikdāʾ concerns giving and withdrawing from its continuation. Retention is the resulting condition after giving began.

ك د ي (إِكْدَاء) and و ف ي (وَفَاء)

Wafāʾ concerns completeness, full performance, and fidelity to commitments.

وَفَّىٰ in Al-Najm 53:37 describes Abraham as one who fulfilled completely.

أَكْدَىٰ in Al-Najm 53:34, by contrast, describes someone who did not continue giving.

This contextual contrast clarifies the distinction between beginning an act and completing it.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

Arabic word or expressionSuggested rendering
كُدْيَةhard, stony ground
أَكْدَى الْحَافِرُthe digger met hard ground and could go no farther
إِكْدَاءceasing an effort; stopping giving
أَكْدَىٰstopped giving
وَأَعْطَىٰ قَلِيلًا وَأَكْدَىٰgave a little and then stopped giving

For Al-Najm 53:34, “stopped giving” is more precise than “became miserly” or “gave little”: it conveys both withholding and discontinuation.

Conclusion

ك د ي concerns hard ground, encountering obstacles, inability to continue, failure, and stopping giving.

Its familiar physical image is a digger reaching stony ground and being unable to proceed.

Al-Najm 53:34 uses it for someone who gave a little and then stopped.

The distinctive feature is not merely the small amount, but beginning and then refusing to continue.

Within Al-Najm, this behavior contrasts with fulfillment and personal responsibility: humanity will encounter its efforts and deeds.
