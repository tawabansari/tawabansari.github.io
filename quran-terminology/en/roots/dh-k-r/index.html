Foundational Meaning

The root DH K R fundamentally indicates remembering, mentioning, bringing truth into awareness, preserving a sign in mind, speech, and action, and bringing the human being out of heedlessness. The meaning of dhikr is not, as commonly reduced, merely saying or repeating a few words. Dhikr means that a truth which may have moved away from the human being's awareness becomes present again in their awareness, and this presence affects their life.

Dhikr as an Epistemic and Ethical Principle

In the Qur'an, dhikr is an act of the human being, the work of revelation, the mission of the messenger, the aim of salat, the opposite of heedlessness, and a name for the divine message. The God calls the Qur'an dhikr, and He also places previous books and messages within the field of dhikr. So dhikr is not only verbal repetition or personal memory. Dhikr in the Qur'an is an epistemic and ethical principle: truth must not be forgotten; it must be reminded; and it must remain present in the soul, speech, action, and society.

Important Qur'anic Forms

Important forms of this root in the Qur'an include dhikr, dhikran, dhikruk, dhikrā, tadhkirah, yadhkuru, yadhkurūn, udhkur, udhkurū, fadhakkir, dhakkirhum, yatadhakkarūn, tadhakkara, muddakir, mudhakkir, mudhakkirah, and dhākirīn. Dhikr can mean remembrance, reminder, honorable mention, a reminding message, or revelation. Dhikrā is reminder and receptivity to admonition. Tadhkirah is something placed for reminder. A mudhakkir is one who reminds. A muddakir is one who benefits from reminder and becomes mindful.

Linguistic Core

The linguistic core of DH K R is the renewed presence of truth in awareness. Heedlessness means truth has moved out of the human field of attention. Dhikr means that truth becomes present again in heart, intellect, speech, and action. Therefore complete dhikr is not merely repetition of wording. If the tongue says something but the heart and action remain unaware of it, dhikr in its Qur'anic sense has not fully occurred.

Material Image

The material image of this root is a sign preserved in memory so the human being does not lose the way. Whoever forgets the sign becomes separated from the path. Dhikr brings that sign to life again. For this reason, dhikr is also connected with āyah: an āyah is a sign, and dhikr is keeping that sign present in awareness.

Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic usage, DH K R is connected with remembering, mentioning, bringing to mind, making a name manifest, and preserving something in memory. In some lexical layers, dhikr also stands opposite forgetfulness. In the Qur'an, this meaning becomes deeper and expands into revelation, divine reminding, ethical responsibility, salat, warning, history, blessing, the Hereafter, and the presence of The God in the life of the human being.

Dhikr as Revelation and Divine Message

One foundational field of this root is dhikr as revelation and divine message:

إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا الذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ

Indeed, We ourselves sent down the dhikr, and indeed We are its guardian.

Al-Ḥijr 15:9

In this verse, dhikr is the name of the revealed revelation. The Qur'an is not only a book to be read; it is dhikr, a reminding message that brings truth into the awareness of humanity. This verse shows that preserving the Qur'an is not only preserving letters. It is preserving the dhikr, the reminding message that brings the human being out of heedlessness.

Another verse says:

وَهَٰذَا ذِكْرٌ مُّبَارَكٌ أَنزَلْنَاهُ

And this is a blessed dhikr that We sent down.

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:50

Here the Qur'an is "blessed dhikr." Its blessedness means that it carries increasing and life-giving good. Its being dhikr means that it reminds the human being of The God, the Hereafter, truth, justice, blessing, responsibility, and the path of return.

Dhikr in Previous Books and Messages

The second field is dhikr in relation to previous books and messages:

وَمَا أَرْسَلْنَا قَبْلَكَ إِلَّا رِجَالًا نُّوحِي إِلَيْهِمْ ۖ فَاسْأَلُوا أَهْلَ الذِّكْرِ إِن كُنتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

And before you We sent only men to whom We revealed; so ask the people of dhikr if you do not know.

Al-Naḥl 16:43

The same meaning appears in al-Anbiyāʾ 21:7. "Ahl al-dhikr" in this context points to those who were familiar with previous messages and divine reminding. So dhikr is not only a name for the Qur'an. It also includes the field of revelation and divine reminder among previous communities.

Surat al-Anbiyāʾ says:

وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا مُوسَىٰ وَهَارُونَ الْفُرْقَانَ وَضِيَاءً وَذِكْرًا لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ

And We certainly gave Moses and Aaron the furqān, light, and dhikr for those of taqwa.

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:48

Here what was given to Moses and Aaron is also named dhikr. This is very important: the Qur'an names itself dhikr, but the Mosaic message is also called dhikr. Therefore dhikr is a general name for the divine reminding message, not merely an exclusive label for the final text.

Surat Ghāfir also says:

وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا مُوسَى الْهُدَىٰ وَأَوْرَثْنَا بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ الْكِتَابَ * هُدًى وَذِكْرَىٰ لِأُولِي الْأَلْبَابِ

And We certainly gave Moses guidance, and We made the Children of Israel inherit the Book, as guidance and reminder for people of deep understanding.

Ghāfir 40:53-54

Here the Book of the Children of Israel is introduced as "guidance and dhikrā." Thus the work of a divine book is not only giving law; it is reminder. The human being is forgetful, and revelation comes so that truth may become present again.

Dhikr as Remembering The God

The third field is dhikr as remembering The God:

فَاذْكُرُونِي أَذْكُرْكُمْ

So remember Me, and I will remember you.

Al-Baqarah 2:152

This verse shows a living relation between servant and The God. Remembering The God is not only verbal mention; it is conscious turning toward The God. When the human being keeps The God present in decision, action, intention, gratitude, fear, hope, and repentance, they enter the field of dhikr.

Another verse says:

الَّذِينَ يَذْكُرُونَ اللَّهَ قِيَامًا وَقُعُودًا وَعَلَىٰ جُنُوبِهِمْ

Those who remember The God standing, sitting, and on their sides.

Āl ʿImrān 3:191

Dhikr in this verse is not reduced to one limited ritual state. Standing, sitting, and on their sides means in the varied conditions of life. Qur'anic dhikr is the presence of The God in human awareness, not merely verbal repetition at a specific time.

Dhikr Against Heedlessness

The fourth field is dhikr against heedlessness:

وَاذْكُر رَّبَّكَ فِي نَفْسِكَ تَضَرُّعًا وَخِيفَةً

And remember your Lord within yourself, with humility and fear.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:205

It continues:

وَلَا تَكُن مِّنَ الْغَافِلِينَ

And do not be among the heedless.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:205

Here dhikr is within the self, meaning that remembrance of The God must become present inwardly. Its opposite is heedlessness. Heedlessness means that the human being lives in the outward flow of life while truth has left their awareness. Dhikr is the remedy for heedlessness.

Remembering Blessing

The fifth field is remembering blessing:

وَاذْكُرُوا نِعْمَتَ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ

And remember the blessing of The God upon you.

Al-Baqarah 2:231

Remembering blessing is not merely saying with the tongue, "We have blessing." Remembering blessing means understanding where the blessing comes from, why it was given, how it must be thanked, and how it must not become wrongdoing, arrogance, or excess. Dhikr of blessing connects blessing to responsibility.

Dhikr and Salat

The sixth field is dhikr and salat:

إِنَّنِي أَنَا اللَّهُ لَا إِلَـٰهَ إِلَّا أَنَا فَاعْبُدْنِي وَأَقِمِ الصَّلَاةَ لِذِكْرِي

Indeed, I am The God; there is no god except Me. So worship Me and establish salat for My remembrance.

Ṭā Hā 20:14

In this verse, salat is connected with dhikr. Salat is for the dhikr of The God; that is, for keeping The God present in awareness and life. If salat becomes movement and habit while remembrance of The God is not present in soul and action, its central aim has been harmed.

Surat al-Jumuʿah says:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا نُودِيَ لِلصَّلَاةِ مِن يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ فَاسْعَوْا إِلَىٰ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ

O you who have faith, when the call is made for salat on the day of gathering, hasten toward the dhikr of The God.

Al-Jumuʿah 62:9

Dhikr Allāh here is connected with gathering for salat and divine reminding. Again, dhikr is not only a word. It is the axis that brings the believers back from trade and dispersion to presence before The God and His message.

The Qur'an Made Easy for Dhikr

The seventh field is the Qur'an made easy for dhikr:

وَلَقَدْ يَسَّرْنَا الْقُرْآنَ لِلذِّكْرِ فَهَلْ مِن مُّدَّكِرٍ

And We have certainly made the Qur'an easy for reminder; so is there one who will take reminder?

Al-Qamar 54:17

This verse is repeated several times in Surat al-Qamar. The Qur'an has been made easy for dhikr; that is, so the human being may remember truth, awaken, take admonition, and return. The Qur'an did not come to be monopolized by a special class. If the Qur'an is dhikr and has been made easy for dhikr, then its central message must be able to bring the human being back to the remembrance of truth.

Muddakir is one who learns, becomes mindful, and allows the reminder to affect them. Therefore the making easy of the Qur'an for dhikr does not mean shallow simplification. It means that for awakening the human being and returning them to truth, the Qur'an has not made the path closed and impossible.

This verse also shows that making religion difficult and turning the Qur'an into an unreachable text is incompatible with the function of dhikr. The Qur'an came for reminder, and reminder must be accompanied by understanding, awakening, and return.

The Messenger as One Who Reminds

The eighth field is the messenger as one who reminds:

فَذَكِّرْ إِنَّمَا أَنتَ مُذَكِّرٌ

So remind; you are only one who reminds.

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:21

It continues:

لَّسْتَ عَلَيْهِم بِمُصَيْطِرٍ

You are not a controller over them.

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:22

These verses clarify the mission of the messenger. The messenger reminds; he does not control people's faith. This also connects with the root B L GH: the messenger conveys, reminds, warns, but does not own people's hearts by force.

So tadhkīr differs from compulsion. Reminder brings truth before the human being, but choice, response, and responsibility remain upon the human being.

Turning Away From Dhikr

The ninth field is turning away from dhikr:

وَمَنْ أَعْرَضَ عَن ذِكْرِي فَإِنَّ لَهُ مَعِيشَةً ضَنكًا

And whoever turns away from My dhikr, for him there will be a constricted life.

Ṭā Hā 20:124

Turning away from dhikr means that the human being turns away from divine reminder. Its result is not only an external punishment; the inward life of the human being becomes constricted. When truth leaves human awareness, even if the person has resources, the horizon of life becomes tight.

Surat al-Zukhruf also says:

وَمَن يَعْشُ عَن ذِكْرِ الرَّحْمَـٰنِ نُقَيِّضْ لَهُ شَيْطَانًا فَهُوَ لَهُ قَرِينٌ

And whoever turns blind from the dhikr of the Raḥmān, We appoint for him a shayṭān, and he becomes his close companion.

Al-Zukhruf 43:36

This verse shows that distance from dhikr is not a neutral emptiness. When the dhikr of the Raḥmān is set aside, other companionships take its place. The human being does not remain without dhikr; either the remembrance of truth holds them, or false companions carry them away.

Dhikr as Honor, Name, and Lasting Remembrance

The tenth field is dhikr as honor, name, and lasting remembrance:

وَرَفَعْنَا لَكَ ذِكْرَكَ

And We raised your remembrance/name for you.

Al-Sharḥ 94:4

Here dhikr means remembrance, honorable mention, and the raising of the Prophet's remembrance. So dhikr does not always mean inner reminder. Sometimes it means name, lasting remembrance, and the true reputation that remains. Yet even this meaning is connected with presence in people's awareness: a name that is not forgotten and remains in the path of truth.

Surat al-Zukhruf says:

وَإِنَّهُ لَذِكْرٌ لَّكَ وَلِقَوْمِكَ ۖ وَسَوْفَ تُسْأَلُونَ

And indeed, it is dhikr for you and your people; and you will be questioned.

Al-Zukhruf 43:44

This verse can point to reminder, honor, and responsibility. The Qur'an is dhikr for the Prophet and his people, meaning it reminds of truth, brings responsibility, and connects their station with the divine message. But this honor does not benefit the human being without a right response to revelation, because the verse says: "you will be questioned."

Difference Between Dhikr, Dhikrā, Tadhkirah, and Tadhkīr

A distinction must be made between dhikr, dhikrā, tadhkirah, and tadhkīr. Dhikr is the root idea of remembrance and reminder; it can point to revelation, remembrance of The God, name, or the presence of truth in awareness. Dhikrā more often carries the sense of admonition and reminder for one who has the capacity to receive. Tadhkirah is something placed for reminder. Tadhkīr is the act of reminding. A mudhakkir is one who reminds. A muddakir is one who benefits from reminder.

Structural Notes

Structurally, the combinations of this root are important. Dhikr Allāh means remembrance of The God or the reminding message of The God, according to context. Dhikrī means My remembrance or My dhikr; in verses of turning away, it points to divine reminder. Al-dhikr in many contexts is a name for revelation. Ahl al-dhikr, in the context of asking previous communities, are those familiar with the reminder and previous Book. Dhikrā li-ulī al-albāb means reminder for people of deep understanding; thus dhikr requires right reception.

Nearby Roots

DH K R differs from several nearby roots. ʿIlm is knowing; dhikr is keeping what is known or what is true present in awareness. Fikr is the movement of thought toward meaning; dhikr is the becoming-present or making-present of meaning. ʿAql is binding and connecting data rightly; dhikr is keeping truth in the field of attention. Waḥy is the hidden and divine conveying of a message; dhikr can be the revealed message itself. Tilāwah is reciting and following the signs in sequence; dhikr is the reminding effect of those signs. Ḥifẓ is preservation; dhikr is living presence in awareness. Nisyān is forgetfulness; dhikr is the opposite of forgetfulness and heedlessness. Ghaflah is unawareness and inattention; dhikr is wakefulness and presence.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

In Forqan translation, DH K R must be translated according to context. Dhikr is sometimes "remembrance," sometimes "reminder," sometimes "reminding message," sometimes "dhikr," sometimes "name and remembrance," and sometimes "reminding revelation." Al-dhikr in the context of revelation is sometimes best preserved as "dhikr" with an explanation that what is meant is the divine reminding message. Dhikr Allāh, according to context, can be "remembrance of The God" or "dhikr of The God." Ahl al-dhikr is best rendered "people of dhikr" or "those familiar with the dhikr/previous message," not needlessly limited to a specific clerical class. Fadhakkir means "remind." Tadhkirah means "reminder" or "something that brings reminder." Muddakir means "one who takes reminder," "one who takes admonition," or "one who becomes mindful."

Linguistic Summary

The root DH K R indicates remembering, bringing truth into awareness, and preserving a sign in mind, speech, and action. Dhikr in the Qur'an is not only verbal saying; it is the presence of truth in the life of the human being. The Qur'an is dhikr because it reminds of truth. Previous messages and books also stand in the field of dhikr, because they too came for the reminder of guidance. Salat is for dhikr, the messenger is a mudhakkir, the Qur'an has been made easy for dhikr, and turning away from dhikr draws the human being into constriction, heedlessness, and companionship with falsehood. The opposite of dhikr is forgetfulness and heedlessness. Dhikr means that truth must not be forgotten: not in the heart, not in speech, not in action, and not in society.
