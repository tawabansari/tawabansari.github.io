The Qur'anic Root KH W F

Fear, apprehension, readiness before future danger, and the direction of the soul before threat

This page studies the Qur'anic semantic field of the root خ و ف (kh w f): fear that can make the human being alert, watchful, and reforming, or, if directed toward falsehood, can hold him back from truth.

Foundational Meaning

The root خ و ف (kh w f) originally points to fear, apprehension, anxiety, and an inner state of readiness before an approaching danger or harm. خوف (khawf) is mostly oriented toward the future: something may arrive, a danger may happen, a harm may occur, or an accounting and consequence may be on the way. From here, خوف (khawf) differs from حزن (ḥuzn): fear looks toward future danger, while grief is more the heaviness of the heart from loss, a painful event, or the pressure of the past and present.

Important Qur'anic Forms

The important forms of this root in the Qur'an include خاف (khāfa), يخاف (yakhāf), تخافون (takhāfūn), يخافون (yakhāfūn), خوف (khawf), خيفة (khīfah), مخافة (makhāfah), خائف (khā'if), خائفين (khā'ifīn), تخويف (takhwīf), and يخوف (yukhawwif). خاف (khāfa) means he feared or had apprehension. يخاف (yakhāf) and تخافون (takhāfūn) show a state of fear or anxiety. خوف (khawf) is the general noun for fear and apprehension. خيفة (khīfah) is close to inner apprehension, a hidden state of fear, or inward trembling. مخافة (makhāfah) can point to the cause of fear or acting out of fear. تخويف (takhwīf) means frightening, giving warning, or making someone apprehensive. خائف (khā'if) means fearful or apprehensive.

Linguistic Core

The linguistic core of خ و ف (kh w f) is the feeling of danger before it arrives. Fear is not yet the blow itself; it is the response of the soul and body to the possibility of a blow. In fear, the human being gathers himself, becomes alert, takes care, and becomes sensitive toward the future. If this state is directed correctly, it makes the human being awake and watchful; but if it is directed toward falsehood, it breaks him, frightens him, and holds him back from the path of truth.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is a living creature that senses a sign of threat before danger arrives: the ears become sharp, the body tightens, the gaze turns around, and readiness for defense or escape appears. خوف (khawf) is the state of vigilance before danger occurs. On the human level, this same state can become caution, تقوى (taqwā), flight from oppression, or flight from truth, depending on what the human being fears and toward which direction his fear takes him.

Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic usage, خ و ف (kh w f) is connected to fear, apprehension, caution, anxiety before danger, and expectation of harm. This root points to a state in which a person sees or anticipates something ahead and becomes apprehensive about its consequence. In the final version, if a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or ancient Arabic usage is found, it can be added.

Negation of Fear for the People of Guidance

One foundational use of this root is the negation of fear for those who follow the guidance of The God:

فَمَن تَبِعَ هُدَايَ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

So whoever follows My guidance, there shall be no fear upon them, nor shall they grieve.

Al-Baqarah 2:38

In this verse, fear and grief come together. Fear is more related to future danger; grief is more related to sorrow and the heaviness of the heart toward the past or a situation that has occurred. Following guidance takes the human being out from under the domination of these two pressures: fear of the future and corrosive grief over the past or present.

The same structure appears in another verse:

أَلَا إِنَّ أَوْلِيَاءَ اللَّهِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

Be aware: the allies of The God shall have no fear upon them, nor shall they grieve.

Yunus 10:62

This negation of fear does not mean that a believing human being experiences no natural reaction of fear at all. The Qur'an speaks of final security: destructive, fate-breaking fear does not dominate them. They do not see their final future as being in the hands of false threats.

Fear of The God

The second field is fear of The God:

إِنَّمَا ذَٰلِكُمُ الشَّيْطَانُ يُخَوِّفُ أَوْلِيَاءَهُ فَلَا تَخَافُوهُمْ وَخَافُونِ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ

That is only Satan who frightens his allies; so do not fear them, but fear Me, if you are believers.

Al-Imran 3:175

This verse corrects the field of fear. The problem is not only fear itself; the problem is what or whom the human being fears. Satan wants human fear to become tied to false powers and worldly threats. The Qur'an changes the direction of fear: do not fear them; fear Me. Fear of The God frees the human being from captivity to smaller fears.

Fear of The God in the Qur'an is not raw and blind terror; it is awareness of greatness, accounting, truth, and the station of the Lord. This fear does not paralyze the human being; it restrains him from crossing the boundary.

Fear with Taqwa and Reform

The third field is fear together with تقوى (taqwā) and reform:

فَمَنِ اتَّقَىٰ وَأَصْلَحَ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

So whoever has taqwa and reforms, there shall be no fear upon them, nor shall they grieve.

Al-A'raf 7:35

Here the removal of fear comes together with تقوى (taqwā) and reform. تقوى (taqwā) is not merely fear; it is guarding and protecting oneself from falling. Reform is also the repair of one’s path and behavior. When the human being enters the path of تقوى (taqwā) and reform, final fear about the future and destructive grief over the past do not dominate him.

The Fear of Prophets in Danger

The fourth field is the fear of prophets in situations of danger:

فَأَوْجَسَ فِي نَفْسِهِ خِيفَةً مُّوسَىٰ

So Moses sensed an inner fear within himself.

Ta-Ha 20:67

In this verse, خيفة (khīfah) points to the inner apprehension of Moses. The Qur'an does not deny this state. A prophet, too, can feel apprehension in his heart before threat, collective pressure, or a terrifying scene.

قُلْنَا لَا تَخَفْ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ الْأَعْلَىٰ

We said: Do not fear; indeed, you are the higher one.

Ta-Ha 20:68

So natural fear is restrained by divine address. The God corrects the fear of Moses with promise and assurance, not by denying his humanity.

About Moses at the time of leaving Egypt, it says:

فَخَرَجَ مِنْهَا خَائِفًا يَتَرَقَّبُ

So he left it fearful and watching carefully.

Al-Qasas 28:21

Here fear comes together with ترقب (taraqqub). ترقب (taraqqub) means watching, looking out, and keeping guard against danger. This fear is not cowardice or lack of faith; it is rational fear in a dangerous situation. Moses leaves the city, but he does not move blindly or without prudence; his fear keeps him watchful and awake.

The Fear of Moses' Mother

The fifth field is the fear of Moses’ mother:

وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَىٰ أُمِّ مُوسَىٰ أَنْ أَرْضِعِيهِ ۖ فَإِذَا خِفْتِ عَلَيْهِ فَأَلْقِيهِ فِي الْيَمِّ وَلَا تَخَافِي وَلَا تَحْزَنِي

And We inspired the mother of Moses: Nurse him; then when you fear for him, cast him into the river, and do not fear and do not grieve.

Al-Qasas 28:7

This verse is very precise. خفتي عليه (khifti ʿalayhi) means “you feared for him”; that is, you became anxious about future danger for the child. Then it says لا تخافي ولا تحزني (lā takhāfī wa-lā taḥzanī): do not fear and do not grieve. Fear looks toward the child’s future; grief looks toward the mother’s heaviness of heart from separation and the difficult situation. The divine promise answers both.

Fear in Family and Social Relations

The sixth field is fear in family and social relations:

وَإِنِ امْرَأَةٌ خَافَتْ مِن بَعْلِهَا نُشُوزًا أَوْ إِعْرَاضًا

And if a woman fears from her husband nushūz or turning away...

An-Nisa 4:128

In this verse, fear does not mean terror before an immediate danger; it is apprehension from the signs of a family harm: نشوز (nushūz), coldness, turning away, or the breaking of the bond. Fear here is an instrument of early diagnosis. When the sign of danger is seen, reform must be pursued before complete collapse.

Another verse says:

وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ شِقَاقَ بَيْنِهِمَا

And if you fear a split between the two...

An-Nisa 4:35

Here too fear means perceiving signs of danger. When society or family sees the sign of شقاق (shiqāq), it must enter into reform before the harm intensifies. So fear can sometimes be rational and preventative vigilance.

Fear in War and Insecurity

The seventh field is fear in war and insecurity:

فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ فَرِجَالًا أَوْ رُكْبَانًا

So if you fear, then on foot or riding.

Al-Baqarah 2:239

In this verse, fear points to real conditions of insecurity that even change the normal form of prayer. The Qur'an recognizes real fear and dangerous conditions, and it harmonizes responsibility with the human situation.

In another verse about prayer in the field of danger, it says:

وَإِذَا كُنتَ فِيهِمْ فَأَقَمْتَ لَهُمُ الصَّلَاةَ

And when you are among them and establish the prayer for them...

An-Nisa 4:102

The continuation of the verse explains the structure of vigilance in a state of danger. This field shows that fear in the Qur'an is not only psychological; it can be an objective state of danger that demands practical planning.

Fear of the Standing before the Lord

The eighth field is fear of the station of the Lord:

وَأَمَّا مَنْ خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ وَنَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَىٰ

But as for the one who feared the standing before his Lord and restrained the self from desire.

An-Nazi'at 79:40

Here fear comes together with restraining the self from desire. Fear of the station of the Lord means serious awareness of standing before The God, accounting, truth, and His station of greatness. This fear does not crush the human being; it frees him from servitude to desire.

In Ar-Rahman it says:

وَلِمَنْ خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ جَنَّتَانِ

And for the one who feared the standing before his Lord are two gardens.

Ar-Rahman 55:46

This fear is a formative fear: a fear that restrains the human being from crossing the boundary and leads him toward safety.

Fear of the Hereafter and the Day of Account

The ninth field is fear of the Hereafter and the Day of Account:

يَخَافُونَ يَوْمًا تَتَقَلَّبُ فِيهِ الْقُلُوبُ وَالْأَبْصَارُ

They fear a day in which hearts and eyes will be overturned.

An-Nur 24:37

In this verse, the fear is of a day when truth becomes manifest and hearts and eyes do not remain settled. This fear brings the human being out of everyday heedlessness and keeps him connected to remembrance, صلاة (ṣalāt), and زكاة (zakāt).

Takhwif: Warning and Frightening

The tenth field is تخويف (takhwīf) and frightening:

وَمَا نُرْسِلُ بِالْآيَاتِ إِلَّا تَخْوِيفًا

And We do not send the signs except as warning and fear-giving.

Al-Isra 17:59

تخويف (takhwīf) in this verse is not aimless frightening; it is warning for awakening. The signs sometimes shake the human being so that he can see the danger of the wrong path.

In contrast, Satan too has تخويف (takhwīf):

إِنَّمَا ذَٰلِكُمُ الشَّيْطَانُ يُخَوِّفُ أَوْلِيَاءَهُ

That is only Satan who frightens his allies.

Al-Imran 3:175

So تخويف (takhwīf) is of two kinds: divine تخويف (takhwīf), warning for rescue; and satanic تخويف (takhwīf), frightening in order to hold people back from truth.

Distinguishing the Uses

One must distinguish between the uses of خ و ف (kh w f). Fear of The God is a formative fear that frees from false fears. Fear of the enemy can be natural, but it must not hold the human being back from truth. The fear of Moses’ mother is maternal anxiety about the future danger to the child. The fear of Moses before threat is a human and natural reaction. Fear in family life is diagnosis of danger before collapse. Fear in war is an objective condition of insecurity. Fear of the standing before the Lord is awareness of accounting and divine greatness. Satanic frightening is a tool for terrifying and pushing believers back; divine warning is an alert for awakening and return.

Structural Notes

Structurally, particles and combinations are important. خاف من (khāfa min) can show fear of a source or danger. خاف عليه (khāfa ʿalayhi) means to fear for someone, meaning to be anxious about danger for that person, as with the mother of Moses. لا تخافوا (lā takhāfū) in divine address is calming and stabilizing. خاف مقام ربه (khāfa maqāma rabbih) means he feared the standing before the Lord and the greatness of accounting. يخوف أولياءه (yukhawwifu awliyā'ah) means that by frightening, a psychological atmosphere of threat is constructed.

Difference from Nearby Roots

خ و ف (kh w f) differs from several nearby roots. حزن (ḥuzn) is grief and heaviness of the heart, mostly toward the past or an event that has happened; خوف (khawf) is apprehension before future danger or harm. وجل (wajal) is trembling of the heart and inward awe; خوف (khawf) can be broader and more sustained. خشية (khashyah) is fear joined with knowledge and awareness of greatness; for this reason it is closely connected with knowledge in the Qur'an. رهب (rahb) is fear with awe and intense caution. فزع (fazaʿ) is sudden fear and intense agitation. روع (rawʿ) is close to an inward sudden shock. تقوى (taqwā) is not simple fear; it is guarding and protecting oneself from the danger of falling. حذر (ḥadhar) is practical caution and readiness before danger.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For translation in Forqan, خ و ف (kh w f) must be translated according to context. خوف (khawf) can be “fear,” “apprehension,” “anxiety,” “dread,” or “sense of danger.” لا خوف عليهم (lā khawfun ʿalayhim) is better rendered “there is no fear upon them” or “fear does not dominate them.” خاف مقام ربه (khāfa maqāma rabbih) is better rendered “he had apprehension before the standing of his Lord,” not simply “he was afraid.” خفت عليه (khifta ʿalayhi) means “you feared for him” or “you were worried about him.” تخويف (takhwīf) in a divine context is better rendered “warning” or “giving apprehension”; in a satanic context it can be “frightening” or “fear-mongering.” خيفة (khīfah) can be “inner apprehension” or “hidden fear.”

Linguistic Summary

Linguistic summary: the root خ و ف (kh w f) points to fear, apprehension, anxiety, and a state of readiness before future danger or harm. Its material image is a human being or living creature that, before danger arrives, becomes alert, gathered, and ready to respond. In the Qur'an, this root extends from the negation of fear for the people of guidance to fear of The God, from the natural fear of prophets to maternal fear, from social and family fear to fear in war, and from divine warning to satanic frightening. Its difference from grief is that fear is more future-oriented, while grief is sorrow and heaviness of the heart toward loss, an event, or a difficult condition.
