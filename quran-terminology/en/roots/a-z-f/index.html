Qur’anic Root أ ز ف

Drawing near and approaching: from the time of departure to the approach of the Resurrection

Qur’anic and lexical forms

أَزِفَتِ
(azifati)
Qur’anic feminine singular perfect verb: has drawn near, Al-Najm 53:57

الْآزِفَةُ
(al-āzifatu)
Feminine active participle used as a noun; nominative subject in Al-Najm 53:57

الْآزِفَةِ
(al-āzifati)
The same noun, genitive in yawma al-āzifati; Ghāfir 40:18

أَزِفَ / يَأْزَفُ
(azifa / yaʾzafu)
Lexical past and imperfect citation forms: to draw near; the feminine perfect occurs in the Qur’an

أَزَف
(azaf)
Lexical verbal noun: drawing near; this form is absent from the Qur’an

أُزُوف
(uzūf)
Another lexical verbal noun for drawing near; not Qur’anic

آزِف
(āzif)
Lexical masculine active participle: including a person hurrying; not Qur’anic

مُتَآزِف
(mutaʾāzif)
Lexical description of a short compact build or confined place; not Qur’anic

The Arabic root أ ز ف concerns the approaching time of an event. أَزِفَ الرَّحِيلُ means that departure has drawn near, rather than that the caravan has necessarily set off. The same meaning applies to the end of a period, an approaching appointment or an event drawing near before it has occurred.

The Qur’an uses this root in three words across two verses. Al-Najm 53:57 says أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ, while Ghāfir 40:18 speaks of يَوْمَ الْآزِفَةِ. In both, الْآزِفَة refers to the great event of resurrection and reckoning, presented as approaching.

Drawing near is distinct from occurring. An event’s nearness does not, by itself, establish its exact date. In the Qur’an, al-āzifah appears within a setting of warning and human responsibility: people may consider the event distant, but should not exclude its occurrence and reckoning from their lives.

1. Qur’anic forms and the root’s semantic core

The root أ ز ف occurs three times in two Qur’anic verses:

أَزِفَتِ in Al-Najm 53:57 is a perfect verb from أَزِفَ, denoting drawing near.

الْآزِفَةُ in the same verse is a feminine active participle from this root, used substantivally for the approaching event.

الْآزِفَةِ in Ghāfir 40:18 is the same noun in the construction يَوْمَ الْآزِفَةِ.

The Qur’an thus has one verbal and one nominal form from this root. The different case endings of الْآزِفَةُ and الْآزِفَةِ reflect their grammatical positions, rather than different roots or meanings.

The basic Arabic verb is أَزِفَ يَأْزَفُ. Its recorded verbal nouns include أَزَف and أُزُوف. آزِف is the masculine active participle; آزِفَة is its feminine form.

Arabic records أَزِفَ for the approach of an appointed time or event, and also for a person hurrying. Its Qur’anic use, however, concerns the approaching time of an event’s occurrence.

The semantic core can be expressed as:

The time of an affair or event drawing near, without the word itself specifying the interval or establishing that the event has fully occurred.

In the principal Qur’anic use, this nearness is temporal rather than spatial. An event’s time is presented as approaching, rather than one body reducing its physical distance from another.

Arabic also records uses within this family concerning closely spaced parts or a confined place. Temporal nearness should therefore not be treated as the only possible meaning throughout the entire أ ز ف family.

2. The root in early Arabic

أَزِفَ الرَّحِيلُ: the approaching time of departure

One of the clearest early uses is أَزِفَ الرَّحِيلُ or أَزِفَ التَّرَحُّلُ: the time of departure and travel has drawn near.

The following verse is attributed to Al-Nābighah Al-Dhubyānī, a pre-Islamic poet. Another transmission has أَفِدَ in place of أَزِفَ; the precise date of composition is unknown:

أَزِفَ التَّرَحُّلُ غَيْرَ أَنَّ رِكَابَنَا
لَمَّا تَزُلْ بِرِحَالِنَا وَكَأَنْ قَدِ

azifa al-taraḥḥulu ghayra anna rikābanā / lammā tazul bi-riḥālinā wa-ka-an qadi

“Departure has drawn near, though our riding camels have not yet set off with our saddles and loads; yet it is as though they already have.”

This verse is especially relevant for understanding أَزِفَ.

The poet first says that departure has drawn near, then makes clear that the caravan’s camels have not yet moved. أَزِفَ here therefore concerns the approaching time of departure, rather than departure having occurred.

The closing expression وَكَأَنْ قَدِ conveys a strong expectation of the event, as though it were so near as to be on the threshold of realization.

This witness shows that in early Arabic an event could be āzif while it had not yet begun.

Nearness and occurrence are therefore different stages. The interval may be short in a particular situation, but the root itself does not measure it.

أَزِفَ الْوَقْتُ and أَزِفَ الْأَجَلُ

Arabic also uses this root for an approaching time or the nearing end of a period.

أَزِفَ الْوَقْتُ concerns the approach of an appointed time; أَزِفَ الْأَجَلُ reports the approaching end of a period or term.

In these uses, أَزِفَ describes the event’s relationship to the present more directly than the event itself.

An approaching time of travel, for example, is distinct from travel beginning. The nearing end of a term likewise does not in itself mean that the term has expired.

The word can create a sense of urgency in such settings, since an approaching time leaves less opportunity to prepare. Practical urgency nevertheless follows from the situation and context; أَزِفَ does not always mean haste or anxiety.

أَزِفَ الرَّجُلُ: a person hurrying

Lexicons also record أَزِفَ الرَّجُلُ for a person who hurries. آزِف in such a use can denote someone hurrying.

This sense has an intelligible connection with an approaching time: the nearing time for a task may prompt someone to hurry.

This relationship does not establish that أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ means the Resurrection itself hurries as a person might hurry to perform a task.

The Qur’anic verse concerns the event drawing near. Human haste and preparation may follow from the warning, but are not the word’s direct meaning.

مُتَآزِف: being close and compact

Arabic also records مُتَآزِف for someone with a short, compact build. مَكَانٌ مُتَآزِفٌ can denote a narrow, confined place.

These uses highlight the closeness of parts or the small spaces between them. The أ ز ف family has therefore not been confined to the temporal field.

Such spatial uses do not establish that the Resurrection’s approach in the Qur’an is a bodily movement through space.

These are different uses within a semantic family. Each case requires us to determine whether closeness concerns time, space or a person’s haste.

In the Qur’anic al-āzifah, the setting of resurrection and reckoning highlights temporal nearness and approaching occurrence.

3. أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ: the approaching event has drawn near

Al-Najm states:

أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ

azifati al-āzifah

“The approaching event has drawn near.”

Al-Najm 53:57

This short verse consists of two words from the same root. أَزِفَتِ reports drawing near; الْآزِفَةُ is the very event characterized as approaching.

Grammatically, أَزِفَتِ is a feminine singular perfect verb, and الْآزِفَةُ is its subject.

آزِفَة follows the pattern فاعِلَة and is structurally a feminine active participle. Here, however, it functions as a recognized name for the expected event.

Al-āzifah is thus more than an incidental adjective for something unidentified. In this context it names the great event of resurrection, presented as characterized by its approach.

Repetition of the root in verb and subject

أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ presents one meaning from two directions: first through the report of drawing near, then through the event’s own name.

The construction gives the sentence particular emphasis. What is known as the approaching event has now drawn near.

The repeated root should not be made into two separate events. The verse does not say that one incident occurs first, followed by another event called al-āzifah.

Repetition also does not establish an exact time interval. Linguistic emphasis on nearness differs from announcing a date.

Why does al-āzifah refer to the Resurrection?

Al-Najm 53:57 itself contains neither الساعة nor القيامة. The end of the surah nevertheless shows that the subject is a warning about the great final outcome and human reckoning.

Al-Najm 53:56 speaks of a warner in continuity with earlier warnings. أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ follows; then Al-Najm 53:58 emphasizes that its affair belongs to The God and that no one independently of Him can unveil it or avert it.

The remaining passage also addresses the human response to the warning: laughter, weeping and, finally, the command to prostrate and worship.

This connection shows that al-āzifah here is no ordinary journey or social appointment. It concerns a tremendous event before which people must answer.

The Resurrection is nevertheless the word’s referent here, rather than the general independent meaning of أ ز ف. In Arabic, departure, the end of a term or any other event may also draw near.

Does أَزِفَتْ mean that the event has fully occurred?

This question matters for translation.

أَزِفَتِ is a perfect verb, but what is presented as accomplished is the drawing near, rather than necessarily the event itself.

Just as departure had drawn near in Al-Nābighah’s verse while the caravan had not yet moved, أَزِفَتِ here reports an approach.

“The Resurrection has occurred” therefore does not fit the verb’s meaning.

“The Resurrection has drawn near” naturally and clearly conveys the contextual sense. To preserve the form and emphasis of the two related words, “The approaching event has drawn near” is more suitable.

4. يَوْمَ الْآزِفَةِ: the day of the approaching event

The root’s other occurrence is in Ghāfir 40:18:

وَأَنْذِرْهُمْ يَوْمَ الْآزِفَةِ إِذِ الْقُلُوبُ لَدَى الْحَنَاجِرِ كَاظِمِينَ مَا لِلظَّالِمِينَ مِنْ حَمِيمٍ وَلَا شَفِيعٍ يُطَاعُ

wa-andhirhum yawma al-āzifati idhi al-qulūbu ladā al-ḥanājiri kāẓimīna mā li-al-ẓālimīna min ḥamīmin wa-lā shafīʿin yuṭāʿ

“And warn them of the day of the approaching event, when hearts are at the throats in terror and they suppress their fear and grief. The wrongdoers will have neither an intimate friend nor an intercessor whose plea is accepted.”

Ghāfir 40:18

الْآزِفَةِ follows يَوْمَ in this verse. The phrase therefore concerns the day of that approaching event.

The verse does more than state that the day is near: it also describes the condition in which people face it.

إِذِ الْقُلُوبُ لَدَى الْحَنَاجِرِ depicts intense fear, as though hearts had risen to the throats in terror.

كَاظِمِينَ describes people suppressing their fear and grief. It belongs to ك ظ م; its meaning should not be attributed to al-āzifah itself.

Study of K Ẓ M

The absence of an intimate friend and an intercessor whose plea is accepted further clarifies the wrongdoers’ situation that day.

Al-āzifah here therefore participates in more than a statement of temporal distance. The name appears alongside fear, reckoning and the loss of expected support.

These features are not the lexical meaning of أ ز ف. The root expresses drawing near; context identifies what approaches and why people should be warned about it.

Is yawm al-āzifah an individual’s death or the Day of Resurrection?

Discussion of this phrase has also raised a possible connection with a person’s death, since death ends the individual’s worldly opportunity, and the scene of fear and constricted breathing may relate to it.

The continuity of Ghāfir’s verses nevertheless supports the reading of resurrection and final reckoning more strongly.

Ghāfir 40:15 speaks of warning about the day of meeting. Ghāfir 40:16 describes people being exposed, and Ghāfir 40:17 speaks of every soul being recompensed for what it has done. Ghāfir 40:18 then commands a warning about يَوْمَ الْآزِفَةِ.

This progression places yawm al-āzifah within the description of that same great day of reckoning.

The nearness of death can certainly remind each person that opportunity is limited. That practical implication should not replace the Qur’anic phrase’s direct meaning.

The verse’s principal concern is a warning about the approaching event of the Hereafter, rather than identifying each person’s particular moment of death.

5. The Resurrection’s nearness: temporal proximity or certainty of occurrence?

A fundamental question about أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ is how to understand the Resurrection’s nearness.

In ordinary language, “the time of departure has drawn near” may lead a listener to imagine a short interval before travel begins. The sentence itself does not specify the remaining hours or days.

The Qur’an’s statements about the Resurrection’s nearness likewise require consideration alongside other verses about time, occurrence and knowledge of it.

Near and far from two different perspectives

Al-Maʿārij states:

إِنَّهُمْ يَرَوْنَهُ بَعِيدًا

innahum yarawnahu baʿīdā

“They see it as distant.”

Al-Maʿārij 70:6

وَنَرَاهُ قَرِيبًا

wa-narāhu qarībā

“And We see it as near.”

Al-Maʿārij 70:7

These verses draw a clear distinction between those who consider its occurrence distant and the divine statement of its nearness.

Seeing an event as distant can here be accompanied by considering its occurrence unlikely or disregarding it. Seeing it as near, by contrast, highlights a reality that should not be neglected.

Nearness should nevertheless not be reduced entirely to certainty.

“Near” and “certain” are distinct concepts. An event may be certain yet distant in time; another may seem near while its occurrence remains uncertain.

The Qur’an speaks both of the Resurrection’s nearness and of a reality whose occurrence cannot be denied. These features are related, but neither replaces the other.

The meaning of drawing near must therefore remain in the translation of أَزِفَتِ. Certainty of occurrence and human responsibility emerge from the wider network of verses.

Does the Qur’an specify a time interval?

Al-Qamar 54:1 states that the Hour has drawn near. Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:1 likewise speaks of people’s reckoning drawing near while they turn away in heedlessness.

Their message is close to أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ: reckoning and the human final outcome cannot be treated as insignificant or unrelated to present life.

None of these expressions, taken alone, sets a particular date for the Resurrection.

The long period since the Qur’an’s revelation does not license changing أَزِفَ into “being distant.” Nor should one introduce hypothetical time intervals, cosmological calculations or invented calendars in defense of the word.

Nearness is a real description in the verse’s language, but that verse does not measure the interval in units of time.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:187, Al-Nāziʿāt 79:42 and Al-Nāziʿāt 79:43 also address questions about the Hour’s timing and the limits of human knowledge. These verses prevent its nearness from becoming a claim to know the day or year of its occurrence.

Both principles should therefore be maintained: the Qur’an speaks of the Resurrection drawing near, while withholding its precise timing from definitive human calculation and prediction.

Nearness and human responsibility

In Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:1, people’s reckoning drawing near is juxtaposed with their heedlessness and turning away.

This juxtaposition shows that temporal distance is not the only concern. People’s response to the reality toward which they are moving also matters.

People may know their lives will end, yet behave as though the opportunity to answer for themselves will never arrive.

The nearness of reckoning and resurrection can thus be a practical warning: human opportunity is limited, and today’s responsibility should not be postponed on the assumption of an uncertain tomorrow.

This conclusion follows from the verses’ relationship, rather than أَزِفَ lexically meaning repentance, preparation or righteous action.

6. Distinguishing أ ز ف from related roots

أ ز ف (أَزِفَ) and ق ر ب (قَرُبَ)

قَرُبَ and its derivatives encompass a wide field of nearness: spatial, temporal, genealogical, or concerning relationship and standing.

Study of Q R B

أَزِفَ also denotes drawing near, but the approach of an event’s appointed time is prominent in its familiar Qur’anic use.

Al-Qamar 54:1 expresses the Hour’s approach with اقْتَرَبَ, whereas Al-Najm 53:57 uses أَزِفَتِ.

Both may concern the Resurrection drawing near. ق ر ب, however, has a broader word family and operates in more settings.

This does not establish that أَزِفَ necessarily denotes greater nearness or a shorter interval than اقْتَرَبَ. These two verses alone do not prove such a fixed scale.

أ ز ف (أَزِفَ) and د ن و (دَنَا)

In Arabic, دَنَا concerns drawing near and can describe spatial or temporal distance.

Study of D N W

In temporal uses, دَنَا and أَزِفَ are close. Early lexicographers likewise used دَنَا to explain أَزِفَ.

دَنَا can also express closeness between people or things, whereas أَزِفَ in its Qur’anic use concerns an approaching event.

The distinction is more apparent in their range of application than in two completely separate meanings.

أ ز ف (أَزِفَ) and ز ل ف (زُلْفَىٰ)

ز ل ف also concerns nearness, but its Qur’anic uses can involve bringing close, standing and proximity.

Study of Z L F

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:90 and Qāf 50:31 express the Garden being brought near to the mindful with a verb from this root.

Both verses use the passive verb أُزْلِفَتْ for the Garden being brought near. أَزِفَتِ in Al-Najm 53:57, by comparison, is intransitive and reports the event itself drawing near.

زُلْفَىٰ in some verses likewise concerns nearness and standing, rather than simply an appointed time arriving.

Despite their semantic proximity, أ ز ف and ز ل ف are independent roots. Similarity in sound or a shared idea of nearness should not merge them.

أ ز ف (أَزِفَ) and ز ف ف (زَفَّ)

These roots are close in sound, but differ in structure and meaning.

ز ف ف occurs in Al-Ṣāffāt 37:94:

فَأَقْبَلُوا إِلَيْهِ يَزِفُّونَ

fa-aqbalū ilayhi yaziffūn

“Then they came toward him in haste.”

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:94

يَزِفُّونَ belongs to ز ف ف and concerns swift movement.

Study of Z F F

أَزِفَتِ in Al-Najm 53:57, however, belongs to أ ز ف and concerns the approaching time of occurrence.

Someone’s haste may bring that person closer to a destination, but that relationship does not establish a common root for the two words.

زَفَّ has other Arabic uses, including escorting a bride in a wedding procession. These should not be imported into the meaning of al-āzifah either.

أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ therefore does not mean that the Resurrection moves swiftly like a caravan or a group of people.

أ ز ف (أَزِفَ) and أ ت ي (أَتَىٰ)

أَتَىٰ concerns coming and arriving. In some constructions it can express an affair’s occurrence or arrival.

Study of A T Y

Al-Naḥl 16:1 describes the divine command or affair with a perfect verb from أ ت ي, while the continuation tells people not to seek to hasten it.

This construction has a noteworthy relationship with أَزِفَتِ: both passages speak of an anticipated event in language of assurance and approach.

The verbs nevertheless differ.

أَزِفَ expresses drawing near; أَتَىٰ basically concerns coming and arriving. In each verse, we must establish whether the verb describes an accomplished occurrence or is used rhetorically to express a future event’s certainty.

Verbs concerning the Resurrection should therefore not all receive the same lexical translation merely because they share a subject matter.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

For أَزِفَ, the principal English equivalent is “draw near.”

In general Arabic use, “an appointed time drawing near,” “the time of departure approaching” and “the time arriving” may be suitable according to context.

“Arriving” needs care because it may suggest an event beginning or having fully come. Where the distinction between approach and occurrence matters, “drawing near” is clearer.

Two renderings of Al-Najm 53:57 are defensible.

A direct rendering preserving the repeated root:

“The approaching event has drawn near.”

A smoother rendering identifying the contextual referent:

“The Resurrection has drawn near.”

The first better displays the verse’s wording. The second is more natural for a reader seeking the sentence’s sense, but supplies “the Resurrection” from context.

In Ghāfir 40:18, يَوْمَ الْآزِفَةِ can be rendered “the day of the approaching event.” “The day of the approaching Resurrection” likewise clarifies the contextual meaning.

Renderings such as “the very near promised day,” “the last day arriving tomorrow” or “the day whose exact time has arrived” introduce more than the root itself conveys.

Al-āzifah alone gives no count of the remaining days, years or generations.

“The terrifying day” alone is also insufficient. The terror in Ghāfir 40:18 comes from the verse’s other elements; al-āzifah directly expresses being approaching.

Translation should therefore first preserve nearness, then clarify the Resurrection as referent and the warning in a contextual explanation.

8. Conclusion

The Arabic root أ ز ف concerns the approaching time of an affair. The early use أَزِفَ الرَّحِيلُ shows that departure may draw near while the caravan has not yet moved.

Some Arabic uses also concern a person hurrying or parts being close and compact. Its Qur’anic use, however, focuses on an event’s temporal nearness.

In Al-Najm 53:57, أَزِفَتِ الْآزِفَةُ repeats the root in verb and subject, highlighting the great event of resurrection drawing near. In Ghāfir 40:18, يَوْمَ الْآزِفَةِ names the day when people face the reality of reckoning and the consequences of their actions.

Drawing near is distinct from occurring. Nearness is also distinct from knowing the precise time of occurrence. The Qur’an speaks of the Resurrection’s nearness, but the expression cannot yield a particular calendar for the world’s end.

The idea of drawing near should nevertheless not disappear from translation or be replaced solely by certainty of occurrence. Being near and being certain are related but distinct meanings.

In the Qur’an, أ ز ف participates in a warning about an event people should not imagine so distant that they forget their responsibility. This message emerges from the verses’ context; the root’s basic meaning remains drawing near.

Forqan’s final definition of the root أ ز ف:

“The appointed time of an affair or event drawing near, without the event itself necessarily having occurred. In the Qur’an, أَزِفَتْ reports drawing near, and الْآزِفَة names the approaching event of the Resurrection. Without specifying an exact interval, this nearness places its occurrence within the setting of warning and human responsibility.”
