Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root M W T

Death, the quieting or stopping of active life, departure from worldly living, and transition from a living active state into a condition where known life no longer functions

Important Forms from This Root

مَاتَ / يَمُوتُ (māta / yamūtu)

māta / yamūtu; died, dies, fell out of active life, or passed out of a level of living.

مَوْت (mawt)

mawt; death, mortality, or the quieting of active life.

مَيِّت / مَيْت (mayyit / mayt)

mayyit / mayt; one who is to die, or something already in the state of death or life-quieting.

أَمْوَات / مِيتَة (amwāt / maytah)

amwāt; dead ones or beings in a state of mawt. Maytah; carrion, an animal dead outside proper slaughter.

أَمَاتَ / يُمِيتُ / مُمِيت (amāta / yumītu / mumīt)

amāta / yumītu / mumīt; caused to die, brings into death, causes death, or the One who causes death.

Method Note

The root M W T should be read more carefully than the English word “death” may first suggest. In modern English, death can sound like complete ending, disappearance, or absolute non-being. In the Qur’an, مَوْت (mawt) can mean the end of worldly life, but it does not always mean absolute nonexistence. The Qur’an speaks of human beings as أَمْوَات (amwāt) before worldly life, then of The God causing them to die, then of Him giving them life again and bringing them back in return.

For Forqan, it is therefore useful not to flatten mawt into one narrow idea. In Qur’anic usage, mawt often names a condition of being: active, manifest, biological, worldly, or guiding life has stopped at a certain level. Something may be mayt, yet still remain within The God’s order of existence, revival, account, and return.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of M W T is the stopping or quieting of active life. Life is appearance, motion, growth, perception, effect, and active presence at a level of existence. Mawt means that this active life has been removed from that level. But removal of active life is not the same as absolute annihilation.

A seed in the soil may appear still and quiet, while the possibility of growth remains hidden within it. Dead earth can be revived by water. A human being dies, yet the Qur’an speaks of resurrection and return. In this sense, mawt is a stage-word. It may end one phase without ending the whole reality of the being.

Material Image

The material image is a body or land from which the signs of life have gone quiet. A dead body no longer breathes, moves, responds, or acts within the field of worldly living. Dead land is dry, barren, and still. Yet the Qur’an uses that very dead land as an image of revival: when water descends, motion, growth, and visible life return.

In this image, matter has not necessarily vanished. What has subsided is active force and living function. This is important for understanding mawt: it is the quieting of active life, not necessarily the destruction of matter, possibility, or relation to The God.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, مَوْت (mawt) stands opposite حَيَاة (ḥayāh), life, but its use is not limited to human beings and animals. Arabic can speak of dead land, a dead fire, a wind that has died down, a dead heart, or a condition of stillness and loss of movement. The field is the loss of motion, warmth, growth, or active effect.

Within this field, مِيتَة (maytah) refers to an animal that has fallen into death outside proper slaughter. أَمْوَات (amwāt) can refer to the dead after worldly life, and in some Qur’anic contexts to a condition before worldly life. Translating mawt as “death” is often correct, but if the reader hears “death” as absolute nothingness, explanation is needed.

There is also overlap between مَيِّت (mayyit) and مَيْت (mayt). Still, one helpful tendency can be noticed: mayyit may point to a living being who is on the way to death and will die, as in إِنَّكَ مَيِّتٌ وَإِنَّهُم مَّيِّتُونَ (innaka mayyitun wa-innahum mayyitūn). Mayt may point more to something already in a state of death or life-quieting, such as dead land or a person who was dead in guidance and then revived. This distinction should not be made absolute, but it can help with context.

Qur’anic Usage

Mawt Before Worldly Life

In كَيْفَ تَكْفُرُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَكُنتُمْ أَمْوَاتًا فَأَحْيَاكُمْ ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ (kayfa takfurūna billāhi wakuntum amwātan fa-aḥyākum thumma yumītukum thumma yuḥyīkum thumma ilayhi turjaʿūn), the Qur’an presents the human being in a sequence: amwāt, life, death, life again, and return 2:28. The verse does not say, “you were nothing.” It says, “you were amwāt.” That wording names a condition, not necessarily absolute non-being.

Here, amwāt can point to the human state before emergence into worldly life: before the body, historical experience, personal memory, and practical responsibility. Then The God gives life, brings the human being into the field of experience and choice, causes death, gives life again, and finally brings the human being back to Himself.

Two Deaths and Two Lives

The verse قَالُوا رَبَّنَا أَمَتَّنَا اثْنَتَيْنِ وَأَحْيَيْتَنَا اثْنَتَيْنِ (qālū rabbanā amattanā ithnatayni wa-aḥyaytanā ithnatayn) strengthens the structure of al-Baqarah 2:28 by speaking of two causings-to-die and two givings-of-life 40:11. If death meant only absolute nothingness, counting it alongside life would become difficult.

This verse matters for Forqan because it shows that mawt and life in the Qur’an are not only simple biological concepts. They are countable stages in the human path. The human being passes through different conditions of death and life within The God’s order.

Mawt as the End of Worldly Life

In many verses, mawt is the end of worldly life. In كُلُّ نَفْسٍ ذَائِقَةُ الْمَوْتِ (kullu nafsin dhāʾiqatu al-mawt), every self is described as tasting death 3:185. The word “tasting” is important. Death is something encountered and passed through. The verse does not say every self is annihilated; it says every self tastes mawt.

Tasting death means entering an unavoidable experience beyond human control. This death ends the opportunity of worldly action, but it does not end the human being’s relation to The God. The continuation of the verse speaks of reward, account, and final outcome.

All Are on the Way to Mawt

In إِنَّكَ مَيِّتٌ وَإِنَّهُم مَّيِّتُونَ (innaka mayyitun wa-innahum mayyitūn), The God says to the Messenger: you will die, and they will die 39:30. The form mayyit is used while the Messenger is alive. It reminds us that mawt is not only the final moment; it is a law already shadowing worldly life.

In translation, one may say, “You will die, and they will die.” A more structural rendering would be, “You are on the way to mawt, and they are on the way to mawt.” The first is smoother; the second preserves more of the word’s force.

The God Causes Death and Gives Life

In many verses, يُمِيتُ (yumīt) appears beside يُحْيِي (yuḥyī): وَاللَّهُ يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ (wallāhu yuḥyī wayumīt); The God gives life and causes death 3:156. This pairing shows that life and death both belong to The God’s act. He is not only the Creator of the beginning; He is the One who changes states of being.

In this structure, causing death means bringing a being into the state of mawt, and giving life means bringing it into the state of life. The creature does not independently control its own state. The One who gives life also places death in its proper position, and the One who causes death can give life again.

Dead Earth and Revival

In وَتَرَى الْأَرْضَ هَامِدَةً فَإِذَا أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْهَا الْمَاءَ اهْتَزَّتْ وَرَبَتْ (watarā al-arḍa hāmidatan fa-idhā anzalnā ʿalayhā al-māʾa ihtazzat warabat), the Qur’an shows the earth as still and lifeless, then stirred by water 22:5. Elsewhere, the same meaning appears with the language of dead earth: فَأَحْيَيْنَا بِهِ الْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا (fa-aḥyaynā bihi al-arḍa baʿda mawtihā); We gave life to the earth after its death 35:9.

This natural image is important. The Qur’an speaks of dead earth, but the earth has not been annihilated. Soil remains, the ground remains, possibility remains; only growing life is quiet. When water comes, the earth moves and brings forth growth. This helps us understand that mawt is not always total removal. It can be the quieting of active life until the command of revival arrives.

Guidance-Death and Inner Life

The Qur’an sometimes speaks of deadness and life at the level of guidance and recognition. أَوَمَن كَانَ مَيْتًا فَأَحْيَيْنَاهُ وَجَعَلْنَا لَهُ نُورًا يَمْشِي بِهِ فِي النَّاسِ (awaman kāna maytan fa-aḥyaynāhu wajaʿalnā lahu nūran yamshī bihi fī al-nās) is a clear example 6:122. A person was mayt, then The God gave him life and placed for him a light by which he walks among people.

This is not bodily death. The person was already walking in society and living outwardly, but inwardly he lacked guiding life and light. His revival is not bodily revival, but guidance-life: recognition, light, direction, and the ability to move among people with discernment.

The Dead and Hearing

In verses such as إِنَّكَ لَا تُسْمِعُ الْمَوْتَىٰ (innaka lā tusmiʿu al-mawtā), mawt appears in the context of hearing and invitation 27:80. The meaning is not only that physically dead people do not hear. The context concerns people who have become unreceptive to the call of truth. They are like the dead because the route of receiving the message no longer functions in them.

This use belongs near roots such as Ṣ M M, B K M, and AYN M Y. The body may be alive, but the ear that receives truth and the heart that responds are not alive. Mawt can therefore be an ethical and perceptive condition, not only a biological event.

Maytah and Food-Law

In حُرِّمَتْ عَلَيْكُمُ الْمَيْتَةُ (ḥurrimat ʿalaykumu al-maytah), maytah refers to carrion: an animal that has fallen out of life and whose eating is forbidden 5:3. This shows that mawt has practical consequences even at the physical and legal level.

Here, death is tied to corruption, loss of food-purity, and the absence of the proper way of benefiting from the animal. Even in food-law, mawt is not only biological information; it changes the legal and practical relationship.

Mawt and Return

In al-Baqarah 2:28, the sequence ends with ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ (thumma ilayhi turjaʿūn); then to Him you are returned. Return means going back, not moving toward a destination unrelated to the beginning. This matters for mawt. The human being passes from one condition to another, but the whole path remains defined in relation to the same Origin.

If mawt were absolute annihilation, return would lose much of its meaning. The Qur’an places death inside the path of return. The human being passes through mawt and life, but finally returns to the Lord. Mawt is not the wall of final ending; it is a gate of changed state on the path of return.

Difference from Nearby Words

Halāk is destruction or collapse of a person, people, or structure. It may include death, but it is not identical to mawt. Qatl is killing: a death caused through an external act, aggression, or battle. Every killing leads to death, but not every death is killing.

Wafāh is full taking or receiving of the self. In the Qur’an, wafāh is connected with sleep: اللَّهُ يَتَوَفَّى الْأَنفُسَ حِينَ مَوْتِهَا وَالَّتِي لَمْ تَمُتْ فِي مَنَامِهَا (allāhu yatawaffā al-anfusa ḥīna mawtihā wallatī lam tamut fī manāmihā); The God takes the selves at the time of their death, and those that have not died in their sleep 39:42. This verse shows that wafāh and mawt are not identical, though they meet in worldly death. Wafāh concerns taking the self; mawt concerns the stopping of life at a level.

Nawm is sleep. Sleep is not death, though it is connected to a temporary taking of the self. Jumūd is stiffness or lack of movement, but not necessarily death. Sukūn is rest or stillness, and can occur in a living being. Mawt is the quieting of active life, and depending on context it may be biological, natural, perceptive, social, or existential.

Ṣaʿq should also be distinguished from mawt. Ṣaʿq may lead to death, but it is not always death. In the story of Moses, ṣaʿq is closer to unconscious collapse from an overwhelming encounter. Mawt is the broader law of life being quieted at a level; ṣaʿq is the effect of a strike, intensity, or disabling event.

Translation Method

When translating verses of mawt, the translator must avoid making “death” sound like absolute annihilation. In many verses, “death” is natural and correct. But in foundational verses such as al-Baqarah 2:28 and Ghāfir 40:11, it can be better to preserve “mawt” or explain it as a state.

For كُنتُمْ أَمْوَاتًا (kuntum amwātan), “you were dead” may confuse an English reader, because the question immediately arises: how were human beings dead before birth? A more careful rendering is: “you were in a state of mawt,” or “you lacked active worldly life.” This stays closer to the wording and does not close the Qur’anic field too quickly.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

كُنتُمْ أَمْوَاتًا (kuntum amwātan): you were in a state of mawt; you lacked active worldly life.

فَأَحْيَاكُمْ (fa-aḥyākum): then He gave you life; then He brought you into life.

ثُمَّ يُمِيتُكُمْ (thumma yumītukum): then He causes you to die; then He brings you into the state of mawt.

ثُمَّ يُحْيِيكُمْ (thumma yuḥyīkum): then He gives you life again; then He brings you into another life.

إِنَّكَ مَيِّتٌ وَإِنَّهُم مَّيِّتُونَ (innaka mayyitun wa-innahum mayyitūn): you will die, and they will die; you are on the way to mawt, and they are on the way to mawt.

كُلُّ نَفْسٍ ذَائِقَةُ الْمَوْتِ (kullu nafsin dhāʾiqatu al-mawt): every self tastes mawt; every self will taste death.

فَأَحْيَيْنَا بِهِ الْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا (fa-aḥyaynā bihi al-arḍa baʿda mawtihā): with it We gave life to the earth after its mawt; We revived the earth after its life had gone quiet.

أَوَمَن كَانَ مَيْتًا فَأَحْيَيْنَاهُ (awaman kāna maytan fa-aḥyaynāhu): is one who was dead, then We gave him life; is one who was in the quieting of guidance-life, then We revived him.

إِنَّكَ لَا تُسْمِعُ الْمَوْتَىٰ (innaka lā tusmiʿu al-mawtā): you do not make the dead hear; you cannot make those in a state of dead reception hear.

Summary

The root M W T in the Qur’an is not only the end of worldly life. Mawt is the quieting or stopping of active life at a level of existence. Sometimes that level is the body, sometimes land, sometimes the heart and its capacity to receive, and sometimes a stage in the human being’s path. Life, in contrast, is not only breathing; it is appearance, movement, light, response, and active reality.

In Forqan’s reading, mawt should be understood as one state in the path of the human being, not absolute annihilation. The human being is described as being in a state of mawt before worldly life, then given life, then caused to die, then given life again, and finally returned to The God. Mawt is therefore not a wall; it is a stage in the sequence of life, causing-death, giving-life, and return.
