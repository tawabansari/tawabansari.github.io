From intense heat and dark smoke to closeness and intimate relationships

The Arabic root ح م م has several familiar semantic fields. حَمِيم denotes very hot, scalding water; حُمَم concerns charcoal and black remnants of burning; another حَمِيم denotes an intimate friend or close relation. يَحْمُوم also describes intensely black smoke or things of pronounced darkness.

These uses resemble one another formally but cannot be reduced to one meaning without evidence. Water’s heat, charcoal’s blackness, and human closeness are different characteristics. Some may have historical or figurative connections, but Arabic evidence must establish them, rather than imaginative accounts of affection’s warmth or friendship’s fire.

The root occurs 21 times in 20 Qur’anic verses. Fourteen occurrences of حَمِيم concern very hot water or liquid; six concern an intimate friend or close person, including twice in Al-Maʿārij 70:10. يَحْمُوم occurs once for intensely black smoke.

The distinction matters: one verse describes something given to drink whose heat causes injury; another describes someone intimately related to another person. Construction and accompanying words identify the intended sense.

Qur’anic forms: حَمِيم in different case forms, definite and indefinite, occurs 20 times; يَحْمُومٍ occurs once.

Arabic also records حَمَّ, حَمَّمَ, حُمَم, حُمَمَة, حُمَّى, حَمَّة, حَمِيمَة, حَامَّة, حَمَّام, حِمَام, أَحَمّ, and اسْتَحَمَّ, but these exact derivatives do not occur in the Qur’an. حَمْحَمَ is also discussed in these lexical entries; morphologically, however, it is the reduplicated quadriliteral ح م ح م.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: 21 occurrences, ḥamīm and yaḥmūm

1. Semantic Fields and Early Arabic Uses

Heat and very hot water

Ibn Fāris identifies heat as one principle of ح م م in Maqāyīs al-Lughah.

Al-ʿAyn and Lisān al-ʿArab define حَمِيم as hot water. حَمَّ الْمَاءُ is recorded for water becoming hot; another vocalization has حُمَّ الْمَاءُ. Transitive حَمَّ الْمَاءَ is recorded for heating it and must be distinguished from حَمَّمَ in the sense of blackening a face.

In fire and heat contexts, حَمِيم can denote water that has become extremely hot.

Heat must nevertheless be distinguished from boiling. The word concerns heat; a precise temperature and whether boiling occurs require further contextual evidence.

Expressions such as غَلْيِ الْحَمِيمِ in Al-Dukhān 44:46 and حَمِيمٍ آنٍ in Al-Raḥmān 55:44 further clarify the intensity.

Ordinary Arabic could use حَمِيم for hot water prepared for washing or everyday use. Its lexical meaning is therefore not inherently punishment.

In punishment passages, context and accompanying descriptions identify an intensely hot, harmful liquid.

Lisān al-ʿArab: hot water, becoming hot, and blackening

حَمَّام and bathing

حَمَّام denotes a place for washing, particularly one providing hot water.

Early اسْتَحَمَّ originally concerned washing with hot water; its use broadened to washing with water even when not necessarily very hot.

A derivative’s ordinary usage can thus extend beyond its initial image.

حَمَّة also denotes a hot spring whose heated water emerges from the ground.

Early accounts describe such springs used for washing and sometimes to treat illnesses.

Therapeutic use is not inherent in the root; it concerns people’s experience and purpose in using water.

حُمَّى: bodily heat

حُمَّى denotes an illness or condition accompanied by increased bodily heat.

مَحْمُوم describes someone suffering a fever.

It relates to heat but should not become every illness or unpleasant sensation.

حُمَّى also resembles حِمَى in writing, but the two must remain distinct. حِمَى, a protected or restricted place, belongs to ح م ي.

No Qur’anic حَمِيم means fever or illness.

Sweat and intense heat

Some lexical accounts also use حَمِيم for sweat.

This relates to bodily heat and the liquid emerging through the skin during heat or activity.

Lisān al-ʿArab records expressions for people and animals sweating within this family.

اسْتَحَمَّ therefore did not exclusively concern washing in every early usage; it could also mean sweating.

That lexical sense does not establish that حَمِيم in descriptions of the Fire’s drink means bodily sweat.

In Muḥammad 47:15, مَاء accompanies حَمِيم and identifies the intended substance.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ, Lisān al-ʿArab, and Tāj al-ʿArūs: bathing, fever, and sweat

حُمَم: charcoal and black remains of burning

Another branch concerns blackness.

حُمَم denotes charcoal and black remnants of burning; its singular is حُمَمَة.

Ibn Fāris identifies blackness as a separate principle of the family.

حَمَّمَ وَجْهَهُ can mean blackening a face with soot or charcoal.

أَحَمّ can likewise describe something intensely dark or black.

Darkness can accompany fire and burning, but not every black thing has been burned.

Black human hair or an animal’s dark feathers can be described through this family without fire causing them.

Blackness should therefore not be treated as the direct result of burning in every derivative.

يَحْمُوم: intense darkness

يَحْمُوم is recorded for intensely black smoke.

Other uses apply it to very dark things, including an animal, plant, or black horse.

Lisān al-ʿArab mentions a horse named Yaḥmūm, its name associated with its intensely dark colour.

The word was therefore not exclusively the name of one particular smoke in Arabic.

In Al-Wāqiʿah 56:43, however, fire and punishment favour intensely black smoke.

It does not itself mean cool shade, tranquillity, or refuge. When accompanied by ظِلّ, their relationship must be examined within the complete construction.

حَمِيم: an intimate friend or close relation

Another branch uses حَمِيم for someone very close: an intimate friend or close relative.

In Al-ʿAyn, Al-Khalīl relates it to someone connected through closeness, affection, and concern.

Lisān al-ʿArab likewise describes one’s حَمِيم as a close person for whom one cares and who cares in return.

حَامَّة also denotes someone’s particular intimates, including family and relatives.

Closeness can therefore arise through kinship or deep affection and friendship.

Fuṣṣilat 41:34 is important: someone formerly hostile can come to resemble a وَلِيٌّ حَمِيمٌ, an intimate ally.

حَمِيم in this branch is therefore not confined to blood relations.

Does the friend sense derive from affection’s warmth?

Al-Rāghib explains in Al-Mufradāt that حَمِيم as an intimate can relate to intense affection and concern for relatives, as though someone hastens warmly and earnestly to protect them.

The semantic image is understandable; many languages also describe friendship and affection through warmth.

Resemblance alone does not establish that intimate friendship historically derived from hot water or fire in Arabic.

Ibn Fāris treats nearness and presence as a field alongside heat and blackness.

A possible connection between heat and affection should therefore be presented as a lexical explanation, rather than the certain origin of every use.

Al-Rāghib’s Al-Mufradāt: heat, closeness, and the proposed derivation through affection

Further branches: approaching, intending, and sound

Ibn Fāris also records other usages.

أَحَمَّ الْأَمْرُ can describe an affair approaching or becoming imminent.

حُمَّ كَذَا can concern something being decreed or coming due. حِمَام, death or the fate of death, also occurs in this field.

حَمْحَمَة names a sound, particularly a horse’s sound in situations such as asking for feed. حَمْحَمَ is morphologically quadriliteral; its placement alongside ح م م in some dictionaries does not itself make it a triliteral derivative.

The associated lexical entries therefore extend beyond the three Qur’anic senses, while a neighbouring quadriliteral form must remain distinct from triliteral derivatives.

حَمَام also names birds of the pigeon and dove family. Its unvowelled spelling shared with حَمَّام, bathhouse, or حِمَام, death, does not merge their meanings.

These exact forms do not occur as derivatives of the root under discussion in the Qur’an.

2. Early Witnesses: Blackness, Heat, and Closeness

Charcoal’s blackness in Ṭarafah ibn al-ʿAbd

The pre-Islamic poet Ṭarafah ibn al-ʿAbd says:

أَشَجَاكَ الرَّبْعُ أَمْ قِدَمُهْ
أَمْ رَمَادٌ دَارِسٌ حُمَمُهْ

a-shajāka al-rabʿu am qidamuh / am ramādun dārisun ḥumamuh

Meaning: Has the abandoned dwelling saddened you, or its age, or ashes whose charcoal has weathered and faded? دَارِس concerns traces becoming effaced and indistinct, without necessarily denying every surviving remnant.

حُمَم denotes coals and remnants of burning.

The poet describes an old dwelling whose habitation and fire have left traces.

The witness shows حُمَم established for black remains of fire in pre-Islamic poetry.

It does not make the word mean grief, ruin, or passing time. Those arise from the poem’s whole image and the abandoned dwelling; حُمَم names the remaining material.

Ashʿār al-Shuʿarāʾ al-Sittah: Ṭarafah’s charcoal witness

Midday heat in Arabic poetry

A verse attributed to Abū Kabīr al-Hudhalī reads:

وَلَقَدْ رَبَأْتُ إِذَا الصِّحَابُ تَوَاكَلُوا
حَمَّ الظَّهِيرَةِ فِي الْيَفَاعِ الْأَطْوَلِ

wa-la-qad rabaʾtu idhā al-ṣiḥābu tawākalū / ḥamma al-ẓahīrati fī al-yafāʿi al-aṭwali

Meaning: When my companions left the watch to one another, I kept lookout at the height of midday heat on the tallest elevation.

حَمَّ الظَّهِيرَةِ concerns the height and intensity of midday heat.

It belongs to the heat field without meaning hot water itself.

Nor is heat equally intense in every form: some usages concern ordinary hot water, others extreme heat. A جَمَّ الظَّهِيرَةِ transmission is also recorded for this verse; the present quotation follows the ḥāʾ reading.

Al-Muḥkam: Abū Kabīr’s witness with ḥāʾ

Closeness and concern for another

Arabic used حَمِيم for a relative or friend closely connected to someone.

Al-Farrāʾ explains وَلَا يَسْأَلُ حَمِيمٌ حَمِيمًا through close kinship.

Al-Jawharī likewise identifies one’s حَمِيم as a close person whose affairs matter to one.

The relationship itself is prominent, rather than a particular act such as feeding, defending, or requesting help.

An intimate friend may help another, but helping is not the word’s inherent meaning.

Kinship and friendship also differ. Blood relations may lack emotional intimacy; intimacy may exist between people who are not relatives.

Intimate friend and close relative are therefore alternatives selected according to context.

Lisān al-ʿArab: Al-Farrāʾ and Al-Jawharī on the intimate

3. حَمِيم as Very Hot Water in the Qur’an

Fourteen Qur’anic occurrences concern very hot water or liquid. All occur in punishment contexts, but their uses differ.

It can be a drink, something poured over heads, a comparison for another substance’s boiling, or something between which and the Fire people move.

Al-Anʿām 6:70 and Yūnus 10:4: a scalding drink

Al-Anʿām 6:70 and Yūnus 10:4 both have شَرَابٌ مِنْ حَمِيمٍ.

شَرَاب names what is drunk; حَمِيم characterizes the liquid through its heat.

Both passages place the drink beside painful punishment.

حَمِيم therefore concerns intensely hot water or liquid in these usages.

The root itself does not mean drinking or punishment. شَرَاب supplies drinking; the other clauses supply punishment’s circumstances.

Nor does every hot water become punishment in Arabic. Warm water can be useful in ordinary life; the severity and context make this a harmful drink.

Al-Ḥajj 22:19: poured over heads

Al-Ḥajj 22:19 reads:

يُصَبُّ مِنْ فَوْقِ رُءُوسِهِمُ الْحَمِيمُ

yuṣabbu min fawqi ruʾūsihimu al-ḥamīmu

Meaning: The intensely hot liquid is poured over their heads.

الْحَمِيمُ is definite and the subject of passive يُصَبُّ.

صَبّ concerns pouring liquid, consistent with very hot water or liquid here.

Al-Ḥajj 22:20, the following verse, describes its effect upon what is in the bellies and upon the skin.

The connection indicates the heat’s severity.

حَمِيم itself does not mean skin-burning, belly-tearing, or an instrument of punishment; the complete passages express these consequences.

Muḥammad 47:15: hot water’s bodily effects

After describing the Garden’s blessings, Muḥammad 47:15 says of the contrasting group:

وَسُقُوا مَاءً حَمِيمًا فَقَطَّعَ أَمْعَاءَهُمْ

wa-suqū māʾan ḥamīman fa-qaṭṭaʿa amʿāʾahum

Meaning: They were given intensely hot water to drink, which tore apart their intestines.

This clearly establishes the Qur’anic heat-related sense.

مَاءً explicitly names water; حَمِيمًا describes it.

Unlike usages where حَمِيم independently names the liquid, the adjectival construction is explicit here.

فَقَطَّعَ أَمْعَاءَهُمْ expresses its effect.

The water therefore has intensely damaging heat.

The verse does not establish a numerical temperature, chemical composition, or precise biological mechanism for the hereafter. It describes punishment’s severity and effect rather than laboratory measurements.

Earlier in the verse, unstaled water, milk, delightful wine, and purified honey in the Garden contrast with this water.

The drinks and their effects contribute to the contrast between the two conditions.

Al-Dukhān 44:46: boiling hot water

كَغَلْيِ الْحَمِيمِ

ka-ghalyi al-ḥamīmi

Translation: Like the boiling of intensely hot water.

The preceding verses mention شَجَرَةَ الزَّقُّومِ, the tree of Zaqqūm.

Its condition within the bellies is then compared with حَمِيم’s boiling.

غَلْي comes from غ ل ي and means boiling.

الْحَمِيم names the hot substance whose boiling supplies the comparison.

Treating every حَمِيم as the act of boiling itself would erase غَلْي’s contribution.

Al-Dukhān 44:48 also has عَذَابِ الْحَمِيمِ, punishment associated with intensely hot liquid.

The expression does not make حَمِيم the general name of every punishment. It identifies a particular punishment involving the liquid.

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:67, Ṣād 38:57, and Ghāfir 40:72

شَوْبًا مِنْ حَمِيمٍ in Al-Ṣāffāt 37:67 means an admixture of intensely hot liquid.

شَوْب belongs to another root and concerns mixing.

حَمِيم is thus something mixed with another substance.

Ṣād 38:57 places حَمِيمٌ وَغَسَّاقٌ together; غَسَّاق is independent and must not have its meaning transferred to حَمِيم.

Ghāfir 40:72, connected to the preceding verse, also describes being dragged through intensely hot liquid and then set ablaze in the Fire.

The Qur’an thus uses حَمِيم in related images without identifying it with every substance or condition of punishment.

Al-Raḥmān 55:44: حَمِيمٍ آنٍ

يَطُوفُونَ بَيْنَهَا وَبَيْنَ حَمِيمٍ آنٍ

yaṭūfūna baynahā wa-bayna ḥamīmin ānin

Translation: They move between it and intensely hot water whose heat has reached its utmost.

حَمِيمٍ is accompanied by آنٍ.

آنٍ belongs to أ ن ي and here concerns heat reaching its utmost.

The ultimate intensity is expressed by a separate adjective; آنٍ’s whole meaning should not be merged into حَمِيم.

Some translations use boiling water, conveying the general idea, but a word-by-word study should distinguish each form’s contribution.

Study of ʾ N Y

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:42, Al-Wāqiʿah 56:54, and Al-Wāqiʿah 56:93

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:42 reads:

فِي سَمُومٍ وَحَمِيمٍ

fī samūmin wa-ḥamīmin

Meaning: Amid a scorching wind and intensely hot water.

سَمُوم belongs to another root and concerns scorching wind or heat.

حَمِيم beside it identifies the hot liquid.

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:54 has فَشَارِبُونَ عَلَيْهِ مِنَ الْحَمِيمِ, expressing drinking it.

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:93 also has فَنُزُلٌ مِنْ حَمِيمٍ.

نُزُل concerns reception or what is prepared for someone’s arrival. Its combination with حَمِيم depicts a harmful reception.

Reception is supplied by نُزُل and the construction, rather than حَمِيم itself.

Al-Nabaʾ 78:25: حَمِيم and غَسَّاق

إِلَّا حَمِيمًا وَغَسَّاقًا

illā ḥamīman wa-ghassāqan

Translation: Except intensely hot water and ghassāq.

The two are independent words.

The preceding verse mentions tasting neither coolness nor drink.

حَمِيم therefore contrasts with coolness and desirable drink.

غَسَّاق must not be rendered hot water merely because it accompanies حَمِيم; its root requires separate study.

Study of GH S Q

Together, the 14 heat-related occurrences concern intensely hot liquid in punishment contexts. Accompanying verbs and descriptions, such as being given to drink, pouring, boiling, and tearing organs, further reveal its effects.

4. حَمِيم as an Intimate Friend or Close Person

Six further occurrences in five verses concern close human relationships.

The word refers to neither water, fire, nor bodily heat in these passages.

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:101: an intimate friend

وَلَا صَدِيقٍ حَمِيمٍ

wa-lā ṣadīqin ḥamīmin

Translation: Nor an intimate friend.

حَمِيم describes صَدِيق.

صَدِيق names the friend; حَمِيم emphasizes the relationship’s closeness and intimacy.

حَمِيم therefore does not mean hot water here.

Nor does it merely repeat صَدِيق: friendship can be distinguished from deeper intimacy.

Someone may have many friends and acquaintances but an especially close relationship with only some.

صَدِيقٍ حَمِيمٍ expresses that distinction.

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:100, the preceding verse, mentions the lack of intercessors. Intercession and intimate friendship occur together but differ in root and role.

حَمِيم concerns the close person; intercession comes from another family.

Ghāfir 40:18: no intimate or accepted intercessor

وَأَنْذِرْهُمْ يَوْمَ الْآزِفَةِ إِذِ الْقُلُوبُ لَدَى الْحَنَاجِرِ كَاظِمِينَ مَا لِلظَّالِمِينَ مِنْ حَمِيمٍ وَلَا شَفِيعٍ يُطَاعُ

wa-andhirhum yawma al-āzifati idhi al-qulūbu ladā al-ḥanājiri kāẓimīna mā li-l-ẓālimīna min ḥamīmin wa-lā shafīʿin yuṭāʿu

Translation: Warn them of the approaching Day, when hearts are at the throats and they suppress their anguish. The wrongdoers have neither an intimate nor an intercessor whose plea is accepted.

حَمِيم denotes a friend or close person.

The verse describes such a person’s absence for wrongdoers in this scene.

شَفِيع follows, belonging to another root and expressing intercession’s role.

The concepts can be close in life because an intimate may offer support or help, but friendship itself is not intercession.

يُطَاعُ describes the intercessor, rather than حَمِيم.

That grammatical distinction should be retained.

The verse cannot redefine حَمِيم as someone empowered to rescue another from punishment.

It identifies the close person; absence of help and accepted intercession belongs to the complete statement.

Fuṣṣilat 41:34: closeness after enmity

وَلَا تَسْتَوِي الْحَسَنَةُ وَلَا السَّيِّئَةُ ادْفَعْ بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ فَإِذَا الَّذِي بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُ عَدَاوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُ وَلِيٌّ حَمِيمٌ

wa-lā tastawī al-ḥasanatu wa-lā al-sayyiʾatu idfaʿ bi-allatī hiya aḥsanu fa-idhā alladhī baynaka wa-baynahu ʿadāwatun ka-annahu waliyyun ḥamīmun

Translation: Good and evil are not equal. Repel with what is better; then the one between whom and you is enmity becomes as though an intimate ally.

This is a clear instance of emotional and social closeness.

Enmity between two people is described, followed by a command to respond to evil with something better.

The change is pictured as though the former enemy became an especially close friend.

وَلِيّ belongs to و ل ي, concerning closeness, connection, and support.

حَمِيم describes it and conveys intimacy and nearness.

The words complement one another without being complete synonyms.

The verse also has كَأَنَّهُ, as though he were.

That simile should survive translation: the relationship’s change is likened to an intimate friendship.

It does not establish that every enemy, under every condition and regardless of their conduct, necessarily becomes an actual friend.

Fuṣṣilat 41:35 also mentions the patience and great portion of those able to respond this way.

Responding better to evil is thus associated with restraint and patience.

حَمِيم itself does not mean patience, forgiveness, or ethical reform; these arise from accompanying acts and verses.

The passage also shows that حَمِيم is not restricted to kinship: a former enemy need have no blood relationship with the addressee.

Al-Ḥāqqah 69:35: no close friend

فَلَيْسَ لَهُ الْيَوْمَ هَاهُنَا حَمِيمٌ

fa-laysa lahu al-yawma hāhunā ḥamīmun

Translation: He has no close friend here today.

حَمِيم names a close person.

The preceding verses concern someone facing the outcome of their conduct.

The intimate’s absence is then stated.

This does not itself mean that no other humans exist in the hereafter’s scene; the person lacks a close or supportive friend there.

الْيَوْمَ and هَاهُنَا should be retained, anchoring the statement to its particular scene.

The word alone cannot determine all human social relationships in the hereafter.

Al-Maʿārij 70:10: one intimate not asking about another

وَلَا يَسْأَلُ حَمِيمٌ حَمِيمًا

wa-lā yasʾalu ḥamīmun ḥamīman

Translation: No close friend asks about their close friend.

This is the only verse with two occurrences of this root.

The first حَمِيمٌ is the subject of يَسْأَلُ; the second حَمِيمًا is its object.

Both concern close persons, rather than hot water.

Al-Maʿārij 70:11 has يُبَصَّرُونَهُمْ: they are shown one another. The verb is passive; it does not establish an absolute inability to see or recognize intimates.

This matters because verse 10 should not simply be taken to deny recognition or any possibility of seeing each other.

The following passage depicts the guilty person wishing to sacrifice even loved ones to escape punishment.

Failure to inquire about intimates relates to the intensity of distress and preoccupation with one’s own fate.

حَمِيم itself does not mean indifference or a broken relationship. It still names the close person; the verb and scene express the failure to act or ask.

Emotional or familial closeness therefore does not itself guarantee being able to help another in every situation.

Nor does this scene erase friendship and kinship throughout every stage of the hereafter. Extending its judgement requires examining other verses.

Al-Ṭabarī: seeing and recognizing intimates while not asking after them

5. يَحْمُوم: Black Smoke in Al-Wāqiʿah 56:43

وَظِلٍّ مِنْ يَحْمُومٍ

wa-ẓillin min yaḥmūmin

Translation: And shade of intensely black smoke.

This is the only Qur’anic يَحْمُوم.

Arabic records it for something intensely black, particularly dark smoke.

Ibn Fāris places it under blackness, relating it to charcoal and substances blackened by fire.

Lisān al-ʿArab also explains it as intensely black smoke.

It follows فِي سَمُومٍ وَحَمِيمٍ in Al-Wāqiʿah 56:42.

The three images are scorching wind, intensely hot liquid, and shade of dark smoke.

Why does ظِلّ not mean cool shade here?

ظِلّ denotes shade, without necessarily guaranteeing pleasantness or coolness.

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:44 immediately describes it:

لَا بَارِدٍ وَلَا كَرِيمٍ

lā bāridin wa-lā karīmin

Meaning: Neither cool nor pleasant and gracious.

The shade is therefore not a comforting refuge.

ظِلٍّ مِنْ يَحْمُومٍ depicts dense, dark smoky shade that provides neither ease nor coolness.

Conversely, يَحْمُوم should not become heat alone, erasing its blackness.

Heat is present in context, but the established usage concerns intense darkness and smoke.

Does يَحْمُوم mean fire?

Some exegetical explanations relate it to fire or smoke rising from fire.

The interpretation fits heat and punishment, but fire and smoke remain lexically distinct.

Fire can produce smoke; smoke is nevertheless not flame.

Intensely black smoke is therefore a more direct rendering.

The verse does not specify fuel, the quantity of smoke, or the mechanism of its production. It must not be confined to a particular chemical substance or fuel.

Maqāyīs al-Lughah: yaḥmūm within blackness

6. Distinctions from Nearby Roots and Words

ح م م (ḥamīm) and ح م ي (ḥamiya)

The heat-related uses of ح م ي concern becoming hot and intense heat.

ح م م also has a heat branch that includes حَمِيم.

Their root forms nevertheless differ.

عَيْنٍ حَامِيَةٍ, a reading in Al-Kahf 18:86, denotes a hot spring and belongs to ح م ي.

حَمِيم, conversely, concerns very hot water in relevant Qur’anic passages and close friends in others.

Shared heat imagery must not merge all derivatives of the two families.

ح م م (ḥamīm) and ح م أ (ḥamaʾ)

حَمَأ concerns dark mud and wet sediment.

حَمِيم’s heat branch concerns very hot water.

Hot water can naturally contain dark mud, but the characteristics remain lexically independent.

حَمِئَة in Al-Kahf 18:86 describes a muddy spring; حَمِيم in punishment passages describes intensely hot water.

حَمِيم should therefore not become muddy water, nor حَمْأَة boiling water.

Study of Ḥ M ʾ

ح م م (ḥamīm) and و د د (wudd)

وُدّ concerns affection and love.

The human sense of حَمِيم identifies a close and intimate person.

Both can occur in one relationship: an intimate friend ordinarily participates in affection and concern.

وُدّ names affection or loving itself; حَمِيم characterizes someone closely related to another.

حَمِيم in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:101 therefore cannot be rendered merely affection; an intimate friend is meant.

Study of W D D

ح م م (ḥamīm) and ص د ق (ṣadīq)

صَدِيق denotes a friend in Qur’anic usage.

حَمِيم can likewise describe someone close, but it qualifies صَدِيق in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:101.

It emphasizes the friendship’s degree of closeness and intimacy.

صَدِيقٌ حَمِيمٌ therefore contributes more than simply repeating two equivalent words.

Study of Ṣ D Q

ح م م (ḥamīm) and و ل ي (waliyy)

وَلِيّ relates in different usages to closeness, connection, support, and guardianship.

Fuṣṣilat 41:34 has وَلِيٌّ حَمِيمٌ.

حَمِيم characterizes the relationship’s closeness and intimacy.

It does not itself mean a ruler, guardian, or holder of authority.

The expression alone therefore cannot establish a particular legal or religious authority for that person.

Study of W L Y

ح م م (ḥamīm) and ش ف ع (shafīʿ)

شَفِيع belongs to ش ف ع and concerns intercession in Qur’anic usage.

The human sense of حَمِيم concerns a friend or intimate.

Ghāfir 40:18 places them together without making them identical.

An intimate may intervene or seek help for another in life, but closeness does not itself grant authority to intercede.

Nor does the absence of an intimate in one scene necessarily deny every relationship or request in all other scenes.

Intercession requires an independent study of its verses.

Study of SH F ʿ

ح م م (ḥamīm) and غ ل ي (ghaly)

غَلْي concerns boiling.

حَمِيم concerns hot water, intensely hot in punishment contexts.

Al-Dukhān 44:46 places both concepts together.

Lexical analysis should therefore assign boiling to غ ل ي and the material’s heat to ح م م.

This prevents assuming a precise physical boiling state for every حَمِيم irrespective of context.

ح م م (yaḥmūm) and د خ ن (dukhān)

دُخَان is the general name for smoke.

Qur’anic يَحْمُوم concerns intensely black smoke.

دُخَان can therefore name the substance generally, while يَحْمُوم emphasizes extreme darkness.

Intensely black smoke suits Al-Wāqiʿah 56:43.

Not every smoke in Arabic is therefore يَحْمُوم.

ح م م (ḥumam) and ر م د (ramād)

رَمَاد denotes ash.

حُمَم commonly concerns charcoal and black remains of burning.

Ṭarafah’s verse combines them in one image.

Ash and charcoal can occur together at an extinguished fire’s location while remaining different in substance and lexical name.

يَحْمُوم is related through intense darkness, but Al-Wāqiʿah 56:43 does not name actual ash or charcoal fragments.

ح م م (ḥamm) and the opening letters حم

Ghāfir, Fuṣṣilat, Al-Shūrā, Al-Zukhruf, Al-Dukhān, Al-Jāthiyah, and Al-Aḥqāf open with حم.

Their written resemblance does not make these opening letters occurrences of ح م م.

They are disconnected opening letters, differing in form and function from حَمِيم and يَحْمُوم.

They are therefore excluded from the root’s count of 21 occurrences.

7. Translation Proposals for Forqan

For the 14 heat-related occurrences, very hot water or, where appropriate, scalding water are the principal equivalents.

Muḥammad 47:15 explicitly names مَاء, making intensely hot water suitable for مَاءً حَمِيمًا.

الْحَمِيمُ in Al-Ḥajj 22:19 can be intensely hot water: pouring and the following verses identify its liquid nature and effect.

كَغَلْيِ الْحَمِيمِ in Al-Dukhān 44:46 is like the boiling of intensely hot water.

حَمِيمٍ آنٍ in Al-Raḥmān 55:44 is intensely hot water whose heat has reached its utmost, preserving both words’ contributions.

Very hot water also suits Al-Wāqiʿah 56:42 and Al-Nabaʾ 78:25 without incorporating the name of another accompanying liquid.

For people, intimate friend or very close person are appropriate.

صَدِيقٍ حَمِيمٍ in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:101 is an intimate friend.

مِنْ حَمِيمٍ in Ghāfir 40:18 is any close friend within the negative construction.

وَلِيٌّ حَمِيمٌ in Fuṣṣilat 41:34 is a close, intimate friend; كَأَنَّهُ should remain in the sentence.

حَمِيمٌ in Al-Ḥāqqah 69:35 suits close friend.

وَلَا يَسْأَلُ حَمِيمٌ حَمِيمًا in Al-Maʿārij 70:10 is no close friend asks about their close friend. Commentary can also include close relatives among its contextual referents.

يَحْمُومٍ in Al-Wāqiʿah 56:43 is intensely black smoke.

Cool shade is incorrect for that word: ظِلّ supplies shade, and the following verse denies coolness.

Outside the Qur’an, حُمَم is charcoal and black remains of fire; حُمَّى fever; حَمَّة a hot spring; حَمَّام a bathhouse; اسْتَحَمَّ washed; حَامَّة one’s particular intimates; and quadriliteral حَمْحَمَة a horse’s characteristic sound.

One invariant equivalent cannot suit all حَمِيم usages. Construction and what the word describes determine its sense.

Conclusion

Brief root meaning: In the heat branch, warm or intensely hot water; in the closeness branch, an intimate friend or relative; in the darkness branch, charcoal and dense black smoke.

The first branch concerns heat: حَمِيم denotes hot water and, in Qur’anic punishment contexts, water or liquid of extreme heat.

The second concerns blackness: حُمَم names charcoal and black remains of fire; Qur’anic يَحْمُوم denotes intensely black smoke.

The third concerns nearness and presence: حَمِيم is an intimate friend or very close relation.

Dictionaries also record fever, hot-water washing, sweat, approaching, and being decreed. The horse’s sound in quadriliteral حَمْحَمَة is discussed alongside this entry, but not all these forms are triliteral derivatives or Qur’anic forms of the root.

Its 21 Qur’anic occurrences belong to three principal fields: intensely hot water, a close person, and black smoke.

Fourteen concern hot water in punishment contexts; six concern human closeness; one depicts black smoke in punishment.

These usages show that an apparently comprehensive definition such as intensity or extreme heat cannot explain the whole family. It becomes either too vague or erases the independent characteristics of its branches.

Possible semantic connections between heat and affection can be discussed, but cannot become the certain historical origin of حَمِيم as an intimate friend without evidence.

Context is decisive in Qur’anic usage.

مَاءً حَمِيمًا describes intensely hot water; صَدِيقٍ حَمِيمٍ an intimate friend; ظِلٍّ مِنْ يَحْمُومٍ shade of dense black smoke.

Each remains distinct from accompanying words. Drinking, boiling, pouring, intercession, friendship, support, and shade occur within complete constructions, but not all are components of حَمِيم itself.

The root has three distinct Qur’anic senses: intense heat in حَمِيم, closeness and intimacy in حَمِيم, and intensely dark smoke in يَحْمُوم. Morphology, what the word describes, and context identify the meaning.

For Forqan, very hot water, intimate friend, and intensely black smoke are the principal equivalents.

Keeping these distinct preserves the direct meaning and prevents replacing the word with an unsupported symbolic interpretation, material image, or ethical conclusion.
