The Qur'anic Root Ḥ Y N

A time, moment, interval, period, deadline, or bounded segment within the flow of time.

Central Meaning

The root ح ي ن (ḥ-y-n) fundamentally points to time, a moment, an interval, a period, or a defined segment within the flow of time. This root does not present time as an entirely open and borderless expanse. Rather, it points to a piece or phase of time: a specific moment, a limited term, a defined period, or a moment in which an event settles.

The linguistic core of ح ي ن (ḥ-y-n) is the delimiting of a portion of time. Time in this root is not seen as an endless and unnamed flow, but as a part of that flow recognized through an event, a term, an expectation, a change of state, or an end. حِين (ḥīn) can be short or long; but its context usually gives it a ceiling and direction.

Qur'anic Form of the Root

The Qur'anic form of this root is highly concentrated. In the Qur'an, its main form is حِين (ḥīn). Sometimes it appears as a noun, as in إِلَىٰ حِين (ilā ḥīn) or حَتَّىٰ حِين (ḥattā ḥīn), and sometimes it functions adverbially, as in حِينَ تُمْسُونَ (ḥīna tumsūn), حِينَ تُصْبِحُونَ (ḥīna tuṣbiḥūn), حِينَ الْوَصِيَّةِ (ḥīna al-waṣiyyah), حِينَ مَوْتِهَا (ḥīna mawtihā), and حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ (ḥīni ghaflah). Therefore, the Qur'anic field of this root is understood through the very idea of “a time” and “a temporal interval.”

Material Image

The material image of this root may be seen in fruit reaching its proper time, the transition of day into night, the moment of bequest near death, or a period that continues until a result arrives. حِين (ḥīn) is not time without quality or boundary; it is time identified by a particular state or event.

Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic usage, حِين (ḥīn) is used for a time, moment, period, or interval of time. In some lexical layers, there is also a connection between حِين (ḥīn) and the arrival of a time at its point of occurrence or ending; in some usages, حِين (ḥīn) can even come close to destruction or the arrival of a decisive event. For the final version, if a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or early Arabic usage is found, this layer may be added with caution.

Provision and Settlement Until a Time

One important use of this root appears beside provision and settlement after Adam's descent:

وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

And for you on the earth there will be a settlement and provision until a time. Al-Baqarah 2:36

In this ayah, حِين (ḥīn) marks the time-limit of the human being's earthly provision and settlement. The earth is a place of life, but only until a time. Human provision is real, but only until a time. Thus حِين (ḥīn) here reminds the reader of the temporal boundary of the earthly stage.

The same structure appears in Al-A'raf:

وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

And for you on the earth there will be a settlement and provision until a time. Al-A'raf 7:24

The repetition of this phrase shows that earthly life is accompanied by a term. The human being has not been placed on earth to remain without end; the human being has been placed within an interval. حِين (ḥīn) names this interval without always stating its exact measure.

A distinction must be made between حِين (ḥīn) and أَجَل (ajal). أَجَل (ajal) more strongly points to the appointed end and deadline of a term, the point where the period ends. But حِين (ḥīn) is the interval or temporal segment itself. In إِلَىٰ حِين (ilā ḥīn), attention is not only on the ending moment, but on the period that extends toward that end.

A Term Until an End or Punishment

وَفِي ثَمُودَ إِذْ قِيلَ لَهُمْ تَمَتَّعُوا حَتَّىٰ حِينٍ

And in Thamud, when it was said to them: Enjoy yourselves until a time. Adh-Dhariyat 51:43

Here حَتَّىٰ حِين (ḥattā ḥīn) indicates a granted interval. Provision exists, but it has a ceiling. حِين (ḥīn) in this context is a period in which the opportunity for life and enjoyment still exists, but its end is near or certain.

The same logic appears in ayahs where the Prophet is commanded to turn away until a time:

فَتَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ حَتَّىٰ حِينٍ

So turn away from them until a time. As-Saffat 37:174

And again:

وَتَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ حَتَّىٰ حِينٍ

And turn away from them until a time. As-Saffat 37:178

In these ayahs, حِين (ḥīn) points to an interval that continues until the result is made clear or the command of The God arrives. Its measure is not stated, but it is not endless. Turning away is until حِين (ḥīn), meaning until a point known by The God.

Fruit at Every Time

تُؤْتِي أُكُلَهَا كُلَّ حِينٍ بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهَا

It gives its fruit at every time, by the permission of its Lord. Ibrahim 14:25

In this ayah, حِين (ḥīn) comes with كُلّ (kull). كُلَّ حِين (kulla ḥīn) means at every suitable period, every necessary interval, every time when fruit should appear. This usage is not merely a natural calendar. In the example of the good word, truth is alive and gives its proper fruit in different intervals of time. Every حِين (ḥīn) carries the capacity for a fresh fruit.

The Moment of Encountering Truth

لَوْ يَعْلَمُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا حِينَ لَا يَكُفُّونَ عَن وُجُوهِهِمُ النَّارَ

If those who disbelieved knew, when they will not be able to hold back the Fire from their faces... Al-Anbiya 21:39

Here حِين (ḥīn) is the time of contact with a decisive event: the moment when claim, denial, and escape no longer work.

Another ayah says:

وَسَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ حِينَ يَرَوْنَ الْعَذَابَ مَنْ أَضَلُّ سَبِيلًا

And they will come to know, when they see the punishment, who is more astray in path. Al-Furqan 25:42

حِين (ḥīn) here is the time of seeing truth: the interval when the veil of claims is removed and reality becomes manifest. Knowledge comes at that time, but it is not necessarily saving knowledge anymore.

Daily Rhythm and the Borders of Day and Night

فَسُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ حِينَ تُمْسُونَ وَحِينَ تُصْبِحُونَ

So glorify The God when you enter evening and when you enter morning. Ar-Rum 30:17

And the following ayah continues:

وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَعَشِيًّا وَحِينَ تُظْهِرُونَ

And to Him belongs praise in the heavens and the earth, and in the late afternoon, and when you reach noon. Ar-Rum 30:18

Here حِين (ḥīn) relates to defined times of the daily cycle: evening, morning, and noon. These are moments of transition and daily order. Through these times, the human being regulates worship, work, rest, and remembrance of The God.

Privacy, the Home, and Social Adab

مِن قَبْلِ صَلَاةِ الْفَجْرِ وَحِينَ تَضَعُونَ ثِيَابَكُم مِّنَ الظَّهِيرَةِ

Before the prayer of dawn, and when you put aside your garments at midday. An-Nur 24:58

In this ayah, حِين (ḥīn) marks a specific time for observing the privacy of the home. Not all times are the same. Some times are more sensitive because of the body, rest, seclusion, and the privacy of family life. حِين (ḥīn) here joins time to social adab and the rights of the household.

Death and Bequest

شَهَادَةُ بَيْنِكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ حِينَ الْوَصِيَّةِ

Testimony among you, when death comes to one of you, at the time of bequest... Al-Ma'idah 5:106

Here حِين (ḥīn) points to the legal and sensitive time of bequest. This is not ordinary time; it is a moment when death has drawn near and the transfer of rights, trusts, and testimony becomes important.

In another ayah, the time of death is linked with the taking of the self:

اللَّهُ يَتَوَفَّى الْأَنفُسَ حِينَ مَوْتِهَا وَالَّتِي لَمْ تَمُتْ فِي مَنَامِهَا

The God takes the selves at the time of their death, and those that have not died during their sleep. Az-Zumar 39:42

In this ayah, حِينَ مَوْتِهَا (ḥīna mawtihā) is the time when the self moves from the state of worldly life. حِين (ḥīn) specifies the moment of the great event: death.

Heedlessness and the Suitable Moment of an Event

وَدَخَلَ الْمَدِينَةَ عَلَىٰ حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا

And he entered the city at a time of heedlessness from its people. Al-Qasas 28:15

In this ayah, حِين (ḥīn) is not merely time; it is time accompanied by a social state. حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ (ḥīni ghaflah) means a time when the people of the city were heedless: an interval with a particular quality, in which the event occurs within that very quality.

The Human Being in Relation to Dahr

هَلْ أَتَىٰ عَلَى الْإِنسَانِ حِينٌ مِّنَ الدَّهْرِ لَمْ يَكُن شَيْئًا مَّذْكُورًا

Has there come over the human being a time from dahr when he was not a thing mentioned? Al-Insan 76:1

This ayah is very important for understanding the root. دَهْر (dahr) is the broad and extended expanse of time; حِين (ḥīn) is a slice of that expanse. The human being passed through an interval of دَهْر (dahr) in which he was not yet “a thing mentioned.” Thus حِين (ḥīn) has a part-to-whole relationship with دَهْر (dahr): دَهْر (dahr) is the larger span, and حِين (ḥīn) is a piece or stage within it.

Important Distinctions

The uses of ح ي ن (ḥ-y-n) must be distinguished. إِلَىٰ حِين (ilā ḥīn) shows the temporal limit of enjoyment or provision. حَتَّىٰ حِين (ḥattā ḥīn) is a term until an unknown but limited point. كُلَّ حِين (kulla ḥīn) is repetition in every suitable period or every interval of fruit-bearing. حِينَ تُمْسُونَ (ḥīna tumsūn) and حِينَ تُصْبِحُونَ (ḥīna tuṣbiḥūn) are boundary-times of the daily cycle. حِينَ الْوَصِيَّةِ (ḥīna al-waṣiyyah) is the legal time of bequest. حِينَ مَوْتِهَا (ḥīna mawtihā) is the time of the event of death. حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ (ḥīni ghaflah) is time accompanied by a social state. حِينٌ مِّنَ الدَّهْرِ (ḥīnun mina al-dahr) is an interval within a wider expanse of time.

Structurally, particles and constructions matter in this root. إِلَىٰ حِين (ilā ḥīn) shows time moving toward the end of a term. حَتَّىٰ حِين (ḥattā ḥīn) conveys the continuation of something until a point arrives. كُلَّ حِين (kulla ḥīn) shows repetition across multiple periods. حِينَئِذٍ (ḥīna'idhin) means “at that time.” When حِين (ḥīn) is attached to a state, such as حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ (ḥīni ghaflah), time is defined by the quality of that state.

ح ي ن (ḥ-y-n) differs from several nearby words. W Q T, as وَقْت (waqt), is usually closer to an appointed and measured time; حِين (ḥīn) can be broader and can indicate an interval or the time of an event. A J L, as أَجَل (ajal), is more strongly the appointed end and deadline of a term; حِين (ḥīn) is the interval or temporal segment itself. D H R, as دَهْر (dahr), is the long and encompassing span of time; حِين (ḥīn) is a piece of دَهْر (dahr). Y W M, as يَوْم (yawm), is a day or a defined period, but حِين (ḥīn) can be part of a day, the moment of an event, or an undefined period. S W ʿ, as سَاعَة (sāʿah), is a moment, an hour, or sometimes the Hour of Judgment; حِين (ḥīn) does not necessarily carry that same intensity or precision.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For Forqan translation, حِين (ḥīn) should not always be rendered simply as “time.” Depending on context, it may be translated as “when,” “at the time,” “until a time,” “for a time,” “an interval,” “a period,” “a segment,” “an appointed moment,” or “a granted term.” إِلَىٰ حِين (ilā ḥīn) is often “until a time” or “until a point.” حَتَّىٰ حِين (ḥattā ḥīn) is also “until a time” or “for a while,” but sometimes carries the color of waiting and respite. كُلَّ حِين (kulla ḥīn) is better rendered as “at every time,” “in every suitable period,” or “at every interval.” حِينٌ مِّنَ الدَّهْرِ (ḥīnun mina al-dahr) is better translated as “an interval of dahr” or “a period within the wider span of time.”

Linguistic Summary

Linguistic summary: the root ح ي ن (ḥ-y-n) points to a time, moment, interval, or segment of time. Its material image is a slice of the flow of time recognized by an event, a state, a term, a phase-change, or an end. In the Qur'an, this root extends from provision until a time, respite until a time, fruit-bearing at every time, morning and evening times, the time of bequest, the time of death, a time of heedlessness, and an interval within دَهْر (dahr). Its difference from أَجَل (ajal) is that أَجَل (ajal) more strongly indicates the appointed end and deadline of a term, while حِين (ḥīn) indicates the temporal segment or interval itself. Its difference from دَهْر (dahr) is that دَهْر (dahr) is the broad expanse of time, while حِين (ḥīn) is a piece of that expanse.
