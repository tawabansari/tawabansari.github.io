Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root B K M

Mute speech, blocked expression, inability to speak clearly, and the closed path by which meaning exits through the tongue

Important Forms from This Root

بَكِمَ / يَبْكَمُ

bakima / yabkamu; became mute, became unable to speak clearly, or had the ability of expression closed.

بَكَم

bakam; muteness, inability of expression, the closure of the path of speech.

أَبْكَم

abkam; mute, tongue-bound, unable to speak clearly, unable to express with function.

بُكْم

bukm; the mute, the tongue-bound, those whose path of expressing truth is closed.

بَكْمَاء

bakmāʾ; feminine of abkam; a woman unable to express clearly.

Central Meaning

The root B K M means muteness, the closing of the ability to express, inability to speak clearly, and the blocked path by which meaning exits through the tongue. This root is mainly about inability of expression, not inability to hear or see.

If Ṣ M M is the blocked path of receiving sound, B K M is the blocked path of sending out speech and response. Bukm means that meaning, response, or the capacity for speech exists or could have existed within, but its clear expression is blocked. In some uses, bukm also carries inability of effective understanding and practical function.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of B K M is the closing of the instrument of expression. In this root, the issue is not hearing; the issue is speaking. A person may have something within, yet be unable to bring it out through language. Or a person may physically have a tongue, yet have no clear and truthful response before truth.

Lexically, bukm differs from ordinary silence. Silence may be chosen, but bukm indicates inability or the closure of expression. A silent person may be able to speak but chooses not to. An أَبْكَم person cannot bring speech out clearly.

Some lexical sources distinguish أَبْكَم from أَخْرَس. Akhras is closer to physical inability of speech, while abkam may include inability of speech together with blocked understanding or impaired communicative function. This distinction is not identical in every use, but it matters for understanding the example in Surat al-Naḥl.

Material Image

The material image of this root is a mouth from which clear speech does not emerge. In a healthy condition, meaning moves from heart and mind to the tongue and becomes manifest through speech. In bukm, this outgoing route is blocked or impaired. There may be no sound, or there may be sound without clear, meaningful expression.

Bukm is therefore tied to the throat, tongue, mouth, and the ability to turn meaning into speech. In image, bukm means that meaning is trapped on its way out. It is not like ṣamam, where the path of entry is blocked, nor like ʿamā, where the path of seeing is blocked. Here the path of expression has been closed.

The sharper image is this: the outgoing channel of meaning is locked. As a result, a person cannot give a clear answer, cannot clarify truth, and cannot produce useful expression in the field of argument or testimony.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, أَبْكَم refers to someone who lacks the ability to speak clearly; one who is mute or whose tongue is closed from expressing the intended meaning. Lexical uses connect bukm mainly with inability of speech and the closure of expression. This differs from voluntary silence. Arabic has other roots for silence, quietness, and withholding speech; bukm points to defect, blockage, or closure in the ability to express.

In some lexical definitions, abkam is described as one who does not have clear speech from the beginning, and whose understanding or communicative function may also be impaired. This is contrasted with akhras, who may understand but whose speech instrument is closed. This point should be used carefully, because lexical usage is not always uniform, but it helps distinguish bukm from silence and kharas.

More broadly, bukm can approach inability to state an argument, give a response, or articulate clear speech. A person may physically have a tongue, yet be bukm in the field of truth because his speech has no path to truth or he has lost the ability of clear testimony.

Qur’anic Usage

Bukm with Ṣumm and ʿUmy

One famous Qur’anic use appears in Surat al-Baqarah: صُمٌّ بُكْمٌ عُمْيٌ فَهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ; deaf, mute, blind, so they do not return 2:18.

Here, bukm appears beside ṣumm and ʿumy. Together, the three form a complete image of the tools of relation to truth being cut off: ṣumm means the path of hearing is closed; bukm means the path of response and expression is closed; and ʿumy means the path of seeing and discernment is closed. So bukm here is not merely medical inability to speak. In the verse’s context, it describes those who have lost the ability to express truth, give a right response, or return in speech and action before guidance.

Abkam in the Qur’anic Example

The Qur’an gives an example in Surat al-Naḥl: وَضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا رَّجُلَيْنِ أَحَدُهُمَا أَبْكَمُ لَا يَقْدِرُ عَلَىٰ شَيْءٍ وَهُوَ كَلٌّ عَلَىٰ مَوْلَاهُ أَيْنَمَا يُوَجِّههُّ لَا يَأْتِ بِخَيْرٍ; The God gives an example of two men: one of them is abkam, unable to do anything, a burden upon his master; wherever he directs him, he brings no good 16:76.

In this verse, abkam is accompanied by three features: he has no ability over anything, he is a burden upon his caretaker, and wherever he is sent, he brings no good. Thus abkam in this example is not merely someone who cannot speak. He represents a being unable to express, understand effectively, produce impact, or bring a good result.

This use shows that bukm can indicate the absence of clear function, not only the absence of physical speech. Opposite him stands one who commands justice and is upon a straight path. The real contrast is between a closed and ineffective being, and a directed, truth-speaking, justice-commanding being.

Bukm and Inability to Testify to Truth

In Qur’anic logic, the tongue is not only an instrument of sound. It is an instrument of expressing truth, testimony, commanding what is recognized as right, forbidding wrong, supplication, response, and acknowledgment. When a person becomes bukm, these functions are taken away or disabled. He may have sound, yet not speak truth. He may talk, yet his speech has no path to reality. In this sense, guidance-level bukm is the closure of the tongue toward truth.

Bukm on the Day of Rising

The Qur’an says of a group of deniers: وَنَحْشُرُهُمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ عَلَىٰ وُجُوهِهِمْ عُمْيًا وَبُكْمًا وَصُمًّا; and We will gather them on the Day of Rising upon their faces: blind, mute, and deaf 17:97.

Here, bukm appears in the final image of consequence. Those who closed the routes of receiving and responding to truth in the world are depicted in the Hereafter with those same channels closed. Bukm here means the closure of expression, response, and argument. They no longer have a clear tongue for defense, excuse, or explanation.

Bukm and Silence

Bukm is not the same as silence. Silence may be wise. A person may be silent in order to listen, reflect, be patient, or avoid false speech. But bukm is inability of expression. In bukm, the instrument of expression or its function has been closed. In a guidance context, it means a person has no clear tongue before truth.

So not every silence is bukm. Sometimes silence is a sign of wisdom, while bukm in the Qur’an often signals the closure of the path of response and expression of truth.

Human Note

As with ṣamam and ʿamā, physical bukm and moral bukm must be distinguished. People with speech disabilities are not being degraded morally or humanly. When the Qur’an uses bukm beside ṣumm and ʿumy in a context of misguidance, it speaks about people who possess the tools of understanding and response, yet close the path of truth.

Translation and commentary must be careful here. Guidance-level bukm means the closure of expression toward truth, not an insult toward mute people or people with speech disabilities.

Difference from Nearby Roots

Ṣ M T means silence, quietness, or not speaking. Silence may be chosen and wise; bukm is inability or closure of expression. S K T points to becoming silent, speech stopping, or sound settling down; silence is more about the stopping of speech, while bukm is about the closed ability to express.

KH R S is muteness or inability of speech and is close to bukm. Kharas is closer to physical inability of articulation; bukm may also include inability of expression together with weakness of understanding or communicative function. N Ṭ Q is speaking, articulation, and rational expression; bukm stands opposite clear articulation.

Q W L is saying and verbal expression; bukm is inability of clear qawl. B Y N is making clear and manifest; bukm is tied to the absence of clear linguistic manifestation. L S N is tongue and speech capacity; bukm is defect or blockage in the function of the tongue.

Ṣ M M is the closed path of hearing; bukm is the closed path of speaking. AYN M Y (ʿ M Y) is the closed path of seeing; bukm is the closed path of expression. AYN Y Y (ʿ Y Y) is inability of expression through helplessness, semantic stammering, or failure to convey meaning; bukm is more closed and more foundational than ʿayy.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, bukm should not always be taken only in the medical sense of “the mute,” especially in guidance contexts. In physical contexts, “mute,” “tongue-bound,” or “unable to speak” may fit, while avoiding wording that sounds degrading in contemporary English.

In guidance contexts, “tongue-bound toward truth,” “unable to express truth,” or “those whose path of clear response has been closed” may be more precise. In the phrase ṣumm bukm ʿumy, the translation should show that three main paths of relation to truth have been closed: hearing, speaking, and seeing. In translating abkam in Surat al-Naḥl, inability of speech should be seen together with functional inability: “unable to express or act effectively,” not merely “mute.”

Forqan Translation Suggestions

بَكَم (bakam): muteness, inability of expression, closure of the path of speech.

أَبْكَم (abkam): mute, tongue-bound, unable to express clearly, unable to express and function clearly.

بُكْم (bukm): the mute, the tongue-bound; in guidance context, those unable to express truth.

بَكْمَاء (bakmāʾ): a woman unable to express clearly.

صُمٌّ بُكْمٌ عُمْيٌ: deaf, mute, blind; or closed in hearing, closed in expression, closed in sight.

أَحَدُهُمَا أَبْكَمُ: one of them is mute; or one of them is unable to express and function clearly.

وَنَحْشُرُهُمْ ... بُكْمًا: and We gather them tongue-bound; or while their path of expression and argument is closed.

Linguistic Summary

The root B K M denotes muteness, the closing of expressive ability, and inability to speak clearly. At the material level, bukm means the tongue or speech system cannot turn meaning into clear speech. At the Qur’anic level, bukm can also refer to guidance-level inability: the human being has no tongue of response, testimony, and clear expression before truth.

Its difference from ṣamam is that ṣamam closes the path by which sound enters, while bukm closes the path by which meaning exits. Its difference from silence is that silence may be chosen and wise, while bukm is the closure of expression. Its difference from kharas is that kharas is closer to physical muteness, while bukm may also include the closing of understanding and expressive function. The main image is this: meaning or response should move from within to the tongue, but the path of expression is closed; the tongue is present, yet clear, truth-speaking, effective speech does not appear.
