Calling by a nickname: from naming and addressing to humiliation, stigmatization and Quranic tanābuz

1. The root in early Arabic

The Arabic root ن ب ز concerns giving someone a nickname or calling them by a name or description beyond their original name. نَبَزَ is especially associated with a nickname used to disparage, humiliate or recall an unwelcome trait.

نَبَزَهُ يَنْبِزُهُ نَبْزًا means that someone called another by a nickname or bestowed one. النَّبَز names the nickname itself, with the plural أَنْبَاز. تَنَابَزَ القَوْمُ means that members of a group called one another by nicknames, most commonly disparaging or unwelcome ones.

Arabic distinguishes a person's original name from an additional designation. A name may identify someone from the outset; a nickname usually reflects a physical characteristic, conduct, social position, background, profession or another feature.

A لَقَب can be complimentary or derogatory. Knowledge, courage, skill or a valued quality may produce an honorific. A physical difference, poverty, ancestry, past or disliked behavior may instead be used to create a degrading name.

The general lexical meaning of نَبْز concerns nickname-giving; in its disparaging use, naming becomes a means of lowering someone's standing.

In the usage condemned by the Quran, تَنَابُز is therefore more than mentioning another person: it employs a nickname to humiliate, reproach or hurt them.

Historical meaning and evidence from early naming practices

In pre-Islamic Arab society, personal names, teknonyms, nicknames and genealogical affiliations were familiar means of identification. People might be known by their own name, a teknonym or a nickname bestowed by others.

A nickname could convey respect and recognition or become an instrument of ridicule. Tribal affiliations, appearance and life events also contributed to their formation.

A verse attributed in literary works to a poet of Fazarah states:

أُكْنِيهِ حِينَ أُنَادِيهِ لِأُكْرِمَهُ
وَلَا أُلَقِّبُهُ وَالسَّوْأَةُ اللَّقَبَا

uknīhi ḥīna unādīhi li-ukrimahu / wa-lā ulaqqibuhu wa-al-sawʾatu al-laqabā

That is: When I call him, I use his teknonym to honor him; I do not give him a nickname, for such a nickname is a disgrace.

The verse contrasts respectful address with disparaging nicknames. However, نَبْز itself does not occur in it, and its attribution and date are not sufficiently established to make it certain pre-Quranic evidence for this root.

It helps explain naming practices and the social significance of nicknames, but cannot replace independent historical evidence for نَبَزَ.

Early dictionaries record نَبَزَ, نَبْز, نَبَز and تَنَابَزَ for giving and using nicknames. The Quran's own usage also shows that the family was recognized in the language of revelation.

The evidence examined here does not establish the root's exact origin or prove that, in a particular pre-Quranic period, it invariably referred only to bad nicknames.

A supported definition is that نَبْز concerns giving or using a nickname, which in its disparaging application makes the address humiliating or unwelcome. This negative use is central to the Quranic prohibition.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important Arabic forms include:

  • نَبَزَ / يَنْبِزُ: calling someone by a nickname or bestowing one.
  • نَبْز: the verbal noun, giving or using a nickname.
  • نَبَز: the nickname itself, particularly a disparaging one.
  • أَنْبَاز: the plural of نَبَز, nicknames.
  • نَبَّزَ / يُنَبِّزُ: giving nicknames; repetition or frequency may accompany some uses, but cannot be assumed from the pattern alone.
  • تَنَابَزَ / يَتَنَابَزُ: using nicknames among people or calling one another by them.

Only تَنَابَزُوا occurs in the Quran from this family.

It appears in al-Hujurat 49:11:

وَلَا تَنَابَزُوا بِالْأَلْقَابِ

wa-lā tanābazū bi-al-alqāb

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:11

And do not call one another by nicknames.

تَنَابَزُوا is a second-person plural form-VI imperfect verb in the jussive. One of the initial tāʾ letters in تَتَنَابَزُوا has been omitted. The prohibitive لا makes the verb jussive, marked by the loss of the final nūn found in the indicative plural form.

Its structure expresses a relationship among people: using nicknames in social interaction.

Form VI alone does not establish that the act always requires both sides to respond with nicknames.

Someone may address another with a hurtful nickname and receive no reply. The humiliated person's silence does not legitimize the first person's behavior.

بِالْأَلْقَابِ belongs to ل ق ب, not ن ب ز. It identifies the means or manner of address: using nicknames.

The sentence thus combines two related elements: تَنَابُز describes the behavior, while الْأَلْقَاب identifies the kind of names used.

3. The prohibition in its Quranic context

Al-Hujurat 49:11 states:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا يَسْخَرْ قَوْمٌ مِنْ قَوْمٍ عَسَىٰ أَنْ يَكُونُوا خَيْرًا مِنْهُمْ وَلَا نِسَاءٌ مِنْ نِسَاءٍ عَسَىٰ أَنْ يَكُنَّ خَيْرًا مِنْهُنَّ وَلَا تَلْمِزُوا أَنْفُسَكُمْ وَلَا تَنَابَزُوا بِالْأَلْقَابِ بِئْسَ الِاسْمُ الْفُسُوقُ بَعْدَ الْإِيمَانِ وَمَنْ لَمْ يَتُبْ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الظَّالِمُونَ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā yaskhar qawmun min qawmin ʿasā an yakūnū khayran minhum wa-lā nisāʾun min nisāʾin ʿasā an yakunna khayran minhunna wa-lā talmizū anfusakum wa-lā tanābazū bi-al-alqābi biʾsa al-ismu al-fusūqu baʿda al-īmāni wa-man lam yatub fa-ulāʾika humu al-ẓālimūn

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:11

You who have believed, let no group ridicule another; they may be better than them. Nor let women ridicule other women; they may be better than them. Do not find fault with your own people, and do not call one another by nicknames. How evil a designation is disobedience after faith. Those who do not repent are the wrongdoers.

Several social behaviors appear together: ridiculing others, finding fault and using disparaging nicknames.

Their association matters for understanding Quranic تَنَابُز.

Isolating the phrase could suggest that every kind of nickname is forbidden. Reading it beside ridicule and fault-finding clarifies that the concern is behavior damaging others' dignity.

The prohibition therefore concerns degrading and hurtful nickname use, not every description used for identification, respect or friendly address.

Its relationship to ridicule

The verse begins with this command:

لَا يَسْخَرْ قَوْمٌ مِنْ قَوْمٍ

lā yaskhar qawmun min qawm

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:11

Let no group ridicule another.

Ridicule may employ speech, imitation, gestures, laughter, performance or other actions. One person or group belittles another and makes them an object of derision.

Disparaging nicknames are one means of such social humiliation.

Someone might be given a name intended to provoke laughter at their height, disability, finances or appearance.

The nickname then does more than identify: it recalls the users' contemptuous attitude.

سُخْرِيَة and نَبْز are nevertheless different. Ridicule need not involve nicknames, while nickname-giving can occur respectfully.

تَنَابُز should consequently not be translated simply as ridicule, despite their close contextual relationship.

Its relationship to fault-finding

The verse continues:

وَلَا تَلْمِزُوا أَنْفُسَكُمْ

wa-lā talmizū anfusakum

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:11

And do not find fault with your own people.

أَنْفُسَكُمْ addresses the group. In the context of believers' relationships, it forbids disparaging one another, as though harming another's dignity also harmed the community itself.

لَمْز concerns finding fault, disparagement and hostile criticism.

نَبْز concerns calling a person by a nickname.

The behaviors can coincide, but are not identical.

Fault-finding may discuss a real or alleged failing. In disparaging nickname use, that failing or allegation can become a label repeatedly applied to the person.

An isolated comment about a mistake differs from turning that mistake into a permanent name. The latter moves beyond discussing behavior toward degrading identification.

Yet نَبْز does not necessarily mean constant repetition or public circulation. A single insulting address may also fall within the condemned behavior.

Repetition and notoriety can intensify the harm, but are not invariant parts of the root.

4. Is every nickname forbidden?

لَقَب has a broader range than the disparaging use of نَبْز.

It may honor knowledge, courage or service, or merely identify someone through a family nickname or familiar designation.

Conversely, a nickname can be intended to demean someone or recall a flaw, failure, sin or unwelcome past.

The context of al-Hujurat 49:11 identifies this latter use as the object of prohibition.

The verse therefore does not establish a ban on every pseudonym, family nickname, academic title or respectful description.

Is the person's dislike the criterion?

Established usage often associates تَنَابُز with nicknames the person dislikes or experiences as degrading.

Recognizing humiliation does not, however, require waiting for an explicit objection.

Fear, embarrassment, financial dependence or unequal social standing may prevent someone from protesting. Silence need not indicate consent.

Someone else may accept a friendly, respectful nickname. Merely differing from their original name does not make it condemned name-calling.

Relevant considerations include the nickname's meaning, how it is used, the relationship, social circumstances and its degrading or hurtful effect.

Intent may also matter ethically, but saying “I meant no harm” does not by itself make every degrading address harmless.

Turning someone's past into a permanent identity

One harmful practice makes a person's past into a label that continually defines them.

A person may commit a wrong, correct it and follow another path. Continuing to address them by a name recalling that wrong uses past behavior to define their present character.

Early commentaries likewise include addressing someone by their former religious affiliation or behavior after their situation has changed among examples of disparaging nickname use.

This can be examined alongside the following phrase:

بِئْسَ الِاسْمُ الْفُسُوقُ بَعْدَ الْإِيمَانِ

biʾsa al-ismu al-fusūqu baʿda al-īmān

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:11

How evil a designation is disobedience after faith.

Two interpretive directions have been proposed.

One concerns calling a believer by a derogatory designation connected with former disbelief or disobedience.

The other concerns believers themselves acquiring the designation of disobedience through ridicule, disparagement and hurtful name-calling after professing faith.

Both deserve consideration; the expression should not be restricted without evidence to one historical example.

What is clear in context is the incompatibility of degrading conduct with faith calling for respect, justice and avoidance of wrongdoing.

5. Nicknames and social identity

A nickname can be more than a word: it may shape how others perceive a person.

A derogatory designation can reduce someone in others' minds to a physical difference, disability, family background or particular behavior.

If someone is known for years by a name referring to poverty, illness or a past mistake, their other qualities and abilities may be overlooked.

The problem then goes beyond hearing an unwelcome word: others' naming places the person within an imposed social position.

Linguistically, نَبْز concerns nickname-giving. Socially, repeating a degrading label can make it a lasting sign of contempt.

This describes an effect of the behavior, not an independent lexical meaning of the root.

Recognizing peoples and groups

Al-Hujurat 49:13 states:

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ إِنَّا خَلَقْنَاكُمْ مِنْ ذَكَرٍ وَأُنْثَىٰ وَجَعَلْنَاكُمْ شُعُوبًا وَقَبَائِلَ لِتَعَارَفُوا إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ أَتْقَاكُمْ

yā ayyuhā al-nāsu innā khalaqnākum min dhakarin wa-unthā wa-jaʿalnākum shuʿūban wa-qabāʾila li-taʿārafū inna akramakum ʿinda allāhi atqākum

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:13

People, We created you from a male and a female, and made you peoples and tribes so that you might know one another. The most honored of you before The God is the most mindful of Him.

This appears two verses after the prohibition of disparaging nicknames.

Differences among peoples and tribes serve mutual recognition, rather than turning another's name and identity into a means of humiliation.

Ethnicity, language, birthplace and family affiliation can help identify someone. Used as degrading labels, however, they depart from that identifying function.

Naming to recognize and naming to demean are clearly different.

This distinction is not confined to the Arabs of the Quran's time. Any society can turn ethnicity, nationality, residence, occupation or economic status into disparaging names.

Applying the principle today extends the Quranic prohibition to present circumstances, while the root's lexical meaning remains giving and using nicknames.

6. Distinguishing related roots

ن ب ز and ل ق ب

The two families are closely connected in the field of naming.

لَقَب names an additional designation beyond a person's original name. It may praise, disparage or simply identify.

نَبْز also concerns nickname-giving, but its association with disparagement and unwelcome naming is more prominent.

In al-Hujurat 49:11, تَنَابُز accompanies الْأَلْقَاب: the prohibited use belongs to the setting of ridicule and fault-finding among the various ways people may be named.

لَقَب thus names the designation used, while تَنَابُز describes the behavior of addressing people by it.

ن ب ز and ل م ز

لَمْز concerns fault-finding and disparagement.

نَبْز concerns naming someone with a nickname.

They may combine: a person may disparage another's characteristic or behavior and then use it to construct a humiliating label.

Fault-finding need not become a nickname, and a nickname need not identify a real defect.

لَمْز more particularly describes the act of disparagement, while نَبْز concerns the manner of naming and address.

ن ب ز and س خ ر

In its condemnatory use, سَخِرَ concerns mocking and belittling another.

Mockery may involve laughter, imitation, insinuation, speech or action. تَنَابُز specifically concerns nicknames.

One can mock without inventing a nickname. Conversely, a derogatory nickname may become so commonplace that it is used without overt laughter.

The two share an ethical field, but are not complete lexical synonyms.

ن ب ز and ن ب ذ

These roots differ only in the final letter, but have distinct meanings.

ن ب ذ, with dhāl, concerns throwing, casting aside or discarding.

For example, al-Baqarah 2:101 states:

نَبَذَ فَرِيقٌ مِنَ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ وَرَاءَ ظُهُورِهِمْ

nabadha farīqun mina alladhīna ūtū al-kitāba kitāba allāhi warāʾa ẓuhūrihim

Al-Baqarah 2:101

A group of those given the Scripture cast the Scripture of God behind their backs.

نَبَذَ belongs to ن ب ذ and denotes casting aside.

تَنَابَزُوا in al-Hujurat 49:11 belongs to ن ب ز and concerns nickname use.

Similar spelling does not prove historical kinship or identical meanings. Nor should نَبْز be defined as throwing a nickname at someone without independent evidence for that historical connection.

ن ب ز and ن ب أ

نَبَأ concerns news and the communication of information in the Quran.

Al-Hujurat 49:6 states:

إِنْ جَاءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌ بِنَبَإٍ فَتَبَيَّنُوا

in jāʾakum fāsiqun bi-nabaʾin fa-tabayyanū

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:6

If a disobedient person brings you news, examine and clarify it.

نَبَإ here means news and belongs to ن ب أ.

نَبْز, by contrast, concerns nickname-giving.

The families share their first two letters; that resemblance establishes no certain etymological or semantic connection.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

Depending on context, نَبَزَهُ can be rendered “called him by a nickname,” “gave him a nickname,” or, in a disparaging use, “called him by a degrading nickname.”

نَبْز can be translated as giving or using a nickname.

The noun نَبَز means a nickname; context can justify explaining it as a disparaging or degrading designation.

For the prohibition containing تَنَابَزُوا in al-Hujurat 49:11, several renderings can be considered:

“Do not call one another by nicknames.”

“Do not call one another by degrading nicknames.”

“Do not address one another with hurtful names.”

The first follows the Arabic wording more closely and corresponds to Forqan's current Persian rendering.

The second makes the negative contextual sense clearer. “Degrading” is not an independent adjective in بِالْأَلْقَابِ; it comes from the use of تَنَابُز and the context of ridicule and disparagement.

The third is natural but lexically broader: hurtful names can include forms of address that are not necessarily nicknames.

The basic rendering “and do not call one another by nicknames” can be retained if the lexical and contextual explanation clarifies that disparaging nickname use is meant, not every respectful or identifying designation.

To make the prohibited sense explicit within the translation itself, “and do not call one another by degrading nicknames” is also contextually defensible. These proposals do not alter the installed verse translations.

Conclusion

ن ب ز concerns giving a nickname or addressing someone by an additional name or description. In its disparaging use, the nickname humiliates, reproaches or hurts the person.

The root occurs once in the Quran, as تَنَابَزُوا in al-Hujurat 49:11.

The context joins ridicule, fault-finding and nickname use, showing that the prohibition concerns conduct damaging others' dignity.

Not all nicknames are objectionable: they may honor, identify or express friendship. What is condemned here is using a designation to lower someone's standing and hurt them.

Although form VI expresses a relationship among people, the prohibition is not restricted to reciprocal exchanges. A degrading nickname used by one party can also fall within it.

The behavior may be one brief address or become a lasting stigma through repetition and social circulation. People may then be identified by a flaw, past or imposed label rather than their present character and conduct.

Al-Hujurat 49:13 is also relevant: peoples and tribes are presented as a basis for mutual recognition, not humiliation or superiority.

Final definition: ن ب ز concerns giving and using a nickname that, in its disparaging application, humiliates, reproaches or hurts its bearer. Quranic تَنَابُز describes degrading nickname use among people, associated with ridicule and fault-finding. It must not be equated with every act of naming, respectful designation or accurate identification.

The verse's clear message is that another person's name and identity must not be made instruments of humiliation. Names should enable recognition, not perpetuate contempt or deprive people of human dignity.
