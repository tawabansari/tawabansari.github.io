The Qur’anic Root F W T (فَاتَ)

Qur’anic and lexical forms

فَاتَكُمْ
(fātakum)
Escaped you or was lost to you; three occurrences

فَوْتَ
(fawta)
Escape from reach; one occurrence

تَفَاوُتٍ
(tafāwutin)
Inconsistency; one occurrence

Being lost, remaining beyond reach, and failing to attain something: from missed opportunities to the absence of inconsistency in creation

1. The root’s meaning in early Arabic

The three-letter root ف و ت concerns passing beyond reach, failing to attain something, and losing the opportunity to reach it.

فَاتَهُ الشَّيْءُ means something escaped a person, did not reach them, or became unavailable. فَاتَنِي الْأَمْرُ likewise means that a matter or opportunity was missed.

Classical lexical sources explain this family as خِلَافُ إِدْرَاكِ الشَّيْءِ وَالْوُصُولِ إِلَيْهِ: the opposite of reaching or attaining something.

The relationship between two parties matters: something that could have reached a person, or that the person could have attained, is now beyond their reach.

Someone might catch a caravan and travel with it. Arriving after it has departed, however, they say فَاتَتْنِي الْقَافِلَةُ: “I missed the caravan.”

The caravan itself has not been destroyed. The person simply no longer has access to it.

The same meaning applies to opportunities in time. Once the time for an action has passed, فَاتَ وَقْتُهُ means “its time has passed.”

Thus فَوْت does not itself mean destruction, but the loss of an opportunity to reach, obtain, or benefit from something.

Evidence from pre-Islamic Arabic poetry

A poem attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā says:

هَلْ فِي تَذَكُّرِ أَيَّامِ الصِّبَا فَنَدُ

أَمْ هَلْ لِمَا فَاتَ مِنْ أَيَّامِهِ رِدَدُ

Hal fī tadhakkuri ayyāmi l-ṣibā fanadu / am hal li-mā fāta min ayyāmihi ridadu

“Is there foolishness in recalling the days of youth? Can any of those departed days return?”

Here فَاتَ describes the days of life that have passed.

The poet remembers youth and asks whether its lost days can be restored.

The subject is not the physical destruction of days, but the passage of time no longer at one’s disposal.

This illustrates the use of فَوْت in early poetry for passing time and lost opportunities in life.

The poem is recorded in the collection attributed to Zuhayr and is recognized within the pre-Islamic poetic tradition. Because that poetry survives through later transmission and compilation, attribution alone does not establish the date of every individual line with certainty.

With this qualification, فَاتَ has a clear meaning here, consistent with the family’s Qur’anic uses.

A distance that prevents reaching

Classical Arabic also records فَوْت for a gap between things, such as the space between fingers.

هُوَ مِنِّي فَوْتَ الرُّمْحِ describes something beyond the reach of one’s spear.

جَعَلَ اللَّهُ رِزْقَكَ فَوْتَ فَمِكَ evokes food visible to someone but beyond their mouth’s reach.

These usages do not always imply great distance. Even a small gap may prevent someone from obtaining something.

What matters is not the distance itself, but failure to attain the desired thing.

فَوْت and فَوَات both denote missing out or losing an opportunity. فَائِت describes something past or beyond reach.

Another branch, اِفْتِيَات, denotes acting without consulting or obtaining permission from someone expected to participate in the decision: acting ahead of that person’s opportunity to take part.

This relates to getting ahead and excluding another from participation, but اِفْتِيَات itself does not occur in the Qur’an.

2. Qur’anic forms and semantic core

The root occurs five times in three forms:

  • فَاتَكُمْ in Āl ʿImrān 3:153, al-Ḥadīd 57:23, and al-Mumtaḥanah 60:11.
  • فَوْتَ in Sabaʾ 34:51.
  • تَفَاوُتٍ in al-Mulk 67:3.

These forms differ in construction and use.

فَاتَ is a Form I past-tense verb. In all three Qur’anic occurrences, the attached pronoun كُمْ refers to the addressees: something has escaped them or failed to reach them.

فَوْت is a noun or verbal noun associated with this verb, expressing being missed, failure to attain, or escape from reach.

تَفَاوُت is the Form VI verbal noun of تَفَاوَتَ. It concerns distance or disparity between parts or things.

تَفَاوَتَ الشَّيْئَانِ means two things differed or became separated in quantity, condition, or characteristics.

Form VI does not necessarily imply two conscious agents intentionally producing disparity. Two sizes, conditions, or components may differ without independent agency.

The family’s semantic core may be stated as:

Remaining beyond the limits of reaching or attainment; and, between multiple things, distance or disparity that in some contexts signifies inconsistency.

This explains the connection between فَاتَ, فَوْت, and تَفَاوُت, while each Qur’anic form must still be examined in its own context.

3. Āl ʿImrān 3:153: what was missed and what befell them

إِذْ تُصْعِدُونَ وَلَا تَلْوُونَ عَلَىٰ أَحَدٍ وَالرَّسُولُ يَدْعُوكُمْ فِي أُخْرَاكُمْ فَأَثَابَكُمْ غَمًّا بِغَمٍّ لِكَيْلَا تَحْزَنُوا عَلَىٰ مَا فَاتَكُمْ وَلَا مَا أَصَابَكُمْ وَاللَّهُ خَبِيرٌ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ

Idh tuṣʿidūna wa-lā talwūna ʿalā aḥadin wa-l-rasūlu yadʿūkum fī ukhrākum fa-athābakum ghamman bi-ghammin li-kaylā taḥzanū ʿalā mā fātakum wa-lā mā aṣābakum wa-llāhu khabīrun bi-mā taʿmalūn.

“When you were moving away, turning toward no one, while the Messenger called you from behind; then He repaid you with grief upon grief, so that you would not grieve over what had escaped you or what had befallen you. And The God is fully aware of what you do.”

Āl ʿImrān 3:153

This verse concerns the believers’ situation during the battle of Uḥud.

The preceding verses discuss disagreement, disobedience, a reversal in battle, and differing personal desires.

مَا فَاتَكُمْ concerns what had become unavailable to them in that situation.

It could include anticipated victory, an opportunity for the desired outcome, or an expected gain. The verb alone does not specify any one of these.

Āl ʿImrān 3:153 also addresses what had befallen the addressees:

The verse places grief over what was missed alongside grief over what befell them.

أَصَابَ belongs to another root and denotes what reached or happened to them.

Two situations are therefore juxtaposed:

First, what was lost or not obtained; second, what happened and befell them.

Someone may grieve over both a missed opportunity and an injury or misfortune suffered.

These experiences are related but linguistically distinct.

Here فَوْت concerns what was not obtained or was lost, while “befalling” concerns an event or consequence that reached someone.

فَاتَكُمْ should therefore not be translated as “what harmed you.”

Nor should every sorrow over loss be identified, without regard to context, with the grief discussed here after Uḥud.

4. Al-Ḥadīd 57:23: losing and receiving

لِكَيْلَا تَأْسَوْا عَلَىٰ مَا فَاتَكُمْ وَلَا تَفْرَحُوا بِمَا آتَاكُمْ وَاللَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ كُلَّ مُخْتَالٍ فَخُورٍ

Li-kaylā taʾsaw ʿalā mā fātakum wa-lā tafraḥū bi-mā ātākum wa-llāhu lā yuḥibbu kulla mukhtālin fakhūr.

“So that you do not grieve over what has escaped you or exult over what He has given you; and The God does not love any conceited boaster.”

Al-Ḥadīd 57:23

This is among the clearest uses of فَوْت concerning human life.

People sometimes lose things and sometimes obtain them.

Wealth, opportunity, status, or an available means may pass beyond reach. Conversely, possessions, success, or a blessing may come to them.

The verse addresses human conduct in these two situations.

مَا فَاتَكُمْ denotes what was lost or not obtained.

مَا آتَاكُمْ denotes what was given.

The expressions contrast semantically, but their roots must remain distinct.

فَاتَ concerns missing or losing; آتَى concerns giving or delivering something.

Does the Qur’an prohibit joy and sorrow?

The verse addresses sorrow over loss and joy over receiving.

Its continuation, however, condemns arrogance and boastfulness.

It should not be read to mean that a believer must never feel loss or rejoice in a blessing.

Yūsuf 12:84 describes Jacob’s deep sorrow, and Yūnus 10:58 calls people to rejoice in God’s bounty and mercy.

Joy and sorrow are therefore not intrinsically blameworthy.

The concern is attachment so strong that deprivation destroys one’s balance or prosperity produces pride and boasting.

This interpretation comes from the wider verses and al-Ḥadīd’s context, not from assigning patience, contentment, or indifference to worldly life to ف و ت itself.

The root expresses missing or losing; the Qur’an discusses how people respond.

What is missed is not always destroyed

If someone misses the opportunity to buy a house, the house is not destroyed. Another person may have bought it.

Someone who falls behind in a competition may lose that opportunity while retaining the ability to enter another contest.

A missed opportunity to meet someone may not recur in that particular form.

In each example, فَوْت concerns a person’s relationship to something beyond their reach.

These examples illustrate the root without restricting the verse to economic matters or employment opportunities.

Nor does the root establish that every loss is permanently irreparable in every respect. A particular opportunity may end while another means of recovery or a similar outcome remains possible.

5. Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:11: loss of a marital relationship and financial compensation

وَإِنْ فَاتَكُمْ شَيْءٌ مِنْ أَزْوَاجِكُمْ إِلَى الْكُفَّارِ فَعَاقَبْتُمْ فَآتُوا الَّذِينَ ذَهَبَتْ أَزْوَاجُهُمْ مِثْلَ مَا أَنْفَقُوا وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ الَّذِي أَنْتُمْ بِهِ مُؤْمِنُونَ

Wa-in fātakum shayʾun min azwājikum ilā l-kuffāri fa-ʿāqabtum fa-ātū lladhīna dhahabat azwājuhum mithla mā anfaqū wa-ttaqū llāha lladhī antum bihi muʾminūn.

“And if any of your spouses depart from you to the disbelievers, and your turn for recompense comes, give those whose spouses have departed the equivalent of what they spent; and be mindful of The God, in whom you believe.”

Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:11

This is a different use of فَاتَكُمْ.

Āl ʿImrān 3:153 concerns something missed during battle; al-Ḥadīd 57:23 contrasts loss with receiving a blessing.

Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:11 concerns marriage and the financial consequences of spouses’ departure.

The preceding verse, al-Mumtaḥanah 60:10, discusses women who have emigrated and repayment of marital expenditure.

Verse 11 then concerns spouses who leave their former marital relationship and go to the other group.

فَاتَكُمْ here is not the loss of an inanimate object: the subject is the loss of a marital relationship and its continuation.

The verb’s application to spouses does not establish that its lexical meaning makes a woman her husband’s property.

The verb can also concern losing a relationship, companion, or opportunity.

The verse then addresses financial compensation, directing payment equivalent to the expenditure.

The historical and financial details depend on the wider passage and فَعَاقَبْتُمْ, not on فَاتَكُمْ itself.

The lexical point is that what passes beyond reach is not always money or an object: it may be a human relationship or the possibility of continuing it.

6. Sabaʾ 34:51: when escape is no longer possible

وَلَوْ تَرَىٰ إِذْ فَزِعُوا فَلَا فَوْتَ وَأُخِذُوا مِنْ مَكَانٍ قَرِيبٍ

Wa-law tarā idh faziʿū fa-lā fawta wa-ukhidhū min makānin qarīb.

“If you could see when they are terrified: there is no escape, and they are seized from a nearby place.”

Sabaʾ 34:51

Here فَوْت is a noun.

فَلَا فَوْتَ denies the possibility of escape or passing beyond reach.

Ordinarily, someone who escapes a pursuer and moves beyond reach can be said to have outstripped or eluded them.

The verse denies that possibility.

لَا in لَا فَوْتَ is the particle of categorical negation, and فَوْتَ is its noun. The construction denies the existence of escape in this scene.

The continuation of Sabaʾ 34:51 describes their seizure from a nearby place: they are caught and cannot get beyond reach.

Here فَوْت does not mean the captive’s loss of wealth or opportunity, but escape from the one who seizes them.

The direction of the relationship changes.

In فَاتَكُمْ, something passes beyond the addressees’ reach.

In فَلَا فَوْتَ, the possibility of escaping capture is denied.

The shared core remains failure to reach or passing beyond reach, but the parties’ positions differ.

فَوْت itself does not mean fear, punishment, or capture. Those meanings come from فَزِعُوا, أُخِذُوا, and the surrounding verses.

7. Al-Mulk 67:3: difference or inconsistency in creation?

الَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا مَا تَرَىٰ فِي خَلْقِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ مِنْ تَفَاوُتٍ فَارْجِعِ الْبَصَرَ هَلْ تَرَىٰ مِنْ فُطُورٍ

Alladhī khalaqa sabʿa samāwātin ṭibāqan mā tarā fī khalqi l-raḥmāni min tafāwutin fa-rjiʿi l-baṣara hal tarā min fuṭūr.

“He who created seven heavens in layers. You see no inconsistency in the creation of the Entirely Merciful. Look again: do you see any fissure?”

Al-Mulk 67:3

This is the only Qur’anic occurrence of تَفَاوُت.

In contemporary Persian, the corresponding word usually means difference or nonidentity.

Two people may differ in height, color, language, or ability.

But does مِنْ تَفَاوُتٍ here deny every difference and all diversity in creation?

The context does not support that interpretation.

Al-Rūm 30:22 identifies differences in human languages and colors as signs of creation. Al-Ḥujurāt 49:13 also describes peoples and tribes.

Differences in creatures’ form, size, color, or characteristics are therefore not intrinsically defects in creation.

The meaning of تَفَاوُت in this construction

تَفَاوُت is a Form VI verbal noun. Arabic uses it for things becoming separated, differing, or lacking proportion.

Al-Mulk 67:3 concerns the creation of seven heavens described as طِبَاقًا.

The addressee is then invited to examine creation for inconsistency or a fissure.

Here the disparity in question would disrupt the whole’s continuity, proportion, or harmony.

Parts of a building may differ in shape and size while each occupies its appropriate place. Different dimensions do not by themselves make the building incoherent.

If parts are incompatible and disrupt the structure’s connections, however, there is inconsistency.

This illustrates diversity versus inconsistency; it does not liken the heavens to a building made of earthly materials.

Thus تَفَاوُت here cannot mean merely that things differ. Its contextual meaning concerns inconsistency, incompatibility, or lack of proportion.

The distinction from فُطُور

At the end of al-Mulk 67:3, the addressee is invited to look again:

The question is whether any fissure can be seen in creation.

فُطُور belongs to ف ط ر, not ف و ت.

Here it denotes fissures, ruptures, or cracks.

تَفَاوُت expresses inconsistency and lack of proportion; فُطُور evokes visible fissures and breaks.

These concepts are related but not perfectly synonymous.

Two parts may be incompatible without a physical gap between them. A fissure, conversely, can visibly mark structural discontinuity.

Al-Mulk 67:4 repeats the command to look again and describes the gaze returning unsuccessful and weary from searching for such a defect.

The passage invites observation and renewed examination, presenting creation’s harmony.

It does not supply the details of a physical theory or the unknown structure of the heavens.

The readings تَفَاوُت and تَفَوُّت

The common reading transmitted by Ḥafṣ has تَفَاوُتٍ, the Form VI verbal noun.

Ḥamzah and al-Kisāʾī read تَفَوُّتٍ, with doubled wāw and no alif after fāʾ: a Form V verbal noun.

Reading traditions and Arabic lexical sources record both forms for disparity and lack of proportion.

They differ morphologically, but do not produce contradictory meanings here.

Both fit the denial of inconsistency in creation.

Different verbal forms do not always require a decisive difference in the verse’s meaning.

Harmony and diversity do not conflict

The contemporary Persian word for difference is often neutral: two things may differ without either being defective or wrong.

In al-Mulk 67:3, however, the negation occurs in the context of harmonious creation.

Human languages differ in sounds, grammar, and vocabulary. Al-Rūm 30:22 presents this very diversity as one of God’s signs.

Al-Mulk 67:3 should therefore not be used to make the diversity of humans, plants, animals, or other creatures a defect in creation.

Two concepts must remain distinct:

Diversity means things are not identical in their characteristics.

Inconsistency means the necessary proportion or compatibility between parts is lacking.

The latter fits the context of al-Mulk.

For a fuller examination of created diversity, see Forqan’s article on the Principle of Diversity in creation.

8. Distinctions from related roots

ف و ت and س ب ق: missing and preceding

سَبَقَ concerns preceding or getting ahead.

فَاتَ can describe the result when something has gone ahead and can no longer be reached.

Arabic sometimes glosses فَاتَنِي with سَبَقَنِي, since what gets ahead may move beyond reach.

The roots are not identical, however.

سَبَقَ emphasizes precedence; فَوْت emphasizes failure to reach or passing beyond reach.

One competitor may overtake another who can still close the gap. Precedence has occurred, but the opportunity to catch up is not necessarily lost.

Conversely, an opportunity may pass without competition, as when the time for an action expires.

Not every missed opportunity is defeat in competition, and not every lead makes catching up impossible.

ف و ت and د ر ك: failing to reach and reaching

د ر ك concerns reaching, attaining, and apprehending.

إِدْرَاك can semantically contrast with فَوْت.

Reaching or apprehending something is attainment; its passing beyond one’s reach is missing it.

In Ṭā Hā 20:77, دَرَكًا concerns pursuers overtaking a fleeing group.

This can be compared with escape in Sabaʾ 34:51, though the words have different roles.

دَرَك concerns reaching and catching, whereas فَوْت can express getting beyond reach.

The denial of escape in Sabaʾ 34:51 accordingly relates to people being caught.

ف و ت and ف ر ر: fleeing and escaping reach

فَرَّ concerns fleeing or moving away to seek safety.

فَوْت can denote the successful result: getting beyond a pursuer’s reach.

But not every flight ends in escape.

Someone may flee and still be caught.

In Sabaʾ 34:51, فَلَا فَوْتَ denies successful escape: those described cannot get beyond reach.

Flight is the act of fleeing; فَوْت here describes moving beyond reach.

ف و ت and خ س ر: missing and suffering loss

خ س ر concerns loss, diminution, and losing capital or gains in the Qur’an.

Missing an opportunity may also cause loss, for example in an economic setting.

The roots are nevertheless not synonyms.

Someone may miss an opportunity without suffering actual harm; missing an unsuitable opportunity may even prove beneficial.

Conversely, a poor decision may cause loss without anything having outstripped the person or become unavailable.

فَوْت thus emphasizes nonattainment or passing beyond reach; خُسْر emphasizes loss and diminution.

تَفَاوُت and اخْتِلَاف: difference and inconsistency

اخْتِلَاف belongs to خ ل ف and may denote difference, dissimilarity, or disagreement.

تَفَاوُت also denotes distance or disparity between things in Arabic.

They can be close in meaning, but al-Mulk 67:3 emphasizes the absence of inconsistency.

Al-Rūm 30:22, conversely, counts differing human languages and colors among creation’s signs.

This does not establish that اخْتِلَاف is always commendable and تَفَاوُت always blameworthy.

Context determines the kind of difference and its evaluation.

ف و ت and ن ف د: missing and exhaustion

نَفِدَ concerns a supply or quantity running out.

فَاتَ does not necessarily mean the thing itself has ended.

If a vessel’s water is exhausted, its supply has run out.

If someone cannot reach water still in the vessel, however, the water is not necessarily exhausted; it is merely unavailable to that person.

فَوْت should therefore not be confined to destruction or depletion.

ف و ت and و ف ي: فَوَات and وَفَات

In Persian, the expression based on فَوْت is also commonly used for dying.

This Persian usage should not be projected onto every Qur’anic form of ف و ت.

فَوَات belongs to ف و ت and concerns being missed or passing away from reach.

وَفَات and تَوَفَّى, by contrast, belong to و ف ي and require separate study.

Despite their visual resemblance, وَفَات and فَوَات do not have the same root.

Qur’anic فَاتَكُمْ does not mean the death of the addressees or those discussed, nor does فَلَا فَوْتَ itself name death.

This distinction prevents conflation of two lexical families.

9. Translation proposals for Forqan

Principal equivalents for فَاتَ include “was missed,” “passed,” and “went beyond reach.”

In some constructions, “failed to obtain” or “could not reach” may convey the sentence’s meaning.

For فَوْت, “missing,” “nonattainment,” or, in a flight context, “escape from reach” may fit.

For فَوَات, “loss of opportunity” or “being missed” is natural.

فَائِت may be “missed,” “past,” or “no longer attainable,” but this active participle does not occur in the Qur’an.

تَفَاوَتَ can mean “differed,” “became separated,” or “became inconsistent,” depending on context.

Possible equivalents for تَفَاوُت include “difference,” “inconsistency,” “incompatibility,” and “lack of proportion.”

Qur’anic translation must determine each form’s precise contextual meaning.

Equivalents for the five Qur’anic occurrences

Āl ʿImrān 3:153 — فَاتَكُمْ

“What escaped you.”

Here an opportunity or outcome was missed or not obtained.

Al-Ḥadīd 57:23 — فَاتَكُمْ

“What has escaped you.”

This contrasts with what has been given.

Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:11 — فَاتَكُمْ

“Departed from your marital relationship,” or, closer to the literal construction, “were lost to you.”

The subject is spouses and loss of a marital relationship. Contextual explanation matters to avoid interpreting a person as property.

Sabaʾ 34:51 — فَوْتَ

“Escape” in فَلَا فَوْتَ.

“There is no escape” is natural and accurate.

Al-Mulk 67:3 — تَفَاوُتٍ

“Inconsistency.”

“You see no inconsistency” is more precise here than “you see no difference”: the verse denies incompatibility in creation, not created diversity.

Conclusion

ف و ت concerns passing beyond reach, failing to attain, and losing the opportunity to obtain something.

The poem attributed to Zuhayr uses فَاتَ for youth’s departed days. Classical usages also concern missed opportunities, distance that prevents reaching, and moving beyond another’s reach.

فَاتَكُمْ occurs three times. Āl ʿImrān 3:153 and al-Ḥadīd 57:23 concern missed opportunities, outcomes, or things possessed or anticipated; al-Mumtaḥanah 60:11 concerns loss of a marital relationship.

فَوْت in Sabaʾ 34:51 denotes the possibility of escaping reach; its negation denies escape.

تَفَاوُت in al-Mulk 67:3 concerns inconsistency and lack of proportion, not creatures’ natural diversity. The Qur’an itself presents diverse languages, colors, and other features as signs of creation.

Final root definition for Forqan:

ف و ت: something passing beyond reach, failure to attain it, or the passing of an opportunity, whether involving an object, opportunity, outcome, relationship, or possible escape.

In تَفَاوَتَ, this extends to distance or disparity between things, which may denote ordinary difference or lack of proportion and harmony according to context.

In Qur’anic use, فَاتَكُمْ concerns what is missed, فَوْت the possibility of escape, and تَفَاوُت in al-Mulk 67:3 inconsistency in creation.

All three belong to one lexical family, but each precise meaning comes from its construction and the verse’s context.
