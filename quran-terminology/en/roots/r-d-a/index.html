Support, assistance, and strengthening another

In one of its principal branches, Arabic R D ʾ denotes helping, supporting, and strengthening someone or something. Ridʾ names a person or means that helps another, increases their strength, or provides backing in a task.

This appears both in human assistance and in physical uses, such as supporting a wall with a prop.

R D ʾ is not restricted to assistance. Radīʾ, poor in quality, objectionable, or corrupt, also belongs to this family with final hamzah. The branches differ fundamentally in meaning; shared letters alone do not justify deriving one from the other.

The root occurs once in the Qur’an, as ridʾan. Moses asks The God to send his brother Aaron with him as support in his mission.

Qur’anic form: ridʾan (Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:34).

Arabic also knows ridʾ, ardāʾ, radaʾa, ardaʾa, tarādaʾa, raduʾa, radīʾ, and radāʾah. These do not occur as such in the Qur’an, and their different meanings require separate examination.

1. Semantic branches and early Arabic usage

Support and assistance

Ridʾ can denote someone who helps and supports another.

Fulānun ridʾun li-fulānin means that someone helps another person, supports them, and reinforces their strength.

Lexical explanations use words such as ʿawn, nāṣir, quwwah, and ʿimād to explain this meaning.

These words are related but not identical.

ʿAwn means help; nāṣir is someone who supports, particularly amid opposition or confrontation; ʿimād is a support on which something rests securely.

Ridʾ can approach these meanings in different settings, with a central use of providing support and added strength or backing.

The supporter need not replace the principal person. They can accompany another and provide greater capacity for the same task.

Nor does ridʾ necessarily imply someone inferior in character, knowledge, or standing to the person assisted. Different roles in a mission do not establish inherent superiority or inferiority.

Radaʾa al-ḥāʾiṭa: supporting a wall

Supporting a wall is a physical use of this root.

Early lexical usage records the expression:

رَدَأْتُ الْحَائِطَ أَرْدَؤُهُ

radaʾtu al-ḥāʾiṭa ardaʾuhu

“I propped the wall with a support.”

It concerns a wall strengthened with timber or a supporting structure to prevent its collapse.

Radaʾtu fulānan bi-kadhā is also used for strengthening someone by providing something.

This is compared to strengthening a wall with a structure attached to it.

Support is thus established both in human relationships and in maintaining a physical structure.

The example does not establish wall construction as the root’s earliest historical meaning, with all human uses derived from building practice.

Semantic similarity alone cannot establish such a history.

What can confidently be identified is two related uses: reinforcing something with a support, and helping someone who needs backing in a task.

Ridʾ in baggage and loads

Ridʾ is also recorded for one of two heavy loads placed on opposite sides of a mount.

Its plural in this use is ardāʾ.

ʿIdl names one of the two counterbalancing loads carried on a mount’s sides.

Explanations of ridʾ mention these loads because the two parts accompany and balance one another.

The image is close to backing and reinforcement.

It does not establish the origin of every meaning. Just as assistance cannot certainly be derived from propping a wall, its meaning of helper cannot certainly be derived from paired camel loads.

These are actual, related usages, but their historical order is unclear.

The balance of two loads is not an invariable meaning of ridʾ in human relations. One person can support another without equal capacities, responsibilities, or standing.

Ardaʾa and tarādaʾa

Ardaʾa fulānan means helping someone in one of its uses.

Tarādaʾa al-qawmu describes a group helping one another.

Support can thus be unilateral or mutual.

In ardaʾa fulānan, one person helps another.

In tarādaʾa al-qawmu, group members cooperate and assist one another.

Form VI alone nevertheless does not specify the quality of cooperation, complete equality, or each person’s contribution. Usage and context must supply those details.

Poetic witnesses and their limits

Early literary and exegetical transmissions attribute this verse to the pre-Islamic poet Salāmah ibn Jandal:

وَرِدْئِي كُلُّ أَبْيَضَ مَشْرَفِيٍّ
شَحِيذِ الْحَدِّ عَضْبٍ ذِي فُلُولِ

wa-ridʾī kullu abyaḍa mashrafiyyin / shaḥīdhi al-ḥaddi ʿaḍbin dhī fulūli

“My support is every shining Mashrafī sword, keen of edge and sharp, with notches in its blade.”

Ridʾī means my support here.

The poet calls his sword his support. Assistance is not confined to another human being: an instrument used amid danger and confrontation can receive the same description.

Transmissions differ over some sword epithets, including ʿaḍbin and abyaḍa in the second half.

Although early transmissions attribute it to Salāmah ibn Jandal, that attribution alone does not establish its composition date or every transmitted reading. It should be treated as a transmitted early literary witness, with caution about calling it a certainly dated pre-Islamic text.

Another verse, with no identified poet, states:

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ أَصْرَمَ كَانَ رِدْئِي
وَخَيْرَ النَّاسِ فِي قُلٍّ وَمَالِ

a-lam tara anna aṣrama kāna ridʾī / wa-khayra al-nāsi fī qullin wa-māli

“Did you not see that Aṣram was my support, and among the best of people in poverty and wealth?”

Ridʾī here directly describes a person who supported and helped the speaker.

Without an identified poet or composition date, it should not unreservedly be treated as certain evidence from before the Qur’an.

These witnesses and lexical accounts establish ridʾ for human support and support provided through an instrument or means.

2. Radīʾ: the separate branch of poor quality and corruption

Alongside support, R D ʾ has another branch concerning poor quality, corruption, and objectionable character.

Raduʾa al-shayʾu means that something became poor, corrupt, or objectionable.

Radīʾ describes something undesirable or low in quality.

Radāʾah names that condition.

Ardaʾa al-shayʾa can mean spoiling something or making it poor in quality.

This differs clearly from ardaʾa fulānan meaning helping someone.

One lexical analysis distinguishes these branches as two very different uses of the family with final hamzah. Other connections have also been proposed, but those proposals alone do not establish a historical derivation.

Shared root letters do not establish support and poor quality as historically one meaning.

Nor should a story be invented in which a supporter was originally weak or lowly, thereby supposedly producing radīʾ’s meaning of poor quality.

Without historical evidence, that explanation is indefensible.

Radīʾ may describe a weak thing or incapable person in some uses, but that does not justify translating Qur’anic ridʾ as a weak or subordinate person.

Conversely, the positive use of ridʾ in Moses’ story should not cause radīʾ to be excluded from the family’s study.

Both branches are recorded; Qur’anic ridʾan belongs to assistance and support.

3. Fa-arsilhu maʿiya ridʾan: the Qur’anic occurrence

The only Qur’anic occurrence is in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:34:

وَأَخِي هَارُونُ هُوَ أَفْصَحُ مِنِّي لِسَانًا فَأَرْسِلْهُ مَعِيَ رِدْءًا يُصَدِّقُنِي إِنِّي أَخَافُ أَنْ يُكَذِّبُونِ

wa-akhī hārūnu huwa afṣaḥu minnī lisānan fa-arsilhu maʿiya ridʾan yuṣaddiqunī innī akhāfu an yukadhdhibūni

“My brother Aaron is more eloquent than I. Send him with me as support, to confirm me; I fear they will reject me as false.”

Moses asks The God to send his brother Aaron with him.

The verse itself gives his reasons: Aaron has greater eloquence, and Moses fears rejection by his audience.

Why does Moses request support?

The request should be read with the preceding verse, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:33:

قَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّي قَتَلْتُ مِنْهُمْ نَفْسًا فَأَخَافُ أَنْ يَقْتُلُونِ

qāla rabbi innī qataltu minhum nafsan fa-akhāfu an yaqtulūni

“He said: My Lord, I killed one of them, and I fear they will kill me.”

Moses describes a danger he may encounter on returning to Pharaoh and his associates.

The next verse raises eloquence and possible rejection.

Aaron’s company is thus requested amid both physical danger and the difficulty of conveying and defending the message.

Neither concern should become part of ridʾ’s definition.

Ridʾ means support; Moses’ words and mission establish the kind of support needed.

Afṣaḥu minnī lisānan

Part of Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:34 states:

هُوَ أَفْصَحُ مِنِّي لِسَانًا

huwa afṣaḥu minnī lisānan

“He is more eloquent than I.”

Afṣaḥ is a comparative from F Ṣ Ḥ, concerning Aaron’s greater ability to express speech here.

Lisānan is a specifying accusative, identifying the field of this comparison.

The request for Aaron’s company is directly connected to his expressive ability.

Eloquence is not part of ridʾ’s own meaning.

Not every supporter is eloquent, nor is every eloquent person a supporter of someone else.

Aaron is requested as a companion and supporter because of his particular ability in this situation.

Fa-arsilhu maʿiya

Fa-arsilhu requests that Aaron be sent; maʿiya expresses his accompanying Moses.

Ridʾan is accusative, functioning as a circumstantial expression concerning the object pronoun of arsilhu. Moses asks for Aaron to be sent with him in the role of a supporter.

Ridʾan thus describes Aaron’s role while accompanying him.

The construction does not make Aaron a powerless servant, repeating Moses’ words without thought or independent responsibility.

The supporting role is clear, while other verses establish the mission’s fuller character.

Yuṣaddiqunī: support through confirmation

In Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:34, ridʾan is followed by:

يُصَدِّقُنِي

yuṣaddiqunī

“He confirms me.”

The verb comes from Ṣ D Q and concerns confirmation and affirmation.

Aaron is requested as a supporter who confirms Moses.

Being a supporter and confirming someone must nevertheless be distinguished.

Ridʾ names the supporting role; yuṣaddiqunī explains a manner or result of that support in Moses’ particular situation.

Confirmation should not be restricted to one brief statement such as “Moses speaks truth.”

Moses’ reference to Aaron’s eloquence indicates that his ability to express and clarify speech matters for the mission.

Explanation and argument emerge from the connected sentences and parallel passages, rather than from ridʾ itself.

Variant readings and the syntax of yuṣaddiqunī

Recognized Qur’anic readings include two familiar forms concerning the verb’s ending.

One has the indicative yuṣaddiqunī.

Its clause can describe ridʾan: Aaron is a supporter who confirms Moses.

Another has the jussive yuṣaddiqnī.

This can answer the request fa-arsilhu: send him with me, so that he will confirm me.

The syntax differs, but both connect Aaron’s support with confirmation of Moses.

The variation therefore does not alter ridʾ’s basic meaning.

Ridʾan itself is also transmitted without hamzah as ridan. A familiar explanation transfers the hamzah’s vowel to dāl and omits hamzah for easier pronunciation.

This phonetic difference does not itself establish another root.

Some early analyses also associate the form without hamzah with increase or addition. The proposal involves disputed lexical and poetic evidence and does not displace the clear contextual meaning of support.

4. Moses’ request and the divine answer: related passages

Wa-ijʿal lī wazīran min ahlī

Ṭā Hā 20:29, Ṭā Hā 20:30, Ṭā Hā 20:31, and Ṭā Hā 20:32 give a fuller version of Moses’ request:

وَاجْعَلْ لِي وَزِيرًا مِنْ أَهْلِي ۝ هَارُونَ أَخِي ۝ اشْدُدْ بِهِ أَزْرِي ۝ وَأَشْرِكْهُ فِي أَمْرِي

wa-ijʿal lī wazīran min ahlī / hārūna akhī / ishdud bihi azrī / wa-ashrikhu fī amrī

“Appoint for me a helper from my family: Aaron, my brother. Strengthen my back through him, and let him share in my task.”

Several expressions approach ridʾ’s meaning here.

Moses first requests a wazīr, someone to help with a task and its responsibility.

He then asks for his strength and backing to be reinforced through Aaron.

Finally he asks that Aaron share in his task.

Moses does not merely seek traveling company, but someone participating in the mission.

The different words should nevertheless be distinguished.

Wazīr belongs to another root and concerns assistance in bearing responsibility.

Azr concerns backing and strength.

Ashrikhu comes from SH R K and expresses participation.

These words are close in Moses’ request without being complete synonyms of ridʾ.

The divine answer: sa-nashuddu ʿaḍudaka bi-akhīka

The next verse, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:35, states:

قَالَ سَنَشُدُّ عَضُدَكَ بِأَخِيكَ وَنَجْعَلُ لَكُمَا سُلْطَانًا فَلَا يَصِلُونَ إِلَيْكُمَا بِآيَاتِنَا أَنْتُمَا وَمَنِ اتَّبَعَكُمَا الْغَالِبُونَ

qāla sa-nashuddu ʿaḍudaka bi-akhīka wa-najʿalu lakumā sulṭānan fa-lā yaṣilūna ilaykumā bi-āyātinā antumā wa-mani ittabaʿakumā al-ghālibūna

“He said: We will strengthen your arm through your brother and give you both authority, so they will not reach you. Through Our signs, you and your followers will prevail.”

This answer clarifies Moses’ request for support.

The God says that He will strengthen Moses’ arm through his brother.

ʿAḍud denotes the upper arm; shadd concerns strengthening and making firm.

Strengthening an arm can image increased ability and backing in Arabic.

This approaches ridʾ: someone’s assistance increases another’s capacity for a task.

The similarity does not make ridʾ originally mean an arm or bodily member.

The present root concerns support; the arm image comes from another expression.

Did Aaron merely confirm Moses?

In Ṭā Hā, Moses requests Aaron’s participation in the mission.

Moses also says in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:13:

وَيَضِيقُ صَدْرِي وَلَا يَنْطَلِقُ لِسَانِي فَأَرْسِلْ إِلَى هَارُونَ

wa-yaḍīqu ṣadrī wa-lā yanṭaliqu lisānī fa-arsil ilā hārūna

“My breast tightens and my tongue does not flow freely; send for Aaron.”

The divine command then comes in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:15:

قَالَ كَلَّا فَاذْهَبَا بِآيَاتِنَا إِنَّا مَعَكُمْ مُسْتَمِعُونَ

qāla kallā fa-idhhabā bi-āyātinā innā maʿakum mustamiʿūna

“He said: No. Go, both of you, with Our signs; We are with you, listening.”

The dual command fa-idhhabā directs both of them to go.

Ṭā Hā 20:43 and Ṭā Hā 20:44 also commission Moses and Aaron together to go to Pharaoh and speak to him.

Together these passages give Aaron a real role in the mission.

Ridʾ should therefore not be restricted to someone who merely repeats another’s words.

Nor do these passages establish identical roles for Moses and Aaron in every detail.

The initial request is Moses’, seeking Aaron’s support. Subsequently both participate in the mission.

Their roles can differ while complementing one another.

Support and the ability to communicate

Expressive ability and answering opposition clearly matter in Moses’ story.

Moses speaks of rejection and difficulty of expression, and requests Aaron because of his greater eloquence.

The support sought is more than an increased number of companions.

Someone who can explain more clearly and help convey the message amid opposition meets a particular need of Moses.

This story does not make ridʾ always mean spokesperson, media representative, defense lawyer, or person responsible for argument.

Those can exemplify support in different settings, but are not inherent in the word.

5. Comparison with related roots

R D ʾ (ridʾ) and ʿ W N (ʿawn)

ʿAwn denotes help and assistance.

Ridʾ likewise names a supporter or helper in its principal supporting use.

They can be close in many sentences.

ʿAwn is, for example, used to explain ridʾ lexically.

Some ridʾ uses emphasize reinforcement and a supporting role.

No absolute boundary makes ʿawn merely temporary help and ridʾ invariably permanent support.

One limited Qur’anic occurrence does not establish that distinction.

Study of ʿ W N

R D ʾ (ridʾ) and N Ṣ R (naṣr)

Naṣr also means help and support in the Qur’an, particularly amid hostility and opposition.

Ridʾ can approach nāṣir in that respect.

In Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:34, Moses fears rejection and opposition from Pharaoh and his people. Aaron’s assistance can therefore have a defensive and supportive aspect.

Ridʾ also has physical uses in reinforcing a wall or supporting loads.

It should not always be restricted to victory over an enemy.

Study of N Ṣ R

R D ʾ (ridʾ) and W Z R (wazīr)

Moses requests a wazīr in Ṭā Hā 20:29, and a ridʾ in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:34.

Both refer to Aaron in the same setting, while belonging to different word families.

Wazīr concerns helping to bear a burden and responsibility in Arabic.

Ridʾ concerns supporting and strengthening another.

Their use for Aaron shows how these semantic fields can converge in one person.

Wazīr here should not directly be equated with the modern political office of minister.

Nor does their association make ridʾ an administrative or governmental title in every Arabic usage.

R D ʾ (ridʾ) and ʾ Z R (azr)

Azr in ishdud bihi azrī, Ṭā Hā 20:31, concerns firm backing and strengthened capacity.

Its imagery is close to support.

ʾ Z R and R D ʾ should nevertheless remain distinct roots.

The expressions converge because they describe the same need and mission in Moses’ story.

One expresses reinforcement; the other identifies the supporter’s role.

Study of ʾ Z R

R D ʾ (ridʾ) and R D D (radd)

The roots look similar but differ in structure.

R D ʾ has final hamzah, whereas R D D repeats its second consonant.

Qur’anic radd concerns returning, answering, or repelling.

For example, part of Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:13 states:

فَرَدَدْنَاهُ إِلَى أُمِّهِ

fa-radadnāhu ilā ummihi

“We returned him to his mother.”

Radadnāhu here comes from R D D, unrelated to ridʾan meaning a supporter.

Shared first letters do not make the roots identical.

Study of R D D

R D ʾ (ridʾ) and R D Y (radā)

R D Y also resembles R D ʾ in form.

Qur’anic taraddā and ardā belong to another field concerning falling, perishing, and causing destruction.

Al-Layl 92:11 states:

وَمَا يُغْنِي عَنْهُ مَالُهُ إِذَا تَرَدَّى

wa-mā yughnī ʿanhu māluhu idhā taraddā

“What will his wealth avail him when he falls to destruction?”

Taraddā here concerns falling and perishing, rather than helping.

Ridʾ should not acquire that meaning through superficial resemblance.

Ridāʾ, an outer garment or covering, should likewise not be equated with ridʾ meaning support without derivational evidence.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

The assistance and poor-quality branches must be distinguished.

Ridʾ: a supporter, helper, or means of strengthening another.

Ridʾan: as support, or in the role of a helper. “To help” or “for support” can be natural in the Qur’anic context.

Ardāʾ: supporters, plural of ridʾ; in another use, heavy counterbalancing loads.

Radaʾa fulānan: helped or supported someone.

Radaʾa al-ḥāʾiṭa: propped a wall with a support.

Ardaʾa fulānan: helped someone, in the supporting branch.

Tarādaʾa al-qawmu: the group helped one another.

Raduʾa: became poor in quality or corrupt, in the other semantic branch.

Radīʾ: poor in quality, objectionable, corrupt, inferior.

Radāʾah: poor quality, corruption, objectionable condition.

Part of Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:34, the root’s only Qur’anic occurrence, reads:

فَأَرْسِلْهُ مَعِيَ رِدْءًا يُصَدِّقُنِي

fa-arsilhu maʿiya ridʾan yuṣaddiqunī

“Send him with me as support, to confirm me.”

This naturally conveys support and companionship.

“Helper” and “supporter” also suit an independent explanation of the word.

The basic translation should not restrict ridʾ to spokesperson, minister, legal defender, substitute, or obedient subordinate.

Those titles either come from other words or describe particular instances of cooperation.

“A backing that strengthens the mission” may help explain the word, but is heavier than “helper” or “supporter” and unnecessary for direct translation.

7. Conclusion

R D ʾ has two distinct semantic branches in Arabic.

The first concerns support, assistance, and strengthening another. Ridʾ names someone or something providing backing.

Lexical evidence shows this in human relationships and physical uses such as propping a wall.

The second concerns poor quality, corruption, and objectionable character, in forms such as raduʾa, radīʾ, and radāʾah. It should not be derived historically from support without evidence.

The sole Qur’anic occurrence, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:34, belongs to the first branch.

Moses asks The God to send Aaron with him as support. The context supplies his reasons: Aaron is more eloquent, and Moses fears rejection and opposition.

Other passages express the request through wazīr, azr, and strengthening the arm, and describe Aaron’s participation in the mission.

Ridʾ here is more than one additional companion: it concerns real support in carrying out the mission.

The mission’s every feature should not be transferred into the root’s definition.

Being a supporter does not inherently mean spokesperson, fully equal partner in all powers, powerless subordinate, or inferior person. Such features require context and independent evidence.

Final definition: In R D ʾ’s supporting branch, ridʾ names a person or means whose assistance and company strengthen another’s capacity or backing. The Qur’an requests Aaron as Moses’ helper and supporter. The same family’s other branch, including radīʾ and radāʾah, concerns poor quality and corruption and should not be equated with support.
