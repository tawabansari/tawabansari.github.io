Foundational Meaning

The root س ك ن fundamentally means to become calm, for movement or anxiety to subside, to settle into shelter and stability, and to reach a state of rest after movement, fear, dispersal, or restlessness.

This root does not merely indicate motionlessness. It more often concerns becoming calm, finding a place, finding shelter, and becoming settled in a safer and more stable condition.

At the same time, not every form of this root is positive calm. س ك ن has a sheltering and life-giving branch, such as rest, home, night, spouse, and sakinah. It also has a lowering and weakening branch, such as maskanah and miskin, which point to being lowered, weakened, and pinned down under the pressure of need.

Important Forms

سَكَنَ in Form I means he became calm, dwelled, settled, or found rest in a place. أَسْكَنَ in Form IV means to cause to dwell, to place, to give dwelling, or to settle something somewhere.

سَكَن as a noun can mean calm, a source of calm, or a place of rest. سَكِينَة is sent-down calm, stability of heart, and divine calm in conditions of anxiety, fear, or trial.

مَسْكَن and مَسَاكِن point to a place of dwelling and living. مَسْكُونَة means inhabited or populated. سَاكِن means calm, still, stable, or motionless.

مِسْكِين and مَسْكَنَة are a specific branch of this root. They refer to a person or condition lowered and limited in power, social movement, or ability to escape pressure. This branch must not be confused with sakinah. The miskin is not safely calm; he is someone whom poverty or weakness has placed in a low and powerless condition.

Linguistic Note

The semantic center of س ك ن turns around movement subsiding and reaching settledness. This movement may be bodily, such as moving from one home to another, or psychological, such as anxiety, fear, restlessness, or inner disturbance.

Therefore, sukun in the Qur'an is not always dry or dead stillness. Night is for sukun because it settles the movement and outward activity of the day. A house is sakan because a human being returns from dispersal and finds rest in it. A spouse can be a place of sukun because the heart and self come from loneliness and anxiety into settledness through a deep relation.

Sakinah is a calm that The God sends upon the heart so that, in a difficult moment, the heart moves from trembling to stability. But when this semantic field turns from shelter and calm toward weakness and being lowered, it reaches maskanah. Maskanah is not calm; it is a kind of lowered condition, humiliation, and restricted capacity to move.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is wind or water becoming calm after turbulence; or a person entering the home and sitting after travel, fatigue, and dispersal; or a heart becoming stable after fear and trembling.

In س ك ن, there is always a kind of descent from movement into settledness. Not necessarily lifeless stoppage, but resting in the proper place. Home, night, spouse, earth, heart, water, and even wind can all be fields of this root.

In the branch of maskanah, the same settling appears negatively: the ability to move and rise is reduced, the human being is held low under need or humiliation, and social agency is weakened.

Older and Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic, سكن is used for becoming calm, dwelling, taking residence, and the subsiding of movement. سكن الدار means he dwelled in the house. سكن إليه means he found calm toward him or settled near him. سكنت الريح means the wind became still. سكن القلب means the heart became calm.

In a later expanded version, a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or early Arabic usage may be added. Without a clear witness, no specific historical or poetic claim should be forced into the text.

Qur'anic Usage

One foundational use of this root is Adam and his spouse dwelling in the garden:

وَقُلْنَا يَا آدَمُ اسْكُنْ أَنْتَ وَزَوْجُكَ الْجَنَّةَ

And We said: O Adam, dwell, you and your spouse, in the garden. Al-Baqarah 2:35

Here اسْكُنْ is not merely entering a place. It is settling and living in a space given to them as a place of staying, eating, and finding rest.

The same meaning appears regarding dwelling in a land or town:

وَإِذْ قِيلَ لَهُمُ اسْكُنُوا هَٰذِهِ الْقَرْيَةَ

And when it was said to them: Dwell in this town. Al-A'raf 7:161

اسْكُنُوا here points to settling in a living environment. Dwelling is not passing presence; it is a kind of social and living settlement.

Night, Spouse, Salat, and Home

One of the clearest uses of the root concerns the night:

هُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ اللَّيْلَ لِتَسْكُنُوا فِيهِ وَالنَّهَارَ مُبْصِرًا

He is the One who made the night for you so that you may find rest in it, and the day as visible. Yunus 10:67

In this verse, the night is لِتَسْكُنُوا فِيهِ: the container of rest after the movement, work, seeing, going and coming, and activity of the day. In contrast, the day is مُبْصِرًا: a field of visibility, activity, seeking, and movement.

وَمِنْ رَحْمَتِهِ جَعَلَ لَكُمُ اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ لِتَسْكُنُوا فِيهِ وَلِتَبْتَغُوا مِنْ فَضْلِهِ

And from His mercy, He made for you the night and the day so that you may find rest in it and seek from His bounty. Al-Qasas 28:73

Here rest and seeking bounty stand together. Life is not only movement and not only stillness. Night and day are placed in a balance between finding rest and seeking provision.

Another use is rest in the marital relation:

وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ أَنْ خَلَقَ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا لِتَسْكُنُوا إِلَيْهَا

And among His signs is that He created for you spouses from yourselves so that you may find rest toward them. Ar-Rum 30:21

In this verse, إِلَيْهَا matters. The Qur'an does not merely say that you find rest in them or beside them; it says that you find rest toward them. The sukun here has direction: the heart and self move toward another and find emotional and existential shelter there.

وَجَعَلَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا لِيَسْكُنَ إِلَيْهَا

And He made from her her spouse so that he may find rest toward her. Al-A'raf 7:189

سكن is also used as a source of calm:

وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ إِنَّ صَلَاتَكَ سَكَنٌ لَهُمْ

And perform salat over them; indeed your salat is a source of calm for them. At-Tawbah 9:103

The home is also called sakan:

وَاللَّهُ جَعَلَ لَكُمْ مِنْ بُيُوتِكُمْ سَكَنًا

And The God made for you, from your houses, a place of rest. An-Nahl 16:80

A home is not only roof and wall. From this root's perspective, it is a place of rest, safety, return from dispersal, and human calm.

Sakinah

سَكِينَة is one of the most important derivatives of this root. Sakinah is calm that The God sends upon hearts in moments of anxiety, fear, battle, instability, or trial:

هُوَ الَّذِي أَنْزَلَ السَّكِينَةَ فِي قُلُوبِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ لِيَزْدَادُوا إِيمَانًا مَعَ إِيمَانِهِمْ

He is the One who sent down sakinah into the hearts of the believers so that they may increase in faith along with their faith. Al-Fath 48:4

Here sakinah is something in the heart: a calm that makes faith firmer. Sakinah is not merely a pleasant feeling or psychological comfort. It is a divine stability in the heart that makes a human being steadier on the path of faith.

ثُمَّ أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ سَكِينَتَهُ عَلَىٰ رَسُولِهِ وَعَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ

Then The God sent down His sakinah upon His Messenger and upon the believers. At-Tawbah 9:26

In this context, sakinah comes after the anxiety of battle and trial. This calm is the descent of stability from The God.

فَأَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ سَكِينَتَهُ عَلَيْهِ

So The God sent down His sakinah upon him. At-Tawbah 9:40

Here sakinah is linked to fear, pursuit, and danger. The God sends down a calm that moves the heart from trembling into settledness.

Maskan and Masakin

مَسْكَن is a place of dwelling and settlement:

لَقَدْ كَانَ لِسَبَإٍ فِي مَسْكَنِهِمْ آيَةٌ

There was certainly for Saba in their dwelling place a sign. Saba 34:15

مَسَاكِن is the plural of maskan and refers to dwelling places and homes of a people:

وَسَكَنْتُمْ فِي مَسَاكِنِ الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ

And you dwelled in the dwellings of those who wronged themselves. Ibrahim 14:45

Masakin can also become places of reflection, because places that were once sites of rest and stability for a people become, after destruction, signs of the instability of their power and life.

The root is also used regarding water:

وَأَنْزَلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً بِقَدَرٍ فَأَسْكَنَّاهُ فِي الْأَرْضِ

And We sent down from the sky water in measure, then We settled it in the earth. Al-Mu'minun 23:18

Here أَسْكَنَّاهُ means that the descending and flowing water was settled in the earth. This use clarifies the material image of the root: something fluid and moving reaches a stable and stored place.

Miskin and Maskanah

The branch of مِسْكِين and مَسْكَنَة does not mean positive calm. A miskin is someone whose poverty, weakness, or life pressure has placed him in a lowered and weakened condition. Socially or economically, he does not have easy power of movement or exit from his condition.

وَضُرِبَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ الذِّلَّةُ وَالْمَسْكَنَةُ

And humiliation and maskanah were struck upon them. Al-Baqarah 2:61

مَسْكَنَة here is not calm. It is being lowered, weakened, and humiliated. Therefore, سَكِينَة and مَسْكَنَة must be kept fully distinct: sakinah is divine calm and stability of heart; maskanah is being brought low and deprived of power.

The Qur'an repeatedly commands care for the مِسْكِين:

وَيُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِ مِسْكِينًا وَيَتِيمًا وَأَسِيرًا

And they feed food, despite loving it, to the miskin, the orphan, and the captive. Al-Insan 76:8

The miskin here is someone whose need and weakness must be answered with generosity and giving. From the root perspective, the miskin is one whose need has lowered his movement and capacity for life.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

سكن in the night and سكن in marriage are not identical, though they come from the same semantic center. Night is the container of rest from the movement of the day; the spouse is an existential and emotional shelter of rest.

سكن as home is also not the same as inward سكينة. The home is an outward place of rest; sakinah is calm sent down into the heart.

مسكن is not the same as مسكين. Maskan is a place of dwelling; miskin is a person in a condition of need, weakness, or being lowered.

The sukun of wind or shadow is also not the same as the sakinah of believers. In wind and shadow, sukun is close to stopping or becoming still. In the heart of the believer, sakinah is spiritual and faithful stability.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In لِتَسْكُنُوا فِيهِ, the preposition فِي shows that the night is the container of rest; the human being finds calm in it.

In لِتَسْكُنُوا إِلَيْهَا and لِيَسْكُنَ إِلَيْهَا, the preposition إِلَى matters. Here rest is directional: a human being finds rest toward the spouse, not merely beside or inside the spouse.

In سَكَنٌ لَهُمْ, the لِـ shows that the Prophet's salat is a source of calm for them, something that works for their benefit and for the settling of their hearts.

In أَنْزَلَ السَّكِينَةَ فِي قُلُوبِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ, sakinah descends into hearts. This shows that sakinah has an inward location and relates to stability of faith.

In أَسْكَنَّاهُ فِي الْأَرْضِ regarding water, Form IV shows that The God placed and settled the water in the earth; the movement and flow of water became storage and settledness in the earth.

Difference From Nearby Roots

ه د أ can be close to becoming calm and settling down, but س ك ن focuses more on finding settledness and shelter after movement or anxiety.

ث ب ت relates to stability and firmness. Sukun can lead to stability, but thabat focuses more on remaining firm and not slipping; sukun focuses on the settling of movement and becoming calm.

ق ر ر relates to settledness, coolness of the eye, and establishment. سكن is close to qarar, but it more strongly carries the sense of becoming calm after movement or anxiety.

ر ك د relates to stopping and becoming motionless, especially regarding water or wind. سكن is broader and includes dwelling, calm of the heart, and human shelter.

ط م أ ن relates to deep reassurance and calm of the heart. Sakinah is close to this field, but the root س ك ن comes from the image of movement settling and finding rest.

أ و ي relates to taking refuge and finding shelter. سكن may include shelter, but its main axis is becoming calm and settled.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

سكن: became calm, settled, dwelled, found rest.

اسكن: caused to dwell, placed, gave dwelling, settled.

لِتَسْكُنُوا فِيهِ: so that you may find rest in it.

لِتَسْكُنُوا إِلَيْهَا: so that you may find rest toward them / so that you may find settledness and calm with them.

سَكَنٌ لَهُمْ: a source of calm for them, a calming support for them.

سَكَن: calm, shelter, place of rest.

سَكِينَة: sakinah, divine calm, sent-down stability of heart.

مَسْكَن: dwelling place, home, shelter.

مَسَاكِن: homes, dwelling places.

مَسْكُونَة: inhabited, populated, dwelled-in.

مِسْكِين: needy one, weakened needy person, one whose need has lowered his capacity.

مَسْكَنَة: maskanah, lowered condition, humiliation, weakness, and lack of power.

Linguistic Summary

The root س ك ن indicates becoming calm, the settling of movement or anxiety, finding settledness, and finding shelter. In the Qur'an, this root appears in relation to night, home, spouse, earth, water, wind, the hearts of believers, and places of dwelling, and in another branch it reaches miskin and maskanah.

Sukun in the Qur'an is not dead motionlessness. It is a kind of settledness after movement, stability after anxiety, and shelter after dispersal. But in the branch of maskanah, this settling appears negatively and socially: lowered condition, humiliation, poverty, and weakened capacity.

The difference between سكن and stability, settledness, stillness, reassurance, and refuge must be preserved, because س ك ن has material, psychological, social, and faithful dimensions.
