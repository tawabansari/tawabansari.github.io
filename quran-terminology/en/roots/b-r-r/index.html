Core Meaning

The root ب ر ر in the Qur'an points to expansive goodness, upright conduct, faithfulness, ihsan, openness in good, and standing on firm moral ground. This root appears both in the material field, meaning dry land and open ground opposite the sea, and in the ethical field, meaning stable goodness opposite turbulence, ambiguity, appearance-based religion, sin, and transgression.

In the material image, barr is open, visible, and reliable dry land. In the sea, the human being faces waves, hidden depth, danger, and instability; but in barr, he reaches clear and expansive ground. He can see a path, stand, build, and find direction. In the ethical field, birr plays this same role: firm, visible, expansive, reliable goodness on which a human being and society can build.

So birr is not merely “goodness.” Birr is goodness formed on the firm ground of faith, sidq, faithfulness, taqwa, ihsan, spending, covenant, justice, and care for the human being. Birr is not hidden goodness behind appearances; it is goodness that becomes visible in behavior.

Important Forms

البر in the Qur'an means both dry land and earth, and expansive goodness and upright conduct:

ظَهَرَ الْفَسَادُ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ

Corruption has appeared on land and sea.

Ar-Rum 30:41

And in the ethical field:

لَّيْسَ الْبِرَّ أَن تُوَلُّوا وُجُوهَكُمْ

Birr is not that you turn your faces.

Al-Baqarah 2:177

Birr points to expansive goodness, upright conduct, and firm good:

لَن تَنَالُوا الْبِرَّ حَتَّىٰ تُنفِقُوا مِمَّا تُحِبُّونَ

You will never reach birr until you spend from what you love.

Ali 'Imran 3:92

برا is an adjective or state and appears for good, faithful, gentle, and fitting conduct:

وَبَرًّا بِوَالِدَيْهِ

And he was good and faithful toward his parents.

Maryam 19:14

وَبَرًّا بِوَالِدَتِي

And He made me good and faithful toward my mother.

Maryam 19:32

بررة is plural and points to honored ones who are good, upright, and pure in conduct:

كِرَامٍ بَرَرَةٍ

Honored, good, and upright.

'Abasa 80:16

أبرار is plural and points to the good, the upright, and those who stand on the firm ground of goodness:

إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ لَفِي نَعِيمٍ

Indeed, the abrar are in bliss.

Al-Infitar 82:13

البر also appears as a divine description:

إِنَّهُ هُوَ الْبَرُّ الرَّحِيمُ

Indeed, He is The Barr, The Merciful.

At-Tur 52:28

In this use, البر is a description for the Lord: the source of expansive goodness, faithfulness to promise, lasting ihsan, and mercy.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of ب ر ر moves between two connected fields: the material field of barr opposite bahr, and the ethical field of birr opposite sin, unfaithfulness, appearance-based religion, moral narrowness, and the turbulence of falsehood.

In the material field, barr means dry land, open ground, a visible space, and a reliable surface. In bahr, the human being faces hiddenness, depth, waves, and instability; but in barr, he reaches a visible, clear, standable space. This same image becomes firm goodness in the ethical field. Birr is goodness that is not hidden, ambiguous, performative, or fragile. It is visible, wide, and trustworthy.

For this reason, the Qur'an in Al-Baqarah 2:177 separates birr from appearance-based religion. Birr is not that the human being only turns his face toward a direction. Birr is a whole set of faith, spending, salat, zakat, fulfilling covenant, patience, sidq, and taqwa. That is, birr is a complete moral ground, not one outward marker separated from action.

Material Image

The material image of this root is reaching open and visible dry land after the sea. The sea is moving, wave-filled, and slippery. Its depth is hidden, and the human being cannot stand in it the way he stands on dry land. Dry land is the ground of standing, walking, seeing the path, building, cultivating, and finding direction.

Birr in ethics is like this too. A human being may be lost among the waves of desire, fear, habit, display, power, poverty, wealth, anger, or partisanship. Birr means reaching a ground where truth and righteous action stand together. The human being stands on it, and his conduct comes out of turbulence.

This image does not show only stability; it also shows expansiveness and visibility. Barr is like open land, not a small closed point. Ethical birr too is not one scattered good deed. It is a wide field of upright conduct, faithfulness, spending, ihsan, taqwa, and responsibility.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, barr opposite bahr points to dry land and earth. This same contrast is clear in the Qur'an: البر والبحر. This material use is one important foundation for understanding the root, because it shows that the root is connected with expansiveness, visibility, stability, and being outside the turbulence of water.

In the ethical field, birr is also used for goodness, upright conduct, faithfulness, and ihsan. بر الوالدين in Arabic means goodness and faithfulness toward parents. A barr person is good, faithful, upright, and given to ihsan.

For precise pre-Qur'anic usage, reliable evidence from pre-Qur'anic poetry or ancient Arabic usage should be examined. If reliable evidence from pre-Qur'anic poetry or ancient Arabic usage is found, it can be added. But the two material and ethical fields of the root are clear in Arabic and in the Qur'an: open dry land opposite bahr, and firm goodness opposite moral instability.

Barr Opposite Bahr: The Material Field of the Root

The Qur'an uses barr opposite bahr:

ظَهَرَ الْفَسَادُ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ

Corruption has appeared on land and sea.

Ar-Rum 30:41

In this verse, barr points to dry land and earth. Corruption does not remain in only one part of human life; it appears on land and sea. This material use keeps the root image: barr is visible and expansive ground, opposite bahr, the field of water, depth, and turbulence.

Another verse says:

هُوَ الَّذِي يُسَيِّرُكُمْ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ

He is the One who moves you through land and sea.

Yunus 10:22

Here barr is the field of human movement on land. This use reminds us that the ethical meaning of birr is not separated from the concrete experience of ground, road, stability, visibility, and leaving turbulence.

True Birr Beyond Appearance-Based Religion

The most important verse for the ethical meaning of birr is Al-Baqarah 2:177:

لَّيْسَ الْبِرَّ أَن تُوَلُّوا وُجُوهَكُمْ قِبَلَ الْمَشْرِقِ وَالْمَغْرِبِ وَلَٰكِنَّ الْبِرَّ مَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَالْمَلَائِكَةِ وَالْكِتَابِ وَالنَّبِيِّينَ وَآتَى الْمَالَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِ ذَوِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْيَتَامَىٰ وَالْمَسَاكِينَ وَابْنَ السَّبِيلِ وَالسَّائِلِينَ وَفِي الرِّقَابِ وَأَقَامَ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَى الزَّكَاةَ وَالْمُوفُونَ بِعَهْدِهِمْ إِذَا عَاهَدُوا وَالصَّابِرِينَ فِي الْبَأْسَاءِ وَالضَّرَّاءِ وَحِينَ الْبَأْسِ ۗ أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ صَدَقُوا ۖ وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُتَّقُونَ

Birr is not that you turn your faces toward the east and the west. Rather, birr is in one who has faith in The God, the Last Day, the mala'ikah, the Book, and the prophets; gives wealth, despite loving it, to near kin, orphans, the needy, the wayfarer, those who ask, and in freeing necks; establishes salat; gives zakat; fulfills the covenant when they make covenant; and is patient in hardship, harm, and at the time of pressure. Those are the ones who were truthful, and those are the people of taqwa.

Al-Baqarah 2:177

This verse separates birr from appearance-based religion. Birr is not merely the outward direction of the body or turning the face toward one side. Birr is a whole set of faith, spending, salat, zakat, fulfilling covenant, patience, sidq, and taqwa.

The structure of the verse is very important. The Qur'an does not say, “Birr is the act of one who has faith.” It says: ولكن البر من آمن, meaning birr is embodied in the human being who holds faith and action together. This grammatical shift shows that birr is not an abstract concept hidden behind forms. Birr must be seen in the human being of faith, spending, faithfulness, patience, and sidq.

The end of the verse confirms the same meaning: أولئك الذين صدقوا وأولئك هم المتقون. The people of birr are those who have sidq and are people of taqwa. So birr without sidq and taqwa is only a performance of goodness.

Reaching Birr Through Giving What Is Loved

The Qur'an says:

لَن تَنَالُوا الْبِرَّ حَتَّىٰ تُنفِقُوا مِمَّا تُحِبُّونَ

You will never reach birr until you spend from what you love.

Ali 'Imran 3:92

In this verse, birr is something to be reached. Reaching birr is not only by claim or general intention. The human being must give out from what he loves. This shows that birr is tied to passing beyond self-centeredness and breaking attachment to material beloved things.

Spending here is the path to reaching birr because it brings the human being out of the turbulence of ownership, stinginess, and attachment, and places him on the firm ground of good.

Commanding Birr and the Danger of Forgetting the Self

The Qur'an criticizes those who command people to birr while forgetting themselves:

أَتَأْمُرُونَ النَّاسَ بِالْبِرِّ وَتَنسَوْنَ أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَنتُمْ تَتْلُونَ الْكِتَابَ

Do you command people to birr while forgetting yourselves, while you recite the Book?

Al-Baqarah 2:44

This verse shows that birr can enter language and teaching, but if the speaker himself does not stand on the ground of birr, his speech becomes split. Birr is not only a subject of instruction; it must become real in the person himself.

The religious danger here is clear: a human being may read the Book and call people to birr while he himself is far from it. Such a state turns birr into a slogan.

Cooperation Upon Birr and Taqwa

In Surat Al-Ma'idah it says:

وَتَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْبِرِّ وَالتَّقْوَىٰ وَلَا تَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْإِثْمِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ

Cooperate upon birr and taqwa, and do not cooperate upon sin and transgression.

Al-Ma'idah 5:2

In this verse, birr is joined with taqwa and placed opposite ithm and 'udwan. The contrast is clear: birr is expansive goodness and upright conduct; taqwa is self-guarding and carefulness before the limits of right; ithm is sin and moral burden; 'udwan is transgression and crossing the limit.

So birr is not only individual goodness. The Qur'an speaks of cooperating upon birr. That means society must work together on the ground of good and taqwa, not upon sin and transgression.

Birr Toward Parents: The Opposite of Being Domineering

The Qur'an says about Yahya:

وَبَرًّا بِوَالِدَيْهِ وَلَمْ يَكُن جَبَّارًا عَصِيًّا

And he was good and faithful toward his parents, and he was not domineering and disobedient.

Maryam 19:14

And about 'Isa it says:

وَبَرًّا بِوَالِدَتِي وَلَمْ يَجْعَلْنِي جَبَّارًا شَقِيًّا

And He made me good and faithful toward my mother, and He did not make me domineering and wretched.

Maryam 19:32

In both verses, birr toward parents comes opposite being jabbar. This contrast is very important. A jabbar is someone who behaves with hardness, imposition, pressure, and domination. A barr person, opposite that, creates for the other person a space that is safe, gentle, wide, and respectful.

So birr toward parents is not only a few outward services. Birr toward parents means goodness joined with gentleness, faithfulness, humility, care, ihsan, and avoiding domination. The Qur'an places birr opposite jabbar because birr brings openness and moral safety; being jabbar brings narrowness, pressure, and imposition.

Birr Toward Those Who Did Not Fight You

In Surat Al-Mumtahanah it says:

لَا يَنْهَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنِ الَّذِينَ لَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَلَمْ يُخْرِجُوكُم مِّن دِيَارِكُمْ أَن تَبَرُّوهُمْ وَتُقْسِطُوا إِلَيْهِمْ

The God does not forbid you from showing birr and acting with qist toward those who did not fight you in din and did not drive you out of your homes.

Al-Mumtahanah 60:8

This verse expands the range of birr beyond the inner faith community. Those who have not fought the believers and have not driven them from their homes are the subject of birr and qist. This shows that birr is not only intra-group goodness.

The phrase أن تبروهم comes beside تقسطوا إليهم. Birr is expansive, ihsan-oriented goodness; qist is justice and care for right. The Qur'an uses both regarding relations with non-aggressors. Ihsan should not replace justice, and justice should not prevent the human being from expansive goodness.

Divine Birr: The God as Al-Barr

In Surat At-Tur it says:

إِنَّا كُنَّا مِن قَبْلُ نَدْعُوهُ ۖ إِنَّهُ هُوَ الْبَرُّ الرَّحِيمُ

Before this, we used to call upon Him. Indeed, He is The Barr, The Merciful.

At-Tur 52:28

البر in this verse is a description of The God. When this description is used for The God, its meaning does not remain limited at the created level. He is البر: the source of expansive goodness, faithfulness to promise, lasting ihsan, mercy, and bringing servants to good.

The companionship of البر with الرحيم is also important. Divine birr is not separated from mercy. The expansive goodness of The God is joined with His mercy.

Abrar: The Upright Good

The Qur'an mentions the abrar often:

إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ لَفِي نَعِيمٍ

Indeed, the abrar are in bliss.

Al-Infitar 82:13

And in Surat Al-Mutaffifin:

كَلَّا إِنَّ كِتَابَ الْأَبْرَارِ لَفِي عِلِّيِّينَ

No indeed; the record of the abrar is in 'Illiyyin.

Al-Mutaffifin 83:18

Abrar are those in whom birr has become a stable description. They are not people of one accidental good deed; the direction of their life is placed on the firm ground of goodness, sidq, faithfulness, and taqwa.

In Surat Al-Insan it also says:

إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ يَشْرَبُونَ مِن كَأْسٍ كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا كَافُورًا

Indeed, the abrar drink from a cup whose mixture is kafur.

Al-Insan 76:5

Then their descriptions come in the same context:

يُوفُونَ بِالنَّذْرِ وَيَخَافُونَ يَوْمًا كَانَ شَرُّهُ مُسْتَطِيرًا

They fulfill the vow and fear a day whose evil is widespread.

Al-Insan 76:7

وَيُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِ مِسْكِينًا وَيَتِيمًا وَأَسِيرًا

And they feed food, despite loving it, to the needy, the orphan, and the captive.

Al-Insan 76:8

These verses show that the abrar are people of faithfulness, awareness of the Day of Account, feeding others, and passing beyond beloved things. This continues the same field as Al-Baqarah 2:177 and Ali 'Imran 3:92.

Bararah: Honored Good Ones

In Surat 'Abasa it says:

بِأَيْدِي سَفَرَةٍ

In the hands of envoys,

'Abasa 80:15

كِرَامٍ بَرَرَةٍ

Honored, good, and upright.

'Abasa 80:16

Bararah here comes with kiram. Their goodness is joined with honor, purity of mission, and upright conduct. This use shows that ب ر ر can also describe honored beings who are obedient in carrying the message.

Similar Uses

ب ر ر in the Qur'an has two main fields: the material field and the ethical field. In the material field, barr is open land and dry ground, opposite bahr. In the ethical field, birr is expansive goodness, upright conduct, faithfulness, and standing on the firm ground of good.

Birr in Al-Baqarah 2:177 differs from appearance-based religion. Turning the face toward one side, if separated from faith, spending, fulfilling covenant, patience, salat, zakat, sidq, and taqwa, is not itself birr.

Birr in Ali 'Imran 3:92 is tied to spending from beloved things. Here birr is something a human being reaches, not something he merely claims.

Birr in Maryam 19:14 and 19:32 relates to the relationship with parents. This birr is measured against its opposite: jabbar and 'asi, or jabbar and shaqi.

Birr in Al-Mumtahanah 60:8 relates to non-aggressors. This verse shows that birr is not only intra-group and that it stands together with qist.

Abrar in the verses of resurrection and the Hereafter is a title for those whose direction of life was stable upon goodness.

البر as a divine description shows the expansive and mercy-centered goodness of The God, and it is not the same as limited human goodness.

Grammar and Structure

In ليس البر أن تولوا وجوهكم, the structure of negation separates birr from an outward sign. The verse does not say the direction of qiblah is unimportant; rather, it says birr must not be reduced to turning the face.

In ولكن البر من آمن, from an outward expectation one might expect, “Birr is the act of one who has faith,” or “Birr is that you have faith.” But the verse says, “Birr is one who has faith.” This structure connects birr from an abstract concept to the acting human being. Birr must become concrete in the being and conduct of the human person.

In لن تنالوا البر, the verb تنالوا shows that birr is something to be reached. Reaching birr requires a practical crossing beyond attachment.

In وتعاونوا على البر والتقوى, the letter على shows that cooperation takes place on the basis and axis of birr and taqwa. Birr is the ground of cooperation, not only an individual goal.

In أن تبروهم وتقسطوا إليهم, the verb تبروهم comes with a direct object, while تقسطوا comes with إلى. This difference can show that birr is direct, beneficent goodness, while qist is orientation toward justice and observing right in relation to them.

In إنه هو البر الرحيم, the pronoun of separation هو emphasizes that He is indeed البر الرحيم. The divine description of birr comes beside mercy and must be understood with mercy.

Nearby Roots

ح س ن points to beauty, goodness, and good quality. Hasan can describe the beauty of action, speech, or state. Birr is broader and firmer, and is tied to faithfulness, upright conduct, taqwa, and moral ground.

خ ي ر points to good, superiority, and real benefit. Khayr may be a result, direction, or general value; birr is the broad realization of good in action and relation.

ص ل ح points to soundness, becoming right, and repairing corruption. Salih is something that has become sound and fitting; birr is expansive and faithful goodness standing on the ground of truth.

إ ح س ن points to ihsan, deep beneficence, doing well, and goodness beyond the minimum obligation. Ihsan can show the quality of action; birr is a wide field of faith, faithfulness, spending, and upright conduct.

ص د ق points to truthfulness, sidq, and the matching of claim with reality. In Al-Baqarah 2:177, the people of birr are those who have sidq; so sidq is one of the pillars of birr, but not all of birr.

ت ق و points to carefulness, self-guarding, and placing a shield against moral danger. In Al-Baqarah 2:177 and Al-Ma'idah 5:2, birr and taqwa come together. Taqwa is the direction of guarding and care; birr is the field of expansive goodness and firm action.

و ف ي points to fulfillment, fully paying what is due, and not falling short in covenant. Wafa is one important component of birr, especially in Al-Baqarah 2:177 and Surat Al-Insan.

ق س ط points to measured justice and giving shares according to right. In Al-Mumtahanah 60:8, birr and qist come together. Birr is expansive and beneficent goodness; qist is justice and observing right.

ر ح م points to mercy, soft and compassionate connection, and care from a place of tenderness. Birr can be joined with mercy, as البر الرحيم appears as a description of The God.

ج ب ر points to repairing, binding a break, and in its negative branch, hardness, imposition, and domination. The contrast between birr and jabbar in Surat Maryam shows that birr is gentle and opening goodness, not benefiting others from a position of pressure and domination.

ب ح ر points to sea, the expanse of water, and the field of turbulence. Barr in its material meaning stands opposite bahr. This contrast also helps at the ethical image level: birr is firm and visible ground opposite turbulence.

Summary

The root ب ر ر points to dry and open land opposite bahr, and in the ethical field to expansive, visible, upright, faithful, and firm goodness. Its material image is reaching barr after the turbulence of bahr, a place where the human being can stand, see the path, and build.

In the Qur'an, this root appears for dry land opposite sea, true birr opposite appearance-based religion, spending from beloved things, cooperation upon goodness and taqwa, goodness to parents opposite being jabbar, goodness and justice toward non-aggressors, abrar and bararah, and the divine description البر الرحيم.

The difference between birr and hasan is that hasan more often shows good quality and beauty of action, while birr is the broad and firm ground of goodness. Its difference from khayr is that khayr is real value and benefit, while birr is the broad realization of good in action and relation. Its difference from taqwa is that taqwa is carefulness and self-guarding, while birr is expansive goodness on which the human being stands and by which he acts faithfully.

In Forqan translation, birr should not be translated only as “goodness.” Depending on context, “expansive goodness,” “upright conduct,” “firm goodness,” “visible goodness,” “faithful goodness,” or “moral ground of good” may be more precise.
