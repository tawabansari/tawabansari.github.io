Qur'anic Root T W R

One occurrence, one turn, or a distinct stage in which an act takes place, especially in the phrase taratan ukhra.

Core Meaning

The root ت و ر appears through the word tarah. It occurs in the Qur'an in two phrases:

أَمْ أَمِنْتُمْ أَنْ يُعِيدَكُمْ فِيهِ تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ

Or do you feel secure that He will return you into it another time?

Al-Isra' 17:69

مِنْهَا خَلَقْنَاكُمْ وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمْ وَمِنْهَا نُخْرِجُكُمْ تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ

From it We created you, into it We return you, and from it We bring you out another time.

Ta Ha 20:55

The clear Qur'anic form of this material is tarah. Al-Tawrah is the proper name of a previous revealed book and should not be counted as a certain derivative of T W R merely because of written similarity.

In Qur'anic usage, T W R indicates one time, one turn, or a distinct stage in which an act occurs. Tarah is not itself returning, emerging, or repetition. It is the turn in which an act takes place.

The phrase taratan ukhra means another time, another turn, or another stage of the same event or process.

The semantic core is this: one specific turn of an act's occurrence; and with ukhra, that act taking place in another turn after a previous one.

Important Forms

تَارَة is a noun meaning one time, one turn, one occasion, or a stage of occurrence. In the Qur'an it always appears with ukhra.

تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ means another time or another turn.

تَارَات is the lexical plural of tarah: times, turns, occurrences. This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

تِئَر and تِئَار are plural forms reported in lexical sources for tarah or ta'rah. They do not occur in the Qur'an and do not play an essential role in translating the two Qur'anic verses.

تَأْرَة is recorded in some lexical sources as meaning one time, one turn, or one occasion. Tarah may be a lightened or developed form of it, but this historical note should not be turned without further evidence into a broad theory about delay, blood vengeance, or waiting for a turn of retaliation.

تَوْر is reported in classical Arabic with other meanings as well, including a kind of vessel. That branch does not occur in the Qur'an, and its historical relation to tarah is not clear. Not every similarly shaped word should be forced under one semantic image.

التَّوْرَاة is the proper name of a previous revealed book. Its outward similarity to T W R is not enough to prove shared derivation. The etymology of the name Tawrah should be studied separately with attention to Hebrew, transmission into Arabic, and different lexical proposals.

In a separate entry for Tawrah, one could examine the Hebrew form of the name, its possible relation to teaching and guidance, its entry into Arabic, traditional Arabic analyses such as relation to W R Y, and the difference between the name of the book and the Qur'anic root of tarah.

Linguistic Note

Tarah does not determine the kind of act. It only specifies its turn.

In yu'idakum fihi taratan ukhra, yu'idakum names the act: returning. fihi names the destination: into it, meaning the sea. taratan ukhra names the turn of the act: another time.

Likewise, in nukhrijukum taratan ukhra, nukhrijukum names the act: bringing out. minha marks the point of emergence: from the earth. taratan ukhra identifies the turn of emergence: another time.

Therefore tarah should not be merged with roots such as 'A W D, R J 'A, Kh R J, B 'A Th, or K R R. Those roots express returning, emerging, being raised, or repetition. Tarah only says that the act occurs in another turn.

Material Image

The material image of tarah is an act that occurs in one turn, ends or passes beyond that stage, and then occurs again in another turn.

A person enters the sea once and, after returning to land, may enter it again in another turn. Something emerges once from the earth, returns to the earth, and then emerges again from that same earth. A person may move sometimes mounted and sometimes walking; each state is a tarah, a turn, of his movement.

Tarah does not by itself mean a circle, reincarnation, endless repetition, or return to the first point. Those ideas enter only if the verbs and context show them.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, tarah was used for one time, one turn, and one occasion. Structures such as taratan kadha wa-taratan kadha mean sometimes this and sometimes that; taratan ba'da tarah means turn after turn; and fa'ala dhalika tarat means he did that several times.

This usage shows that tarah was suited to distinguishing turns of a behavior or condition.

Some lexical reports also record the form ta'rah. But attributing its primary meaning to blood vengeance, granting respite until revenge, or waiting for a turn of retaliation is not certain for this entry.

Even if related words have some historical link, one should not conclude that taratan ukhra in Ta Ha lexically refers to the interval between death and resurrection. The interval between return to the earth and emergence from it is understood from the context of the verse, not from the meaning of tarah itself.

Returning Again to the Sea

أَمْ أَمِنْتُمْ أَنْ يُعِيدَكُمْ فِيهِ تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ فَيُرْسِلَ عَلَيْكُمْ قَاصِفًا مِنَ الرِّيحِ فَيُغْرِقَكُمْ بِمَا كَفَرْتُمْ

Or do you feel secure that He will return you into it another time, then send against you a crushing wind and drown you because of your kufr?

Al-Isra' 17:69

The context speaks of people who were caught in the sea, saved, and brought to land. The movement is structured as first presence in the sea, distress and seeking rescue, reaching land, the possibility of returning to the sea again, and being exposed again to danger.

Taratan ukhra marks the second turn of presence in the same space of danger. But tarah itself does not mean sea, danger, or return. Those meanings come from fihi, yu'idakum, and the continuation of the verse.

Tarah in Movement Between Two Spaces

In Al-Isra' 17:69, tarah appears in a structure where the human being moves between two spaces: sea, land, and sea again.

This use shows that tarah can identify a turn within movement or alternation. But one should not conclude that the intrinsic meaning of tarah is “alternation between two spaces.” In general language, tarah can also be used for different turns of a behavior or state. In this verse, alternation comes from the narrative structure.

Creation, Return, and Another Emergence From the Earth

مِنْهَا خَلَقْنَاكُمْ وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمْ وَمِنْهَا نُخْرِجُكُمْ تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ

From it We created you, into it We return you, and from it We bring you out another time.

Ta Ha 20:55

The verse places three stages beside one another: minha khalaqnakum, from it We created you; wa-fiha nu'idukum, into it We return you; and wa-minha nukhrijukum taratan ukhra, from it We bring you out another time.

Minha marks the earthly origin and material of creation. Fiha points to being returned into the earth. Then minha again marks the point of emergence, and taratan ukhra identifies this emergence as another turn.

Geometry of the Prepositions in Ta Ha

Three prepositions draw the path stage by stage: minha, from the earth; fiha, in the earth; and again minha, from the earth.

This structure shows a linear and returning movement: first emergence or origin, return and settling, and emergence again. Taratan ukhra comes at the end of the clause and identifies the turn of the second emergence.

This emergence is not presented as unrelated to the first creation. It belongs to the same structure: from the earth, into the earth, and again from the earth.

Is Tarah a Turn Within a Structured Process?

In both Qur'anic uses, taratan ukhra appears in a context where the new turn is related to a previous turn. In Al-Isra', there is presence in the sea, exit to land, and return again to the sea. In Ta Ha, there is creation from the earth, return to the earth, and another emergence from the earth.

Therefore, in these two verses, tarah is not merely “one accidental occurrence.” It is a turn within a meaningful sequence. Still, this feature comes from the Qur'anic usage of these two verses. It should not be stated as the fixed meaning of the word in every Arabic use.

Another Emergence and the Answer to Doubting Resurrection

وَمِنْهَا نُخْرِجُكُمْ تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ

And from it We bring you out another time.

The second emergence stands in continuity with the first creation. Instead of presenting resurrection as something completely without precedent, the verse presents it as another turn of bringing the human being out from the same earth.

This structure can challenge the doubt that resurrection is impossible: the same origin linked to the first creation becomes the place of bodily return and will also be the origin of another emergence.

But tarah itself does not mean “proof of resurrection.” That argument comes from the whole arrangement of the verse.

Tarah and Ba'th

In Ta Ha 20:55, the word ba'th does not occur. The verb is nukhrijukum.

Emergence again from the earth belongs to the context of resurrection, but the meaning of ba'th should not be inserted directly into the root T W R. Taratan ukhra means another turn; nukhrijukum means We bring you out; ba'th means raising and setting in motion after death.

Does Tarah Indicate Reincarnation?

No. The verse in Ta Ha states three specific stages: creation from the earth, return to the earth, and another emergence from the earth.

It says nothing about unlimited cycles of earthly lives or the transfer of a human being from one body to another. Taratan ukhra only marks one further emergence within this specific sequence. The word by itself does not indicate an endless cycle.

Similar Uses

Tarah and marrah: Both may mean one time or one occurrence. Marrah, from M R R, is closer to one passing or one occurrence. Tarah, in uses such as taratan kadha wa-taratan kadha, separates different turns or alternating states. As a tendency, marrah is one occurrence, while tarah is one turn among different turns or states. This distinction is not absolute.

Tarah and hin: Hin is a time, occasion, or span. Tarah is a turn of occurrence. Hin asks when; tarah asks in which time or turn.

Tarah and i'adah: I'adah is the act of doing again or returning. Tarah is the turn in which that i'adah takes place.

Tarah and rujū': Rujū' is returning to a place, reference point, or former state. Tarah does not determine the direction of movement; even a non-returning act may happen taratan ukhra.

Tarah and 'awd: 'Awd indicates coming again or returning. Tarah indicates the number or turn of the act.

Tarah and karrah: Karrah can indicate a return, another attack, or another turn of overcoming. Tarah is more general and is not limited to one kind of movement.

Tarah and repetition: Repetition is the process of occurring again. Tarah identifies one turn within that process.

Tarah and tawr: Tawr from T W R here should not be confused with Ṭ W R. Ṭawr indicates a stage, state, or the known Mount Ṭur. Tarah is related to a time or turn. They are only phonetically near.

Tarah and Tawrah: Tarah is an Arabic word for time or turn. Tawrah is the proper name of a revealed book. One should not say that Tawrah means “book of repetition,” “book of another turn,” or “a book that came again.” Such meanings are built from outward similarity without sufficient historical evidence.

Grammar and Structure

In both verses, tarah is accusative: taratan ukhra. This form functions adverbially and specifies the turn of the verb's occurrence: another time, in another turn. It may be understood as an adverb of number or of the turn in which the act occurs.

Ukhra is the feminine adjective of tarah. It shows that a previous occurrence exists and another turn is now being mentioned.

In an yu'idakum fihi taratan ukhra, the structure is: yu'idakum, He returns you; fihi, into it, meaning the sea; taratan ukhra, another time. Natural English: He will return you into it another time.

In Ta Ha, minha khalaqnakum marks origin; wa-fiha nu'idukum marks the place of return and settling; wa-minha nukhrijukum marks again the point of emergence. The contrast of minha, fiha, minha makes the verse ordered and staged.

Tarah is not the absolute object of the verb. It is not the verbal noun of a'ada or akhraja. The verbal nouns of those verbs could be i'adah or ikhraj. Tarah only specifies the time or turn of their occurrence.

Nearby Roots

T W R and M R R: Marrah from M R R is close to one passing or one occurrence. Tarah emphasizes a distinct turn of occurrence.

T W R and K R R: K R R indicates bringing again or repeating. Tarah is one turn of repetition.

T W R and 'A W D: 'Awd is the return itself or coming again. Tarah only states the time or count of that turn.

T W R and R J 'A: R J 'A indicates returning to a reference point or previous state. Tarah is neutral regarding the direction of movement.

T W R and karrah: Karrah is a return or another attack. Tarah can express any other turn.

T W R and H Y N: Hin is a time or duration. Tarah is one occurrence in time.

T W R and Ṭ W R: Ṭawr indicates a stage, state, or the known mount. Tarah indicates time or turn.

T W R and the name Tawrah: The name Tawrah must be studied historically on its own. Its possible relation to a Hebrew word connected with teaching and guidance, or Arabic analyses such as relation to W R Y, belongs to the entry on the proper name Tawrah. These differences themselves show that placing Tawrah certainly under T W R is not a sound method.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For تَارَة: one time; one turn; one occasion; a stage of occurrence.

For تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ: another time; one more time; another turn; in another stage. “Another time” is more natural in English. “Another turn” preserves the noun-like and staged image more clearly.

يُعِيدَكُمْ فِيهِ تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ

He will return you into it another time.

In another turn, He will return you into it again.

وَمِنْهَا نُخْرِجُكُمْ تَارَةً أُخْرَىٰ

And from it We bring you out another time.

And in another turn We bring you out from it.

And one more time We bring you out from the earth.

Suggested translation for Ta Ha 20:55: From it We created you, into it We return you, and from it We bring you out another time.

Suggested translation for Al-Isra' 17:69: Or do you feel secure that He will return you into it [the sea] another time, then send against you a crushing wind?

Summary

In Qur'anic usage, T W R indicates one time, one turn, or another stage in which an act occurs. Its Qur'anic form is tarah, and in both occurrences it appears with ukhra: returning again to the sea and emerging again from the earth.

Tarah is not the act of returning, emerging, or being raised. It only specifies the turn of the event. The kind of act is understood from the verb that accompanies it: yu'idakum, returning; nukhrijukum, bringing out.

In the two Qur'anic verses, taratan ukhra appears within a structured sequence. In one verse, the human being moves from sea to land and may be returned again to the sea. In the other, the human being is created from the earth, returned to the earth, and then brought out from it again.

This use brings tarah close to “another turn within a process,” but it should not be treated as meaning alternation, reincarnation, or an endless cycle by itself.

Tarah is close to marrah, i'adah, 'awd, rujū', karrah, repetition, and hin, but it is not identical with any of them. Marrah expresses one occurrence, i'adah the act of doing again, rujū' return to a reference point, and hin a time or occasion.

Tawrah, despite spelling similarity, should not be presented as a certain derivative of T W R. The name and its etymology should be studied separately with attention to Hebrew and different historical analyses.

The special core of T W R is one specific turn in which an act occurs; and in taratan ukhra, that same act occurring in another turn after the previous one.
