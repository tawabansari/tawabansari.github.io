Core meaning: a tall, immense mountain; a prominent, towering mass of striking size and height.

1. The root in early Arabic

In early Arabic, the triliteral root ط و د is best known through طَوْد, a word for a great, tall mountain. In its principal use, it names a natural feature; it is not a verb from which meanings such as raising or making firm can be conclusively derived.

طَوْد was especially used for a mountain of striking size, height, and prominence. Dictionaries also record it for elevated ground or a hill. The common feature of these uses is the visible prominence and height of a large mass; the material composing it should not be inferred from the word alone.

One early witness is a verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Al-Aswad ibn Yaʿfur:

نَزَلُوا بِأَنْقِرَةٍ يَسِيلُ عَلَيْهِمُ

nazalū bi-anqiratin yasīlu ʿalayhimu

مَاءُ الْفُرَاتِ يَجِيءُ مِنْ أَطْوَادِ

māʾu al-furāti yajīʾu min aṭwād

Translation: They settled at Anqirah, where the water of the Euphrates flowed upon them, coming from mountains.

أَطْوَاد is the plural of طَوْد and refers here to mountains from whose direction the water flows. The word has a literal geographical meaning, naming natural elevations of the land.

The verse is transmitted in early collections of Arabic poetry, with some versions beginning حَلُّوا rather than نَزَلُوا. This variation does not alter the meaning of أطواد. Attribution to a pre-Islamic poet does not, by itself, establish the exact date of this verse's composition.

The historically supported meaning of this family is therefore a great, tall mountain. Dictionaries record other prominent elevations as well, but not all such uses can be traced to pre-Islamic times with equal certainty.

2. Lexical forms and the central meaning

طَوْد is the principal noun and أَطْوَاد its familiar plural. Some dictionaries also record the plural طِوَدَة.

Other forms, including طَوَّدَ, تَطْوَاد, طَادَ, and طَادِي, appear alongside this family in dictionaries, but they do not all have a single clear meaning in common.

طَوْد: a tall, great mountain

In its principal sense, طَوْد denotes a mountain of conspicuous height and size. It need not be the tallest mountain in a region or meet a specified measurement. Arabic assigns no numerical threshold to the word.

أَطْوَاد: mountains and great elevations

أَطْوَاد is the plural of طود. This is the form found in the verse attributed to Al-Aswad ibn Yaʿfur.

In figurative usage, something rising prominently above its surroundings can also be likened to a طود.

For example, in a poem cited by Thaʿlab, أطواد describes camels' high humps. The hump is not literally a mountain: its height and prominence are compared to one.

The literal meaning of طود can thus describe the size or height of other things through comparison.

طَوَّدَ and تَطْوَاد

Some dictionaries record طَوَّدَ as travelling about or traversing different regions, as when someone journeys through lands to seek a livelihood.

تَطْوَاد likewise concerns travelling or moving about.

The historical relationship between this use and the noun طَوْد is unclear. Their occurrence under the same dictionary entry does not establish that travelling developed specifically from traversing mountains.

طَادَ and طَادِي

Dictionaries record طَادَ for remaining firm and طَادِي for being fixed or stable. Their relationship to ط و د requires caution, however: طادي has also been explained through a transposition of letters from و ط د.

Stability and firmness should therefore not be made the primary meaning of طَوْد solely on the basis of these forms.

The defensible core of the principal branch is a great, prominent mountain or elevation. Height and magnitude characterize this noun; stability, obstruction, protection, or dominance require independent contextual or lexical support.

3. Quranic usage

The root occurs once in the Quran, in the form الطَّوْد in Al-Shuʿarāʾ.

فَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَىٰ مُوسَىٰ أَنِ اضْرِبْ بِعَصَاكَ الْبَحْرَ ۖ فَانْفَلَقَ فَكَانَ كُلُّ فِرْقٍ كَالطَّوْدِ الْعَظِيمِ

fa-awḥaynā ilā mūsā ani iḍrib bi-ʿaṣāka al-baḥra fa-infalaqa fa-kāna kullu firqin ka-l-ṭawdi al-ʿaẓīm

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:63

Then We revealed to Moses: Strike the sea with your staff. It split apart, and each separate part became like an immense mountain.

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:63

The verse belongs to the account of Moses and his companions leaving Pharaoh's land. When the two groups saw one another, Moses' companions feared being overtaken by Pharaoh's forces, while Moses remained confident in his Lord's guidance.

The God then commands Moses to strike the sea with his staff. The sea splits, and each separated part is compared to an immense mountain.

The significance of طَوْد in the verse

The verse does not say that seawater became stone or an actual mountain. The comparative particle كَـ states that each part became like an immense mountain.

The principal features of طود in this image are magnitude and height. After splitting, the sea is described as forming immense, prominent parts that resemble mountains in scale and appearance.

طود emphasizes the magnitude of these separated parts. جبل would still convey “mountain,” but the established use of طود places greater emphasis on a tall, great mountain.

The role of الْعَظِيمِ

الْعَظِيمِ qualifies الطَّوْدِ and belongs to ع ظ م.

Although طود can itself denote a great mountain, العظيم reinforces its magnitude. الطود العظيم can therefore be rendered “an immense mountain” or “a very great, tall mountain.”

The adjective does not define a separate category of mountain; it intensifies the scale of the image presented by the verse.

The role of فِرْقٍ in the comparison

فِرْقٍ belongs to ف ر ق and here denotes each separated portion of the water.

The distinction matters: فِرْق identifies the separated portion, whereas طود supplies the object to which it is compared.

فَانْفَلَقَ, from ف ل ق, expresses the sea's splitting. فِرْق presents the resulting separation as distinct portions, while كالطود العظيم depicts their magnitude and height.

These three expressions perform distinct semantic functions and should not be collapsed into one another.

Connections with other verses

Al-Baqarah 2:50 also speaks of the sea being divided and the Children of Israel being saved.

In Ṭā Hā 20:77, Moses is commanded to make a dry path through the sea for his companions, without fearing pursuit or being afraid. Drowning is an explanation arising from the setting of the sea, rather than a separate word in the verse.

These passages illuminate different aspects of the event. Al-Shuʿarāʾ compares the separated water to mountains, emphasizing its great scale; Ṭā Hā emphasizes the passage and the companions' safety.

The comparison with طود alone cannot determine the number or precise shape of the passages, the water's numerical height, or the physical process by which the sea parted. The verse portrays the magnitude of the separated parts without providing geometrical measurements.

4. Related roots

Ṭ W D and J B L: a great mountain and a mountain

جَبَل is the broader, more usual word for a mountain. The Quran uses it in various contexts: earthly mountains, events of the Hereafter, and comparisons.

طَوْد, in its principal use, particularly evokes a tall, immense mountain.

For example, Al-Aʿrāf 7:143 uses الجبل for the mountain that crumbles in the account of Moses. That passage focuses on what happens to the mountain rather than on magnitude as the noun's principal feature.

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:63 compares immense portions of water to a tall mountain. طود provides a fitting image of that magnitude.

The distinction is not absolute. جبل can denote a very tall, immense mountain, and طود does not always mean the greatest mountain. The difference is one of semantic emphasis, not a fixed, measurable boundary.

Ṭ W D and Ṭ W R: a great mountain and a mountain or geographical designation

طُور also concerns a mountain in Arabic. In the Quran it appears particularly in expressions such as الطُّور and طُورِ سَيْنِينَ, including Al-Ṭūr 52:1 and Al-Tīn 95:2.

In these uses, طور may refer to a particular mountain or a known place associated with the events concerning Moses.

طود in Al-Shuʿarāʾ does not name a particular mountain. It provides a familiar comparison for the magnitude of the portions of water.

Their shared first consonant and wāw do not make ط و د and ط و ر the same root. Each must be understood through its own linguistic evidence and usage.

Ṭ W D and R S W: magnitude and firm settlement

رَوَاسِي belongs to ر س و and describes mountains in relation to their settlement in the earth, as in Al-Naḥl 16:15.

Settlement and remaining in place are prominent in that family.

طود characteristically denotes a mountain's magnitude and height. Although great mountains generally appear stable to human observers, that feature should not be added to the word's direct meaning without evidence.

This matters in translation. “A stable mountain” may suggest a plausible image, but it inserts stability into الطود. “A great, tall mountain” is closer to the established meaning.

5. Translation proposals for Forqan

For طَوْد in its principal use, “a tall mountain,” “an immense mountain,” and “a great, towering mountain” are suitable equivalents.

Where the context concerns a hill or another natural elevation, “a great elevation” or “a high hill” may fit. These should not replace the principal meaning of mountain without a contextual indication.

For أَطْوَاد in poetry and comparable uses, “mountains” or “high mountains” is appropriate.

For Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:63, the proposed translation is:

“Then We revealed to Moses: Strike the sea with your staff. It split apart, and each separate part became like an immense mountain.”

This preserves the comparison in كَالطَّوْدِ and conveys the magnitude emphasized by الْعَظِيمِ.

Proposed Quranic word-box content:

Word: الطَّوْدِ

Root: ط و د

Brief root meaning: a great, tall mountain; an immense, prominent elevation.

Meaning in this verse: an immense mountain to which each separated portion of seawater is compared.

6. Conclusion

The principal, established branch of ط و د centers on طَوْد as a tall, immense mountain. Early poetic evidence, particularly أطواد in the verse attributed to Al-Aswad ibn Yaʿfur, shows its use for natural mountains.

Dictionaries also record other elevations and prominences, sometimes extending the word figuratively to things such as camel humps. These uses rely on visible height and prominence and should not be made separate Quranic meanings.

The Quran uses the root once, in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:63, comparing each portion of the parted sea to an immense mountain. طود is neither a place name nor a term specifying the water's physical stability here. It supplies an image of magnitude and height in the account of Moses and his companions being saved.

Final definition: the principal branch of ط و د denotes a tall, immense mountain. Broader uses can describe great, prominent elevations or compare towering objects to mountains. الطَّوْد in the Quran supplies the immense mountain whose scale and height form the basis of the comparison with the separated portions of seawater.
