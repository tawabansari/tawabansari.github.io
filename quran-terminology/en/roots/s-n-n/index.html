Established ways, teeth, and shaping: earlier peoples' practices, the divine way, and masnun clay in the Quran

1. Meaning in early Arabic

S N N is a branching Arabic lexical family. Its principal senses include a path or way, a pattern followed in behavior or events, teeth and sharp edges, and shaping or alteration of material.

In the behavioral branch, سُنَّة denotes a way, practice, or course adopted by a person or group. سَنَن also denotes a recognized road, direction, or course followed.

سَنَّ الطَّرِيقَ means opening a way or establishing a course that others can follow. Performing an act differs from establishing a practice: someone may act once, but when that act becomes a recognizable example others can follow, it may be called that person's sunna.

Sunna need not mean an ancient custom or immutable law. Someone can initiate a practice that others follow; a community can also acquire an established custom through repetition and continuity.

In this branch, sunna is not inherently positive or negative. A practice may be commendable or objectionable, just or oppressive, beneficial or harmful. Its value depends on its content, practitioner, and consequences, rather than the word alone.

In the bodily branch, سِنّ names a human or animal tooth. سَنَّ السِّكِّينَ means sharpening a knife, مِسَنّ a sharpening implement or stone, and سِنان a spearhead.

Another branch appears in سَنَّ الماءَ, pouring water in a stream. مَسْنُون also describes something shaped, smoothed, or altered in recorded Arabic usage.

Early dictionaries bring these senses together, but they cannot all be derived from one image without historical investigation.

Evidence from early Arabic usage

A verse attributed to al-Aʿsha, a well-known poet close to the period of revelation, reads:

كَرِيمٌ شَمَائِلُهُ مِنْ بَنِي
مُعَاوِيَةَ الْأَكْرَمِينَ السُّنَنِ

karīmun shamāʾiluhu min banī / muʿāwiyata al-akramīna al-sunan

Meaning: Noble in character, from the descendants of Muʿawiya, whose ways and qualities are noble.

السُّنَن here concerns ways, qualities, and patterns of behavior.

This illustrates that sunna and sunan in early Arabic were not confined to religious commands or formal laws; they also described human character and conduct.

A verse attributed to Khalid ibn Zuhayr al-Hudhali likewise reads:

فَلَا تَجْزَعَنْ مِنْ سُنَّةٍ أَنْتَ سِرْتَهَا
فَأَوَّلُ رَاضٍ سُنَّةً مَنْ يَسِيرُهَا

fa-lā tajzaʿan min sunnatin anta sirtahā / fa-awwalu rāḍin sunnatan man yasīruhā

Meaning: Do not complain about a way you yourself have followed; the first to accept a way should be the one who follows it.

The verse recalls responsibility for conduct one has initiated. Sunna is a practice the person has adopted and that another may imitate.

The verse does not itself declare that practice good. It can even rebuke someone now unhappy with the consequences of conduct they previously pursued.

The verse is transmitted in the Hudhali collection and early literary works. Sources vary in attribution or wording, and its precise date is not independently established. It is valuable evidence of early usage, but does not alone establish when this meaning arose or securely date this particular verse before revelation.

Overall, early linguistic evidence shows that sunna as a way or practice was known before the development of specialized jurisprudential and hadith terminology.

Is flowing the core of every sense?

Ibn Faris explains this family by connecting its senses with the flow and continuation of something.

In that account, سَنَّ الماءَ concerns pouring water in a stream. A road people follow also has extension and direction, while a human practice can continue through people's behavior.

There is an intelligible semantic relationship between a traveled path, a recurring practice, and a continuing flow.

This explanation should not become a categorical claim about the earliest historical meaning of every branch.

For example, sinn, tooth, is ancient in Arabic and other Semitic languages. A tooth's sharpness alone does not establish that its name derives from sharpening tools.

Masnun as a description of clay also receives different lexical explanations that require separate consideration.

The family is therefore best approached through three principal branches: ways and practices; teeth and sharp implements; and pouring, shaping, or altering material. Connections can be investigated, but the apparent unity of the root cannot replace historical evidence.

2. Quranic forms and derivatives

The root occurs 21 times in 15 Quranic verses: 16 instances of sunna and its plural sunan, two of sinn, and three of masnun. These form three main groups:

  • سُنَّة and its plural سُنَن: a way, practice, pattern, or established course in behavior and events.
  • سِنّ: a tooth.
  • مَسْنُون: a passive participle; in the creation passages, it qualifies حَمَإ and has several established lexical interpretations.

سُنَنٌ and سُنَنَ occur in Al ʿImran 3:137 and Al-Nisa 4:26.

Singular sunna occurs in Al-Anfal 8:38, Al-Hijr 15:13, Al-Isra 17:77, Al-Kahf 18:55, Al-Ahzab 33:38, Al-Ahzab 33:62, Fatir 35:43, Ghafir 40:85, and Al-Fath 48:23. Some verses contain it more than once.

Sinn occurs twice in Al-Ma'idah 5:45, in وَالسِّنَّ بِالسِّنِّ.

Masnun occurs in Al-Hijr 15:26, Al-Hijr 15:28, and Al-Hijr 15:33.

Quranic usage is thus not confined to social or divine practices: teeth and the description of the material of human creation also belong to this lexical family.

Forms such as سَنَّ, يَسُنُّ, اسْتَنَّ, مِسَنّ, and سِنَان are familiar in Arabic dictionaries but do not occur as independent derivatives of this root in the Quran.

3. سُنَن: the ways and patterns of earlier peoples

قَدْ خَلَتْ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ سُنَنٌ

Al ʿImran 3:137 states:

قَدْ خَلَتْ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ سُنَنٌ فَسِيرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ فَانْظُرُوا كَيْفَ كَانَ عَاقِبَةُ الْمُكَذِّبِينَ

qad khalat min qablikum sunanun fa-sīrū fī al-arḍi fa-unẓurū kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu al-mukadhdhibīn

Āl ʿImrān 3:137

Ways and patterns have passed before you; travel through the land and observe the outcome of those who denied.

Sunan here is the plural of sunna.

The Quran describes ways and patterns preceding its present audience whose traces and consequences can be examined.

The connection with the following command matters:

فَسِيرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ فَانْظُرُوا

fa-sīrū fī al-arḍi fa-unẓurū

Āl ʿImrān 3:137

Travel through the land and observe.

Knowing earlier patterns is therefore not limited to accepting inherited stories. People can travel, observe the remains of earlier societies, and reflect on the consequences of their conduct.

Sunan itself does not mean archaeological remains or history's final outcome. It names established ways or patterns; their consequences become clear in the rest of the verse.

The outcome of those who denied is specifically the object of observation here.

Sunan should therefore not be restricted to natural regularities such as celestial movement, rainfall, or plant growth. Here it concerns human behavior and societies' historical outcomes.

Nor does this imply that every historical event follows a simple, repetitive formula allowing prediction of every detail of the future.

The Quran calls for understanding and learning, rather than a formula erasing historical complexity.

سُنَنَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ

Al-Nisa 4:26 states:

يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُبَيِّنَ لَكُمْ وَيَهْدِيَكُمْ سُنَنَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ وَيَتُوبَ عَلَيْكُمْ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ

yurīdu allāhu li-yubayyina lakum wa-yahdiyakum sunana alladhīna min qablikum wa-yatūba ʿalaykum wa-allāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm

Al-Nisāʾ 4:26

The God wishes to make things clear to you, guide you to the ways of those before you, and turn toward you; The God is Knowing, Wise.

Sunan is an object of yahdiyakum here, referring to earlier people's ways and paths.

There is nevertheless an important difference from Al ʿImran 3:137.

In Al ʿImran, the audience is asked to observe the outcome of those who denied. In Al-Nisa, God speaks of guiding the audience to earlier ways.

Al-Nisa 4:26 cannot therefore mean every past custom indiscriminately. The preceding verses concern family relationships and their boundaries, and this verse speaks of divine clarification and guidance.

The intended ways belong within divine guidance, rather than every historical habit earlier people possessed.

The verse also does not state that every detail of earlier religious legislation was identical for all communities. Al-Ma'idah 5:48 speaks of differing prescribed ways and courses among groups.

Continuity in divine guidance and knowledge of earlier ways do not entail identical practical regulations in every society.

Lexically, sunan denotes ways and paths. Their goodness and suitability for following here come from guidance and the context, rather than the word alone.

4. سُنَّةُ الْأَوَّلِينَ: earlier practice or earlier fate?

Several verses mention سُنَّةُ الْأَوَّلِينَ or سُنَّتَ الْأَوَّلِينَ.

Each requires contextual examination: the emphasis may fall on earlier people's established conduct or on the outcome following that conduct.

Al-Anfal 8:38

قُلْ لِلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا إِنْ يَنْتَهُوا يُغْفَرْ لَهُمْ مَا قَدْ سَلَفَ وَإِنْ يَعُودُوا فَقَدْ مَضَتْ سُنَّتُ الْأَوَّلِينَ

qul li-alladhīna kafarū in yantahū yughfar lahum mā qad salafa wa-in yaʿūdū fa-qad maḍat sunnatu al-awwalīn

Al-Anfāl 8:38

Tell those who disbelieve: if they desist, what has passed will be forgiven them; if they return, the precedent of earlier peoples has already passed.

The verse presents two different conditions.

Desisting opens the possibility of forgiveness; returning to the same course brings a reminder of earlier peoples' fate.

Their precedent here thus concerns prior conduct and its consequences.

Sunna itself does not mean punishment. The conditions and context supply the warning and behavioral consequences.

The verse also shows that people are not powerless over their conduct. Those warned about earlier outcomes are also called to desist and receive forgiveness.

Al-Hijr 15:13

لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهِ وَقَدْ خَلَتْ سُنَّةُ الْأَوَّلِينَ

lā yuʾminūna bihi wa-qad khalat sunnatu al-awwalīn

Al-Ḥijr 15:13

They do not believe in it, although the precedent of earlier peoples has already passed.

Nearby verses describe messengers arriving and groups responding with mockery.

In this setting, the expression can relate to earlier patterns of denial and rejection; another established interpretation emphasizes God's treatment and destruction of those who denied.

Under the first interpretation, sunna can describe objectionable human conduct. Under the second, the emphasis falls on the consequences of that conduct.

The expression should therefore neither be confined to a good practice worthy of imitation nor used to exclude its established interpretation in terms of the fate of earlier deniers.

Al-Kahf 18:55

وَمَا مَنَعَ النَّاسَ أَنْ يُؤْمِنُوا إِذْ جَاءَهُمُ الْهُدَىٰ وَيَسْتَغْفِرُوا رَبَّهُمْ إِلَّا أَنْ تَأْتِيَهُمْ سُنَّةُ الْأَوَّلِينَ أَوْ يَأْتِيَهُمُ الْعَذَابُ قُبُلًا

wa-mā manaʿa al-nāsa an yuʾminū idh jāʾahumu al-hudā wa-yastaghfirū rabbahum illā an taʾtiyahum sunnatu al-awwalīna aw yaʾtiyahumu al-ʿadhābu qubulā

Al-Kahf 18:55

Nothing prevented people from believing when guidance came to them and seeking their Lord's forgiveness, except that the precedent of earlier peoples should come upon them, or punishment confront them.

Here the earlier precedent appears alongside the arrival of punishment.

The context concerns more than earlier lifestyles: it also invokes a history of responses to guidance and their consequences.

Nevertheless, sunnat al-awwalin should not simply become punishment in every verse. Sunna has a broader range; in some settings punishment is the outcome or part of the historical pattern.

Fatir 35:43

After describing arrogance and evil plotting, the verse states:

فَهَلْ يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَّا سُنَّتَ الْأَوَّلِينَ

fa-hal yanẓurūna illā sunnata al-awwalīn

Fāṭir 35:43

Do they await anything but the precedent of earlier peoples?

It immediately follows with the unchangeability of God's way.

The connection shows that earlier human conduct and God's response to it are related subjects.

People may repeat denial, arrogance, and plotting, but the consequences do not fall outside the framework described by the Quran.

Human practice and divine practice are nevertheless not identical concepts: the former describes people's behavior, the latter God's treatment of them.

5. سُنَّةَ اللَّهِ: God's way in creation and human life

The Quranic expression refers to a way God establishes in a particular matter or follows in dealing with His servants.

It occurs in different verses whose subjects are not identical.

Understanding it requires more than isolating a phrase such as لَنْ تَجِدَ لِسُنَّةِ اللَّهِ تَبْدِيلًا and applying it indiscriminately to every event.

God's way concerning messengers

Al-Isra 17:76 states:

وَإِنْ كَادُوا لَيَسْتَفِزُّونَكَ مِنَ الْأَرْضِ لِيُخْرِجُوكَ مِنْهَا وَإِذًا لَا يَلْبَثُونَ خِلَافَكَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

wa-in kādū la-yastafizzūnaka mina al-arḍi li-yukhrijūka minhā wa-idhan lā yalbathūna khilāfaka illā qalīlā

Al-Isrāʾ 17:76

They nearly unsettled you from the land to expel you; then they would have remained after you only a little while.

The following verse, Al-Isra 17:77, states:

سُنَّةَ مَنْ قَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا قَبْلَكَ مِنْ رُسُلِنَا وَلَا تَجِدُ لِسُنَّتِنَا تَحْوِيلًا

sunnata man qad arsalnā qablaka min rusulinā wa-lā tajidu li-sunnatinā taḥwīlā

Al-Isrāʾ 17:77

The way concerning Our messengers whom We sent before you; you will find no alteration in Our way.

The subject is clear: attempts to expel the messenger of truth from the land and the consequences of that action.

The constancy of God's way here concerns that specific matter.

It does not establish that every act or decision of anyone claiming prophetic or religious representation at any time is covered by the same judgment.

Sunnatina also does not mean a collection of reports about messengers. It describes God's treatment of the situation under discussion.

God's way concerning prophetic responsibilities

Al-Ahzab 33:38 states:

مَا كَانَ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ مِنْ حَرَجٍ فِيمَا فَرَضَ اللَّهُ لَهُ سُنَّةَ اللَّهِ فِي الَّذِينَ خَلَوْا مِنْ قَبْلُ وَكَانَ أَمْرُ اللَّهِ قَدَرًا مَقْدُورًا

mā kāna ʿalā al-nabiyyi min ḥarajin fīmā faraḍa allāhu lahu sunnata allāhi fī alladhīna khalaw min qablu wa-kāna amru allāhi qadaran maqdūrā

Al-Aḥzāb 33:38

There is no constraint upon the prophet concerning what God has appointed for him: God's way concerning those who passed before; God's command is a determined decree.

Here God's way concerns the prophets and their carrying out what has been appointed for them.

Sunna again denotes an established way or rule, not necessarily punishment or the fate of deniers.

This distinction matters: translating sunnat Allah invariably as the law of destroying peoples would fail to account for Al-Ahzab 33:38.

God's way concerning corruption and agitation

Al-Ahzab 33:60, Al-Ahzab 33:61, and Al-Ahzab 33:62 concern groups persisting in harmful and disruptive behavior in Medina.

At the end of this sequence, Al-Ahzab 33:62 states:

سُنَّةَ اللَّهِ فِي الَّذِينَ خَلَوْا مِنْ قَبْلُ وَلَنْ تَجِدَ لِسُنَّةِ اللَّهِ تَبْدِيلًا

sunnata allāhi fī alladhīna khalaw min qablu wa-lan tajida li-sunnati allāhi tabdīlā

Al-Aḥzāb 33:62

God's way concerning those who passed before; you will find no replacement for God's way.

This belongs within its historical context, with attention to the audience, conduct, and conditions of the judgment.

Sunna alone specifies neither the offense, its penalty, nor the judgment's temporal and social scope.

The phrase cannot alone justify imposing the same judgment on every intellectual or religious opponent in every age. Such extension requires independent Quranic evidence.

The divine way and the consequences of plotting

Fatir 35:43 states in part:

وَلَا يَحِيقُ الْمَكْرُ السَّيِّئُ إِلَّا بِأَهْلِهِ فَهَلْ يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَّا سُنَّتَ الْأَوَّلِينَ فَلَنْ تَجِدَ لِسُنَّتِ اللَّهِ تَبْدِيلًا وَلَنْ تَجِدَ لِسُنَّتِ اللَّهِ تَحْوِيلًا

wa-lā yaḥīqu al-makru al-sayyiʾu illā bi-ahlihi fa-hal yanẓurūna illā sunnata al-awwalīna fa-lan tajida li-sunnati allāhi tabdīlan wa-lan tajida li-sunnati allāhi taḥwīlā

Fāṭir 35:43

Evil plotting encompasses only its own people. Do they await anything but the precedent of earlier peoples? You will find no replacement for God's way, nor any diversion of God's way.

Three subjects connect here: evil plotting, earlier peoples' outcome, and God's way.

Human conduct has consequences; plotting and corruption are not beyond responsibility and accounting.

This does not establish that every plotter must visibly suffer within a short time and exactly as we expect.

The timing and character of consequences also require other Quranic evidence. Promises of final accounting should not be confined to immediate worldly events.

The divine way and abandoning wrongdoing

Ghafir 40:85 states:

فَلَمْ يَكُ يَنْفَعُهُمْ إِيمَانُهُمْ لَمَّا رَأَوْا بَأْسَنَا سُنَّتَ اللَّهِ الَّتِي قَدْ خَلَتْ فِي عِبَادِهِ وَخَسِرَ هُنَالِكَ الْكَافِرُونَ

fa-lam yaku yanfaʿuhum īmānuhum lammā raʾaw baʾsanā sunnata allāhi allatī qad khalat fī ʿibādihi wa-khasira hunālika al-kāfirūn

Ghāfir 40:85

Their belief did not benefit them when they saw Our might: God's way already established among His servants; there the disbelievers lost.

The subject here is belief after witnessing a punishment that ends the ordinary opportunity for choice and return.

This differs from repentance and reform before that stage.

The same Quran, in Al-Anfal 8:38, describes forgiveness for those who desist from wrongdoing.

The judgments are not contradictory; the conditions differ.

A person may reform and change course before the decisive outcome. Once the real opportunity to choose and return has ended, the situation differs.

Continuity in God's way therefore does not eliminate choice or differing circumstances.

The divine way and promised aid

Al-Fath 48:22 states:

وَلَوْ قَاتَلَكُمُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لَوَلَّوُا الْأَدْبَارَ ثُمَّ لَا يَجِدُونَ وَلِيًّا وَلَا نَصِيرًا

wa-law qātalakumu alladhīna kafarū la-wallaw al-adbāra thumma lā yajidūna waliyyan wa-lā naṣīrā

Al-Fatḥ 48:22

Had those who disbelieved fought you, they would have turned their backs; then they would find neither protector nor helper.

The following verse, Al-Fath 48:23, states:

سُنَّةَ اللَّهِ الَّتِي قَدْ خَلَتْ مِنْ قَبْلُ وَلَنْ تَجِدَ لِسُنَّةِ اللَّهِ تَبْدِيلًا

sunnata allāhi allatī qad khalat min qablu wa-lan tajida li-sunnati allāhi tabdīlā

Al-Fatḥ 48:23

God's way, already established before; you will find no replacement for God's way.

Here the divine way relates to a promise in a particular confrontation.

It does not guarantee military victory to every group in every war merely because it claims belief or religious affiliation.

That generalization conflicts with the context and with Quranic passages on defeat, testing, weakness, and the consequences of mistaken decisions.

For example, Al ʿImran 3:152 connects battlefield events with disagreement, loss of resolve, and disobedience among believers.

The divine way should therefore not become an unconditional promise of victory for every claimant to faith.

6. Replacement and diversion: what does not change?

Several verses state that no tabdil or tahwil will be found in God's way.

These expressions concern its constancy, but understanding them requires identifying the judgment's subject.

Fatir 35:43 places the two terms together:

فَلَنْ تَجِدَ لِسُنَّتِ اللَّهِ تَبْدِيلًا وَلَنْ تَجِدَ لِسُنَّتِ اللَّهِ تَحْوِيلًا

fa-lan tajida li-sunnati allāhi tabdīlan wa-lan tajida li-sunnati allāhi taḥwīlā

Fāṭir 35:43

Tabdil concerns replacement or exchange; tahwil more particularly concerns turning, transferring, or changing direction or condition.

Both emphasize the stability of the way in question; they do not introduce two wholly separate laws.

Is the divine way identical with natural laws?

Recognized natural regularities, including celestial movement, the water cycle, and biological processes, can be considered within divine creation and governance.

But verses using sunnat Allah chiefly concern God's treatment of people, messengers, communities, and their behavior.

One therefore cannot assume that the expression is a technical name for all laws of physics and biology in each such verse.

Nor does its unchangeability mean God must prescribe an identical practical ruling for everyone in every period.

The Quran discusses differing prescribed ways, human capacities, repentance, reform, respite, testing, and consequences.

Constancy in God's way is compatible with differing circumstances and corresponding judgments.

The divine way and human responsibility

Al-Raʿd 13:11 states:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُغَيِّرُ مَا بِقَوْمٍ حَتَّىٰ يُغَيِّرُوا مَا بِأَنْفُسِهِمْ

inna allāha lā yughayyiru mā bi-qawmin ḥattā yughayyirū mā bi-anfusihim

Al-Raʿd 13:11

God does not change a people's condition until they change what is within themselves.

Al-Anfal 8:53 similarly connects the alteration of a blessing with people changing what is within themselves.

Although neither verse contains a derivative of S N N, they matter for understanding divine practice and human responsibility.

People do not control all the conditions of their lives, but their choices and behavior still have effects.

The divine way should therefore not excuse determinism, as if oppression, corruption, poverty, war, or defeat were inevitably imposed regardless of human agents and actual choices.

Nor do these verses establish that every natural disaster, illness, or deprivation punishes wrongdoing by the affected person or community.

The Quran also addresses testing, oppression by others, differing living conditions, and the responsibility of those causing corruption.

Constancy in God's way does not erase these distinctions; understanding an event requires examining its actual circumstances.

7. Quranic sunna and the later technical expression “the Prophet's Sunna”

In Arabic, sunna denotes a way or practice. One can accordingly speak of a person's, community's, or prophet's sunna: the course followed in life and conduct.

Later, the word acquired technical meanings in hadith scholarship and jurisprudence.

Some definitions include sayings, actions, and tacit approvals attributed to the Prophet. In some jurisprudential definitions, sunna describes a commendable act contrasted with obligation, whose omission is not treated like omission of an obligation.

These technical senses relate to the general idea of practice but are not identical with every Quranic use.

Does Quranic sunna mean a hadith collection?

The Quran uses expressions such as سُنَّةَ اللَّهِ, سُنَّةَ الْأَوَّلِينَ, سُنَنَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ, and سُنَنٌ.

These uses do not contain “the Prophet's Sunna” as a designation for collections of hadith reports compiled in later centuries.

The distinction should remain clear.

God's way cannot be translated without evidence as hadith attributed to the Prophet.

Likewise, the precedent of earlier peoples means their ways, behavior, or established patterns of life and outcome, rather than collections of historical reports about them.

This does not deny the prophets' actual conduct. The Quran itself calls for following the messenger and taking him as an example; Al-Ahzab 33:21 presents God's messenger as a good example.

Nevertheless, a prophet's actual practice, a historical report about it, and a binding religious ruling inferred from a report are distinct matters.

A report's attribution, meaning, scope, and relationship to the Quran require separate examination. Calling a report sunna does not automatically make it a certain command from God.

The lexical meaning of S N N alone consequently cannot establish the legislative authority of later hadith collections.

Nor can the label sunna alone justify setting aside a clear Quranic ruling or declaring something forbidden without divine evidence.

A fuller examination should separately consider the Quran's authority, the Prophet's actual practice, the reliability and interpretation of reports, and the evidence for any legal conclusion; the root's lexical meaning alone cannot settle these questions.

8. مَسْنُون: altered or shaped clay?

Al-Hijr 15:26 states:

وَلَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا الْإِنْسَانَ مِنْ صَلْصَالٍ مِنْ حَمَإٍ مَسْنُونٍ

wa-la-qad khalaqnā al-insāna min ṣalṣālin min ḥamaʾin masnūn

Al-Ḥijr 15:26

We created the human being from dry, sounding clay, from altered dark mud.

The same expression recurs in this excerpt from Al-Hijr 15:28:

إِنِّي خَالِقٌ بَشَرًا مِنْ صَلْصَالٍ مِنْ حَمَإٍ مَسْنُونٍ

innī khāliqun basharan min ṣalṣālin min ḥamaʾin masnūn

Al-Ḥijr 15:28

I am creating a human being from dry, sounding clay, from altered dark mud.

In Al-Hijr 15:33, Iblis uses the same expression:

قَالَ لَمْ أَكُنْ لِأَسْجُدَ لِبَشَرٍ خَلَقْتَهُ مِنْ صَلْصَالٍ مِنْ حَمَإٍ مَسْنُونٍ

qāla lam akun li-asjuda li-basharin khalaqtahu min ṣalṣālin min ḥamaʾin masnūn

Al-Ḥijr 15:33

He said: I am not one to prostrate before a human being whom You created from dry, sounding clay, from altered dark mud.

In these three verses, masnun qualifies hamaʾ; it is not an independent name for the human being or all the material of creation.

Hamaʾ concerns dark mud, while salsal describes clay in a dry, resonant condition.

Lexical sources nevertheless give differing explanations of masnun.

First interpretation: altered

One established explanation defines masnun as altered or foul-smelling. Lexicographers differ about the derivational explanation for this sense; it should not be treated as a settled historical derivation.

On this interpretation, حَمَإٍ مَسْنُونٍ describes mud that has undergone alteration.

Some lexicographers connect the change with smell and deterioration, explaining masnun as mutaghayyir or muntin.

This fits mud's character: moist and organic materials can change over time.

The meaning alone, however, cannot establish the precise substance, duration, chemical process, or biological origin.

The expression concerns the material of creation, rather than laboratory details or a specified scientific process.

Second interpretation: shaped or formed

Another lexical explanation describes masnun as shaped, smoothed, or molded.

For example, وَجْهٌ مَسْنُونٌ can describe a tapered, elongated, smooth face; some explanations also emphasize its beauty. It should not be reduced to just any defined shape.

Lexical explanations of forming clay also connect masnun with giving something a recognizable shape, although the precise derivational relationship remains disputed.

On this basis, حَمَإٍ مَسْنُونٍ can be understood as shaped or formed mud.

This interpretation has a semantic connection with creation and the formation of the human being.

After mentioning human creation, Al-Hijr 15:29 states:

فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِنْ رُوحِي فَقَعُوا لَهُ سَاجِدِينَ

fa-idhā sawwaytuhu wa-nafakhtu fīhi min rūḥī fa-qaʿū lahu sājidīn

Al-Ḥijr 15:29

When I have fashioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, fall down before him in prostration.

Sawwaytuhu expresses fashioning and bringing into proportion.

This may be considered when assessing the shaped interpretation, but its meaning cannot automatically be transferred to masnun, since the roots differ.

Third interpretation: poured

Another explanation connects masnun with سَنَّ الماءَ, pouring water in a stream.

The word may accordingly describe material that has been poured or placed in a particular condition.

Ibn Faris likewise discusses connections between flowing material, its formation, and masnun.

This is an account of semantic connections, not an independently established history of their development or the uniquely certain Quranic interpretation.

Which meaning best fits the verse?

Quranic masnun occurs only in حَمَإٍ مَسْنُونٍ. There is no independent Quranic use with another material that conclusively resolves the disagreement.

Altered has lexical support and suits hamaʾ. The current Persian Forqan translation also adopts this sense.

Shaped likewise has lexical support and merits consideration within the creation context.

Preferring the first should not erase the second; nor does the second establish a particular physical mold or shape in human creation.

Rendering the word simply as foul-smelling or rotten without explanation restricts the verse to a particular lexical account.

Retaining altered in Forqan's base rendering is defensible, with shaped discussed in the lexical explanation.

In Al-Hijr 15:33, Iblis uses the expression while refusing to prostrate. Repeating the description of the material does not itself validate his arrogant judgment about humanity.

A word's meaning must be distinguished from the conclusion a speaker seeks to draw from it.

9. سِنّ: teeth and the limits of retribution

Al-Ma'idah 5:45 states in part:

وَكَتَبْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ فِيهَا أَنَّ النَّفْسَ بِالنَّفْسِ وَالْعَيْنَ بِالْعَيْنِ وَالْأَنْفَ بِالْأَنْفِ وَالْأُذُنَ بِالْأُذُنِ وَالسِّنَّ بِالسِّنِّ وَالْجُرُوحَ قِصَاصٌ

wa-katabnā ʿalayhim fīhā anna al-nafsa bi-al-nafsi wa-al-ʿayna bi-al-ʿayni wa-al-anfa bi-al-anfi wa-al-udhuna bi-al-udhuni wa-al-sinna bi-al-sinni wa-al-jurūḥa qiṣāṣ

Al-Māʾidah 5:45

We prescribed for them therein: life for life, eye for eye, nose for nose, ear for ear, tooth for tooth, and proportionate retribution for wounds.

Al-sinn means a tooth here.

This use has no direct semantic connection to sunna as a practice or rule, although Arabic dictionaries discuss both under S N N.

وَالسِّنَّ بِالسِّنِّ places a tooth against a tooth, expressing proportionality in responding to injury.

Lexically, sinn remains a tooth; retribution comes from the full construction and the relationship among its parts.

The verse begins “We prescribed for them therein” and concerns provisions written for an earlier audience. Its legal scope requires examination alongside other verses and audiences; a complete legal ruling cannot be extracted from sinn alone.

Teeth, sharpness, and age

سَنَّ السِّكِّينَ means sharpening a knife, and مِسَنّ names a sharpening implement.

سِنان names a spearhead.

Shape and sharpness connect these uses semantically with teeth, but resemblance alone cannot establish whether the tooth's name derives from the spearhead or vice versa.

Sinn also denotes age or a stage of life in Arabic. Assessing human or animal development through teeth helps explain this use.

In Al-Ma'idah 5:45, however, tooth for tooth refers to teeth, not two people being the same age.

Age must not replace the clear bodily meaning in this verse.

10. Neighboring roots and similar forms

S N N and S Y R

Sira belongs to S Y R and concerns a way, condition, state, or manner of movement or behavior.

The practice branch of sunna overlaps with sira.

Dictionaries sometimes explain one by the other.

Sira can nevertheless denote something's state, as in Ta Ha 20:21:

سَنُعِيدُهَا سِيرَتَهَا الْأُولَىٰ

sa-nuʿīduhā sīratahā al-ūlā

Ṭā Hā 20:21

We shall restore it to its former state.

Quranic sunna more often foregrounds an established course in conduct or events.

This is a tendency, not an absolute boundary between all uses of the families.

S N N and ʿ W D

ʿAwd concerns returning and recurrence of a condition or behavior.

Sunna describes a practice that may continue or recur.

A sunna need not arise through prolonged repetition; a practice can begin and become available for imitation.

Conversely, not every recurring event is an established social practice.

In Al-Anfal 8:38, وَإِنْ يَعُودُوا concerns returning to earlier behavior, while سُنَّتُ الْأَوَّلِينَ concerns an established precedent.

Returning to behavior and a historical pattern with consequences are related but different ideas.

S N N and SH R ʿ

Sharʿ and shariʿa in the Quran concern a prescribed way for conduct and guidance.

Sunna can also describe a way established by God.

The terms are not complete synonyms.

Quranic sunna can concern earlier practices, God's treatment of societies, and historical consequences.

Sharʿ and shariʿa concern establishing a religious and practical path.

A prescribed religious way may contain practices and regulations, but not every historical precedent means divine legislation.

S N N and S N W / S N H

سُنَّة, a practice, and سَنَة, a year, look very similar without vowel marks.

Their morphology and derivation must nevertheless be distinguished.

Sunna, a practice, belongs to S N N.

Arabic morphological accounts connect sana, a year, with S N W or S N H.

Quranic sana and sinin concern years and passing time. Written resemblance alone does not make them derivatives of S N N meaning practice.

Likewise, sinatun in Al-Baqarah 2:255 occurs in:

لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ

lā taʾkhudhuhu sinatun wa-lā nawm

Al-Baqarah 2:255

The expression means “Neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes Him.” Sinatun denotes drowsiness or the onset of sleep and belongs to W S N.

Three nearby forms must therefore remain distinct: sunna, practice; sana, year; and sina, drowsiness.

Similarity of letters does not establish identity of root.

11. Translation suggestions for Forqan

Depending on context, sunna can be rendered as way, practice, course, established pattern, or, where appropriate, established rule.

In Al ʿImran 3:137:

قَدْ خَلَتْ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ سُنَنٌ

qad khalat min qablikum sunan

Āl ʿImrān 3:137

A suitable rendering is:

“Ways and precedents have passed before you.”

The word concerns recognizable historical patterns and should not be limited to religious regulations.

In Al-Nisa 4:26:

وَيَهْدِيَكُمْ سُنَنَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ

wa-yahdiyakum sunana alladhīna min qablikum

Al-Nisāʾ 4:26

One may say:

“And guide you to the ways of those before you.”

For Persian readers, a plain word for ways may be clearer than sunan, which might be understood exclusively through later religious terminology.

For سُنَّةَ اللَّهِ, God's way or God's established practice is suitable.

In the expression:

وَلَنْ تَجِدَ لِسُنَّةِ اللَّهِ تَبْدِيلًا

wa-lan tajida li-sunnati allāhi tabdīlā

Al-Aḥzāb 33:62

One may say:

“You will find no replacement for God's sunna.”

Or, more directly:

“You will find no replacement for God's established way.”

Because Fatir 35:43 places tabdil and tahwil together, translation should preserve their distinction as far as possible rather than automatically render both with one word.

The practice of earlier peoples suits some uses of سُنَّةَ الْأَوَّلِينَ. Where their outcome is prominent, an explanation such as the established precedent concerning earlier peoples can clarify the range.

Sinn in Al-Ma'idah 5:45 is simply a tooth.

For حَمَإٍ مَسْنُونٍ, the current Persian Forqan rendering, altered mud, is defensible.

Shaped and formed also have lexical support and should be examined in the word's explanation.

The base translation should not stack differing interpretations as “altered, molded, foul-smelling mud” unless the construction supports all those assertions together.

Select one interpretation for the base rendering and explain the other supported readings separately. These suggestions do not change the installed verse translations.

Conclusion

S N N is a branching Arabic family with three Quranic fields: ways and practices, teeth, and material described as formed or altered.

Sunna and sunan principally denote a way or pattern recognized in human behavior or events.

The practice need not be good; a society's way may rest on justice or on oppression, denial, and plotting.

The precedent of earlier peoples can emphasize their conduct or the outcome following it. Context determines the precise sense.

God's way concerns a course established in a specific matter. The expression occurs concerning prophets, societies, punishment, forgiveness, and divine aid.

Its constancy does not negate human choice or make all conditions identical. The Quran also describes repentance, reform, differing circumstances, and responsibility.

Quranic sunna should not be equated with later technical hadith and jurisprudential senses. A prophet's actual practice, a historical report, and a ruling derived from that report are distinct matters. Giving all three one label cannot replace examination of attribution, meaning, and Quranic evidence.

Quranic sinn clearly means a tooth. Its connections with sharp implements and age can be studied, but cannot displace the verse's direct meaning.

Masnun qualifies hamaʾ in the human-creation passages. Lexical sources support different explanations, including altered and shaped. Without decisive evidence, neither should become a certain creation history or scientific theory.

Final definition: In the practice branch, S N N denotes an established way followed in conduct or events. In the bodily branch, sinn names a tooth, with connections to sharpness and shaping. Masnun qualifies mud as altered or formed, with an established disagreement about the precise interpretation.

Quranic sunna must be understood through its practitioner and context. Human practices can be right or wrong; the divine way concerns God's treatment of circumstances and actions. Its constancy neither removes human responsibility nor authorizes anyone to turn their own practice or report into God's ruling without evidence.
