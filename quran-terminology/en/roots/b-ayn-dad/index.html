Linguistic Note

The semantic core of B ʿ Ḍ is taking or considering a part of a whole. When the Qur’an says بَعْض (baʿḍ), it is not speaking about all of the set, but about some part of it. The word itself does not fix the amount. It may be small or significant; context decides.

This matters deeply in translation. If بَعْض (baʿḍ) is ignored or turned into “all,” a precise limited statement can become a sweeping absolute claim. In verses about human, social, or family relations, this small word can change the whole direction of interpretation.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of B ʿ Ḍ is partness in relation to a whole. بَعْض (baʿḍ) moves attention away from the entire set and toward one portion of it. Without a whole to relate to, the meaning is incomplete.

For this reason, the root carries precision, boundary, and measure. It prevents unwarranted totalizing. In Qur’anic reasoning, this small word protects a statement from being expanded beyond what it says.

Material Image

The material image is something separated or distinguished from a whole: a piece of bread, a part of land, a portion of a book, a limb of a body, or a group within a population. “Some people” does not mean all people. “Part of the Book” does not mean the whole Book.

In the special form بَعُوضَة (baʿūḍah), the sense of smallness appears vividly. A mosquito is tiny in human estimation, yet its smallness does not prevent it from being used in a divine example.

Historical and Arabic Usage Note

In classical Arabic, بَعْض (baʿḍ) is used for a part, portion, or piece of a whole. It does not name a fixed quantity; the context supplies that.

As for بَعُوضَة (baʿūḍah), the mosquito is recognized as something small and slight. Some lexical discussions connect the word with smallness or partness. That image may help, but it should not be overburdened. In the Qur’an, the main point is that The God does not refrain from setting an example even with something as small as a mosquito.

Some Versus All

One basic use of this root is marking part of a whole. In Al-Baqarah 2:85, the Qur’an says:

أَفَتُؤْمِنُونَ بِبَعْضِ الْكِتَابِ وَتَكْفُرُونَ بِبَعْضٍ (afa-tu'minūna bi-baʿḍi al-kitābi wa-takfurūna bi-baʿḍ)

Do you then have faith in part of the Book and cover truth regarding another part?

The issue here is selective treatment of the Book. They have not rejected the whole Book, but they accept part and cover over another part. بَعْض (baʿḍ) exposes a fragmented relationship with revelation shaped by advantage.

Some Over Some Others: Internal Relation, Not Absolute Rule

The Qur’anic expression بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ (baʿḍahum ʿalā baʿḍ) shows a relation within a set. In Al-Baqarah 2:253, the Qur’an says:

تِلْكَ الرُّسُلُ فَضَّلْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ (tilka al-rusulu faḍḍalnā baʿḍahum ʿalā baʿḍ)

Those messengers: We gave some of them advantage over some others.

The verse does not erase the value of the other messengers. It speaks about differences of rank, mission, signs, and station within the group of messengers. The same structure appears in Al-Isra 17:55: وَلَقَدْ فَضَّلْنَا بَعْضَ النَّبِيِّينَ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ (wa-laqad faḍḍalnā baʿḍa al-nabiyyīna ʿalā baʿḍ).

This precision is crucial in An-Nisa 4:34:

الرِّجَالُ قَوَّامُونَ عَلَى النِّسَاءِ بِمَا فَضَّلَ اللَّهُ بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ (al-rijālu qawwāmūna ʿalā al-nisā'i bimā faḍḍala Allāhu baʿḍahum ʿalā baʿḍ)

بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ (baʿḍahum ʿalā baʿḍ) must not be turned into “all men over all women.” The phrase itself says some over some. The verse is not making an absolute claim of inherent superiority for one sex over the other. It points to real differences in capacities, responsibilities, provision, and family ordering.

Here, بَعْض (baʿḍ) is not a decorative qualifier. It is central to the verse. Removing it pushes the reading toward domination.

Some of You from Some Others: Shared Belonging

In contrast with comparative expressions, the Qur’an also uses بَعْضُكُم مِّن بَعْضٍ (baʿḍukum min baʿḍ). In Ali 'Imran 3:195, it says:

أَنِّي لَا أُضِيعُ عَمَلَ عَامِلٍ مِّنكُم مِّن ذَكَرٍ أَوْ أُنثَىٰ ۖ بَعْضُكُم مِّن بَعْضٍ (annī lā uḍīʿu ʿamala ʿāmilin minkum min dhakarin aw unthā baʿḍukum min baʿḍ)

I do not cause the work of any worker among you, male or female, to be lost; some of you are from some others.

This expression speaks of human kinship and shared belonging. Men and women are not alien to one another in the reward of righteous work. They stand in one human and faithful field.

In An-Nisa 4:25, the Qur’an also says: وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِإِيمَانِكُم ۚ بَعْضُكُم مِّن بَعْضٍ (wa-Allāhu aʿlamu bi-īmānikum baʿḍukum min baʿḍ). Social status, gender, or class should not make human beings strangers to one another.

Some as Allies of Some: Internal Support

The Qur’an uses بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَاءُ بَعْضٍ (baʿḍuhum awliyā'u baʿḍ) to show internal support within a group. In At-Tawbah 9:71, it says:

وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتُ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَاءُ بَعْضٍ (wa-l-mu'minūna wa-l-mu'minātu baʿḍuhum awliyā'u baʿḍ)

The believing men and the believing women are allies and supporters of one another.

The relation is reciprocal. Believing men and believing women are not detached from each other; they share responsibility, support, commanding what is recognized, restraining what is rejected, salat, zakat, and obedience to The God and His Messenger.

By contrast, At-Tawbah 9:67 says of the hypocrites: الْمُنَافِقُونَ وَالْمُنَافِقَاتُ بَعْضُهُم مِّن بَعْضٍ (al-munāfiqūna wa-l-munāfiqātu baʿḍuhum min baʿḍ). The same form of internal kinship appears, but in the field of hypocrisy.

Some Suspicion, Not Every Suspicion

In Al-Hujurat 49:12, the Qur’an says:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اجْتَنِبُوا كَثِيرًا مِّنَ الظَّنِّ إِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ إِثْمٌ (yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū ij'tanibū kathīran mina al-ẓanni inna baʿḍa al-ẓanni ithm)

O you who have faith, avoid much assumption; indeed, some assumption is sin.

The Qur’an does not say that every assumption is sin. It says some assumptions are sinful, and therefore much assumption should be avoided. Because a person may not always know which assumption is polluted and sinful, caution is required in this field.

Part of the Promised Outcome

Several verses speak of seeing or not seeing part of what is promised. In Yunus 10:46, the Qur’an says:

وَإِمَّا نُرِيَنَّكَ بَعْضَ الَّذِي نَعِدُهُمْ أَوْ نَتَوَفَّيَنَّكَ فَإِلَيْنَا مَرْجِعُهُمْ (wa-immā nuriyannaka baʿḍa alladhī naʿiduhum aw natawaffayannaka fa-ilaynā marjiʿuhum)

If We show you part of what We promise them, or take you in death, then to Us is their return.

In Ar-Ra'd 13:40, it says: وَإِن مَّا نُرِيَنَّكَ بَعْضَ الَّذِي نَعِدُهُمْ أَوْ نَتَوَفَّيَنَّكَ فَإِنَّمَا عَلَيْكَ الْبَلَاغُ وَعَلَيْنَا الْحِسَابُ (wa-in mā nuriyannaka baʿḍa alladhī naʿiduhum aw natawaffayannaka fa-innamā ʿalayka al-balāghu wa-ʿalaynā al-ḥisāb).

Here, بَعْض (baʿḍ) marks the limit of human sight. Not every outcome becomes visible during the Prophet’s life. His duty is delivery; the accounting belongs to The God.

Baʿūḍah: The Mosquito in Divine Example

A special use of this root appears in Al-Baqarah 2:26:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَسْتَحْيِي أَن يَضْرِبَ مَثَلًا مَّا بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (inna Allāha lā yastaḥyī an yaḍriba mathalan mā baʿūḍatan fa-mā fawqahā)

The God does not refrain from setting an example, even of a mosquito or what is beyond it.

بَعُوضَةً (baʿūḍatan) means a mosquito, a tiny creature that may seem insignificant to human beings. The verse says that smallness does not prevent an example from being true. The measure is not the outward size of the example, but its truth and guiding function.

بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (baʿūḍatan fa-mā fawqahā) can be understood in two directions: a mosquito and what is beyond it in smallness, or a mosquito and what is above it in size. In either case, the message remains: divine examples are not bound by outward scale.

Distinguishing Qur’anic Uses

In Al-Baqarah 2:85, بَعْض (baʿḍ) exposes selective treatment of the Book. In Al-Baqarah 2:253 and Al-Isra 17:55, بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ (baʿḍahum ʿalā baʿḍ) shows relative distinction within a set. In An-Nisa 4:34, the same expression prevents the verse from becoming a claim that all men are superior to all women.

In Ali 'Imran 3:195 and An-Nisa 4:25, بَعْضُكُم مِّن بَعْضٍ (baʿḍukum min baʿḍ) expresses shared human belonging. In At-Tawbah 9:71, believing men and believing women are mutual allies. In Al-Hujurat 49:12, بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ (baʿḍa al-ẓann) shows that not every assumption is sin. In Yunus 10:46 and Ar-Ra'd 13:40, بَعْض (baʿḍ) marks partial visibility of a promised outcome. In Al-Baqarah 2:26, بَعُوضَة (baʿūḍah) is the mosquito in a divine example.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

بَعْض (baʿḍ) often appears in an idafah construction: بَعْضُهُمْ (baʿḍuhum), بَعْضَ الَّذِي (baʿḍa alladhī), بِبَعْضِ الْكِتَابِ (bi-baʿḍi al-kitāb). This shows that baʿḍ always relates to a whole.

In بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ (baʿḍuhum ʿalā baʿḍ), على (ʿalā) indicates a relation of advantage, precedence, or relative authority. But because both sides are expressed with بَعْض (baʿḍ), the meaning is not absolute. In بَعْضُكُم مِّن بَعْضٍ (baʿḍukum min baʿḍ), مِن (min) signals connection, source, shared kind, or inner belonging.

In إِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ إِثْمٌ (inna baʿḍa al-ẓanni ithm), بَعْض (baʿḍ) prevents the ruling from being generalized to every assumption. In بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (baʿūḍatan fa-mā fawqahā), the main point is that the truth of an example is not blocked by its smallness or greatness.

Difference from Nearby Words

B ʿ Ḍ contrasts with K L L. كُلّ (kull) takes the whole set; بَعْض (baʿḍ) takes part of it. If this difference is ignored, a partial statement becomes a universal one.

B ʿ Ḍ is close to J Z ʾ, but not identical. جُزْء (juz') usually points to a component or structural part of a whole. بَعْض (baʿḍ) can be any unspecified portion of a set without necessarily being a technical structural component.

B ʿ Ḍ also differs from Q S M, N Ṣ B, Ṭ ʾ F, F R Q, and Sh Ṭ R. A qism is a divided share; a naṣīb is an allotted portion or share that reaches someone; a ṭā'ifah or farīq carries clearer group identity; shaṭr is closer to half, side, or direction. Baʿḍ is broader and does not name a fixed amount.

Forqan Translation Method

In Forqan translation, بَعْض (baʿḍ) must be taken seriously. This small word prevents false generalization. بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ (baʿḍuhum ʿalā baʿḍ) should not be understood as “all of them over all of them.” بَعْضِ الْكِتَابِ (baʿḍi al-kitāb) should not be flattened into “the Book” generally. بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ (baʿḍa al-ẓann) should not become “every assumption.”

In An-Nisa 4:34, the verse does not say that The God gave all men advantage over all women. Translation should show that the statement is about some in relation to some others: real differences in capacity, responsibility, provision, circumstance, and family ordering, not inherent and absolute privilege for one sex.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For بَعْض (baʿḍ): some; part; a portion; some part of.

For بِبَعْضِ الْكِتَابِ (bi-baʿḍi al-kitāb): in part of the Book; in a portion of the Book.

For وَتَكْفُرُونَ بِبَعْضٍ (wa-takfurūna bi-baʿḍ): and cover truth regarding another part.

For بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ (baʿḍahum ʿalā baʿḍ): some over some others; some of them over others; some were given advantage over some others in capacities and responsibilities, in explanation.

For بَعْضُكُم مِّن بَعْضٍ (baʿḍukum min baʿḍ): some of you are from some others; you belong to one another, in explanation.

For بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَاءُ بَعْضٍ (baʿḍuhum awliyā'u baʿḍ): some are allies of some others; they are supporters and protectors of one another.

For إِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ إِثْمٌ (inna baʿḍa al-ẓanni ithm): indeed, some assumption is sin.

For بَعْضَ الَّذِي نَعِدُهُمْ (baʿḍa alladhī naʿiduhum): part of what We promise them.

For بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (baʿūḍatan fa-mā fawqahā): a mosquito or what is beyond it; a mosquito or something above it, in explanation.

Summary

The root B ʿ Ḍ in the Qur’an points to a part, portion, or some of a whole. Its role in translation is large because it prevents unwarranted generalization. Wherever the Qur’an says بَعْض (baʿḍ), the statement must not be turned into كُلّ (kull).

In verses about the Book, baʿḍ exposes selective faith. In verses about prophets and human relations, it marks limited internal relations. In verses about men and women, it blocks absolute domination readings. In the verse of the mosquito, even the smallest example can carry truth and guidance.
