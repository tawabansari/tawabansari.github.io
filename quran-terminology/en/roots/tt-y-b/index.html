Qur’anic Root Ṭ Y B

Purity, pleasantness, wholesomeness, and fitting goodness: from food and land to speech, conduct, and human life

Qur’anic and lexical forms

طَابَ
(ṭāba)
Qur’anic verb: was agreeable or suitable; Al-Nisāʾ 4:3

طِبْنَ
(ṭibna)
Qur’anic feminine plural verb: the women’s willingness; Al-Nisāʾ 4:4

طِبْتُمْ
(ṭibtum)
Qur’anic past-tense verb addressing a plural audience; Al-Zumar 39:73

طَيِّب
(ṭayyib)
Qur’anic adjective or noun; precise meaning follows its referent and context

طَيِّبَة
(ṭayyibah)
Qur’anic feminine adjective; nine occurrences

طَيِّبَات
(ṭayyibāt)
Qur’anic feminine plural; 21 occurrences, extending beyond food

طَيِّبُون / طَيِّبِين
(ṭayyibūn / ṭayyibīn)
Qur’anic masculine plurals; included among the 16 ṭayyib occurrences

طُوبَىٰ
(ṭūbā)
Qur’anic form in the Ṭ Y B family: happiness and blessedness; Al-Raʿd 13:29

الطِّيب
(al-ṭīb)
Lexical noun for fragrance or perfume; this form is not Qur’anic

تَطْيَاب
(taṭyāb)
Lexical form in the ʿAlqamah example: fragrance; not Qur’anic

The Arabic root Ṭ Y B concerns purity, pleasantness, wholesomeness, and a desirable quality. Depending on its nature and use, something described as طَيِّب (ṭayyib) may be clean, pleasant, sound, good, or fitting. Its opposite, خَبِيث (khabīth), concerns something impure, undesirable, corrupt, or unworthy.

The Qur’an does not use this root only for food. Fertile land, a favorable wind, good speech, people of good character, worthy offspring, a good life, pleasant dwellings, and good provisions can all be described through this family. Restricting ṭayyib to “lawful,” “free of contamination,” or “tasty” therefore overlooks much of its semantic field.

The uses share a goodness or desirability understood according to each thing’s nature and function. Ṭayyib land suits growth; a ṭayyib wind helps a ship travel favorably; ṭayyib speech is commendable in meaning and value; and a willing person may relinquish something freely. In each case, context identifies the aspect of desirability intended.

1. Qur’anic forms and early Arabic use

The principal Qur’anic forms are the verbs طَابَ (ṭāba), طِبْنَ (ṭibna), and طِبْتُمْ (ṭibtum); طَيِّب as an adjective or noun with its various inflections; طَيِّبَة (ṭayyibah); the plural طَيِّبَات (ṭayyibāt); and طُوبَىٰ (ṭūbā). In the Qur’anic corpus dictionary’s grouping, the root occurs 50 times in 46 verses: three verbs, sixteen occurrences of ṭayyib including its singular and masculine plural forms, twenty-one ṭayyibāt, nine ṭayyibah, and one ṭūbā. These are lexical groupings; every case change is not an independent derivative.

Ṭayyib can be an adjective, as in صَعِيدًا طَيِّبًا, or function nominally opposite al-khabīth. Ṭayyibāt does not always refer to food: some verses concern broader provisions and benefits, while another connects it with people or their words.

The root’s meaning in Arabic

Older lexicons commonly place ṭayyib opposite khabīth. The contrast extends across several fields beyond bodily cleanliness.

طَابَ الشيءُ (ṭāba al-shayʾ) describes something pleasant, wholesome, or agreeable. الطِّيب (al-ṭīb) concerns fragrance and aromatic substances. Ṭayyib food can be palatable and suitable, ṭayyib land suitable for vegetation, and a ṭayyib wind gentle and favorable.

The family also appears in human relationships. طِيبُ النَّفْس (ṭību al-nafs) concerns inward willingness and contentment. A person may agree to something without compulsion or resentment.

Qur’anic evidence and lexical reports thus reveal a field broader than outward cleanliness. A later lexical report alone does not establish when each meaning originated.

An example from poetry attributed to a pre-Islamic poet

An ode attributed to the pre-Islamic poet ʿAlqamah Al-Faḥl contains:

يَحْمِلْنَ أُتْرُجَّةً نَضْخُ الْعَبِيرِ بِهَا
كَأَنَّ تَطْيَابَهَا فِي الْأَنْفِ مَشْمُومُ

yaḥmilna utrujjatan naḍkhu al-ʿabīri bihā / ka-anna taṭyābahā fī al-anfi mashmūmu

“[The camels] carry a woman like a citron, sprinkled with a blend of perfume; as though her fragrance were sensed in the nose.” In the ode’s context, utrujjah, “citron,” is an image for the woman riding in the litter, rather than simply a fruit being transported. Older literary explanations likewise connect the image with the woman’s fragrance.

تَطْيَاب (taṭyāb) concerns fragrance here. The line is attributed to ʿAlqamah in his transmitted diwan, Lisān al-ʿArab, and commentaries on Adab al-Kātib. Readings of its first part include naḍkh, naḍḥ, and naḍj. These transmission differences should remain distinct from the clear sense of fragrance in the word being examined.

Fragrance should nevertheless not be treated as the sole material image or certain source of every meaning. Taste, land, wind, willingness, and fitting goodness show a broader field. The evidence does not establish that all these meanings necessarily developed from perfume.

2. Ṭayyib in nature: land, wind, and cleanliness

Ṭayyib land: suitable for growth

Al-Aʿrāf states:

وَالْبَلَدُ الطَّيِّبُ يَخْرُجُ نَبَاتُهُ بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهِ وَالَّذِي خَبُثَ لَا يَخْرُجُ إِلَّا نَكِدًا كَذَٰلِكَ نُصَرِّفُ الْآيَاتِ لِقَوْمٍ يَشْكُرُونَ

wa-al-baladu al-ṭayyibu yakhruju nabātuhu bi-idhni rabbihi wa-alladhī khabutha lā yakhruju illā nakidan ka-dhālika nuṣarrifu al-āyāti li-qawmin yashkurūna

“The good land brings forth its vegetation by its Lord’s permission, while the poor land yields only meagerly and with difficulty. Thus We present the signs in varied ways for people who give thanks.” (Al-Aʿrāf 7:58)

Ṭayyib describes land here, and the contrast between two kinds of land clarifies its meaning. Ṭayyib land is fertile and suitable for plants; the opposite is land where growth is difficult or scant.

“Clean land” can mislead if it suggests only freedom from contamination. “Fertile land” or “land suitable for growth” is more precise.

The use also shows that ṭayyib need not be restricted to what pleases human senses. The land’s natural function and yield matter here. Land may be unremarkable in appearance yet well suited to cultivation.

Sabaʾ 34:15 also speaks of بَلْدَةٌ طَيِّبَةٌ (baldatun ṭayyibatun). Two gardens beside the settlement, provision, and the call to gratitude frame this description. It can express a flourishing, pleasant, well-provided land; neither scenic beauty nor soil fertility alone exhausts its meaning.

A ṭayyib wind: favorable, not necessarily fragrant

In Yūnus 10:22, ships travel with رِيحٍ طَيِّبَةٍ, and their passengers rejoice. A violent wind and waves then transform the situation.

Rīḥin ṭayyibah here concerns a wind favorable for sailing. The expression does not primarily mean a fragrant wind, although Arabic can also describe wind in terms of smell.

The contrast with the storm wind shows ṭayyib describing a quality favorable to the voyage.

This matters for the root’s study. Its derivatives cannot all be reduced to one sensory experience, such as fragrance or pleasant taste. Context identifies the quality suitable and desirable in the situation discussed.

صَعِيدًا طَيِّبًا: earth suitable for tayammum

Al-Nisāʾ 4:43 and Al-Māʾidah 5:6 contain صَعِيدًا طَيِّبًا in their instructions concerning tayammum.

Ṣaʿīd concerns the earth’s surface or material of the earth, and ṭayyiban describes it. Cleanliness and suitability for the prescribed act matter here.

“Clean earth” is natural in this context. Ṭayyib alone, however, does not supply every legal detail about soil, sand, stone, and other materials. Determining the permitted referents requires separate examination of ṣaʿīd, the command’s construction, and both verses’ contexts.

Nor can ṭayyib simply mean fertile here. The same adjective concerns plant growth in Al-Aʿrāf 7:58, whereas the tayammum provision emphasizes cleanliness and suitability for that act.

3. Ṭayyib in people: fitting goodness, willingness, and purity

مَا طَابَ لَكُمْ: what is agreeable and suitable

Al-Nisāʾ 4:3 uses مَا طَابَ لَكُمْ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ while discussing marriage.

Ṭāba concerns being agreeable or suitable in this construction. Lakum relates that suitability to the addressees.

“Women who are agreeable and suitable for you” conveys the expression more naturally than “women who are clean.” Nor should it be confined to physical attractiveness: the verse concerns marriage and justice.

This lexical analysis does not by itself determine every moral and legal condition of marriage. The provision is understood from the construction and context as a whole.

فَإِنْ طِبْنَ لَكُمْ: actual willingness

The next verse states:

وَآتُوا النِّسَاءَ صَدُقَاتِهِنَّ نِحْلَةً فَإِنْ طِبْنَ لَكُمْ عَنْ شَيْءٍ مِنْهُ نَفْسًا فَكُلُوهُ هَنِيئًا مَرِيئًا

wa-ātū al-nisāʾa ṣaduqātihinna niḥlatan fa-in ṭibna lakum ʿan shayʾin minhu nafsan fa-kulūhu hanīʾan marīʾan

“Give women their bridal dues as an obligatory gift. If they willingly relinquish any part of it to you, then enjoy it agreeably and wholesomely.” (Al-Nisāʾ 4:4)

Ṭibna concerns the women and their willingness. Nafsan specifies the inward aspect of that willingness: willing consent and contentment.

The expression does not mean the women have become morally pure or something has become tasty. It concerns voluntarily relinquishing part of a financial entitlement.

Fa-in ṭibna makes the women’s willingness a condition. A relinquishment arising from pressure, fear, or compulsion cannot be treated without further examination as a clear instance of such willing consent.

Ṭayyib in human relationships can therefore concern an inward state of agreement with a decision or relinquishment.

A ṭayyib person: worthy character and conduct

Al-Naḥl 16:32 describes those whose lives the angels take as طَيِّبِينَ, and the angels greet them with peace.

Ṭayyib describes people here and relates to their moral and believing condition. “Pure and worthy” or “of good character” suits the context. Bodily cleanliness or physical health is not necessarily intended.

In Al-Zumar 39:73, the Garden’s keepers say to those mindful of their Lord: سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ طِبْتُمْ فَادْخُلُوهَا خَالِدِينَ.

Ṭibtum is a past-tense verb addressed to a plural audience: you have been ṭayyib, or have reached a pure and worthy condition. “Welcome” alone may convey the reception’s atmosphere but leaves the verb’s own meaning unexpressed.

In Āl ʿImrān 3:38, Zakariyyā asks his Lord for ذُرِّيَّةً طَيِّبَةً. Ṭayyib here concerns worthy, good offspring, rather than merely a beautiful, fragrant, or physically healthy child.

Applied to people, purity, goodness, and moral worth can thus become prominent. Context brings these aspects forward; every use should not be restricted to a moral definition of a human being.

4. Ṭayyib in speech, conduct, and relationships

كَلِمَةً طَيِّبَةً: a good word with roots and fruit

Ibrāhīm states:

أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ ضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا كَلِمَةً طَيِّبَةً كَشَجَرَةٍ طَيِّبَةٍ أَصْلُهَا ثَابِتٌ وَفَرْعُهَا فِي السَّمَاءِ

a-lam tara kayfa ḍaraba allāhu mathalan kalimatan ṭayyibatan ka-shajaratin ṭayyibatin aṣluhā thābitun wa-farʿuhā fī al-samāʾi

“Have you not seen how The God sets forth a parable? A good word is like a good tree, its root firm and its branches in the sky.” (Ibrāhīm 14:24)

Ṭayyib modifies both word and tree, but its meaning with these two nouns is not translated identically in every respect.

Within the parable, the good tree has firm roots, and the following verse describes its fruit. The good word is likewise worthy, sound speech compared with that tree.

The comparison should not turn rootedness or fruit-bearing into the lexical definition of ṭayyib. Those features belong to the parable and illuminate the good word, but the adjective does not carry them in every other Qur’anic use.

Fāṭir 35:10 also contains الْكَلِمُ الطَّيِّبُ alongside الْعَمَلُ الصَّالِحُ. Good speech and righteous action are related here but remain distinct expressions.

Al-Ḥajj 22:24 speaks of guidance toward الطَّيِّبِ مِنَ الْقَوْلِ: pure and good speech. Ṭayyib concerns speech’s worth and quality.

Good speech is therefore more than pleasing sounds or attractive wording. Speech may sound beautiful yet be false or deceptive; true and worthy speech may displease its audience without thereby losing its goodness.

الطَّيِّبَاتُ لِلطَّيِّبِينَ: people or speech?

Al-Nūr states:

الْخَبِيثَاتُ لِلْخَبِيثِينَ وَالْخَبِيثُونَ لِلْخَبِيثَاتِ وَالطَّيِّبَاتُ لِلطَّيِّبِينَ وَالطَّيِّبُونَ لِلطَّيِّبَاتِ أُولَٰئِكَ مُبَرَّءُونَ مِمَّا يَقُولُونَ لَهُمْ مَغْفِرَةٌ وَرِزْقٌ كَرِيمٌ

al-khabīthātu li-l-khabīthīna wa-al-khabīthūna li-l-khabīthāti wa-al-ṭayyibātu li-l-ṭayyibīna wa-al-ṭayyibūna li-l-ṭayyibāti ulāʾika mubarraʾūna mimmā yaqūlūna lahum maghfiratun wa-rizqun karīmun

“Impure things are for impure people, and impure people for impure things; pure things are for pure people, and pure people for pure things. Those are innocent of what is said about them. For them are forgiveness and generous provision.” (Al-Nūr 24:26)

The masculine and feminine forms merit attention. Al-ṭayyibūn and al-ṭayyibīn are masculine sound plurals; al-ṭayyibāt is feminine and can grammatically refer to female people or to feminine nouns such as words.

Two readings are discussed: the association of pure and impure people with one another, and the relation of pure and impure speech to its speakers. The context of accusations, particularly the closing mimmā yaqūlūn, “what they say,” matters to the second reading.

Grammatical gender alone should not turn this verse into a categorical law governing everyone’s marriage. Al-Taḥrīm 66:10 and Al-Taḥrīm 66:11 themselves describe husbands and wives following different paths of belief.

For this root study, ṭayyib clearly concerns purity and worthiness. Identifying the feminine forms’ precise referents and the statement’s scope requires contextual examination.

A ṭayyib greeting

In Al-Nūr 24:61, a greeting is described as مُبَارَكَةً طَيِّبَةً.

Ṭayyib concerns the greeting’s goodness, with blessing as a further description. These meanings are related without necessarily being identical.

A ṭayyib greeting can be good, pure, and fitting, expressing respect and goodwill. The meaning extends beyond sensory experience to the quality of human relationships.

5. Ṭayyib in food and provision, and its relation to lawful and impure

حَلَالًا طَيِّبًا: two words within one command

Al-Baqarah states:

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ كُلُوا مِمَّا فِي الْأَرْضِ حَلَالًا طَيِّبًا وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا خُطُوَاتِ الشَّيْطَانِ إِنَّهُ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ مُبِينٌ

yā ayyuhā al-nāsu kulū mimmā fī al-arḍi ḥalālan ṭayyiban wa-lā tattabiʿū khuṭuwāti al-shayṭāni innahu lakum ʿaduwwun mubīnun

“People, eat of what is on earth, lawful and wholesome, and do not follow Satan’s footsteps; he is a clear enemy to you.” (Al-Baqarah 2:168)

Ḥalālan and ṭayyiban appear together. Ḥalāl concerns permissibility and the absence of prohibition; ṭayyib concerns goodness, purity, and desirability.

A referent can possess both qualities without the words being lexically identical. If ṭayyib invariably meant lawful, its uses for wind, land, inward willingness, and speech would remain unexplained.

The expression also occurs in Al-Māʾidah 5:88, Al-Anfāl 8:69, and Al-Naḥl 16:114. Enjoyment of provision is connected with mindfulness or gratitude.

The two descriptions allow permissibility and desirable quality to be distinguished. That does not mean the Qur’an supplies two separate, fixed lists in every instance, one of lawful things and another of ṭayyib things.

The referents of each provision must be understood through the verses, context, and appropriate criteria.

Ṭayyibāt: good and wholesome provisions

Several verses use ṭayyibāt for provisions and blessings from The God.

In Al-Baqarah 2:57, Al-Baqarah 2:172, Al-Aʿrāf 7:160, Ṭā Hā 20:81, and Al-Muʾminūn 23:51, it concerns eating the provision bestowed. Al-Baqarah 2:57 and Al-Aʿrāf 7:160 mention manna and quails in that setting.

Al-Māʾidah 5:4 and Al-Māʾidah 5:5 discuss ṭayyibāt in answering what has been made lawful. Al-Aʿrāf 7:32 places الطَّيِّبَاتِ مِنَ الرِّزْقِ alongside divine adornment.

Al-Anfāl 8:26, Yūnus 10:93, Al-Naḥl 16:72, Al-Isrāʾ 17:70, Ghāfir 40:64, and Al-Jāthiyah 45:16 also concern receiving ṭayyibāt as provision. These can include the good, beneficial, desirable things people enjoy.

Al-Aḥqāf 46:20 tells a group of disbelievers that they have used up their ṭayyibāt in worldly life and enjoyed them. The same verse identifies wrongful arrogance and defiance as grounds for punishment.

Ṭayyibāt can thus refer to actual pleasures and benefits of worldly life. Enjoying pleasant things is not itself necessarily presented as the offense; conduct’s moral value must be assessed through the verse’s other components.

Ṭayyibāt therefore does not designate a fixed food list throughout the Qur’an. Depending on context, it can concern good food or a broader range of life’s provisions and benefits.

Ṭayyib and khabīth in giving and property

Al-Baqarah states:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَنْفِقُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا كَسَبْتُمْ وَمِمَّا أَخْرَجْنَا لَكُمْ مِنَ الْأَرْضِ وَلَا تَيَمَّمُوا الْخَبِيثَ مِنْهُ تُنْفِقُونَ وَلَسْتُمْ بِآخِذِيهِ إِلَّا أَنْ تُغْمِضُوا فِيهِ وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ غَنِيٌّ حَمِيدٌ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū anfiqū min ṭayyibāti mā kasabtum wa-mimmā akhrajnā lakum mina al-arḍi wa-lā tayammamū al-khabītha minhu tunfiqūna wa-lastum bi-ākhidhīhi illā an tughmiḍū fīhi wa-iʿlamū anna allāha ghaniyyun ḥamīdun

“You who have believed, give from the good things you have earned and what We have brought forth for you from the earth. Do not select the poor-quality part to give away, when you would not accept it yourselves except by overlooking its defects. Know that The God is self-sufficient and praiseworthy.” (Al-Baqarah 2:267)

The contrast concerns giving, earnings, and the earth’s produce. Wa-lastum bi-ākhidhīhi illā an tughmiḍū fīhi provides an important criterion: what you would not accept except by overlooking its defects should not be offered to others as a worthy gift.

Khabīth can here refer to undesirable, poor-quality goods or property. The verse does not itself state that everything so described is also forbidden in ownership or trade.

A commodity’s poor quality and its legal prohibition are distinct matters.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:2 also prohibits replacing good with bad in the context of orphans’ property. More than outward quality is at issue: wrongful appropriation and improper substitution belong to the context.

Āl ʿImrān 3:179 discusses distinguishing khabīth from ṭayyib in a context concerning believers. Al-Anfāl 8:37 also admits a reading concerning people and their fate, while the preceding verse’s discussion of expenditure has supported interpretations concerning property or conduct. The contrast should therefore neither be confined to food or soil nor treated as having identical referents without contextual examination.

Al-Māʾidah 5:100 states:

قُلْ لَا يَسْتَوِي الْخَبِيثُ وَالطَّيِّبُ وَلَوْ أَعْجَبَكَ كَثْرَةُ الْخَبِيثِ فَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ يَا أُولِي الْأَلْبَابِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ

qul lā yastawī al-khabīthu wa-al-ṭayyibu wa-law aʿjabaka kathratu al-khabīthi fa-ittaqū allāha yā ulī al-albābi laʿallakum tufliḥūna

“Say: Bad and good are not equal, even if the abundance of the bad impresses you. So be mindful of The God, people of understanding, so that you may prosper.” (Al-Māʾidah 5:100)

The abundance of khabīth does not change its worth. Popularity, quantity, or outward appeal alone is therefore not a criterion of being ṭayyib.

The verse does not enumerate every referent of khabīth. The value contrast is clear, but identifying its application requires grounds and context.

Is everything ṭayyib necessarily lawful?

Al-Nisāʾ states:

فَبِظُلْمٍ مِنَ الَّذِينَ هَادُوا حَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ طَيِّبَاتٍ أُحِلَّتْ لَهُمْ وَبِصَدِّهِمْ عَنْ سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ كَثِيرًا

fa-bi-ẓulmin mina alladhīna hādū ḥarramnā ʿalayhim ṭayyibātin uḥillat lahum wa-bi-ṣaddihim ʿan sabīli allāhi kathīran

“Because of wrongdoing by those who became Jews, We forbade them good things previously made lawful for them, and because they greatly obstructed the way of The God.” (Al-Nisāʾ 4:160)

This verse makes the relationship between ṭayyib and lawful particularly clear. The God speaks of things that were ṭayyib and had been lawful, but were subsequently forbidden to that group because of their conduct.

Ṭayyib remains a description despite a change in the ruling. If it simply meant lawful, forbidding ṭayyibāt would have a different significance.

Al-Māʾidah also states:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تُحَرِّمُوا طَيِّبَاتِ مَا أَحَلَّ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ وَلَا تَعْتَدُوا إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ الْمُعْتَدِينَ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā tuḥarrimū ṭayyibāti mā aḥalla allāhu lakum wa-lā taʿtadū inna allāha lā yuḥibbu al-muʿtadīna

“You who have believed, do not forbid the good things The God has made lawful for you, and do not transgress; The God does not love transgressors.” (Al-Māʾidah 5:87)

The prohibition of self-imposed forbidding accompanies a prohibition of transgression. Calling something ṭayyib should not become a pretext for restricting it through a claim of divine prohibition when The God has made it lawful.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:157 also describes the Messenger as making good things lawful and impure things forbidden. The contrast matters to rulings, but the words alone do not supply an independent list of permitted and forbidden things without examination of their referents and other verses.

Personal taste, cultural habit, actual harm or benefit, purity, and divine judgment should be distinguished. A person’s dislike of a food does not itself establish that it is khabīth and divinely forbidden for everyone. Conversely, something’s pleasant taste or appeal to one person does not alone authorize its consumption.

Understanding actual benefit and harm and exercising care in consumption are serious questions. Establishing religious prohibition is a further claim requiring its own appropriate grounds.

6. A good life, pleasant dwellings, and ṭūbā

حَيَاةً طَيِّبَةً: a good and wholesome life

Al-Naḥl states:

مَنْ عَمِلَ صَالِحًا مِنْ ذَكَرٍ أَوْ أُنْثَىٰ وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ فَلَنُحْيِيَنَّهُ حَيَاةً طَيِّبَةً وَلَنَجْزِيَنَّهُمْ أَجْرَهُمْ بِأَحْسَنِ مَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

man ʿamila ṣāliḥan min dhakarin aw unthā wa-huwa muʾminun fa-la-nuḥyiyannahu ḥayātan ṭayyibatan wa-la-najziyannahum ajrahum bi-aḥsani mā kānū yaʿmalūna

“Whoever does righteous work, male or female, while believing, We will surely bring them to a good, wholesome life, and We will reward them according to the best of what they did.” (Al-Naḥl 16:97)

Ḥayātan ṭayyibah describes life’s kind and quality. It can involve purity, goodness, peace, benefit, and a well-ordered life.

It should not be confined without evidence to one psychological condition. A good life is not necessarily only inward contentment, nor does it necessarily mean great wealth or freedom from all suffering.

The Qur’an discusses good provision, life’s adornment, security, and worldly benefits. It also recognizes trials, harm, and difficulties that righteous people may face.

The lexical meaning remains a good, wholesome, desirable life. Determining every dimension of the promise requires a broader study of life, belief, righteous action, and divine patterns.

Al-Tawbah 9:72 and Al-Ṣaff 61:12 also describe مَسَاكِنَ طَيِّبَةً in gardens of lasting residence. Ṭayyib describes desirable, pleasant, fitting dwellings, rather than mere cleanliness of buildings.

طُوبَىٰ: happiness be theirs

Al-Raʿd states:

الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ طُوبَىٰ لَهُمْ وَحُسْنُ مَآبٍ

alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāti ṭūbā lahum wa-ḥusnu maʾābin

“Those who have believed and done righteous deeds: happiness be theirs, and a good return.” (Al-Raʿd 13:29)

Ṭūbā belongs to the Ṭ Y B family and expresses happiness, blessedness, goodness, or a desirable outcome. Its structure is related to the feminine pattern fuʿlā, with the change from yāʾ to wāw explained through Arabic morphology.

Exegetical sources variously explain ṭūbā as good and happiness or as the name of a tree in the Garden. Yet ṭūbā lahum itself has a clear Arabic sense: happiness or goodness be theirs.

The accompanying ḥusnu maʾāb, “a good return,” reinforces that positive meaning.

“Happiness be theirs” or “blessedness is theirs” is therefore more suitable for the base translation than restricting the word to the name of a tree without independent linguistic grounds.

7. Neighboring roots and translation proposals

Ṭ Y B (طَيِّب) compared with KH B TH (خَبُثَ)

Study of KH B TH

Khabīth is ṭayyib’s principal opposite in the Qur’an. Their contrast appears in land, property, people, and moral judgment.

Ṭayyib concerns a good, pure, wholesome, or desirable quality; khabīth an undesirable, impure, corrupt, or unworthy quality.

This does not mean every khabīth referent is physically unclean or every ṭayyib one fragrant. Poor-quality property in Al-Baqarah 2:267 and land unsuitable for growth in Al-Aʿrāf 7:58 can be described through KH B TH.

Ṭ Y B (طَيِّب) compared with Ṭ H R (طَهُرَ)

Study of Ṭ H R

Ṭ H R more directly concerns being or becoming clean from impurity, pollution, or a condition requiring purification. Ṭayyib additionally embraces pleasantness, wholesomeness, desirability, and fitting goodness.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:4 expresses willingness through Ṭ Y B, and Yūnus 10:22 describes a favorable wind as ṭayyib. Purification alone does not replace those meanings.

In some contexts, including ṣaʿīdan ṭayyiban, cleanliness and suitability for the prescribed act bring the fields closer together.

Ṭ Y B (طَيِّب) compared with Z K W (زَكَا)

Study of Z K W

Z K W concerns purity, growth, increase, and goodness within its field of use. Ṭayyib more directly describes a thing’s good and desirable quality.

The roots can approach each other when describing people or property. Z K W does not necessarily imply sensory pleasantness, and ṭayyib does not necessarily imply growth or increase.

Ṭ Y B (طَيِّب) compared with Ḥ S N (حَسُنَ) and KH Y R (خَيْر)

Study of Ḥ S N; Study of KH Y R

Ḥ S N emphasizes goodness, beauty, worthiness, and commendable quality. Khayr has a broad range of goodness, benefit, superiority, and what is preferable.

By comparison, ṭayyib leans toward purity, wholesomeness, pleasantness, and freedom from khubth. This is not an absolute boundary: the three families can have very close meanings in some uses.

A ṭayyib life and a ḥasan life may both be rendered “a good life” in English, while the precise choice should remain faithful to context and the actual Qur’anic word.

Translation proposals for Forqan

Depending on construction, ṭāba can mean “was pleasant,” “was agreeable,” “was suitable,” or “was willing.” In Al-Nisāʾ 4:4, the verb with nafsan is best expressed through willing consent. In Al-Zumar 39:73, being pure and worthy is prominent.

Ṭayyib can be rendered pure, clean, pleasant, wholesome, good, agreeable, or suitable. No single equivalent fits every verse. Fertility and suitability matter for al-balad al-ṭayyib; favorability for rīḥ ṭayyibah; goodness of speech for al-kalim al-ṭayyib; and clean, suitable earth for ṣaʿīdan ṭayyiban.

In provision contexts, ṭayyibāt may be “wholesome provisions,” “good blessings,” or “good and pure things.” “Your pleasures and benefits” also suits Al-Aḥqāf 46:20. In Al-Nūr 24:26, translation requires a judgment about the word’s referents.

“A good, wholesome life” suits ḥayātan ṭayyibah without trying to incorporate every dimension of the promise into the short translation.

Ṭūbā lahum can naturally be rendered “happiness be theirs.”

Conclusion

Arabic Ṭ Y B concerns a good and desirable quality that, depending on its subject, can involve purity, wholesomeness, pleasantness, suitability, willingness, or worthiness. Its Qur’anic field extends from nature and food to speech, people, conduct, and life.

The principal contrast is with khabīth, but that contrast varies by context: fertile versus unproductive land, good versus poor-quality property, and pure versus unworthy people. None licenses imposing one narrow meaning, such as hygienic cleanliness or religious prohibition, on the whole family.

Nor are ṭayyib and lawful complete lexical synonyms. The Qur’an places them together and even describes ṭayyib things forbidden to a particular group in a specific situation. Goodness and desirability describe something’s quality; lawful and forbidden concern its ruling. Their relationship requires examination in each verse’s context.

Forqan’s translation should follow the subject described. The same word may require clean, fertile, favorable, wholesome, worthy, or agreeable in different verses. What remains is the positive, desirable orientation, rather than one fixed physical feature or legal ruling.

Thus Ṭ Y B can be understood as a family of purity, pleasantness, wholesomeness, and fitting goodness, with each derivative’s precise meaning determined by its noun, sentence structure, and Qur’anic context.
