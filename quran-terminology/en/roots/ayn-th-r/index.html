Principal meanings: stumbling and having normal movement disrupted by an obstacle; in another branch, discovering something previously unknown or undisclosed.

1. The root in early Arabic

The three-consonant root ع ث ر has several established branches in Arabic. Two principal branches concern stumbling or falling, and discovering something hidden or unknown. Dictionaries also record a sense concerning dust rising from the ground.

In the first branch, عَثَرَ describes a person's or animal's foot encountering something, disrupting movement and balance. Falling may follow, but a stumble does not necessarily entail a complete fall.

For example, a walker may strike a stone with a foot and lose balance, yet recover before falling. This also falls within the semantic field of عَثَرَ.

In the second branch, عَثَرَ عَلَى الشَّيْءِ means becoming aware of something previously hidden, unknown, or unnoticed.

Someone examining accounts might discover an error previously unknown to anyone. Arabic can express this discovery with عَثَرَ عَلَى.

This use does not require physical stumbling or falling. What occurs is recognition of something not previously apparent to the person.

An illustrative connection is possible: a moving person unexpectedly encounters an unnoticed obstacle, just as an investigation may bring someone upon a previously unknown fact.

That image alone does not establish that the discovery sense historically developed from physical stumbling.

Some early dictionaries discuss discovery and stumbling under one semantic principle and rising dust under another. Understanding the family therefore requires examining attested uses rather than forcing every meaning into one image.

A witness from pre-Islamic poetry

A poem attributed to the pre-Islamic poet al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī says:

لَكَ الْخَيْرُ إِنْ وَارَتْ بِكَ الْأَرْضُ وَاحِدًا

laka al-khayru in wārat bika al-arḍu wāḥidan

وَأَصْبَحَ جَدُّ النَّاسِ يَظْلَعُ عَاثِرًا

wa-aṣbaḥa jaddu al-nāsi yaẓlaʿu ʿāthirā

Translation: May good be yours! If the earth conceals you, a man without equal, people's fortune will come to limp and stumble.

جَدّ here means fortune or a person's lot.

يَظْلَعُ belongs to ظ ل ع and concerns limping.

عَاثِرًا belongs to ع ث ر and describes someone stumbling or losing balance while moving.

The poet uses the image of a limping, stumbling person's walk to describe people's loss of good fortune.

Fortune is not being assigned literal feet or limbs. The loss of the praised person would make people's circumstances so difficult that their fortune could no longer move steadily.

This witness shows a figurative use of stumbling to describe adversity and disruption in life in poetry attributed to a pre-Islamic poet.

The verse is transmitted in al-Nābighah's collected poetry and lexical sources. Its attribution is established in that tradition, although its precise date of composition is not independently known.

It provides historical support for stumbling and its figurative use, but does not by itself establish a similarly extensive pre-Islamic poetic use of the discovery sense.

Discovery in early Arabic

Dictionaries record عَثَرَ عَلَى الْأَمْرِ for discovering a matter not previously apparent.

أَعْثَرَهُ عَلَيْهِ is also recorded for making someone aware of it.

The Quran itself uses this sense, establishing its presence in the language of revelation.

Historically, however, attestation in the Quran and attestation in pre-Islamic poetry are distinct matters.

No independent, securely dated pre-Quranic witness for the discovery sense has been established in this article. This limitation does not prove that no such witness exists, nor establish when the sense arose or its sequence relative to the other branch.

Nevertheless, its meaning in the Quranic passages is clear and merits separate treatment within the root family.

2. The lexical family and extensions of meaning

Recorded forms include عَثَرَ, يَعْثُرُ, يَعْثِرُ, عَثْر, عِثَار, عُثُور, عَثْرَة, عَاثِر, تَعَثَّرَ, أَعْثَرَ, عَاثُور, عَثَّرَ, عِثْيَر, and عَثَرِيّ.

عَثَرَ: stumbling and losing balance

In physical movement, عَثَرَ concerns stumbling or a foot striking an obstacle.

In عَثَرَ الرَّجُلُ فِي ثَوْبِهِ, a man catches his foot in his clothing while walking and loses balance.

In عَثَرَ الْفَرَسُ, a horse stumbles or catches a foot on an obstacle while moving.

Dictionaries record the imperfect forms يَعْثُرُ and يَعْثِرُ.

عِثَار names stumbling, particularly the disruption of an animal's movement through a stumble.

عَثْرَة can mean a single stumble.

Here the salient feature is unintended disruption of movement through striking or becoming caught in something.

A fall may accompany a stumble, but a complete fall is not required in every use.

عَثْرَة: a stumble or error

Physically, عَثْرَة refers to a stumble.

Arabic also uses it for a person's error or mistake.

Just as someone can stumble while walking, a person can falter in speech, judgment, or conduct.

Someone might make a mistake in an important decision and revise it after recognizing its consequences.

Such an error can figuratively be called an عثرة.

The issue is no longer physical contact with a stone or obstacle, but a disruption of sound thought or action.

However, a lapse or mistake does not necessarily imply deliberate defiance or persistence in wrongdoing.

A person can err with good intentions; a person can also persist after recognizing an error.

The first situation concerns the lapse itself. The second adds a further behavior: persistence and refusal to correct it.

Ethical analysis should preserve this distinction.

تَعَثَّرَ: stumbling or faltering

تَعَثَّرَ is a Form V verb concerning stumbling or difficulty moving.

Physically, it can describe a person catching on obstacles or losing balance while walking. Repetition may be appropriate in context, but is not required in every use.

In speech, تَعَثَّرَ لِسَانُهُ describes interrupted fluency or a faltering tongue.

This illustrates the extension of stumbling from the feet to the flow of speech.

Arabic also uses the verb for an undertaking whose progress encounters obstacles or disruption.

For instance, implementation of a plan may falter because of insufficient resources or unforeseen difficulties.

No literal fall is meant: the work fails to proceed smoothly because it encounters an obstacle.

Such broader applications should not be projected into the original meaning of every Quranic derivative without historical evidence.

عَثَرَ عَلَى: discovering or becoming aware

In عَثَرَ عَلَى الشَّيْءِ, the verb means finding or discovering something.

For example, عَثَرَ عَلَى سِرٍّ means discovering a secret.

عَثَرَ عَلَى الْأَمْرِ means finding out about a fact or matter.

The important point is that something becomes apparent to a person who did not previously know it.

Sometimes awareness comes without a prior search: a person unexpectedly encounters a sign and learns of a hidden matter.

Yet accidental discovery is not a necessary component of this construction's meaning.

Discovery can also follow investigation, examination, and collection of evidence, and be expressed with عثر على.

“Found by chance” is therefore not an accurate universal rendering.

Suitable Persian equivalents convey “discovered,” “became aware,” or “found out.”

أَعْثَرَ: making another aware

أَعْثَرَ is a Form IV verb.

In أَعْثَرَهُ عَلَى الشَّيْءِ, the subject causes another person to become aware of something.

Its difference from عَثَرَ عَلَى lies in the roles of subject and object.

In عَثَرَ الرَّجُلُ عَلَى السِّرِّ, the man himself discovers the secret.

In أَعْثَرَهُ عَلَى السِّرِّ, a person or other cause makes him aware of it.

This distinction matters in Al-Kahf, where the Quran uses أَعْثَرْنَا.

In that Quranic use, The God is presented as causing the matter to become known to others.

Dictionaries also record أَعْثَرَ for causing someone to stumble. That use differs from أَعْثَرَهُ عَلَى in the sense of making someone aware.

Form IV alone therefore does not determine whether stumbling or awareness is intended; the complement and context are decisive.

عَاثُور: a stumbling place or concealed trap

عَاثُور can name a place where a person or animal may stumble or become trapped.

It is also recorded for a pit prepared to trap animals.

A pit might be dug in an animal's path so that it falls in without noticing it.

Figuratively, عاثور can denote adversity or a concealed trap into which a person unwittingly falls.

وَقَعَ فِي عَاثُورِ شَرٍّ concerns falling into trouble or a difficult predicament.

The connection with stumbling is clear: encountering a hidden or difficult obstacle disrupts movement and leads to entrapment.

Still, the person's stumble differs from the obstacle that causes it.

عَثْرَة can name the stumble itself; عَاثُور names the place or trapping factor.

عِثْيَر: rising dust

Dictionaries record عِثْيَر and عَثْيَر, among the forms used for dust or its traces. This word contains a consonantal yāʾ, not a long vowel as the spelling عَثِير would suggest.

This use concerns particles of earth lifted by movement or impact.

Expressions such as مَا رَأَيْتُ لَهُ أَثَرًا وَلَا عِثْيَرًا convey the absence of any trace of someone or something.

Here عثير concerns dust or a mark left by movement over the ground.

This does not make Quranic عُثِرَ mean that dust rose.

Dust belongs to another lexical branch, whose exact historical relation to discovery is not established here.

عَثَرِيّ: naturally watered crops

عَثَرِيّ describes palms or crops watered by rain, water gathered around their roots, or naturally flowing water, without water being lifted by irrigation equipment. Dictionaries differ in the details of the watering described.

Some explanations connect the term with channels or hollows that bring rainwater to cultivated land.

This agricultural use is attested, but shared consonants do not turn it into stumbling or discovery.

The word does not occur in the Quran.

3. Quranic occurrences

The root occurs twice in the Quran, in two verses and two morphological forms.

عُثِرَ in Al-Māʾidah 5:107 is a passive perfect Form I verb.

أَعْثَرْنَا in Al-Kahf 18:21 is an active perfect Form IV verb.

Both concern becoming aware of, or revealing, something previously unknown.

Forms such as عَثْرَة, عِثَار, عَاثِر, and تَعَثَّرَ do not occur in the Quran.

Although physical stumbling is a principal Arabic sense, neither Quranic occurrence directly describes falling or disrupted bodily movement.

A. عُثِرَ: misconduct in testimony is discovered

فَإِنْ عُثِرَ عَلَىٰ أَنَّهُمَا اسْتَحَقَّا إِثْمًا فَآخَرَانِ يَقُومَانِ مَقَامَهُمَا مِنَ الَّذِينَ اسْتَحَقَّ عَلَيْهِمُ الْأَوْلَيَانِ فَيُقْسِمَانِ بِاللَّهِ لَشَهَادَتُنَا أَحَقُّ مِنْ شَهَادَتِهِمَا وَمَا اعْتَدَيْنَا إِنَّا إِذًا لَمِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

fa-in ʿuthira ʿalā annahumā istaḥaqqā ithman fa-ākharāni yaqūmāni maqāmahumā mina alladhīna istaḥaqqa ʿalayhimu al-awlayāni fa-yuqsimāni bi-llāhi la-shahādatunā aḥaqqu min shahādatihimā wa-mā iʿtadaynā innā idhan la-mina al-ẓālimīn

Al-Māʾidah 5:107

If it is discovered that the two have incurred sin, let two others take their place from among those whose rights have been infringed—the two with the stronger claim—and swear by The God: “Our testimony is truer than theirs, and we have not transgressed; otherwise we would be among the wrongdoers.”

Al-Māʾidah 5:107

عُثِرَ here means that something became known or was discovered.

It is passive in the construction عُثِرَ عَلَىٰ أَنَّهُمَا اسْتَحَقَّا إِثْمًا.

What is discovered is sin incurred by the two people introduced in the testimony passage.

Neither physical stumbling nor the two witnesses falling is intended.

The verse concerns previously unknown misconduct regarding their testimony becoming known.

Testimony and bequests in context

Al-Māʾidah 5:106 discusses testimony when making a bequest as death approaches.

It introduces two witnesses and, under specified conditions, requires them to swear that they will not exchange testimony for gain or conceal it.

Al-Māʾidah 5:107 then addresses the possibility of their sin being discovered.

This sequence places عُثِرَ within examination of testimony and protection of people's rights.

For example, new information after testimony might reveal that the two witnesses failed to deal faithfully with the truth or infringed a right.

The example fits the context, but عُثِرَ does not identify how the misconduct is discovered.

The verse does not specify that discovery must come through confession, a document, a new witness, or examination of property.

The verb means that the matter becomes known; the root itself does not determine how that knowledge is obtained.

The role of عَلَىٰ

The construction عُثِرَ عَلَىٰ uses the preposition عَلَىٰ.

In Arabic, this construction expresses discovering a previously unknown matter.

Here the clause أَنَّهُمَا اسْتَحَقَّا إِثْمًا states what has been discovered.

The verb thus concerns discovering a fact, not bodily movement over something.

Relying exclusively on physical stumbling would prevent an accurate reading of the verse.

Does an accusation alone amount to عُثِرَ?

Within the sentence, عُثِرَ indicates that the matter has become known.

An allegation that the witnesses sinned is therefore distinct from discovery of their misconduct.

Someone dissatisfied with two witnesses might accuse them of lying, but the accusation alone does not establish that sin occurred.

The verse addresses a situation in which their misconduct has become known.

In this legal context, accusation, doubt, and discovery of misconduct must remain distinct. The verb alone does not set out a complete evidentiary procedure.

Nor does عُثِرَ specify whether the sin arose from deliberation, self-interest, or another cause. Those details depend on the misconduct and relevant evidence.

Discovering misconduct and correcting testimony

The verse continues with two others taking the first pair's place and swearing that their testimony is truer and that they have not transgressed.

Discovery is thus accompanied by a step to protect rights and correct the testimony process, rather than merely inform people of a past error.

This distinction matters.

An error can be revealed without any action to remedy its effects.

Alternatively, discovery can lead to a revised decision, restoration of a right, or correction of a faulty procedure.

Here discovery occurs within a provision for replacement witnesses and a further oath.

Al-Māʾidah 5:108 explains that this arrangement makes proper testimony more likely, or makes witnesses fear that other oaths will follow and rebut theirs.

The passage therefore connects establishing the truth with correcting testimony.

That conclusion comes from the verses together, not from the lexical meaning of عثر alone.

B. أَعْثَرْنَا: making the Companions of the Cave known

وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَعْثَرْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ لِيَعْلَمُوا أَنَّ وَعْدَ اللَّهِ حَقٌّ وَأَنَّ السَّاعَةَ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهَا إِذْ يَتَنَازَعُونَ بَيْنَهُمْ أَمْرَهُمْ فَقَالُوا ابْنُوا عَلَيْهِمْ بُنْيَانًا رَبُّهُمْ أَعْلَمُ بِهِمْ قَالَ الَّذِينَ غَلَبُوا عَلَىٰ أَمْرِهِمْ لَنَتَّخِذَنَّ عَلَيْهِمْ مَسْجِدًا

wa-kadhālika aʿtharnā ʿalayhim li-yaʿlamū anna waʿda allāhi ḥaqqun wa-anna al-sāʿata lā rayba fīhā idh yatanāzaʿūna baynahum amrahum fa-qālū ibnū ʿalayhim bunyānan rabbuhum aʿlamu bihim qāla alladhīna ghalabū ʿalā amrihim la-nattakhidhanna ʿalayhim masjidā

Al-Kahf 18:21

Thus We made others aware of them, so that they would know that the promise of The God is true and that there is no doubt about the Hour. As they disputed their affair among themselves, they said, “Build a structure over them; their Lord knows them best.” Those who prevailed over their affair said, “We shall surely establish a place of worship over them.”

Al-Kahf 18:21

أَعْثَرْنَا is Form IV: “We made aware,” or “We caused others to become aware of them.”

The subject is The God.

نَا refers to The God, while عَلَيْهِمْ refers to the young people whose story precedes this verse.

The morphological difference from Al-Māʾidah 5:107 is clear.

In Al-Māʾidah, passive عُثِرَ concerns misconduct being discovered.

In Al-Kahf, أَعْثَرْنَا is active and causative: The God causes others to become aware of the youths.

Who became aware?

The people made aware are not expressly named as a separate object in أَعْثَرْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ.

The construction can be explained as The God making others aware of them.

The verb alone does not identify everyone who first discovered the youths.

The verse continues with people's disagreement about them and decisions concerning a structure or place of worship.

Discovery of the youths had evidently become a matter of public concern, but the verse does not identify the first discoverer or explain how the news spread.

This helps define the limits of the root's meaning.

أَعْثَرْنَا expresses making others aware, not the precise method, the discoverers' names, or every historical detail.

The youths' concern to remain concealed

In Al-Kahf 18:19, after being awakened, the youths discuss how long they have remained.

They send one of their number to the city for food, instructing him to act carefully and discreetly and not reveal them to anyone.

In Al-Kahf 18:20, they fear that if others gain access to them, those people will stone them or force them back into their religion.

These verses show that concealing their identity and location mattered to them.

Al-Kahf 18:21, however, reports The God making them known to others.

Their wish to remain concealed differs from the subsequent discovery.

The story gives this contrast meaning by presenting a purpose beyond their initial concern.

The word أَعْثَرْنَا does not establish that they were identified through a chance incident or a particular mistake.

The verse does not explain the method of discovery.

The purpose of making others aware

The verb is followed by لِيَعْلَمُوا أَنَّ وَعْدَ اللَّهِ حَقٌّ وَأَنَّ السَّاعَةَ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهَا.

لِيَعْلَمُوا belongs to ع ل م and concerns knowing.

The clause states the purpose of making others aware of the youths.

Discovery of them is to enable recognition that the divine promise is true and that the Hour is beyond doubt.

The verse thus presents two levels of awareness.

First, discovering the youths and their condition, expressed by أَعْثَرْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ.

Second, knowing the truth of the divine promise and the Hour, expressed by لِيَعْلَمُوا.

The two levels are connected but not identical.

Someone can learn of an event without reflecting on its importance or message.

Here discovery of the youths provides an occasion for recognizing a broader truth.

Awakening after sleep

Al-Kahf 18:11 describes the youths remaining in the cave for a long period.

Al-Kahf 18:12 describes their awakening in connection with determining which of the two parties better reckoned the length of their stay.

Al-Kahf 18:19 also recounts their awakening and discussion of how long they stayed.

Then Al-Kahf 18:21 connects others' discovery of them with the truth of the divine promise and the Hour.

The sequence makes their discovery part of a Quranic narrative pointing to resurrection; it does not require reading their preceding sleep as death.

أَعْثَرْنَا itself does not mean reviving or awakening.

Awakening is expressed by بَعَثْنَاهُمْ, while making others aware is expressed by أَعْثَرْنَا.

Translation and analysis should preserve this difference.

Discovery and people's disagreement

Al-Kahf 18:21 continues with people disputing the youths' affair.

Some propose a structure over them, while others speak of establishing a place of worship.

These statements report people's reactions to the event.

أَعْثَرْنَا does not establish that all their subsequent decisions were divine commands.

The God describes causing people's awareness; proposals concerning buildings or worship are attributed to human speakers.

Thus making people aware of a truth does not amount to endorsing everything they subsequently do.

Al-Kahf 18:22 discusses differing claims about the youths' number, attributes the best knowledge of their number to their Lord, and says that only a few know it.

Learning of their existence and story therefore need not mean knowing every detail of their lives or their number.

Knowledge can be real yet limited in scope.

4. Comparing the two Quranic uses

Both occurrences belong to the same semantic branch but differ in construction and agency.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:107, عُثِرَ means “it was discovered.” The passive does not name the discoverer or cause of discovery.

In Al-Kahf 18:21, أَعْثَرْنَا means “We made aware.” The active Form IV construction presents The God as causing awareness.

The first concerns misconduct in testimony.

The second concerns young people who had remained concealed.

In both, something previously unknown becomes known.

What is discovered and the consequences of discovery differ.

In Al-Māʾidah, new knowledge can correct testimony and protect people's rights.

In Al-Kahf, discovery of the youths relates to recognizing the truth of the divine promise and the Hour.

Their shared meaning can therefore be described as:

Something becoming apparent to people who were previously unaware of it.

This should not be narrowed into a claim that عثر always concerns discovering sin or something objectionable.

In Al-Kahf, the discovery concerns the youths and their story, not their sin or error.

Nor should either discovery be assumed necessarily accidental or uninvestigated.

The root itself does not specify how awareness is obtained.

5. Comparison with nearby roots

ع ث ر and ز ل ل: stumbling and slipping

ز ل ل concerns slipping or sliding out of position.

Physically, a slip may result from a smooth or unstable surface.

A familiar use of عثر can instead emphasize a foot striking an obstacle and interrupting movement.

Someone sliding on a slippery surface provides a clear illustration of زلل.

Someone catching a foot on a stone and losing balance illustrates عثر.

The distinction is not absolute: both families can express physical slips and errors.

Al-Baqarah 2:209 uses زَلَلْتُمْ for slipping after clear proofs have come.

The Quran, however, uses ع ث ر for discovering a hidden fact.

The families' overlap in stumbling does not justify transferring the discovery branch of ع ث ر to ز ل ل without evidence.

ع ث ر and ك ب ب: stumbling and overturning

ك ب ب concerns falling or casting down, especially face first.

Al-Naml 27:90 describes people cast down on their faces.

Physical عَثَرَ can lead to a fall, but a complete fall is not always entailed.

A person may stumble and recover balance.

كَبَّ in its falling sense emphasizes the overturning or casting down itself.

The semantic fields overlap without being identical.

ع ث ر and و ج د: discovering and finding

و ج د concerns finding, possessing, or perceiving something.

وَجَدَ الشَّيْءَ can mean that a person found something.

In its discovery use, عَثَرَ عَلَى الشَّيْءِ more particularly concerns learning of something previously hidden or unknown.

Someone might find an object they had put somewhere earlier; وجد can express this.

Discovering concealed misconduct while examining documents is a suitable context for عثر على.

Both can nevertheless describe finding something in some contexts; the distinction is not absolute.

For this reason, “was discovered” suits Al-Māʾidah 5:107 better than a bare “was found”: the subject is the witnesses' misconduct becoming known.

ع ث ر and ع ل م: making aware and knowing

ع ل م concerns knowing or knowledge.

عَلِمَ can describe a person's possession of knowledge.

أَعْثَرَ عَلَى in the sense under discussion describes causing awareness of something not previously known.

Both families occur in Al-Kahf 18:21.

أَعْثَرْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ concerns the youths becoming known to others.

لِيَعْلَمُوا concerns knowledge that the divine promise and the Hour are true.

The first identifies the event of making aware; the second states the knowledge intended through it.

Learning of a matter and understanding its significance or consequences may be connected, but are not identical.

ع ث ر and ك ش ف: discovery and removing a covering

ك ش ف concerns removing a covering and revealing something.

Physically, a curtain might be drawn aside, exposing what was behind it.

Other uses concern removing hardship or revealing a truth.

عثر على also concerns discovering something previously hidden.

كشف emphasizes the image of a covering removed; عثر in this branch emphasizes learning of an unknown matter.

Someone uncovering a document might thereby discover misconduct; the two concepts can occur in one event.

The roots كشف and عثر nonetheless remain distinct.

ع ث ر and ظ ه ر: becoming apparent and becoming aware

Some uses of ظ ه ر concern appearing or becoming manifest.

In Al-Kahf 18:20, the youths fear that others gaining access to them will harm them or force them back into their religion.

In Al-Kahf 18:21, The God uses أَعْثَرْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ.

Both expressions relate in context to identifying or gaining access to the youths, but their constructions and semantic contributions differ.

يَظْهَرُوا عَلَيْكُمْ concerns others gaining access to or mastery over the youths.

أَعْثَرْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ concerns causing people to become aware of them.

Shared reference to identifying them does not make the verbs complete synonyms.

ع ث ر and أ ث ر: independent roots

أ ث ر concerns a trace, sign, remnant, or what something leaves behind.

ع ث ر concerns stumbling and, in the Quranic branch, discovering an unknown matter.

One can say in Persian that finding traces and signs led to discovering a truth.

That conceptual connection does not merge the Arabic roots.

أَثَر begins with hamzah; عَثَر begins with ʿayn.

آثار, أثر, and other derivatives of أ ث ر therefore do not belong to ع ث ر.

Nor is lexical عِثْيَر, dust, identical with أَثَر, although some Arabic expressions pair them to convey the absence of any trace.

6. Morphological and semantic observations

عَثَرَ and عُثِرَ

عَثَرَ is the active perfect Form I verb.

In the discovery sense, its subject is the person who learns of a matter.

عُثِرَ is the passive form of the same verb.

Al-Māʾidah 5:107 uses the passive and does not name the discoverer.

Translation should preserve that distinction.

“They learned that the two had incurred sin” introduces a plural subject.

“If it is discovered that the two have incurred sin” better preserves the passive construction.

عُثِرَ and أَعْثَرْنَا

عُثِرَ is passive perfect Form I.

أَعْثَرْنَا is active perfect Form IV, first person plural.

In the latter, the subject causes someone else to gain awareness.

The construction of Al-Kahf 18:21 therefore explicitly attributes the action to The God.

Form IV alone does not specify how that awareness is brought about.

An event, sign, or encounter with a fact could make people aware; identifying the details requires independent evidence.

The role of عَلَىٰ

Both Quranic occurrences use عَلَىٰ.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:107:

عُثِرَ عَلَىٰ أَنَّهُمَا اسْتَحَقَّا إِثْمًا

ʿuthira ʿalā annahumā istaḥaqqā ithmā

Al-Māʾidah 5:107

In Al-Kahf 18:21:

أَعْثَرْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ

aʿtharnā ʿalayhim

Al-Kahf 18:21

In both constructions, عَلَىٰ introduces what becomes known.

The first has a clause about the misconduct of two people.

The second concerns the youths themselves.

The complement may therefore concern a fact, a person, or a group.

عَلَىٰ here does not mean physical position above something.

The discovery construction expresses finding out or becoming aware.

Is discovery with عثر always accidental?

Physical stumbling is usually unintended.

That can suggest that عثر على must also always refer to finding by chance.

In the discovery use, however, chance is not a necessary inference from the word.

Someone may learn of a matter through investigation.

A truth may also become apparent without a prior search.

Both situations can involve discovery.

“By chance” should therefore not be added to Quranic عثر as part of its fixed meaning.

Al-Kahf 18:21 attributes making others aware to The God. This does not identify the discoverers' human method: neither chance from their perspective nor deliberate investigation is established by the verb alone.

Is discovering truth always welcome?

The discovery branch of ع ث ر does not inherently carry a positive or negative moral judgment.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:107, what is discovered is misconduct and sin.

In Al-Kahf 18:21, it is the youths and an event connected with knowing that the divine promise is true.

Discovery may concern something objectionable or a beneficial truth.

The verb does not entail that its object is a sin, shameful secret, or unwelcome incident.

The value of what is discovered comes from its object and context, not the root ع ث ر itself.

A lapse and persistence in error

In the stumbling branch, someone may unintentionally lose balance.

Figuratively, a person can also err in speech or conduct.

Error and persistence in error are distinct situations.

Someone may recognize a mistake and correct it.

Another may persist out of pride or self-interest despite the truth becoming clear.

Such deliberate persistence adds a behavior beyond the original lapse.

عثرة alone cannot determine intent, awareness, or ultimate responsibility.

This distinction also fits the Quranic use: in Al-Māʾidah 5:107, discovery of misconduct differs from the nature of the sin and its legal consequences.

7. Proposed renderings for Forqan

Translation should distinguish bodily movement from discovery.

عَثَرَ, physically: stumbled; caught a foot on an obstacle; lost balance.

عِثَار: stumbling or disrupted movement, particularly in walking or an animal's movement.

عَثْرَة: a stumble; figuratively, a lapse or mistake.

عَاثِر: stumbling; a person who has lost balance while moving.

تَعَثَّرَ: stumbled or moved with difficulty; more broadly, faltered in speech or an undertaking.

عَثَرَ عَلَى: discovered, became aware, or found something previously unknown.

أَعْثَرَ عَلَى: made another aware of a matter; caused something to become known to that person.

عَاثُور: a stumbling place, pit, or concealed trap; figuratively, a predicament or trap.

عِثْيَر: rising dust.

عَثَرِيّ: palms or crops watered by rain or natural water without water-lifting equipment.

Al-Māʾidah 5:107

For فَإِنْ عُثِرَ عَلَىٰ أَنَّهُمَا اسْتَحَقَّا إِثْمًا:

“If it is discovered that the two have incurred sin.”

This preserves both discovery and the passive voice.

In Persian, an equivalent of “if it becomes known” is more natural than some expressions for “if it is discovered,” while making no claim about the method.

Al-Kahf 18:21

For وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَعْثَرْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ:

“Thus We made others aware of them.”

This preserves the causative meaning of Form IV.

“Thus We caused others to discover them” also conveys the meaning, though the first is shorter and closely follows the construction.

Brief meanings for word boxes

Brief root meaning: stumbling or encountering an obstacle; in its discovery branch, learning of something hidden or unknown.

عُثِرَ — Al-Māʾidah 5:107

Context: it was discovered or became known; discovery of the two witnesses' misconduct.

أَعْثَرْنَا — Al-Kahf 18:21

Context: We made aware; We caused others to discover the youths of the cave.

8. Conclusion

ع ث ر has two principal Arabic branches: stumbling and disrupted movement, and discovering something previously hidden or unknown.

Physically, عَثَرَ concerns stumbling and losing balance through encountering an obstacle. A fall may follow, but is not required in every use.

Figurative applications concern error, mistakes, and encountering difficulties.

The verse attributed to al-Nābighah pairs عَاثِرًا with يَظْلَعُ, using limping and stumbling to depict people's loss of fortune. It witnesses the root's early use for stumbling and adversity.

In its discovery branch, عَثَرَ عَلَى means learning of something unknown, and أَعْثَرَ عَلَى means making someone else aware of it.

An illustrative relationship between the branches is possible, but their historical order and exact development are not established by the evidence examined here.

Dictionaries also record stumbling places, concealed traps, dust, and naturally watered crops. Each belongs in its own context and should not be transferred into Quranic uses without evidence.

Both of the Quran's two occurrences belong to the discovery branch.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:107, عُثِرَ concerns discovery of two witnesses' misconduct, within protection of rights and correction of testimony.

In Al-Kahf 18:21, أَعْثَرْنَا concerns making others aware of the youths of the cave, in relation to recognizing the truth of the divine promise and the Hour.

Their morphology matters: passive عُثِرَ does not name the discoverer, while active Form IV أَعْثَرْنَا attributes causing awareness to The God.

Neither verse uses the root for falling, physical stumbling, or making a mistake.

The verb alone does not determine the method of discovery, whether it was accidental, or what kind of matter is revealed. Construction and context supply those details.

Final definition: physically, ع ث ر concerns stumbling and disrupted movement through an obstacle; figuratively, it can describe a lapse or predicament. In the discovery branch, عَثَرَ عَلَى means learning of something previously unknown, and أَعْثَرَ عَلَى means making another aware of it. Quranic عُثِرَ and أَعْثَرْنَا both concern something becoming known or being made known, not physical stumbling.
