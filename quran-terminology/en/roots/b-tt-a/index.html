Arabic B Ṭ ʾ denotes slow movement, late arrival, or an undertaking failing to progress at the expected speed. Slowness may be a natural feature of movement, arise from external circumstances, or result from deliberate human hesitation. In certain verbal constructions, the meaning also extends beyond the subject’s own slowness to slowing other people’s movement or action.

The root occurs only once in the Qur’an, in Al-Nisāʾ 4:72 as لَيُبَطِّئَنَّ. Its context concerns people who hold back from accompanying others when the community faces danger. Their response to others’ misfortune or success reveals the character of their conduct.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: the root’s sole occurrence

Three things must be distinguished: slowness as a condition, delaying an expected action, and holding others back from that action. These meanings are related but not necessarily identical in every sentence.

1. The semantic core and word family

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris identifies one principle for B Ṭ ʾ, calling it الْبُطْءُ فِي الْأَمْرِ: slowness in an undertaking or its performance. Other early lexicons likewise contrast بُطْء with إِسْرَاع, speeding up.

Ibn Fāris, Maqāyīs al-Lughah: B Ṭ ʾ

The family’s central meaning is therefore a movement or undertaking progressing slowly in relation to speed. This may be measured by time, as when someone arrives later than expected, or through the course of movement or action, as with a slow-moving horse or hesitation in fulfilling a responsibility.

Important forms include:

بَطُؤَ: to be slow or progress slowly. بَطُؤَ فِي مَشْيِهِ describes walking slowly. The verb alone does not judge the person’s motive.

بُطْء: slowness or delay; the condition of movement or action proceeding at a lower speed.

بَطِيء: slow, slow-moving, or late, according to the noun described and its context. The adjective may describe a person, animal, movement, or process.

أَبْطَأَ: to be late or proceed slowly. Familiar uses concern late arrival or falling behind an expected time. In some constructions it also means delaying something.

تَبَاطَأَ: to proceed slowly or hesitate in acting. It commonly focuses on the subject’s own conduct, but context must establish whether this is deliberate; the verbal pattern alone does not prove intention.

بَطَّأَ: to slow down or delay. This form is especially important: Arabic records it both for the subject’s own slowness or falling behind and for slowing or holding back another person.

اسْتَبْطَأَ: to consider someone or something slow or overdue. Someone awaits an action, news, or a person’s arrival and judges it late. The verb thus incorporates an assessment of speed or timing.

The family extends from describing the speed of movement to human conduct in fulfilling responsibilities. Yet a verbal pattern alone cannot establish deceit, fear, or moral weakness. Circumstances and sentence clues supply those features.

2. Early Arabic usage and poetic witnesses

One clear poetic witness comes from Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā, praising Harim ibn Sinān:

فَضْلَ الْجَوَادِ عَلَى الْخَيْلِ الْبِطَاءِ فَلَا
يُعْطِي بِذَلِكَ مَمْنُونًا وَلَا نَزِقَا

faḍla al-jawādi ʿalā al-khayli al-biṭāʾi fa-lā / yuʿṭī bi-dhālika mamnūnan wa-lā naziqā

Translation, following the explanation of mamnūn as diminished or cut short: “[His superiority is] like that of a swift horse over slow horses; his giving is neither diminished and cut short nor rash.”

Al-Durr al-Maṣūn: mamnūn in the Zuhayr witness

الْبِطَاءِ is the plural of بَطِيء, describing slow horses. The poet uses differences in horses’ speed and ability to move as an image of the praised man’s superiority.

The witness places the root in a concrete, ordinary feature of Arab life: horses in motion. Slowness primarily describes movement here, rather than naming a sin or religious trait.

A verbal form of the family also occurs in Labīd ibn Rabīʿah’s poetry:

وَهُمُ الْعَشِيرَةُ أَنْ يُبَطِّئَ حَاسِدٌ
أَوْ أَنْ يَلُومَ مَعَ الْعِدَى لَوَّامُهَا

wa-humu al-ʿashīratu an yubaṭṭiʾa ḥāsidun / aw an yalūma maʿa al-ʿidā lawwāmuhā

Translation following Al-Ṭabrisī’s explanation: “They are [worthy and noble] kinsmen, beyond an envious person’s accusing them of slowness or a reproacher’s blaming them alongside their enemies.”

Al-Ṭabrisī, Majmaʿ al-Bayān: ascribing slowness

Commentators record disagreements about the construction, wording, and sense of يُبَطِّئَ in this verse. Al-Ṭabrisī explains it as ascribing slowness; Lisān al-ʿArab also transmits a different account from Ibn al-Aʿrābī. The translation above therefore follows one identified explanation, rather than claiming a definitive, exclusive meaning. The form’s presence in early poetry illustrates the family’s range of uses.

Sharḥ al-Qaṣāʾid al-ʿAshr: alternative explanations of Labīd’s verse

Lexicons also record بَطَّأَ فُلَانٌ بِفُلَانٍ, describing one person holding another back from something the latter intended to do.

Lisān al-ʿArab: slowing or holding another person back

Here the meaning extends beyond directly slow movement. Through speech, conduct, or influence, the first person may cause another to act slowly or postpone action.

The connection is clear: slowness may affect the subject or be caused by the subject in another person. The root’s presence alone does not determine which applies. The verbal construction, object, accompanying prepositions, and context must establish this.

3. The sole Qur’anic occurrence: Al-Nisāʾ 4:72

Qur’anic text:

وَإِنَّ مِنْكُمْ لَمَنْ لَيُبَطِّئَنَّ فَإِنْ أَصَابَتْكُمْ مُصِيبَةٌ قَالَ قَدْ أَنْعَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيَّ إِذْ لَمْ أَكُنْ مَعَهُمْ شَهِيدًا

wa-inna minkum la-man la-yubaṭṭiʾanna fa-in aṣābatkum muṣībatun qāla qad anʿama Allāhu ʿalayya idh lam akun maʿahum shahīdan

Translation:

Among you there is certainly someone who will surely hold back. If misfortune strikes you, he says, “The God has favored me, since I was not present with them.”

Al-Nisāʾ 4:72

The preceding command calls on believers to take precautions and move out to face danger in separate groups or all together. That command appears in Al-Nisāʾ 4:71.

Verse 72 therefore concerns more than ordinary slow walking or arriving late for a personal appointment. It describes hesitation when the community faces danger and participation and solidarity are expected.

In this setting, لَيُبَطِّئَنَّ extends beyond low physical speed. The person delays taking an action toward which the community has moved, or creates conditions that slow it.

The rest of the verse reveals a significant feature of his conduct: when misfortune befalls others, he considers his own absence from their company a blessing.

شَهِيدًا here means being present and witnessing the event. He is not saying that he was not killed or martyred; he says he was not there with them.

The distinction matters for understanding the conduct criticized. He has kept away from a communal action’s painful consequences and feels pleased about his absence when others suffer misfortune.

The next verse, Al-Nisāʾ 4:73, shows the other side. If favor and success reach the community, the same person wishes he had accompanied them and achieved a great gain.

Together, the verses clarify his behavior: he holds back in danger but wants a share in others’ success.

The ethical characterization comes from the whole context, rather than B Ṭ ʾ alone. The root supplies the semantic field of slowness and delay; the verses identify the undertaking and the person’s conduct.

Two possible senses of la-yubaṭṭiʾanna

لَيُبَطِّئَنَّ permits two related but distinct interpretations.

In the first, the verb concerns the person himself: he hesitates to move and accompany others, acts late, or stays behind.

In the second, he influences others: his speech or conduct slows them too or holds them back from participation.

Both interpretations appear in the lexical and grammatical study of this verse.

In the first, the subject is the person mentioned and slowness describes his conduct. This connects directly with the continuation, which says he was not present with the others.

In the second, the verb is transitive and its object is not explicitly stated. It may then concern slowing others’ movement or discouraging participation. Recorded Arabic uses of بَطَّأَ support this meaning too.

Form II alone does not prove that the second interpretation must be intended. Arabic also uses this verbal family for the person’s own delay and hesitation.

Conversely, the absence of an explicit object does not rule out the second interpretation: Arabic may omit a transitive verb’s object when context supplies it.

Neither the pattern alone nor the omitted object can therefore settle the interpretation. The continuation makes the person’s own holding back clearer, while influence over others remains a defensible possibility.

The base translation should preserve the person’s hesitant conduct, while explaining the possibility of holding others back separately. This avoids importing one interpretive possibility as the word’s exclusive, certain meaning.

Grammatical structure and emphasis

لَيُبَطِّئَنَّ consists of the imperfect verb يُبَطِّئُ, an emphatic lām, and the heavy emphatic nūn.

The verb is third-person masculine singular in Form II. The doubled ṭā belongs to that pattern; the final nūn supplies emphasis.

At the sentence’s beginning, إِنَّ and the lām in لَمَنْ add emphasis. A familiar grammatical analysis treats the lām in لَيُبَطِّئَنَّ as introducing the response to an implied oath.

Al-Durr al-Maṣūn: intransitive and transitive readings, and emphasis in Al-Nisāʾ 4:72

The resulting construction emphatically reports the presence of such a person among those addressed and the occurrence of his conduct.

The emphasis does not change the root’s lexical meaning. It strengthens the assertion about the conduct rather than making B Ṭ ʾ mean hypocrisy, treachery, or hostility.

مِنْكُمْ is another point: the person belongs to the community addressed. This social connection alone does not determine every member’s true faith, but shows that the criticized conduct arises within the group that heard the call to participate.

4. Related roots distinguished

B Ṭ ʾ and TH B Ṭ

Study of TH B Ṭ

Their semantic fields are close but not identical.

B Ṭ ʾ primarily concerns an undertaking or movement progressing slowly. A person may be slow himself or cause another’s slowness.

TH B Ṭ emphasizes holding back, weakening forward movement, or preventing action. Al-Tawbah 9:46 uses it for people whose departure is held back.

The central focus differs: B Ṭ ʾ foregrounds slowness and delay; TH B Ṭ foregrounds restraint or obstruction of progress.

Their transitive uses can nevertheless overlap. Holding someone back from an undertaking may in practice slow or stop him.

The distinction does not mean that B Ṭ ʾ can never describe holding others back or that TH B Ṭ always entails a complete, final stop. Construction and context determine the precise meaning.

B Ṭ ʾ and TH Q L

Study of TH Q L

TH Q L primarily concerns heaviness and weight. Some uses connect that weight with difficulty moving or reluctance to act.

Al-Tawbah 9:38 describes people who, when called to move out in The God’s way, cling heavily to the earth and fail to move.

Heaviness here concerns a response to a call to action, but its linguistic image differs from B Ṭ ʾ.

B Ṭ ʾ directly expresses slowness and delay; TH Q L reaches such a setting through heaviness and difficulty moving.

This should not become a categorical theory about everyone’s psychological state. The words may overlap in describing conduct, but their semantic paths differ.

B Ṭ ʾ and ʾ KH R

Study of ʾ KH R

ʾ KH R concerns being behind, coming afterward, or postponing. B Ṭ ʾ emphasizes slowness of movement or action.

Someone may arrive late because he moved slowly: both slowness and lateness occur. Yet not every delay results from slowness. Another person may decide to postpone an undertaking that, once begun, proceeds quickly.

Likewise, moving slowly does not always entail missing a set arrival time.

Translation must respect this: “being late,” “progressing slowly,” “staying behind,” and “postponing” are not interchangeable everywhere.

5. The Qur’anic meaning’s limits and ethical judgment

Slowness is not inherently a moral failing. Illness, disability, necessary caution, or external circumstances may make someone move slowly. Slow progress may also result from care and avoidance of inappropriate haste.

B Ṭ ʾ alone does not judge between these situations.

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:72, the criticism arises because the person’s slowness or holding back occurs within a communal responsibility, and the continuation reveals his attitude toward others’ fate.

The verse therefore does not establish that everyone who stays behind or does not participate in a communal undertaking has this condemned character. Al-Nisāʾ 4:95 itself distinguishes people with an impediment from others.

Nor should لَيُبَطِّئَنَّ be translated “being a hypocrite.” Hypocrisy, lying, or preferring personal advantage may be examined through behavioral clues and other verses, but they are not the verb’s direct meaning.

The verse contributes a clear social use of the root: slowness or hesitation can arise where communal action and shared responsibility are expected. When deliberate and accompanied by indifference to others’ suffering, it becomes an ethical issue rather than merely a matter of timing.

This conclusion combines the word’s meaning with its context; it does not redefine the word lexically.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

In general Arabic, بُطْء can be translated “slowness” or “delay.” For بَطِيء, “slow” or “slow-moving” fits; for أَبْطَأَ, depending on the sentence, “was late,” “arrived late,” or “fell behind.”

For تَبَاطَأَ, “progressed slowly” or “hesitated to act” is appropriate. Where the context establishes deliberate conduct, “delayed taking action” is more precise.

For transitive بَطَّأَ, “slowed,” “delayed,” or “held back from action” is possible when the construction and surrounding clues support that role.

For the sole Qur’anic form, لَيُبَطِّئَنَّ, the proposed base rendering is:

“Will surely hold back.”

This fits the person’s staying behind and absence. It explains his conduct without supplying an unstated object.

The explanation may add that the verb also permits slowing or holding others back. If grammatical analysis and contextual clues favor the transitive reading, “will surely induce others to delay” or “will surely slow others’ movement” would suit that interpretation.

The translation should not combine both possibilities into an established meaning by adding “he holds back himself and discourages others.” Two linguistic possibilities do not prove both occur simultaneously in the verse.

7. Conclusion

Arabic B Ṭ ʾ concerns slowness and delay in movement or action. Concrete uses include a horse’s movement; human uses include arriving late or hesitating to fulfill a responsibility.

Some constructions also have a causative meaning: someone may slow another or hold him back from progress. This connects with the core of slowness but must be established for each sentence.

The Qur’an’s sole form, لَيُبَطِّئَنَّ, occurs in Al-Nisāʾ 4:72. Its context makes the person’s own hesitation and holding back clearer, while influence on others’ movement remains lexically and grammatically defensible.

The root’s semantic core is slowness and delay in the progress of movement or an undertaking; causative constructions also express slowing or holding back another person.

The root alone does not determine the conduct’s ethical value. Here communal responsibility and the person’s response to others’ misfortune or success make his hesitation blameworthy.
