Linguistic Note

The core meaning of ز و ج (Z W J) is pairhood and being joined as counterparts. Zawj can refer to one of the two sides of a pair, and also to the relation of pairhood itself. Therefore, zawj does not necessarily mean “wife,” nor necessarily “husband.” A woman can be the zawj of a man, and a man can be the zawj of a woman, because both stand in a pair-relation.

A common mistake is to translate zawj everywhere as spouse, wife, or husband. In some verses, “spouse” is smooth and correct, but if that meaning is forced onto every use, the wider Qur’anic field disappears. The Qur’an uses zawj for humans, animals, plants, kinds of phenomena, counterparts in the next life, and even like-groups.

So each verse asks its own question: is zawj here marital relation, natural pair, plant kind, like-group, counterpart, companion, or the paired structure of creation?

Central Meaning

Z W J points to pair, counterpart, mate, complement, and something being placed in a dual or linked relation with another thing.

Material Image

The material image is two things known in relation to one another: two shoes, two hands, two wings, two sides of a phenomenon, male and female, or two complementary parts of one structure. Each may be seen alone, but its fuller meaning appears in relation to the other.

In this image, zawj is not merely the number two. Two things may stand next to each other without being a zawj. Pairhood means there is relation, proportion, nearness, complementarity, structural kinship, or shared grouping between the two. Z W J is close to “two,” but it is not identical to it.

Classical Language Note

In classical Arabic, zawj means pair and counterpart. It appears for humans and marriage, but also for animals, plants, tools, and different kinds of things. For that reason, zawj can be used for both man and woman, because each is the counterpart of the other.

In eloquent Arabic, zawj can be used for a woman without the feminine ending. This matches Qur’anic usage: the Qur’an says zawjuka and zawjahā; it does not restrict the word to woman or man alone. Qur’anic usage itself is enough to show that zawj should not be reduced to “wife” or “husband.”

Zawj in General Creation

One foundational verse for this root appears in 51:49: وَمِن كُلِّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقْنَا زَوْجَيْنِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ (wa-min kulli shay’in khalaqnā zawjayni laʿallakum tadhakkarūn).

Zawjayni here points to paired structure in creation. It should not be limited only to man and woman. The verse says min kulli shay’, from every thing, opening the field to a wider order of created things and phenomena.

Pairhood here can point to dualities of creation, complementarities, constructive contrasts, male and female, or linked kinds. The aim is not merely biological information. Laʿallakum tadhakkarūn shows that this paired creation is a sign for remembering and understanding the oneness of the Creator.

Zawj, Woman, and Man

In human relation, zawj can refer to spouse, but the root meaning is still pair and counterpart, not one specific sex. In 2:35, the Qur’an says: يَا آدَمُ اسْكُنْ أَنتَ وَزَوْجُكَ الْجَنَّةَ (yā Ādam uskun anta wa-zawjuka al-jannah).

Zawjuka may be translated smoothly as “your spouse,” but from the root angle it means your pair and counterpart. Translating it only as “your wife” narrows the root and weakens the sense of counterpart relation.

In 7:189, the Qur’an says: خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ وَجَعَلَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا لِيَسْكُنَ إِلَيْهَا (khalaqakum min nafsin wāḥidah wa-jaʿala minhā zawjahā li-yaskuna ilayhā). Zawjahā means the pair and counterpart of that soul. The verse shows tranquility, nearness, and relation.

Azwāj in Family Relation

In many verses, azwāj refers to spouses in family relation. In 64:14, the Qur’an says: إِنَّ مِنْ أَزْوَاجِكُمْ وَأَوْلَادِكُمْ عَدُوًّا لَّكُمْ فَاحْذَرُوهُمْ (inna min azwājikum wa-awlādikum ʿaduwwan lakum fa-iḥdharūhum).

Azwājikum is normally translated as “your spouses,” and that is natural. But the root remains pair and counterpart. The verse speaks about the closest family bonds, which in some situations can pull a person away from the right path.

Zawj in Plants and Growth

The Qur’an uses zawj for plants and kinds of growth. In 50:7, it says: وَأَنبَتْنَا فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍ بَهِيجٍ (wa-anbatnā fīhā min kulli zawjin bahīj).

Here zawj refers to a kind, type, or plant pair. It clearly does not mean spouse. In 26:7, the Qur’an says: كَمْ أَنبَتْنَا فِيهَا مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍ كَرِيمٍ (kam anbatnā fīhā min kulli zawjin karīm). Zawjin karīm can point to a noble kind, type, or category of growth.

Zawjayni in Human and Animal Life

In verses about male and female creation, zawjayni appears. In 53:45, the Qur’an says: وَأَنَّهُ خَلَقَ الزَّوْجَيْنِ الذَّكَرَ وَالْأُنثَىٰ (wa-annahu khalaqa al-zawjayni al-dhakara wa-al-unthā).

Here the verse itself explains that zawjayni, in this context, are male and female. This shows that zawjayni can refer to the two sexual sides of a living structure, but it does not mean that zawj always only means male and female.

In the story of Noah’s ark, 11:40 says: احْمِلْ فِيهَا مِن كُلٍّ زَوْجَيْنِ اثْنَيْنِ (iḥmil fīhā min kullin zawjayni ithnayn). Here zawjayni appears in the context of preserving animal kinds. A smooth translation may be “from every kind, a pair of two.”

Azwāj as Like-Groups

In the Qur’an, azwāj sometimes refers to like-groups and counterparts, not marital spouses. In 37:22, it says: احْشُرُوا الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا وَأَزْوَاجَهُمْ وَمَا كَانُوا يَعْبُدُونَ (uḥshurū alladhīna ẓalamū wa-azwājahum wa-mā kānū yaʿbudūn).

Azwājahum should not be rushed into “their spouses.” The context is the gathering of wrongdoers and those aligned with them. Azwāj here can refer to counterparts, companions, like-groups, or those of the same path and conduct. This use is important because it shows that zawj in the Qur’an is not only marital relation; it can also be relation of kind and counterpart.

Zuwwijat: Souls Joined with Like-Kinds

In Sūrat al-Takwīr, the Qur’an says: وَإِذَا النُّفُوسُ زُوِّجَتْ (wa-idhā al-nufūsu zuwwijat) in 81:7.

Zuwwijat does not mean “were married.” The verse speaks about souls, not human marriage contracts. The meaning is closer to being paired, joined, or grouped with their like-kinds. Translating it as “souls are given to their spouses” damages the verse. Better: souls are paired and joined, or souls are joined with their like-kinds.

Azwāj in the Next Life

Azwāj also appears in descriptions of paradise. In 2:25, the Qur’an says: وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا أَزْوَاجٌ مُّطَهَّرَةٌ وَهُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ (wa-lahum fīhā azwājun muṭahharah wa-hum fīhā khālidūn).

Azwājun muṭahharah is often translated “purified spouses.” That may work as smooth English, but from the root angle azwāj are purified counterparts and pairs. It should not be limited by earthly, gendered, or possessive images. The Qur’an speaks of purified counterparts in the paradisal realm, and the quality of that should be approached with restraint.

Zawwajnā: Pairing in Human Context

In Sūrat al-Aḥzāb, the form zawwajnākahā is used. In 33:37, the Qur’an says: فَلَمَّا قَضَىٰ زَيْدٌ مِّنْهَا وَطَرًا زَوَّجْنَاكَهَا (fa-lammā qaḍā Zaydun minhā waṭaran zawwajnākahā).

Here zawwajnākahā is in a marital context. Yet the root meaning remains pairing and making a counterpart-relation. The verse does not use nakaḥnāhā; it uses zawwajnākahā. That choice shows that the issue is not only a legal contract, but a structural pairing against a mistaken social custom.

The difference from nikāḥ matters. Nikāḥ is closer to the social-legal mechanism of marriage; zawjiyyah emphasizes becoming pair and counterpart.

Pairhood and Tranquility

One of the most subtle uses of this root is the link between pairhood and tranquility. In 30:21, the Qur’an says: وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ أَنْ خَلَقَ لَكُم مِّنْ أَنفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا لِّتَسْكُنُوا إِلَيْهَا وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَكُم مَّوَدَّةً وَرَحْمَةً (wa-min āyātihi an khalaqa lakum min anfusikum azwājan li-taskunū ilayhā wa-jaʿala baynakum mawaddatan wa-raḥmah).

Azwājan refers to the human relation of woman and man, but the center is not only legal marriage; it is tranquility, love, and mercy. Zawj here is a counterpart in relation to whom a person finds rest. This verse shows that Qur’anic pairhood, when speaking of human relation, should be understood through tranquility, love, and mercy, not ownership, domination, or reducing one side to the other.

Distinguishing Qur’anic Uses

In 51:49, zawjayni concerns the paired structure of creation. In 2:35 and 7:189, zawj refers to the human counterpart of Adam and the single soul. In 50:7 and 26:7, zawj refers to plant kinds.

In 53:45, zawjayni refers to male and female. In 11:40, zawjayni appears in preserving animal pairs. In 37:22, azwāj refers to the counterparts or like-kinds of wrongdoers. In 81:7, pairing souls concerns being joined with like-kinds.

In 2:25, purified azwāj appear in the paradisal realm. In 30:21, azwāj is joined to tranquility, love, and mercy. In 33:37, zawwajnā is in the context of marital pairing.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In اسْكُنْ أَنتَ وَزَوْجُكَ الْجَنَّةَ (uskun anta wa-zawjuka al-jannah), zawj is attached to Adam: his counterpart in dwelling and trial. The dual forms in kulā and shi’tumā show that two sides are addressed together.

In خَلَقْنَا زَوْجَيْنِ (khalaqnā zawjayni), zawjayni is the object of khalaqnā and points to a dual structure in creation. Min kulli shay’ opens the scope and prevents limiting the verse to human marriage.

In مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍ بَهِيجٍ (min kulli zawjin bahīj), zawj with min kull points to a kind of plant. In أَزْوَاجَهُمْ (azwājahum) in 37:22, the plural is attached to the wrongdoers, but the gathering context and companions of wrongdoing show that “their spouses” is not necessarily right.

In زُوِّجَتْ (zuwwijat), the verb is passive Form II. Focus falls on souls being paired with counterparts or like-kinds. In زَوَّجْنَاكَهَا (zawwajnākahā), the Form II verb means pairing or joining.

Difference from Nearby Roots

Z W J is close to nikāḥ, but they are not the same. Nikāḥ concerns the contractual, social, and legal mechanism of marital union. Zawjiyyah concerns the quality of being pair, counterpart, and relational mate. Nikāḥ may be a means of establishing the bond, but zawjiyyah names the paired relation itself.

Z W J differs from ṣāḥib. Ṣāḥib is companion or associate, while zawj is a counterpart in a pair relation. Every zawj may be a companion, but not every companion is a zawj.

Z W J is close to qarīn. Qarīn is a close companion, sometimes positive and sometimes negative. But qarīn is not necessarily complementary or structurally paired. Zawj more strongly emphasizes pairhood, proportion, complementarity, or shared structure.

Z W J is not the same as mithl. Mithl shows likeness or example. Zawj shows pair and counterpart. Two things may be alike without being a pair, or a pair without being alike.

Z W J approaches kind and class in plant verses, but kind and class are only categories. Zawj also carries pair-relation or structural kinship. Z W J is also not the same as ithnayn. Ithnayn is only the number two; zawj is a two-ness with relation and bond.

Forqan Translation Method

In Forqan translation, zawj should not always be translated as spouse. In human verses, depending on fluency, spouse may be suitable, but the root explanation should keep clear that zawj means pair and counterpart. In plant verses, zawj means kind, type, or plant pair. In creation verses, zawjayni points to dual and paired structure. In verses of the next life or gathering, azwāj can mean counterparts, like-kinds, or purified pairs.

Zawj should also not be restricted to woman or husband alone. Zawj is a relational word: each side is zawj in relation to the other.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

  • For زَوْجُكَ (zawjuka): your zawj; or in smooth text: your spouse / your counterpart.
  • For زَوْجَهَا (zawjahā): its/her zawj; or: its/her pair and counterpart.
  • For زَوْجَيْنِ (zawjayni): two pairs; or: two sides of a pair / a dual pairing, depending on context.
  • For أَزْوَاجًا (azwājā): pairs; or: counterparts / spouses, depending on context.
  • For وَمِن كُلِّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقْنَا زَوْجَيْنِ (wa-min kulli shay’in khalaqnā zawjayni): and from every thing We created two pairs; or: in every thing We created a paired structure.
  • For مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍ بَهِيجٍ (min kulli zawjin bahīj): from every beautiful pair; or: from every paired and beautiful kind.
  • For أَزْوَاجَهُمْ (azwājahum) in 37:22: their counterparts; or: their like-kinds / accomplices, not necessarily their spouses.
  • For وَإِذَا النُّفُوسُ زُوِّجَتْ (wa-idhā al-nufūsu zuwwijat): when souls are paired and joined; or: when souls are joined with their like-kinds.
  • For أَزْوَاجٌ مُّطَهَّرَةٌ (azwājun muṭahharah): purified azwāj; or in root explanation: purified counterparts.
  • For زَوَّجْنَاكَهَا (zawwajnākahā): We paired and joined her with you; or in smooth text: We gave her to you as spouse, with explanation that the root indicates pairing.

Summary

Z W J in the Qur’an points to pair, counterpart, structural mate, and complement. It begins from dual relation, but is not limited to human marriage. Zawj may be a woman or man in marital relation, but it may also be a plant kind, animal pair, dual structure of creation, counterparts in the next life, or like-groups.

The main difference between zawj and spouse is that spouse is only one use of zawj in human relation, while zawj has a wider field. Its difference from nikāḥ is that nikāḥ concerns the mechanism and contract of marriage, while zawjiyyah concerns pairhood and counterpart relation. Its difference from qarīn is that qarīn is a close companion, while zawj usually has a complementary, like-kind, or paired relation. Its difference from ithnayn is that ithnayn is simply the number two, while zawj is a two-ness with relation and bond.

In summary: Z W J in the Qur’an points to being paired, being a counterpart, structural kinship, and complementarity; sometimes in humans, sometimes in plants and animals, sometimes in the structure of creation, and sometimes in counterparts and like-groups. Mechanical translation of zawj as spouse, wife, or husband hides many Qur’anic uses of the root.
