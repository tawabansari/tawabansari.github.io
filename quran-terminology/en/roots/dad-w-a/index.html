Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root Ḍ W A

Lighting, shining, radiating, and making things visible through active brightness

Important Forms from This Root

ضَاءَ / يُضِيءُ

ḍāʾa / yuḍīʾu; became bright, shone, radiated, or gave light.

أَضَاءَ

aḍāʾa; lit, illuminated, made the surroundings bright.

يُضِيءُ لَهُمْ

yuḍīʾu lahum; it lights for them; makes the path visible for them.

ضَوْء

ḍawʾ; brightness, radiance, revealing light.

ضِيَاء

ḍiyāʾ; radiant brightness, active light, guiding illumination.

مُضِيء / إِضَاءَة

muḍīʾ / iḍāʾah; bright, shining, light-giving; or the act of lighting.

Central Meaning

The root Ḍ W A means lighting, shining, radiating, and making things visible through brightness.

It points to light that has an outward effect: it illuminates the environment, makes seeing possible, and brings something out of darkness or ambiguity.

In Qur’anic usage, this root appears especially in the forms ضِيَاء, أَضَاءَ, and يُضِيءُ.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of Ḍ W A is radiant and revealing brightness. In this root, light is not only a general state. ضَوْء is close to a brightness that radiates from a source and affects the surrounding space.

Linguistically, ḍawʾ and ḍiyāʾ are tied to seeing, appearance, radiance, and removal of darkness. In many contexts, ḍiyāʾ carries a stronger, clearer, or more active light than ordinary light, but this difference should not be imposed mechanically in every case.

Material Image

The material image of this root is brightness shining over a dark environment. When a lamp, fire, lightning, moon, or sun shines, things come out of darkness and become distinguishable to the eye.

Three elements matter in this image: the source of light, the radiation and spread of brightness, and the appearance of things within that brightness.

In some material uses, ḍawʾ may also be accompanied by heat, especially when the brightness comes from the sun or fire. For this reason, calling the sun ḍiyāʾ in the Qur’an fits its strong radiance, active shining, and warming effect. But the root meaning should not be reduced to heat.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, ḍawʾ and ḍiyāʾ are used for perceptible brightness, especially the brightness of the sun, fire, lamp, or any source that makes the surroundings visible.

In ancient Arab life, brightness had practical importance. The desert night was dark, and a person needed light for movement, recognizing the path, seeing danger, identifying place, and finding direction. So ḍawʾ was not merely beauty; it was tied to guidance, safety, and discernment.

Some lexicographers distinguish between nūr and ḍiyāʾ, but this distinction is not identical in every use. Earlier linguistic evidence may illuminate the atmosphere of the root, but it should not restrict Qur’anic meaning.

Ḍawʾ and Nūr

Ḍawʾ and nūr are close, but they are not always identical. Nūr has a wider semantic field. It may refer to sensory light, guidance, manifestation, inner clarity, or spiritual light. In the Qur’an, nūr is related to The God, the Book, faith, guidance, and outward light.

Ḍawʾ or ḍiyāʾ is closer to radiant and revealing brightness: light that affects the environment and makes things visible. In some contexts, ḍiyāʾ also carries intensity, active radiance, and even heat.

In Surat Yūnus, this difference appears importantly: هُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَ الشَّمْسَ ضِيَاءً وَالْقَمَرَ نُورًا 10:5. The sun is called ḍiyāʾ and the moon nūr. This shows that in this context, ḍiyāʾ is tied to radiant, active, powerful brightness suitable to the sun.

Still, we should not say that ḍiyāʾ is always only for an original light source or that nūr is always only reflected light. The Qur’an does not set up a rigid physical rule here. The main difference in this context is the intensity and mode of brightness.

Qur’anic Usage

The root Ḍ W A appears in the Qur’an in several forms and contexts: ḍiyāʾ for the brightness of the sun, aḍāʾa for illuminating surroundings, aḍāʾa lahum for lighting a path in darkness, yuḍīʾu for inner readiness of a substance to give light, and ḍiyāʾ for revelation and criterion.

The Ḍiyāʾ of the Sun

هُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَ الشَّمْسَ ضِيَاءً وَالْقَمَرَ نُورًا 10:5

Here, ḍiyāʾ refers to the brightness of the sun: a light that makes the earthly world visible, brings day into being, and makes sight and the order of time possible.

Aḍāʾat in the Example of Fire

فَلَمَّا أَضَاءَتْ مَا حَوْلَهُ ذَهَبَ اللَّهُ بِنُورِهِمْ 2:17

In this verse, أَضَاءَتْ points to lighting the surroundings. Fire makes the environment visible for a moment. The verse first speaks of the surroundings being lit, then of their nūr being taken away. This shows a distinction between environmental illumination and possessing lasting light.

Aḍāʾa Lahum in the Image of Lightning

كُلَّمَا أَضَاءَ لَهُم مَّشَوْا فِيهِ 2:20

Here the brightness is momentary. Lightning or sudden illumination makes the path visible for a short time, and they move in that moment. This use shows that ḍawʾ may be intense but unstable.

Yuḍīʾu in the Verse of Light

يَكَادُ زَيْتُهَا يُضِيءُ وَلَوْ لَمْ تَمْسَسْهُ نَارٌ 24:35

Here يُضِيءُ points to a brightness that seems almost ready to appear within an extremely pure and prepared substance, even before external fire touches it. Here we should distinguish Ḍ W A from W Q D. W Q D concerns kindling and setting aflame, while yuḍīʾu points to brightness and radiance.

Ḍiyāʾ beside Furqān and Dhikr

وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا مُوسَىٰ وَهَارُونَ الْفُرْقَانَ وَضِيَاءً وَذِكْرًا لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ 21:48

In this verse, ḍiyāʾ appears beside furqān and remembrance. This shows that ḍiyāʾ can also be cognitive and guiding light: something that illuminates the path and makes discernment possible.

Ḍiyāʾ against Continuous Night

مَنْ إِلَٰهٌ غَيْرُ اللَّهِ يَأْتِيكُم بِضِيَاءٍ 28:71

Here, ḍiyāʾ is set against continuous night. The absence of ḍiyāʾ means the absence of the brightness that makes life, movement, sight, and discernment possible.

Difference from Nearby Roots

N W R concerns light, guidance, appearance, and sensory or spiritual clarity, and has a wider field. W Q D concerns kindling fire and activating flame from combustible material. B R Q is sudden lightning-like brightness. S R J is a lamp or ordered source of illumination. SH AYN L (SH ʿ L) concerns flame and tongues of fire. Ḍ Ḥ W / Ḍ Ḥ Y is tied to daylight and the brightness of morning.

Ḍ W A, however, emphasizes radiating brightness, its effect upon the environment, and the appearance of things within that brightness.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, ḍiyāʾ should not always be translated merely as “light,” because part of the root’s intensity, radiance, and revealing effect would be lost. In many places, “radiant brightness,” “radiance,” “revealing light,” or “guiding brightness” may be more precise.

At the same time, the difference between nūr and ḍiyāʾ should not be made too rigid or physical. The Qur’an distinguishes the sun and moon in 10:5, but this does not create an absolute scientific rule for every use of nūr and ḍiyāʾ.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

ضَوْء (ḍawʾ): brightness, radiant light, revealing light.

ضِيَاء (ḍiyāʾ): radiance, radiant brightness, active light, guiding brightness.

أَضَاءَ (aḍāʾa): lit, illuminated, made the surroundings bright.

أَضَاءَ لَهُمْ: lit for them, made the path visible for them.

يُضِيءُ (yuḍīʾu): gives light, radiates, comes near to giving light.

مُضِيء (muḍīʾ): bright, shining, light-giving.

جَعَلَ الشَّمْسَ ضِيَاءً: made the sun a radiant brightness.

يَكَادُ زَيْتُهَا يُضِيءُ: its oil almost radiates light by itself.

الْفُرْقَانَ وَضِيَاءً وَذِكْرًا: the Furqān, guiding brightness, and remembrance.

Linguistic Summary

The root Ḍ W A denotes radiant and revealing brightness. It relates to the moment when brightness affects an environment and makes things visible.

In the Qur’an, ḍiyāʾ is used both for the perceptible brightness of the sun and for the cognitive brightness of revelation. The connection between the two is disclosure: just as sensory ḍiyāʾ makes the world visible to the eye, revelatory ḍiyāʾ makes the path of truth visible to understanding and taqwā.

Its difference from nūr is that nūr has a wider field, while ḍiyāʾ more strongly emphasizes radiance, intensity, and revealing effect. Sometimes this radiance is accompanied by heat and environmental effect, especially in the context of the sun and fire, but the root meaning should not be limited to heat.
