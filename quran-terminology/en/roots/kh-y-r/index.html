1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root خ ي ر in the Qur'an moves in the field of goodness, benefit, value, worthiness, and superiority. Khayr is something that is truly beneficial for a person, a society, or the final outcome of an act, and is worthy of being preferred. Yet this benefit is not always the same as immediate desire, surface pleasure, or the limited judgment of the human being.

The Qur'an states clearly that a person may dislike something while it is khayr for him, and may love something while it is sharr for him. Therefore, khayr is not merely what appears pleasant; it is what is good and beneficial in truth and outcome.

This root has several main Qur'anic uses:

  • goodness and good action;
  • something useful and worthy;
  • being better or superior in comparison;
  • wealth and valuable property;
  • various good deeds or good things in the plural khayrat;
  • choosing in the verb ikhtara;
  • and the right or possibility of choice in al-khiyarah.

These branches are connected to one another, but they should not all be reduced to one flat meaning. For example, khayr in the verse of bequest points to wealth, while in fastabiqu al-khayrat it concerns good deeds and fields of goodness.

The shortest expression for this root is:

What is truly good, beneficial, valuable, or worthy of preference; and in the branch of choice, selecting one option from among options.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

خَيْر

Goodness, khayr, benefit, a valuable thing, or what is worthy of preference.

This form can also carry the comparative meaning “better” without changing its outward form.

الْخَيْر

Khayr and goodness in a known, general, or definite sense.

خَيْرًا

A good, a goodness, a good deed, or valuable wealth, according to context.

خَيْرٌ مِنْ

Better than.

خَيْرَات

Good deeds, good things, or qualities of goodness.

In some contexts it may also describe good-natured persons.

اخْتَارَ / يَخْتَارُ

He chose, or he chooses.

الْخِيَرَة

Choice, the right of choosing, or the possibility of selection in a matter.

مُخْتَار

Chosen or choosing in Arabic usage, but it is not among the central Qur'anic forms of this root.

اسْتَخَارَ / اسْتِخَارَة

Seeking khayr or seeking the better choice in Arabic and later religious usage. This form does not occur in the Qur'an and should not be presented as a main Qur'anic derivative.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of KH Y R can be expressed as:

A real goodness or benefit that makes something worthy of being desired, done, or chosen.

This khayr may be ethical, material, individual, social, related to the present, or revealed only in the final outcome.

Khayr does not always mean “the greatest return” or “the lowest cost.” Sometimes an act is difficult, costly, and unpleasant, but from the standpoint of justice, truth, or final outcome it is khayr. At other times something has immediate benefit, but because of injustice, corruption, or distant consequences it is sharr.

Therefore, the measure of khayr cannot be limited to pleasure, financial gain, material growth, the survival of a system, or the preservation of power.

In the Qur'an, khayr is connected with truth, justice, guidance, righteous action, real benefit, and a sound outcome. The subject and context determine which aspect is prominent in each verse.

In comparative use, khayr means “better”: one option is recognized as more worthy than another in a specific comparison.

5. The Material Image of the Root

One tangible use of khayr in Arabic and the Qur'an is wealth and valuable property.

In the verse of bequest, the phrase appears:

إِنْ تَرَكَ خَيْرًا

If he leaves khayr behind.

The context shows that the intended meaning is wealth and property. Wealth is called khayr because it can be a means of meeting real needs, supporting family, fulfilling responsibility, and doing good.

Yet wealth in itself, and in every condition, is not absolute khayr. Wealth may be acquired through oppression, become a means of corruption, pull a person away from truth, or be accumulated at the expense of others.

So the use of khayr for wealth points to its potential value and benefit, not to the claim that every possession is morally good in every context.

The suitable material image of this root is something whose real value and benefit appear when it is properly weighed: wealth that meets a true need, or a means used in its proper place.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, khayr was used for goodness, benefit, wealth, property, and the better option.

It can function as a noun:

هذا خير

This is good, or this is khayr.

It can also function comparatively:

هذا خير من ذاك

This is better than that.

In comparative usage, khayr gives the meaning “better” without moving to the common outward pattern of af'al. The same feature is seen with sharr.

Ikhtara al-shay' means he selected a thing from among options.

Al-khiyarah is related to choice, the right of selection, or what is selected.

One should not conclude from the use of khayr for wealth that the single historical meaning of the root was “wealth.” Wealth is one instance of khayr, not the whole field.

Likewise, it is not correct to say that khayr only means what materially helps a system survive. Its ethical, spiritual, and hereafter-oriented uses in the Qur'an are very broad.

7. Semantic Restrictions and Misunderstandings

One restriction is reducing khayr to a fixed set of known religious acts.

The Qur'an uses khayr in very broad fields: faith, spending, reform, wealth, bequest, justice, guidance, good action, and better choice.

So khayr is not merely a ritual list.

Another error is equating khayr with whatever a person loves. The Qur'an explicitly rejects this assumption.

It is also wrong to call everything that benefits an individual, group, or government khayr. A group's benefit may be built on oppression of others. Qur'anic khayr is not the same as selfish advantage.

Another error is making khayr completely relative, as though whatever each person considers suitable for himself becomes khayr. The Qur'an recognizes real khayr, even when a person does not know it or does not love it.

8. Methodological Note: Real Khayr and Claimed Khayr

Several levels must be kept distinct when understanding khayr.

Khayr in Human Perception

Something a person imagines to be desirable, beneficial, or pleasant.

Real Khayr

Something that is good and beneficial in truth and final outcome for him or for society.

Khayr in Comparison

An option that is better and more worthy than another option.

Khayr in Material Context

Wealth, property, or a blessing that has value and benefit.

Khayr in Ethical Context

An act, position, or quality that is compatible with guidance, justice, and repair.

Not every khayr is recognized immediately, and not everything called khayr is absolute khayr. Sometimes khayr is relative:

خَيْرٌ مِنْ

That is, one option is better than another, not that it has no deficiency at all.

Thus one must distinguish between absolute khayr, relative khayr, apparent khayr, and real khayr.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

a. Real Khayr Versus Surface Desire

وَعَسَىٰ أَنْ تَكْرَهُوا شَيْئًا وَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ وَعَسَىٰ أَنْ تُحِبُّوا شَيْئًا وَهُوَ شَرٌّ لَكُمْ وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنْتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

Perhaps you dislike something while it is khayr for you, and perhaps you love something while it is sharr for you; and The God knows, while you do not know.

Al-Baqarah 2:216

The verse separates khayr from immediate feeling and desire. Human beings judge through limited knowledge, the present moment, and their own wants. Real khayr or sharr may lie in consequences not yet visible to them.

This verse does not mean that every suffering is necessarily khayr or that a person should not repel harm. It means that human love and dislike are not a complete measure of truth.

b. Calling to Khayr

وَلْتَكُنْ مِنْكُمْ أُمَّةٌ يَدْعُونَ إِلَى الْخَيْرِ وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ

And let there be from among you a community calling to khayr, commanding what is recognized as right, and forbidding what is rejected as wrong.

Ali 'Imran 3:104

Khayr in this verse is a broad heading. Ma'ruf and munkar then clarify its social and practical manifestations.

Yet khayr and ma'ruf are not fully identical. Khayr is the broad field of real goodness and benefit, while ma'ruf is tied to what is recognized and acceptable in the measure of truth.

c. Racing Toward Khayrat

فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ

So race ahead in good deeds.

Al-Baqarah 2:148

And:

فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ

So race ahead in good deeds.

Al-Maidah 5:48

Khayrat is the plural of khayr and points to various fields of goodness. Racing ahead in khayr is not the same as immature haste. Speed in something whose goodness is clear can be praiseworthy.

d. Doing Khayr

وَافْعَلُوا الْخَيْرَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ

And do khayr, so that you may succeed.

Al-Hajj 22:77

This verse shows that khayr is not only an inward quality or theoretical judgment; it can become action and conduct.

Still, “action” is not itself the translation of khayr. The verse commands people to do khayr.

e. Khayr Meaning Wealth

كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ إِنْ تَرَكَ خَيْرًا الْوَصِيَّةُ لِلْوَالِدَيْنِ وَالْأَقْرَبِينَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

It has been prescribed for you, when death comes to one of you, if he leaves wealth behind, that he make a bequest for parents and near relatives in a recognized proper manner.

Al-Baqarah 2:180

Khayr in this context points to wealth and property. Translating it as “goodness” would make the verse unclear. Here the more natural translation is wealth or property.

This use shows that wealth can be khayr, but the verse simultaneously places it within responsibility, bequest, and ma'ruf.

f. Khayr in the Hand of The God

بِيَدِكَ الْخَيْرُ إِنَّكَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

In Your hand is khayr; indeed, You are powerful over every thing.

Ali 'Imran 3:26

“Hand” here should not be understood bodily. The expression points to authority, power, and sovereignty of The God.

The verse says that the source and final disposal of khayr is with Him. One should not conclude from the mention of khayr alone that sharr lies outside His authority. The verse is in the setting of praise and His command over khayr, not a complete philosophical division of all events.

g. Khayr in Comparison

وَلَأَمَةٌ مُؤْمِنَةٌ خَيْرٌ مِنْ مُشْرِكَةٍ وَلَوْ أَعْجَبَتْكُمْ

A believing bondwoman is better than a mushrik woman, even if she amazes you.

Al-Baqarah 2:221

Khayrun min here means “better than.” The verse sets outward standards, social status, and attractiveness against faith.

Khayr in this use is comparison between two options. It does not say that the preferred option is complete from every side; it says it is superior in the criterion being discussed.

h. What Is with The God Is Better

وَمَا عِنْدَ اللَّهِ خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَىٰ

And what is with The God is better and more lasting.

Ash-Shura 42:36

Khayr appears beside abqa, more lasting. The verse compares passing worldly enjoyment with what is with The God. Khayr is not only immediate pleasure or benefit; permanence and outcome also enter the measure.

i. Every Atom's Weight of Khayr Is Seen

فَمَنْ يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ

So whoever does an atom's weight of khayr will see it.

Az-Zalzalah 99:7

Khayr here is a good deed. The smallness of the act does not prevent it from being seen. The verse does not limit moral value to outward size.

j. Whatever Khayr Is Sent Ahead

وَمَا تُقَدِّمُوا لِأَنْفُسِكُمْ مِنْ خَيْرٍ تَجِدُوهُ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ

And whatever khayr you send ahead for yourselves, you will find it with The God.

Al-Baqarah 2:110

By doing khayr, a person sends something ahead for his own future. The expression shows that the final benefit of khayr also returns to the one who does it.

k. Khayr in Spending

وَمَا تُنْفِقُوا مِنْ خَيْرٍ فَلِأَنْفُسِكُمْ

And whatever khayr you spend, it is for yourselves.

Al-Baqarah 2:272

Khayr here can include the wealth that is spent. Yet material value alone is not the whole matter; purity of intention, manner of spending, and destination are also important in the wider set of verses.

l. Choosing by The God

وَرَبُّكَ يَخْلُقُ مَا يَشَاءُ وَيَخْتَارُ

And your Lord creates whatever He wills and chooses.

Al-Qasas 28:68

Yakhtaru points to choosing. Divine choice is not separate from His complete knowledge and wisdom. But the verse does not turn His choosing into a technical algorithm or a central-system calculation.

m. “There Is No Choice for Them”

مَا كَانَ لَهُمُ الْخِيَرَةُ

There is no choice for them.

Al-Qasas 28:68

This sentence comes after divine choosing. It does not mean that human beings have no will or choice at all. Rather, human beings do not determine what The God chooses in creation and messengerhood.

The verse negates absolute human choice against divine choosing; it does not negate all human freedom.

n. Choice Before the Judgment of The God and His Messenger

وَمَا كَانَ لِمُؤْمِنٍ وَلَا مُؤْمِنَةٍ إِذَا قَضَى اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَمْرًا أَنْ يَكُونَ لَهُمُ الْخِيَرَةُ مِنْ أَمْرِهِمْ

It is not for a believing man or a believing woman, when The God and His Messenger have decided a matter, to have a choice in their own matter against it.

Al-Ahzab 33:36

The verse speaks from the position of faith. Once divine judgment is clear, the believer does not place himself as an independent lawgiver against it.

But this verse should not be turned into a denial of reason, questioning, investigation, or human moral choice. “The judgment of The God and His Messenger” must be understood within the valid message and command, not every later claim made in the name of religion, the Prophet, or a religious ruler.

o. The Choice of Musa

وَاخْتَارَ مُوسَىٰ قَوْمَهُ سَبْعِينَ رَجُلًا لِمِيقَاتِنَا

And Musa chose from his people seventy men for Our appointed meeting.

Al-A'raf 7:155

Ikhtara here is human choosing. This use shows that the root of choice is not limited to divine choosing.

p. Khayrat Hisan

فِيهِنَّ خَيْرَاتٌ حِسَانٌ

In them are good-natured and beautiful ones.

Ar-Rahman 55:70

In this context, khayrat points to their goodness and pleasing quality, while hisan highlights beauty or outward goodness.

This pairing shows that khayr and husn are not complete synonyms, even though their fields overlap.

10. Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

خَيْر

The most frequent form of the root. It can mean goodness, benefit, wealth, a valuable thing, or “better” in a comparative structure. Context determines the suitable meaning.

خَيْرٌ مِنْ

Better than. The presence of min usually makes the comparative role clear, though khayr can also carry comparative meaning in some structures without min.

خَيْرَات

Good deeds, good things, or good persons and qualities, according to context.

اخْتَارَ

He chose. Form VIII here is related to selecting from among options. One should not infer from the form alone that the subject always chose the best thing. When the verb is attributed to The God, His choosing rests on knowledge and wisdom; but with human beings, the choice may be correct or incorrect.

الْخِيَرَة

Choice, option, or right of selection. This word does not necessarily mean “choosing the best”; its subject is the possibility or right of choice.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

Khayr as a Noun

يَدْعُونَ إِلَى الْخَيْرِ

Khayr here is a noun and means goodness.

Khayr in Comparative Role

خَيْرٌ مِنْ

In this construction, khayr means better.

Khayr and sharr are among forms that can carry comparative meaning without the ordinary outward pattern of an elative. This morphological feature does not mean that khayr and sharr are absolute physical measures. It is simply their known linguistic behavior.

Khayran in the Context of Wealth

إِنْ تَرَكَ خَيْرًا

The context of bequest shows that khayr points to wealth and property. The meaning of wealth is not created merely by indefiniteness or accusative form; it comes from context.

Al-Khayr and Al-Khayrat

The singular may express the general concept of khayr, while the plural may point to various instances of goodness.

Yakhtaru

It is a transitive verb and can take the thing chosen as its object.

Al-Khiyarah min Amrihim

Al-khiyarah points to the possibility or right of choice, and min amrihim points to the matter or domain in which choice is being discussed.

12. KH Y R Compared with SH R R

Khayr and sharr are repeatedly placed opposite one another in the Qur'an.

Khayr is what is truly good, beneficial, or worthy of preference.

Sharr is what carries harm, evil, or a bad outcome.

This opposition should not be measured only by material survival or destruction. An act may help an unjust system survive while still being sharr. Standing against oppression may be costly while still being khayr.

In Al-Baqarah 2:216, khayr and sharr are independent of human desire:

وَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ
وَهُوَ شَرٌّ لَكُمْ

So the final measure is not only feeling or immediate benefit.

13. KH Y R Compared with H S N

Husn emphasizes goodness, beauty, pleasing quality, or good form.

Khayr emphasizes goodness, benefit, and worthiness of preference.

With caution one may say: husn points to good and pleasing quality; khayr points to goodness, benefit, or being better in evaluation.

But these two fields overlap, and an absolute boundary should not be drawn between them.

14. KH Y R Compared with T Y B

Tayyib is connected with purity, pleasantness, wholesomeness, and suitability.

Khayr is connected with goodness, benefit, and superiority.

Something may be tayyib and also khayr, but the two words do not express the same angle.

For example, tayyib food emphasizes purity and pleasantness, while khayr may express its wider value or benefit.

15. KH Y R Compared with S L H

Salah concerns becoming sound, being proper, repair, and being in a fitting condition.

Khayr points to goodness and real benefit.

Righteous action is usually khayr, but salih and khayr are not complete synonyms.

Salah is correctness and harmony with the proper measure; khayr is goodness, benefit, or worthiness of preference.

16. KH Y R Compared with B R R

Birr is more closely connected with broad ethical goodness, faithfulness, truthfulness, and fulfilling due rights.

Khayr has a wider field and can include wealth, action, quality, outcome, or the better option.

Birr is one deep form of ethical khayr, but not every use of khayr is necessarily birr.

17. Specialized Structural Note: Khayr, Choice, and Divine Judgment

The connection between khayr and ikhtiyar is important at the level of the root, but it must not become the claim that choice always means choosing the best.

A human being can choose, but his choice may be khayr or sharr.

Ikhtara expresses the act of selecting. The correctness of the choice is understood from the doer, the knowledge, the standard, and the result.

In Al-Qasas 28:68, the choosing of The God is based on His will, knowledge, and wisdom, and the human being is not the one who determines that choice.

In Al-Ahzab 33:36, the subject is a believer who, after the divine command has become clear, does not place his own choice as a rival judgment against it.

These verses do not negate the principle of human choice. If human beings had no choice at all, faith, obedience, disobedience, and moral responsibility would become meaningless.

The negation of al-khiyarah in these verses is limited and contextual: the human being is not the final decision-maker before the creative choosing of The God; and after a valid divine command has become clear, the believer does not have the right to cancel it through personal legislation.

These verses should not be used to derive blind obedience to rulers, jurists, transmitters, or structures that present their own command in the name of The God. It must first be established that the ruling truly belongs to the divine message.

18. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

خَيْر

Khayr. Goodness. A good thing. Benefit, where context supports it. Wealth or property, in contexts such as Al-Baqarah 2:180.

“Optimized phenomenon” or “growth-producing flow” is not the base translation.

الْخَيْر

Khayr. Goodness.

خَيْرٌ مِنْ

Better than. Superior to, where context supports it.

“Higher return” is not the translation of the word.

خَيْرَات

Good deeds. Good things. Good qualities. Good-natured ones in the context of Ar-Rahman 55:70.

اخْتَارَ

Chose. Selected.

يَخْتَارُ

Chooses. Selects.

الْخِيَرَة

Choice. Option. Right or possibility of selection, according to context.

19. Conclusion

The root KH Y R in the Qur'an points to goodness, benefit, value, and worthiness of preference.

Khayr is not always identical with human desire, pleasure, or immediate gain. The Qur'an says a person may love something that is sharr for him and dislike something that is khayr for him.

Real khayr must therefore be measured by truth, outcome, justice, and guidance, not only by feeling or near benefit.

In the Qur'an, khayr can mean goodness, a good deed, wealth and property, a valuable thing, and the better option in comparison.

In:

إِنْ تَرَكَ خَيْرًا

khayr points to wealth.

In:

خَيْرٌ مِنْ

it means better or superior.

In:

فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ

it points to good deeds and fields of goodness.

The form khayr can carry comparative meaning without the outward pattern of af'al. This morphological feature should not be turned into a theory about khayr being an absolute physical measure.

Ikhtara means to choose, and al-khiyarah concerns choice or the right of selection. Choosing itself is not necessarily khayr; a human being can also make a wrong choice.

The negation of al-khiyarah before divine choice and judgment is not the negation of all human choice. It belongs to a specific domain in which a valid divine command has become clear.

Khayr overlaps with husn, tayyib, salah, and birr, but it is not identical with them. Husn emphasizes goodness and pleasing quality; tayyib emphasizes purity and wholesomeness; salah emphasizes soundness and repair; birr emphasizes deep ethical goodness and fulfilling due rights; and khayr emphasizes goodness, benefit, and worthiness of preference.

Khayr must not be limited to return, energy, system survival, or material advantage. Something may benefit a system while being sharr because of injustice.

The shortest expression for the core of this root is:

What is truly good, beneficial, valuable, or worthy of preference; and in the branch of choice, selecting one option from among options.
