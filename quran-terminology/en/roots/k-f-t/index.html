Qur’anic root ك ف ت

Gathering, containing, and retaining; the earth as home to living and dead

Qur’anic forms

كِفَاتًا

1. Meaning in early Arabic

ك ف ت concerns gathering, joining, containing, and retaining something.

Ibn Faris identifies gathering and joining as its foundation. Bringing scattered things together and keeping them close fits this meaning.

A verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Zuhayr ibn Abi Sulma describes armor:

وَمُفَاضَةٍ كَالنَّهْيِ تَنْسُجُهُ الصَّبَا بَيْضَاءَ كَفَّتَ فَضْلَهَا بِمُهَنَّدِ

And a broad, bright coat of mail, like a pool woven by the east wind, its excess gathered with an Indian sword.

The hanging portions of the armor are gathered toward the body and held by the sword so they do not impede movement.

كَفَّتَ is Form II here, expressing gathering and securing loose or hanging parts of the armor.

Recorded in Zuhayr’s attributed collection and early lexicons, the line supports gathering and joining in the poetic tradition attributed to the pre-Islamic period; its exact composition date is not independently established.

Lexicons also record:

  • كَفَتَ الشَّيْءَ إِلَيْهِ: gathered it toward himself.
  • كَفَتَ الْمَتَاعَ: gathered possessions together.
  • كَفَتَ الثَّوْبَ: gathered up a garment.
  • جِرَابٌ كَفِيتٌ: a bag retaining what is placed inside.
  • كِفَات: a place where things are gathered and kept.

The last sense, a containing place, directly relates to Qur’anic usage.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important Arabic forms include:

  • كَفَتَ ـ يَكْفِتُ: gathered; contained; kept close.
  • كَفْت: gathering, containing, and retaining.
  • كَفَّتَ: gathered and joined together.
  • كَافِت: one who gathers or contains.
  • كَفِيت: retaining; in another branch, swift or agile.
  • كِفَات: a containing place or vessel.
  • اِنْكَفَتَ: returned; turned toward a place; gathered.
  • اِكْتَفَتَ: gathered or contained something completely.
  • تَكَفَّتَ الثَّوْبُ: the garment gathered up and became shorter.

Other recorded branches concern returning, restraining, swift motion, and overturning.

Form examined in the Qur’an:

كِفَاتًا

The sole Qur’anic derivative is كِفَات in al-Mursalat 77:25.

3. Core meaning and material imagery

The relevant branch concerns gathering and containing something so that it is retained in a place.

Consider a bag holding scattered objects together: it both gathers and retains them.

جِرَابٌ كَفِيتٌ describes this capacity to keep contents from spilling or scattering.

كَفْت is not restricted to enclosed vessels. Gathering clothing, assembling people, and drawing something close also belong to its range.

Containment therefore need not mean complete enclosure on every side.

The noun كِفَات identifies something or somewhere capable of gathering and containing.

The Qur’an describes the earth this way.

4. Qur’anic usage

كِفَاتًا: the earth containing living and dead

These two verses describe two aspects of humanity’s relation to the earth.

During life, people dwell and travel on it, obtain food and necessities from its resources, and grow within its environment.

After death, the body returns to the earth and its materials rejoin nature’s material cycles.

The earth is a place of habitation and activity during life, and of bodily return afterward.

Classical Arabic also calls homes كِفَاتُ الْأَحْيَاءِ and burial grounds كِفَاتُ الْأَمْوَاتِ.

Both retain containment, differently realized: the living inhabit the surface and their dwellings; the dead are placed in the soil.

The earth and human creation

Al-Mursalat 77:25 and al-Mursalat 77:26 connect clearly with another verse:

Three stages are expressed: creation from the earth, return to it, and emergence from it again.

This deepens the relation: the earth is not merely a temporary home but also connected with humanity’s material origin and bodily return.

Al-Inshiqaq also states:

Al-Mursalat describes the earth containing; al-Inshiqaq describes it casting out its contents.

The images connect retention with subsequent emergence.

Resurrection and renewed creation are not themselves lexical meanings of كِفَات; they arise from the relationship with other Qur’anic descriptions.

The earth providing life’s necessities

After describing the earth as containing living and dead, the Qur’an says:

The verse describes environmental features and resources with which people live.

The earth is more than a surface beneath them: it provides water, soil, and conditions for plant growth.

Al-Mulk also states:

This too connects the earth with life, movement, provision, and eventual resurrection.

Together these verses associate earth with human life, necessities, death, and return.

Does كِفَات mean gravity?

Gravity keeps people, water, air, and other materials near the earth.

This physical fact accords with the broad image of earth as a containing place and helps scientifically explain how things remain upon it.

But كِفَات is not the lexical name of gravity.

The verse describes the observable state of earth containing living and dead, without specifying its precise physical mechanism.

“Possessing gravity” therefore restricts a broader meaning to one particular mechanism.

5. Morphology and syntax

كِفَات follows the pattern فِعَال.

Lexicographers and grammarians offer several analyses:

A noun naming a place or vessel where things are gathered.

A verbal noun of كَفَتَ, gathering and joining, describing the earth’s containing capacity.

Or a plural of كَافِت, comparable to قِيَام as a plural of قَائِم.

Gathering and containment unite these analyses.

In al-Mursalat 77:25, الْأَرْضَ is the first object of نَجْعَلِ and كِفَاتًا, in the principal analysis, its second.

أَحْيَاءً وَأَمْوَاتًا in the following verse specifies what the earth contains.

If كِفَات is a verbal noun, living and dead can be its objects. If a place noun, their accusative is explained by an implied construction or the clause’s verbal meaning.

Another traditional interpretation takes living and dead as fertile and barren portions of the earth itself.

Containing living and dead people, however, better fits the lexical definition and passages concerning human life on earth and return to it, especially Ta-Ha 20:55.

Al-Mursalat 77:25 and al-Mursalat 77:26 are separate verses but form a connected sentence and meaning.

6. Comparison with related roots

ك ف ت compared with ج م ع

جَمْع concerns gathering things into a whole.

كَفْت includes gathering, but كِفَات particularly emphasizes containing and retaining what is gathered.

People may assemble temporarily: that is gathering. The place accommodating them can be described from another perspective as كِفَات.

ك ف ت compared with ض م م

ضَمّ concerns bringing things close and joining them.

Here ضَمّ describes moving the hand toward the side.

The words are close; lexicographers define كَفْت through ضَمّ.

Qur’anic كِفَات nevertheless focuses on a place capable of containing and retaining.

ك ف ت compared with و ع ي

و ع ي concerns taking in, gathering, and retaining.

وِعَاء names a vessel, used for storage containers in Yusuf 12:76.

وِعَاء and كِفَات are close, and some lexicographers use the former to define the latter.

و ع ي also has cognitive uses in the Qur’an: receiving and retaining a reminder in the mind.

The sole Qur’anic ك ف ت concerns earth as a containing place.

ك ف ت compared with ح و ي

احْتِوَاء concerns accommodating and containing.

It is very close to كِفَات.

It can describe a vessel, place, or collection containing other things.

كَفْت similarly includes gathering, joining, and retaining.

Despite the proximity, احْتِوَاء in this form does not occur in the Qur’an.

ك ف ت compared with ق ب ض

قَبْض concerns taking, gathering, and bringing into one’s possession.

Lexicographers also use this idea to explain كَفْت.

قَبْض can describe taking something; كِفَات describes a place containing and retaining it.

ك ف ت compared with ق ر ر

قَرَار concerns stability, settlement, and a place to remain.

Ghafir 40:64 and al-Naml 27:61 describe the earth as قَرَار.

قَرَار emphasizes stable settlement; كِفَات emphasizes containment and retention.

The earth is both humanity’s place of settlement and a place containing living and dead: two aspects of the relationship.

7. Suggested renderings for Forqan

Arabic word or expressionSuggested rendering
كَفَتَgathered; contained
كَفْتgathering and containing
كَافِتgathering; containing
كِفَاتa containing place
جِرَابٌ كَفِيتٌa bag retaining its contents
أَلَمْ نَجْعَلِ الْأَرْضَ كِفَاتًاHave We not made the earth a containing place?

“A containing place” suits al-Mursalat 77:25, preserving gathering and retention without reducing earth to a closed vessel or particular physical mechanism.

“Burial place” alone is insufficient: these verses include living as well as dead.

Conclusion

ك ف ت concerns gathering, joining, containing, and retaining.

The Qur’an uses كِفَات for the earth, where the living dwell and the dead return.

Alongside other passages, this illuminates humanity’s relation to earth from creation through death and renewed emergence.

The central Qur’anic meaning is a place that contains and retains beings.
