Foundational Meaning

The root س ج د fundamentally means to lower oneself, to humble oneself, to bow down, and to enter a lowered state of submission.

At the material level, sujud is connected with the body lowering, bending, and the head or face entering a posture of humility. But in the Qur'an, its meaning is not limited to bodily movement. Sujud can indicate humility, obedience, submission, honoring, or standing before a higher rank.

The key point is that sujud directed to The God is joined with faith, honoring, worship, will, and obedience. But sujud directed toward other than The God must not automatically be understood with the same meaning of worship. The sujud of the angels for Adam, or the sujud of Yusuf's family before him, is not worship in the sense of devotion to the one receiving it. In context, it means humility, honoring, recognition of rank, or carrying out the command of The God.

Important Forms

سَجَدَ is the past-tense verb and means he prostrated, fell down, or humbled himself. يَسْجُدُ and يَسْجُدُونَ are present forms and refer to prostrating or humbling oneself. اسْجُدُوا is the imperative and appears both in relation to sujud for The God and in the divine command to the angels regarding Adam.

سُجُود is a verbal noun or noun of state, pointing to prostration, humility, or lowering oneself. سَاجِد and its plurals such as سَاجِدُون and سَاجِدِينَ refer to someone or something in a state of sujud, humility, or servitude.

مَسْجِد is a place noun: a place of sujud, a place of humility, or a place where worship and remembrance of The God take place. مَسَاجِد is its plural. Qur'anically, masjid should not be reduced to a building, tribe, sect, or particular religious faction. Its root meaning is the place of sujud and remembrance of The One God.

Linguistic Note

The semantic center of س ج د turns around lowering oneself before a higher rank. This lowering can be bodily, such as falling upon the forehead, or existential and spiritual, such as the submission of created beings before the command of The God.

In the Qur'an, the direction of sujud determines its meaning. When sujud is for The God, its meaning reaches worship, obedience, submission, and faithful honoring.

But when sujud is attributed to someone other than The God, one must ask: who gave the command, what is the context, and is the verse speaking about worship, respect, humility, recognition of rank, or obedience to divine command?

Material Image of the Root

The material image of sujud is the lowering of the most elevated and honored visible part of the human being, the head and face, toward the ground. Normally a person holds the head high. In sujud, the head descends, the body lowers, and the person enters a posture that signals humility and lowering before another.

From this image, the wider meaning of the root becomes clear: sujud is leaving the state of standing, self-elevation, or resistance, and entering a state of humility, acceptance, and being lower before a higher rank.

When this humility is for The God, it becomes worship and servitude. In another context, it may mean honoring, respect, or submission to divine command.

Qur'anic Usage

One of the clearest Qur'anic uses of this root is worshipful sujud for The God:

فَاسْجُدُوا لِلَّهِ وَاعْبُدُوا

So prostrate to The God and worship. An-Najm 53:62

In this verse, sujud is directly joined with worship. The direction of sujud is لِلَّهِ, and its meaning is faithful humility, servitude, and obedience before The God. This kind of sujud is worship and belongs to The God alone.

The same meaning appears in the prohibition against sujud to the sun and moon:

لَا تَسْجُدُوا لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ وَاسْجُدُوا لِلَّهِ الَّذِي خَلَقَهُنَّ

Do not prostrate to the sun or to the moon, but prostrate to The God who created them. Fussilat 41:37

Here the Qur'an clarifies the boundary of worship. The sun and moon are signs of The God, but worshipful sujud must not be directed to them. Sujud for The God is sujud to the Creator, not to created things, however great and wondrous they may be.

Existential Sujud of Created Beings

In many verses, all who are in the heavens and the earth are described as being in sujud or humility before The God:

وَلِلَّهِ يَسْجُدُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ طَوْعًا وَكَرْهًا

And to The God prostrates whoever is in the heavens and the earth, willingly or unwillingly. Ar-Ra'd 13:15

Here sujud is not only the bodily movement of the human being. The verse speaks of the submission of all beings before the order, command, and authority of The God. The phrase طَوْعًا وَكَرْهًا shows that this sujud can include existential and unavoidable submission of created beings, not only conscious human worship.

The same field of meaning appears in another verse:

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَسْجُدُ لَهُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَنْ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَالشَّمْسُ وَالْقَمَرُ وَالنُّجُومُ وَالْجِبَالُ وَالشَّجَرُ وَالدَّوَابُّ وَكَثِيرٌ مِنَ النَّاسِ

Have you not seen that to The God prostrates whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on the earth, and the sun, the moon, the stars, the mountains, the trees, the animals, and many of the people? Al-Hajj 22:18

In this verse, sujud includes human beings, animals, plants, mountains, stars, and heavenly bodies. Therefore, sujud here must be understood more broadly than human bodily movement: each being is, in its own way, subject to the law and command of The God.

Sujud for Adam and Yusuf

One of the most sensitive uses of this root is the command for the angels to make sujud for Adam:

وَإِذْ قُلْنَا لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ اسْجُدُوا لِآدَمَ فَسَجَدُوا إِلَّا إِبْلِيسَ

And when We said to the angels, "Prostrate for Adam," they prostrated, except Iblis. Al-Baqarah 2:34

In this verse, sujud for Adam does not mean worshiping Adam, because the command-giver is The God and the Qur'an assigns worship to The God. This sujud must be understood within the context of divine command: humility before the command of The God, recognition of Adam's rank in that scene, and lowering oneself before divine wisdom in the creation and teaching of Adam.

Iblis outwardly refused sujud to Adam, but in reality he rejected the command of The God. This point becomes clearer in Surat al-A'raf:

قَالَ مَا مَنَعَكَ أَلَّا تَسْجُدَ إِذْ أَمَرْتُكَ

He said: What prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you? Al-A'raf 7:12

The phrase إِذْ أَمَرْتُكَ is important. The issue was not worship of Adam; the issue was the command of The God and the disobedience of Iblis.

Another use of sujud toward other than The God appears in the story of Yusuf:

وَرَفَعَ أَبَوَيْهِ عَلَى الْعَرْشِ وَخَرُّوا لَهُ سُجَّدًا

And he raised his parents upon the throne, and they fell down in sujud for him. Yusuf 12:100

In this verse too, the sujud of Yusuf's family for him should not be understood as worship of Yusuf. The context is the fulfillment of Yusuf's dream and the manifestation of his rank. This sujud is humility, honoring, and recognition of rank, not worship.

A Focused Reflection on Masjid

مَسْجِد comes from this same root and is, structurally, a place noun: a place of sujud, a place of humility, a place of lowering oneself. In the Qur'an, however, masjid should not be restricted to the worship-place of one sect, one school, one tribe, or one inherited religious system. A masjid is a masjid because it is a place of humility and worship of The One God.

The verse in Surat al-Jinn states this principle clearly:

وَأَنَّ الْمَسَاجِدَ لِلَّهِ فَلَا تَدْعُوا مَعَ اللَّهِ أَحَدًا

And the masajid belong to The God, so do not call upon anyone along with The God. Al-Jinn 72:18

In this verse, the ownership and direction of the masajid are returned to The God: لِلَّهِ. The masjid is spiritually for The God, not the property of a sect, tribe, government, school, or clerical class. The continuation of the verse also gives the criterion: فَلَا تَدْعُوا مَعَ اللَّهِ أَحَدًا. In a masjid, calling, prayer, worship, and humility must not be mixed with taking partners beside The God.

The verse in Surat al-Hajj widens the horizon further:

وَلَوْلَا دَفْعُ اللَّهِ النَّاسَ بَعْضَهُمْ بِبَعْضٍ لَهُدِّمَتْ صَوَامِعُ وَبِيَعٌ وَصَلَوَاتٌ وَمَسَاجِدُ يُذْكَرُ فِيهَا اسْمُ اللَّهِ كَثِيرًا

And if The God did not repel people by means of others, monasteries, churches, places of prayer, and masajid in which the name of The God is remembered much would have been demolished. Al-Hajj 22:40

This verse is important. Alongside masajid, the Qur'an mentions monasteries, churches, and places of prayer: places of worship and remembrance of The God in other communities. The phrase يُذْكَرُ فِيهَا اسْمُ اللَّهِ كَثِيرًا shows that the Qur'anic criterion is remembrance of the name of The God and the presence of monotheistic worship.

At the same time, precision is needed: Al-Hajj 22:40 does not erase the historical names of these places. Monastery, church, place of prayer, and masjid remain distinct terms. But the verse places them in a larger field: the field of remembering the name of The God and protecting places where The One God is remembered.

From here, it becomes clear that masjid is not merely the worship-place of Sunnis, Shi'is, or followers of a particular inherited report tradition. Wherever human beings turn to The One God, call upon Him without partner, and lower themselves before Him, that place stands within the Qur'anic reality of sujud and the semantic field of masjid.

Masjid and the World as a Field of Sujud

When the Qur'an says that whoever is in the heavens and the earth prostrates to The God, the meaning of masjid also stretches beyond human walls. The world, from the standpoint of existential submission, is a vast scene of sujud. But the human masjid is the place where this submission becomes conscious, faithful, and worshipful.

So one can say: all existence is subject to The God, but the masjid is where the human being consciously turns this submission into worship, remembrance, prayer, and obedience.

If a masjid becomes empty of tawhid and is closed around the name of other than The God, the power of other than The God, or sectarian ownership, it moves away from its root reality, even if the name masjid remains on it.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

Sujud for The God is not the same as sujud for Adam or Yusuf. In sujud for The God, the direction is worship, faith, honoring, and servitude. In the sujud of the angels for Adam, the axis is the command of The God and the recognition of Adam's rank in that scene. In the sujud of Yusuf's family, the axis is honoring and humility before the fulfillment of Yusuf's rank, not worship of him.

The sujud of a human being is also not identical to the sujud of a mountain, tree, star, or shadow. The human being can prostrate consciously and by will, while the sujud of other beings in the Qur'an can refer to their existential submission before the command of The God.

Masjid should also not be restricted to the meaning of a religious building. From the root, masjid is a place of sujud. In Qur'anic usage, it points to a place of worship, humility, and remembrance of The God. Al-Jinn 72:18 and Al-Hajj 22:40 show that masjid and places of divine remembrance should not be reduced to sectarian ownership or religious monopoly.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

The letter لِـ in اسْجُدُوا لِآدَمَ and خَرُّوا لَهُ سُجَّدًا matters. This لِـ marks the direction or relation of sujud, but by itself it does not prove worship. To understand the type of sujud, one must consider the context, the command-giver, and the subject of the verse.

In فَاسْجُدُوا لِلَّهِ وَاعْبُدُوا, sujud is joined with worship and directed to The God. Here the worshipful meaning is clear.

In لَا تَسْجُدُوا لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ وَاسْجُدُوا لِلَّهِ, the contrast shows that worshipful sujud to created things is rejected. The verse also gives the reason: The God created them. Worshipful sujud must belong to the Creator, not to signs and created beings.

In وَأَنَّ الْمَسَاجِدَ لِلَّهِ, the lam in لِلَّهِ clarifies direction and belonging. The masjid, in its truth, is for The God; the place of sujud must not be turned into the possession of a partner, a human power, or sectarian ownership.

In يُذْكَرُ فِيهَا اسْمُ اللَّهِ كَثِيرًا, the pronoun فِيهَا returns to the places of worship. This phrase shows that the Qur'an views different places of divine remembrance within a shared horizon.

Difference From Nearby Roots

خ ض ع points to general humility and submission. Sujud can be a more intense and lower form of humility, especially when joined to bodily movement or worship.

ر ك ع relates to bowing and ruku'. Ruku' is lowering to the level of bending, while sujud is lower and more complete; in sujud, the head and face move near the lowest level.

ط و ع relates to obedience and willing compliance. Sujud can be a sign of obedience, but obedience does not necessarily include the movement of sujud.

ع ب د relates to servitude and worship. Sujud for The God can be worship, but sujud in every context is not worship, as with the angels' sujud for Adam or Yusuf's family's sujud for him.

ذ ل ل relates to being softened, made tractable, humbled, or made subject. Sujud can be a sign of lowliness before The God, but Dh L L covers a broader field of being subdued, softened, or lowered.

ق ن ت relates to sustained obedient humility. Sujud can be a moment or state of humility, while qunut more strongly conveys continuity in obedient devotion.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

سَجَدَ: prostrated, fell down, bowed down, humbled himself; depending on context.

اسْجُدُوا لِلَّهِ: prostrate to The God, lower yourselves in prostration and worship before The God.

اسْجُدُوا لِآدَمَ: prostrate for Adam, bow down before Adam; in the context of divine command, not worship of Adam.

خَرُّوا لَهُ سُجَّدًا: they fell down in sujud for him, they bowed down before him, they lowered themselves for him; in the context of honoring and fulfillment of the dream, not worship.

يَسْجُدُ لَهُ: he prostrates to Him, he is humbled before Him, he is lowered before His command; depending on the subject.

سُجُود: prostration, humility, falling down, a state of humble obedience.

سَاجِد: one who prostrates, one who is humbled, one who bows down.

مَسْجِد: place of sujud, place of worship of The One God, place of humility and remembrance of The God.

مَسَاجِد: places of sujud and remembrance of The God; not the property of a sect or tribe.

الْمَسَاجِدُ لِلَّهِ: the places of sujud belong to The God / the masajid are for The God.

Linguistic Summary

The root س ج د indicates lowering oneself, humbling oneself, bowing down, and entering a lowered state before a higher rank. Sujud for The God is worshipful and faithful sujud. But sujud toward other than The God, such as Adam or Yusuf, must be understood in its own context and should not be mechanically equated with worship.

مَسْجِد comes from this root and is, at root, the place of sujud and humility before The God. With the verse وَأَنَّ الْمَسَاجِدَ لِلَّهِ, the Qur'an removes the masjid from sectarian ownership and relates it to The God.

Likewise, Al-Hajj 22:40 shows that different places of divine remembrance in different communities have sanctity. Therefore, in its Qur'anic truth, masjid is not merely the building of one school or the worship-place of one sect. Wherever The One God is remembered and worshiped without partner, that place stands in the field of sujud and the truth of masjid.
