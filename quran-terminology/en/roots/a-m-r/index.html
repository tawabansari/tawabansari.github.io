Core Meaning

The root أ م ر points, at its base, to commanding, directing, seeking that something be done, and placing an action or affair on the path of execution. From this field, the word أمر also expands to command, order, affair, matter, concern, event, management, and an effective matter.

The shared core of this root is directing a behavior or an affair. Sometimes that direction appears as a direct command, such as command to justice, ihsan, salat, or ma'ruf. Sometimes أمر names the affair itself that must be managed, resolved, carried out, or judged. Sometimes it points to a divine event or management, an affair that affects the world or history and changes the path.

So أ م ر should not be limited to “giving an order.” In the Qur'an, أمر can be divine command, divine affair, the management of existence, social work, collective responsibility, decisive event, or a matter that affects the behavior of human beings and society.

Important Forms

أمر is a past-tense verb and points to commanding or ordering. In many cases this verb comes with the letter ba, meaning commanding toward an action, value, or direction:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُ بِالْعَدْلِ وَالْإِحْسَانِ

Indeed, The God commands justice and ihsan.

An-Nahl 16:90

يأمر is the present-tense verb and shows ongoing, general, or repeated commanding:

أَتَأْمُرُونَ النَّاسَ بِالْبِرِّ وَتَنسَوْنَ أَنفُسَكُمْ

Do you command people to birr while forgetting yourselves?

Al-Baqarah 2:44

أمر or مر is the imperative form: command, direct, or place an affair on the path of execution:

وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِالصَّلَاةِ وَاصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا

Command your family to salat, and remain steadfast over it.

Taha 20:132

أمروا and أمرنا, depending on context, can point to commanding, being commanded, or being placed within the path of a command. Its passive form also appears in the Qur'an:

وَمَا أُمِرُوا إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ

And they were not commanded except to worship The God, making the din sincere for Him.

Al-Bayyinah 98:5

أمر is the main noun of the root. Depending on context, it can point to command, affair, matter, event, decision, governance, or effective ordering:

أَلَا لَهُ الْخَلْقُ وَالْأَمْرُ

Be aware: creation and the amr belong to Him.

Al-A'raf 7:54

أمور is the plural of أمر and points to affairs, matters, concerns, or events:

وَإِلَى اللَّهِ تُرْجَعُ الْأُمُورُ

And all affairs are returned to The God.

Al-Baqarah 2:210

أولي الأمر is an important phrase. It points to those entrusted with the affair, those bearing responsibility, or people from within the community who carry responsibility for a matter of collective life:

وَأُولِي الْأَمْرِ مِنكُمْ

And those entrusted with the affair from among you.

An-Nisa 4:59

ائتمر belongs to the same field of the root and points to consultation, coordination, deliberation, or gathering around a decision. This form can come in a sound context or a corrupt one:

وَأْتَمِرُوا بَيْنَكُم بِمَعْرُوفٍ

And consult and coordinate among yourselves in a recognized and fitting way.

At-Talaq 65:6

And in another context:

إِنَّ الْمَلَأَ يَأْتَمِرُونَ بِكَ لِيَقْتُلُوكَ

Indeed, the chiefs are deliberating about you so that they may kill you.

Al-Qasas 28:20

أمير in classical Arabic is from this same root and refers to someone who is entrusted with command or the management of an affair. This form does not appear in the Qur'an in this form, but it expanded greatly in later political history. When reading the Qur'an, later sultanic, caliphal, or class-based meanings should not be imposed on every use of أمر.

إمر also appears in the Qur'an:

لَقَدْ جِئْتَ شَيْئًا إِمْرًا

You have truly brought something grave and astonishing.

Al-Kahf 18:71

This form in Qur'anic usage points to something weighty, hard, reprehensible, or astonishing. It should not be merged with أمر as a simple command.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of أ م ر turns around directing an action, ordering an affair, and bringing a matter into the path of doing or management. When someone commands, he does not merely utter an ineffective statement. He says something that asks the addressee to carry out an action or follow a path. Amr is speech that presses toward action.

But أمر is not only a verbal command. In Arabic and in the Qur'an, أمر can be the affair itself, the matter, the concern, or the event. When one says “this amr,” the meaning can be an affair that must be handled, a matter that must be understood, a concern that has effect, or an event that changes the path of life.

This is why translation requires care. If أمر is translated everywhere as “command,” many verses become too narrow or are misunderstood. In إن الله يأمر بالعدل, commanding is clear. But in وأمرهم شورى بينهم, أمر means the collective affair or work. In يدبر الأمر, أمر points to the flow of affairs and the ordering of existence. In أتى أمر الله, أمر is closer to an event or a command that comes into realization.

In Arabic, the word أمارة is also close to sign and mark. This kinship can help us understand the field of meaning: at the behavioral level, amr is a kind of directing and marking out for action. Even so, one should not say without firm evidence that the original historical meaning of the root began only from stone markers or road signs in the desert. If reliable evidence from pre-Qur'anic poetry or ancient Arabic usage appears, it can be added to the discussion.

So أ م ر is one of the roots that carries the language of command and also the language of work, affair, governance, responsibility, and event.

Material Image

The material image of this root can be seen in a scene where an affair is loose, scattered, or unordered and then receives direction. Several people in a field are moving in different ways; with the issuing of an amr, the movements go toward a specified destination. In this image, amr is the point that turns scattered behavior into a defined path.

Another image is an affair standing before a human being: a problem that must be solved, a responsibility that must be fulfilled, a concern that must be managed, or an event that must be faced. From this angle, أمر moves from the meaning of command to the meaning of affair and concern.

If أمارة is also considered as a nearby word, the image of sign and mark helps this field as well. A sign on a road removes pathlessness. Amr plays a similar role in behavior, work, or society: it gives direction, sets a limit, clarifies a path, or sets an affair in motion.

At the ontological level of the Qur'an, أمر can be deeper: the divine command and ordering that places creation on the path of management, measure, outcome, and realization. In such a context, أمر is not merely a linguistic order. It is an affair that has effect and carries the world, history, or behavior toward an outcome.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, أ م ر is used in two main fields: commanding and ordering, and affair, concern, matter, and event. One says أمره بكذا, meaning he commanded him to something. One also says أمر الرجل, meaning his affair and concern.

From this root, words such as أمير took shape in social and political usage: someone entrusted with command or the management of an affair. Later, this use took on heavy political meaning in Muslim history. But in studying the Qur'anic root, one must be careful not to impose later sultanic, caliphal, pharaonic, or class-based meanings onto the Qur'anic text.

أمارة in Arabic also points to sign, mark, and indicator. This connection is useful for understanding direction-giving in the root, but it must be used with caution. As long as precise pre-Qur'anic evidence for the origin of the root is not in hand, it should not be presented as the certain origin of the meaning.

If reliable evidence from pre-Qur'anic poetry or ancient Arabic usage appears, it can be added to this discussion.

The Divine Command to Justice and Ihsan

One of the clearest uses of the root is the divine command toward ethical and social values:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُ بِالْعَدْلِ وَالْإِحْسَانِ وَإِيتَاءِ ذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَيَنْهَىٰ عَنِ الْفَحْشَاءِ وَالْمُنكَرِ وَالْبَغْيِ

Indeed, The God commands justice, ihsan, and giving to the near kin, and He forbids fahsha, munkar, and baghy.

An-Nahl 16:90

In this verse, أمر stands opposite prohibition. Amr is directing toward justice, ihsan, and giving due right. Prohibition is holding back from fahsha, munkar, and baghy. This contrast shows that أ م ر is tied to directing behavior.

The verse does not only forbid fahsha; it also forbids baghy. Baghy means rebellion, crossing the limit, oppression, transgression, and power that goes beyond the boundary of right. So Qur'anic munkar is not only a small individual behavior. Rebellious power, structural oppression, transgression, corruption, and coercive imposition also fall within the field of divine prohibition.

Another verse clarifies the boundary of divine command:

قُلْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَأْمُرُ بِالْفَحْشَاءِ

Say: The God does not command fahsha.

Al-A'raf 7:28

This verse is an important criterion. Not everything attributed to The God is divine command. If a command in the name of religion draws the human being toward fahsha, oppression, humiliation, coercion, rebellion, or corruption, it must be measured by the Qur'an itself.

Commanding Ma'ruf and Forbidding Munkar

One important use of the root is the phrase commanding ma'ruf and forbidding munkar:

وَلْتَكُن مِّنكُمْ أُمَّةٌ يَدْعُونَ إِلَى الْخَيْرِ وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ

And let there be from among you an ummah that calls to good, commands ma'ruf, and forbids munkar.

Ali 'Imran 3:104

In this verse, commanding ma'ruf comes beside calling to good. This order is important. If commanding ma'ruf is separated from good, it leaves its Qur'anic path. Here the main discussion is the root أ م ر, not the roots ع ر ف or ن ك ر. It is enough to say that ma'ruf in this compound is an action that stands in the horizon of good, sound recognition, fittingness, and truth, not every habit that a tribe or dominant power names acceptable.

Another verse says:

كُنتُمْ خَيْرَ أُمَّةٍ أُخْرِجَتْ لِلنَّاسِ تَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَتَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ وَتُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ

You were the best ummah brought out for the people: you command ma'ruf, forbid munkar, and have faith in The God.

Ali 'Imran 3:110

In this verse, the responsibility is given to the ummah, not to a self-made religious class or a power that imagines itself the owner of people's faith. Commanding ma'ruf in the Qur'an is a social responsibility in the field of faith, good, and reform. It is not a license for inspection, humiliation, religious coercion, or domination over people's conscience.

This use must also be measured by the Qur'anic principle that there is no compulsion in din:

لَا إِكْرَاهَ فِي الدِّينِ

There is no compulsion in din.

Al-Baqarah 2:256

So commanding ma'ruf, in its Qur'anic meaning, is directing toward good and standing against recognized ugliness. But when it becomes coercion in din, imposing human fiqh, frightening people, or acting as guardian over the faith of others, it leaves its Qur'anic direction and can itself become munkar.

Command and the Danger of Making Religion Without Permission

The Qur'an speaks of those who legislate for people something from religion that The God has not permitted:

أَمْ لَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ شَرَعُوا لَهُم مِّنَ الدِّينِ مَا لَمْ يَأْذَن بِهِ اللَّهُ

Or do they have partners who legislated for them from the din what The God did not permit?

Ash-Shura 42:21

This verse is very important for understanding the limits of command. Every religious command receives its truth from its relation to The God, not from government, sect, a juristic class, or political power. If someone creates a ruling in the name of religion that The God has not permitted and then imposes it on people, he has not merely made an administrative mistake; he has entered the field of making partners in legislation.

So command in the Qur'an cannot be a license for religious lawmaking without permission from The God. Commanding ma'ruf too, if separated from the Book of The God, justice, mercy, freedom of faith, and right, becomes an instrument for making human religion in the name of The God.

Human Command and the Danger of Duality

The Qur'an criticizes those who command people to birr while forgetting themselves:

أَتَأْمُرُونَ النَّاسَ بِالْبِرِّ وَتَنسَوْنَ أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَنتُمْ تَتْلُونَ الْكِتَابَ

Do you command people to birr while forgetting yourselves, while you recite the Book?

Al-Baqarah 2:44

This verse shows that commanding can become separated from sidq. A person may have the language of command, yet not be on the path of that very command. So أ م ر is not only the power to direct others. It also reveals the responsibility and truthfulness of the one who commands.

The religious danger here is clear. A person may recite the Book and command people, while he himself is far from its truth. Such a command is emptied of its root, because command should be direction toward truth, not an instrument of superiority or control over others.

Command in Family Formation

In Taha it says:

وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِالصَّلَاةِ وَاصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا

Command your family to salat, and remain steadfast over it.

Taha 20:132

In this verse, command to salat is joined with steadfast endurance. This point is important. Command in the field of formation is not merely issuing an order. It requires steadiness, companionship, patience, and continuity. The one who commands his family to salat must himself remain steady on this path.

So formative command in the Qur'an is not violence, blind imposition, or dry ordering. Command is direction, and when this direction appears in the home and in formation, it is joined with patience and continuity.

Divine Amr and the Realizing Command

In the Qur'an, divine أمر sometimes points to a command joined with realization:

إِنَّمَا أَمْرُهُ إِذَا أَرَادَ شَيْئًا أَن يَقُولَ لَهُ كُن فَيَكُونُ

His amr, when He wills a thing, is only that He says to it, “Be,” and it is.

Ya-Sin 36:82

In this verse, أمر is not merely an ethical or social command. Divine أمر relates to realization, bringing into being, and the penetration of divine will in existence. Here أمر is joined with كن فيكون; the divine command at the level of creation has realizing effect.

Another verse shows the same connection:

بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ وَإِذَا قَضَىٰ أَمْرًا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُ كُن فَيَكُونُ

Originator of the heavens and the earth; and when He settles an amr, He only says to it, “Be,” and it is.

Al-Baqarah 2:117

Here أمر is tied to qada and realization. Qada shows the stage of settlement and decisiveness; أمر is the affair or concern that enters the path of realization.

Amr as Divine Affair

In a foundational verse it says:

أَلَا لَهُ الْخَلْقُ وَالْأَمْرُ

Be aware: creation and the amr belong to Him.

Al-A'raf 7:54

Here أمر is not merely verbal command. Creation and amr come together. Creation points to bringing into being; أمر points to command, governance, ordering, and effective direction by The God in existence.

This verse shows that أمر in the Qur'an sometimes moves to a level beyond legislative command. The God is not only Creator; the amr also belongs to Him. That means the ordering, direction, governance, and effective command of existence return to Him.

Managing the Affair

In several verses, managing the affair is attributed to The God:

يُدَبِّرُ الْأَمْرَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ إِلَى الْأَرْضِ

He manages the affair from the heaven to the earth.

As-Sajdah 32:5

And also:

يُدَبِّرُ الْأَمْرَ ۖ يُفَصِّلُ الْآيَاتِ

He manages the affair; He details the ayat.

Ar-Ra'd 13:2

In this context, أمر points to the flow of affairs and the effective ordering of existence. Tadbir means managing an affair with regard for outcome, measure, order, and stages. So أمر in يدبر الأمر should not be translated only as “command.” “Affairs,” “the ordering of the affair,” “the flow of affairs,” or “the affair of existence” are more precise.

The Ruh from the Amr of the Lord

In Surat Al-Isra it says:

وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الرُّوحِ ۖ قُلِ الرُّوحُ مِنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّي

And they ask you about the ruh. Say: The ruh is from the amr of my Lord.

Al-Isra 17:85

In this verse, أمر cannot mean only ordinary command. The ruh is from the amr of the Lord, meaning it belongs to a domain of divine affair, governance, and truth about which human knowledge is limited. The continuation of the verse shows the same limitation:

وَمَا أُوتِيتُم مِّنَ الْعِلْمِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

And you have not been given of knowledge except a little.

Al-Isra 17:85

This use shows that أمر in the Qur'an can relate to the unseen and divine domain, not only to behavioral instruction.

The Amr of The God as Decisive Event

The Qur'an speaks of the coming of the amr of The God:

أَتَىٰ أَمْرُ اللَّهِ فَلَا تَسْتَعْجِلُوهُ

The amr of The God has come, so do not seek to hasten it.

An-Nahl 16:1

In this verse, أمر الله is pictured as something that arrives. Here أمر can point to a decisive event, a realizing command, or the final affair of The God. Translating this context as “The order of The God came” is not enough.

In another verse it says:

وَكَانَ أَمْرُ اللَّهِ مَفْعُولًا

And the amr of The God was something to be done.

An-Nisa 4:47

When أمر الله enters the path of realization, it is not only speech. It is a reality that is done and leaves its effect.

Social Affair and Consultation

In Surat Ash-Shura it says:

وَأَمْرُهُمْ شُورَىٰ بَيْنَهُمْ

And their affair is consultation among them.

Ash-Shura 42:38

In this verse, أمر does not mean commanding. It points to the collective affair, concern, and matters of the believers. “Their command is consultation among them” is not right. The more precise meaning is: their work, affairs, or collective concern moves by consultation among themselves.

This verse is very important for understanding the root أ م ر, because it shows that أمر can relate to a social matter or work, not only to an order. It also shows that collective affairs should not turn into individual autocracy. أمر at the social level must be understood in the field of shura and participation.

I'timar: Consultation, Coordination, or Collusion Around a Decision

The form i'timar is also important in the Qur'an. In At-Talaq 65:6 it says:

وَأْتَمِرُوا بَيْنَكُم بِمَعْرُوفٍ

And consult and coordinate among yourselves in a recognized and fitting way.

At-Talaq 65:6

Here i'timar comes in a family context and in responsibility after separation. Its meaning is not that one side merely commands the other. Rather, it is consultation, coordination, and arriving at a fitting decision between the two sides.

But the same form can also appear in a negative context:

إِنَّ الْمَلَأَ يَأْتَمِرُونَ بِكَ لِيَقْتُلُوكَ

Indeed, the chiefs are deliberating about you so that they may kill you.

Al-Qasas 28:20

Here i'timar points to collusion and deliberation over killing. So from the angle of the root, i'timar means gathering around an affair or decision, but its value depends on its direction and context. If it is in ma'ruf, it is constructive. If it is in oppression, it is corrupting.

Uli al-Amr: Those Entrusted with Affairs, Not Absolute Owners of People

One of the most sensitive uses of this root is the phrase أولي الأمر:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ وَأُولِي الْأَمْرِ مِنكُمْ ۖ فَإِن تَنَازَعْتُمْ فِي شَيْءٍ فَرُدُّوهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَالرَّسُولِ

O you who have faith, obey The God, obey the Messenger, and those entrusted with the affair from among you. Then if you dispute in anything, return it to The God and the Messenger.

An-Nisa 4:59

In this phrase, one must avoid imposing royal and pharaonic meaning on the word. أمر in the Qur'an does not always mean absolute rule. Just as in وأمرهم شورى بينهم, أمر points to the collective work and concern of the believers, in أولي الأمر it can point to those entrusted with work, holders of responsibility, stewards of social affairs, or those who hold the reins of a public concern.

Therefore, أولي الأمر in its Qur'anic meaning does not necessarily mean king, caliph, sacred class, uncriticizable clergy, or absolute ruler. The expression is functional before anything else: people within the community who carry responsibility for managing an affair, resolving a matter, executing a work, or ordering part of collective life.

The qualification منكم is very important. The Qur'an does not say that أولي الأمر are a class outside you, above you, or separate from society. It says from among you. This qualification limits every pharaonic interpretation of أولي الأمر. They are of the community, they have meaning inside the community, and their responsibility must be measured in relation to truth, justice, and the real good of the people.

The structure of the verse is also worth attention. The Qur'an says:

أَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ وَأُولِي الْأَمْرِ مِنكُمْ

The verb أطيعوا comes for The God and is repeated for the Messenger, but it is not repeated before أولي الأمر. This point by itself does not determine the whole meaning, but from the structure of the verse it shows that obedience to أولي الأمر is not independent, essential, and unrestricted like obedience to The God. أولي الأمر have meaning under the system of obedience to The God and the Messenger, not as an independent source of truth.

The continuation of the verse clarifies the final reference point of dispute:

فَإِن تَنَازَعْتُمْ فِي شَيْءٍ فَرُدُّوهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَالرَّسُولِ

In this verse, whenever dispute occurs, أولي الأمر themselves are not the final reference. The Qur'an returns the matter to The God and the Messenger. So أولي الأمر are not an absolute final word; they too must be understood under the criterion of The God and the Messenger.

From here it becomes clear that أولي الأمر should not be turned into an instrument for sanctifying political or religious power. Whoever uses this word to present himself as owner of people's lives, wealth, conscience, and faith has passed beyond the structure of the verse itself. The verse does not create pharaonic authority, a sacred unaccountable class, or absolute rule. It speaks of responsibility, order, obedience within the frame of truth, and final return to the divine criterion.

The Amr of Pharaoh: A Misguiding and Ungrowing Command

The Qur'an also speaks of the amr of Pharaoh:

فَاتَّبَعُوا أَمْرَ فِرْعَوْنَ ۖ وَمَا أَمْرُ فِرْعَوْنَ بِرَشِيدٍ

So they followed the amr of Pharaoh; and the amr of Pharaoh was not rightly guiding.

Hud 11:97

This verse is very important, because it shows that not every أمر is right and guiding. Pharaoh's أمر too was a kind of direction and command, but the Qur'an says it was not rashid. That means command can lead people toward growth and truth, or toward rebellion, domination, and destruction.

This verse is also a warning for understanding أولي الأمر. Having an affair, command, position, or executive power does not by itself bring legitimacy. The criterion is rushd, truth, justice, and relation to divine guidance. A pharaonic amr, even if it comes from power, is not rashid.

Amr as Final Affair, Destiny, and Governance

In the story of Yusuf, it says:

وَاللَّهُ غَالِبٌ عَلَىٰ أَمْرِهِ

And The God is prevailing over His amr.

Yusuf 12:21

Here أمر points to the affair, governance, and work of The God through the path of events. Human beings plan, but the divine أمر passes through the surface of events and reaches the outcome The God wills.

In another verse it says:

إِنَّ الْأَمْرَ كُلَّهُ لِلَّهِ

Indeed, the whole amr belongs to The God.

Ali 'Imran 3:154

أمر here points to the reins of the affair, final governance, and true authority over the outcome. This use again shows that أمر is not only spoken command. It can be the concern and final outcome of affairs.

Similar Uses

أمر in the Qur'an has several fields, and they should not all be translated with one fixed word.

Sometimes أمر means command, as in إن الله يأمر بالعدل. Here “commands” is precise.

Sometimes أمر means work, affair, or matter, as in وأمرهم شورى بينهم. Here “their command” is not right; “their affair” or “their affairs” is better.

Sometimes أمر means divine governance and ordering, as in يدبر الأمر. Here أمر points to the flow of affairs and the ordering of existence.

Sometimes أمر means a decisive divine event or realization, as in أتى أمر الله. Here أمر is something that arrives and has effect.

Sometimes أمر means social responsibility, as in أولي الأمر. Here أمر belongs to the field of work, administration, decision, and responsibility, not absolute rule.

Sometimes commanding ma'ruf is the responsibility of calling to good and directing toward recognized fittingness. This use should not be merged with religious coercion, imposed fiqh, inspection of people, or social repression.

Sometimes i'timar means consultation and coordination, but its value depends on its direction. I'timar in ma'ruf is constructive; i'timar for killing and oppression is corrupting.

Sometimes أمر becomes pharaonic amr: a command and direction that is not rashid and drives society toward corruption and collapse.

Grammar and Structure

In يأمر بالعدل, the letter ba comes after أمر and shows that the command is directed toward a value or action. يأمر بالعدل means He commands justice and directs behavior toward justice.

In ينهى عن, prohibition comes with عن, meaning holding back and distancing from something. The contrast between يأمر بـ and ينهى عن in An-Nahl 16:90 shows the positive and negative direction of behavior.

In يدعون إلى الخير ويأمرون بالمعروف, calling to good comes before commanding ma'ruf. This order shows that commanding ma'ruf must remain in the field of good, not in the field of force and imposition.

In وأمرهم شورى بينهم, أمر comes as a noun and the pronoun refers back to the community. Here أمر means their collective affair and concern, not a command issued by them.

In وأتمروا بينكم بمعروف, form VIII carries the sense of participation and coordination. This is not one-sided command, but consultation and coordination between the sides.

In أولي الأمر منكم, the qualification منكم is important. Those entrusted with affairs must be from among the community itself, not a class separate from, outside, or above the people.

In An-Nisa 4:59, the verb أطيعوا comes for The God and the Messenger, but is not repeated before أولي الأمر. This structure shows that obedience to أولي الأمر is understood under obedience to The God and the Messenger, not as independent and unrestricted obedience.

In the same verse, the phrase فإن تنازعتم في شيء identifies the final reference of dispute. Wherever dispute occurs, it must be returned to The God and the Messenger; so أولي الأمر are not the final and unaccountable criterion.

In ألا له الخلق والأمر, creation and أمر come together. This pairing shows that أمر here is not only legislative command, but also points to divine ordering and governance.

In أتى أمر الله, the verb أتى pictures amr as something arriving. Here أمر is close to decisive event or realizing command.

In قل الروح من أمر ربي, the letter من shows that the ruh relates to a domain of the amr of the Lord. The context also speaks of the limitation of human knowledge.

In وما أمر فرعون برشيد, أمر is measured by the description rashid. This shows that command can be right or wrong, growth-giving or misleading.

Nearby Roots

ح ك م points to judging, measuring, preventing disorder, and putting each thing in its right place. أمر is command or effective affair; حكم is judgment and the establishing of right relation among affairs.

ق ض ي points to ending, settling, completing, or bringing an affair to completion. أمر can be command, affair, or work, but qada highlights the stage of decisiveness or completion.

ش ر ع points to opening a path, setting a clear way, or legislating. أمر can be command or affair; shar' relates more to the path opened for action. Ash-Shura 42:21 shows that religious legislation without permission from The God is a very dangerous field.

و ص ي points to advising and entrusting responsibility. Wasiyyah usually carries the color of continuity, emphasis, and entrusting; أمر is more direct and broader command.

ن ه ي is the opposite of أمر in behavior. أمر directs toward an action; prohibition restrains from an action or distances from a path.

د ب ر points to what is behind the affair, governance, attention to outcome, and staged administration. When the Qur'an says يدبر الأمر, أمر is the object, and tadbir is the method of ordering it.

س ل ط points to power, proof, or dominance. أمر may be joined with power, but the root itself points to command or the concern of an affair, not necessarily domination.

م ل ك points to ownership, power of disposal, or kingship. أمر can be in someone's hand, but ملك and أمر are not the same. Someone may possess apparent power while the true amr remains with The God.

ش أ ن points to concern, state, and important affair. Sha'n is close to the condition or standing of an affair; أمر also includes command and executive direction.

ع ر ف points to recognition, discernment, familiarity, and being known. Ma'ruf in the phrase commanding ma'ruf is an action that stands in the field of good and recognized fittingness. But the full study of ma'ruf belongs in the independent entry for ع ر ف.

ن ك ر points to unfamiliarity, reprehensibility, alienness, and being rejectable. Munkar in the phrase forbidding munkar is an action that conflicts with good and recognized fittingness. Its full study belongs in the independent entry for ن ك ر.

خ ل ف and khilafah point to coming after another, succession, and later responsibility. This root is not the same as أمر. أولي الأمر should not be equated with caliph, sultan, or owner of people's religion.

ر ش د points to right guidance, growth, guided maturity, and right direction. When the Qur'an says وما أمر فرعون برشيد, it shows that every command and affair must be measured by rushd and right direction.

أ م ر and أمارة can be close in the field of sign and direction toward a path, but أمارة should not be treated, without firm historical evidence, as the original source of all uses of أمر.

أمر: command, order, affair, matter, concern, event, effective ordering; depending on context.

أمر الله: the command of The God, the affair of The God, divine amr, decisive divine event, divine governance; depending on context.

لا يأمر بالفحشاء: does not command fahsha; does not direct behavior toward fahsha.

أمرهم شورى بينهم: their affair is by consultation among them; their affairs are consultative among themselves.

أولي الأمر منكم: those entrusted with the affair from among you; holders of responsibility among you; people from within the community who bear a work or concern from collective affairs.

يدبر الأمر: He manages the affair, orders the affairs, administers the flow of affairs in stages.

أتى أمر الله: the amr of The God has arrived, the decisive divine event has come, the realizing command of The God has come.

وكان أمر الله مفعولا: the amr of The God was to be done; the affair of The God was realizable.

إنما أمره: the concern of His realizing command; His amr in the station of realization.

الروح من أمر ربي: the ruh is from the amr of my Lord; the ruh belongs to the domain of my Lord's amr.

أتمروا بينكم بمعروف: consult and coordinate among yourselves in a recognized and fitting way.

يأتمرون بك: they deliberate and collude about you; they decide over your affair.

أمر فرعون: the command and path of Pharaoh; pharaonic direction; the unrashid amr of Pharaoh.

الأمور: affairs, works, concerns, reins of events.

إمرا: a hard affair, a weighty thing, an astonishing or reprehensible matter; depending on context.

Summary

The root أ م ر points to commanding, directing, and placing an action or affair on the path of effect. From this core, أمر expands to command, concern, work, matter, event, governance, and effective ordering.

At the material level, أمر is like a direction that brings scattered movement into a defined path, or an affair that must be managed, solved, or carried out. Its connection with أمارة can also strengthen the image of sign and indicator, but that connection should be understood with historical caution.

In the Qur'an, this root appears for ethical and worshipful commands, social affairs and consultation, divine amr in creation and governance, the ruh from the amr of the Lord, those entrusted with the affair, decisive divine event, and the unrashid amr of Pharaoh.

Its difference from حكم is that حكم is judgment and the establishing of right relation, while أمر is command or effective affair. Its difference from qada is that qada highlights completion and settlement, while أمر can appear before execution, during execution, or as the affair itself. Its difference from domination and ownership is that أمر does not necessarily mean ownership or absolute power.

In Forqan translation, أمر should not always be translated as “command.” In many contexts “affair,” “matter,” “concern,” “event,” “governance,” or “ordering of the affair” is more precise. Especially in the phrase أولي الأمر, a pharaonic and royal reading must be avoided, because the verse places those entrusted with the affair from within the community and returns the final reference of dispute to The God and the Messenger.

Also, in the phrase commanding ma'ruf and forbidding munkar, أمر must not become religious coercion, imposed human fiqh, or guardianship over people's faith. This compound must be understood within calling to good, no compulsion in din, justice, and avoiding legislation without permission from The God.
