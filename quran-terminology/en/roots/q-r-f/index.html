Qur’anic root qāf rā fāʾ (ق ر ف)

Peeling, acquiring, and performing an act attributable to a person

In early Arabic, ق ر ف concerns removing a skin or crust, taking something from a surface, contact and involvement, and acquiring or performing an act.

Historical and literary witness

The branch concerning peeling and the separation of a crust appears in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period. A verse transmitted under the name of ʿAntarah ibn Shaddād describes persistence in battle:

عُلَالَتُنَا فِي كُلِّ يَوْمِ كَرِيهَةٍ / بِأَسْيَافِنَا وَالْقَرْحُ لَمْ يَتَقَرَّفِ

In every day of fierce battle, our remaining strength is spent with our swords, while the wound’s crust has not yet peeled away.

Here يَتَقَرَّفِ refers to the peeling away of a wound’s crust. Some transmissions read الْجُرْحُ instead of الْقَرْحُ; both refer to a wound. The literary attribution does not independently establish the poem’s exact date.

The branch concerning acquisition and the performance of acts is clearly recorded in classical Arabic lexicons, and its Qur’anic uses attest it in the language of revelation. This study does not establish an independently dated pre-Qur’anic witness for that branch.

Forms examined: قَرَفَ، قَرْف، قِرْف، قِرْفَة، قَارَفَ، مُقَارَفَة، تَقَرَّفَ، اِقْتَرَفَ، اِقْتِرَاف، مُقْتَرِف and مَقْرُوف.

Qur’anic forms: the Form VIII verb اِقْتَرَفَ and its active participle مُقْتَرِف. There are five occurrences in four verses: two in 6:113, and one each in 6:120, 9:24, and 42:23.

1. Central meaning and physical image

In Arabic, قِرْف denotes bark or a crust on a surface. قَرَفَ الشَّجَرَةَ means to strip a tree’s bark, and قَرَفَ الْقَرْحَةَ means to remove a wound’s dried crust.

The family also names the crust that separates from bread and remains attached to the oven.

In Arabic, قِرْفَة also names cinnamon, an aromatic substance obtained from tree bark.

Alongside this physical image, ق ر ف can express approaching, coming into contact with, or becoming involved with something. مُقَارَفَة may refer to involvement in an act or contact with something.

In the branch concerning acquisition, قَرَفَ لِعِيَالِهِ means to earn a livelihood for one’s family, and اِقْتَرَفَ الْمَالَ means to acquire wealth.

Lexical explanations connect these uses with taking something away from a surface, or with contact, involvement, and something becoming associated with a person.

These images can illuminate acquisition and iqtirāf, but the historical sequence in which the senses developed is not firmly established.

2. Iqtirāf: from accepting deceptive speech to acting

وَلِتَصغىٰ إِلَيهِ أَفـِٔدَةُ الَّذينَ لا يُؤمِنونَ بِالـٔاخِرَةِ وَلِيَرضَوهُ وَلِيَقتَرِفوا ما هُم مُقتَرِفونَ

Wa-li-taṣghā ilayhi afʾidatu lladhīna lā yuʾminūna bi-l-ākhirati wa-li-yarḍawhu wa-li-yaqtarifū mā hum muqtarifūn.

And so that the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter may incline toward it, and that they may approve of it, and that they may do what they are doing.

Al-An'am 6:113

The preceding verse, al-Anʿām 6:112, speaks of enemies of the Prophets and the embellished, deceptive speech they suggest to one another.

The present verse places three states together:

  • The heart inclining toward deceptive speech.
  • Approving of and accepting it.
  • Proceeding to act.

وَلِيَقْتَرِفُوا is a Form VIII imperfect verb, subjunctive after the lām, with deletion of the final nūn. مُقْتَرِفُونَ is the masculine plural active participle of the same form.

The verb and participle in مَا هُمْ مُقْتَرِفُونَ associate the acts directly with those who perform them.

A person may hear deceptive speech without accepting it, or incline toward it without yet acting on it. In this verse, iqtirāf concerns proceeding to act following that inclination and acceptance.

For example, speech justifying oppression of a group may first find acceptance in the listener’s heart and then prepare the way for participation in that oppression.

Iqtirāf has a negative sense in this context because of the deceptive speech and the resulting acts, not because the root itself necessarily means wrongdoing.

3. Committing sin and human responsibility

وَذَروا ظٰهِرَ الإِثمِ وَباطِنَهُ إِنَّ الَّذينَ يَكسِبونَ الإِثمَ سَيُجزَونَ بِما كانوا يَقتَرِفونَ

Wa-dharū ẓāhira l-ithmi wa-bāṭinah. Inna lladhīna yaksibūna l-ithma sa-yujzawna bi-mā kānū yaqtarifūn.

Leave both outward sin and its inward aspect. Those who acquire sin will be recompensed for what they used to commit.

Al-An'am 6:120

This verse places two related roots side by side: ك س ب and ق ر ف.

In يَكْسِبُونَ الْإِثْمَ, sin is something a person acquires. بِمَا كَانُوا يَقْتَرِفُونَ concerns the acts they have performed and for which they will be recompensed.

One may read the acquisition of sin here as emphasizing the moral outcome of conduct, and iqtirāf as emphasizing engagement in the acts that yield it. This is a contextual distinction, not a fixed lexical boundary.

The verbs overlap semantically. One cannot always restrict one to the act itself and the other to its result.

The verse also does not restrict sin to outward conduct: it speaks of its outward and inward aspects. Iqtirāf here should therefore not be confined to visible bodily acts.

A person may outwardly avoid wrongdoing while secretly engaging in deception, conspiracy, or other conduct unknown to others.

Whether an act is public or hidden does not remove responsibility for it.

4. Acquiring wealth: the economic use

قُل إِن كانَ ءاباؤُكُم وَأَبناؤُكُم وَإِخوٰنُكُم وَأَزوٰجُكُم وَعَشيرَتُكُم وَأَموٰلٌ اقتَرَفتُموها وَتِجٰرَةٌ تَخشَونَ كَسادَها وَمَسٰكِنُ تَرضَونَها أَحَبَّ إِلَيكُم مِنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسولِهِ وَجِهادٍ فى سَبيلِهِ فَتَرَبَّصوا حَتّىٰ يَأتِىَ اللَّهُ بِأَمرِهِ وَاللَّهُ لا يَهدِى القَومَ الفٰسِقينَ

Qul in kāna ābāʾukum wa-abnāʾukum wa-ikhwānukum wa-azwājukum wa-ʿashīratukum wa-amwālun iqtaraftumūhā wa-tijāratun takhshawna kasādahā wa-masākinu tarḍawnahā aḥabba ilaykum mina llāhi wa-rasūlihi wa-jihādin fī sabīlihi fa-tarabbaṣū ḥattā yaʾtiya llāhu bi-amrih. Wa-llāhu lā yahdī l-qawma l-fāsiqīn.

Say: If your fathers, your sons, your brothers, your spouses, your clan, wealth you have acquired, trade whose decline you fear, and dwellings you find pleasing are dearer to you than The God and His Messenger and striving in His way, then wait until The God brings His command. The God does not guide the defiantly disobedient people.

At-Tawbah 9:24

اقْتَرَفْتُمُوهَا is a Form VIII perfect verb addressing a plural subject. Its attached feminine pronoun refers back to أَمْوَال, wealth.

Here iqtirāf means obtaining or acquiring wealth.

The word itself does not imply that the wealth was acquired unlawfully. In Arabic, it can describe wealth gained through work, trade, and other means of earning.

The Qur’an places three economic circumstances together: acquired wealth, trade whose decline is feared, and dwellings that give satisfaction.

The verse does not condemn earning wealth, loving one’s family, or possessing a home and a trade. It condemns placing these attachments above The God, His Messenger, and striving in His way.

Acquired wealth may represent years of work. That relationship between people and the fruits of their effort can produce a powerful attachment to possessions.

Yet the effort expended in acquiring something is not sufficient reason to place it above truth and justice.

5. Acquiring a good deed: clear evidence against an exclusively negative meaning

ذٰلِكَ الَّذى يُبَشِّرُ اللَّهُ عِبادَهُ الَّذينَ ءامَنوا وَعَمِلُوا الصّٰلِحٰتِ قُل لا أَسـَٔلُكُم عَلَيهِ أَجرًا إِلَّا المَوَدَّةَ فِى القُربىٰ وَمَن يَقتَرِف حَسَنَةً نَزِد لَهُ فيها حُسنًا إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفورٌ شَكورٌ

Dhālika lladhī yubashshiru llāhu ʿibādahu lladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū l-ṣāliḥāt. Qul lā asʾalukum ʿalayhi ajran illā l-mawaddata fī l-qurbā. Wa-man yaqtarif ḥasanatan nazid lahu fīhā ḥusnā. Inna llāha ghafūrun shakūr.

That is what The God announces as good news to His servants who believe and do righteous deeds. Say: I ask you for no reward for it, except affection in kinship. Whoever performs a good deed, We increase its goodness for them. Indeed, The God is Forgiving, Appreciative.

Ash-Shura 42:23

This verse provides particularly clear evidence for understanding iqtirāf in the Qur’an. “Affection in kinship” follows one interpretation of الْمَوَدَّةَ فِي الْقُرْبَىٰ; the phrase has also been interpreted as affection for close relatives. That interpretive question does not alter the explicit use of iqtirāf with a good deed.

يَقْتَرِفْ is a jussive imperfect verb in a conditional construction, and حَسَنَةً is its object. نَزِدْ supplies the response to the condition.

The same verb used in al-Anʿām for wrongful conduct and in al-Tawbah for acquiring wealth is used here for performing a good deed.

Iqtirāf occurs frequently in Arabic in contexts of sin and wrongdoing. That frequency does not restrict its meaning to committing sin.

Al-Shūrā 42:23 not only recognizes the performance of a good deed through iqtirāf, but also speaks of increasing its goodness.

The increase comes from نَزِدْ and the divine promise; it should not be made part of the lexical meaning of iqtirāf itself.

Righteous action and the acquisition of a good deed appear together in this verse. A person acts rightly, and that good is recognized as their deed and attainment.

In al-Shūrā 42:20, The God likewise speaks of increasing the harvest of someone who desires the harvest of the Hereafter.

In both images, a person’s good can grow beyond its initial measure and yield a greater return.

6. Comparison with related roots

ق ر ف (iqtirāf) and ك س ب (kasb)

Kasb concerns seeking, acquiring, and obtaining something. The Qur’an uses it for wealth, conduct, sin, good, and human responsibility.

Iqtirāf belongs to this semantic field and may concern acquiring wealth or performing a good or bad act.

Their conjunction in al-Anʿām 6:120 shows the close relationship between performing acts and acquiring sin.

Kasb nevertheless has a wider distribution in Qur’anic usage, repeatedly expressing human acquisition and the consequences that return to a person.

In contexts of conduct, iqtirāf emphasizes engaging in an act and performing it; in its economic use it also means acquiring or obtaining.

ق ر ف (iqtirāf) and ج ر ح (ijtirāḥ)

The root ج ر ح denotes wounding in its physical use, and acquiring or performing acts in another branch.

Al-Jāthiyah 45:21 speaks of acquiring evil deeds:

أَم حَسِبَ الَّذينَ اجتَرَحُوا السَّيِّـٔاتِ أَن نَجعَلَهُم كَالَّذينَ ءامَنوا وَعَمِلُوا الصّٰلِحٰتِ سَواءً مَحياهُم وَمَماتُهُم ساءَ ما يَحكُمونَ

Am ḥasiba lladhīna jtaraḥū l-sayyiʾāti an najʿalahum ka-lladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū l-ṣāliḥāti sawāʾan maḥyāhum wa-mamātuhum. Sāʾa mā yaḥkumūn.

Or do those who have committed evil deeds suppose that We will make them like those who believe and do righteous deeds, their life and death being alike? How badly they judge!

Al-Jathiyah 45:21

Ijtirāḥ and iqtirāf are close in expressing the performance of wrongful acts.

The Qur’an also uses iqtirāf for a good deed. Ijtirāḥ in al-Jāthiyah concerns evil deeds, but that difference does not show that ijtirāḥ necessarily has a negative meaning throughout Arabic.

The physical image of jarḥ involves making a wound, while that of qarf involves removing a skin or crust. This lexical difference should not be turned into two distinct theories about the spiritual effects of sin.

ق ر ف (iqtirāf) and ع م ل (ʿamal)

ʿAmal denotes work or an act and its performance by an agent.

In contexts of conduct, iqtirāf overlaps with performing and acquiring an act.

In al-Shūrā 42:23, belief and righteous action characterize the servants who receive good news, while acquiring a good deed appears in the promise of increasing its goodness.

ʿAmal is a more general expression for human action, whereas iqtirāf may also foreground acquisition and the attribution of an act to a person.

ق ر ف (iqtirāf) and أ ث م (ithm)

Ithm concerns sin and its moral liability, while iqtirāf expresses performing or acquiring something.

In al-Anʿām 6:120, sin is the object of acquisition, and the people’s acts of iqtirāf are described in relation to it.

Iqtirāf may concern sin, but it may also concern a good deed or wealth. Sin is therefore one possible object of iqtirāf, not its definition.

7. Translation choices for Forqan

Depending on its object and context, اِقْتَرَفَ may be translated “acquired,” “earned,” “performed,” or “committed.”

  • In al-Anʿām 6:113, the verb concerns doing the acts attributed to the people described; the participle names those who perform them.
  • In al-Anʿām 6:120, “what they used to commit” fits the context of sin.
  • In al-Tawbah 9:24, “wealth you have acquired” fits the economic context.
  • In al-Shūrā 42:23, “whoever performs a good deed” preserves the positive object.

“Committing” is suitable in a context of sin. In contemporary Persian, however, its equivalent ارتکاب has a negative association and is not a natural choice for acquiring wealth or performing a good deed. English should likewise follow the object and context.

Brief root meaning: Removing a skin or crust; contact and involvement; and, in the branch of iqtirāf, acquiring or performing an act attributable to its agent, whether good, bad, or material.

Conclusion

In Arabic, ق ر ف concerns peeling, contact and involvement, acquisition, and performing an act.

The Qur’an uses this root only as the Form VIII verb and its active participle. Its uses cover three fields: wrongful conduct, acquiring wealth, and performing a good deed.

Iqtirāf does not in itself mean committing sin. The explicit association with a good deed in al-Shūrā 42:23 rules out that restriction.

Whether iqtirāf is good or bad depends on what a person acquires or does. Just as one may acquire sin, one may also acquire a good deed whose goodness The God increases.
