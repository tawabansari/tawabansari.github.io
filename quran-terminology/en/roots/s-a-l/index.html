1. Short Introduction

The root س أ ل in the Qur'an denotes turning to another in order to receive something. What is sought may be information, an answer, wealth, help, benefit, an action, or accountability for conduct. Therefore it cannot always be translated only as “to ask”; depending on structure and context it may mean asking, requesting, seeking, or questioning in accountability. The short definition is: س أ ل denotes turning to another to receive an answer, knowledge, a thing, or an action; in judgment it expands into seeking an answer concerning conduct.

2. Forms Examined

The examined forms are: سَأَلَ, يَسْأَلُ, اسْأَلْ, سُئِلَ, يُسْأَلُ, تَسَاءَلَ, يَتَسَاءَلُونَ, سُؤَال, سُؤْل, سَائِل, and مَسْؤُول.

3. Reminder About the Root

Only forms that truly belong to س أ ل are included. إسرائيل is not an Arabic word built from this root and should not be analyzed as “one who asks The God.” Forms such as سَوَّلَتْ, سَوَّلَ, and تَسْوِيل belong to the separate root س و ل. A possible semantic resemblance between desire and making something attractive to the self is not enough to merge the roots.

4. Semantic Core

In all central uses, someone seeks something from another. The thing sought may be tangible or intangible: knowledge of a truth, an answer, receiving something, requesting an action, or receiving an explanation about conduct. Asking and requesting are therefore linked: informational asking seeks an answer; practical or financial asking seeks a thing or action; accountability seeks an answer about conduct.

5. Base Image of the Root

The base image is a needy, unaware, questioning, or demanding person turning toward someone from whom the answer or desired thing is sought. He may ask what happened, ask someone to give something, ask someone to act, or ask someone to answer for conduct. In short: seeking something from an addressee; structure and context specify what is sought.

6. Three Main Fields of Use

The three main fields are: asking for knowledge, requesting to receive something, and questioning in accountability. In asking for knowledge, the structure سأل عن شيء marks the topic with عن. In requesting, the person wants wealth, food, bounty, reward, forgiveness, or an action. In accountability, the question occurs in judgment and seeks an answer about conduct; this is still connected to the base meaning because an answer is sought.

7. Grammatical Structures of the Verb Sa-ala

The meaning of سأل depends on who asks, whom he asks, what is requested, which preposition is used, and the surrounding context. سأل عن usually means asking or being questioned about a topic. سأل الشخص makes the person asked the direct object. سأل الشيء may make the requested thing the direct object. With two objects, سأل شخصا شيئا means requesting something from someone. يسألكموها has two object pronouns, كم and ها; فيحفكم is a separate verb.

يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْأَهِلَّةِ

They ask you about the crescents.

Al-Baqarah 2:189

وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الرُّوحِ

And they ask you about the spirit.

Al-Isra 17:85

عَمَّا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

About what they used to do.

Al-Hijr 15:93

سَلْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ

Ask the Children of Israel.

Al-Baqarah 2:211

فَاسْأَلْهُ مَا بَالُ النِّسْوَةِ

Then ask him what was the matter of the women.

Yusuf 12:50

فَإِنَّ لَكُمْ مَا سَأَلْتُمْ

Then you shall have what you asked for.

Al-Baqarah 2:61

وَآتَاكُمْ مِنْ كُلِّ مَا سَأَلْتُمُوهُ

And He gave you from everything you asked of Him.

Ibrahim 14:34

8. Asking With Two Objects

When requesting something from someone, the first object is the person asked and the second object is the thing requested. سَأَلْتُهُمْ أَجْرًا means I asked them for a reward.

إِنْ يَسْأَلْكُمُوهَا فَيُحْفِكُمْ تَبْخَلُوا

If He asks you for them and presses you, you would be stingy.

Muhammad 47:37

9. “yas’alunaka”: The People’s Questions

The Qur'anic pattern يسألونك... قل shows people asking the Prophet about many matters. Asking itself is not condemned. The Qur'an reports and answers many questions. Sometimes the answer corrects the direction of the question and gives what is more beneficial. Sometimes the answer makes clear that not every knowledge is available to human beings.

يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْأَهِلَّةِ قُلْ هِيَ مَوَاقِيتُ لِلنَّاسِ وَالْحَجِّ

They ask you about the crescents. Say: they are time-markers for people and for the Hajj.

Al-Baqarah 2:189

يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ السَّاعَةِ أَيَّانَ مُرْسَاهَا

They ask you about the Hour: when is its anchoring?

Al-Araf 7:187

10. Asking to Remove Ignorance

Asking can remove ignorance. فاسألوا أهل الذكر means ask those who have knowledge of the relevant matter when you do not know. It does not mean accepting every claimant without examination; asking does not replace reasoned evaluation.

فَاسْأَلُوا أَهْلَ الذِّكْرِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

So ask the people of the remembrance if you do not know.

An-Nahl 16:43

11. Questions That Create Burden

Al-Maidah 5:101 does not forbid all questions. It forbids a kind of unnecessary, burden-making question that may turn what was pardoned into difficult obligation or create grounds for denial after disclosure. The cow narrative shows how repeated questions can make a simple command difficult. This should not silence scientific, ethical, or critical questions.

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَسْأَلُوا عَنْ أَشْيَاءَ إِنْ تُبْدَ لَكُمْ تَسُؤْكُمْ

O you who have faith, do not ask about things which, if made clear to you, would distress you.

Al-Maidah 5:101

12. Requesting Without Knowledge

In Hud 11:46, سؤال is request, not merely informational asking. Noah had made a request concerning his son. The verse teaches that request must be joined with humility before knowledge; a person may want something emotionally without knowing its truth or outcome.

فَلَا تَسْأَلْنِ مَا لَيْسَ لَكَ بِهِ عِلْمٌ

So do not ask Me for that of which you have no knowledge.

Hud 11:46

13. Requesting From The God

واسألوا الله من فضله means ask The God from His bounty. The context warns against envying advantages given to others. The right path is not claiming another’s share, but asking from The God’s bounty. Ar-Rahman 55:29 shows that all in the heavens and earth ask Him; this may be by speech, state, or need, but “existential need” remains explanation, not replacement for translation.

وَاسْأَلُوا اللَّهَ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ

And ask The God from His bounty.

An-Nisa 4:32

يَسْأَلُهُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ

Whoever is in the heavens and the earth asks Him.

Ar-Rahman 55:29

14. Requesting a Reward

A repeated use is the denial of requesting a reward from people for delivering the message: وما أسألكم عليه من أجر. Here سؤال clearly means requesting, and the message is not a tool for indebtedness or financial authority.

15. Sa’il: Asker or Requester

سائل is an active participle: one who asks or requests. Its meaning is contextual. In Al-Ma'arij 70:1 it is one who requests punishment. In Ad-Duha 93:10, the requester should not be driven away; the term can include someone seeking aid or guidance. In Adh-Dhariyat 51:19, the financial context makes سائل the one who states need and asks for help, but “beggar” is unnecessarily limiting and degrading.

سَأَلَ سَائِلٌ بِعَذَابٍ وَاقِعٍ

A requester asked for a punishment that will occur.

Al-Ma'arij 70:1

وَأَمَّا السَّائِلَ فَلَا تَنْهَرْ

And as for the requester, do not drive him away.

Ad-Duha 93:10

وَفِي أَمْوَالِهِمْ حَقٌّ لِلسَّائِلِ وَالْمَحْرُومِ

And in their wealth was a right for the requester and the deprived.

Adh-Dhariyat 51:19

16. Su’l: The Requested Thing

سؤل is the thing requested. In Ta-Ha 20:36, Moses is told that his request has been granted. It refers to the requests he had made, not merely an informational question.

قَالَ قَدْ أُوتِيتَ سُؤْلَكَ يَا مُوسَىٰ

He said: You have been given your request, O Moses.

Ta-Ha 20:36

17. Su’al: The Act of Asking or Requesting

سؤال is the verbal noun and can denote asking or requesting. Translation must choose by context: question, request, demand, or accountability-questioning.

18. Tasa’ala: Mutual Questioning

Form VI can indicate mutual participation: they asked one another. An-Naba 78:1 and As-Saffat 37:27 show this reciprocal questioning.

عَمَّ يَتَسَاءَلُونَ

About what are they asking one another?

An-Naba 78:1

وَأَقْبَلَ بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ يَتَسَاءَلُونَ

And some of them turned toward others, asking one another.

As-Saffat 37:27

19. Tasa’aluna Bihi

In An-Nisa 4:1, تساءلون به is not a scholarly question about The God. People ask one another by invoking His name or sanctity and the ties of kinship. The role of ب here is not identical with سأل بعذاب; each structure must be read separately.

وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ الَّذِي تَسَاءَلُونَ بِهِ وَالْأَرْحَامَ

And have taqwa of The God, by whom you ask one another, and of the womb-ties.

An-Nisa 4:1

20. Mas’ul

مسؤول is the passive participle: someone or something toward which question, request, or accountability is directed. It may mean object of question, object of accountability, answerable, or requested. Hearing, sight, and heart are asked about; the covenant is asked about; the promise can be requested.

إِنَّ السَّمْعَ وَالْبَصَرَ وَالْفُؤَادَ كُلُّ أُولَٰئِكَ كَانَ عَنْهُ مَسْئُولًا

Indeed, hearing, sight, and heart: each of those will be asked about.

Al-Isra 17:36

وَكَانَ عَهْدُ اللَّهِ مَسْئُولًا

And the covenant of The God will be asked about.

Al-Ahzab 33:15

كَانَ عَلَىٰ رَبِّكَ وَعْدًا مَسْئُولًا

It is a promise upon your Lord, requested.

Al-Furqan 25:16

21. Questioning and Responsibility

Responsibility is connected with being answerable: an action is done, it is asked about, a person must answer, and answerability reveals responsibility. But the abstract noun “responsibility” should not replace مسؤول in every verse.

22. Accountability in Judgment

Divine questioning in judgment is not to acquire unknown knowledge. It manifests answers, establishes proof, fixes responsibility, and enacts judgment. The topic of accountability is often marked by عن, such as deeds or blessing.

فَلَنَسْأَلَنَّ الَّذِينَ أُرْسِلَ إِلَيْهِمْ وَلَنَسْأَلَنَّ الْمُرْسَلِينَ

We will surely question those to whom messengers were sent, and We will surely question the messengers.

Al-Araf 7:6

فَوَرَبِّكَ لَنَسْأَلَنَّهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ عَمَّا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

So by your Lord, We will surely question all of them about what they used to do.

Al-Hijr 15:92-93

ثُمَّ لَتُسْأَلُنَّ يَوْمَئِذٍ عَنِ النَّعِيمِ

Then, that day, you will surely be questioned about the blessing.

At-Takathur 102:8

23. Is There Questioning on the Day of Rising or Not?

Verses about no questioning and verses about questioning address different functions or settings. In Ar-Rahman 55:39, criminals are recognized by their marks; identifying them does not require questioning. Full staging should not be invented without decisive text.

فَيَوْمَئِذٍ لَا يُسْأَلُ عَنْ ذَنْبِهِ إِنْسٌ وَلَا جَانٌّ

So on that day no human and no jann will be asked about his sin.

Ar-Rahman 55:39

24. “He Is Not Questioned”

Al-Anbiya 21:23 separates The God as Judge from creation. He is not under a higher court, while creatures are answerable. This does not mean His action lacks wisdom or justice; it means there is no higher authority over Him.

لَا يُسْأَلُ عَمَّا يَفْعَلُ وَهُمْ يُسْأَلُونَ

He is not questioned about what He does, while they are questioned.

Al-Anbiya 21:23

25. Asking a Place

Yusuf 12:82 says to ask the town and caravan. The buildings do not speak; the intended meaning is asking the people of the place. Translation may preserve the image while explaining the intent.

وَاسْأَلِ الْقَرْيَةَ الَّتِي كُنَّا فِيهَا وَالْعِيرَ الَّتِي أَقْبَلْنَا فِيهَا

And ask the town in which we were, and the caravan in which we came.

Yusuf 12:82

26. Asking the Idols

In Al-Anbiya 21:63, asking the idols is tied to whether they can speak. Their inability to answer exposes their inability to be final objects of need and worship.

فَاسْأَلُوهُمْ إِنْ كَانُوا يَنْطِقُونَ

So ask them, if they speak.

Al-Anbiya 21:63

27. Questioning to Expose Contradiction

Questions such as “who created the heavens and earth?” expose contradiction between verbal confession and behavior. سؤال can therefore be an argumentative tool, not merely a request for information.

28. Questioning the Truthful

Even truthfulness is questioned in the field of action, covenant, and loyalty. عن صدقهم marks the subject of accountability.

لِيَسْأَلَ الصَّادِقِينَ عَنْ صِدْقِهِمْ

So that He may question the truthful about their truthfulness.

Al-Ahzab 33:8

29. Questioning the Victim

In At-Takwir 81:8-9, the questioned one is the victim, not the criminal. The question reveals her innocence and exposes the killers. Being questioned does not always mean being accused.

وَإِذَا الْمَوْءُودَةُ سُئِلَتْ بِأَيِّ ذَنْبٍ قُتِلَتْ

And when the girl buried alive is asked for what sin she was killed.

At-Takwir 81:8-9

30. Requesting With Insistence

In Al-Baqarah 2:273, insistence is supplied by إلحافا, not by سأل itself. This matters for Al-Ma'arij 70:1: haste or insistence should not be inserted into every سأل بـ structure without contextual proof.

لَا يَسْأَلُونَ النَّاسَ إِلْحَافًا

They do not ask people with insistence.

Al-Baqarah 2:273

31. Comparison With Nearby Words

س أ ل differs from د ع و, ط ل ب, استفهام, and حاسب. دعا is calling and turning; سؤال presents the request. طلب is seeking or pursuing; سؤال is often seeking from an addressee. استفهام is informational interrogation, while سؤال is broader. حاسب is reckoning; سؤال in judgment may be part of reckoning but is not identical to it.

32. The Role of Prepositions

سأل عن marks the topic of question or accountability. سأل من فضله marks the field from which the request is made. سأل به must be read separately in each construction. With two objects, the first is the person asked and the second is the requested thing. Passive forms focus on the one or thing toward which question or accountability is directed.

33. Sa’ala Bihi

The role of ب must be examined in each structure: in An-Nisa 4:1 it means asking one another by His name or sanctity; in Al-Ma'arij 70:1 it connects the requester to punishment. A single mechanical meaning should not be extracted from both.

34. Tasa’aluna Bihi

In An-Nisa 4:1, تساءلون به is not a scholarly question about The God. People ask one another by invoking His name or sanctity and the ties of kinship. The role of ب here is not identical with سأل بعذاب; each structure must be read separately.

سَأَلَ سَائِلٌ بِعَذَابٍ

Someone requested the punishment or called it forth.

Al-Ma'arij 70:1

35. Sa’ala With Two Objects

When requesting something from someone, the first object is the person asked and the second object is the thing requested. سَأَلْتُهُمْ أَجْرًا means I asked them for a reward.

36. Passive Forms

Passive forms such as سئل, يسأل, and تسألون focus on the one or thing questioned. Al-Baqarah 2:134 shows that one group does not automatically bear another group’s deeds; each is answerable for his own conduct.

وَلَا تُسْأَلُونَ عَمَّا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

And you will not be questioned about what they used to do.

Al-Baqarah 2:134

37. Must Every Question Be Answered?

Qur'anic answers differ: sometimes direct, sometimes corrective, sometimes limiting human knowledge, and sometimes forbidding useless or burden-making questions. Not every question is blameworthy, and not every question creates a right to every detail. Topic, motive, need, and consequence matter.

38. Is Asking a Sign of Weakness?

The Qur'an invites asking from The God and directs the ignorant to ask the knowledgeable. سؤال itself is not weakness; it may show recognition of ignorance, awareness of need, humility, search for truth, or demand for right. In other contexts it may arise from stubbornness, mockery, needless complication, or haste.

39. Challenges of Understanding and Translation

Main translation challenges include translating every case as “ask,” every case as “request,” confusing asking with accountability, limiting سائل to beggar, translating سؤل as question, always translating مسؤول as responsible, treating يسألكموها as three-object, including سولت under س أ ل, and assigning fixed insistence to سأل بـ.

40. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

Translation suggestions: سأل عن: asked about, or was questioned about in judgment; سأل شخصا: asked someone; سأل شخصا شيئا: requested something from someone; يسألونك: they ask you or request from you according to context; واسألوا الله من فضله: ask The God from His bounty; فاسألوا أهل الذكر: ask the people of remembrance; لا أسألكم عليه أجرا: I do not ask you for a reward; لتسألن عما كنتم تعملون: you will surely be questioned about what you used to do; السائل: asker, requester, or needy requester in financial context; سؤلك: your request; مسؤول: object of question, accountability, answerable, or requested.

41. Mas’ul

مسؤول is the passive participle: someone or something toward which question, request, or accountability is directed. It may mean object of question, object of accountability, answerable, or requested. Hearing, sight, and heart are asked about; the covenant is asked about; the promise can be requested.

42. Final Definitions of the Forms

Final definitions: سأل means turning to another to receive an answer, knowledge, thing, or action; يسأل asks, requests, or questions in judgment; تساءل means mutual asking or requesting by the name/sanctity of something; سؤال is the act of asking or requesting; سؤل is the requested thing; سائل is one who asks or requests; مسؤول is someone or something toward whom question, request, or accountability is directed.

43. Conclusion

The root س أ ل denotes seeking something from an addressee. What is sought may be an answer, knowledge, explanation, wealth, goods, bounty, an action, or answerability. From this arise asking, requesting, and accountability-questioning. The shared meaning is seeking something from an addressee; عن usually marks the topic; two-object structures have person and requested thing; يسألكموها has two objects; سائل may be asker or requester; سؤل is request; مسؤول varies by context; سولت and إسرائيل do not belong to the Arabic root س أ ل; insistence and haste require contextual evidence; the Qur'an distinguishes questions for understanding from burden-making questions; divine questioning in judgment manifests responsibility, not ignorance. Final definition: س أ ل denotes turning to another to receive what is sought with him, whether answer, knowledge, wealth, bounty, or action; in judgment this becomes accountability for conduct.
