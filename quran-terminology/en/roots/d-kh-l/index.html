Qur'anic Root D KH L

Entering from outside into a place, group, condition, or domain; or causing another to enter it.

1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root D KH L moves in the semantic field of entering, causing to enter, and moving from outside a boundary into its inside.

In its simplest use, a person passes through a gate, house, city, garden, or another space and comes to be within it. Yet Qur'anic usage is not limited to physical entry. Faith can enter the heart, people can enter the religion of The God, a person can be entered into mercy or among the righteous, and an oath can become an inward corruption between people.

Therefore, “inside” in this root is not always a bodily space. At times it is a social, religious, ethical, or existential domain. Entry also does not always mean permanent residence. A person may enter upon someone for a visit, enter a house for a time, or enter his garden. Duration, security, acceptance, and lasting residence must be understood from context, not from the verb alone.

In form IV, أَدْخَلَ, the agent causes another to enter or places him within a condition:

أَدْخِلْ يَدَكَ فِي جَيْبِكَ

Insert your hand into your bosom.

وَأَدْخِلْنَا فِي رَحْمَتِكَ

And enter us into Your mercy.

From the same family, the noun دَخَل appears in the Qur'an, but not with the meaning of entering. In the context of covenants, it points to corruption, defect, or a hidden unsound intent that makes the relation unhealthy from within.

Short meaning: to move from outside into a place, group, condition, or domain; or to cause another to enter it. In the nominal form دَخَل: hidden corruption, defect, or unsound intent inside a relation.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

The root D KH L appears 126 times in the Qur'an in six forms: 76 times as the simple verb دَخَلَ, 42 times as the form IV verb أَدْخَلَ, twice as the noun دَخَل, twice as the active participle دَاخِلُون, three times as مُدْخَل, and once as the form VIII expression مُدَّخَل.

دَخَلَ / يَدْخُلُ

The simple verb, intransitive or transitive in Arabic usage, points to entering a place, coming in upon a person, being placed among a group, or entering a state.

ادْخُلُوا هَٰذِهِ الْقَرْيَةَ

Enter this habitation.

دَخَلُوا عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ

They entered upon Yusuf.

يَدْخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ اللَّهِ

They enter into the religion of The God.

أَدْخَلَ / يُدْخِلُ

Form IV means causing another to enter or placing someone in a place, group, condition, or mercy.

يُدْخِلْهُ جَنَّاتٍ

He enters him into gardens.

أَدْخِلْنَا فِي رَحْمَتِكَ

Enter us into Your mercy.

أَدْخِلْ يَدَكَ فِي جَيْبِكَ

Insert your hand into your bosom.

دَخَل

A noun that appears twice in An-Nahl:

تَتَّخِذُونَ أَيْمَانَكُمْ دَخَلًا بَيْنَكُمْ

In this construction, دَخَل points to corruption, defect, deception, or unsound intent that turns oaths into a means of ruining trust between people.

دَاخِلُون

A plural active participle:

مَعَ الدَّاخِلِينَ

With those who enter.

مُدْخَل

This form belongs to form IV and, depending on construction, may indicate an entry, a place of entry, or the quality of being entered. It appears in the Qur'an in expressions such as مُدْخَلَ صِدْقٍ, مُدْخَلًا كَرِيمًا, and مُدْخَلًا يَرْضَوْنَهُ. In all three, the meaning is not merely a physical gate; the quality or status of the entry is also present.

مُدَّخَل

A form VIII form, once in At-Tawbah 9:57, pointing to a place or way of entering and hiding.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of D KH L is crossing the boundary between outside and inside and coming to be on the inner side of that boundary.

The boundary may be physical: a city gate, house, mosque, prison, garden, or the bosom of a garment. It may be social: entering upon a person, being among a people, or joining the righteous servants. It may be religious or inward: entering the religion of The God, faith entering the heart, or entering into silm. It may be eschatological: entering the Garden, entering the Fire, or being entered into mercy.

The shared point in all these uses is passage from outside to inside. But the result of that passage is not always the same. Entry may be secure, accompanied by peace, fearful, forbidden, corrupting, or the result of final judgment. Security, refuge, surrender, complete absorption, or permanent residence cannot be extracted from the root in every occurrence. Those elements are understood from other words and structures in the context.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The clearest material image of this root is passing through a door. A person stands outside a house. Between him and the inner space there is a wall and a threshold. When he passes through the door, he is no longer outside; he is within the house.

وَادْخُلُوا الْبَابَ سُجَّدًا

Enter the gate in prostrating humility.

لَا تَدْخُلُوا بُيُوتًا غَيْرَ بُيُوتِكُمْ

Do not enter houses other than your own houses.

ادْخُلُوا عَلَيْهِمُ الْبَابَ

Enter upon them through the gate.

In this image, the crossing itself is central. After entry, the relation to the inner space may differ: the person may be a guest, a resident, an attacker, or only a visitor. Therefore, permanent settlement is not a necessary part of the meaning of dakhala.

وَأَدْخِلْ يَدَكَ فِي جَيْبِكَ

Insert your hand into your bosom.

This simple use with Musa shows that the core is movement into an inner space, without needing to add notions such as refuge, safety, or absorption into an environment.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, دَخَلَ المكانَ or دَخَلَ فِي المكان points to entering a place. دَخَلَ عَلَيْهِ means he entered upon him or came to him. دَخَلَ بِالْمَرْأَة refers to the consummation of marital relations; this construction appears in the Qur'an in An-Nisa 4:23. أَدْخَلَهُ المكان means he made him enter the place.

Alongside the verb of entry, Arabic lexicographers record for دَخَل and دَخِل meanings of corruption, unsoundness, defect, and unhealthy inwardness. Dakhal may indicate something outwardly sound but inwardly corrupt or defective. This fits the Qur'anic construction دَخَلًا بَيْنَكُمْ: oaths become a means of creating defect and corruption in mutual trust.

One should not explain this noun by presenting unsupported stories such as a worm hidden in a tree trunk, a blight in a bird's feather, or moisture in the core of a fruit as the certain origin of the word. Later Persian uses such as “dakhl,” “modakheleh,” and “income” are also not enough to determine the Qur'anic meaning.

7. Translation Challenges and Restricting the Meaning

The simplest translation of dakhala is “to enter.” This works in many verses, but some structures need more precision. دَخَلُوا عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ must not be translated “they entered into Yusuf”; the preposition على builds the meaning of entering upon someone and coming into his presence. دَخَلْتُمْ بِهِنَّ cannot be translated literally as “you entered with them”; in Arabic it refers to marital consummation. وَلَمَّا يَدْخُلِ الْإِيمَانُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ is not bodily entry; faith has not yet settled inwardly in their hearts.

At the same time, not every nonphysical entry should be translated with expressions such as “dissolving completely into the atmosphere of the destination.” The Qur'an uses independent words for intensity, comprehensiveness, and duration. Another challenge is translating دَخَلًا only as “plot.” Plot may be part of the result, but in An-Nahl 16:92 and 16:94, the word points to making covenants unhealthy and turning oaths into a means of defect, deception, and social corruption.

8. Methodological Note

Several levels must be distinguished: entry itself, the manner of entry, the destination of entry, and the result of entry. Entry itself is crossing from outside to inside. The manner may be with peace, safety, fear, permission, or force. The destination may be a house, city, religion, heart, mercy, Garden, or Fire. The result may be residence, visit, joining, punishment, rescue, or access.

Manner, destination, and result should not be merged into the fixed meaning of the verb. Prepositions must also be read separately: دَخَلَ المكانَ, دَخَلَ فِي الشيء, دَخَلَ عَلَى الشخص, and دَخَلَ بِالمرأة do not all carry the same constructional force. Yet these differences should not become mechanical rules; direct object does not always mean “quick and complete entry,” and في does not always mean deep dissolution into an environment.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

Entering the habitation and the gate

ادْخُلُوا هَٰذِهِ الْقَرْيَةَ فَكُلُوا مِنْهَا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمْ رَغَدًا وَادْخُلُوا الْبَابَ سُجَّدًا

Enter this habitation and eat from it wherever you wish in ease, and enter the gate in prostrating humility.

Al-Baqarah 2:58

In one verse, two objects appear: entering the qaryah and entering the gate. The qaryah is the general destination; the gate is the way of entry. The verb alone does not say that they will reside there forever.

Entering the sacred land

يَا قَوْمِ ادْخُلُوا الْأَرْضَ الْمُقَدَّسَةَ الَّتِي كَتَبَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ

O my people, enter the sacred land that The God has written for you.

Al-Ma'idah 5:21

إِنَّا لَنْ نَدْخُلَهَا حَتَّىٰ يَخْرُجُوا مِنْهَا

We will never enter it until they leave it.

Al-Ma'idah 5:22

ادْخُلُوا عَلَيْهِمُ الْبَابَ

Enter upon them through the gate.

Al-Ma'idah 5:23

The contrast ندخلها / يخرجوا منها is clear: one group's entry is tied to another group's exit. The further command also becomes entry into confrontation and responsibility, but that added force comes from context.

Secure entry into Egypt

ادْخُلُوا مِصْرَ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ آمِنِينَ

Enter Egypt, if The God wills, secure.

Yusuf 12:99

Security is understood from آمنين, not from the verb ادخلوا. This verse shows that entry can be described with safety; safety is not a fixed component of the root.

Entering houses with permission

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَدْخُلُوا بُيُوتًا غَيْرَ بُيُوتِكُمْ حَتَّىٰ تَسْتَأْنِسُوا وَتُسَلِّمُوا عَلَىٰ أَهْلِهَا

O you who have faith, do not enter houses other than your own houses until you seek familiarity and permission and greet their people.

An-Nur 24:27

فَإِنْ لَمْ تَجِدُوا فِيهَا أَحَدًا فَلَا تَدْخُلُوهَا حَتَّىٰ يُؤْذَنَ لَكُمْ

If you do not find anyone in them, do not enter them until permission is given to you.

An-Nur 24:28

Entry into private space is limited by the rights of others. Permission comes from the surrounding verbs and conditions, not from the root itself.

Entering upon people

كُلَّمَا دَخَلَ عَلَيْهَا زَكَرِيَّا الْمِحْرَابَ وَجَدَ عِنْدَهَا رِزْقًا

Whenever Zakariyya entered upon her in the sanctuary, he found provision with her.

Al 'Imran 3:37

The structure دخل عليها means he came to Maryam and entered upon her, not into her body.

Entry into prison, garden, and settlement

وَدَخَلَ مَعَهُ السِّجْنَ فَتَيَانِ

Two young men entered the prison with him.

Yusuf 12:36

وَدَخَلَ جَنَّتَهُ وَهُوَ ظَالِمٌ لِنَفْسِهِ

He entered his garden while wronging himself.

Al-Kahf 18:35

إِنَّ الْمُلُوكَ إِذَا دَخَلُوا قَرْيَةً أَفْسَدُوهَا

Kings, when they enter a habitation, corrupt it.

An-Naml 27:34

In each case, the verb marks entry; the value and result are supplied by context: imprisonment, arrogance, or corrupting domination.

Faith entering the heart and people entering the religion

قُولُوا أَسْلَمْنَا وَلَمَّا يَدْخُلِ الْإِيمَانُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ

Say: We have submitted; for faith has not yet entered your hearts.

Al-Hujurat 49:14

وَرَأَيْتَ النَّاسَ يَدْخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ اللَّهِ أَفْوَاجًا

And you see the people entering the religion of The God in groups.

An-Nasr 110:2

These verses show that outward entry and inward entry are not identical. People may enter a religious community, while faith entering the heart is another matter.

Entry into mercy, among the righteous, the Garden, and the Fire

وَأَدْخِلْنَا فِي رَحْمَتِكَ وَأَنْتَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ

Enter us into Your mercy; You are the most merciful of the merciful.

Al-A'raf 7:151

وَأَدْخِلْنِي بِرَحْمَتِكَ فِي عِبَادِكَ الصَّالِحِينَ

Enter me, by Your mercy, among Your righteous servants.

An-Naml 27:19

فَادْخُلِي فِي عِبَادِي ۝ وَادْخُلِي جَنَّتِي

So enter among My servants, and enter My Garden.

Al-Fajr 89:29

فَادْخُلُوا أَبْوَابَ جَهَنَّمَ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا

So enter the gates of Jahannam, abiding therein.

An-Nahl 16:29

The same root is used for entry into mercy, the righteous, the Garden, and the Fire. The root itself is ethically neutral; the destination and context determine the value of the entry.

Madkhal and hidden dakhal

وَقُلْ رَبِّ أَدْخِلْنِي مُدْخَلَ صِدْقٍ وَأَخْرِجْنِي مُخْرَجَ صِدْقٍ

Say: My Lord, enter me with an entry of truth and bring me out with an exit of truth.

Al-Isra 17:80

تَتَّخِذُونَ أَيْمَانَكُمْ دَخَلًا بَيْنَكُمْ

You take your oaths as hidden corruption between yourselves.

An-Nahl 16:92

مُدْخَلَ صِدْقٍ asks for an entry, way of entering, or station of entry joined to truth. دَخَلًا بَيْنَكُمْ shows the other branch: an inward defect or corrupt intent hidden inside social relations.

10. Examination of Important Qur'anic Derivatives

دَخَلَ is the most frequent form. Its main constructions include entering a place, entering upon a person, entering a religion or group, an inward state entering the heart, and the idiom دخل بها for marital consummation. This diversity shows that translation must attend to construction.

أَدْخَلَ indicates causing another to enter. The agent may be The God, a human being, or a person with respect to his own body part: يُدْخِلُهُ جَنَّاتٍ, أَدْخِلْ يَدَكَ, أَدْخِلْنِي مُدْخَلَ صِدْقٍ. Form IV by itself does not always mean guidance, powerful driving, or compulsion; the type of causation is known from context.

دَخَل appears in the Qur'an only in the context of oaths. This stable use brings the translation close to hidden defect, corruption, and social deception.

دَاخِلُون is the active participle for those who enter: ادْخُلَا النَّارَ مَعَ الدَّاخِلِينَ.

مُدْخَل appears with description or qualification: truth, noble, and pleasing. This shows that the quality, value, or station of entry is being emphasized.

مُدَّخَل appears only in At-Tawbah 9:57 beside refuge and caves. The meaning “a place or way to enter and hide” is suitable in that context.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

The verb can take a direct object of place: دَخَلَ الْمَدِينَةَ, ادْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ, دَخَلَ جَنَّتَهُ. This is normal Arabic and does not by itself prove speed, ease, or completeness of entry.

In ادْخُلُوا فِي السِّلْمِ, يَدْخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ اللَّهِ, and يَدْخُلِ الْإِيمَانُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ, في marks the domain or container of entry. It may occur in physical and nonphysical usage; it is not by itself a sign of total absorption.

دَخَلَ عَلَىٰ marks entry upon a person: دَخَلُوا عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ, دَخَلُوا عَلَىٰ دَاوُودَ, دَخَلَ عَلَيْهَا زَكَرِيَّا. دَخَلَ مِنْ marks route or point of entry. دَخَلَ بِـ may indicate state or accompaniment, as in دَخَلُوا بِالْكُفْرِ, or a specific idiom of marital relations, as in دَخَلْتُمْ بِهِنَّ.

مُدْخَلَ صِدْقٍ may be understood as a verbal noun, noun of place, or something close to the quality of entry. In every case, its annexation to truth makes the entry or station of entry truthful and authentic.

12. D KH L Compared with KH R J

KH R J is the closest direct opposite of D KH L. Dakhala means moving from outside to inside; kharaja means moving from inside to outside. The Qur'an places them together in expressions such as دَخَلُوا بِالْكُفْرِ وَهُمْ قَدْ خَرَجُوا بِهِ, أَدْخِلْنِي مُدْخَلَ صِدْقٍ وَأَخْرِجْنِي مُخْرَجَ صِدْقٍ, and إِنَّا لَنْ نَدْخُلَهَا حَتَّىٰ يَخْرُجُوا مِنْهَا. Entry and exit mark two directions across a boundary, whether physical, social, or spiritual.

13. D KH L Compared with W L J

W L J also concerns entering, but often highlights entering through an opening, slit, or inner passage. The Qur'an says يَعْلَمُ مَا يَلِجُ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَمَا يَخْرُجُ مِنْهَا and حَتَّىٰ يَلِجَ الْجَمَلُ فِي سَمِّ الْخِيَاطِ. In the second verse, entry through the eye of the needle is foregrounded. Dakhala is the more general word for entry into houses, cities, religion, the Garden, the Fire, and human groups. It is not safe to say that wuluj always means gradual, slippery, and hidden entry while dukhul always means open and complete entry.

14. D KH L Compared with N F DH

N F DH is close to passing through, penetrating, or having the ability to cross a boundary or barrier:

إِنِ اسْتَطَعْتُمْ أَنْ تَنْفُذُوا مِنْ أَقْطَارِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ فَانْفُذُوا

Nafadh may foreground crossing a limit without making the inner destination and residence the main point. Dukhul emphasizes arriving inside. As a tendency: dukhul is reaching the inside; nafadh is passing through a limit or barrier. But nafadh should not always be defined as entering from one side and leaving from the other; each use must be read separately.

15. D KH L Compared with A T Y

A T Y points to coming and arriving. D KH L marks a more specific stage: crossing from outside to inside. A person may arrive at a city without entering it. Thus, أتى means he came or reached, while دخل means he crossed the boundary and entered. In the story of Khidr, حَتَّىٰ إِذَا أَتَيَا أَهْلَ قَرْيَةٍ says they came to the people of a habitation; not every أتى can be replaced with دخل.

16. D KH L Compared with H L L

H L L may point to settling, alighting, or taking residence in a place. Dakhala foregrounds the act of entry itself. One may enter a place without residing in it; one may settle somewhere while the moment of entry is not the topic. Thus: dakhala is crossing inward; halla is alighting or settling.

17. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For دَخَلَ: entered, went in, came in; in دخل عليه, entered upon him or came to him; in social contexts, came to be among them.

For ادْخُلُوا: enter, go in, come into. For nonphysical domains such as religion, silm, or a human group, “enter into” or “come into” may be more natural.

For دَخَلَ فِي: entered into it; came into it; became included among it; found a way into it.

For دَخَلَ عَلَىٰ: entered upon him; came to him; came into his presence.

For دَخَلَ بِهَا in An-Nisa 4:23: consummated marital relations with her. A literal translation is not suitable.

For أَدْخَلَ: entered, made enter, caused to enter, placed within, included among. For أَدْخِلْنِي فِي عِبَادِكَ الصَّالِحِينَ: enter me among Your righteous servants, or place me among Your righteous servants.

For دَخَل: hidden corruption, inner defect, means of deception, unsound intent, means of ruining trust. In An-Nahl 16:92: you make your oaths a means of corruption and deception among yourselves.

For مُدْخَلَ صِدْقٍ: truthful entry, entry of truth, true station of entry, entry joined with truth. For مُدْخَلًا كَرِيمًا: noble entry, honored station, noble place of entry. For مُدْخَلًا يَرْضَوْنَهُ: a station they will be pleased with; an entry they will approve; a pleasing place of entry. For مُدَّخَلًا: a place of entering, a way to hide, a tight refuge into which one may enter.

18. Conclusion

The root D KH L in the Qur'an revolves around crossing from outside to inside. A proposed final definition is: to cross the boundary of a place, group, condition, or domain and come to be on its inner side; and in form IV, to cause another to enter it.

The Qur'an uses this root for entering houses, cities, habitations, mosques, prisons, gardens, the Garden, and the Fire. Faith can enter the heart, people can enter the religion of The God, and a human being can be entered into mercy or among the righteous. This range shows that “inside” is not only physical space; a group, religion, mercy, or final state can also be pictured as a domain one enters.

Yet security, refuge, complete surrender, permanent residence, or absorption into the destination should not be extracted from the verb itself. The Qur'an expresses those features through independent words such as آمنين, خالدين فيها, بسلام, and في رحمتنا.

The root is ethically neutral. Entry may be into the Garden or the Fire, into a house with permission or without permission, into a habitation for reform or for corruption, and among the righteous or among the people of the Fire. The value of the entry comes from destination, intention, and manner.

The noun دَخَل in An-Nahl highlights another branch: an oath or covenant may look sound outwardly, but through corrupt intent, group advantage, and betrayal, it becomes a means of defect and deception among people. مُدْخَل also shows that the Qur'an does not measure only the destination; the quality of entry can be truthful, noble, or pleasing.
