Warmth and protection from cold: from feeling warm to clothing and warming livestock products

1. Introducing the root and its semantic field

Arabic triliteral D F ʾ denotes warmth in contrast with cold. Difʾ names warmth that protects a person from cold or leaves them warm and comfortable.

In early Arabic, this family was not limited to the sensation of warmth. Difʾ could name clothing, covering, or something used to keep oneself warm.

Saying that someone has no difʾ, for example, may mean they lack sufficient clothing or covering against cold.

In pastoral language, difʾ also described livestock products and benefits, especially wool, camel hair, and other animal hair. These materials provided garments, blankets, floor coverings, and coverings protecting people from cold.

Dictionaries also record a broader use encompassing animals’ offspring, milk, and other benefits derived from them.

This matters: the root should neither be restricted to bodily heat nor have every economic benefit treated as its basic meaning.

The root occurs only once in the Qur’an, as difʾun in Al-Naḥl 16:5.

The verse speaks of creating livestock and names three aspects of human benefit: warmth, various advantages, and eating from them.

Qur’anic usage therefore requires distinguishing warmth itself, its means of provision, and livestock’s other benefits.

It also calls for explaining why difʾ is named separately from benefits, although warmth is itself a livestock benefit.

2. Root forms and Qur’anic usage

The Qur’anic form

D F ʾ occurs once in the Qur’an, in one form:

Difʾun — Al-Naḥl 16:5

Difʾ is a singular, indefinite, nominative noun.

In lakum fīhā difʾun, difʾun may be the postponed subject and fīhā its preceding predicate.

The construction thus states that livestock provide warmth for people.

Livestock products and materials may supply this warmth, but difʾ alone does not name a particular garment.

The family’s other forms do not occur in the Qur’an.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: sole occurrence in Al-Naḥl 16:5

Recorded Arabic forms

Early sources record these D F ʾ forms:

Dafiʾa: became warm, felt warmth, or protected oneself from cold through a covering.

Dafuʾa: was or became warm, especially of weather, a day, night, or place.

Difʾ: warmth, and something producing warmth.

Dafaʾ, dafāʾ, and dafāʾah: verbal-noun forms connected with becoming warm.

Dafīʾ: warm or having mild warmth.

Dafʾān: a person who is warm or protected from cold.

Dafʾā: the feminine of dafʾān.

Adfaʾa: warmed another or supplied a means of warming them.

Tadaffaʾa: warmed oneself.

Istadfaʾa: used something for warmth or sought warmth.

Iddafaʾa: another form for warming oneself with clothing.

Mudfiʾah: in livestock usage, a numerous herd capable of providing warmth.

Mudfaʾah: in some uses, livestock protected from cold by abundant hair or fat.

Adfāʾ: a plural of difʾ in the sense of warming things.

These belong to one linguistic field, but their morphological and semantic differences should remain visible.

Dafiʾa, for example, concerns the condition of someone who becomes warm, whereas adfaʾa may express warming someone else.

Istadfaʾa likewise concerns using or seeking a means of warmth.

None of these verbs occurs in the Qur’an. Only the noun difʾun appears.

3. The semantic core in early Arabic

Warmth in contrast with cold

Ibn Fāris writes in Maqāyīs al-Lughah:

الدَّالُ وَالْفَاءُ وَالْهَمْزَةُ أَصْلٌ وَاحِدٌ يَدُلُّ عَلَى خِلَافِ الْبَرْدِ

al-dālu wa-al-fāʾu wa-al-hamzatu aṣlun wāḥidun yadullu ʿalā khilāfi al-bardi

“Dāl, fāʾ, and hamzah have one principle indicating the opposite of cold.”

He continues:

فَالدِّفْءُ خِلَافُ الْبَرْدِ

fa-al-difʾu khilāfu al-bardi

“Difʾ is the opposite of cold.”

This provides a clear starting point for the study.

Difʾ concerns a condition in which cold diminishes or a person is protected from its effects.

It is not necessarily identical with extremely intense heat.

In ordinary use, pleasantly warm weather may be called dafīʾ, while scorching or uncomfortable heat may receive other descriptions.

Difʾ should therefore not invariably be taken to mean intense or burning heat.

Nor does the root itself specify whether warmth comes from sunlight, fire, a living body, clothing, or another source.

The source of warmth and means of protection from cold require the sentence’s context.

Maqāyīs al-Lughah: warmth in contrast with cold

Human warmth and warm weather

Dictionaries record distinctions between some verbal forms used for people and those used for weather.

Of a person, Arabic says:

“Dafiʾa al-rajulu.”

The person became warm or protected themselves from cold.

For weather and time, forms such as dafuʾa yawmunā are recorded: our day became warm.

Ibn Fāris also notes differences in ordinary usage for people and time.

One semantic family may thus have different morphological formations for different subjects in Arabic.

This should not become an absolute rule excluding any other form from every Arabic dialect.

Dictionaries record several variants of the verb’s inflection.

Warmth and the means of warming

Lisān al-ʿArab states:

وَالِاسْمُ الدِّفْءُ، بِالْكَسْرِ، وَهُوَ الشَّيْءُ الَّذِي يُدْفِئُكَ

wa-al-ismu al-difʾu bi-al-kasri wa-huwa al-shayʾu alladhī yudfiʾuka

“The noun difʾ, with kasrah, is the thing that warms you.”

It also records:

مَا عَلَيْهِ دِفْءٌ

mā ʿalayhi difʾun

Meaning that the person wears nothing to keep them warm.

Difʾ here is not restricted to an inner sensation: it also names clothing or a means that provides warmth.

This distinction particularly matters for Al-Naḥl 16:5.

When the Qur’an says lakum fīhā difʾun, the direct meaning “warmth” can remain while clothing and livestock products are recognized as instances.

It does not follow that the word means only clothing.

Warmth is the shared outcome or property of various things used to protect oneself from cold.

Lisān al-ʿArab and Al-Ṣiḥāḥ: warmth and warming items

4. Covering, shelter, and protection from cold

Difʾ al-ḥāʾiṭ: a wall’s warm shelter

Ibn Fāris gives an example:

وَاقْعُدْ فِي دِفْءِ هَذَا الْحَائِطِ، أَيْ كِنِّهِ

wa-uqʿud fī difʾi hādhā al-ḥāʾiṭi ay kinnihi

“Sit in the difʾ of this wall: its shelter.”

The wall may protect someone from cold wind and provide a calmer, warmer place.

Difʾ thus need not concern directly producing heat.

Warmth is sometimes obtained through protection from a cooling influence.

A wall, for example, may be neither fire nor a heat source, yet create a better place to sit by blocking cold wind.

Clothing may perform a similar function.

Wool clothing does not necessarily generate new heat; by reducing heat transfer and retaining bodily warmth, it helps a person stay warm.

The lexical use of difʾ therefore concerns both warmth and retaining warmth.

Taking shelter should nevertheless not be made the independent meaning of every root form.

Shelter from wind is one way of obtaining warmth.

Warming clothes and coverings

When Arabic says:

“Thawbun dhū difʾin.”

It means a garment providing warmth.

This use relates to wool, camel hair, and other clothing materials.

Animal hair and wool were important sources of clothing and blankets in pastoral life.

Warm clothing is nevertheless not livestock’s only benefit.

Wool and hair can provide mats, tents, ropes, and other items.

Not all these products are necessarily used for warmth.

Distinguish livestock’s raw materials, the goods made from them, and the warmth provided by some of those goods.

5. Difʾ in Arabic pastoral language

Wool, camel hair, and other hair

Lisān al-ʿArab transmits from Thaʿlab:

الدِّفْءُ مَا أَدْفَأَ مِنْ أَصْوَافِ الْغَنَمِ وَأَوْبَارِ الْإِبِلِ

al-difʾu mā adfaʾa min aṣwāfi al-ghanami wa-awbāri al-ibili

Difʾ is what provides warmth from sheep’s wool and camels’ hair.

This definition directly relates to Al-Naḥl 16:5.

People can turn livestock wool and hair into materials and clothing protecting them from cold.

Livestock are presented here as a source of warmth.

They need not, however, be a direct human heat source in the same manner as fire.

In the verse’s more salient application, livestock products enable protection from cold.

Other Qur’anic verses support this reading, especially Al-Naḥl 16:80, which explicitly mentions livestock hides, wool, camel hair, and other hair.

Warmth from the animals’ presence

Dictionaries also record ibilun mudfiʾatun for numerous camels.

The explanation is that many animals gathered together benefit from each other’s bodily heat and breath.

Warmth here therefore comes not merely from clothing: living beings’ presence also relates to warmth.

Ibilun mudfaʾatun is also recorded for animals having sufficient hair or fat against cold.

These uses are close without being identical.

One emphasizes numerous animals and warmth from gathering together.

The other concerns the animals’ natural covering and capacity to retain warmth.

D F ʾ thus had a broad field of use in Arabic pastoral life.

Not all these applications should be simultaneously included in translating difʾun in Al-Naḥl 16:5.

Al-Muḥkam and Lisān al-ʿArab: mudfiʾah and mudfaʾah

A poetic witness from Al-Shammākh

Al-Shammākh ibn Ḍirār, an Arabic poet who lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods, says of his livestock:

وَكَيْفَ يُضِيعُ صَاحِبُ مُدْفِئَاتٍ
عَلَى أَثْبَاجِهِنَّ مِنَ الصَّقِيعِ

wa-kayfa yuḍīʿu ṣāḥibu mudfiʾātin / ʿalā athbājihinna mina al-ṣaqīʿi

“How could the owner of such well-covered, warming camels neglect them, with [protective covering] upon their backs against frost?” This rendering follows yuḍīʿu as neglecting or wasting; the bracketed phrase supplies the image’s implied element.

Mudfiʾāt describes camels in this verse.

Dictionaries record variant forms with kasrah and fatḥah on fāʾ.

On one explanation, the camels’ abundance and products provide their owner with warmth. Both mudfiʾāt and mudfaʾāt are transmitted. Camels warming one another through their breath must also be distinguished from their owner obtaining warmth through the herd’s products.

On another explanation, warmth comes from the animals’ own hair and bodily fat.

This matters because D F ʾ could describe both animals providing warmth for humans and animals possessing natural protection from cold.

Al-Shammākh lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods. Without dating this particular verse, it should not be treated as certainly pre-Islamic evidence.

The verse nevertheless illustrates this family’s pastoral use near the period of revelation.

Al-Basīṭ: Al-Shammākh’s witness and the mudfiʾāt/mudfaʾāt variants

Difʾ as livestock offspring and benefits

Maqāyīs al-Lughah transmits from Al-Umawī, an early transmitter and lexicographer:

الدِّفْءُ عِنْدَ الْعَرَبِ نِتَاجُ الْإِبِلِ وَأَلْبَانُهَا وَالِانْتِفَاعُ بِهَا

al-difʾu ʿinda al-ʿarabi nitāju al-ibili wa-albānuhā wa-al-intifāʿu bihā

“Difʾ among the Arabs means camels’ offspring, their milk, and benefiting from them.”

This definition extends beyond warmth in a narrow sense.

On that account, some pastoral uses of difʾ could express a range of livestock benefits.

This application should nevertheless be handled cautiously in Al-Naḥl 16:5.

The verse itself places difʾ alongside manāfiʿ.

This overlap does not make the broader reading linguistically impossible: a particular benefit may be foregrounded and followed by a more general term. However, this analysis would produce greater overlap between difʾ and manāfiʿ.

Nor does that necessarily rule out a linguistic reading, since Arabic may name one benefit specifically and then mention other benefits collectively.

The verse’s structure and nearby evidence nevertheless favor warmth and its means of provision.

The broader pastoral use should therefore remain in the lexical article, while Qur’anic translation preferably conveys the direct meaning “warmth.”

Maqāyīs al-Lughah: Al-Umawī’s broader pastoral usage

6. Qur’anic usage: Al-Naḥl 16:5

Al-Naḥl 16:5

وَالْأَنْعَامَ خَلَقَهَا لَكُمْ فِيهَا دِفْءٌ وَمَنَافِعُ وَمِنْهَا تَأْكُلُونَ

wa-al-anʿāma khalaqahā lakum fīhā difʾun wa-manāfiʿu wa-minhā taʾkulūna

“And the livestock He created; for you in them are warmth and benefits, and from them you eat.”

This is the sole Qur’anic occurrence of D F ʾ.

The verse first speaks of creating livestock.

Lakum fīhā difʾun wa-manāfiʿu follows: these animals provide warmth and various benefits for people.

The ending also mentions eating from them.

The verse thus recalls several forms of benefiting from livestock.

Difʾ in this verse

In light of the dictionaries, difʾ here may concern warmth and the means derived from livestock for protection from cold.

Important examples include garments, blankets, and coverings made from wool, camel hair, and other animal hair.

These examples plainly relate to life in cold, mountainous, and desert environments.

The noun difʾ itself does not mean wool or camel hair.

Those are materials from which warming items may be made.

In direct translation, “warmth” is therefore preferable to restricting the word to one material or particular product.

An explanation may add that this warmth is especially provided through clothing and items derived from livestock.

Difʾ and manāfiʿ: why name both?

In the expression:

“Fīhā difʾun wa-manāfiʿu.”

Manāfiʿ belongs to N F ʿ.

Difʾ concerns warmth; manāfiʿ expresses a range of other benefits.

This pairing can be understood as naming a particular benefit alongside varied benefits.

People obtain warmth and clothing from livestock, for example, while also benefiting from milk, carrying loads, labor, offspring, and other resources.

These examples should not all be treated as a closed list constituting the direct meaning of manāfiʿ.

Manāfiʿ is deliberately broad and can have different instances in different settings.

Conversely, translating difʾ simply as “benefits” loses the particular feature the Qur’an names separately.

The verse specifically mentions warmth and then other benefits.

Wa-minhā taʾkulūna: eating from livestock

The verse ends by speaking of eating from livestock.

This expression differs from difʾ.

Difʾ concerns warmth.

Manāfiʿ expresses varied benefits.

Wa-minhā taʾkulūna concerns use for food.

The three may be related but should not be merged in translation.

Difʾ should not, for instance, be restricted here to milk or meat, since use for food is clarified through the independent expression wa-minhā taʾkulūna.

Nor can D F ʾ itself establish rulings on the permissibility or prohibition of all kinds of animal products.

Such questions require the relevant verses.

Creating livestock and responsible use

Livestock are presented as created beings from which people may benefit.

Benefiting does not mean every treatment of animals is morally right regardless of harm, waste, or destruction of resources.

Nor should khalaqahā lakum establish that people absolutely own nature without responsibility toward creation.

These matters require examining verses about blessings, waste, corruption on earth, and human responsibility.

For a root article, it suffices to establish difʾ as a named livestock benefit, without deriving an entire human-animal ethic from this word.

Al-Ṭabarī: warmth, clothing, and livestock benefits

7. Connections with other Qur’anic verses

Al-Naḥl 16:80: wool, hair, and coverings

Al-Naḥl 16:80 is the most important related verse for Al-Naḥl 16:5.

It includes:

وَمِنْ أَصْوَافِهَا وَأَوْبَارِهَا وَأَشْعَارِهَا أَثَاثًا وَمَتَاعًا إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

wa-min aṣwāfihā wa-awbārihā wa-ashʿārihā athāthan wa-matāʿan ilā ḥīnin

“And from their wool, camel hair, and other hair, furnishings and enjoyment for a time.”

The verse explicitly identifies materials obtained from livestock.

Wool and hair may provide clothing, blankets, tents, and other items.

Al-Naḥl 16:80 is thus among the clearest internal Qur’anic evidence for instances of difʾ in Al-Naḥl 16:5.

Two levels of meaning should nevertheless remain distinct.

Difʾ in Al-Naḥl 16:5 expresses warmth.

Aṣwāf, awbār, and ashʿār in Al-Naḥl 16:80 are materials derived from livestock.

Their relationship is clear, but their lexical meanings are not identical.

Dwellings and protection from cold

Alongside wool and hair, Al-Naḥl 16:80 mentions dwellings made from livestock hides, usable during travel and settled residence.

This relates to nomadic life.

Such coverings may protect people from wind, rain, cold, or heat.

Difʾ itself does not mean a house or tent.

Houses and tents are means that may provide warmth and shelter under particular conditions.

This distinction fits early difʾ al-ḥāʾiṭ: warmth or shelter provided by a wall.

Al-Naḥl 16:81: clothing and protection

Al-Naḥl 16:81 mentions sarābīl protecting people from heat.

Qur’anic clothing therefore concerns more than warmth against cold.

Clothing can serve different protective functions in different circumstances.

Difʾ in Al-Naḥl 16:5 emphasizes warmth.

Protection from heat is expressed separately in Al-Naḥl 16:81.

Difʾ should therefore not be defined as every kind of protection against any natural influence.

Warmth is this root’s particular field, though warming items may have other uses.

Al-Naḥl 16:6 and Al-Naḥl 16:7: beauty and carrying loads

Al-Naḥl 16:6 speaks of livestock’s beauty when brought home and taken out to pasture.

Al-Naḥl 16:7 mentions carrying people’s loads to distant lands.

These passages clarify some livestock benefits.

Jamāl and ḥaml nevertheless do not belong to difʾ’s lexical meaning.

Warmth, beauty, and carrying loads may belong to one range of human livestock benefits, yet each is expressed through its own root and wording.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:21: benefits and food

Al-Muʾminūn 23:21 also presents livestock as a lesson and mentions milk, benefits, and eating from them.

The Qur’an thus speaks in several surahs of livestock’s role in human life.

D F ʾ does not occur in that verse, however.

It helps explain livestock’s overall benefits without constituting another occurrence of the root.

8. Broader Arabic uses

Warmth as generosity and provision

Lisān al-ʿArab and Asās al-Balāghah also discuss adfaʾahu for generous giving.

In this use, someone gives another something bringing relief and comfort.

Some accounts likewise use difʾ for a gift or grant.

This may relate to an image of warmth and comfort through provision.

It does not establish that difʾ in Al-Naḥl 16:5 directly means a financial gift.

The verse concerns livestock and benefiting from them.

Warmth fits that context more clearly.

Warmth and numerous livestock

Arabic can describe numerous livestock through mudfiʾah.

Numerous livestock are not inherently the meaning of difʾ.

A person might have a large, economically valuable herd, while a particular expression emphasizes its number.

With mudfiʾah, the relation between abundance and warmth comes from animals gathering together, their coverings, or benefits supplied to their owner.

Abundance and warmth are thus related concepts that should not become equivalents in every use.

Warmth and curvature

Some lexical sources record al-dafaʾ for curvature.

Ibn Fāris suggests a possible connection with covering and bending something over another thing.

Something bending over and covering another, for example, may protect it from cold.

This is a proposed explanation of the meanings’ relationship.

It does not show that Qur’anic difʾ originally meant bending.

Lexical D F W also has uses concerning long or curved wings, horns, and necks.

Distinguish these similar forms from hamzated D F ʾ.

Asās al-Balāghah and Lisān al-ʿArab: generosity, numerous animals, and curvature

9. Differences from related words

D F ʾ (difʾ) and B R D (barada): warmth and cold

These contrast in the field of temperature.

Difʾ concerns warmth and protection from cold.

Bard refers to coldness.

Qur’anic bard can express coldness, while some uses relate to shade, relief, or drink.

Difʾ occurs only once, in the context of livestock and warmth derived from them.

The roots’ difference should therefore not become a complete classification of every kind of temperature.

Study of B R D

D F ʾ (difʾ) and S KH N (sakhuna): comfortable warmth and heat

Arabic S KH N concerns becoming warm or hot.

Difʾ likewise concerns warmth.

In ordinary use, however, difʾ more readily suggests warmth against cold or warmth bringing human comfort.

S KH N, by contrast, may simply describe a high temperature or something becoming hot.

This is a tendency rather than an absolute rule.

A warm object may be described as either dafīʾ or sākhin, depending on the feature being emphasized.

Al-Naḥl 16:5 nevertheless concerns warmth from livestock and warming items, rather than their becoming intensely hot.

D F ʾ (difʾ) and Ḥ R R (ḥarra): warmth and heat

Arabic ḥarr refers to heat and may involve its intensity and discomfort.

Difʾ also means warmth, but in clothing and shelter uses it foregrounds protection from cold.

Al-Naḥl 16:81 mentions clothing providing protection from ḥarr.

The Qur’an thus uses different terms for different thermal and protective conditions.

Difʾ should not therefore indiscriminately become every possible kind of heat without attention to context.

Study of Ḥ R R

D F ʾ (difʾ) and K N N (kanna): warmth and shelter

Arabic K N N concerns covering and shelter.

In difʾ al-ḥāʾiṭ, a wall’s shelter may provide a warmer place.

Shelter and warmth are nevertheless different concepts.

Shelter may protect from wind, rain, sunlight, danger, or others’ sight.

Difʾ concerns warmth.

Protection from cold is therefore a shared setting for these families, but neither replaces the other in every use.

D F ʾ (difʾ) and Ṣ W F (ṣūf): warmth and wool

Ṣūf names wool.

Wool may be a material for warm garments and blankets.

Difʾ does not itself name wool as a material.

This distinction is clear across Al-Naḥl 16:5 and Al-Naḥl 16:80.

Al-Naḥl 16:5 speaks of warmth; Al-Naḥl 16:80 speaks of raw materials from which household goods are made.

Translating difʾ as wool thus highlights one instance and restricts its broader meaning of warmth.

D F ʾ (difʾ) and N F ʿ (nafaʿa): warmth and benefit

Nafʿ has a broad field concerning advantage and benefit.

Difʾ is one particular human benefit.

Both words occur together in Al-Naḥl 16:5.

Naming warmth alongside benefits may therefore emphasize an important provision among various benefits.

In the preferred reading of this verse, however, difʾ does not mean the entirety of livestock benefits; the broader recorded pastoral sense should be distinguished from this contextual preference.

Nor should benefit be restricted to warmth.

Study of N F ʿ

D F ʾ (difʾ) and D F W (dafā): two similar families

Dictionaries also record D F W.

Some of its uses concern length and curvature of wings, horns, or necks.

Hamzated D F ʾ, by contrast, has warmth as its established core.

Though hamzah may be weakened in some dialects or pronunciations, that alone does not justify equating every use of the families.

Qur’anic difʾun has hamzah and belongs to D F ʾ.

D F ʾ (difʾ) and D F ʿ (dafaʿa): warmth and pushing

These share their first two consonants but have different third consonants.

D F ʾ concerns warmth.

D F ʿ concerns pushing, warding off, and in some uses handing something over.

Warm clothing may protect people from cold’s effects, but this does not mean difʾ derives from dafʿ.

Similarity between protection and warding off danger does not sufficiently establish shared derivation.

10. Translation proposals for Forqan

Al-Naḥl 16:5

Arabic text:

وَالْأَنْعَامَ خَلَقَهَا لَكُمْ فِيهَا دِفْءٌ وَمَنَافِعُ وَمِنْهَا تَأْكُلُونَ

wa-al-anʿāma khalaqahā lakum fīhā difʾun wa-manāfiʿu wa-minhā taʾkulūna

The current Persian Forqan translation, rendered in English:

“And the livestock He created; for you in them are warmth and benefits, and from them you eat.”

This translation fits the root’s basic meaning.

Warmth conveys the outcome of using clothing and warming materials while avoiding restriction to a particular product.

Other equivalents may also be considered:

“Heat” is a direct, natural equivalent.

“Provision of warmth” emphasizes supplying warmth but is slightly more interpretive.

“Warm clothing” identifies an important instance while limiting the meaning to clothing.

“Warming items” fits lexical mā yudfiʾ, but emphasizes the means providing warmth rather than warmth itself.

“Wool and camel hair” names raw materials used for warming items, rather than directly translating difʾ.

Warmth is therefore appropriate for the base translation.

The root explanation may add that this warmth can come through garments, blankets, tents, and other items derived from livestock.

That explanation should not become an interpretive addition to the concise translation itself.

Non-Qur’anic forms

Dafiʾa: became warm or protected oneself from cold.

Dafuʾa al-yawmu: the day became warm.

Tadaffaʾa bi-al-thawbi: warmed oneself with clothing.

Istadfaʾa: used something for warmth or sought warmth.

Thawbun dhū difʾin: a warming garment.

Ibilun mudfiʾatun: a numerous herd providing warmth, depending on usage.

Difʾ al-ḥāʾiṭ: warmth and shelter provided by a wall.

These equivalents must be assessed against each sentence’s subject and construction.

11. Conclusion

Arabic D F ʾ concerns warmth in contrast with cold.

Ibn Fāris identifies its core as khilāf al-bard, the opposite of cold.

Dictionaries apply this both to people, weather, and places being warm and to clothing and items keeping people warm.

In Arabic pastoral life, the family relates to wool, camel hair, other hair, clothing, and additional livestock products.

Some broader uses also record difʾ for offspring, milk, and a range of pastoral benefits.

This extension should not remove warmth and protection from cold from the root’s definition.

The root occurs once in the Qur’an, in Al-Naḥl 16:5.

The verse names warmth alongside livestock’s other benefits and use for food.

Al-Naḥl 16:80 mentions wool, camel hair, other hair, and hides, which may supply materials for clothing, blankets, and various coverings.

This internal Qur’anic connection identifies warmth from livestock, especially through their warming products, as a clear instance of difʾ.

In directly translating this verse, however, difʾ should not be restricted to clothing, wool, milk, meat, or replaced by the entirety of livestock benefits. Broader recorded lexical uses remain valid subjects for independent discussion.

Warmth is therefore appropriate in Al-Naḥl 16:5, with detailed instances remaining in the root explanation.

Difʾ does not inherently mean intensely scorching heat; in human applications it foregrounds warmth and protection from cold.

Proposed final root definition: D F ʾ has warmth in contrast with cold as its established core. It extends to being warm, becoming warm, and clothing or items providing or preserving warmth. In pastoral usage, difʾ particularly relates to warmth and coverings from livestock wool and hair; some early uses also encompass broader pastoral benefits. In Al-Naḥl 16:5, its direct and preferred meaning is warmth. Warming items are instances of that meaning, rather than its necessary replacement. Brief root meaning: Warmth in contrast with cold; becoming and remaining warm, and clothing or items providing and preserving warmth.
