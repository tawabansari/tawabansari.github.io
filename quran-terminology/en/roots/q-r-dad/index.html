Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root Q R Ḍ

Cutting off a portion of wealth, separating it and giving it with expectation of return or reward, lending, and temporarily transferring something from one’s possession

Important Forms from This Root

ق ر ض (q r ḍ)

The root field; cutting, severing, and separating a portion of something.

قَرَضَ / يَقْرِضُ (qaraḍa / yaqriḍu)

He cut; he severed; he lent.

قَرْض (qarḍ)

Loan; something separated from wealth and given so that it may return.

قَرْضًا حَسَنًا (qarḍanā ḥasananā)

A good loan; clean giving in the way of God without pressure.

مِقْرَاض (miqrāḍ)

Scissors; a tool for cutting and separating.

مُقْرِض / مُقْتَرِض (muqriḍ / muqtariḍ)

Lender and borrower; the two sides of a loan relation.

Central Meaning

The root Q R Ḍ originally means to cut, sever, or separate a portion of something. From this meaning comes the idea of a loan: a person separates part of his wealth or possession from himself and gives it to another, expecting either its like to return or a better reward with God.

So a loan is not merely a simple financial act. At the root of it is a kind of separating from oneself. A person takes something under his control out of his direct use and places it with another for a time.

Linguistic Note

The root Q R Ḍ is tied to cutting and separating. A loan is called qarḍ because a person cuts off a portion of his wealth and removes it from his direct possession.

This wealth is separated from him, but the relation of return is not completely cut, because a loan usually carries expectation that its like or equivalent will return.

This distinguishes a loan from charity. In charity, a person gives and does not demand return from the receiver. In a loan, the original wealth or its like must return, unless it is forgiven.

So a loan stands between two fields: from one side, wealth is separated from the lender; from the other, the separation is not final, because return is expected.

Material Image of the Root

One clear image of this root is مِقْرَاض (miqrāḍ), meaning scissors. Scissors are a tool for cutting and separating. This image shows that in a loan, too, something is separated from a person’s possession.

Arabic also uses this root for cutting across a path or passing aside from something. In Surat al-Kahf, concerning the sun and the companions of the cave, it says: تَقْرِضُهُمْ ذَاتَ الشِّمَالِ (taqriḍuhum dhāta al-shimāli); that is, it passed them on the left, turned away from them, or kept its light aside from them. This wording appears in 18:17.

This use shows that Q R Ḍ is not only physical cutting. It may also point to passing aside, turning away, or separation of path. In financial lending, the person also turns aside from direct use of his wealth so that it can serve another person’s need.

Loan and a Defined Portion

In the material sense of the root, qarḍ is close to cutting a defined portion. What is separated is not vague or worthless; it is a specific portion of a person’s possession or capacity.

For this reason, when the Qur’an speaks of قَرْضًا حَسَنًا (qarḍanā ḥasananā), it does not mean giving whatever is worthless or merely left over. A good loan means that a person separates something clean, clear, and worthy from his possession; something that truly has value for help and renewal.

This possession may be wealth, but it is not limited to wealth. Time, knowledge, support, reputation, strength, opportunity, and skill can also enter the wider meaning of a good loan when they are used in the way of God to relieve the life of His servants.

Loan and Ribā

A sound loan differs from ribā. In a sound loan, the foundation is help, trust, and the return of the principal. The lender separates part of his wealth so that another person’s difficulty may be eased or an action may become possible.

Ribā appears when the loan becomes an instrument of guaranteed increase: the lender demands increase merely because time has passed or because the other side is in need, without sharing real risk, work, or outcome.

The core difference is this: a loan is separating wealth for help or temporary transfer; ribā is an increase that can be imposed upon that same loan and turn the relation from mercy into pressure.

Loan and Sale

A loan is also not the same as sale. In sale, something is transferred in exchange for compensation and the transaction is completed. In a loan, wealth or its like is transferred temporarily, and its return is expected.

In sale, the aim is exchange. In loan, the aim is relief of need, trust, or temporary enabling.

For this reason, if a loan is disguised as a sale in order to take ribā-based increase, its reality does not change. Names matter, but actual function matters more.

Good Loan

One of the most beautiful uses of this root in the Qur’an is قَرْضًا حَسَنًا (qarḍanā ḥasananā), a good loan. The Qur’an even asks who will give God a good loan: مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يُقْرِضُ اللَّهَ قَرْضًا حَسَنًا (man dhā alladhī yuqriḍu allāh qarḍanā ḥasananā). This wording appears in 2:245 and in other verses.

God does not need a loan from the human being. This expression trains the human being. When a person separates something in the way of God, it may outwardly decrease his wealth or strength, but in truth he has placed it into a relation whose return with God is multiplied and purer.

So lending to God means cutting from one’s limited possession and entrusting it to the path of divine bounty. A person separates part of his wealth, time, strength, or capacity, but he does not send it into nothingness. He places it in a path where it grows with God.

For this reason, the Qur’an speaks about multiplication in relation to the good loan: فَيُضَاعِفَهُ لَهُ (fayuḍāʿifahu lahu). This multiplication is not only numerical increase. It can also mean increase in blessing, effect, purity, human growth, and better return with God.

Qualities of a Good Loan

A good loan must be clean of pressure, humiliation, reminding someone of favor, exploitation, and unhealthy demand for increase. If a person gives something but uses it to imprison another, it is no longer good.

A good loan must preserve the dignity of the receiver and open a path of return, breathing, and repair for him. In a good loan, intention matters. The goal is not merely transfer of wealth; the goal is to open a way, lighten a burden, and place one’s possession in the path of life.

Loan and Time

A loan is usually tied to time. Something is given today and returned in the future. This same time can take two directions.

In the logic of mercy, time gives the debtor opportunity. If he is in hardship, he should be given delay. Time here becomes a space for breathing and rebuilding.

In the logic of ribā, time becomes an instrument of pressure. As time passes, the burden grows heavier. Instead of moving closer to freedom, the debtor becomes more trapped.

So the important question is: what does time do inside a loan structure? Does it give the human being opportunity, or does it tighten the chain?

Loan and Society

A sound loan is one of the important tools of social trust. In a society where people can help each other without exploitation and without humiliation, crises become lighter and human relations become stronger.

But if loan becomes an instrument of domination, society becomes sick. Heavy debts, crushing penalties, and imposed increases can take the human being away from independence, dignity, and peace.

So depending on its ethical structure, loan can become either a path of mercy or a gate to ribā.

A Note on Today’s World

In today’s economy, loans have become much more complex than in the past. Sometimes a loan is for real need, such as housing, medical care, or starting work. At other times, debt is used for luxury, display, or uncontrolled consumption.

In an inflationary world, one must also distinguish between returning a nominal number and returning real value. If money loses value over time, returning the same number may not always be just. But this discussion must not become an excuse for pressure, guaranteed gain, or exploitation of another person’s need.

The Qur’anic measure remains: لَا تَظْلِمُونَ وَلَا تُظْلَمُونَ (lā taẓlimūna walā tuẓlamūna): do not wrong, and do not be wronged. This wording appears in 2:279.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

قَرَضَ / يَقْرِضُ (qaraḍa / yaqriḍu): cut; separated; lent.

قَرْض (qarḍ): loan; wealth separated and temporarily given so that it may return.

قَرْضًا حَسَنًا (qarḍan ḥasanan): good loan; clean, pressure-free giving in the way of God; separating wealth or strength for good.

مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يُقْرِضُ اللَّهَ قَرْضًا حَسَنًا (man dhā alladhī yuqriḍu allāh qarḍanā ḥasananā): who is the one who will lend God a good loan; who will separate part of his possession cleanly and well in the way of God?

تَقْرِضُهُمْ ذَاتَ الشِّمَالِ (taqriḍuhum dhāta al-shimāli): it passed them on the left; it turned its light aside from them; it went around them on the left side.

فَيُضَاعِفَهُ لَهُ (fayuḍāʿifahu lahu): so He multiplies it for him; so God increases and multiplies it for him.

مُقْرِض (muqriḍ): lender; one who separates part of his wealth and gives it to another.

مُقْتَرِض (muqtariḍ): borrower; one who receives a loan and bears responsibility for repayment.

Summary

The root Q R Ḍ refers to cutting, separating, and removing a portion of something from oneself. From this meaning comes the concept of loan: a person separates part of his wealth or capacity from direct control and places it with another, usually with expectation that its like or equivalent will return.

A sound loan stands on trust, mercy, and relief of need. But if the loan becomes an instrument of guaranteed increase and pressure upon the needy, it approaches the logic of ribā.

So qarḍ in the Qur’an’s ethical balance is a sensitive field. It can be a path of help, life, and growth; or, if separated from justice, it can become a path of domination, pressure, and human exhaustion.

Its most beautiful form is qarḍ ḥasan: separating part of one’s wealth, strength, time, or opportunity for God and the servants of God, without reminding of favor, without humiliation, and without draining another person’s capacity.
