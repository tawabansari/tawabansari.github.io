Core Meaning

The root ر ه ب fundamentally points to a state of awe, wary reverence, inward contraction, and vigilance before something great, heavy, powerful, or serious in consequence. This root should not be reduced to simple “fear.” In rahb, a person or society gathers itself, becomes cautious, watches carefully, and enters readiness before greatness, danger, power, or an important result.

This state can be directed rightly or wrongly. If rahbah is directed toward The God and the responsibility of truth, it helps with vigilance, care, and fulfillment of covenant. If it is transferred to fading powers, deceptive displays, or social pressure, it makes the human being intimidated, closed, and less able to discern properly.

In the Qur'an, this root appears in several important fields: awe and wary reverence before The God, the balance of prayer between raghbah and rahbah, the natural bodily fear before a tremendous matter, fear-making through deception, defensive deterrence against the enemy, and then the words ruhban and rahbaniyyah in the field of asceticism, withdrawal, and monastic structures.

Important Forms

Several important forms of this root appear in the Qur'an.

رهب / يرهب points to wary reverence, awe, or careful fear.

وَإِيَّايَ فَارْهَبُونِ

And only toward Me have wary reverence.

Al-Baqarah 2:40

يرهبون is the imperfect form and shows continuity or a stable orientation:

وَفِي نُسْخَتِهَا هُدًى وَرَحْمَةٌ لِلَّذِينَ هُمْ لِرَبِّهِمْ يَرْهَبُونَ

And in its copy was guidance and mercy for those who have wary reverence toward their Lord.

Al-A'raf 7:154

أرهب / ترهبون is from form IV and means causing rahbah, creating awe, or creating deterrence in the other side:

تُرْهِبُونَ بِهِ عَدُوَّ اللَّهِ وَعَدُوَّكُمْ

With it you place the enemy of The God and your enemy in a state of awe and deterrence.

Al-Anfal 8:60

استرهب / استرهبوهم is from form X. In Qur'anic context, it means that a group made others enter rahbah, fear, or intimidation:

وَاسْتَرْهَبُوهُمْ وَجَاءُوا بِسِحْرٍ عَظِيمٍ

And they made them afraid, and they came with a great sorcery.

Al-A'raf 7:116

الرهب is a noun of state and points to fear, contraction, or gathering oneself before something terrifying:

وَاضْمُمْ إِلَيْكَ جَنَاحَكَ مِنَ الرَّهْبِ

And draw your arm to yourself from fear.

Al-Qasas 28:32

رغبا ورهبا is an important pairing in which rahb stands opposite raghb. Raghb is the direction of hope, attraction, desire, and turning toward. Rahb is the direction of wary reverence, awe, vigilance, and gathering oneself from consequence:

إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَيَدْعُونَنَا رَغَبًا وَرَهَبًا

They used to hasten in good works and call upon Us with raghbah and rahbah.

Al-Anbiya 21:90

رهبان is the plural of rahib, meaning those known by monastic life, asceticism, wary reverence, and a kind of religious withdrawal. The Qur'an mentions ruhban in different contexts: sometimes with reference to the humility of some of them, and sometimes with criticism when they become corrupt religious authorities or lord-like figures.

رهبانية points to a monastic way or system, a religious form of asceticism and withdrawal. The Qur'an says that the followers of Jesus brought it into being, but did not observe it with its rightful observance:

وَرَهْبَانِيَّةً ابْتَدَعُوهَا مَا كَتَبْنَاهَا عَلَيْهِمْ

And a monasticism they initiated; We had not prescribed it for them.

Al-Hadid 57:27

رهبة is a noun of state and points to awe, fear, or severe contraction in the chests:

لَأَنْتُمْ أَشَدُّ رَهْبَةً فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ

You are surely more rahbah-inducing in their chests than The God.

Al-Hashr 59:13

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of ر ه ب is contraction and caution before greatness, danger, power, or heavy consequence. This root speaks of more than a simple feeling. In rahb, the body, heart, and behavior of the human being change state. A person gathers himself, slows down, thinks about consequence, and becomes vigilant before what he sees as great or dangerous.

For this reason, rahb is not the same as khawf. Khawf is more general fear of harm or danger. Rahb is usually accompanied by awe, weight, and a restraining effect. A person may have khawf of a small danger, but rahbah usually arises before something that creates heaviness, awe, or contraction in the heart.

Rahb is also not identical with khashyah. Khashyah is more connected with knowledge, awareness, and perception of greatness. Rahb highlights the state of gathering oneself and having wary reverence before that greatness or consequence.

Nor is it identical with taqwa. Taqwa is a broad direction in life: guarding oneself, caring for limits, and taking a shield against the consequence of sin and corruption. Rahb can be one inner state among the people of taqwa, but taqwa is not only rahbah.

In the Qur'an, when rahb is directed toward The God, it relates to wary reverence, fulfilling covenant, and practical tawhid. But if a person has more rahbah before people, political power, sorcery, propaganda, or earthly systems than before The God, that shows the displacement of his inner reference.

Material Image

The material image of ر ه ب can be seen in the human body. When someone suddenly faces a terrifying or tremendous matter, his body contracts, his chest tightens, his hands and arms draw closer to the body, and he takes a defensive posture. The verse in Al-Qasas makes this image visible:

وَاضْمُمْ إِلَيْكَ جَنَاحَكَ مِنَ الرَّهْبِ

And draw your arm to yourself from fear.

Al-Qasas 28:32

Here rahb is not merely a mental fear. It affects the body. “Drawing the wing” means drawing the arm or hand back toward oneself. The material image of the root, then, is gathering oneself and taking a guarded posture before something terrifying or tremendous.

The same image appears socially. When a society is prepared and strong before an enemy, the enemy is restrained from reckless attack. Here rahb does not mean blind violence or frightening the innocent. It means creating deterrent awe. When the enemy sees the cost of aggression, his will to advance contracts and he holds back.

At the religious level, the monk is also understood from this semantic field: someone who, out of intense wary reverence and rahbah, restricts and gathers his life and separates from many ordinary social contacts. This contraction may begin from a sincere intention, but if it reaches excess, cuts off social responsibility, or creates human religious authority, it leaves Qur'anic balance.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, rahb is close to fear accompanied by awe, caution, and contraction. A rahib is someone whose religious life is known through wary reverence, asceticism, withdrawal, and a state of fear and awe. From the same field, rahbaniyyah points to a monastic way or a religious system of withdrawal.

This root can be used both inwardly and outwardly. Sometimes a person himself has rahbah; that is, before greatness or consequence, he enters a state of wary reverence and contraction. Sometimes someone places another in a state of rahbah; by power, display, or preparedness, he intimidates or deters him.

Special lexical examples, such as ancient uses connected with animals, exhaustion, or bodily contraction, must be treated cautiously. The surest foundation is Qur'anic usage itself, because the Qur'an uses this root across many fields: covenant, tawhid, the tablets of Moses, sorcery, defense, prayer, monks, monasticism, and social or political rahbah.

Rahbah Before The God: Tawhidi Vigilance

One of the main Qur'anic uses of ر ه ب is directing rahbah toward The God:

وَأَوْفُوا بِعَهْدِي أُوفِ بِعَهْدِكُمْ وَإِيَّايَ فَارْهَبُونِ

And fulfill My covenant; I will fulfill your covenant. And only toward Me have wary reverence.

Al-Baqarah 2:40

In this verse, rahb comes beside covenant. This is very important. Wary reverence before The God is not merely an emotion of fear. It is linked with fulfilling covenant, responsibility, and taking seriously the consequence of the divine covenant. The phrase وإياي فارهبون also has an emphatic structure. The fronting of إياي shows that the final direction of rahbah must be only toward The God.

A simple translation such as “fear Me” does not show the full capacity of the verse. The more precise sense is: make Me alone the reference point of your wary reverence and awe. Earthly powers, social pressure, corrupted traditions, and human lords must not take the place of The God inside the human being.

إِنَّمَا هُوَ إِلَٰهٌ وَاحِدٌ فَإِيَّايَ فَارْهَبُونِ

He is only one deity; so only toward Me have wary reverence.

Al-Nahl 16:51

Here rahbah is directly tied to tawhid. Since the deity is one, the final direction of rahbah must also be one. If a person has such wary reverence before other than The God that it controls his command, conscience, and point of decision, practical tawhid is harmed.

The Rahbah of the Guided Before Their Lord

In Surat al-A'raf, after Moses calms down and takes up the tablets, their content is described:

وَفِي نُسْخَتِهَا هُدًى وَرَحْمَةٌ لِلَّذِينَ هُمْ لِرَبِّهِمْ يَرْهَبُونَ

And in its copy was guidance and mercy for those who have wary reverence toward their Lord.

Al-A'raf 7:154

Here rahb comes with guidance and mercy. This shows that Qur'anic rahbah toward The God, when properly understood, does not drive a person away from mercy. Rather, this wary reverence creates the capacity to receive guidance and mercy.

Rahb in this context is not paralyzing fear. It is a state that restrains a person from carelessness, arrogance, breaking covenant, and violating limits.

Raghb and Rahb: The Balance of Hope and Wary Reverence

In Surat al-Anbiya, rahb appears beside raghb:

إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَيَدْعُونَنَا رَغَبًا وَرَهَبًا وَكَانُوا لَنَا خَاشِعِينَ

They used to hasten in good works and call upon Us with raghbah and rahbah, and they were humble before Us.

Al-Anbiya 21:90

This pairing is very important for understanding the root. Raghb is the direction of hope, desire, attraction, and turning toward. Rahb is the direction of wary reverence, awe, vigilance, and gathering oneself from consequence. Prayer and servanthood in the Qur'an are not built on one feeling alone. Hope without wary reverence can make the human being careless; wary reverence without hope can make him closed and despairing.

In this verse, raghb and rahb appear with hastening in good works and khushu'. Qur'anic rahb is part of the balance of servanthood: hope in mercy together with vigilance toward limits and consequence.

Rahb in the Body of Moses: Natural Contraction Before a Tremendous Matter

One of the most concrete uses of this root in the Qur'an is the verse in Al-Qasas:

وَاضْمُمْ إِلَيْكَ جَنَاحَكَ مِنَ الرَّهْبِ

And draw your arm to yourself from fear.

Al-Qasas 28:32

Here الرهب points to bodily fear and contraction. The command to draw the arm in shows that rahbah affected the body. Before a tremendous or terrifying matter, a human being may naturally gather himself and take a defensive posture.

This verse is very important for the material meaning of the root. Rahb is not just a belief or an idea in the mind; the body experiences it too. Yet the Qur'an brings this state in the story of Moses on the path of divine mission. Natural rahbah before a tremendous matter, if guided properly, does not prevent the mission.

Istarhab: Fear-Making Through Deception and Display

In the story of Moses and the sorcerers, the root ر ه ب appears in form X:

قَالَ أَلْقُوا ۖ فَلَمَّا أَلْقَوْا سَحَرُوا أَعْيُنَ النَّاسِ وَاسْتَرْهَبُوهُمْ وَجَاءُوا بِسِحْرٍ عَظِيمٍ

He said: Throw. So when they threw, they bewitched the eyes of the people, made them afraid, and came with a great sorcery.

Al-A'raf 7:116

استرهبوهم means they made them enter rahbah. Here rahbah does not come through truth, but through sorcery, display, and manipulation of public perception. The sorcerers affected the people's eyes and built awe and fear in their hearts.

This usage shows that not every intense rahbah is a sign of truth. Rahbah may come from the greatness of truth, or it may come from deception, propaganda, sorcery, display of power, or psychological manipulation. In this context, rahb is connected with the locking of public perception: before people measure the truth, they are intimidated by the display.

Turhibun in Al-Anfal: Defensive Deterrence, Not Blind Violence

In Surat al-Anfal, the root appears in form IV:

وَأَعِدُّوا لَهُمْ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ مِنْ قُوَّةٍ وَمِنْ رِبَاطِ الْخَيْلِ تُرْهِبُونَ بِهِ عَدُوَّ اللَّهِ وَعَدُوَّكُمْ وَآخَرِينَ مِنْ دُونِهِمْ

And prepare for them whatever you can of strength and of tethered horses; with it you place the enemy of The God, your enemy, and others besides them in a state of awe and deterrence.

Al-Anfal 8:60

This verse must be translated with great care. ترهبون here does not mean limitless terror-making, blind killing, or frightening the innocent. The context is defensive preparedness before an enemy. The closer meaning is creating deterrent awe: a society becomes so prepared and strong that the enemy is discouraged from aggression.

In this usage, rahb relates to the enemy's calculation. When the enemy sees preparedness and defensive power, his will to attack contracts and he holds back from advancing. So ترهبون in this context is close to defensive deterrence, not unjust fear-making.

This point is very important for translation today. The modern political meaning of “terrorism” must not be imposed on this verse. The Qur'an speaks here about preparedness before an enemy, not violence against innocent people. The verse's own context, with words such as قوة, رباط الخيل, عدو الله, and عدوكم, shows the field of defense and deterrence.

Rahbah in the Chests: Displacing the Reference of Awe

In Surat al-Hashr, rahbah appears in a psychological and social context:

لَأَنْتُمْ أَشَدُّ رَهْبَةً فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ قَوْمٌ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ

You are surely more rahbah-inducing in their chests than The God; that is because they are a people who do not understand deeply.

Al-Hashr 59:13

This is one of the most precise uses of the root. The issue is not only fear; the issue is what becomes greater and more awe-inducing in the human chest. When people have more rahbah before earthly power than before The God, their inner reference has been displaced.

The verse also gives the cause: ذلك بأنهم قوم لا يفقهون. This displacement of rahbah comes from weakness in deep understanding. If a person understands reality properly, he sees earthly powers in their place: neither imagining them as nothing, nor making them more awe-inducing than The God.

So rahb in the Qur'an is not only a feeling. It is also an indicator for measuring the inner reference of the human being. What makes a person contract more: The God and the responsibility of truth, or people and passing powers?

Ruhban: Monks and the Qur'an's Two-Sided Evaluation

The word ruhban appears in different Qur'anic contexts. In Al-Ma'idah 5:82, monks are mentioned in a relatively positive context:

ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ مِنْهُمْ قِسِّيسِينَ وَرُهْبَانًا وَأَنَّهُمْ لَا يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ

That is because among them are priests and monks, and because they are not arrogant.

Al-Ma'idah 5:82

Here ruhban appears beside qissisin, and the important feature of the context is that they do not act arrogantly. The Qur'an does not condemn all monks in one flat way. In this verse, the humility of some of them is part of their moral and spiritual nearness to the believers.

But in Surat al-Tawbah, the same title appears in a critical context:

اتَّخَذُوا أَحْبَارَهُمْ وَرُهْبَانَهُمْ أَرْبَابًا مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ

They took their scholars and monks as lords apart from The God.

Al-Tawbah 9:31

Here the criticism is not aimed at being a monk as such. It is aimed at turning scholars and monks into lords. People gave the place of final obedience, authority over lawful and unlawful, and religious command-following to human beings who should not sit in the place of The God.

Another verse gives a financial and social critique:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنَّ كَثِيرًا مِنَ الْأَحْبَارِ وَالرُّهْبَانِ لَيَأْكُلُونَ أَمْوَالَ النَّاسِ بِالْبَاطِلِ وَيَصُدُّونَ عَنْ سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ

O you who have faith, many of the scholars and monks consume people's wealth in falsehood and turn away from the path of The God.

Al-Tawbah 9:34

Here the Qur'an says كثيرا with precision: many of them, not all of them. Therefore Forqan's text should neither sanctify monks nor condemn all of them in one undifferentiated way. The Qur'anic critique is aimed at religious, financial, and human-authority corruption.

Rahbaniyyah: An Ascetic Structure of Contraction and the Failure to Observe Its Right

In Surat al-Hadid, rahbaniyyah appears:

ثُمَّ قَفَّيْنَا عَلَىٰ آثَارِهِمْ بِرُسُلِنَا وَقَفَّيْنَا بِعِيسَى ابْنِ مَرْيَمَ وَآتَيْنَاهُ الْإِنْجِيلَ وَجَعَلْنَا فِي قُلُوبِ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوهُ رَأْفَةً وَرَحْمَةً وَرَهْبَانِيَّةً ابْتَدَعُوهَا مَا كَتَبْنَاهَا عَلَيْهِمْ إِلَّا ابْتِغَاءَ رِضْوَانِ اللَّهِ فَمَا رَعَوْهَا حَقَّ رِعَايَتِهَا

Then We followed in their traces with Our messengers, and We followed with Jesus son of Mary, and We gave him the Gospel, and We placed in the hearts of those who followed him tenderness and mercy; and a monasticism they initiated, We had not prescribed it for them, except seeking the pleasure of The God; then they did not observe it with its rightful observance.

Al-Hadid 57:27

This verse is subtle. The Qur'an does not say that monasticism was a divine command. It says they brought it into being themselves. At the same time, it connects the initial motive with seeking the pleasure of The God. Then it criticizes them for not observing it with its rightful observance.

Rahbaniyyah can be understood as a contracted structure in religious life: out of fear of pollution, worldliness, desire, or corruption, a person restricts and separates his life. Such withdrawal may begin from a pure intention, but if it does not remain joined with balanced fitrah, social responsibility, justice, and truthfulness, it becomes an unstable structure.

For this reason, the Qur'an in this verse neither presents rahbaniyyah as divine law nor denies its initial motive completely. The main critique is at two points: first, The God had not prescribed it for them; second, they themselves did not fulfill its rightful observance.

Similar Uses

Rahb before The God is not the same as rahb before human beings. In Al-Baqarah 2:40 and Al-Nahl 16:51, rahbah must be directed toward The God. But in Al-Hashr 59:13, the problem is that people have more rahbah before human beings than before The God.

Rahb in the story of Moses also has two faces. In Al-Qasas 28:32, al-rahb is the natural state of Moses before a tremendous matter, and The God calms him and prepares him for mission. But in Al-A'raf 7:116, the sorcerers place the people into rahbah through display and sorcery. One is the natural state of the human being before greatness; the other is fear created through deception.

ترهبون in Al-Anfal 8:60 differs from استرهبوهم in Al-A'raf 7:116. Turhibun is in the context of defense, preparedness, and deterrence before an enemy. Istarhabuhum is in the context of sorcery and manipulation of public perception. These two should not be placed under one simple and identical meaning.

Ruhban in Al-Ma'idah 5:82 is also not evaluated the same way as ruhban in Al-Tawbah 9:31 and 9:34. In Al-Ma'idah, some monks are mentioned alongside humility and lack of arrogance. In Al-Tawbah, the critique is aimed at turning them into lords and at the financial and religious corruption of many of them.

Rahbaniyyah in Al-Hadid 57:27 must also be understood separately from rahb itself. Rahb is a state of awe and wary reverence. Rahbaniyyah is a religious and social form of ascetic withdrawal that human beings initiated and then failed to observe properly.

Grammar and Structure

In وإياي فارهبون, the fronting of إياي creates restriction and emphasis. The direction of rahbah must be toward The God alone. A simple “fear Me” does not fully show this emphasis. Better translations include: “only toward Me have wary reverence” or “make Me alone the reference point of your rahbah.”

In لربهم يرهبون, the lam in لربهم shows direction and specification. Their rahbah is toward their Lord; the inward direction of this awe returns to their own Rabb.

In ترهبون به, the ba' marks the instrument: with that preparedness and strength, you place the enemy in awe and deterrence. The structure shows that rahbah is created through defensive preparedness, not through oppression and aggression.

In استرهبوهم, form X shows that the sorcerers drew the people into a state of rahbah or created rahbah in them. The structure shows the active role of the fear-making agent.

In من الرهب in Al-Qasas 28:32, من can show the source or cause of the state: from fear, because of fear, or to calm the state of fear.

In رهبة في صدورهم, the place of rahbah is named as the chests. This shows that the Qur'an pays attention to the inward and psychological dimension of this state. Rahbah is not only outward behavior; it forms in the chest.

Nearby Roots

خ و ف points to general fear of danger, harm, or an unpleasant consequence. Khawf is broader and may be used in simpler situations. Rahb is usually heavier and is accompanied by awe, contraction, and vigilance before something great or impactful.

خ ش ي points to khashyah: a more knowledgeable fear usually connected with awareness, perception of greatness, and knowledge. Khashyah leans more on understanding greatness and consequence; rahb leans on awe and contraction before it.

و ج ل points to inner trembling and disturbance of the heart. Wajal is the heart being shaken. Rahb can be broader and include the body, chest, social behavior, and outward deterrence.

ر ع ب is close to intense fear and terror being cast into the heart. Ru'b is usually stronger, more sudden, and more forceful. Rahb can be steadier, more vigilant, or accompanied by awe.

ح ذ ر points to caution, care, and readiness before danger. Hadhar is more practical and precautionary; rahb also shows the inner state of awe and contraction.

ت ق ي / تقوى points to guarding oneself, taking a shield, and broad moral vigilance. Taqwa is a life-direction; rahb is one state of awe and wary reverence that can be part of taqwa.

ش ف ق is close to concern mixed with tenderness or a gentle fear of consequence. Rahb is heavier and more awe-filled.

ر غ ب appears opposite rahb in Al-Anbiya 21:90. Raghb is the direction of hope, desire, and attraction; rahb is the direction of wary reverence, awe, and vigilance. These two should be balanced in prayer and servanthood.

ز ه د points to lack of desire, detachment from the world, or wanting little. Rahbaniyyah may be accompanied by zuhd, but zuhd and rahbaniyyah are not identical. Zuhd is a state or orientation; rahbaniyyah is a religious/social form of withdrawal.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

رهب / يرهب: to have wary reverence, to stand in awe, to stand with vigilance and contraction before greatness or consequence.

وإياي فارهبون: only toward Me have wary reverence; make Me alone the reference point of your rahbah; have your final awe and vigilance only before Me.

لربهم يرهبون: they have wary reverence toward their Lord; they stand in awe and vigilance before their Rabb.

الرهب: fear, rahbah, contraction from awe, a state of gathering oneself from fear.

رغبا ورهبا: with raghbah and rahbah; with hope and wary reverence; with attraction toward mercy and vigilance toward consequence.

ترهبون به: with it you create deterrence; with it you place the enemy in awe; with it you deter the enemy from aggression.

استرهبوهم: they made them afraid; they intimidated them; through display and deception they created rahbah in their hearts.

رهبة في صدورهم: rahbah in their chests; inward awe and fear in their hearts.

رهبان: monks; people of ascetic and religious withdrawal; those connected with a monastic way, depending on context.

رهبانية: monasticism; the monastic way; an initiated system of ascetic and religious withdrawal.

Summary

The root ر ه ب points to awe, wary reverence, contraction, and vigilance before greatness, power, danger, or serious consequence. At the material level, it is understood through the body's gathering, the tightening of the chest, caution, and defensive posture before a terrifying matter. In the Qur'an, rahb sometimes points to wary reverence before The God and taking covenant and tawhid seriously; sometimes to the balance of prayer between raghbah and rahbah; sometimes to natural human fear; sometimes to fear-making through deception; sometimes to defensive deterrence; and sometimes to monks and monasticism.

Its difference from khawf is that khawf is more general fear, while rahb is more awe-filled and accompanied by contraction and vigilance. Its difference from khashyah is that khashyah is more connected with knowledge of greatness, while rahb highlights the state of awe and gathering oneself before greatness or consequence. Its difference from taqwa is that taqwa is a broad direction of self-guarding and moral vigilance, while rahb is one inner and behavioral state of wary reverence.

In Forqan translation, ر ه ب should not always be translated as simple “fear.” In many contexts, “wary reverence,” “awe,” “rahbah,” “deterrence,” or “contraction before greatness and consequence” is more precise. The defensive usage in Al-Anfal 8:60 should also not be equated with blind violence or the modern meaning of terrorism. The verse's context speaks of defensive preparedness and deterrence before an enemy, not terrorizing the innocent.
