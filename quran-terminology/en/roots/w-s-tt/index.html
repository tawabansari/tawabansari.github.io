Qur’anic root و س ط

Middle, an intervening position and moderation: from being among things to justice, sound judgement and the Qur’anic meaning of أُمَّةً وَسَطًا

The three-letter Arabic root و س ط concerns being in the middle of something, away from its two sides, or in an intermediate position. The middle of a house or an assembly lies among its surrounding parts. In other uses, however, the same word can denote an intermediate level between two quantities, measured conduct, or even a superior or more worthy position. These meanings are related but distinct. Something spatially in the middle is not necessarily more just or better than its surroundings.

The Qur’an uses this root in five different settings: the description أُمَّةً وَسَطًا, الصَّلَاةِ الْوُسْطَىٰ, intermediate food in expiation for an oath, the more judicious person among the garden owners, and entering the midst of a gathering in Al-ʿĀdiyāt. These uses show that وسط can denote a spatial position, a standard of quantity and quality, or a description of human conduct and judgement.

The root occurs five times in the Qur’an, in four morphological categories: وَسَطًا in Al-Baqarah 2:143; الْوُسْطَىٰ in Al-Baqarah 2:238; أَوْسَطِ in Al-Māʾidah 5:89; أَوْسَطُهُمْ in Al-Qalam 68:28; and فَوَسَطْنَ in Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:5. أَوْسَطِ and أَوْسَطُهُمْ share one formation, differing in grammatical role and the attached pronoun. وَسَطْنَ is the root’s only Qur’anic verb; its other occurrences are nominal or adjectival.

Arabic also has وَسْط, وَاسِط, وَسِيط, وَاسِطَة, تَوَسُّط and وَسَاطَة, but these exact forms do not occur in the Qur’an.

1. The semantic core and usage in early Arabic

Wasaṭ: the middle of something, away from its edges

Spatial وَسَطُ الشَّيْءِ denotes the middle part of something. وَسَطُ الدَّارِ is the middle of the house, while وَسَطُ الرَّأْسِ denotes the middle part of the head. Here the middle belongs to the thing itself; it is not something independent outside it.

Arabic also recognises a distinction between وَسَط, with a vowel on the sīn, and وَسْط, with the sīn unvowelled. In one lexical account, وَسَط more commonly names the middle part of the thing itself, whereas وَسْط functions like بَيْن, between or among, to express a position among other people or things. وَسْطَ الْقَوْمِ, for example, means among the people.

This boundary is not absolute in every Arabic usage. Lexicographers record instances in which the two forms substitute for each other. The vowel on the sīn can therefore help identify the word’s role, but should not produce two wholly unrelated semantic families.

Lexical accounts record the word in relation to houses, assemblies, pasture, riding equipment and people’s positions within a gathering. Each use requires its own evidence for dating; its inclusion in a later dictionary does not alone establish a pre-Qur’anic date.

Some lexical explanations describe the middle of a pasture as better than its edges because it may contain more water and vegetation. A central position on a mount can also be more suitable for balance. These features nevertheless depend on that particular place or piece of equipment. They do not establish that the centre of everything in nature is always its best and most valuable part.

Wasaṭ in pre-Islamic poetry

A poem by the pre-Islamic poet Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā says:

«هَذَا وَلَيْسَ كَمَنْ يَعْيَا بِخُطَّتِهِ
وَسْطَ النَّدِيِّ إِذَا مَا نَاطِقٌ نَطَقَا»

“This man is unlike one who falters in his judgement and deliberation amid the assembly when a speaker speaks.”

النَّدِيّ here denotes an assembly or council, and وَسْطَ النَّدِيِّ locates the person among that gathering. The poet praises someone’s capacity for speech and deliberation, but the praise does not come from وسط itself. It emerges from the comparison between the capable person and someone who falters in the assembly.

This witness shows that early Arabic used وسط for a person’s actual position among a gathering. Being present amid an assembly is neither a virtue nor a fault in itself.

Ṭarafah ibn al-ʿAbd’s Muʿallaqah also says:

«وَإِنْ شِئْتُ سَامَى وَاسِطَ الْكَوْرِ رَأْسُهَا
وَعَامَتْ بِضَبْعَيْهَا نَجَاءَ الْخَفَيْدَدِ»

“If I wish, her head rises to the height of the front of her saddle, and she swims with her forelimbs at the speed of a running male ostrich.”

وَاسِطُ الْكَوْرِ here names a part of the camel’s saddle, identified in lexical explanations as its front. The word describes the components of an actual piece of equipment.

This witness helps establish spatial usage within the root family. The name of one saddle part, however, does not mean that واسط always identifies the precise geometrical centre. Equipment parts can have their own particular naming conventions.

Lexical and exegetical works also transmit a verse containing هُمْ وَسَطٌ يَرْضَى الْأَنَامُ بِحُكْمِهِمْ, sometimes attributed to Zuhayr. In it, وسط approaches worthiness and acceptable judgement. Its wording and attribution nevertheless vary. An editorial note in al-Ṭabarī’s commentary states that the transmitted opening in the collected poems is لِحَيٍّ حِلَالٍ يَعْصِمُ النَّاسَ أَمْرُهُمْ. The verse therefore cannot be used without caution as certain pre-Qur’anic evidence for وسط meaning justice.

From an intermediate position to sound judgement and excellence

Alongside their spatial meaning, وَسَط and أَوْسَط can describe something between two limits. شَيْءٌ وَسَطٌ, for example, can denote something neither very good nor very bad, but between the two.

Another usage applies أَوْسَطُ الْقَوْمِ to one of a group’s most worthy or respected people. This need not mean someone sitting in the centre of the assembly or occupying the middle of the group’s age range.

This difference matters for understanding the root. Being in the middle can describe a measurable position. Applied to conduct, opinion or human standing, however, it can acquire the sense of sound judgement, justice or worthiness.

One way to explain this semantic extension is to consider someone who does not incline unjustifiably towards one side in judgement and respects both parties’ rights, avoiding excess and deficiency. Here the image of being away from a thing’s two sides transfers to human judgement and conduct.

This image should not become a certain historical account of justice developing from the spatial meaning of middle. Available evidence shows both uses in Arabic, but does not alone establish that every ethical meaning of the root arose from one material image.

Excellence and justice are also distinct. Someone can be socially prominent without being just. When أوسط describes a person, the sentence must determine which characteristic it highlights.

2. فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا: entering the midst of a gathering

The root’s clearest movement and spatial use appears in Al-ʿĀdiyāt:

«وَالْعَادِيَاتِ ضَبْحًا ۝ فَالْمُورِيَاتِ قَدْحًا ۝ فَالْمُغِيرَاتِ صُبْحًا ۝ فَأَثَرْنَ بِهِ نَقْعًا ۝ فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا»

“By those running, panting; then those striking sparks; then those raiding at dawn; then raising dust by it; then entering the midst of a gathering by it.”

(Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:1–5)

فَوَسَطْنَ comes from the basic Form I verb وَسَطَ. It is third-person feminine plural and refers to the same group described in the preceding verses, first through active participles and then through a verb.

These verses describe rapid movement, striking sparks, a dawn raid and rising dust. At the end, the same moving creatures enter the midst of a gathering.

The familiar interpretation identifies the scene as swift horses in a raid. Some other interpretations identify moving camels and understand جَمْعًا as the place name Jamʿ, meaning Muzdalifah. This difference in interpreting the scene must be distinguished from a difference in recitation or the verb’s form.

The disagreement concerns identifying the scene and the verses’ referents. In the horse interpretation, the verb denotes entering the midst of a gathering; in the Muzdalifah interpretation, it denotes reaching the middle of a place. The verb’s spatial core remains in both, but جمع does not necessarily mean a group of people in both interpretations.

The role of بِهِ in the sentence

The pronoun in بِهِ also needs attention in فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا. Various accounts of its antecedent exist, including the preceding dawn, movement or place, and نَقْع, dust. In the last interpretation, the phrase can describe entering the gathering while enveloped in dust. The basic rendering “by it” retains the pronoun; identifying its antecedent requires a separate explanation.

Disagreement about the pronoun nevertheless does not remove وَسَطْنَ’s spatial core. When جمع denotes a human gathering, the verb concerns entering its midst; when Jamʿ is taken as a place name, it concerns reaching the middle of that place.

وسط has no necessary ethical meaning here. Entering a group’s centre is neither justice nor excellence in itself. Even if the scene is understood as a military raid, the conduct’s value must be examined through other contextual evidence, rather than through the root alone.

This use is an important test for the root’s final definition. If وسط always means just or best, فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا no longer admits a natural and accurate translation.

3. مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ: an intermediate standard in a financial responsibility

Al-Māʾidah 5:89 says:

«لَا يُؤَاخِذُكُمُ اللَّهُ بِاللَّغْوِ فِي أَيْمَانِكُمْ وَلَٰكِنْ يُؤَاخِذُكُمْ بِمَا عَقَّدْتُمُ الْأَيْمَانَ فَكَفَّارَتُهُ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ أَوْ كِسْوَتُهُمْ أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ فَمَنْ لَمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ ذَٰلِكَ كَفَّارَةُ أَيْمَانِكُمْ إِذَا حَلَفْتُمْ وَاحْفَظُوا أَيْمَانَكُمْ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ آيَاتِهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ»

“The God does not hold you accountable for unintentional speech in your oaths, but holds you accountable for the oaths you bind firmly. Its expiation is therefore feeding ten people in need from the intermediate food you feed your families, or clothing them, or freeing a person in bondage. Whoever cannot find the means must fast three days. That is the expiation for your oaths when you have sworn. Guard your oaths. Thus The God makes His signs clear to you, so that you may give thanks.”

(Al-Māʾidah 5:89)

أَوْسَط here concerns food. The subject is expiation for an oath for which someone is responsible, and one means of expiation is feeding ten people in need.

مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ refers to food at an intermediate level within what people feed their families. The standard therefore comes from the family’s own life and ordinary food.

This differs from فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا. There the subject was entering the midst of a gathering; here being intermediate concerns food’s quantity or quality.

Does awsaṭ mean the best food?

Lexical sources allow أوسط to denote either an intermediate standard or something’s best or most worthy form. Accordingly, explanations of this verse bring intermediate food, equitable food and suitable, worthy food into semantic proximity.

The verse does not simply say to give one’s best food, nor to give the least valuable food one can find. It connects the standard to what someone feeds their own family.

This qualification prevents two extreme readings. Someone cannot be required solely on this basis to provide the most expensive food they occasionally purchase. Equally, intermediate food cannot automatically be reduced to the cheapest and most unsuitable food.

أوسط here therefore relates to an ordinary, measured standard of food. Determining the exact amount goes beyond the root’s lexical meaning and requires a complete examination of the ruling and its circumstances.

Intermediate relative to what?

This question matters for understanding the verse. أوسط does not itself establish an independent standard such as a fixed weight, a uniform price or a particular food.

The Qur’an says مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ: what you feed your families. The reference standard thus appears within the sentence itself.

One family’s ordinary food may differ from another’s. The root و س ط alone therefore cannot produce a fixed list of foods for everyone in every period.

This use shows that being intermediate does not always concern two spatial points. It can also concern two limits of quantity, quality or cost.

Intermediate here nevertheless does not mean the mathematical average of all the family’s food. The verse offers an intelligible practical standard for everyday life, rather than a numerical calculation whose procedure it specifies.

Here أوسط approaches sound judgement and proportion. This proximity should not become a claim that the word means legal justice in every use.

4. قَالَ أَوْسَطُهُمْ: the most judicious of the garden owners

Al-Qalam recounts the story of garden owners who decided to harvest their produce early in the morning and prevent anyone in need from entering their garden.

The Qur’an quotes their words as follows:

«أَنْ لَا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا الْيَوْمَ عَلَيْكُمْ مِسْكِينٌ»

“That no person in need should enter it upon you today.”

(Al-Qalam 68:24)

On reaching the garden, however, they find it in a different condition and realise that what they expected has been lost. At this point the text says:

«قَالَ أَوْسَطُهُمْ أَلَمْ أَقُلْ لَكُمْ لَوْلَا تُسَبِّحُونَ»

“The most judicious of them said: ‘Did I not tell you: Why do you not glorify?’”

(Al-Qalam 68:28)

أَوْسَطُهُمْ is one member of that same group. Unlike Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:5, however, this passage is not describing a position in a spatial centre.

His speech distinguishes him from the others. He had previously called them to glorification and now recalls that warning.

In the next verse, the others say:

«قَالُوا سُبْحَانَ رَبِّنَا إِنَّا كُنَّا ظَالِمِينَ»

“They said: ‘Glory be to our Lord; we were indeed wrongdoers.’”

(Al-Qalam 68:29)

This response places the person’s words in the context of remembering The God and recognising their wrongful conduct.

Awsaṭuhum: age or judgement?

Arabic أَوْسَط can describe someone among the members of a group or at an intermediate level of standing. It can also refer to their most worthy and just member.

The garden owners’ story provides no clear indication that the Qur’an intends to specify this person’s age, height, place of standing or birth order.

His words, by contrast, differ from the others’ behaviour. Understanding أَوْسَطُهُمْ as their most judicious or just member, or the person with the sounder judgement, therefore fits the context better.

This description should not establish that he was the group’s best person in every ethical quality or that he never shared in their common decision. The verse quotes particular words from him and highlights his position in that episode.

Does being in the middle mean neutrality?

The person called أَوْسَطُهُمْ speaks against the others’ conduct in this story. He does not remain silent between right and wrong to maintain an apparent balance.

This matters. Ethical وسط does not necessarily mean standing equidistant between two claims. Sometimes sound conduct requires recognising error and speaking against it.

Being intermediate here relates both to sound judgement and to recalling responsibility before The God. The root و س ط itself, however, means neither glorification, repentance nor condemnation of injustice. These concepts emerge from the characters’ words and the story’s continuation.

Comparison with Al-Māʾidah 5:89 shows أوسط applied to food in one verse and a person in the other. Both involve an intermediate, measured standard, but what is assessed differs.

5. وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا: a community described as wasaṭ

Al-Baqarah 2:143 says:

«وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا لِتَكُونُوا شُهَدَاءَ عَلَى النَّاسِ وَيَكُونَ الرَّسُولُ عَلَيْكُمْ شَهِيدًا وَمَا جَعَلْنَا الْقِبْلَةَ الَّتِي كُنْتَ عَلَيْهَا إِلَّا لِنَعْلَمَ مَنْ يَتَّبِعُ الرَّسُولَ مِمَّنْ يَنْقَلِبُ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيْهِ وَإِنْ كَانَتْ لَكَبِيرَةً إِلَّا عَلَى الَّذِينَ هَدَى اللَّهُ وَمَا كَانَ اللَّهُ لِيُضِيعَ إِيمَانَكُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِالنَّاسِ لَرَءُوفٌ رَحِيمٌ»

“And thus We made you an intermediate and judicious community, so that you may be witnesses over people and the Messenger may be a witness over you. We appointed the qiblah you previously followed only so that We may know who follows the Messenger as distinct from who turns back on his heels. It was indeed difficult, except for those whom The God guided. The God would never let your faith go to waste. Surely The God is deeply compassionate and merciful towards people.”

(Al-Baqarah 2:143)

This is the root و س ط’s best-known Qur’anic occurrence. وَسَطًا describes أُمَّةً, community, and relates directly to لِتَكُونُوا شُهَدَاءَ عَلَى النَّاسِ.

Two matters must be examined separately: what وسط means when describing a community, and the responsibility the Qur’an subsequently sets out for that community.

Does ummatan wasaṭan mean a community at the world’s centre?

Spatial وسط can denote the centre or middle of something. Here, however, the subject is a human community, and no geographical boundaries are specified for measuring its centre.

أُمَّةً وَسَطًا alone therefore does not establish that the community lies geographically at the earth’s centre, between the continents or at the central point of the world.

The verse continues with witnessing over people and the Messenger witnessing over those addressed. This associates the description with human standing and responsibility rather than geographical position.

Arabic وَسَط can describe a group as just, judicious or worthy. Of these meanings, justice and sound judgement have an evident connection with responsibility for witnessing.

This relationship does not make وسط lexically identical to witness. The Qur’an uses two distinct words, first describing the community and then stating a purpose or responsibility introduced by لِتَكُونُوا.

Wasaṭ and justice

In judgement, a just person must not disregard another party’s rights merely through affection, hostility or allegiance to one side. This conduct can be explained through the image of being in the middle: the judge does not incline unjustifiably towards one side.

Being in the middle here does not mean equal physical distance from two people. One party may be entirely in the right, and justice may require the judge to state that right.

Justice therefore means neither reconciling two parties at any cost nor always dividing the right between them. A just person can oppose a false claim while remaining free of improper partiality.

This explanation also fits أَوْسَطُهُمْ in Al-Qalam 68:28. That person spoke against his group’s conduct rather than avoiding judgement to preserve an intermediate position.

In أُمَّةً وَسَطًا, then, being intermediate can relate to justice and sound judgement. It should not become neutrality between truth and falsehood or perpetual compromise between two positions.

لِتَكُونُوا شُهَدَاءَ عَلَى النَّاسِ

Immediately after describing the community, the Qur’an says:

«لِتَكُونُوا شُهَدَاءَ عَلَى النَّاسِ وَيَكُونَ الرَّسُولُ عَلَيْكُمْ شَهِيدًا»

“So that you may be witnesses over people and the Messenger may be a witness over you.”

This phrase presents responsibility for witnessing in two directions. Those addressed have a position of witnessing over people, while the Messenger is a witness over them.

Witnessing in Qur’anic language concerns testimony and awareness. Its full meaning, its time and manner, and the responsibilities of those identified as witnesses require examination of the root ش ه د and the other relevant verses.

What matters for و س ط is the description’s connection with this responsibility: being intermediate and judicious.

The phrase does not establish that every individual is just in every judgement merely through nominal membership of a community. The verse itself speaks of testing whether people follow or turn back on their heels and distinguishes different human behaviours.

أُمَّةً وَسَطًا therefore should not become a guarantee of each person’s ethical superiority or unrestricted permission to judge others. The verse describes a position and responsibility associated with witnessing; the validity of any testimony is inseparable from its truth and justice.

The relationship between ummatan wasaṭan and the qiblah

Verse 143 occurs among the qiblah verses. Immediately before it, the Qur’an says:

«قُلْ لِلَّهِ الْمَشْرِقُ وَالْمَغْرِبُ يَهْدِي مَنْ يَشَاءُ إِلَىٰ صِرَاطٍ مُسْتَقِيمٍ»

“Say: ‘The east and west belong to The God. He guides whom He wills to a straight path.’”

(Al-Baqarah 2:142; part of the verse)

أُمَّةً وَسَطًا follows, and the same verse discusses the former qiblah, following the Messenger and turning back on one’s heels.

This continuity places the community’s description within a discussion of orientation and following. The qiblah in these verses is a spatial reality and direction, while people’s responses to it also concern faith and following.

The presence of وسط in the qiblah verses nevertheless does not restrict its meaning to a geographical centre or an intermediate position between two qiblahs.

Equally, the verse’s concern with justice and witnessing does not make the qiblah’s spatial direction in the following verses merely symbolic. Each word and command must be examined in its own position.

وسط here describes the community; qiblah concerns the direction of facing; and testimony concerns the responsibility for witnessing. The topics connect within one context without cancelling each other’s lexical meanings.

Does wasaṭ mean superiority over all communities?

Lexical sources sometimes apply وسط and أوسط to a group’s worthy and distinguished members. Hence أُمَّةً وَسَطًا has also been understood as a chosen or worthy community.

This accords with some positive uses of the word, but should not become an unqualified claim of a group’s absolute, hereditary superiority over all people.

The verse itself connects the description with witnessing and a test of following. In the same passage, the Qur’an distinguishes those following the Messenger from those turning back.

Elsewhere in the Qur’an, justice, taqwa, faith and good conduct are expressed through standards that mere family or religious affiliation cannot replace.

If وسط here conveys worthiness and excellence, these should therefore be understood in relation to justice, guidance and responsibility for witnessing, rather than as a fixed characteristic of anyone claiming a community’s name.

6. وَالصَّلَاةِ الْوُسْطَىٰ: the middle salat or the superior salat?

Al-Baqarah 2:238 says:

«حَافِظُوا عَلَى الصَّلَوَاتِ وَالصَّلَاةِ الْوُسْطَىٰ وَقُومُوا لِلَّهِ قَانِتِينَ»

“Maintain the salats and the middle salat, and stand obediently for The God.”

(Al-Baqarah 2:238)

الْوُسْطَىٰ is the feminine form of أَوْسَط and describes الصَّلَاة here. This formation can denote something intermediate among several items or distinguished in standing and worthiness.

Just as Arabic أوسط can denote an intermediate level or a more worthy option, وسطى allows both semantic fields.

الصلاة الوسطى therefore admits two linguistic interpretations: a salat intermediate in its ordering among the other salats, or one distinguished by its importance and excellence.

Does wusṭā necessarily mean a salat at midday?

وسطى alone does not identify a salat’s exact time.

An interpretation based on temporal ordering must establish which salats belong to the relevant set and what makes one intermediate among them. Verse 238 alone, however, does not specify their exact number or each one’s time.

Older reports identify الصلاة الوسطى with the afternoon salat, the dawn salat and, in some accounts, other salats. This disagreement shows that its precise identification did not come from the form وسطى alone.

If وسطى instead means superior or more worthy, one must still ask what distinguishes this salat. The word alone states neither the reason for its superiority nor the practice’s details.

One therefore cannot assert without evidence that وسطى necessarily denotes only the middle of the day. Nor can its temporal meaning be excluded from the outset and replaced solely with excellence or an inward state.

Why is one salat mentioned separately?

The verse first says حَافِظُوا عَلَى الصَّلَوَاتِ and then separately mentions وَالصَّلَاةِ الْوُسْطَىٰ.

This construction draws particular attention to one salat. Mentioning a specific item after a set to which it can belong is an established Arabic way of highlighting it.

This coordination alone does not establish that الصلاة الوسطى is a practice wholly unrelated to the other salats. Nor can its entire form and timing be derived solely from this emphasis.

The verse ends with وَقُومُوا لِلَّهِ قَانِتِينَ. This command concerns standing and obedience before The God within the context of maintaining the salats.

Qunūt, obedient devotion, nevertheless comes from a different root and should not be inserted into وسطى’s meaning. Being intermediate, obedience and maintaining salat are three connected but distinct concepts.

Is al-ṣalāt al-wusṭā simply moderation in worship?

Moderation in worship can be discussed ethically, but that discussion alone does not determine الصلاة الوسطى’s meaning.

الْوُسْطَىٰ here describes one particular صلاة alongside other salats. The text concerns maintaining them and singles one out through the description wusṭā.

Rendering the phrase as balanced worship or moderation in all religious acts therefore introduces a meaning broader than the verse’s direct construction.

By contrast, وسطى’s possible sense of greater worthiness or excellence is a real linguistic possibility and should not be excluded solely because the temporal meaning is familiar.

The basic rendering “the middle salat” preserves the linguistic image. Explanation should distinguish identifying its precise referent from choosing between ordering and excellence as its meaning.

7. Differences from neighbouring roots and translation suggestions

و س ط (وَسَط) and ع د ل (عَدَلَ)

ʿAdl concerns justice, correct assessment and avoidance of improper partiality. Wasaṭ approaches justice in some ethical and social uses, but can also express a spatial position or an intermediate standard.

In Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:5, فَوَسَطْنَ denotes entering the midst of a gathering, without meaning justice. In Al-Qalam 68:28, أَوْسَطُهُمْ relates to sound and just judgement.

Justice is therefore one associated semantic use of wasaṭ, rather than the fixed equivalent of every derivative.

A just person also does not always stand halfway between truth and falsehood. Justice may require rejecting one claim entirely and accepting the other.

و س ط (وَسَط) and ق ص د (قَصَدَ)

Arabic qaṣd concerns turning towards a goal, a straight course and, in some uses, moderation.

Luqmān 31:19 says:

«وَاقْصِدْ فِي مَشْيِكَ وَاغْضُضْ مِنْ صَوْتِكَ إِنَّ أَنْكَرَ الْأَصْوَاتِ لَصَوْتُ الْحَمِيرِ»

“Be measured in your walking and lower your voice; surely the most objectionable of voices is the voice of donkeys.”

(Luqmān 31:19)

وَاقْصِدْ here concerns measured conduct in walking. This approaches some ethical uses of wasaṭ, but the two roots are distinct.

Qaṣd can emphasise direction and aiming at a goal; wasaṭ more commonly concerns a position between two sides or an intermediate standard. These fields can meet in human conduct.

و س ط (وَسَط) and ب ي ن (بَيْن)

Bayn concerns the interval or relationship between two or more things. Spatial وَسْط can likewise approach among or between.

جَلَسَ وَسْطَ الْقَوْمِ, for example, means that someone sat among the people. This approaches بَيْنَ الْقَوْمِ.

Bayn does not itself mean best or most just. Something between two things also need not occupy the precise centre or divide their distance equally.

The meaning of bayn therefore cannot establish all of wasaṭ’s evaluative uses.

و س ط (أَوْسَط) and خ ي ر (خَيْر)

Some Arabic uses give أوسط the sense of a group’s more worthy or best member. It consequently overlaps with khayr and afḍal, better or best.

Qur’anic khayr can denote goodness, benefit or superiority, whereas other uses of wasaṭ express only a spatial middle or an intermediate level.

أَوْسَط concerns food in Al-Māʾidah 5:89 and a person in Al-Qalam 68:28. The difference shows why every awsaṭ should not immediately be rendered best.

Wasaṭ and wasīṭ: being intermediate and acting as an intermediary

Arabic وَسِيط and وَسَاطَة can concern a person or activity standing between two parties to facilitate contact or resolve a dispute.

The spatial image of standing between two parties makes this use intelligible. The form wasīṭ itself, however, does not occur in the Qur’an.

أُمَّةً وَسَطًا therefore cannot be translated as a community mediating between The God and people solely on the basis of a shared root. The verse concerns the community’s witnessing; وسط itself does not confer a position of mediation or authority to intercede.

Similarly, contemporary terms such as wasaṭiyyah or moderation can acquire particular political and religious meanings. These later terms should not replace وسط’s actual Qur’anic uses without examination.

Translation suggestions for Forqan

In the human-gathering interpretation of Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:5, فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا is best rendered “then, by it, they entered the midst of a gathering.” The explanation must clarify بِهِ’s antecedent. If it refers to dust, for example, the rendering is “then, in that dust, they entered the midst of a gathering.” This retains the verb’s spatial and movement meaning.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:89, مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ can be rendered “from the intermediate food you feed your families.” Explanation can identify an ordinary, measured food standard, rather than necessarily the most or least expensive food.

قَالَ أَوْسَطُهُمْ in Al-Qalam 68:28 is aptly rendered “the most judicious of them said” or “the most just of them said.” The context provides no clear support for someone standing in the middle.

أُمَّةً وَسَطًا in Al-Baqarah 2:143 can be rendered “an intermediate and judicious community.” “A just community” also conveys a valid contextual meaning, but makes the image of an intermediate position less apparent. Explanation should clarify its relationship with justice, worthiness and responsibility for witnessing.

الصَّلَاةِ الْوُسْطَىٰ in Al-Baqarah 2:238 should be rendered “the middle salat,” with the established disagreement about an intermediate position or superiority explained separately.

None of these equivalents should be transferred mechanically to every verse. Wasaṭ is spatial in one verse, ethical in another and a standard of quantity elsewhere. Translation must preserve this actual difference.

8. Conclusion

Arabic و س ط is primarily recognised through being in the middle of something or among its surrounding parts. The same linguistic field also has uses concerning an intermediate standard, sound judgement, justice and worthiness. These uses are nevertheless distinct; not every intermediate position is automatically best or most just.

The root’s five Qur’anic uses make this difference clear. Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:5 concerns entering the midst of a gathering; Al-Māʾidah 5:89, food at an intermediate level within what a family eats; and Al-Qalam 68:28, someone with sounder judgement within his group. In Al-Baqarah 2:143, wasaṭ describes a community introduced through its responsibility for witnessing. In Al-Baqarah 2:238, wusṭā describes a salat singled out among the salats.

This set of uses shows that being intermediate does not always mean mathematically equal distances. It can concern a spatial position, an ordinary level between quantities, or a description of human judgement and conduct.

Ethical wasaṭ also does not mean perpetual compromise or neutrality between right and wrong. In the garden owners’ story, the more judicious person is the one who called the others to remember The God. In Al-Baqarah 2:143, the description accompanies responsibility for witnessing, which cannot be separated from justice and truthfulness.

These concepts nevertheless should not all become part of the root’s lexical definition. Witnessing, justice, salat and social responsibilities each have their own words and constructions. Within each context, wasaṭ contributes only its own relevant meaning.

The semantic core of و س ط is being in the middle of something or between two sides or limits. In uses concerning quantity and conduct, it can extend to an ordinary standard, sound judgement and avoiding excess and deficiency; in some evaluative uses, it can denote justice or worthiness.

This definition retains فَوَسَطْنَ’s spatial meaning and explains أَوْسَط and وَسَطًا without making justice the fixed meaning of every derivative or every intermediate level the best choice.

أُمَّةً وَسَطًا should consequently be understood in connection with justice, sound judgement and responsibility for witnessing; الصَّلَاةِ الْوُسْطَىٰ should be examined within the construction concerning salats; and أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ should be assessed against the family’s actual food standard. All three belong to one linguistic family, but the Qur’an uses that family in three different settings.
