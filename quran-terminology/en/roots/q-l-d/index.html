Qur’anic Root Q L D

Twisting and hanging; neck-garlands, designation, entrusted responsibility, and keys of authority

1. Basic meaning and linguistic background

Arabic Q L D concerns twisting one thing around another, hanging something around the neck, and attaching it to a person or creature. Material uses include twisting cord, forming metal rings, and hanging neck ornaments.

In the Arabic of revelation, a neck-garland could serve as ornament or identification for a person or animal. Historical accounts of pre-Islamic pilgrimage describe garlands on sacrificial offerings and neck ornaments indicating protection. Recorded in later sources, these accounts are not direct pre-Qur’anic witnesses.

Another classical Arabic branch concerns a share or turn, especially a water allotment or irrigation turn.

Maqālīd appears under Q L D in lexical works and Qur’anic indexes and is explained as keys or treasuries. Its inclusion here follows that arrangement of entries; it does not establish historical derivation from qilādah. It is examined separately from twisting and hanging below.

Qur’anic forms indexed under this entry: four occurrences, two of qalāʾid and two of maqālīd.

  • Qalāʾid: neck-garlands and identifying objects hung around the neck.
  • Maqālīd: keys; in Qur’anic context, an image of God’s authority over the heavens and earth.

Other Arabic forms include qald, twisting; qilādah, a neck ornament; taqlīd, hanging a garland or entrusting responsibility; taqallud, assuming responsibility; and qild, a share or turn. These forms do not occur in the Qur’an.

2. Material imagery and recorded Arabic uses

Twisting and hanging

Qalada al-ḥabla means twisting a rope. Winding a metal strand around another to make a bracelet or ring is also expressed through this root.

Qilādah names something hung around the neck, whether a human ornament, an animal’s collar, or an identifying marker.

The root also describes wearing a sword suspended from the neck and shoulder. Taqallada al-sayfa means putting on and carrying a sword.

A qilādah is not inherently an instrument of captivity or restricted freedom. Its function depends on its kind and purpose.

A garland as a sign of designation

In pilgrimage rites, an animal designated as an offering was distinguished by a marker hung around its neck. It could be made from a strap, cord, tree bark, or other material.

The marker’s significance lies in its message rather than its material or monetary value: the animal bearing it has been designated for a particular purpose.

A share or turn

Qild denotes a person’s specified water share or a turn at irrigation.

Where farmers shared a water source, each could have a designated time to use it. Qild names that allotment or turn.

Yataqāladūna al-māʾ likewise means taking turns using water.

The term also describes the periodic return of a fever.

This branch is recorded in classical Arabic sources, but its historical relationship to twisting and hanging garlands is not established with certainty.

3. Qalāʾid in the Qur’an: signs of inviolability and designation

Qalāʾid is the broken plural of qilādah. Both Qur’anic occurrences are in Al-Māʾidah, in contexts of rites, inviolability, and security.

Respecting the inviolability of qalāʾid

يٰأَيُّهَا الَّذينَ ءامَنوا لا تُحِلّوا شَعٰئِرَ اللَّهِ وَلَا الشَّهرَ الحَرامَ وَلَا الهَدىَ وَلَا القَلٰئِدَ وَلا ءامّينَ البَيتَ الحَرامَ يَبتَغونَ فَضلًا مِن رَبِّهِم وَرِضوٰنًا وَإِذا حَلَلتُم فَاصطادوا وَلا يَجرِمَنَّكُم شَنَـٔانُ قَومٍ أَن صَدّوكُم عَنِ المَسجِدِ الحَرامِ أَن تَعتَدوا وَتَعاوَنوا عَلَى البِرِّ وَالتَّقوىٰ وَلا تَعاوَنوا عَلَى الإِثمِ وَالعُدوٰنِ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ شَديدُ العِقابِ

Yā ayyuhā lladhīna āmanū lā tuḥillū shaʿāʾira llāhi wa-lā sh-shahra l-ḥarāma wa-lā l-hadya wa-lā l-qalāʾida wa-lā āmmīna l-bayta l-ḥarāma yabtaghūna faḍlan min rabbihim wa-riḍwānā. Wa-idhā ḥalaltum fa-ṣṭādū. Wa-lā yajrimannakum shanaʾānu qawmin an ṣaddūkum ʿani l-masjidi l-ḥarāmi an taʿtadū. Wa-taʿāwanū ʿalā l-birri wa-t-taqwā wa-lā taʿāwanū ʿalā l-ithmi wa-l-ʿudwān. Wa-ttaqū llāha inna llāha shadīdu l-ʿiqāb.

“O you who have believed, do not violate the rites of The God, the sacred month, the sacrificial offerings, the garlands, or those proceeding to the Sacred House seeking bounty and approval from their Lord. When you leave the consecrated state, hunt. Do not let hatred of a people who barred you from the Sacred Mosque lead you to transgress. Cooperate in righteousness and mindfulness, and do not cooperate in sin and aggression. Be mindful of The God; indeed, The God is severe in punishment.”

Al-Ma'idah 5:2

Syntactically, al-qalāʾida is coordinated with the other objects whose violation is prohibited by lā tuḥillū.

Hady denotes offerings taken toward the destination of the rites; qalāʾid are the markers making their designation visible.

In explaining the verse, the name of a marker can also refer to its bearer. Some commentaries accordingly interpret qalāʾid as garlanded animals. This is contextual interpretation and does not erase the basic sense of neck-garlands.

Historical explanations propose two referents: markers on sacrificial animals, and ornaments worn by some pilgrims or placed on their mounts for protection.

The association with offerings and people proceeding to the Sacred House makes both uses relevant to examination. The verse does not specify the garlands’ material, shape, or manner of attachment.

Their inviolability does not arise from the ornaments’ monetary value. They signal designation for the rites or a bearer’s association with them. Respecting them contributes to the rites’ security and order.

The context sharpens the point: even hatred of people who previously prevented believers from reaching the Sacred Mosque must not become a pretext for aggression.

Qalāʾid within arrangements sustaining people’s affairs

جَعَلَ اللَّهُ الكَعبَةَ البَيتَ الحَرامَ قِيٰمًا لِلنّاسِ وَالشَّهرَ الحَرامَ وَالهَدىَ وَالقَلٰئِدَ ذٰلِكَ لِتَعلَموا أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَعلَمُ ما فِى السَّمٰوٰتِ وَما فِى الأَرضِ وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ بِكُلِّ شَىءٍ عَليمٌ

Jaʿala llāhu l-kaʿbata l-bayta l-ḥarāma qiyāman li-n-nāsi wa-sh-shahra l-ḥarāma wa-l-hadya wa-l-qalāʾid. Dhālika li-taʿlamū anna llāha yaʿlamu mā fī s-samāwāti wa-mā fī l-arḍi wa-anna llāha bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm.

“The God made the Kaʿbah, the Sacred House, a support for people, and the sacred month, the sacrificial offerings, and the garlands. That is so you may know that The God knows what is in the heavens and what is on earth, and that The God knows all things.”

Al-Ma'idah 5:97

Here again, qalāʾid accompanies the offerings, sacred month, and Kaʿbah. All are related in the sentence to qiyāman li-l-nās.

Qiyām here concerns sustaining, stabilizing, and ordering people’s affairs.

Recognition of inviolable times, places, and signs can support safe travel, gathering, and performance of rites. This explains a social function of the verse; it is not an independent historical account of every traveler’s circumstances.

A garland in such an arrangement communicates designation for the rites. Respecting the marker can prevent aggression; the verse does not report that everyone actually respected it.

Li-l-nās, “for people,” is significant: the arrangement sustains people’s affairs and serves a function beyond an individual’s devotional behavior.

Listing the garlands among inviolable things does not, by itself, independently command garlanding every sacrificial animal. Respecting an existing sign and requiring its production or use are distinct rulings.

4. Maqālīd: the keys of authority over the heavens and earth

The linguistic origin of maqālīd

Classical dictionaries propose forms such as iqlīd, miqlīd, and miqlād in explaining maqālīd. Keys and treasuries are established explanations. Determining a word’s meaning must be distinguished from establishing its historical origin.

Some regard it as the plural of iqlīd, others of miqlīd or miqlād, while others record no current singular for it.

Lexical sources also describe iqlīd as Arabized and connect it with Persian kilīd, “key.” This report alone does not establish the exact transmission route or ultimate source language. This study does not posit a definitive chain of borrowing between languages.

Placing maqālīd under Q L D in Arabic sources therefore does not prove that “keys” developed from twisting rope or hanging garlands.

This article selects “keys” for maqālīd, consistent with both verse contexts. “Treasuries” is also a recorded lexical explanation; choosing “keys” does not invalidate it.

Maqālīd and authority over creation

لَهُ مَقاليدُ السَّمٰوٰتِ وَالأَرضِ وَالَّذينَ كَفَروا بِـٔايٰتِ اللَّهِ أُولٰئِكَ هُمُ الخٰسِرونَ

Lahu maqālīdu s-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ. Wa-lladhīna kafarū bi-āyāti llāhi ulāʾika humu l-khāsirūn.

“To Him belong the keys of the heavens and earth, and those who deny the signs of The God are the losers.”

Az-Zumar 39:63

The preceding verse states:

اللَّهُ خٰلِقُ كُلِّ شَىءٍ وَهُوَ عَلىٰ كُلِّ شَىءٍ وَكيلٌ

Allāhu khāliqu kulli shayʾin wa-huwa ʿalā kulli shayʾin wakīl.

“The God is the Creator of all things, and He is Trustee over all things.”

Az-Zumar 39:62

The relationship between the verses clarifies maqālīd: authority and governance over creation belong to its Creator.

In human life, possession of a house or treasury key can indicate authority to enter, open, and use it. The keys of the heavens and earth present an intelligible image of the scope of divine authority.

Syntactically, lahu is a fronted predicate and maqālīdu the delayed subject. The fronting, together with context, emphasizes that this authority belongs to God.

The key is an image of authority and governance, not a material instrument God needs in order to open the heavens and earth.

Maqālīd and the extent of provision

لَهُ مَقاليدُ السَّمٰوٰتِ وَالأَرضِ يَبسُطُ الرِّزقَ لِمَن يَشاءُ وَيَقدِرُ إِنَّهُ بِكُلِّ شَىءٍ عَليمٌ

Lahu maqālīdu s-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi yabsuṭu r-rizqa li-man yashāʾu wa-yaqdir. Innahu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm.

“To Him belong the keys of the heavens and earth. He expands provision for whom He wills and restricts it. Indeed, He knows all things.”

Ash-Shura 42:12

Here maqālīd accompanies the expansion and restriction of provision.

Qur’anic rizq is not restricted to money, food, or material possessions. Its broad field includes the means of life and what people benefit from.

Possessing a treasury’s key concerns control over access to its contents. In this context, maqālīd points to divine authority over the sources of provision.

Elsewhere the Qur’an expresses this relationship through khazāʾin:

هُمُ الَّذينَ يَقولونَ لا تُنفِقوا عَلىٰ مَن عِندَ رَسولِ اللَّهِ حَتّىٰ يَنفَضّوا وَلِلَّهِ خَزائِنُ السَّمٰوٰتِ وَالأَرضِ وَلٰكِنَّ المُنٰفِقينَ لا يَفقَهونَ

Humu lladhīna yaqūlūna lā tunfiqū ʿalā man ʿinda rasūli llāhi ḥattā yanfaḍḍū. Wa-li-llāhi khazāʾinu s-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi wa-lākinna l-munāfiqīna lā yafqahūn.

“They are the ones who say, ‘Do not spend on those with the Messenger of The God until they disperse.’ Yet to The God belong the treasuries of the heavens and earth, but the hypocrites do not understand.”

Al-Munafiqun 63:7

The hypocrites supposed that withholding their financial assistance would disperse the community around the Messenger. The Qur’an answers that the treasuries of the heavens and earth belong to God.

Khazāʾin and maqālīd approach the shared subject from different angles: the sources and stores of blessings, and authority to open access and enable benefit.

5. Taqlīd and the development of usage

The material image of hanging a garland around a neck helps explain figurative uses involving entrusting and accepting responsibility.

Qalladahu al-amra means entrusting someone with a task and laying it upon them. Taqallada al-amra means accepting and assuming that responsibility.

Arabic can use taqlīd and taqallud when someone is appointed to office or entrusted with responsibility.

Nothing is physically hung around the person’s neck; responsibility is placed upon them.

Taqlīd as a religious technical term

In later jurisprudence and legal theory, taqlīd generally means accepting another’s opinion without independently knowing its detailed evidence. The precise formulation is not identical across schools.

The image of entrusting or assuming something can help explain the term. Such an illustration does not establish its historical development or decide its validity.

This juristic term must not be read back into Qur’anic qalāʾid or maqālīd. The Qur’an does not use the form taqlīd.

Following others, accepting ancestral views, and responsibility for recognizing truth are expressed through other words and constructions:

وَإِذا قيلَ لَهُمُ اتَّبِعوا ما أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ قالوا بَل نَتَّبِعُ ما أَلفَينا عَلَيهِ ءاباءَنا أَوَلَو كانَ ءاباؤُهُم لا يَعقِلونَ شَيـًٔا وَلا يَهتَدونَ

Wa-idhā qīla lahumu ttabiʿū mā anzala llāhu qālū bal nattabiʿu mā alfaynā ʿalayhi ābāʾanā. A-wa-law kāna ābāʾuhum lā yaʿqilūna shayʾan wa-lā yahtadūn.

“When it is said to them, ‘Follow what The God has sent down,’ they say, ‘Rather, we follow what we found our fathers following.’ Even if their fathers understood nothing and were not guided?”

Al-Baqarah 2:170

وَلا تَقفُ ما لَيسَ لَكَ بِهِ عِلمٌ إِنَّ السَّمعَ وَالبَصَرَ وَالفُؤادَ كُلُّ أُولٰئِكَ كانَ عَنهُ مَسـٔولًا

Wa-lā taqfu mā laysa laka bihi ʿilm. Inna s-samʿa wa-l-baṣara wa-l-fuʾāda kullu ulāʾika kāna ʿanhu masʾūlā.

“Do not follow what you have no knowledge of. Indeed, hearing, sight, and the heart—about all of these there will be questioning.”

Al-Isra 17:36

These verses clarify responsibility for assessing what one accepts and follows.

Learning from others and benefiting from specialist knowledge differ from unconditionally surrendering judgment to them. One may learn from others while remaining responsible for using one’s reason, knowledge, and available capacities.

Evaluation of religious taqlīd should consider these verses and the Qur’an’s teachings as a whole, rather than relying only on the lexical image of a garland.

6. Comparison with related roots

Q L D (qilādah) and Ṭ W Q (ṭawq): a neck ornament and encirclement

Qilādah and ṭawq can both name something placed around the neck.

Qilādah highlights hanging, attachment, and identification; ṭawq more prominently conveys encircling and surrounding.

The Qur’an also uses Ṭ W Q for the consequence of miserliness. Āl ʿImrān 3:180 describes something misers will wear around their necks on the Day of Resurrection.

A qilādah may be an ornament or a marker of designation; a ṭawq may also be ornamental. The distinction does not make one invariably positive and the other negative.

Q L D (qalāʾid) and H D Y (hady): a marker and an offering

Hady denotes something taken as an offering toward the destination of the rites.

Qalāʾid mark the animals’ designation. Historical accounts also suggest markers associated with travelers to the rites.

Possible overlap in referents does not make the words synonymous. The designated animal is hady; the marker around its neck is a qilādah.

Q L D (maqālīd) and F T Ḥ (mafātiḥ): keys and opening

F T Ḥ denotes opening, removing an obstruction, and enabling access.

Al-Anʿām states:

وَعِندَهُ مَفاتِحُ الغَيبِ لا يَعلَمُها إِلّا هُوَ وَيَعلَمُ ما فِى البَرِّ وَالبَحرِ وَما تَسقُطُ مِن وَرَقَةٍ إِلّا يَعلَمُها وَلا حَبَّةٍ فى ظُلُمٰتِ الأَرضِ وَلا رَطبٍ وَلا يابِسٍ إِلّا فى كِتٰبٍ مُبينٍ

Wa-ʿindahu mafātiḥu l-ghaybi lā yaʿlamuhā illā huwa wa-yaʿlamu mā fī l-barri wa-l-baḥr. Wa-mā tasquṭu min waraqatin illā yaʿlamuhā wa-lā ḥabbatin fī ẓulumāti l-arḍi wa-lā raṭbin wa-lā yābisin illā fī kitābin mubīn.

“With Him are the keys of the unseen; none knows them except Him. He knows what is on land and in the sea. No leaf falls without His knowing it, nor is there a grain in the darkness of the earth, or anything moist or dry, except in a clear Book.”

Al-An'am 6:59

Mafātiḥ and maqālīd overlap in the sense of keys and means of access.

In Al-Anʿām, mafātiḥ concerns the unseen and divine knowledge; in Al-Zumar and Al-Shūrā, maqālīd concerns the heavens and earth, creation, and provision.

This difference arises from the phrases and contexts. It does not assign mafātiḥ exclusively to knowledge or maqālīd exclusively to material authority. Mafātiḥ also appears concerning Qārūn’s treasures in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:76.

Q L D (maqālīd) and KH Z N (khazāʾin): keys and treasuries

Khazāʾin denotes repositories and sources of stored things; maqālīd, understood as keys, conveys the means of access and authority to make use of them.

Although treasuries is also recorded as an explanation of maqālīd, “keys” better preserves the distinct image selected here.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

Translate qalāʾid in Al-Māʾidah 5:2 and Al-Māʾidah 5:97 as “garlands” or “marking neck-garlands.”

The marker’s role in designation and inviolability should be explained. In contemporary Persian, qilādah often suggests a collar used to restrain a domestic animal, which may obscure this context.

“Marked sacrificial animals” clarifies one referent but may narrow the word’s range and its possible application to other bearers of such signs.

Translate maqālīd in Al-Zumar 39:63 and Al-Shūrā 42:12 as “keys.” Explain authority, governance, and access to the sources of provision in the commentary.

For non-Qur’anic uses, equivalents include hanging, twisting, a collar or neck ornament, entrusting responsibility, assuming it, and a share or turn, according to the branch.

8. Conclusion

One main branch of Q L D concerns twisting, hanging, and attaching something to a person or creature. Qilādah belongs here and can serve as ornament, identification, or a sign of designation. Entrusting and accepting responsibility are established figurative uses.

Shares and turns, especially in using water, form another recorded branch.

The Qur’an uses qalāʾid in the context of pilgrimage rites, inviolability, and security. Maqālīd appears twice for the keys of the heavens and earth belonging to God, portraying His comprehensive authority over creation and provision.

Proposed Forqan definition: Within Q L D, twisting and hanging, neck ornaments, and entrusting responsibility; within qild, a share or turn. Maqālīd is examined here solely in accordance with the arrangement of lexical entries. Its selected equivalent is “keys,” depicting divine authority in Qur’anic context; historical derivation from qilādah has not been established.
