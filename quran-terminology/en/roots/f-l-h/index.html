Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root F L Ḥ

Breaking through obstruction, coming to fruit, and true success

Qur’anic forms of this root

The root ف ل ح (F L Ḥ) appears in the Qur’an mainly through the Form IV verb and the active participle of that form. This limited usage carries one of the Qur’an’s foundational ideas: falāḥ, the human being’s arrival at true fruit, salvation, real success, and a sound outcome.

The central meaning of the root

The root ف ل ح (F L Ḥ) is simple in structure but rich in meaning. In the Qur’an, it appears mainly in two forms: the Form IV verb and the active participle of that form. Yet this limited usage builds one of the Qur’an’s foundational concepts: falāḥ, meaning that the human being reaches the right fruit, salvation, true success, and a sound outcome.

In Arabic, this root is connected with splitting, cutting, and preparing land for cultivation. The farmer is called fallāḥ because he splits the soil, places the seed in it, cares for it, and waits for fruit. From this sensory image comes the wider meaning of falāḥ: opening the path of growth, passing through obstruction, reaching the right crop, and harvesting the fruit of sound effort.

In the Qur’an, falāḥ is not outward success or temporary victory. Falāḥ means that the human being’s path reaches the right fruit. A person may have power, wealth, reputation, or apparent dominance in this world and still not be mufliḥ in Qur’anic terms. Another person may walk the difficult path of taqwa, tazkiyah, patience, and truth, and finally reach falāḥ.

The central meaning of F L Ḥ can be stated this way: breaking through obstruction and reaching the right fruit. In the Qur’anic layer, the human being must prepare the soil of his own soul. Taqwa, tazkiyah, repentance, salat, zakat, remembrance, patience, and righteous action are ways by which the human soul becomes ready to bear fruit. Falāḥ is not the result of a claim; it is the result of right cultivation, purification, care, and direction.

The sensory layer: the farmer and splitting the soil

The agricultural image is the key that opens this meaning. A fallāḥ is one who splits the earth so that growth can become possible. This splitting is not destruction; it is the threshold of formation and sprouting. The soil must be opened so that the seed may be placed within it.

The same image appears in human life. Sometimes the human being, in order to grow, must break through inner hardness: arrogance, the greed of the self, desire, fear, habit, imitation, and attachment to the world. If these shells are not broken open, the seed of guidance does not come to fruit in the human soul.

From this angle, falāḥ is not merely a happy ending; it is the process of coming to fruit. Qur’anic salvation is the product of work, direction, purity, and formation. Just as the farmer does not harvest without planting and care, the human being does not reach falāḥ without tazkiyah and taqwa.

Why falāḥ is not merely success

Translating falāḥ as success is incomplete if it is left unexplained. In everyday language, success can mean reaching any goal, even if the goal is corrupt. A person may succeed in gathering wealth, deceiving people, gaining political dominance, or building outward power. But the Qur’an does not call that falāḥ.

In the Qur’an, falāḥ is connected with truth, taqwa, tazkiyah, guidance, remembrance of God, repentance, good action, and release from injustice. Therefore, falāḥ is a success whose path is right and whose outcome is sound.

For this reason, the Qur’an says about wrongdoers, criminals, magicians, rejecters, and those who invent lies against God that they do not attain falāḥ. They may appear to reach a result, but that result is not true fruit. A false path may produce temporary gain, but it does not produce falāḥ.

Outward success is often sudden, noisy, and temporary. Falāḥ is like healthy growth: it needs roots, prepared soil, time, and fruit that remains under the measure of truth. For this reason, falāḥ must not be collapsed into every kind of worldly success.

Aflaḥa: the realization of falāḥ

أَفْلَحَ (aflaḥa) is the Form IV past verb. It means he attained falāḥ, was saved in the true sense, or reached a right result.

The Qur’an speaks in several verses with the expression قَدْ أَفْلَحَ (qad aflaḥa). This expression shows realization and seriousness of outcome. In Al-Mu’minun 23:1, it says: قَدْ أَفْلَحَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ (qad aflaḥa al-mu’minūn). Then the qualities of the believers are mentioned: humility in salat, turning away from vain speech, giving zakat, guarding chastity, keeping trusts and covenants, and preserving the salawat.

This structure is very important. The Qur’an does not announce the falāḥ of believers only through the label of faith; it also shows the qualities and effects of faith. Falāḥ is not proven by a religious label alone, but becomes visible through the direction and fruit of life.

In Al-A’la 87:14, it says: قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَنْ تَزَكَّىٰ (qad aflaḥa man tazakkā). Here falāḥ is directly tied to tazkiyah. The one who attains falāḥ is the one who purifies himself, causes himself to grow, and comes out of impurity.

In Ash-Shams 91:9, it says: قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَنْ زَكَّاهَا (qad aflaḥa man zakkāhā). This verse concerns the human self. Falāḥ is attributed to the one who purifies his own self. Therefore, Qur’anic salvation is not complete without working upon the self and purifying the inner being.

Yufliḥu / yufliḥūn: falāḥ or a path left fruitless

The forms يُفْلِحُ (yufliḥu) and يُفْلِحُونَ (yufliḥūn) appear many times in negative statements, such as لَا يُفْلِحُ الظَّالِمُونَ (lā yufliḥu al-ẓālimūn), لَا يُفْلِحُ الْمُجْرِمُونَ (lā yufliḥu al-mujrimūn), لَا يُفْلِحُ السَّاحِرُونَ (lā yufliḥu al-sāḥirūn), and لَا يُفْلِحُ الْكَافِرُونَ (lā yufliḥu al-kāfirūn).

This usage is deeply instructive. The Qur’an does not only say who the successful ones are; it also shows which paths do not reach falāḥ at all. Injustice, crime, magic, rejection, and inventing lies against God may produce temporary outward results, but in terms of outcome they are fruitless.

When the Qur’an says that wrongdoers do not attain falāḥ, it does not mean that the wrongdoer never achieves an apparent victory in the world. A wrongdoer may rule, gather wealth, frighten people, and dominate for a time. But such domination is not falāḥ. Falāḥ means a sound end, pure fruit, and a success that remains in the divine measure.

A false path may create a temporary explosion of power, but it has no healthy root and no lasting fruitfulness. The work of the magician, wrongdoer, and criminal is inwardly barren: it has display, but not sound fruit. Therefore yufliḥu in the Qur’an must be understood through outcome, not immediate gain.

Tufliḥūn / tufliḥū: the path that leads toward falāḥ

The form تُفْلِحُونَ (tufliḥūn) usually appears after commands, warnings, and practical calls. The Qur’an says: have taqwa, remember God, do good, avoid impurities, strive, repent, and then says: لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ (laʿallakum tufliḥūn).

This shows that falāḥ is the result of a formative path. The human being does not reach falāḥ by hearing a claim. He must walk a way that prepares his soul and action to bear fruit.

In Al-Hajj 22:77, the command comes to bow, prostrate, worship the Lord, and do good; then it says: لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ (laʿallakum tufliḥūn). This verse ties falāḥ to worship and practical good.

In An-Nur 24:31, after commands about purity of gaze, covering, and chastity, the believers are called to collective repentance: وَتُوبُوا إِلَى اللَّهِ جَمِيعًا أَيُّهَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ (watūbūā ilā allāh jamīʿanā ayyuha al-muʾminūna laʿallakum tufliḥūna). This shows that falāḥ is also tied to repentance and social repair.

In Al-Ma’idah 5:90, after the prohibition of intoxicants, gambling, idols, and divining arrows, the command to avoid them comes: فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ (fājtanibūhu laʿallakum tufliḥūna). Here falāḥ is tied to cutting away from things that corrupt reason, ethics, justice, and social peace.

The expression laʿallakum is also important. It shows that righteous action is not a mechanical and automatic cause of falāḥ; rather, it prepares the ground and creates the proper condition. The farmer splits the soil, plants the seed, and cares for it, but still needs rain, light, time, and God’s mercy. Human action prepares the ground, but falāḥ finally comes to fruit by God’s favor and judgment.

Al-mufliḥūn / al-mufliḥīn: the people of falāḥ

الْمُفْلِحُونَ (al-mufliḥūn) and الْمُفْلِحِينَ (al-mufliḥīn) are active participles of Form IV. They point to those who have attained falāḥ or are truly upon the path of falāḥ.

At the beginning of Al-Baqarah, after the people of taqwa and guidance are described, it says in 2:5: أُولَٰئِكَ عَلَىٰ هُدًى مِنْ رَبِّهِمْ وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ (ʾūlaāika ʿalā hudanā min rabbihim waʾūlaāika humu al-mufliḥūna). This verse shows that the mufliḥūn are those who stand upon guidance from their Lord. Their falāḥ is the fruit of guidance, not mere group belonging.

In Al-Imran 3:104, the group that calls to good, commands what is recognized as right, and restrains from what is rejected is called the mufliḥūn. This shows that falāḥ is not only an individual matter; it is also tied to social reform, calling to good, and preventing corruption.

In other verses, heavy scales, following the light sent down, hearing and obeying, seeking the Face of God, and being saved from the greed of the self are tied to the mufliḥūn. The mufliḥūn are those in whom guidance has come to fruit in being and action.

Falāḥ and tazkiyah

The connection between falāḥ and tazkiyah is one of the clearest Qur’anic links for this root. The Qur’an says in 87:14: قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَنْ تَزَكَّىٰ (qad aflaḥa man tazakkā), and in 91:9: قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَنْ زَكَّاهَا (qad aflaḥa man zakkāhā).

Tazkiyah means purifying and causing to grow. If the human self is left alone, it may sink into desire, fear, greed, arrogance, envy, and stinginess. Tazkiyah means that the human being cleans this inner soil so that it can become capable of growth.

From this view, falāḥ cannot be fully imagined without tazkiyah. The one who wants to reach falāḥ must not only repair the outside; he must also work with his inner being. If the soil of the soul is polluted and hard, the seed of guidance does not grow well in it.

Falāḥ and taqwa

In many verses, falāḥ is connected to taqwa: وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ (wa-ittaqū Allāha laʿallakum tufliḥūn).

Taqwa is conscious care over the human being’s relation with God, truth, and responsibility. Taqwa takes the human being out of careless living. The person of taqwa does not do everything merely because he is able to do it; he weighs, guards himself, and sees himself as responsible before God.

Without taqwa, falāḥ falls into mere outward success. Taqwa corrects the direction of falāḥ. If the human being reaches a result through injustice, deception, usury, corruption, or violation of people’s rights, that result is not falāḥ.

Falāḥ and being saved from the greed of the self

In two verses, the Qur’an says in Al-Hashr 59:9 and At-Taghabun 64:16: وَمَنْ يُوقَ شُحَّ نَفْسِهِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ (waman yūqa shuḥḥa nafsihi faʾūlaāika humu al-mufliḥūna).

Shuḥḥ al-nafs is inner constriction, greed, miserliness, and a diseased attachment to self, possessions, and advantage. The Qur’an makes being saved from the greed of the self a sign of falāḥ. This is very important, because many individual and social corruptions begin from this inward constriction.

In the image of the root, the person trapped in shuḥḥ has a hard and closed soul, like compacted soil that does not receive. The seed of guidance does not enter easily, and goodness does not flow from him to others. Being saved from shuḥḥ is like softening this hard soil: the human soul opens, becomes able to receive and give, and the ground of falāḥ is prepared.

The one who is saved from the greed of the self can give, forgive, be fair, see the right of others, and stop making himself the center of everything. Such a person’s inner soil becomes more open and capable of falāḥ. Therefore falāḥ is not gained only by outward worship; the human being must also be saved from inner constriction and self-centeredness.

Falāḥ and certainty

There is also an important connection between falāḥ and certainty. Certainty, if it is not accompanied by humility, submission to truth, and reform of the self, does not reach falāḥ.

The Qur’an says about Pharaoh and his chiefs in An-Naml 27:14: وَجَحَدُوا بِهَا وَاسْتَيْقَنَتْهَا أَنْفُسُهُمْ ظُلْمًا وَعُلُوًّا (wajaḥadūā bihā wāstayqanathā anfusuhum ẓulmanā waʿuluwwanā). Their souls had become certain of the signs, but they denied them out of injustice and arrogance. Here it becomes clear that knowing the truth is not enough by itself. If the soil of the soul is trapped in arrogance and denial, even the seed of certainty does not reach falāḥ.

On the other hand, the Qur’an says: إِنَّهُ لَا يُفْلِحُ الظَّالِمُونَ (innahu lā yufliḥu al-ẓālimūn). Certainty without breaking through the shell of ego does not bear fruit. Falāḥ appears when recognized truth produces humility, reform, and right action within the human being.

Falāḥ and the negation of fruitless paths

The Qur’an repeatedly says that certain groups do not attain falāḥ: wrongdoers, criminals, magicians, rejecters, and those who invent lies against God. These negations form an important part of the meaning of falāḥ. Falāḥ is not only about who is saved; it is also about which path never reaches the right fruit.

Injustice burns the soil of falāḥ. Lying against God plants corrupt seed. Magic and deception replace truth with display. Crime breaks moral order. Rejection, when it means covering truth after it has become clear, blocks the path of growth.

Therefore, when the Qur’an denies falāḥ from these paths, it teaches us not to confuse outward result with salvation. Every path separated from truth, even if it gives temporary benefit, has no sound outcome.

Suggested rendering in Forqan

The root F L Ḥ should not be forced into a one-dimensional translation. The form of the word and the context of the verse must be considered.

  • أَفْلَحَ (aflaḥa): attained falāḥ, was saved, reached the right fruit.
  • لَا يُفْلِحُ (lā yufliḥu): does not attain falāḥ, is not truly saved, does not come to right fruit.
  • تُفْلِحُونَ / تُفْلِحُوا (tufliḥūn / tufliḥū): you may attain falāḥ, you may be saved, the ground of falāḥ may be prepared for you.
  • الْمُفْلِحُونَ (al-mufliḥūn): the people of falāḥ, those in whom guidance has come to fruit.
  • الْمُفْلِحِينَ (al-mufliḥīn): those counted among the people of falāḥ.

Summary

The root ف ل ح (F L Ḥ) in the Qur’an points to breaking through obstruction, becoming ready for growth, and reaching the right fruit. The image of the farmer splitting the soil gives a clear path into the meaning of this root: falāḥ is the product of right planting, care, time, purity, and correct direction.

In the Qur’an, falāḥ is not outward success. Falāḥ is tied to guidance, taqwa, tazkiyah, salat, zakat, repentance, remembrance of God, good action, commanding right, restraining wrong, weighty scales, following the light, and being saved from the greed of the self.

On the other hand, injustice, crime, magic, rejection, inventing lies against God, and corrupt paths never reach falāḥ, even if they appear to produce temporary results.

So falāḥ means that the human being comes to fruit upon the path of truth. The mufliḥ is the one whose inner soil has been prepared, in whom the seed of guidance has grown, and whose action has come to sound fruit in the divine measure.
