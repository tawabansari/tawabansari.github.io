1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ن ظ ر moves in the semantic field of looking, paying attention, reflecting on something, waiting, and granting delay.

In many Qur'anic uses, nazar is not accidental seeing. A person turns toward something, attends to it, and seeks to reach understanding or judgment through that observation. For this reason the Qur'an repeatedly commands the human being to look at creation, the traces of mercy, the outcome of deniers, and the phenomena of the world.

But this root is not limited to the physical act of the eye. نظر في الأمر can mean examining and thinking about a matter. انتظار and إنظار also belong to this family and point to waiting or granting respite.

Therefore, the derivatives of N Ẓ R cannot all be reduced to a physical action of the eye. The shared core is directing attention toward something, whether that thing is a visible phenomenon, a question for reflection, or a time whose arrival is awaited.

The shortest expression of the root is: directing attention toward something in order to see it, examine it, or await its outcome.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives

نَظَرَ / يَنْظُرُ

He looked, attended, or reflected on something.

نَظَرَ إِلَىٰ

He looked toward something or focused attention upon it.

نَظَرَ فِي

He examined and reflected within a matter.

انْظُرْ / انْظُرُوا

Look; look carefully; attend; examine.

يَنْظُرُونَ

They look, or they wait; the exact meaning is determined by context.

نَاظِر / نَاظِرَة

One who looks, one who gazes, or one who waits.

نَظِرَة

A delay, respite, or time granted.

أَنْظَرَ / يُنْظِرُ

He granted respite and postponed the execution of a matter to another time.

مُنْظَر

One who has been granted respite.

مُنْظَرُون

Those who have been granted respite.

يَنْتَظِرُ / انْتَظَرَ

He waited and remained expectant for something to occur.

مَنْظَر

A scene, place of looking, or what is placed before sight. This form is not central in Qur'anic usage and should not be treated as one of the main Qur'anic derivatives merely because it exists in Arabic.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of N Ẓ R is directing attention toward something in order to see, examine, assess, or await it.

In this root, the mere presence of an image before the eye is not enough. There is an act of attention, turning toward, or pausing in order to receive something.

From this core several branches arise: looking with the eye, attending and examining, reflecting on an outcome, waiting for something to arrive, and granting delay until another time.

Nazar may lead to seeing, but it is not identical with seeing. A person may look and still fail to perceive the truth. The Qur'an says:

وَتَرَاهُمْ يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَيْكَ وَهُمْ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ

You see them looking toward you, while they do not truly see.

Al-A'raf 7:198

This verse shows that outward looking may occur while perception and insight do not occur.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The direct image is a human being turning face and eyes toward something and keeping attention upon it.

In intentional looking, several stages take place: orientation toward the subject, maintaining attention, observing signs, and trying to understand what is seen.

Yet the meaning is not limited to the bodily mechanism of the eye. A person can “look into a matter,” meaning that he examines its sides even if the matter is not a visible object.

The branch of waiting is also understood from this pause and expectancy. A person directs attention toward the future and waits for a time or result to arrive.

The shared image is attention that turns toward a subject and remains with it until perception or outcome arrives.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, نَظَرَ إِلَى الشَّيْءِ means he looked at something.

نَظَرَ فِي الأَمْرِ means he examined a matter and thought about it.

أَنْظَرَهُ means he gave him respite. اِنْتَظَرَهُ means he waited for him.

النَّظِير means counterpart or peer, something placed opposite another and compared with it. It is connected with comparison and equal standing, but it should not be reduced without separate study to the physical act of seeing.

Reports that a soldier fixed his line of sight on a target, or that land “looked,” if found in lexical sources, are secondary or figurative uses and should not be made the certain origin of the root.

7. History of Understanding, Misuse, or Semantic Restriction

One restriction is to turn nazar into a superficial glance without consequence. In many verses, the Qur'an does not merely say to see something; it commands the human being to attend to the manner of creation, the traces of mercy, and the outcomes of earlier peoples.

Qur'anic nazar is therefore often tied to reflection, lesson-taking, and judgment.

The opposite error is to reduce nazar to fully physical and empirical observation. Some uses concern waiting, reflection, judgment, or the state of the Hereafter, and cannot be explained only as eye movement.

Another restriction is to turn every command of “look” into a modern scientific program. These verses call to observation and thought, but each verse is not necessarily a command to design an experiment or construct a natural theory.

The right method is to ask in each context: what is the object of nazar, is sensory observation present, is reflection and lesson-taking present, is waiting or respite present, and what result does the verse seek?

8. Methodological Note

Several branches must be separated in studying N Ẓ R.

First, looking with the eye, such as looking at the creation of the camel or the traces of mercy.

Second, looking with reflection, such as examining the outcome of peoples.

Third, waiting for the fulfillment of a promise or judgment.

Fourth, inzar, granting respite.

Fifth, divine nazar in structures such as “He does not look at them,” which must not be interpreted as bodily looking by The God.

Sixth, ناظرة إلى ربها, which is among the ambiguous and debated verses and must not be forced without caution into bodily seeing of the essence of The God or into a categorical denial of every kind of vision.

Nazar should also not be made into a single tool of “cosmic proof” in every case. Sometimes the command to look is for lesson, sometimes for recognizing blessing, sometimes for waiting, and sometimes for showing the incapacity of the addressee.

9. A Short Look at Related Semitic Languages

There are similarities among Semitic roots connected with seeing, watching, and guarding, but Arabic N Ẓ R should not be merged with N S R or other roots without clear evidence.

Akkadian forms related to watching or guarding may sound close, but this does not necessarily prove direct identity with Arabic N Ẓ R. Differences among ظ, ص, and ض require ordered phonetic study.

For understanding the Qur'an, Arabic and Qur'anic usage come first. Related-language data can only serve as supporting evidence when the correspondence is clear.

10. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

A. Looking at the creation of the camel

أَفَلَا يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَى الْإِبِلِ كَيْفَ خُلِقَتْ

Do they not look at the camels, how they were created?

Al-Ghashiyah 88:17

The object of nazar is the manner of creation. The word كيف directs attention to structure, proportion, and sign-meaning. The verse does not present a specialized lesson in anatomy; its main aim is to awaken attention to creation and the power of the Creator.

B. Looking at the traces of mercy

فَانْظُرْ إِلَىٰ آثَارِ رَحْمَتِ اللَّهِ كَيْفَ يُحْيِي الْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا

So look at the traces of the mercy of The God, how He gives life to the earth after its death.

Ar-Rum 30:50

Nazar is directed here to visible traces of mercy, such as rain, growth, and the earth coming back to life. The aim is to move from observation to recognizing the power to give life.

C. Looking at the outcome of the deniers

قُلْ سِيرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ ثُمَّ انْظُرُوا كَيْفَ كَانَ عَاقِبَةُ الْمُكَذِّبِينَ

Say: travel through the earth, then look how the outcome of the deniers was.

Al-An'am 6:11

Here nazar comes with movement through the earth. It may include seeing historical traces, hearing reports, and examining the outcome of earlier peoples. The subject is not merely ruins, but the relation between denial, corruption, and outcome.

D. Looking at fruit as it emerges and ripens

انْظُرُوا إِلَىٰ ثَمَرِهِ إِذَا أَثْمَرَ وَيَنْعِهِ

Look at its fruit when it bears fruit and at its ripening.

Al-An'am 6:99

This verse calls to direct observation of the process of growth. The human being should see change, order, and gradual ripening, and recognize signs of faith through them.

E. Those who look but do not see

وَتَرَاهُمْ يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَيْكَ وَهُمْ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ

You see them looking toward you, while they do not see.

Al-A'raf 7:198

This verse distinguishes nazar from basar. Nazar may be directing the gaze; basar is clearer seeing and reception. The eyes may be turned toward the matter while understanding does not occur.

F. Granting delay to a debtor

وَإِنْ كَانَ ذُو عُسْرَةٍ فَنَظِرَةٌ إِلَىٰ مَيْسَرَةٍ

And if he is in hardship, then a delay until ease.

Al-Baqarah 2:280

نَظِرَة here means respite. The creditor is commanded to stop pressure until the debtor is able to pay. This is an ethical and legal command against oppressing a debtor who is unable.

G. Iblis requests respite

قَالَ أَنْظِرْنِي إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ
قَالَ إِنَّكَ مِنَ الْمُنْظَرِينَ

He said: Grant me respite until the day they are raised. He said: You are among those granted respite.

Al-A'raf 7:14-15

أنظرني means grant me delay. المنظرين are those who have been granted respite. This delay is not moral approval of Iblis; it is only the continuation of opportunity until a specified time.

H. Waiting for the outcome

فَانْتَظِرُوا إِنِّي مَعَكُمْ مِنَ الْمُنْتَظِرِينَ

So wait; indeed, I am with you among those who wait.

Yunus 10:102

Waiting here means remaining expectant for outcome and judgment. The subject is not physical looking but waiting for the result to be fulfilled.

I. “He does not look at them” in the Hereafter

وَلَا يُكَلِّمُهُمُ اللَّهُ وَلَا يَنْظُرُ إِلَيْهِمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ

The God will not speak to them and will not look toward them on the Day of Resurrection.

Ali 'Imran 3:77

Divine nazar here must not be understood like bodily human looking. The verse is about deprivation from mercy, honoring attention, and a speech of approval. This is clear from its pairing with “He will not speak to them” and “for them is a painful punishment.”

J. Faces looking toward their Lord

وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ نَاضِرَةٌ
إِلَىٰ رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ

Faces on that day are radiant, looking toward their Lord.

Al-Qiyamah 75:22-23

Here ناضرة with ض must be distinguished from ناظرة with ظ. The first belongs to N D R and means radiant and fresh; the second belongs to N Ẓ R and means looking, gazing, or attending.

This verse is debated in the discussion of seeing The God. Some read it as seeing The God in the Hereafter; others read it as waiting for mercy, turning toward the promise, or attending to the Lord. The wording certainly says that faces are radiant and fully turned toward their Lord. The quality of this nazar is ambiguous and must not be interpreted as bodily sight with spatial direction.

11. Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

يَنْظُرُ / يَنْظُرُونَ

Depending on structure, this verb can mean looking or waiting. With إلى, attention and looking toward a subject are usually prominent. Without it, some contexts point to waiting for an outcome.

انْظُرُوا

A command to look and attend. The object may be nature, history, or the result of an act. “Look” is usually broad and accurate.

نَظِرَة

A noun meaning respite. In the debtor verse, it expresses time and opportunity until ease arrives.

أَنْظَرَ

To grant delay. This form postpones the execution of a result to another time.

مُنْظَرُون

Those who have been granted respite. The word is not positive or negative by itself. Iblis was granted respite, but that respite is not his virtue.

نَاظِرَة

A feminine active participle: looking or gazing. In Al-Qiyamah 75:23, it returns to “faces” and must be separated from ناضرة in the previous verse.

12. Grammatical and Structural Notes

نَظَرَ إِلَىٰ

This structure usually marks the direction of attention:

انْظُرْ إِلَىٰ آثَارِ رَحْمَتِ اللَّهِ

Look at the traces of the mercy of The God.

Ar-Rum 30:50

إلى introduces the destination of attention, but this does not always mean a physical line of sight. Cognitive and inward attention may also be present.

نَظَرَ فِي

This structure more often points to examining and reflecting within a matter.

نَظِرَةٌ إِلَىٰ مَيْسَرَةٍ

نظرة is respite, and إلى ميسرة marks its endpoint or temporal destination: until ease.

أَنْظِرْنِي

An imperative of form IV with an object pronoun: grant me respite.

مِنَ الْمُنْظَرِينَ

Among those who have been granted respite.

إِلَىٰ رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ

إلى ربها is connected to ناظرة and marks the direction of nazar. Determining the quality of this nazar requires preserving divine transcendence and avoiding corporeal interpretation.

13. N Ẓ R Compared with B S R

Nazar emphasizes turning, looking, and seeking to observe. Basar points to seeing, visual ability, or clearer perception.

يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَيْكَ وَهُمْ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ

They look toward you, while they do not see.

Al-A'raf 7:198

The verse shows that nazar may occur without basar. With caution: nazar is looking and attending toward a subject; basar is seeing and receiving what stands before a person. Yet basar too can move beyond bodily sight and approach insight in the Qur'an.

14. N Ẓ R Compared with R A Y

The root R A Y points to seeing, receiving, or recognizing something, and it may be physical, dream-like, or intellectual.

Nazar highlights the process of attention and looking. Ru'yah more often highlights the occurrence of seeing or receiving.

One may say: nazar is looking toward something in pursuit of reception; ra'a is seeing or receiving it. This difference is not absolute, and both roots expand according to context.

15. N Ẓ R Compared with SH H D

Shuhud indicates presence, witnessing, and observation on the basis of which one may testify.

Nazar may lead to shuhud, but not every looking creates valid testimony. Nazar is the process of attention; shuhud is presence and reception of an event in a way that grounds testimony.

16. Specialized Discussion: Divine Looking and “Looking Toward Its Lord”

Attributing nazar to human beings and attributing nazar to The God must not be understood with one quality.

The human being looks with eyes, spatial direction, light, and distance. Human looking is limited and may be mistaken.

But The God is beyond body, organ, place, and limitation. Therefore in:

وَلَا يَنْظُرُ إِلَيْهِمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ

And He will not look toward them on the Day of Resurrection.

Ali 'Imran 3:77

one cannot say that The God physically turns His face away from them. The meaning is deprivation from mercy, honor, and approving attention.

The same principle must be respected in:

إِلَىٰ رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ

Looking toward their Lord.

Al-Qiyamah 75:23

The verse describes faces that are radiant and whose attention is fully toward their Lord. But the quality of this nazar is not explained in the text.

Major possibilities include: a special seeing in the Hereafter without resemblance or corporeality; waiting for the mercy and reward of the Lord; or complete turning of attention toward Him.

The root article should not impose one of these as the certain meaning.

The clear Qur'anic principle is:

لَا تُدْرِكُهُ الْأَبْصَارُ وَهُوَ يُدْرِكُ الْأَبْصَارَ

Sight does not encompass Him, while He encompasses sight.

Al-An'am 6:103

Every understanding of “looking toward its Lord” must be harmonized with the denial that eyesight can encompass the essence of The God. It is possible to distinguish nazar from idrak and say that the verse in Al-An'am denies full encompassing, not every kind of vision; but that is a matter of interpretation and cannot be made certain from the root article itself.

The cautious position is: Al-Qiyamah speaks of radiant faces turned toward their Lord; the quality of this nazar is among ambiguous matters and must not be turned into bodily looking, spatial direction, or the encompassing of The God's essence by eyesight.

Also, ناظرة must not be confused with ناضرة. The first belongs to N Ẓ R and concerns nazar; the second belongs to N D R and concerns radiance and freshness of the face.

17. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

نَظَرَ / يَنْظُرُ

looked; looked toward; attended; examined in the proper context; waited in a context of waiting.

انْظُرْ / انْظُرُوا

look; look carefully; attend; examine in the proper context. “Lock your line of sight” is not Qur'anic translation.

نَظِرَة

respite; an opportunity until ease.

أَنْظَرَ

granted respite; gave time; delayed until a time.

مُنْظَرُون

those granted respite; those given opportunity until a time.

نَاظِرَة

looking; gazing; waiting, if the context requires it.

إِلَىٰ رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ

looking toward their Lord; gazing toward their Lord. The quality of this nazar must be discussed separately while preserving divine transcendence.

فَنَظِرَةٌ إِلَىٰ مَيْسَرَةٍ

then a delay should be granted until ease.

18. Conclusion

The root N Ẓ R indicates directing attention toward something in order to see, examine, or await it.

Nazar is not accidental seeing. In many verses, the human being is commanded to look at creation, traces of mercy, fruits, and the outcome of earlier peoples, and to move from observation to understanding and lesson.

Yet nazar is not always bodily. The root also appears in branches of waiting and granting respite. نظرة is respite, أنظرني is the request for time, and منظرين are those granted respite.

Nazar is not identical with basar. A person may look and not truly see:

يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَيْكَ وَهُمْ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ

They look toward you, while they do not see.

Al-A'raf 7:198

Nazar is the process of turning and attending; basar is clearer seeing and reception.

In لا ينظر إليهم, divine nazar must not be understood corporeally. The verse speaks of deprivation from mercy, honor, and approving attention.

In إلى ربها ناظرة, the quality of the nazar is not explained. The verse speaks of radiant faces turned fully toward their Lord, but it must not be turned into bodily looking or eyesight encompassing the essence of The God.

Data from related Semitic languages should not be used without clear phonetic and historical evidence to assert a firm link between N Ẓ R, N S R, and roots of guarding.

The shortest semantic core is: directing attention toward something for seeing, examining, understanding, or waiting, with uses ranging from sensory looking to reflection, waiting, and granting delay.
