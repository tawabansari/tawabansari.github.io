Qur’anic Root

Qur’anic Root q-w-m

Rising, standing, establishing, becoming upright, gaining structure, sustaining a thing upon a sound foundation, and making it active in life.

Forms examined from this root

This page studies the Qur’anic root q-w-m semantically, then explains the most important Qur’anic forms built from this root:

  • qāma / yaqūmu / qum / qūmū / taqūmū — Form I verb; he rose, he stands, rise, rise together, stand or take action
  • aqāma / yuqīmu / aqim / aqīmū — Form IV; established, establishes, establish, establish together
  • istaqāma / yastaqīmu / istaqim — Form X; became upright, remains straight, be upright
  • qawm — noun; a people, a human group or society standing upon a shared bond
  • qiyāmah — noun; resurrection, the final rising and establishment of truth
  • qiyām — verbal noun / noun; standing, rising, being established, active standing
  • qawām — noun; sustaining foundation, balance, structure, and order
  • qayyim / qīmah / qiyam — adjective / noun; upright, sound, sustaining, having lasting value
  • qayyūm / al-Qayyūm — intensive form and divine name; sustaining, establishing, self-subsisting, the One through whom all things stand
  • qāʾim / qāʾimah — active participle; standing, firm, established, active, remaining
  • muqīm — active participle of Form IV; establisher, maintainer, or one who remains
  • mustaqīm — active participle of Form X; straight, upright, without crookedness, on the sound path
  • maqām / maqāmāt — place noun / station; standing-place, station, rank, place of standing
  • muqām / muqāmah — noun / adjective; established place, dwelling, place of remaining
  • taqwīm — Form II verbal noun; straightening, structuring, setting in sound form
  • iqām / iqāmah — Form IV verbal noun; establishing, setting upright, making stand
  • qawwāmīn — intensive plural; those who persistently uphold justice or responsibility
  • aqwam — comparative/superlative; more upright, sounder, firmer, more correct

Reminder: forms such as taqawwama are possible in Arabic morphology, but they are not central Qur’anic forms of this root in our root page. This page focuses on Qur’anic usage.

It is also important to distinguish the branches of the root. Qawm is not the same as qiyām. Qiyāmah is not the same as iqāmat al-ṣalāh. Mustaqīm is not the same as muqīm. They all belong to the field of standing, establishing, structure, and uprightness, but each has its own Qur’anic function.

General meaning of the root q-w-m

The Qur’anic root q-w-m points to rising, standing, becoming established, establishing, being upright, gaining structure, and remaining sustained. It is not limited to physical standing. In many Qur’anic usages, the concern is the standing of a truth, a path, a duty, a society, an act, or an ethical order.

When something is qāʾim, it does not merely exist; it is standing, active, stable, and dependable. When something is established, it is not merely performed; it is set upright, placed in its proper position, made effective, and kept alive in life.

This is why the root is central. The Qur’an speaks of establishing ṣalāh, not merely performing or reciting prayer. It speaks of the straight path, a path that is upright, firm, without crookedness, and grounded in truth. It speaks of a qayyim dīn, a way or order that stands on a sound foundation. It speaks of qiyāmah, the final rising and establishment of truth.

So q-w-m is one of the foundational roots for understanding dīn, ṣalāh, justice, guidance, resurrection, society, and human uprightness before truth.

Q-w-m beyond physical standing

The first meaning that comes to mind is standing. But the Qur’an uses this field in a much wider sense.

A person may stand with the body while the heart does not stand. A person may stand in a row of worship while life is not standing upon truth. A person may perform an act while that act is not established or effective in life.

In the Qur’anic view, standing can mean becoming ready for responsibility. It can mean rising for truth. It can mean remaining upright on the path. It can also mean establishing something that would collapse if abandoned.

Thus this root calls the human being out of passivity, weakness, dispersion, and lack of structure. It calls the human being to stand, establish, become upright, and carry responsibility.

Qāma / yaqūmu: rising, standing, and taking action

The forms qāma and yaqūmu point to rising, standing, or becoming established. This can be physical, but its Qur’anic field is much broader.

Sometimes rising means getting up from sitting or sleeping. Sometimes it means becoming ready for action. Sometimes it means standing before God, people, or responsibility. Sometimes it means that an affair becomes established, remains in place, or becomes active.

In this sense, qiyām is not merely bodily movement. It can be a declaration of presence before truth. One who stands for justice, stands as a witness to truth, or stands in servitude to God is inside this semantic field.

So qāma is not only “he stood”; it can mean he rose, became ready, acted, and placed himself in a state of responsibility.

Aqāma / yuqīmu / aqim / aqīmū: establishing and setting upright

The form IV branch of this root, such as aqāma, yuqīmu, aqim, and aqīmū, is one of its most important Qur’anic branches. These forms do not merely mean doing an act; they mean establishing, setting upright, keeping in place, and making alive.

When the Qur’an commands that something be established, it does not mean that a surface form should be performed and then abandoned. Establishing means that the thing stands in its proper place, becomes effective, and has real presence in life.

For this reason, iqāmah is tied to order, continuity, structure, responsibility, and effect. What is established must not have only a name and outward form; its truth must also stand.

This explains why iqāmat al-ṣalāh is so important. The Qur’an does not speak merely of reading prayer; it speaks of establishing it.

Establishing ṣalāh beyond merely praying

One of the most important applications of q-w-m is the Qur’anic expression aqīmū al-ṣalāh and its related forms. It is often translated as “pray,” but that translation narrows the Qur’anic field.

Iqāmat al-ṣalāh means establishing ṣalāh: keeping it standing, alive, stable, ordered, effective, and present in life. Ṣalāh is not merely a verbal or bodily act detached from life. It should return the human being to remembrance of God, responsibility, purity, justice, and the right orientation of life.

If ṣalāh is only performed outwardly but leaves no trace in ethics, conduct, justice, honesty, trustworthiness, humility, and distance from corruption, has it truly been established? The form may have been performed, but its truth has not yet stood.

There is a major difference between “performing ṣalāh” and “establishing ṣalāh.” Performance can be limited; establishing means setting up a living relationship with God, an inner order, and an ethical effect in life.

For Forqan, the better rendering is: Establish ṣalāh. That is, keep ṣalāh alive and standing in individual and collective life, in remembrance of God, purity of conduct, ethical responsibility, and orientation toward truth.

Istaqāma / yastaqīmu / istaqim: uprightness and steadfastness on truth

The forms istaqāma, yastaqīmu, and istaqim point to becoming upright, straight, steady, and free of crookedness on the path.

Steadfastness is not hard and rigid resistance. It means remaining straight on truth, not bending into falsehood, not being moved off course by pressure, and preserving one’s path on a sound foundation.

A person may know the path of truth but fail to remain upright in it. Fear, greed, anger, bias, social pressure, or desire can pull the human being away. Steadfastness means not abandoning the path under these pressures.

So istaqim is not a simple moral command; it is a call to stand on truth. The human being should be soft before truth, but not weak before falsehood.

Mustaqīm: straight, upright, and without crookedness

Mustaqīm is one of the most important Qur’anic words from this root. It is often translated as “straight,” but its meaning is deeper.

Mustaqīm means straight, upright, stable, without crookedness, and standing in the right direction. In the expression al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm, the concern is not merely a short or simple path; it is a path standing upon truth, not a crooked way, not dispersing the human being, and leading to the proper destination.

The straight path is a path aligned with truth, justice, tawḥīd, mercy, and accountability. It does not lead to extremism, injustice, idol-making, servitude to other than God, or corruption.

Thus mustaqīm is not merely the geometric shape of a path; it is the ethical, divine, and existential state of a path. A path is straight when it stands upon truth.

Qawm: a people standing upon a shared bond

Qawm is one of the most frequent forms of this root. It usually refers to a people, a human group, or a society standing upon a shared bond such as language, lineage, land, history, belief, power, or social condition.

In the Qur’an, however, a qawm is not automatically praised. A people can be upon truth or falsehood. They can support a messenger or stand against him. They can stand upon justice or upon arrogance, injustice, and denial.

Root-wise, qawm is not merely a scattered collection of individuals. It is a group that has gained social structure; it stands upon something, is recognized through something, and responds collectively to the call of truth.

This is crucial: the Qur’an does not judge peoples merely by name or identity, but by their stance toward truth. Belonging to a people does not save anyone. The criterion is truth, taqwā, justice, and response to guidance.

Qiyām and qiyāmah: rising, establishment, and the final exposure of truth

Qiyām points to standing, rising, and being established. It can apply to worship, responsibility, standing before God, or the establishment of an affair.

Qiyāmah is an extremely important branch of this field. Resurrection is not only a name for the future. Root-wise, it is the Day of rising, standing, establishment, and the final disclosure of truth.

In the world, many things remain hidden: intentions, injustices, deceptions, sufferings, acts of sincerity, and betrayals. But qiyāmah is the day when truth is established and the human being stands before the outcome of his or her actions.

So qiyāmah is deeply connected to q-w-m: it is the day when truth, reckoning, witness, and realities stand forth. On that day, the human being cannot hide behind names, powers, peoples, or excuses.

Qāʾim / qāʾimah: standing, active, and established

Qāʾim means standing, firm, established, and active. It can apply to a person, an affair, a scripture, a society, or a state that remains in living presence.

Being qāʾim is different from merely existing. Something may exist but not be qāʾim: it may have no effect, no active presence, no uprightness, or may have collapsed inwardly.

Ethically, a qāʾim person is one who stands before truth. Socially, a qāʾim affair is one that still preserves its function, structure, and effect.

So to be qāʾim is to have living and stable presence, not merely to be present.

Maqām / maqāmāt: station, standing-place, and rank

Maqām belongs to this root and points to a place of standing, a station, a rank, or a position in which someone or something stands.

Maqām is not only a physical place. It can mean a spiritual station, responsibility, position, or rank. Every maqām carries responsibility. Every station is a test. Every rank can become a means of growth or collapse.

In the Qur’anic view, a person’s station is not made valuable merely by title, power, or fame. What matters is how the person stands in that station: with truth or falsehood, justice or injustice, humility or arrogance.

So maqām is the place of standing, but the real question is: upon what does a person stand in that place?

Muqīm: establisher, maintainer, or one who remains

Muqīm is from form IV and can refer to one who establishes, maintains, or remains in a place.

In relation to iqāmah, muqīm is not merely someone who is present; it can be someone who makes an affair stand and preserves it. For this reason, forms such as muqīmī al-ṣalāh are deeply significant: they are those who establish ṣalāh, not merely those who perform its outward form.

If a human being is muqīm of a divine affair, that means he keeps it standing and active in life. If he only performs it occasionally without giving it structure, iqāmah is not complete.

Thus muqīm is tied to permanence, presence, establishment, and maintenance.

Qawām: sustaining foundation and balance

Qawām points to what sustains, orders, balances, and keeps something standing. Everything needs qawām to remain: the body, society, economy, family, and ethics.

When qawām is lost, outward form may remain for a while, but the truth collapses. A society without justice has no qawām. Worship without ethics has no qawām. A dīn in which truth is hidden has no qawām.

So qawām is not mere outward stability; it is inner structure and sustaining foundation. What remains standing must have qawām.

Qayyim / qīmah: uprightness, soundness, and lasting value

Forms such as qayyim and qīmah point to uprightness, soundness, and lasting value. These words are tied to the idea of something standing upon a correct foundation.

When a path, scripture, or dīn is called qayyim, it means that it is not crooked, weak, without structure, or fabricated. It contains what makes it upright, stable, and sustaining.

This word should not be reduced to “valuable” in a shallow sense. Value in this field comes from qawām: something has value because it stands upon truth, has sound structure, and does not pull the human being into collapse.

Thus dīn qayyim means a way that stands upon a sound foundation, not a path built from bias, blind tradition, or human power.

Qayyūm and al-Qayyūm: the Sustainer and Establisher of existence

Qayyūm belongs to the field of q-w-m and means intensely standing, sustaining, establishing, and making things stand. But when the Qur’an uses this attribute for God, the form is al-Qayyūm, and the definite article al- matters deeply.

Qayyūm in a general sense can be a description: one who sustains, upholds, or gives stability. But al-Qayyūm in relation to God is absolute and incomparable: the One who depends on nothing, while everything depends on Him to stand.

Here al- is not merely a grammatical marker. In divine names, it can move a quality from a general attribute into the domain of uniqueness, perfection, and incomparability. Raḥīm can describe one who shows mercy, but al-Raḥīm in relation to God points to the complete and incomparable divine mercy. ʿAlīm can mean knowledgeable, but al-ʿAlīm is God’s encompassing knowledge. Likewise, qayyūm is sustaining, but al-Qayyūm is the final Sustainer and Establisher of all existence.

From the perspective of apophatic theology, this distinction is essential. God is not “standing” or “sustaining” like created beings. He is not one entity inside the system of existence among other entities. He does not need time, place, tools, energy, body, intermediary, or support. Every standing and every structure in creation depends on Him, while He depends on no structure outside Himself.

Thus al-Qayyūm means that God is self-subsisting and that everything else subsists through Him. The heavens, the earth, life, time, law, provision, guidance, and return all stand in relation to Him. Nothing has final independence from itself. Everything, at the foundation of its existence, needs His sustaining and establishing.

This attribute removes the illusion of independence. Human beings, societies, knowledge, power, wealth, governments, worship, and even our breathing do not stand from themselves. Every qawām in the world, when seen correctly, returns to al-Qayyūm.

So al-Qayyūm is the divine center of the root q-w-m: the unique and incomparable Establisher who is upheld by nothing, while everything is upheld through Him.

Qawwāmīn: steadfast upholders of responsibility and justice

Qawwāmīn refers to those who are intensely and persistently engaged in upholding an affair. In Qur’anic usage, the word is closely tied to standing for justice, witness, and ethical responsibility.

To be qawwām means not merely to mention truth occasionally, but to stand for it. Justice must not be desired only when it serves one’s side and abandoned when it is against oneself. Witness must not be altered by fear, kinship, hostility, or interest.

Being qawwāmīn is heavy, because it requires standing under pressure. One who loves justice only in speech is not qawwām. A qawwām is one who keeps truth standing.

This word shows that q-w-m is not only about individual worship; it is also about social justice.

Aqwam: more upright and sounder

Aqwam means more upright, straighter, firmer, and sounder. It introduces the criterion of comparison into this field.

Sometimes a person stands between several paths, statements, rulings, or methods. The question is not which is closer to my habit, my people, my sect, or my interest. The question is: which is aqwam; which is more upright, more just, straighter, and closer to truth?

This word pulls the human being out of bias. The right path is the one with greater qawām: it preserves truth better, reforms the human being better, and keeps society further from ethical collapse.

Thus aqwam is a criterion of intellectual and ethical growth: one must always ask which path stands on truth more firmly.

Taqwīm: setting straight and giving structure

Taqwīm points to straightening, making upright, structuring, and placing something in sound form. It shows that creation and formation can be built upon measure, order, and proper structure.

Taqwīm means that something was not merely made, but arranged in a way that allows it to stand, function, and have a path. In relation to the human being, this is profound: the human being is created with a structure capable of standing upon truth, understanding, choosing, and carrying responsibility.

But the human being can preserve this structure or fall from it. If reason, fiṭrah, ethics, and will are directed toward truth, the human being moves closer to his proper qawām. If falsehood, injustice, lust, arrogance, and denial dominate him, his human qawām is harmed.

So taqwīm is not merely bodily structure; it is also a sign of the human being’s ethical and spiritual possibility.

The root q-w-m and the Qur’anic order of life

The root q-w-m is not merely a lexical root in the Qur’an; it builds a whole way of seeing life.

Ṣalāh must be established. Justice must stand. The human being must remain upright on the path of truth. The path must be mustaqīm. The dīn must be qayyim. Society must have qawām. The human being must be responsible in his maqām. And in the end, qiyāmah will stand so that final truth becomes manifest.

This root shows that dīn is not merely mental belief or outward rite. Dīn must stand, gain structure, and have effect in life. If something only carries a religious name but does not stand upon truth, justice, mercy, and tawḥīd, it has moved away from the field of q-w-m.

Understanding this root is therefore essential for understanding the Qur’an. Without it, establishing ṣalāh is reduced to reading prayer, the straight path to a simple road, qiyāmah to a future label, and dīn qayyim to a religious title. With this root, all these concepts return to establishment, uprightness, structure, and truth.

Summary

The root q-w-m in the Qur’an calls the human being to rise, stand, establish, become upright, give structure, and keep truth alive. It begins with the body but does not stop at the body; it reaches worship, justice, society, guidance, dīn, resurrection, and existence itself.

Qāma and yaqūmu speak of rising and standing. Aqāma and aqīmū speak of establishing and setting upright. Istaqāma and mustaqīm speak of uprightness and freedom from crookedness on the path. Qawm speaks of a people standing upon a bond. Qiyām and qiyāmah speak of rising and the final establishment of truth. Qāʾim speaks of standing presence. Maqām speaks of a station of standing. Qawām speaks of sustaining foundation. Qayyim speaks of uprightness and soundness. Al-Qayyūm speaks of God as the incomparable Sustainer and Establisher of existence. Qawwāmīn speaks of those who uphold responsibility and justice. Aqwam speaks of what is more upright. Taqwīm speaks of straightening and structuring.

If this root is understood properly, many Qur’anic concepts leave the surface level. Iqāmat al-ṣalāh is not merely praying; it is establishing ṣalāh. Al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm is not merely a road; it is a path upright and standing upon truth. Dīn qayyim is not merely a religious title; it is a way standing on a sound foundation. And qiyāmah is not a distant label; it is the day when truth finally stands.

Thus the root q-w-m calls the human being to a life that stands: a life standing upon God, truth, justice, guidance, and responsibility.
