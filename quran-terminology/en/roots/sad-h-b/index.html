Foundational Meaning

The root Ṣ Ḥ B fundamentally indicates companionship, association, staying with, close company, being together in a path or state, and a stable relation between two sides. In this root, merely being beside someone is not enough. Some kind of companionship, nearness, connection, social association, staying-with, or being known with something is present. This companionship can be short or long, physical or meaningful, worldly or otherworldly, positive or negative.

Important Qur'anic Forms

Important forms of this root in the Qur'an include ṣāḥib, ṣāḥibah, ṣāḥibukum, ṣāḥibihi, ṣāḥibihimā, aṣḥāb, aṣḥāb al-jannah, aṣḥāb al-nār, aṣḥāb al-yamīn, aṣḥāb al-shimāl, aṣḥāb al-kahf, aṣḥāb Madyan, aṣḥāb al-aykah, aṣḥāb al-sabt, ṣuḥbatihimā, and muṣāḥiban. Ṣāḥib points to a companion, associate, one who has a relation, or someone who stays with something or someone. Ṣāḥibah in the Qur'an mostly refers to a wife or close companion in a marital relation. Aṣḥāb is plural and refers to a group that is accompanied by and known with a place, state, destiny, location, action, or event.

Linguistic Core

The linguistic core of Ṣ Ḥ B is companionship with relation. A ṣāḥib is not merely "someone next to another person"; it is someone who has a kind of bond, association, companionship, or recognized relation with them. Aṣḥāb are also those who have gained a stable relation with something, such as aṣḥāb al-jannah, aṣḥāb al-nār, aṣḥāb al-kahf, aṣḥāb al-fīl, aṣḥāb al-aykah, or aṣḥāb al-sabt. In this structure, the human being or group becomes known by what they have accompanied.

Material Image

The material image of this root is two travelers walking a road together, or something that stays with another thing for a time and is not seen as separate from it. Their destination may be the same or may differ, but they are together in one path, place, state, or destiny. From this image, its social and otherworldly meanings become clear: the human being accompanies a person, a thing, a path, an action, or a destiny, and that companionship becomes part of their identity.

Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic usage, Ṣ Ḥ B is connected with companionship, staying-with, friendship, sitting together, attachment, and having a relation. A ṣāḥib can be a human companion, someone who stays with something, or one who possesses something. In the Qur'an, this meaning expands across several levels: the companion of the messenger, companionship with parents, the companion in the cave, spouse, companions of the Garden, companions of the Fire, and groups known by a particular history or action.

Ṣāḥibukum and the Rejection of False Accusations Against the Messenger

One important field of this root is the rejection of wrongful accusations against the messenger:

مَا ضَلَّ صَاحِبُكُمْ وَمَا غَوَىٰ

Your known companion has neither gone astray nor gone off the right way.

Al-Najm 53:2

In this verse, ṣāḥibukum is used about the Prophet. This expression is very important because it presents the Prophet among the people and in his lived relation with them: he is not an unknown stranger; he is your companion, someone with whom you have lived and whom you know. Therefore, the accusation of misguidance or going off the way conflicts with the people's lived knowledge of him.

Another verse says:

وَمَا صَاحِبُكُم بِمَجْنُونٍ

And your known companion is not mad.

Al-Takwīr 81:22

Again the Qur'an uses ṣāḥibukum. This means: you have seen him among yourselves; you know his conduct, intellect, trustworthiness, and steadiness. So the accusation of madness is a groundless charge meant to break the credibility of the message. This use shows that ṣāḥib can refer to social recognition and real companionship.

Companionship in the Cave

The second field is companionship in the cave:

إِذْ يَقُولُ لِصَاحِبِهِ لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَنَا

When he was saying to his companion: Do not grieve; indeed, The God is with us.

Al-Tawbah 9:40

In this verse, ṣāḥib refers to the Prophet's companion in the cave. Ṣāḥib here means someone who is with the Prophet in that specific situation. The word by itself does not create absolute faith-rank, protection from error, or independent religious authority; it only states the relation of companionship in that event. The value or station of any person must be understood from the total Qur'anic criteria, not only from the word ṣāḥib.

This is important for the Forqan method: ṣuḥbah in the Qur'an is not by itself proof of absolute rightness or independent authority. Companionship can be a virtue, responsibility, or specific position, but being a ṣāḥib alone does not create legislative rank.

Keeping Company With Parents in Dunya

The third field is companionship with parents in dunya:

وَصَاحِبْهُمَا فِي الدُّنْيَا مَعْرُوفًا

And keep company with them in dunya in what is recognized as good.

Luqmān 31:15

This verse is about parents who pressure the child toward shirk. The Qur'an says not to obey them in shirk, but to keep company with them in dunya in a recognized good way. This verse shows that Ṣ Ḥ B can be ethical companionship and good social association, even when religious obedience to the other side is not allowed.

So keeping company in maʿrūf means preserving the human relation without sacrificing truth and tawḥīd. This verse sets an important boundary between social goodness and creedal obedience. The human being can keep company with parents in maʿrūf, but must not obey them in shirk.

Ṣāḥibah and Denying Spousal Relation From The God

The fourth field is spouse or close life-companion:

أَنَّىٰ يَكُونُ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَلَمْ تَكُن لَّهُ صَاحِبَةٌ

How could He have a child when He has had no ṣāḥibah?

Al-Anʿām 6:101

Ṣāḥibah here refers to a wife or marital companion. The Qur'an uses this expression to deny the attribution of a child to The God. Attributing procreation to The God conflicts with the truth of transcendence, because begetting belongs to the field of pairhood and ṣāḥibah, and The God is far above these created needs and relations.

Surat al-Jinn also says:

وَأَنَّهُ تَعَالَىٰ جَدُّ رَبِّنَا مَا اتَّخَذَ صَاحِبَةً وَلَا وَلَدًا

And that the majesty of our Lord is exalted; He has taken neither ṣāḥibah nor child.

Al-Jinn 72:3

Here too, ṣāḥibah comes beside walad. The use of Ṣ Ḥ B in this context refers to marital companionship or created pairhood, and the Qur'an denies it from The God. The God is not in need of companion, wife, partner, or child.

Aṣḥāb as a Group Known by One Destiny or State

The fifth field is aṣḥāb as a group known by one destiny or state:

وَأَصْحَابُ الْجَنَّةِ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ

And the companions of the Garden will abide in it.

Al-Baqarah 2:82

And:

وَالَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا وَكَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا أُولَـٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ

And those who covered truth and counted Our signs as false, they are the companions of the Fire.

Al-Baqarah 2:39

Aṣḥāb al-jannah and aṣḥāb al-nār mean those who have gained a stable relation with the Garden or the Fire, not merely those who saw it for a moment. Here ṣuḥbah becomes companionship with destiny. In the Hereafter, the human being accompanies the result of their own path.

This expression shows that "aṣḥāb" in the Hereafter is not only the name of inhabitants of a place. It expresses a stable relation between the human being and their final outcome. Whoever lived in dunya with faith, righteous action, denial, kufr, wrongdoing, or reform, accompanies the result of that path in the Hereafter.

Companions of the Right and Companions of the Left

The sixth field is aṣḥāb al-yamīn and aṣḥāb al-shimāl:

فَأَصْحَابُ الْمَيْمَنَةِ مَا أَصْحَابُ الْمَيْمَنَةِ

Then the companions of the right side; what companions of the right side!

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:8

And:

وَأَصْحَابُ الْمَشْأَمَةِ مَا أَصْحَابُ الْمَشْأَمَةِ

And the companions of the left side; what companions of the left side!

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:9

In the same surah, aṣḥāb al-yamīn and aṣḥāb al-shimāl are also presented. These expressions show groups who have become accompanied by a particular direction, position, or destiny. Being aṣḥāb here is an otherworldly identity: the human being is known by their outcome and direction.

Companions of the Cave

The seventh field is aṣḥāb al-kahf:

أَمْ حَسِبْتَ أَنَّ أَصْحَابَ الْكَهْفِ وَالرَّقِيمِ كَانُوا مِنْ آيَاتِنَا عَجَبًا

Did you think that the companions of the Cave and the inscription were among Our wondrous signs?

Al-Kahf 18:9

Aṣḥāb al-kahf means the group who became known with the cave and that divine event. This name is not only a geographical location. Their companionship with the cave became part of their history and of their being a sign. They are known by faith, taking refuge, long sleep, and the sign-character of their story.

Companions of the Elephant

The eighth field is aṣḥāb al-fīl:

أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِأَصْحَابِ الْفِيلِ

Did you not see what your Lord did with the companions of the Elephant?

Al-Fīl 105:1

Aṣḥāb al-fīl means those who became known with the elephant and the military expedition. They were not merely owners of a particular animal. They accompanied an aggressive project and a historical event. Their name comes from their companionship with that power and aim.

Companions of the Thicket, the Well, and Similar Historical Groups

The ninth field is aṣḥāb al-aykah, aṣḥāb al-rass, and similar historical groups:

كَذَّبَ أَصْحَابُ الْأَيْكَةِ الْمُرْسَلِينَ

The companions of the Thicket counted the messengers as false.

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:176

And:

وَعَادًا وَثَمُودَ وَأَصْحَابَ الرَّسِّ

And ʿĀd, Thamūd, and the companions of the Well.

Al-Furqān 25:38

Aṣḥāb in these cases are groups known with a place, people, environment, or specific event. Through these names, the Qur'an does not merely provide historical information; it also reminds of their moral relation and destiny. They are known by an environment, action, or event that gave meaning to their fate.

Companions of the Sabbath

The tenth field is aṣḥāb al-sabt. The Qur'an recalls the story of a group that transgressed in the Sabbath:

وَسْأَلْهُمْ عَنِ الْقَرْيَةِ الَّتِي كَانَتْ حَاضِرَةَ الْبَحْرِ إِذْ يَعْدُونَ فِي السَّبْتِ

And ask them about the town that was by the sea, when they were transgressing in the Sabbath.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:163

Aṣḥāb al-sabt means those who became known with the test of the Sabbath and transgression in it. Ṣuḥbah here is historical and moral relation: a group became tied to a particular violation. This shows that a human being and a society can sometimes become known by one test.

Distinguishing Qur'anic Uses

The uses of Ṣ Ḥ B must be distinguished. Ṣāḥib can be a personal companion, such as the Prophet's companion in the cave. It can be an address of social recognition, such as ṣāḥibukum about the Prophet. It can be ethical companionship in dunya, such as keeping company with parents. Ṣāḥibah can be a spouse or marital companion. Aṣḥāb can be a group related to a place, action, destiny, blessing, punishment, direction, or historical sign. So Ṣ Ḥ B does not always mean "good friend." Its value becomes clear from context.

Structural Notes

Structurally, genitive constructions in this root are very important. Ṣāḥibukum means your known companion, someone with whom you have a relation of recognition and shared life. Ṣāḥibah in the context of having a child points to a wife or marital companion. Aṣḥāb al-jannah means those who have gained a stable relation with the Garden. Aṣḥāb al-nār means those who accompany the destiny of Fire. Aṣḥāb al-kahf means the group known with the cave and that event. Aṣḥāb al-fīl means the group associated with the elephant and the military expedition. In each case, the genitive term shows what kind of companionship is meant.

Nearby Roots

Ṣ Ḥ B differs from several nearby roots. T B ʿ points to following and pursuing the path of another; ṣuḥbah is companionship and staying-with, not necessarily following. R F Q is close to gentleness and soft companionship; ṣuḥbah is broader and can be positive or negative. KH L Ṭ points to mixing and becoming intermingled; ṣuḥbah is companionship, not necessarily becoming one. Q R B is nearness; ṣuḥbah is nearness with relation. W L Y points to guardianship, supportive nearness, or walāyah; ṣuḥbah may exist without walāyah. Q R N is a close and attached companion, sometimes with a stronger and more continuous sense; ṣāḥib can be broader and more contextual. A H L points to family, group belonging, or being the people of something; aṣḥāb more often shows companionship, staying-with, or being known with something.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

In Forqan translation, Ṣ Ḥ B must be translated according to context. Ṣāḥib can be "companion," "associate," "one who keeps company," "known companion," or in some contexts "one who has a relation." Ṣāḥibukum about the Prophet is best rendered "your known companion," not merely "your friend," because it carries social recognition. Ṣāḥib in the cave is "his companion." Ṣāḥibah in the divine context is "wife/marital companion," with an explanation of transcendence. Aṣḥāb al-jannah is "companions of the Garden" or "those who accompany the Garden." Aṣḥāb al-nār is "companions of the Fire" or "those who accompany the Fire." Aṣḥāb al-kahf, aṣḥāb al-fīl, aṣḥāb al-aykah, and similar expressions are sometimes best preserved as Qur'anic terms and then explained.

Linguistic Summary

The root Ṣ Ḥ B indicates companionship, association, staying-with, close company, and stable relation. A ṣāḥib is one who accompanies and becomes known with a person, place, state, path, or destiny. Aṣḥāb are groups that have gained relation with an action, position, blessing, punishment, place, or event. In the Qur'an, this root extends from the Prophet's companion and keeping company with parents to spouse, the companions of the Cave, the companions of the Elephant, the companions of the Garden, and the companions of the Fire. The value of ṣuḥbah is not gained from the word itself; it becomes clear from the context and direction of companionship. Companionship with truth can be honor, and companionship with falsehood can carry human identity toward punishment.
