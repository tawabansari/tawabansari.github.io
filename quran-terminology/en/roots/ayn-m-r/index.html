The Qur’anic Root AYN M R (ʿ M R)

ʿAmara: the continuation of life, inhabited places, and enduring presence

The Qur’anic root ع م ر, AYN M R (ʿ M R), has a varied family of forms. Among the most important are عَمَرَ / يَعْمُرُ, ʿamara / yaʿmuru; عَمَّرَ / يُعَمَّرُ, ʿammara / yuʿammaru; اعْتَمَرَ, iʿtamara; اسْتَعْمَرَ, istaʿmara; عِمَارَة, ʿimārah; عُمُر, ʿumur; عَمْر, ʿamr; عُمْرَة, ʿumrah; مَعْمُور, maʿmūr; and مُعَمَّر, muʿammar.

At first, these forms seem to occupy several different fields: the duration of life, developing and inhabiting land and places, maintaining a mosque, ʿumrah, and something described as maʿmūr. It is tempting to bring all these branches under a single image: to say, for example, that ʿumur means being inhabited and that human life is the body’s habitation, or conversely to derive every use concerning habitation from longevity. The evidence does not establish such a precise direction of historical development.

Two principal branches are nevertheless clearly visible:

Remaining alive and the continuation of life

And:

A place being inhabited, active, and maintained, and keeping it in that condition

The idea of continued presence can bring these branches closer together semantically. ʿUmur is the duration of a person’s continued presence in life, while an ʿāmir place is one in which habitation, presence, or activity continues, rather than a place abandoned and ruined. This explains their semantic proximity; it should not be presented as their proven derivational history.

عِمْرَان, ʿImrān, also occurs in the Qur’an in expressions such as Āl ʿImrān, “the family of ʿImrān.” There, however, it is a personal name. Shared consonants do not justify equating the name’s meaning with the ordinary vocabulary of ʿumrān and ʿimārah, habitation and development. This article therefore does not count that proper name among the derivatives whose lexical meanings it examines.

Older Arabic: enduring life and inhabited places

Both principal branches are recorded in older Arabic linguistic sources. Recording a usage in dictionaries compiled in later centuries does not, by itself, establish its pre-Qur’anic date. Such a claim requires a particular witness whose date is examined separately.

The oath formula لَعَمْرُكَ, laʿamruka, is known in older Arabic. ʿAmr here means life. Lexical sources record laʿamrī and laʿamruka as oaths by one’s own life or another person’s life; the Qur’an itself also uses laʿamruka.

The spatial branch also appears in recorded uses of مَعْمَر, maʿmar, for a place to stay and live. Lexical sources record عَمِرَ بالمكان, ʿamira bi-l-makān, for remaining and dwelling in a place; عامر, ʿāmir, for an inhabited, maintained place; and معمور, maʿmūr, for something inhabited, maintained, used, or attended to.

This helps distinguish ʿimārah from merely constructing a building. A building can have been constructed and yet stand abandoned and ruined. Being ʿāmir involves more than walls and a roof: it also involves presence, use, care, or continued functioning.

In the branch concerning life, ʿumur does not merely designate being alive, but relates to how long life continues. A person can be alive while their lifespan is described as long or short.

The two branches can therefore be considered together without declaring either the proven historical source of the other.

ʿUmur, ʿamr, and ʿammara: the duration and extension of life

One of this root’s clearest Qur’anic fields is the duration of human life.

Yūnus says:

قُلْ لَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ مَا تَلَوْتُهُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَلَا أَدْرَاكُمْ بِهِ ۖ فَقَدْ لَبِثْتُ فِيكُمْ عُمُرًا مِنْ قَبْلِهِ ۚ أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ

Qul law shāʾa llāhu mā talawtuhu ʿalaykum wa-lā adrākum bihi fa-qad labithtu fīkum ʿumuran min qablihi a-fa-lā taʿqilūn.

“Say: Had The God willed, I would not have recited it to you, nor would He have made it known to you. I lived among you for a lifetime before it. Will you not reason?”

Yūnus 10:16

عُمُرًا, ʿumuran, here is the span of life spent among the people before the Qur’an’s revelation.

Al-Naḥl also says:

وَمِنْكُمْ مَنْ يُرَدُّ إِلَىٰ أَرْذَلِ الْعُمُرِ لِكَيْ لَا يَعْلَمَ بَعْدَ عِلْمٍ شَيْئًا

Wa-minkum man yuraddu ilā ardhali l-ʿumuri li-kay lā yaʿlama baʿda ʿilmin shayʾā.

“Among you is one who is returned to the most diminished stage of life, so that after having knowledge he knows nothing.”

Al-Naḥl 16:70

And in Al-Anbiyāʾ:

بَلْ مَتَّعْنَا هَٰؤُلَاءِ وَآبَاءَهُمْ حَتَّىٰ طَالَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْعُمُرُ ۗ أَفَلَا يَرَوْنَ أَنَّا نَأْتِي الْأَرْضَ نَنْقُصُهَا مِنْ أَطْرَافِهَا ۚ أَفَهُمُ الْغَالِبُونَ

Bal mattaʿnā hāʾulāʾi wa-ābāʾahum ḥattā ṭāla ʿalayhimu l-ʿumuru a-fa-lā yarawna annā naʾtī l-arḍa nanquṣuhā min aṭrāfihā a-fa-humu l-ghālibūn.

“Rather, We granted enjoyment to these people and their fathers until life grew long for them. Do they not see that We come to the land, diminishing it at its edges? Are they then the victors?”

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:44

ʿUmur is therefore not abstract time in general, but the duration of a living being’s life.

This branch extends through the form II verb عَمَّرَ, ʿammara:

وَاللَّهُ خَلَقَكُمْ مِنْ تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ مِنْ نُطْفَةٍ ثُمَّ جَعَلَكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا ۚ وَمَا تَحْمِلُ مِنْ أُنْثَىٰ وَلَا تَضَعُ إِلَّا بِعِلْمِهِ ۚ وَمَا يُعَمَّرُ مِنْ مُعَمَّرٍ وَلَا يُنْقَصُ مِنْ عُمُرِهِ إِلَّا فِي كِتَابٍ ۚ إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ عَلَى اللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ

Wa-llāhu khalaqakum min turābin thumma min nuṭfatin thumma jaʿalakum azwājan wa-mā taḥmilu min unthā wa-lā taḍaʿu illā bi-ʿilmihi wa-mā yuʿammaru min muʿammarin wa-lā yunqaṣu min ʿumurihi illā fī kitābin inna dhālika ʿalā llāhi yasīr.

“The God created you from dust, then from a drop, then made you pairs. No female conceives or gives birth except with His knowledge. No one is granted life, nor is anything diminished from his lifespan, except that it is in a record. That is easy for The God.”

Fāṭir 35:11

Three forms occur together in this ayah:

يُعَمَّرُ

Yuʿammaru.

is granted life, or has their life extended;

مُعَمَّر

Muʿammar.

someone who has been granted life;

And:

عُمُرِهِ

ʿUmurihi.

the duration of his life. Two well-known explanations concern the pronoun in ʿumurihi: the passing of days from the same person’s life, or another person’s lifespan being shorter than that of the long-lived person. The root’s lexical definition alone does not resolve this interpretive difference; both explanations concern the duration of life.

Al-Baqarah 2:96 also says:

يَوَدُّ أَحَدُهُمْ لَوْ يُعَمَّرُ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ

Yawaddu aḥaduhum law yuʿammaru alfa sanah.

“One of them wishes to be granted a thousand years of life.”

And in Yā Sīn:

وَمَنْ نُعَمِّرْهُ نُنَكِّسْهُ فِي الْخَلْقِ ۖ أَفَلَا يَعْقِلُونَ

Wa-man nuʿammirhu nunakkis-hu fī l-khalqi a-fa-lā yaʿqilūn.

“Whomever We grant long life, We reverse in creation. Will they not reason?”

Yā Sīn 36:68

In this branch, عَمَّرَ, ʿammara, concerns extending life and granting a lifespan. The later sense of “repairing” an object should not be imported into these ayahs: their subject is a person’s continued life, not the repair of a thing.

Al-Ḥijr presents the same branch concerning life in an oath:

لَعَمْرُكَ إِنَّهُمْ لَفِي سَكْرَتِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ

Laʿamruka innahum la-fī sakratihim yaʿmahūn.

“By your life, they wander blindly in their intoxication.”

Al-Ḥijr 15:72

This established formula uses عَمْر, ʿamr, with a on the first consonant, while عُمُر, ʿumur, and عُمْر, ʿumr, are also recorded for life and its duration. The vowel difference does not create two separate roots: both belong to this family, and لَعَمْرُكَ, laʿamruka, is an oath by life.

Maintaining land, mosques, and houses: more than construction

The root’s other branch concerns a place being inhabited and maintained, and keeping it so.

Al-Rūm says of earlier peoples:

أَوَلَمْ يَسِيرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ فَيَنْظُرُوا كَيْفَ كَانَ عَاقِبَةُ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِهِمْ ۚ كَانُوا أَشَدَّ مِنْهُمْ قُوَّةً وَأَثَارُوا الْأَرْضَ وَعَمَرُوهَا أَكْثَرَ مِمَّا عَمَرُوهَا وَجَاءَتْهُمْ رُسُلُهُمْ بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ فَمَا كَانَ اللَّهُ لِيَظْلِمَهُمْ وَلَٰكِنْ كَانُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ يَظْلِمُونَ

A-wa-lam yasīrū fī l-arḍi fa-yanẓurū kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu lladhīna min qablihim kānū ashadda minhum quwwatan wa-athārū l-arḍa wa-ʿamarūhā akthara mimmā ʿamarūhā wa-jāʾathum rusuluhum bi-l-bayyināti fa-mā kāna llāhu li-yaẓlimahum wa-lākin kānū anfusahum yaẓlimūn.

“Have they not travelled through the land and seen the outcome of those before them? They were stronger than these people, worked the earth, and developed it more than these have developed it. Their Messengers came to them with clear signs. The God would not wrong them; rather, they were wronging themselves.”

Al-Rūm 30:9

عَمَرُوهَا, ʿamarūhā, concerns the land. Rendering it as “they constructed buildings” restricts the word without justification. Developing and maintaining land can include settlement, use, cultivation, construction, and keeping it active and inhabited. Which activities occurred in a particular historical instance must be understood from the context, not from the root alone.

The same issue is clear in the case of mosques:

مَا كَانَ لِلْمُشْرِكِينَ أَنْ يَعْمُرُوا مَسَاجِدَ اللَّهِ شَاهِدِينَ عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِهِمْ بِالْكُفْرِ

Mā kāna li-l-mushrikīna an yaʿmurū masājida llāhi shāhidīna ʿalā anfusihim bi-l-kufr.

“It is not for those who associate partners to maintain the mosques of The God while bearing witness against themselves to kufr.”

Al-Tawbah 9:17

And:

إِنَّمَا يَعْمُرُ مَسَاجِدَ اللَّهِ مَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَأَقَامَ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَى الزَّكَاةَ وَلَمْ يَخْشَ إِلَّا اللَّهَ

Innamā yaʿmuru masājida llāhi man āmana bi-llāhi wa-l-yawmi l-ākhiri wa-aqāma ṣ-ṣalāta wa-ātā z-zakāta wa-lam yakhsha illā llāh.

“Only those who believe in The God and the Last Day, establish Salat, give Zakat, and fear none but The God maintain the mosques of The God.”

Al-Tawbah 9:18

And then:

أَجَعَلْتُمْ سِقَايَةَ الْحَاجِّ وَعِمَارَةَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ كَمَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَجَاهَدَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ

A-jaʿaltum siqāyata l-ḥājji wa-ʿimārata l-masjidi l-ḥarāmi ka-man āmana bi-llāhi wa-l-yawmi l-ākhiri wa-jāhada fī sabīli llāh.

“Have you made giving water to pilgrims and maintaining the Sacred Mosque equal to one who believes in The God and the Last Day and strives in the way of The God?”

Al-Tawbah 9:19

These passages show that عمارة المسجد, ʿimārat al-masjid, cannot be reduced to constructing a mosque building. The mosque already exists and is still something people yaʿmurūna, maintain. Attendance, service, use, care, and sustaining its function belong to the word’s field.

“Maintaining” or “keeping inhabited and functioning” therefore comes closer to the root’s sense here than simply “building a mosque.”

Al-Ṭūr also says:

وَالْبَيْتِ الْمَعْمُورِ

Wa-l-bayti l-maʿmūr.

“By the inhabited and maintained House.”

Al-Ṭūr 52:4

معمور, maʿmūr, is a passive participle from this family: something made ʿāmir and kept in an inhabited, maintained condition.

The root itself does not identify exactly which house this bayt is. Identifying its referent is separate from establishing maʿmūr’s lexical meaning. Accounts of its precise location should therefore not be inserted into the basic translation or the root’s definition.

“The inhabited House” is a possible rendering, but the word should not be understood solely in terms of repair or physical architecture. Continued functioning, presence, and use also belong to maʿmūr’s semantic field.

اسْتَعْمَرَكُمْ فِيهَا — istaʿmarakum fīhā

In Ṣāliḥ’s address, we read:

وَإِلَىٰ ثَمُودَ أَخَاهُمْ صَالِحًا ۚ قَالَ يَا قَوْمِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ مَا لَكُمْ مِنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرُهُ ۖ هُوَ أَنْشَأَكُمْ مِنَ الْأَرْضِ وَاسْتَعْمَرَكُمْ فِيهَا فَاسْتَغْفِرُوهُ ثُمَّ تُوبُوا إِلَيْهِ ۚ إِنَّ رَبِّي قَرِيبٌ مُجِيبٌ

Wa-ilā Thamūda akhāhum Ṣāliḥan qāla yā qawmi ʿbudū llāha mā lakum min ilāhin ghayruhu huwa anshaʾakum mina l-arḍi wa-staʿmarakum fīhā fa-staghfirūhu thumma tūbū ilayhi inna rabbī qarībun mujīb.

“To Thamūd [We sent] their brother Ṣāliḥ. He said: My people, serve The God; you have no god other than Him. He brought you into being from the earth and appointed you to inhabit and develop it. Seek His forgiveness, then turn back to Him. My Lord is near and responsive.”

Hūd 11:61

اسْتَعْمَرَكُمْ فِيهَا, istaʿmarakum fīhā, occurs only once in the Qur’an.

This construction has been understood in two related ways: The God made people the earth’s inhabitants and cultivators, or He called on them to develop and maintain it. The form X pattern alone cannot decisively establish either reading.

The clearer shared ground is that humanity has been placed in a relationship with the earth involving dwelling, remaining, and maintaining habitation and development.

A rendering such as:

“He appointed you to inhabit and develop it”

is therefore a proposal, but it does not fully resolve the difference between the readings. “Appointed” suggests assigning a task. To foreground the first reading, “He made you its inhabitants and cultivators” is clearer. The choice between these renderings requires semantic explanation, rather than reliance on the form X pattern alone.

The modern political meaning of istiʿmār, “colonialism,” should not be read back into this ayah either. In modern Arabic it denotes one power’s domination over another land and its people. That later historical meaning is not what اسْتَعْمَرَكُمْ فِيهَا, istaʿmarakum fīhā, means in the Qur’an.

ʿUmrah and iʿtamara: visitation and a recognized Qur’anic practice

Another branch of this root consists of عُمْرَة, ʿumrah, and اعْتَمَرَ, iʿtamara.

Al-Baqarah says:

إِنَّ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةَ مِنْ شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ ۖ فَمَنْ حَجَّ الْبَيْتَ أَوِ اعْتَمَرَ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِ أَنْ يَطَّوَّفَ بِهِمَا ۚ وَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ شَاكِرٌ عَلِيمٌ

Inna ṣ-ṣafā wa-l-marwata min shaʿāʾiri llāhi fa-man ḥajja l-bayta awi ʿtamara fa-lā junāḥa ʿalayhi an yaṭṭawwafa bihimā wa-man taṭawwaʿa khayran fa-inna llāha shākirun ʿalīm.

“Al-Ṣafā and Al-Marwah are among the sacred signs of The God. Whoever performs Hajj to the House or performs ʿumrah incurs no blame in passing between the two. Whoever voluntarily does good, The God is appreciative and knowing.”

Al-Baqarah 2:158

And:

وَأَتِمُّوا الْحَجَّ وَالْعُمْرَةَ لِلَّهِ

Wa-atimmū l-ḥajja wa-l-ʿumrata li-llāh.

“Complete Hajj and ʿumrah for The God.”

Al-Baqarah 2:196

In Arabic, اعتمار, iʿtimār, has also been associated with setting out toward a destination and visiting. Someone travelling to a place or coming to visit it could be called a muʿtamir.

This background fits the Qur’anic practice of ʿumrah, which also involves setting out for and visiting the House. Etymology should not, however, reduce the Qur’an’s specific usage back to the general meaning “visiting.”

In the Qur’an, العمرة, al-ʿumrah, appears alongside Hajj and within passages concerning the House, Al-Ṣafā, and Al-Marwah, as the name of a recognized practice. The basic translation should therefore retain “ʿumrah,” with its lexical background discussed separately.

Nor should it be asserted without decisive evidence that iʿtimār must have developed from a place being ʿāmir, or that a muʿtamir originally meant someone going to an inhabited place. Such connections can be examined, but the precise direction of the word’s historical development is unclear.

This branch is consequently one of the points that requires caution when proposing a wholly unified definition of the root.

AYN M R (ʿamara), Ḥ Y Y (ḥayiya), and B N Y (banā): lifespan, life, and building

ʿUmur is close to ḥayāh, “life,” but they are not identical.

ح ي ي (حَيِيَ), Ḥ Y Y (ḥayiya), concerns being alive and having life. A being is ḥayy, alive, and possesses life.

In its temporal branch, ع م ر (عَمَر), AYN M R (ʿamara), concerns more particularly the duration and continuation of that life. The Qur’an can therefore speak of a long lifespan, its most diminished stage, granting life, and a lifespan being reduced.

As a general tendency:

Ḥayāh is the state of being alive;

ʿUmur is the duration and continuation of that life.

The boundary is not absolute, but it is useful when translating Qur’anic usage.

In the spatial branch, ʿamara also overlaps with ب ن ي (بَنَى), B N Y (banā), without being equivalent to it.

بنى, banā, relates directly to constructing and bringing a structure into being.

عمر المكان, ʿamara al-makān, can include building, but extends beyond it: habitation, use, care, and sustaining a place’s life and function also belong to its range.

Thus:

عَمَرُوا الْأَرْضَ

ʿAmarū l-arḍ.

should not be rendered only as “they constructed buildings on the land.”

And:

عِمَارَةَ الْمَسْجِدِ

ʿImārata l-masjid.

does not mean only “building a mosque” or “mosque architecture.”

Dwelling is also close to this field, but ʿimārah is not simply residence. A place can be ʿāmir through attendance, use, service, and care; its scope can extend beyond an individual’s dwelling there.

Translation proposals for Forqan

In the branch concerning life:

عُمُر, ʿumur
“Lifespan” or “the duration of life”

عَمْر, ʿamr, in لَعَمْرُكَ, laʿamruka
“Life” in an oath

عَمَّرَ / يُعَمَّرُ, ʿammara / yuʿammaru
“To grant life” / “to be granted life,” “to extend a lifespan,” and, where appropriate, “to grant long life”

مُعَمَّر, muʿammar
“One granted life” or “someone who has received a lifespan”

In the spatial branch:

عَمَرَ الْأَرْضَ, ʿamara al-arḍ
“He developed and inhabited the land”

يَعْمُرُ الْمَسْجِدَ, yaʿmuru al-masjid
“He maintains the mosque and keeps it functioning”

عِمَارَةُ الْمَسْجِدِ, ʿimārat al-masjid
“Maintaining the mosque and keeping it functioning”

مَعْمُور, maʿmūr
“Maintained,” “kept functioning,” or, where appropriate, “inhabited”

For:

اسْتَعْمَرَكُمْ فِيهَا

Istaʿmarakum fīhā.

a rendering such as:

“He appointed you to inhabit and develop it”

is a proposal, not a neutral equivalent that completely preserves both readings. “He made you its inhabitants and cultivators” expresses the first reading more clearly. Neither refers to the modern political sense of istiʿmār, “colonialism.”

For:

عُمْرَة / اعْتَمَرَ

ʿUmrah / iʿtamara.

in the Qur’anic context, “ʿumrah” and “to perform ʿumrah” are appropriate. The general sense of setting out to visit belongs in the lexical explanation.

Conclusion

The Qur’anic root AYN M R (ʿ M R) has two clear principal branches: the continuation of life and continued presence and habitation in a place. In the first, ʿumur is the duration of life, and ʿammara expresses granting life and extending it. In the second, land, a mosque, or a house is ʿāmir or maʿmūr: presence, use, and habitation continue there. “Continued presence” can bring these branches closer together semantically, but should not be treated as the proven historical source of all the derivatives.

The main distinctions follow from these branches. ʿUmur is not identical to ḥayāh: ḥayāh concerns more particularly the state of being alive, while ʿumur concerns its duration and continuation. Nor is ʿimārah identical to bināʾ, “building.” Construction can be part of developing a place, but ʿimārah also includes presence, use, and continued functioning. Later senses such as repairing an object, or the modern political sense of istiʿmār as colonialism, must therefore not be imposed on Qur’anic usage.

Caution remains necessary concerning the historical relationship between the branches, especially ʿumrah and iʿtimār. Iʿtimār is associated in the language with setting out toward a destination and visiting, while Qur’anic al-ʿumrah names a recognized practice. Yet the precise path linking this branch with ʿumur, ʿimārah, and ʿāmir cannot be confidently reconstructed. That caution allows the family’s relationships to be recognized without reducing all its forms to an invented derivational story.

A concise definition of the root can be stated as follows:

AYN M R (ʿ M R): in one branch, the continuation and duration of life; in another, continued presence and habitation in a place, and keeping it inhabited and maintained. ʿUmrah and iʿtimār also concern setting out toward a destination and visiting, but their precise historical relationship to the root’s other uses should remain an open question approached with caution.
