Core meanings: proximity, presence, and being with or in someone's possession; in another branch, departing from a course, opposition, and obstinate persistence

The Arabic family ع ن د has two important semantic branches, both represented in the Quran. Grouping them in one lexical entry does not establish that the adverb عِنْد derives from the verb of opposition.

First, عِنْد means with, in the presence of, or in relation to someone or something. This can concern location, but frequently expresses possession, availability, origin, or standing in someone's estimation.

Second, عَنَدَ and words such as عَنِيد and عِنَاد concern departing from a course, opposing, and persisting in a position against another. In ethical usage, عَنِيد describes someone obstinately resisting the truth.

A spatial relationship can be imagined between the branches: being beside someone and taking a different direction. Such a semantic association does not establish their historical derivation.

The Quran uses two principal forms: عِنْد with various attached elements, and عَنِيد describing obstinate resistance to truth.

1. Meanings in early Arabic

عِنْد: proximity and presence

Early Arabic uses عِنْد to express presence or proximity in relation to a person, place, or situation.

عِنْدَ الرَّجُلِ can mean being with the man; مَا عِنْدَهُ can mean what is with him or in his possession.

The word functions as an adverbial noun and, depending on the construction, can concern place, time, or a relationship to a person.

A clear poetic example occurs in the Muʿallaqah of Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā:

وَمَهْمَا تَكُنْ عِنْدَ امْرِئٍ مِنْ خَلِيقَةٍ
وَإِنْ خَالَهَا تَخْفَى عَلَى النَّاسِ تُعْلَمِ

wa-mahmā takun ʿinda imriʾin min khalīqatin / wa-in khālahā takhfā ʿalā al-nāsi tuʿlami

“Whatever trait a person has, even if he imagines it hidden from people, will become known.”

عِنْدَ امْرِئٍ here concerns a person's character trait.

Such a trait is not an object placed beside the person; it is a quality the person possesses.

عِنْد therefore expresses having a quality rather than merely physical location.

This witness shows that possession and attribution were established uses in pre-Islamic Arabic. The line alone does not date a historical transition from a spatial meaning to these uses.

عَنَدَ: departing from a course

The other branch concerns deviation from a course and opposition.

Arabic dictionaries record عَنَدَ عَنِ الطَّرِيقِ as turning aside or departing from the road.

عَنَدَ عَنِ الْأَمْرِ can describe turning away from or opposing a matter.

Walking along a road provides a concrete illustration of this use.

Imagine a group traveling together while one person leaves the main road for another route. This departure can be expressed through this word family.

نَاقَةٌ عَنُودٌ describes a she-camel that separates from the herd to graze apart or strays from its usual course.

This use does not itself concern morality or sin. The animal may separate through its natural behavior; in human applications, leaving a course can picture opposition or defiance.

طَرِيقٌ عَانِدٌ is also recorded for a road that deviates from a straight course.

These examples associate opposition and obstinacy with turning aside or separating from a particular direction.

They are recorded in later dictionaries, however. Each use cannot confidently be dated to the pre-Islamic period without independent evidence.

From turning aside to resistance to truth

In human conduct, عِنَاد concerns opposition and persistence in a position.

Not every difference of opinion or choice of another course constitutes obstinacy.

Two people may disagree while offering reasons and remaining willing to hear each other. Such disagreement is not necessarily عِنَاد.

Someone else may recognize clear evidence but continue opposing it through pride, self-interest, prejudice, or unwillingness to accept the truth.

That behavior illustrates the ethical use of عِنَاد.

Dictionaries also explain عَنِيد as a defiant opponent who recognizes the truth but refuses to accept it.

Lexical description must nevertheless be distinguished from judging someone's inner state. Disagreement alone does not prove that a person recognizes and knowingly denies the truth.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important forms include:

عِنْد: with, in the presence of, in someone's possession, or in relation to someone or something.

عَنَدَ: departed from a course, opposed, or turned away from something.

عِنَاد: opposition and obstinate persistence.

عَانِد: one who opposes or departs from a course.

عَنِيد: obstinate in opposition, particularly to truth.

عَنُود: separating or straying from a course; in human use, defiant or unyielding in opposition.

عَانَدَ: opposed someone or stood against them.

مُعَانَدَة: obstinate opposition or confrontation.

مُعَانِد: an opponent or obstinate adversary.

تَعَانَدَ: opposed or contended with one another.

Only عِنْد and عَنِيد occur in the Quran. The verbs عَنَدَ and عَانَدَ and the nouns عِنَاد and مُعَانَدَة do not occur there.

عِنْد appears with different pronouns and following مِنْ, as in عِنْدَ اللَّهِ, عِنْدَهُ, عِنْدَكُمْ, مِنْ عِنْدِنَا, and مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ.

3. Quranic uses of عِنْد

A. Proximity and presence

One direct use expresses proximity or presence beside a person or at a particular place.

An-Najm 53:14

عِنْدَ سِدْرَةِ الْمُنْتَهَىٰ

ʿinda sidrati al-muntahā

Al-Najm 53:14

“At the lote tree of the utmost boundary.”

عِنْدَ locates something in relation to سِدْرَةِ الْمُنْتَهَىٰ.

This use concerns proximity.

The word alone does not specify an exact distance, direction, or physical characteristics of the location.

Elsewhere it describes people being in one another's presence.

Al-Hujurat 49:3 describes those who lower their voices عِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ: in the presence of God's Messenger.

Here the relationship concerns an actual situation of human presence and behavior.

An-Naml 27:40 uses عِنْدَهُ عِلْمٌ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ. عِنْدَهُ concerns knowledge that the person possesses, rather than knowledge physically sitting beside him.

The word can thus express either spatial presence or having something.

B. Having and possessing

An-Nahl 16:96

مَا عِنْدَكُمْ يَنْفَدُ وَمَا عِنْدَ اللَّهِ بَاقٍ ۗ وَلَنَجْزِيَنَّ الَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا أَجْرَهُمْ بِأَحْسَنِ مَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

mā ʿindakum yanfadu wa-mā ʿinda allāhi bāqin wa-la-najziyanna alladhīna ṣabarū ajrahum bi-aḥsani mā kānū yaʿmalūn

Al-Naḥl 16:96

“What you have runs out, but what is with The God endures. We will reward those who remained steadfast according to the best of what they used to do.”

عِنْدَكُمْ concerns things people possess or have available.

These may include property, resources, and benefits of life.

مَا عِنْدَ اللَّهِ concerns what is with God, whose endurance the verse emphasizes.

The two constructions express having or being with, but their objects differ.

What people have is finite and exhaustible; what is with God is described as enduring.

Exhaustibility and endurance are expressed by the verb يَنْفَدُ and the active participle بَاقٍ, not by عِنْد itself.

The word alone does not mean permanence, absolute ownership, or reward in the Hereafter; those senses depend on other words and context.

C. Origin and source

Al-Kahf 18:65

فَوَجَدَا عَبْدًا مِنْ عِبَادِنَا آتَيْنَاهُ رَحْمَةً مِنْ عِنْدِنَا وَعَلَّمْنَاهُ مِنْ لَدُنَّا عِلْمًا

fa-wajadā ʿabdan min ʿibādinā ātaynāhu raḥmatan min ʿindinā wa-ʿallamnāhu min ladunnā ʿilmā

Al-Kahf 18:65

“They found a servant from among Our servants to whom We had given mercy from Us and whom We had taught knowledge from Us.”

مِنْ عِنْدِنَا identifies the source of the mercy.

Combining مِنْ with عِنْد supplies the sense of origin or source.

In this context it can be translated “from Us” or “from Our presence.”

The same verse uses مِنْ لَدُنَّا for the knowledge God taught that servant.

Their coexistence shows overlap in expressing the source of a divine gift, but does not establish a fixed, universal distinction between the words.

مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ does not by itself require a specific bodily location for God. It can express origin, issuance, or attribution to God.

This use also appears in passages about revelation and divine gifts.

D. Attributing speech or writing to God

Al-Baqarah 2:79

فَوَيْلٌ لِلَّذِينَ يَكْتُبُونَ الْكِتَابَ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ ثُمَّ يَقُولُونَ هَٰذَا مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ لِيَشْتَرُوا بِهِ ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا ۖ فَوَيْلٌ لَهُمْ مِمَّا كَتَبَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَوَيْلٌ لَهُمْ مِمَّا يَكْسِبُونَ

fa-waylun li-alladhīna yaktubūna al-kitāba bi-aydīhim thumma yaqūlūna hādhā min ʿindi allāhi li-yashtarū bihi thamanan qalīlan fa-waylun lahum mimmā katabat aydīhim wa-waylun lahum mimmā yaksibūn

Al-Baqarah 2:79

“Woe to those who write the writing with their own hands and then say, ‘This is from The God,’ to obtain a small price for it. Woe to them for what their hands have written, and woe to them for what they earn.”

مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ expresses attribution of the writing to God.

The issue is its claimed origin and authority, not its storage location.

Those described produce a writing themselves and then claim it comes from God.

The expression therefore concerns a claim of divine origin for speech or writing.

عِنْد can accordingly participate in expressions of authority and attribution to a source or responsible party.

Actually originating from God differs from a claim to that effect.

The construction expresses origin in either case; context and evidence establish whether the attribution is true.

E. Standing and worth before God

Al-Hujurat 49:13

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ إِنَّا خَلَقْنَاكُمْ مِنْ ذَكَرٍ وَأُنْثَىٰ وَجَعَلْنَاكُمْ شُعُوبًا وَقَبَائِلَ لِتَعَارَفُوا ۚ إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ أَتْقَاكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ خَبِيرٌ

yā ayyuhā al-nāsu innā khalaqnākum min dhakarin wa-unthā wa-jaʿalnākum shuʿūban wa-qabāʾila li-taʿārafū inna akramakum ʿinda allāhi atqākum inna allāha ʿalīmun khabīr

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:13

“People, We created you from a male and a female and made you peoples and tribes so that you may know one another. The most honored of you before The God is the most mindful of Him. The God is Knowing, Aware.”

عِنْدَ اللَّهِ concerns a person's standing and worth before God.

The point is not that some people are physically closer to God than others.

The verse states a criterion of honor and connects it with mindfulness of God.

عِنْد here situates a person in relation to divine judgment and valuation.

As-Saff 61:3 likewise describes saying what one does not do as greatly hateful عِنْدَ اللَّهِ.

That construction also concerns divine judgment rather than physical location.

When عِنْد occurs with God, context determines whether origin, reward, knowledge, valuation, or proximity is intended.

These uses should not all be reduced to a narrow spatial meaning.

F. Knowledge with God

Al-An'am 6:59 says:

وَعِنْدَهُ مَفَاتِحُ الْغَيْبِ

wa-ʿindahu mafātiḥu al-ghayb

Al-Anʿām 6:59

“With Him are the keys of the unseen.”

عِنْدَهُ concerns what lies within God's authority and encompassing knowledge.

The verse should not be reduced to a bodily picture of keys stored at a place beside God.

مَفَاتِحُ الْغَيْبِ needs examination in the context of divine knowledge and the continuation: no one knows them except God.

Luqman 31:34 similarly says عِنْدَهُ عِلْمُ السَّاعَةِ: knowledge of the Hour is with Him.

عِنْد here expresses a relation of possession and knowledge.

The word can concern human property, knowledge, and ability, and divine knowledge, authority, and giving.

Human possession and comprehension are not identical to God's knowledge and power.

G. عِنْدَ رَبِّهِمْ: standing with their Lord

Al Imran 3:169 says of those killed in God's way:

بَلْ أَحْيَاءٌ عِنْدَ رَبِّهِمْ يُرْزَقُونَ

bal aḥyāʾun ʿinda rabbihim yurzaqūn

Āl ʿImrān 3:169

They are alive, receiving provision with their Lord.

عِنْدَ رَبِّهِمْ concerns their relation to and standing with their Lord.

عِنْد alone does not fully specify the nature of this life, provision, or presence.

Uncertainty about its manner does not erase the verse's affirmation that they are alive and receive provision.

“With their Lord” is therefore a suitable direct translation; explaining its manner requires independent examination of the passages on life, death, and provision.

4. عَنِيد: obstinate resistance to truth

The second branch appears in the Quran as the adjective عَنِيد.

Its pattern is فَعِيل, describing someone persistent in opposition and defiance.

Its Quranic occurrences consistently concern censured resistance to truth.

This does not make every instance of firmness, disagreement, or refusal obstinate defiance; the particular use and conduct matter.

Hud 11:59: following an obstinate tyrant

وَتِلْكَ عَادٌ ۖ جَحَدُوا بِآيَاتِ رَبِّهِمْ وَعَصَوْا رُسُلَهُ وَاتَّبَعُوا أَمْرَ كُلِّ جَبَّارٍ عَنِيدٍ

wa-tilka ʿādun jaḥadū bi-āyāti rabbihim wa-ʿaṣaw rusulahu wa-ittabaʿū amra kulli jabbārin ʿanīd

Hūd 11:59

“Such was Ad: they rejected their Lord's signs, disobeyed His Messengers, and followed the command of every obstinate tyrant.”

عَنِيدٍ qualifies جَبَّارٍ.

جَبَّار here describes a domineering person seeking to impose his will on others.

عَنِيد adds another quality: obstinate opposition and turning away from truth.

جَبَّارٍ عَنِيدٍ thus describes tyranny combined with obstinate resistance, not merely strength or firmness.

The verse also concerns the people of Ad following such persons' commands.

Responsibility is not confined to the tyrant's conduct: following oppressive commands is also part of the passage.

The lexical sense of عَنِيد remains obstinacy in opposition; others' obedience belongs to the social context described.

Ibrahim 14:15: the failure of obstinate tyrants

وَاسْتَفْتَحُوا وَخَابَ كُلُّ جَبَّارٍ عَنِيدٍ

wa-istaftaḥū wa-khāba kullu jabbārin ʿanīd

Ibrāhīm 14:15

“They sought a decisive victory, and every obstinate tyrant failed.”

The combination جَبَّارٍ عَنِيدٍ appears again.

Its repetition associates عَنِيد with tyranny and defiance in this context.

The word does not itself simply mean tyrant: جَبَّار supplies that distinct description.

The two adjectives together provide a fuller picture of the person's conduct.

عَنِيد emphasizes persistent opposition; جَبَّار emphasizes domination and overstepping one's bounds.

Both descriptions should therefore be preserved in translation.

Qaf 50:24: disbelief accompanied by obstinacy

أَلْقِيَا فِي جَهَنَّمَ كُلَّ كَفَّارٍ عَنِيدٍ

alqiyā fī jahannama kulla kaffārin ʿanīd

Qāf 50:24

“Cast into Hell every obstinate disbeliever.”

عَنِيدٍ qualifies كَفَّارٍ.

كَفَّار follows the intensive pattern فَعَّال and describes persistent disbelief or denial in this context.

عَنِيد emphasizes obstinate opposition.

Together they portray more than an ordinary mistake or simple lack of knowledge.

Qaf 50:25 continues with withholding good, transgression, and doubt or the causing of doubt; these are recorded explanations of مُرِيب.

The passage thus describes a collection of censured behaviors and qualities.

Each has its own meaning; they should not all be included in the lexical definition of عَنِيد.

عَنِيد concerns obstinate opposition; withholding good and transgression are additional descriptions in the next verse.

Al-Muddaththir 74:16: opposition to the signs

كَلَّا إِنَّهُ كَانَ لِآيَاتِنَا عَنِيدًا

kallā innahu kāna li-āyātinā ʿanīdā

Al-Muddaththir 74:16

“No indeed! He was obstinately opposed to Our signs.”

This is especially explicit because لِآيَاتِنَا directly names the object of the opposition.

The opposition is to the divine signs.

Al-Muddaththir 74:18 describes thinking and calculating; Al-Muddaththir 74:23 mentions turning away and acting arrogantly. Al-Muddaththir 74:24 and Al-Muddaththir 74:25 then report his statements about the Quran.

This sequence portrays more than a simple sudden reaction: thought, positioning, turning away, and arrogance are involved.

عَنِيد here describes stubborn resistance to the signs and refusal to accept them.

It does not follow that everyone who questions or doubts an interpretation is obstinate.

Asking to understand, criticizing an interpretation, requesting evidence, or disagreeing with human explanations is not itself defiance of God's signs.

The distinction is between seeking truth and knowingly persisting in rejection after it becomes clear.

5. Comparison with related words and roots

عِنْد and قُرْب

قَرُبَ and its derivatives express being or becoming near.

عِنْد can express proximity but commonly locates something in relation to a person or place.

عِنْدَ الرَّجُلِ can concern something with the man, while قَرِيبٌ مِنَ الرَّجُلِ directly describes nearness to him.

Their uses overlap, but their grammatical constructions differ.

In مَا عِنْدَكُمْ, عِنْد can express possession or availability, which قَرِيب does not automatically express.

عِنْد and لَدُن

لَدُن also expresses proximity and, in constructions with مِنْ, origin.

Al-Kahf 18:65 contains both مِنْ عِنْدِنَا and مِنْ لَدُنَّا.

Both can identify the source of a divine gift.

Their coexistence alone does not establish a universal rule about degrees of proximity or exclusivity.

“From Us” naturally translates both here; a finer distinction would require examination of their wider uses.

عِنْد and مَعَ

مَعَ expresses accompaniment or togetherness.

عِنْد may relate to presence or company, but principally locates or relates something to another person or place.

Someone may be with a friend without participating in everything that friend does.

Two people may also share a goal without being physically together.

عِنْد and مَعَ therefore should not always receive identical translations.

عَنِيد and جَبَّار

In censured human use, جَبَّار concerns domination and imposing one's will on others.

عَنِيد emphasizes obstinate opposition and defiance.

A person may be obstinate without the social power needed to dominate others.

A tyrant may also persist against truth and possess both qualities.

Hud 11:59 and Ibrahim 14:15 combine them in جَبَّارٍ عَنِيدٍ.

Their combination does not erase the distinction; both can be present in one person.

عَنِيد and اِسْتِكْبَار

اِسْتِكْبَار concerns arrogance and claiming superiority.

عِنَاد concerns obstinate opposition.

Pride may lead someone to reject a truth and persist in opposition; arrogance can therefore motivate obstinacy.

Al-Muddaththir 74:23 uses وَاسْتَكْبَرَ about the person described as عَنِيدًا in Al-Muddaththir 74:16.

The sequence connects these two characteristics in that person's conduct.

They are not exact synonyms: opposition can have other motives, and arrogance takes different forms.

عِنَاد and عَدَاوَة

عَدَاوَة means enmity.

عِنَاد concerns persistent opposition.

They may coexist without being identical.

A person may dislike another yet accept a truth from them on a particular issue.

Someone may persist against truth through prejudice or attachment to an earlier position without personal hostility.

Enmity and obstinacy are thus related but distinct concepts.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

The two principal branches should be treated separately in translation.

عِنْدَ and its attached forms

عِنْدَ

Brief meaning: proximity, presence, and being in relation to someone or something; the other lexical branch concerns opposition and obstinacy.

Contextual meaning: with, in the presence of, in someone's possession, or in relation to a particular person, depending on context.

Proposed translation: “with” or “at.”

عِنْدَكُمْ and عِنْدَهُمْ

Brief meaning: proximity, presence, and possession.

Contextual meaning: with you or in your possession; with them or in their possession, according to the pronoun.

Proposed translation: “with you,” “with them,” or, where the sentence warrants it, “what you have.”

عِنْدَهُ and عِنْدِي

Brief meaning: presence, proximity, and possession.

Contextual meaning: with him or with me; in some constructions, knowledge or something possessed.

Proposed translation: “with him,” “with me,” or where appropriate “he has” and “I have.”

عِنْدَ اللَّهِ

Brief meaning: proximity and presence; attribution or standing.

Contextual meaning: with God; depending on context, in divine judgment, knowledge, authority, or reward.

Proposed translation: “with The God” or “before The God.”

مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ and مِنْ عِنْدِنَا

Brief meaning: proximity and presence; origin in a construction with مِنْ.

Contextual meaning: from God or from Us, according to the pronoun; used of giving, revelation, command, or attribution.

Proposed translation: “from The God” or “from Us.”

“From” comes from مِنْ and should not be treated as an independent meaning of عِنْد itself.

عَنِيد

عَنِيدٍ — Hud 11:59 and Ibrahim 14:15

Brief meaning: obstinate opposition and departing from a course.

Contextual meaning: someone whose tyranny is accompanied by persistent opposition and defiance.

Proposed translation of جَبَّارٍ عَنِيدٍ: “an obstinate tyrant.”

عَنِيدٍ — Qaf 50:24

Brief meaning: opposition and obstinate persistence.

Contextual meaning: someone persistent in disbelief and opposition.

Proposed translation of كَفَّارٍ عَنِيدٍ: “an obstinate disbeliever.”

عَنِيدًا — Al-Muddaththir 74:16

Brief meaning: obstinate opposition to something.

Contextual meaning: someone stubbornly opposing the divine signs.

Proposed translation: “He was obstinately opposed to Our signs.”

“Obstinate,” “stubborn,” or “defiant” may fit, depending on the sentence.

The word should not simply become disbeliever, wrongdoer, arrogant person, or tyrant: these have distinct semantic fields.

“Unyielding” can also be positive, as in resisting injustice. Where ambiguity arises, “obstinate in resisting truth” or “defiant” makes the Quranic application clearer.

7. Conclusion

Two important semantic branches are treated under ع ن د in this Quranic study.

First, عِنْد expresses proximity, presence, and a relation to a person or place. This can concern physical location, possession, availability, source, standing, or authority.

عِنْدَ اللَّهِ is therefore not spatial in every passage: it can concern God's judgment, knowledge, giving, reward, or someone's standing with Him.

Second, عَنِيد describes someone persistent in obstinate opposition and defiance.

The Quran combines it with جَبَّار or كَفَّار, or relates it directly to the divine signs, consistently censuring opposition to truth.

A semantic association between being beside someone and turning aside into opposition may be imagined, but should not become a definite historical derivation of all these uses.

Not all disagreement is obstinacy. Criticism, questions, uncertainty, and disagreement in pursuit of truth differ from knowingly resisting it.

Final definition of ع ن د:

“In the عِنْد branch: proximity, presence, and relation to someone or something, extending to possession, authority, origin, and standing. In the عَنَدَ and عَنِيد branch: turning aside, opposing, and obstinate persistence. In Quranic ethical usage, عَنِيد describes someone persisting in defiance of truth.”
