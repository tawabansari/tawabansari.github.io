Core meaning: taking a mouthful and swallowing food; in some Arabic uses, eating or drinking copiously. In the Quran, zaqqūm names a tree presented as food for sinners in the Fire.

1. The root and its meaning in early Arabic

The root Z Q M concerns eating, taking a mouthful, and swallowing food. The verb زَقَمَ denotes taking and swallowing a mouthful; تَزَقَّمَ is also recorded for taking a mouthful and swallowing something.

Some uses involve haste or excess. For example, تَزَقَّمَ اللَّبَنَ describes someone drinking a large quantity of milk.

In another recorded branch, زَقُّوم names a particular food or plant.

Two matters must therefore be distinguished: the act of eating and swallowing, and the name of what is eaten.

An early lexical explanation describes the root as follows:

أُصَيْلٌ يَدُلُّ عَلَى جِنْسٍ مِنَ الْأَكْلِ

uṣaylun yadullu ʿalā jinsin mina al-akl

Meaning: “A root indicating a kind of eating.”

In this explanation, زَقْم concerns taking a mouthful, and اِزْدِقَام concerns swallowing.

This definition clarifies the recorded classical Arabic semantic field; by itself, it does not establish that every form had precisely the same meaning before Islam.

Pre-Islamic meaning: the limits of the evidence

A definition in dictionaries compiled centuries later does not alone establish pre-Islamic usage. Independent, datable evidence is needed to show how a word was used before the Quran.

Uses concerning mouthfuls, swallowing, and copious drinking are recorded for Z Q M. In the evidence examined here, however, no independent poetic witness for zaqqūm has been securely established as pre-Islamic in attribution and date.

This evidence therefore does not establish with certainty whether pre-Islamic Arabs used zaqqūm for a particular tree or food.

Later lexical reports record zaqqūm both for a food and for a bitter plant. Their existence alone does not establish that these names were current before the Quran.

Etymological explanations connect zaqqūm with zaqama. The connection is intelligible in form and in the field of eating, but dating and explaining the tree’s name requires further historical evidence.

A defensible formulation is therefore:

In classical Arabic, zaqm denotes taking a mouthful and swallowing food; some uses include copious eating or drinking. The datable evidence examined here does not fully establish this family’s precise pre-Quranic range, particularly the name zaqqūm.

This historical limitation does not leave the Quranic meaning unknown: the Quran itself introduces zaqqūm and describes its characteristics.

2. Important forms and derivatives

زَقَمَ يَزْقُمُ

This Form I verb means to take a mouthful, eat, or swallow.

زَقَمَ اللُّقْمَةَ

zaqama al-luqmata

Meaning: “He took and swallowed the mouthful.”

زَقَمَ اللَّحْمَ

zaqama al-laḥma

Meaning: “He swallowed the meat.”

Eating and swallowing food are involved. Haste or excess may occur in some uses, but neither should be made a necessary feature of every occurrence.

زَقْم

The verbal noun denotes taking a mouthful and eating.

Some lexical explanations equate zaqm with laqm; others emphasize vigorous eating.

Zaqm can thus denote a kind of eating and swallowing, while intensity and quantity depend on form and context.

زَقَّمَ يُزَقِّمُ

In uses concerning food, it denotes feeding someone zaqqūm or eating it.

This verb is recorded in Arabic but does not occur in the Quran.

The doubled consonant alone does not establish torment, coercion, or frequent repetition. Such features require contextual evidence.

تَزَقَّمَ يَتَزَقَّمُ

It means to take a mouthful, eat, or swallow something.

تَزَقَّمَ اللُّقْمَةَ

tazaqqama al-luqmata

Meaning: “He swallowed the mouthful.”

In another use:

تَزَقَّمَ اللَّبَنَ

tazaqqama al-labana

Meaning: “He drank a great deal of milk.”

This use extends the family from eating solid food to drinking.

The milk example does not make Quranic zaqqūm the name of a beverage.

اِزْدَقَمَ يَزْدَقِمُ

It means to swallow or ingest.

اِزْدَقَمَ الشَّيْءَ

izdaqama al-shayʾa

Meaning: “He swallowed the thing.”

This is Form VIII; the form’s tāʾ changes to dāl in this construction.

Consequently, اِزْدَقَمَ belongs to Z Q M, not an independent root containing dāl.

Its meaning overlaps with تَزَقَّمَ: both can express swallowing.

أَزْقَمَ

This Form IV verb means to feed something to another or cause them to swallow it.

أَزْقَمَهُ الشَّيْءَ

azqamahu al-shayʾa

Meaning: “He fed it to him or caused him to swallow it.”

The construction distinguishes the person feeding from the recipient.

Causation does not necessarily imply violent compulsion.

زَقُّوم

In the Quran, it names a tree presented as food for sinners in the Fire.

Its morphological pattern is فَعُّول.

The form can be connected with eating and swallowing, but morphology alone does not determine the food’s nature, quantity, or the tree’s physical properties.

Lexical records use zaqqūm for a food and for certain bitter plants.

One report describes a shrub with small round leaves, an unpleasant smell, and a bitter taste called zaqqūm.

The report shows use for an earthly plant, but neither the exact date of naming nor identity with the Quranic tree is established.

زَقْمَة

One lexical use denotes plague.

This sense is recorded in Arabic, but its historical connection with eating and swallowing is not certain.

Sharing a root does not make disease part of the Quranic meaning of zaqqūm.

Of these forms, only زَقُّوم occurs in the Quran.

3. Quranic usage

Z Q M occurs three times: Al-Saffat 37:62, Al-Dukhan 44:43, and Al-Waqiah 56:52.

Each occurrence concerns a tree or food in the context of punishment in the hereafter.

Twice it has the definite article, الزَّقُّوم; once it is indefinite, زَقُّومٍ.

Definiteness alone does not establish several different species of tree.

A. Al-Saffat 37:62: zaqqūm contrasted with the Garden’s hospitality

أَذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ نُّزُلًا أَمْ شَجَرَةُ الزَّقُّومِ

a-dhālika khayrun nuzulan am shajaratu al-zaqqūm

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:62

Translation:

“Is that better as hospitality, or the tree of zaqqūm?”

Here الزَّقُّومِ is the genitive complement of شَجَرَةُ.

The construction identifies the tree by the name zaqqūm; it does not independently establish a separate entity to which the tree belongs.

The preceding verses describe the place and provision of those in the Garden.

The question contrasts that provision with the tree of zaqqūm.

نُزُلًا concerns hospitality and what is prepared for a guest.

The tree is presented as food in contrast with welcome hospitality and the Garden’s blessings.

The verse places zaqqūm in the role of food or hospitality, but the name itself does not lexically mean punishment or hospitality.

A tree emerging from the depths of the Fire

Al-Saffat 37:64 describes zaqqūm as a tree emerging from the depths of the blazing Fire.

Al-Saffat 37:65 likens its emerging growth to the heads of devils.

Al-Saffat 37:66 says that they eat from it and fill their bellies.

Al-Saffat 37:67 then describes a mixture of intensely hot water for them.

These verses specify a tree, growing in the Fire, with emerging growth that is eaten.

طَلْع concerns what emerges from a tree, particularly blossoms or the first appearance of fruit.

The comparison with devils’ heads presents a terrifying appearance.

These verses do not specify the wood, leaves, size, fruit color, or biological structure.

An earthly plant’s characteristics cannot therefore be assigned to zaqqūm without evidence.

Can a tree grow in fire?

In ordinary human experience, fire burns plants and wood. The verse concerns a tree in the blazing Fire without explaining its creation or survival.

An analogy with earthly plants alone cannot reduce the verse’s account to a claim about the impossibility of trees growing in fire.

Nor should unsupported theories about unknown materials, molecular structure, or biological fire resistance be invented.

The Quran reports such a tree in the hereafter; its full manner of existence is not explained.

Zaqqūm and the trial of wrongdoers

Al-Saffat 37:63 says that The God has made the tree a trial for wrongdoers.

One reading relates this trial to people’s response to the divine report. This is a contextual interpretation, not the lexical meaning of zaqqūm.

Nearby verses condemn opposition, mockery, and denial of the truth.

Yet fitnah does not become the lexical definition of zaqqūm.

Zaqqūm names the tree; its role as a trial is expressed separately in إِنَّا جَعَلْنَاهَا فِتْنَةً لِلظَّالِمِينَ.

B. Al-Dukhan 44:43: zaqqūm as the sinner’s food

إِنَّ شَجَرَتَ الزَّقُّومِ

inna shajarata al-zaqqūm

Al-Dukhān 44:43

Translation:

“Indeed, the tree of zaqqūm”

The sentence is completed in the following verse.

Al-Dukhan 44:44

طَعَامُ الْأَثِيمِ

ṭaʿāmu al-athīm

Al-Dukhān 44:44

Translation:

“is the sinner’s food.”

Together, the verses explicitly identify the tree as food for the sinner.

الْأَثِيمِ belongs to the root ʾ TH M and denotes sinfulness.

This description is not part of the lexical definition of zaqqūm.

Nor does the verse say that every person who has committed a sin must eat zaqqūm regardless of repentance, reform, and divine judgment.

The passage concerns people who oppose the truth and face the consequences of their deeds on the Day of Recompense.

Comparing the food with muhl and boiling water

Al-Dukhan 44:45 compares the food with مُهْل and describes its boiling in bellies.

Al-Dukhan 44:46 compares this boiling with intensely hot water.

These descriptions clarify the suffering associated with eating zaqqūm.

Muhl belongs to a different root and should not replace zaqqūm’s definition.

The comparison also does not establish that zaqqūm is identical in every material property to molten metal or a similar substance.

The comparison illustrates a feature of the food within the description of punishment.

Rendering zaqqūm as “molten metal” or “boiling liquid” is therefore inaccurate.

C. Al-Waqiah 56:52: eating from the tree of zaqqūm

لَآكِلُونَ مِن شَجَرٍ مِّن زَقُّومٍ

la-ākilūna min shajarin min zaqqūm

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:52

Translation:

“You will surely eat from the tree of zaqqūm.”

Here زَقُّومٍ appears without the definite article.

مِن شَجَرٍ مِّن زَقُّومٍ directly connects zaqqūm with trees and eating.

شَجَر is a generic or collective term for trees; zaqqūm specifies the name or kind.

Al-Waqiah 56:53 says that they will fill their bellies with it.

Al-Waqiah 56:54 describes drinking intensely hot water afterward.

Al-Waqiah 56:55 compares their drinking with that of hīm, an expression connected with intense thirst.

Al-Waqiah 56:56 identifies this food and drink as their hospitality on the Day of Recompense.

This agrees with the descriptions in Al-Saffat and Al-Dukhan.

In all three, zaqqūm names food associated with a tree and punishment in the hereafter.

The details of one verse should not simply be inserted into the word’s lexical meaning elsewhere.

4. Zaqqūm: a tree’s name or the act of swallowing?

A central question concerns the relationship between zaqama and the name zaqqūm.

In Arabic, zaqama concerns taking a mouthful and swallowing.

In the Quran, zaqqūm names a tree from which sinners eat.

The semantic relationship is intelligible: a word in the eating and swallowing family names something presented as food.

This does not make the word mean “a large mouthful,” “greedy eating,” or “forced swallowing” in the verse.

In Quranic usage, zaqqūm is the tree’s name.

Intensity, full bellies, and suffering from the food are conveyed by other clauses.

Al-Saffat 37:66 and Al-Waqiah 56:53 explicitly express the filling of bellies separately.

Excessive eating should therefore not be inserted into the name itself.

Is zaqqūm the earthly bitter plant?

The name is also recorded for certain bitter, unpleasant plants.

Descriptions include their leaves, flowers, and smell.

A shared name does not establish identical natures.

The Quran describes a tree emerging from the depths of the blazing Fire. This cannot be established for an earthly plant of a particular climate without evidence.

Nor does an earthly plant bearing the name prove that the Quran refers only to that plant.

Comparing plant names can inform word history, but cannot replace the Quran’s own description.

Zaqqūm and a dish of dates and butter

Some historical and lexical reports identify zaqqūm as food made from dates and butter.

Some attribute the name to a language or region outside the Hijaz.

The reports differ in attribution and detail and themselves relate to circumstances after the verses’ revelation.

They do not establish dates and butter as the original, certain pre-Islamic Arabic meaning.

The Quran, moreover, explicitly calls zaqqūm a شجرة, a tree.

Even if some dialects use the name for other food, that different sense should not replace the meaning established by the verses.

5. Related roots and concepts

Z Q M (زَقْم) and L Q M (لَقْم)

Both concern taking mouthfuls and eating.

Laqm particularly denotes taking and eating a mouthful.

Zaqm is also explained through taking mouthfuls and swallowing, with vigorous eating or copious drinking prominent in some uses.

The distinction is not absolute: lexical explanations sometimes treat the words as equivalent.

The root alone does not make eating necessarily painful or unwilling.

Z Q M (زَقْم) and B L ʿ (بَلْع)

Balʿ denotes swallowing something.

Zaqm can include swallowing food, but its familiar uses are more closely associated with taking mouthfuls and eating.

Hud 11:44 uses B L ʿ for the earth swallowing water.

This shows that swallowing can extend beyond human eating.

Quranic zaqqūm, meanwhile, names food associated with a tree, rather than the act of swallowing.

Z Q M (زَقُّوم) and Ḍ R ʿ (ضَرِيع)

Al-Ghashiyah 88:6 mentions food called ضَرِيع.

Al-Waqiah 56:52 also describes food in the context of punishment, naming zaqqūm.

Both occur as unpleasant food in the hereafter, but belong to different roots.

A shared role in punishment does not establish that they name the same plant.

Nor can complete difference in their nature be asserted without evidence.

Each is introduced in a particular context, and the verbal distinction should remain in translation.

Z Q M (زَقُّوم) and SH J R (شَجَر)

Shajar denotes trees or plants with a tree-like structure.

Zaqqūm names a tree introduced in verses concerning punishment.

Al-Saffat 37:62 and Al-Dukhan 44:43 use شجرة الزقوم.

Al-Waqiah 56:52 has شجر من زقوم.

Shajar identifies the kind of entity, while zaqqūm supplies its name.

The root Z Q M alone does not yield the tree’s botanical structure.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

The following equivalents suit important forms. These research proposals do not replace installed verse translations or existing authored word-box explanations.

زَقَمَ: took a mouthful, ate, or swallowed.

زَقْم: taking mouthfuls and swallowing food; vigorous eating in some uses.

تَزَقَّمَ: took a mouthful and swallowed; drank copiously in the milk example.

اِزْدَقَمَ: swallowed or ingested.

أَزْقَمَهُ: fed something to him or caused him to swallow it.

زَقَّمَ: in recorded uses, ate zaqqūm or fed it to someone.

زَقْمَة: in another lexical use, plague.

زَقُّوم: the name of a tree presented in the Quran as food for sinners in the Fire.

Proposed verse translations

In Al-Saffat 37:62:

أَذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ نُّزُلًا أَمْ شَجَرَةُ الزَّقُّومِ

a-dhālika khayrun nuzulan am shajaratu al-zaqqūm

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:62

“Is that better as hospitality, or the tree of zaqqūm?”

In Al-Dukhan 44:43:

إِنَّ شَجَرَتَ الزَّقُّومِ

inna shajarata al-zaqqūm

Al-Dukhān 44:43

“Indeed, the tree of zaqqūm”

The continuation in Al-Dukhan 44:44 identifies it as the sinner’s food.

In Al-Waqiah 56:52:

لَآكِلُونَ مِن شَجَرٍ مِّن زَقُّومٍ

la-ākilūna min shajarin min zaqqūm

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:52

“You will surely eat from the tree of zaqqūm.”

For all three occurrences, retaining zaqqūm is preferable to substituting “bitter food,” “poisonous plant,” or “a mouthful of punishment.”

Those phrases may express a feature or interpretation, but are not the Quranic name.

A brief general root meaning could read:

“Taking mouthfuls and swallowing food; sometimes eating or drinking copiously.”

A verse-specific explanation could read:

“Zaqqūm: the name of a tree presented as food for sinners in the Fire.”

The distinction preserves the lexical family’s meaning without turning the tree’s name into an act of eating.

7. Conclusion

Z Q M concerns taking mouthfuls, eating, and swallowing. Recorded uses sometimes include vigorous eating or copious drinking.

The evidence examined here does not adequately establish the family’s precise pre-Islamic range, particularly the origin of zaqqūm’s name. Later dictionary meanings cannot therefore be assigned to the pre-Quranic period without qualification.

Only the form zaqqūm occurs in the Quran.

In three verses it names a tree associated with sinners’ food in the hereafter.

Al-Saffat 37:62 contrasts it with the Garden’s hospitality.

Al-Dukhan 44:43 introduces the tree; the next verse calls it the sinner’s food.

Al-Waqiah 56:52 describes eating zaqqūm; following verses describe full bellies and drinking intensely hot water.

These descriptions place zaqqūm in the context of punishment, but the name does not lexically mean fire, punishment, poison, or molten metal.

Reports of earthly plants or foods bearing the name in particular languages and dialects do not determine the Quranic tree’s nature.

The recorded root meaning is taking mouthfuls and swallowing. Quranic zaqqūm, however, names a particular tree whose characteristics are learned from the verses themselves, not merely from its name’s derivation.

This distinction preserves lexical accuracy and prevents historical hypotheses, botanical reports, or interpretive explanations from replacing the Quran’s explicit account.
