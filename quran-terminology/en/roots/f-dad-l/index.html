Brief Introduction to the Root

The root F Ḍ L moves in the semantic field of being additional, exceeding a baseline, leaving something beyond the ordinary measure, possessing an extra share, and having an advantage or capacity beyond what is being compared.

From this core arise meanings such as bounty, increase, added gift, abundance, a larger share, relative advantage, goodness beyond obligation, and benefit beyond the usual limit.

In the Qur’an, the root can describe gifts from The God to human beings; differences among persons, messengers, communities, provisions, and crops; or human kindness and forbearance beyond an enforceable right.

Faḍl is not inherently an unchangeable superiority of essence or lineage. It is something added, granted, or manifested in comparison with another level. Tafḍīl is therefore normally relative, contextual, and tied to a particular gift, capacity, or outcome.

Receiving more faḍl can create greater responsibility, but responsibility is not the root’s direct dictionary meaning. It arises when a person receives clearer knowledge, wider opportunity, greater provision, or more signs and is then tested in the response to that increase.

Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

Faḍl is the family’s central noun. Depending on context it can mean increase, bounty, additional gift, abundance, extra benefit, favor, added mercy, provision, or goodness beyond obligation.

Faḍḍala is Form II. It indicates granting someone or something an increase over another within a specified field. A common construction is faḍḍala baʿḍahum ʿalā baʿḍ—He gave some an increase over others.

Fuḍḍilū is its passive form, used of those granted more provision or opportunity.

Tafḍīl is the Form II verbal noun, denoting the act of granting increase or making a difference conspicuous.

Yatafaḍḍalu is Form V. Opponents of Noah used it when alleging that he wanted to place himself above them. Here the root enters the field of claimed self-exaltation rather than genuine, responsible gift.

The Root's Semantic Core

The core of F Ḍ L is:

An increase relative to a baseline, ordinary share, required limit, or compared party.

This increase may appear in property, provision, knowledge, guidance, ability, reward, produce, position, opportunity, or good conduct. Faḍl is often not one specific object but a relation between a baseline and what exceeds it.

This is especially visible in faḍḍala. When the Qur’an says that some messengers were given increase over others, some people received more provision, or some crops were made better than others, it names a specific difference in a specific domain. Increase in one respect does not entail absolute existential or ethical superiority in all respects.

Faḍl is not always equivalent to entitlement. When something comes “from the faḍl of The God,” the emphasis is on a gift exceeding narrow calculation and strict exchange. Action and deserved reward may exist, but faḍl is the added part not confined to wage and equivalence.

The Root's Material Image

The material image is something remaining after the principal amount has been supplied or exceeding an appointed measure.

One may imagine a vessel that has received its baseline amount and then receives more. The addition is faḍl. One may likewise imagine a share larger than need or customary measure.

From this image arise several branches: the person receiving more has faḍl; what exceeds the ordinary limit is faḍl; and one who grants another a greater amount or position has given tafḍīl.

This explains why the word can concern property, knowledge, guidance, reward, social capacity, crops, and human forbearance. What they share is something beyond the baseline.

This image only explains the linguistic structure. The faḍl of The God must not be imagined as a limited human material surplus.

Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, F Ḍ L is connected with excess, remaining beyond what is needed, and surpassing in a particular respect. Usages concerning leftover water, additional property, personal advantage, generosity, and nobility developed within this field.

The relation between “surplus” and “generosity” is understandable from the same structure. One who pays only an enforceable right acts at the prescribed limit; one who gives more, relinquishes a right, or continues kindness beyond legal calculation enters the field of faḍl.

Later historical and cultural usages should not be imposed upon the Qur’an without examination. The Qur’an places this root in its own network: gift, mercy, provision, knowledge, guidance, reward, gratitude, testing, envy, withholding, work, and human relationships.

History of Interpretation, Misuse, and Semantic Distortion

A major distortion is turning tafḍīl into absolute, intrinsic, and permanent superiority.

When the Qur’an says that a person, group, messenger, or creature was given increase over another, the statement must remain within its context. The field may be knowledge, scripture, prophethood, provision, mission, historical experience, produce, or a particular ability.

Tafḍīl in one domain does not establish absolute ethical, racial, or existential superiority for all time. This matters especially in reading verses about the Children of Israel. Turning faḍḍaltukum ʿalā al-ʿālamīn into intrinsic superiority of blood and lineage ignores the root’s structure: the increase is given, not an essence necessarily originating in the recipients.

Their historical tafḍīl should not be denied either. The Qur’an clearly names scripture, judgment, prophethood, rescue, wholesome provision, and signs given to them. The task is to identify the kind and scope of increase.

Another error treats faḍl only as a pleasant gift without consequence. The Qur’an connects it with gratitude, testing, withholding, envy, and action. Added gift may become good, but it may also become an occasion for ingratitude, monopoly, or self-exaltation.

Faḍl is neither a natural right of superiority nor a gift unrelated to response. It is granted increase whose final value becomes clear through the recipient’s response.

Methodological Note

Four levels must be distinguished:

First, the lexical meaning: increase relative to a baseline.

Second, the kind of increase in context: provision, knowledge, guidance, scripture, reward, produce, capacity, or position.

Third, the recipient’s ethical worth: receiving faḍl does not prove superiority or righteousness in every dimension.

Fourth, responsibility resulting from faḍl: this is not derived from the word alone but from its Qur’anic links with gratitude, testing, withholding, knowledge, action, and accountability.

A mechanical translation of faḍḍala as “made superior” may therefore mislead, because “superior” often implies intrinsic value. “Granted more,” “made more endowed,” “gave a greater share,” or “gave an advantage in this respect” may be more precise.

Nor should the meaning of another root be extracted from F Ḍ L. Determining the exact range of al-ʿālamīn, for example, requires separate study of ʿ L M and its context. F Ḍ L shows only that a comparative relation exists.

Qur'anic Uses of the Root

Faḍl as Added Gift from The God

One of the root’s broadest uses attributes faḍl to The God: wa-Allāhu dhū al-faḍli al-ʿaẓīm—The God is possessor of immense faḍl. Here faḍl may encompass guidance, forgiveness, mercy, provision, knowledge, security, or reward.

قُلْ إِنَّ الْفَضْلَ بِيَدِ اللَّهِ يُؤْتِيهِ مَنْ يَشَاءُ

“Say: faḍl is in the hand of The God; He gives it to whomever He wills.”

Ali ‘Imran 3:73

The verse removes faḍl from ethnic or institutional ownership. No lineage, people, or class intrinsically owns it.

Faḍl in Provision and Seeking the Means of Life

لَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ أَنْ تَبْتَغُوا فَضْلًا مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ

“There is no blame upon you for seeking faḍl from your Lord.”

Al-Baqarah 2:198

Here faḍl can be provision and abundance sought through human effort. It is not always a gift arriving without movement; searching, work, travel, and use of the world’s means can belong to its path.

Faḍl as Goodness Beyond Obligation

وَلَا تَنْسَوُا الْفَضْلَ بَيْنَكُمْ

“Do not forget faḍl between yourselves.”

Al-Baqarah 2:237

Here faḍl is neither wealth nor superiority. It is forbearance, generosity, and goodness beyond dry legal calculation. Law can define the minimum right; human relations also need kindness beyond that minimum.

Faḍl and Differences in Provision

وَاللَّهُ فَضَّلَ بَعْضَكُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ فِي الرِّزْقِ

“The God has given some of you increase over others in provision.”

Al-Nahl 16:71

Fī al-rizq defines the domain. The verse concerns provision, not human dignity. Economic increase cannot become proof of greater human worth or a right to domination.

Faḍl and Differences Among Messengers

تِلْكَ الرُّسُلُ فَضَّلْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ

“Those messengers—We granted some increase over others.”

Al-Baqarah 2:253

The verse itself names aspects of the difference: The God spoke to some, raised some in degrees, and gave Jesus clear signs. Tafḍīl concerns mission, degree, or particular gift and does not authorize contempt for another messenger or an arbitrary hierarchy.

Faḍl in the Produce of the Earth

وَنُفَضِّلُ بَعْضَهَا عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ فِي الْأُكُلِ

“We give some of them increase over others in produce.”

Al-Ra’d 13:4

This is decisive for the root: land and produce have no moral virtue. Fruits differ in taste, quality, or yield. Faḍḍala thus concerns an added difference within a specified field.

Faḍl, Knowledge, and Teaching

وَعَلَّمَكَ مَا لَمْ تَكُنْ تَعْلَمُ وَكَانَ فَضْلُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ عَظِيمًا

“He taught you what you did not know, and the faḍl of The God upon you was immense.”

Al-Nisa 4:113

Faḍl is linked with teaching and protection. Knowledge, discernment, and preservation from error may manifest faḍl.

Faḍl Beyond Earned Reward

لِيُوَفِّيَهُمْ أُجُورَهُمْ وَيَزِيدَهُمْ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ

“So that He may pay their rewards in full and increase them from His faḍl.”

Fatir 35:30

Reward corresponds to action; faḍl is increase beyond full payment. This is among the clearest Qur’anic examples of “added gift.”

Faḍl, Testing, and Gratitude

هَٰذَا مِنْ فَضْلِ رَبِّي لِيَبْلُوَنِي أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ

“This is from the faḍl of my Lord, to test me whether I give thanks or cover it.”

Al-Naml 27:40

Solomon does not make added gift evidence of intrinsic superiority; he sees a test of gratitude or kufr. Responsibility is not the word’s lexical meaning, but faḍl places its recipient in a tested position.

Faḍl and Withholding

وَلَا يَحْسَبَنَّ الَّذِينَ يَبْخَلُونَ بِمَا آتَاهُمُ اللَّهُ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ هُوَ خَيْرًا لَهُمْ

“Let those who withhold what The God has given them from His faḍl not think it better for them.”

Ali ‘Imran 3:180

Faḍl does not create closed, responsibility-free ownership. The network is: received increase → possibility of withholding or giving → disclosure of the human response.

Faḍl and Envy

أَمْ يَحْسُدُونَ النَّاسَ عَلَىٰ مَا آتَاهُمُ اللَّهُ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ

“Or do they envy people for what The God has given them from His faḍl?”

Al-Nisa 4:54

Because faḍl creates difference, it can become an object of envy. The Qur’an does not deny the gift but restrains envious objection. Al-Nisa 4:32 directs people away from coveting what others received and toward asking The God from His faḍl.

The Use of F Ḍ L in Al-Baqarah 2:47

يَا بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ اذْكُرُوا نِعْمَتِيَ الَّتِي أَنْعَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَأَنِّي فَضَّلْتُكُمْ عَلَى الْعَالَمِينَ

“O Children of Israel, remember My blessing that I bestowed upon you and that I granted you increase over the worlds.”

Al-Baqarah 2:47

The verse can be read within gift and responsibility or distorted into ethnic, intrinsic superiority. Its verb is faḍḍaltukum: I granted you increase. The God is the agent; the Children of Israel are not presented as the intrinsic source of the faḍl.

The surah relates the increase to historical and guiding gifts: rescue from Pharaoh, crossing the sea, scripture, covenant, messengers, clear signs, teaching, and an opportunity to carry tawhid.

Al-Jathiyah 45:16 makes the content clearer:

وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحُكْمَ وَالنُّبُوَّةَ وَرَزَقْنَاهُمْ مِنَ الطَّيِّبَاتِ وَفَضَّلْنَاهُمْ عَلَى الْعَالَمِينَ

“We gave the Children of Israel scripture, judgment, and prophethood, provided them with wholesome things, and granted them increase over the worlds.”

Scripture, judgment, prophethood, and wholesome provision are named materials of this tafḍīl. It is increased access to means of guidance and historical experience, not a different human essence.

Joseph likewise says:

ذَٰلِكَ مِنْ فَضْلِ اللَّهِ عَلَيْنَا وَعَلَى النَّاسِ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ النَّاسِ لَا يَشْكُرُونَ

“That is from the faḍl of The God upon us and upon people, but most people do not give thanks.”

Yusuf 12:38

His statement connects faḍl with teaching, avoidance of shirk, the way of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and gratitude. Upon us and upon people prevents faḍl from becoming a family’s closed possession.

The sequence after Al-Baqarah 2:46 can also be read reflectively in the horizon of return and account, though it creates no new dictionary meaning. Scripture, knowledge, rescue, messengers, and signs do not terminate in the recipient; the response is tested.

The wider network confirms this: faḍl with testing and gratitude in Solomon’s words; with withholding in Ali ‘Imran; with envy in Al-Nisa; with tawhid and gratitude in Joseph’s words; and with goodness beyond obligation in Al-Baqarah 2:237.

Responsibility is not the morphology of faḍḍaltukum, but a contextual consequence of the gifts and response. The exact range of al-ʿālamīn also requires separate study. The root only establishes a comparative increase; the verses identify its kind.

The Children of Israel’s tafḍīl should therefore neither be denied nor converted into biological or racial superiority or guaranteed salvation. In a historical period they received more scripture, prophethood, judgment, rescue, and signs. The faḍl was for carrying truth, not ethnic ownership of truth.

Examination of Important Qur'anic Derivatives

فَضْل — Granted Increase

Faḍl is the root’s broadest Qur’anic form. Context determines whether it concerns guidance and mercy, knowledge and teaching, provision and economic opportunity, rescue and protection, added reward, natural resources, or human generosity. No single English equivalent suits every occurrence.

فَضَّلَ — Granting Increase Within a Relation

Form II creates or highlights a difference. Each occurrence requires asking: increase in what, relative to whom or what, within what time and context, and whether it is an external gift, result of action, or created difference. Without these answers, “made superior” may carry more than the text.

تَفْضِيل — Emphatic, Manifest Increase

وَفَضَّلْنَاهُمْ عَلَىٰ كَثِيرٍ مِمَّنْ خَلَقْنَا تَفْضِيلًا

“We granted them manifest increase over many of those We created.”

Al-Isra 17:70

The verbal noun emphasizes the conspicuous increase. Yet the verse says “over many,” not “over all”; even emphatic tafḍīl has a boundary.

يَتَفَضَّلَ — Alleged Self-Exaltation

يُرِيدُ أَنْ يَتَفَضَّلَ عَلَيْكُمْ

“He wants to exalt himself over you.”

Al-Mu’minun 23:24

This is an allegation of personal superiority-seeking, not divine gift. Faḍḍalahu Allāh means The God granted him increase in a field; yatafaḍḍalu ʿalā al-nās means he seeks to put himself above people.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

فَضَّلَ … عَلَى …

This structure establishes comparison. ʿAlā introduces the second party but does not make the increase universal. Its subject matter comes from context.

فَضَّلَ … بِـ

مَا فَضَّلَ اللَّهُ بِهِ بَعْضَكُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ

“That by which The God granted some of you increase over others.”

Al-Nisa 4:32

Bihi points to the field or means of difference; tafḍīl is not presented without a subject or basis.

مِنْ فَضْلِهِ

What a human receives is “from His faḍl”; the human reception does not encompass all faḍl.

ذُو الْفَضْل

Dhū introduces possession. Dhū al-faḍli al-ʿaẓīm identifies The God as the source and possessor of immense gift.

Faḍl Alongside Reward

Where full payment of reward is followed by increase from faḍl, the sentence distinguishes recompense proportionate to action from added gift.

Comparison with Related Roots

F Ḍ L and Z Y D: Faḍl and Increase

Z Y D denotes becoming more or increasing in quantity or intensity, whether positive or negative: knowledge, guidance, illness, punishment, or corruption can increase.

Faḍl normally presents increase relative to a baseline share, right, need, gift, or compared party and often includes an added advantage or benefit. Every faḍl is a kind of increase, but not every increase is faḍl.

F Ḍ L and N ʿ M: Faḍl and Blessing

N ʿ M is connected with ease, softness, and pleasant benefit. Faḍl emphasizes what is added beyond a baseline.

بِنِعْمَةٍ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَفَضْلٍ

“With a blessing from The God and faḍl.”

Ali ‘Imran 3:171

Their coordination shows they are not identical: blessing emphasizes good benefit, faḍl its added dimension.

F Ḍ L and R Ḥ M: Faḍl and Mercy

Mercy concerns care, protection, and delivery of good in need and vulnerability. Faḍl concerns increase, abundance, and gift beyond a baseline.

In faḍlu Allāhi ʿalaykum wa-raḥmatuh, the two give different angles: mercy as protection and rescue, faḍl as added gift and expansion.

F Ḍ L and ʾ J R: Faḍl and Reward

Ajr is given in return for action; work and recompense are prominent. Faḍl may go beyond that relation. When the Qur’an first promises full reward and then increase from faḍl, faḍl is what exceeds the calculation of wage and action.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

فَضْل

Depending on context: faḍl, increase, added gift, abundance, additional bounty, greater benefit, goodness beyond obligation, or added advantage/capacity.

“Bounty” alone does not always preserve the element of increase.

فَضَّلَ

Depending on context: granted increase, made more endowed, gave a greater share, granted a greater advantage in this field, or gave greater capacity or opportunity.

“Made superior” is suitable only when the field and limit are clear.

فَضَّلْتُكُمْ عَلَى الْعَالَمِينَ

Base rendering: I granted you increase over the worlds.

Explanatory: I made you more endowed than the worlds in guiding gifts and capacities.

The second is contextual explanation and should not replace direct translation without a marker.

فَضْلُ اللَّهِ

Depending on context: the faḍl of The God, the added gift of The God, the abundance granted by The God, or His gift beyond measure. Often faḍl should be retained and explained.

ذُو الْفَضْلِ الْعَظِيمِ

Possessor of immense faḍl, or possessor of vast and abundant gift.

وَلَا تَنْسَوُا الْفَضْلَ بَيْنَكُمْ

Do not forget added kindness and forbearance between yourselves.

“Superiority” or “bounty” alone does not communicate the meaning here.

Summary

F Ḍ L denotes increase relative to a baseline. It can appear in provision, knowledge, guidance, reward, capacity, produce, position, or good conduct.

Faḍl is something added; faḍḍala grants someone or something increase in a field; tafḍīl is the act or emphatic state of this added difference.

Qur’anic usage shows that tafḍīl is not necessarily absolute, intrinsic, or ethical superiority. Land and crops differ, people receive different provision, messengers differ in gifts and missions, and communities may receive more guiding signs in a historical period.

Faḍl is connected with gratitude, testing, withholding, envy, knowledge, action, and human forbearance. Greater gift can create greater responsibility. That responsibility is not the root’s dictionary core but a major contextual consequence.

In Al-Baqarah 2:47, the tafḍīl of the Children of Israel concerns their historical increase in scripture, messengers, judgment, rescue, signs, and opportunity to carry truth. It is neither racial superiority, ownership of truth, nor guaranteed salvation.

A vessel filled with more guidance stands in a clearer field of gratitude, covenant, and accountability.
