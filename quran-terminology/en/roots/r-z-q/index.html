Qur’anic Root R Z Q

Rizq, provision, delivered gift, fulfillment of need, and the opening of life and growth

Morphological forms of the root R Z Q

General meaning of the root R Z Q

The root “ر ز ق” is usually translated as “provision,” but its range is wider than food, wealth, livelihood, or daily income.

In Arabic usage, rizq refers to a gift or provision that reaches a creature in need and supports the continuation of life. This gift can have a continuous, daily, and unburdened character, like the provision by which a living being continues to exist.

This concrete layer helps us understand that rizq in the Qur’an is not an isolated or one-time gift, but a flow of delivery, care, support, and making-available. When The God is Al-Razzāq, the entire support-system of creation depends on Him at every moment.

In the Qur’an, rizq can refer to anything that reaches a creature from The God and makes survival, growth, movement, tranquility, guidance, gratitude, or responsibility possible.

Rizq can be food, water, fertile earth, wealth, children, security, knowledge, opportunity, ability, understanding, guidance, or even an opening that brings a person out of constriction.

So rizq is not only what the human being holds in his hand or sees in his financial account. Rizq is every delivered gift that helps the human being in life, growth, testing, responsibility, and return to The God.

Rizq is not only money and food

One common error in understanding rizq is to limit it to money, food, livelihood, employment, or income.

Undoubtedly, these are part of rizq, but they are not all of rizq.

In the Qur’anic view, human life does not continue by bread and wealth alone. The human being needs guidance, security, reason, an awakened heart, the opportunity to repent, the ability to work, health, love, tranquility, and the possibility of understanding truth.

All of these can be understood within the realm of rizq, if they reach the human being from The God and open the way for life, growth, reform, or return.

Sometimes a person has wealth but no tranquility. Sometimes he has food but no understanding. Sometimes he has power but no guidance. Sometimes he has opportunity but no gratitude. Such a person may outwardly possess rizq, yet remain deprived in his material or trans-material needs.

Therefore, Qur’anic rizq must not be reduced to wealth. Rizq is a delivered gift from The God, a gift that answers the need of the creature and places him within the path of growth or testing.

Rizq as the delivery of need to creation

In the root “ر ز ق,” the gift itself is not the only important thing; the delivery of that gift is also important.

Rizq means that something reaches a creature in need. The human being does not own the source of rizq. He strives, plants, works, trades, learns, and plans, but the arrival of the result takes place within a vast order that is not fully under his control.

The human being plants the seed, but he does not create the rain. This is the same truth the Qur’an recalls through a powerful question:

أَأَنتُمْ تَزْرَعُونَهُ أَمْ نَحْنُ الزَّارِعُونَ

Is it you who cause it to grow, or are We the Grower?

The human being strives, but he did not create breath, reason, opportunity, body, earth, time, or the possibility of movement. From here, the Qur’an moves the human gaze away from the arrogance of ownership and toward gratitude and responsibility.

Rizq means that the human being understands that what reached him is not merely the result of his personal power. Human effort matters, but effort takes place within an order of divine gift and possibility.

Razaqa and yarzuqu: the act of providing

Verbal forms from this root, such as “رَزَقَ” and “يَرْزُقُ,” show the act of providing, granting, and delivering benefit.

When the Qur’an says that The God provides, it is not speaking only of giving food or money. The God has established an order in which creatures can remain, grow, move, and enter the path of their test.

In this sense, rizq is connected to Lordship. The Lord is not only the initial Creator; He does not abandon creation. He creates, measures, guides, provides, grants time, and opens the way of return.

So the verb of rizq in the Qur’an is a sign of the continuation of The God’s Lordship.

Ruziqa and yurzaqu: being provided for

Passive forms such as “رُزِقَ” and “يُرْزَقُ” shift the gaze from human agency to the state of receiving.

In this state, the human being or creature stands as receiver. He does not possess absolute ownership over rizq; rizq is given to him, reaches him, or is made available for him.

This angle is important because it saves the human being from arrogance. Whoever sees rizq only as the product of his intelligence, power, tribe, class, trade, or effort has missed part of the truth. Human effort may be a cause, but the cause is not the source.

In the Qur’anic view, the human being is a receiver and a responsible being. What reached him is not only for personal consumption and private pride; it is also a test, a trust, and a responsibility.

Rizq: gift, share, provision, and possibility of life

“Rizq” is the central noun of this root. It can refer to provision, gift, share, availability, and that which is delivered for the life and movement of a creature.

Rizq is sometimes understood materially: food, fruit, water, land, wealth, or property. But in the Qur’an, the semantic range of rizq can be much wider.

If The God gives a person understanding, that too is a kind of rizq. If He opens the path of repentance, that too is rizq. If He brings a person out of misguidance, that too is rizq. If He grants him a heart that accepts truth, that is among the greatest forms of rizq.

For this reason, the believer should not pray only for an increase in wealth. He should ask The God for wholesome rizq, pure rizq, the rizq of guidance, the rizq of understanding, the rizq of gratitude, the rizq of righteous action, and the rizq of return.

Rāziqīn: providers

“Rāziq” is the active participle of this root, meaning provider or deliverer of rizq. The plural form “rāziqīn” is prominent in Qur’anic expressions such as “khayru al-rāziqīn,” the best of providers.

In the Qur’an, when the discussion turns to providers, two levels must be distinguished:

1. The true and independent provision of The God.

2. Limited and dependent delivery through causes and human beings.

A human being may become a means of rizq. Parents, employers, farmers, physicians, teachers, just rulers, or any person who delivers to others the possibility of life and growth may serve as a limited means of provision. But the independent source of rizq is The God.

The boss or employer is not the owner of rizq; he is a storekeeper of The God’s rizq. If understood properly, this sentence breaks the arrogance of the powerful and removes the humiliation of the needy before human beings.

No human being has the right, merely because he has become a means of rizq, to imagine himself owner of another person’s life, faith, dignity, or freedom. Whoever gives financial help to another did not create that person’s provision from his own private dominion; he only stood in a chain of causes whose origin is from The God.

This understanding saves the receiver from humiliation before creation and saves the giver from arrogance.

Al-Razzāq: The God as the constant and abundant Provider

“Razzāq” is an intensive form. It points to abundance, continuity, intensity, and breadth in providing.

When The God is introduced as “Al-Razzāq,” it does not mean that He occasionally gives something and then abandons the creature. Rather, He is the continuous source of rizq; His provision is not limited to one time, one people, one class, one creature, or one type of gift.

The provision of The God is not limited to believers. Many who do not know The God, or even respond with ingratitude, still benefit from air, water, earth, body, reason, opportunity, and the possibilities of life. This shows that The God’s rizq comes from His vast mercy and Lordship, not from the complete worthiness of the human being.

But abundant rizq is not always a sign of The God’s approval. Sometimes rizq is a test. Sometimes expansion of rizq brings a person closer to gratitude, and sometimes it drives him toward transgression. Therefore, not every wealth is a sign of honor, and not every outward poverty is a sign of humiliation.

Rizq and Lordship

Rizq is deeply connected to the Lordship of The God.

Lordship means nurturing, delivering, measuring, guiding, and placing a being on the path of its growth and completion. Rizq is one manifestation of this Lordship.

The God did not create beings and then abandon them. Creation continues through rizq. If rizq were cut off, life, growth, and the possibility of testing would be disrupted.

In this sense, rizq is a sign of The God’s Lordship. Every breath the human being takes, every bite he eats, every knowledge he learns, every opportunity he receives for reform, and every path opened for his return to truth is meaningful within the framework of Lordship.

Rizq and gratitude

Rizq in the Qur’an is connected to gratitude.

A person who sees rizq but forgets its source approaches ingratitude. Gratitude is not merely saying “thank you.” Gratitude means recognizing the blessing, acknowledging its source, and using it properly in the way of truth.

If someone uses wealth for oppression, corruption, deception, humiliation, or waste, he has taken rizq out of the path of gratitude. If someone uses knowledge for arrogance and humiliating others, his knowledge too has moved away from gratitude. If someone uses power to block the way of The God and pressure people, his power too becomes ingratitude rather than gratitude.

So gratitude for rizq means that every gift is placed in its proper place.

Rizq and giving

One of the most important signs of understanding rizq correctly is giving.

The Qur’an repeatedly invites the human being to give from what The God has provided. This expression matters because it reminds the human being that he is not the absolute owner; what he has was given to him.

Giving is not only giving money. If rizq is understood broadly, giving also becomes broad. A person can give from his wealth, knowledge, time, ability, skill, security, influence, tongue, and every good thing The God has delivered to him.

Every rizq carries its own responsibility. The rizq of knowledge carries the responsibility of teaching and clarification. The rizq of wealth carries the responsibility of support and generosity. The rizq of power carries the responsibility of justice. The rizq of speech carries the responsibility of truthfulness. The rizq of understanding carries the responsibility of trustworthiness.

Rizq and the test of the human being

Rizq is not always only a reward; sometimes it is a test.

Expansion of rizq can reveal the human being. Will he be grateful or transgress? Will he give or hoard? Will he become just or arrogant? Will he see himself as a means or as the absolute owner?

Constriction of rizq can also test the human being. Will he remain patient? Will he preserve his dignity? Will he move toward the unlawful? Will he become suspicious of The God? Will he abandon proper effort?

The Qur’an shows in a very precise verse that the measuring of rizq is connected to The God’s knowledge of human capacity:

وَلَوْ بَسَطَ اللَّهُ الرِّزْقَ لِعِبَادِهِ لَبَغَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَٰكِن يُنَزِّلُ بِقَدَرٍ مَّا يَشَاءُ

If The God were to expand provision for His servants, they would transgress in the earth; but He sends down in measure whatever He wills.

Based on this verse, unrestrained expansion is not always an outward mercy. Sometimes uncontrolled abundance becomes a cause of transgression. Therefore, the measured nature of rizq can itself be a form of protection, nurturing, and mercy.

The Qur’an prevents us from judging rizq superficially. Not every expansion is a sign of superiority, and not every constriction is a sign of downfall. The real criterion is the human being’s response to that condition.

Lawful and wholesome rizq

Rizq in the Qur’an is also connected to purity, lawfulness, and wholesomeness.

Not everything that reaches a person is necessarily pure and wholesome rizq. A person may acquire something through oppression, theft, deception, usury, corruption, exploitation, or humiliation of others. That thing may be in his possession, but it is not wholesome.

Wholesome rizq is that whose path of arrival is not polluted and whose use does not pull the human being into corruption.

So a person’s prayer should not only be for more rizq, but also for the purity of rizq, the blessing of rizq, and the proper use of rizq.

Abundant but impure rizq can destroy the human being. Small but pure rizq can preserve blessing, tranquility, dignity, and the path of reform.

Material and non-material rizq

Rizq can be seen in two broad domains:

Material rizq: food, water, wealth, home, clothing, land, work, economic security, and the means of life.

Non-material rizq: knowledge, faith, tranquility, guidance, opportunity to repent, love, wisdom, patience, ethical strength, righteous companionship, understanding of the Qur’an, and inner opening.

Sometimes non-material rizq is more important than material rizq. A person who has knowledge and guidance can grow toward The God even with a simple life. But a person who has great wealth while deprived of guidance, reason, mercy, and gratitude may fall through that very wealth.

For this reason, the Qur’anic understanding of rizq must save the human being from a superficial view of life.

The relation between rizq and human effort

Rizq does not negate effort.

The Qur’an invites the human being to effort, movement, responsibility, giving, justice, and proper use of means. It is not correct for the human being to justify laziness, lack of planning, or dependence on others in the name of trust in The God.

But human effort must not make him arrogant. Human effort is “earning,” but the arrival of the result is “rizq.” The responsible human being is charged with movement, effort, and earning; but his heart should find rest in the source of provision, The God.

The human being strives, but the result is not always fully in his control. Many strive and reach different results. Many plan, but events beyond their will occur.

So the Qur’anic view establishes balance between effort and trust. The human being must strive, but he must not imagine himself the absolute owner of the result. He must use the means, but he must not treat the means as God. He must seek provision, but he must not sell his dignity and faith in the pursuit of rizq.

The danger of reducing rizq to wealth

If rizq is translated only as wealth, the human understanding of the Qur’an is damaged.

In such a view, the wealthy person is imagined as more successful and more beloved, while the poor person is seen as more deprived and less valuable. This is dangerous because it replaces divine criteria with the criteria of market, power, and class.

The Qur’an calls us to a deeper view. The worth of the human being does not depend on the amount of his possessions, but on taqwā, gratitude, justice, truthfulness, righteous action, and responsibility before The God.

Abundant rizq without gratitude is danger. Limited rizq with taqwā can be a path of salvation. True rizq is what brings the human being nearer to The God, not what makes him more heedless of The God.

Suggested translation in Forqan

For the root “ر ز ق,” translation must be chosen according to the context of each verse. No single translation is always sufficient.

Possible renderings:

رِزْق: provision, gift, share, availability, what is delivered, possibility of life and growth

رَزَقَ: He provided, gave, granted, delivered benefit, made available

يَرْزُقُ: He provides, gives, grants, delivers benefit

رُزِقَ / يُرْزَقُ: was provided / is provided, was granted / is granted a share

رَازِقِين: providers, deliverers of provision

رَزَّاق: constant and abundant Provider, continuous giver of provision, source of abundant rizq

When translating verses, one must examine whether rizq in that verse points more to food, wealth, blessing, divine gift, possibility of life, or guidance and opening.

The translation “provision” is correct in many places, but it is not always enough. Sometimes “gift,” “share,” “availability,” or “what The God has delivered” better conveys the meaning.

Summary

The root “ر ز ق” is one of the important roots of the Qur’an. It makes the human being aware that his life unfolds within an order of gift, need, effort, gratitude, testing, and responsibility.

Rizq is not only money and food. Rizq is every gift that reaches a creature from The God and makes survival, growth, guidance, reform, or responsibility possible.

The God is the true source of rizq. Human beings may be means, but they are not independent owners. This understanding saves the human being from humiliation before creation and from arrogance in times of possession.

Rizq is connected to Lordship, gratitude, giving, justice, purity, effort, trust, and testing. Every rizq carries its own responsibility, and every gift can become either a path of gratitude or a field of ingratitude.

Understanding rizq correctly frees the human being from a materialistic view of life. True rizq is what helps the human being move toward truth, growth, dignity, and return to The God.
