Core Meaning

The root ت ب ر indicates breaking, shattering, tearing apart, and destroying something until its structure, continuity, and function disappear.

Tatbir is not merely suffering loss or being defeated. In this root, the body of something is broken in such a way that it can no longer continue its previous form, order, and function.

The semantic core may be summarized as shattering and disintegrating a coherent body until its structure and possibility of continuation disappear.

Important Forms

تَبَّرَ is a form II verb: he shattered something, disintegrated it, collapsed its structure, or destroyed it completely.

وَكُلًّا تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا

And We shattered the structure of each completely.

Al-Furqan 25:39

Form II can indicate intensity, comprehensiveness, or causation. Here the issue is not limited damage; a body is brought to a condition in which it loses the ability to continue.

يُتَبِّرُوا is the subjunctive imperfect verb of form II:

وَلِيُتَبِّرُوا مَا عَلَوْا تَتْبِيرًا

And so that they may shatter completely whatever they overpowered.

Al-Isra 17:7

The agents in this verse are human beings who, after overcoming and gaining control, destroy what has come under their power. Tatbir in this context has an outward and historical expression.

تَتْبِير is the verbal noun of form II: complete shattering, severe destruction, breaking the body apart, and collapsing the structure.

وَكُلًّا تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا

And each We shattered completely.

Al-Furqan 25:39

The verbal noun after a verb from the same root emphasizes the intensity and completeness of the act.

تَبَار is a noun or verbal noun indicating destruction, perishing, and an end in which structure and the possibility of remaining disappear:

وَلَا تَزِدِ الظَّالِمِينَ إِلَّا تَبَارًا

And increase the wrongdoers in nothing except destruction.

Nuh 71:28

Tabar here does not describe the act of breaking itself; it points more to the final condition and outcome of what is being destroyed.

مُتَبَّر is the passive participle of form II: something shattered, or something condemned to collapse and destruction.

إِنَّ هَٰؤُلَاءِ مُتَبَّرٌ مَا هُمْ فِيهِ وَبَاطِلٌ مَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

Indeed, what these people are in is doomed to be shattered, and what they used to do is false.

Al-A'raf 7:139

Mutabbar here points to a condition, system, or worship-practice that has no real possibility of lasting.

تِبْر in lexical Arabic is used for gold and silver before being minted or fashioned. It is used for material that has not yet taken an ordered form, a recognized mold, or a final structure.

This form, in the sense of raw metal, does not occur in the Qur'an. Its connection with the field of brokenness and formlessness can help clarify the image of the root, but it should not be treated as the definite origin of all Qur'anic uses.

Linguistic Note

The core of ت ب ر is removing the continuity and body of a thing by breaking and disintegrating it.

In tatbir, something may still have material parts, but it no longer exists as the same previous structure. Walls, people, or pieces may remain, but the order, form, and function that made them one body have disappeared.

For this reason, the root contains several nearby layers: breaking, shattering, scattering the parts, collapsing order, removing function, and destroying the possibility of continuation.

Not every break is tatbir. A vessel may crack and still remain a vessel. Tatbir occurs when the break removes its form and function.

This feature allows the root to function both in material destruction and in the collapse of human, religious, and social systems.

Material Image

The material image of ت ب ر is a unified object shattered into pieces that no longer have the previous form or function.

One can imagine an earthen vessel that, after a blow, does not merely crack but disintegrates so much that it is no longer a vessel. Its material remains, but its body has disappeared.

The lexical use of tibr for raw gold and silver creates a similar image. Valuable material exists, but it has not yet entered the form of coin, ornament, or an ordered shape. This image can explain tatbir as returning an ordered body to a scattered and formless condition.

At the social level too, individuals and buildings may remain, while the system that connected them collapses. In that condition, tatbir is not merely the damage of a building; it is the disappearance of the organized form and the power of the structure to continue.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, T B R was used for breaking, shattering, making something into small pieces, and destroying. The expression تَبَّرَهُ تَتْبِيرًا indicates severe breaking and the removal of a thing's form and continuity.

The word tibr was also used for raw or unworked gold and silver. Its connection with the root may lie in formlessness, separation of pieces, or absence of a final mold.

Yet the Qur'anic use of the root is mainly in the field of destruction and structural collapse. The meaning of raw metal should not be imposed directly upon the verses.

Qur'anic Uses

The Qur'anic uses of ت ب ر stand in the field of structural collapse, the shattering of a false order, and the ending of a body's ability to continue. This collapse is sometimes seen through human action in history, and sometimes attributed to The God as the complete and final outcome of a people or system.

A Structure That Is False Within and Mutabbar in Outcome

إِنَّ هَٰؤُلَاءِ مُتَبَّرٌ مَا هُمْ فِيهِ وَبَاطِلٌ مَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

Indeed, what these people are in is doomed to be shattered, and what they used to do is false.

Al-A'raf 7:139

In this verse, mutabbar and batil stand together, but they are not identical. Batil means that their act lacks truth, validity, and a sound foundation. Mutabbar means that the structure built upon that act has no power to remain and will finally break apart.

A system may be false in terms of truth while remaining socially present for a time. Being mutabbar shows that this outward durability is not true permanence.

The verse separates two layers: the epistemic layer, being false; and the structural and historical layer, being doomed to shattering.

Human Tatbir After Domination

وَلِيَدْخُلُوا الْمَسْجِدَ كَمَا دَخَلُوهُ أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍ وَلِيُتَبِّرُوا مَا عَلَوْا تَتْبِيرًا

And so that they may enter the Masjid as they entered it the first time, and shatter completely whatever they overpowered.

Al-Isra 17:7

In this verse, the agents of tatbir are human beings who, after gaining access and domination, destroy existing structures.

The phrase ما علوا can include what they overpowered or brought under their control. The order of the verse creates this image: entry and domination, gaining access to positions and structures, and tatbir of what has come under power.

In this context, tatbir has a more outward, material, and historical expression. Buildings, centers of power, and the existing arrangement are broken after domination.

Yet this verse should not be turned into a general and permanent permission to destroy cities or places of worship. It reports a punitive event in its particular context.

Tatbir Attributed to The God

وَكُلًّا ضَرَبْنَا لَهُ الْأَمْثَالَ وَكُلًّا تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا

And for each We set forth examples, and each We shattered completely.

Al-Furqan 25:39

In this verse, the agent of tatbir is The God. Tatbir comes after explanation, warning, and the presentation of examples:

وَكُلًّا ضَرَبْنَا لَهُ الْأَمْثَالَ

And for each We set forth examples.

Al-Furqan 25:39

So destruction is not presented as an event without prior warning. Before it, there was clarification and warning.

When tatbir is attributed to The God, it does not describe only a limited material attack. It may include the complete collapse of a society, power, order, and its ability to continue.

Still, the difference between divine and human tatbir should not be turned into an absolute rule. Human tatbir may also go beyond buildings and collapse a social structure. Tatbir attributed to The God may also occur through natural, human, or historical causes.

The main difference lies in the agent and the scope of attribution: the human being is the direct and limited agent of a destruction, while tatbir attributed to The God states the complete and decisive outcome of a structure.

Tabar in Noah's Supplication

وَلَا تَزِدِ الظَّالِمِينَ إِلَّا تَبَارًا

And increase the wrongdoers in nothing except destruction.

Nuh 71:28

This statement comes at the end of a long report of Noah's calling, the society's denial, and the persistence of a structure of wrongdoing.

Earlier, Noah says:

إِنَّكَ إِنْ تَذَرْهُمْ يُضِلُّوا عِبَادَكَ وَلَا يَلِدُوا إِلَّا فَاجِرًا كَفَّارًا

If You leave them, they will mislead Your servants and will beget only wicked, covering deniers.

Nuh 71:27

The issue is not merely the death of a few individuals. Noah speaks of a structure that reproduces misguidance and raises the next generation in the same direction.

For this reason, tabar can include, beyond the destruction of individuals, the collapse of an arrangement that continually reproduces wrongdoing and denial.

At the same time, يلدوا should not be turned only into political or social reproduction. The verse also speaks of birth and upbringing. The structural meaning should remain beside the direct meaning, not replace it.

Nor should this supplication be treated as a general permission to ask for the destruction of every opponent. It is spoken at the end of a special situation after a long period of calling.

The Relation Between Tatbir and Uluww

وَلِيُتَبِّرُوا مَا عَلَوْا تَتْبِيرًا

And shatter completely whatever they overpowered.

Al-Isra 17:7

In this structure, علوا relates to what the overpowering force has gained domination over. Uluww here first indicates overcoming and standing above positions and structures.

The root ʿ L W in other verses can also indicate arrogant elevation and tyranny:

إِنَّ فِرْعَوْنَ عَلَا فِي الْأَرْضِ

Indeed, Pharaoh exalted himself in the earth.

Al-Qasas 28:4

There is a meaningful relation between arrogant uluww and the collapse of oppressive structures. A structure that places itself beyond limit and accountability prepares the ground for its own collapse.

Yet in the phrase ما علوا, uluww is not directly the attribute of the destroyed structure. It is the act of the attacking force: what they gained control over. Therefore the phrase should not be translated as if everything built upon arrogant elevation is the direct grammatical object of tatbir in this verse.

This relation can be studied as a wider Qur'anic pattern, but it should not be presented as the direct meaning of ما علوا.

Human Tatbir and Final Tatbir

In Al-Isra 17:7, human beings are the direct agents of destruction:

وَلِيُتَبِّرُوا مَا عَلَوْا تَتْبِيرًا

In Al-Furqan 25:39, tatbir is attributed to The God:

وَكُلًّا تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا

In the first use, a particular outward historical act is highlighted: a force enters, overpowers, and destroys the structures under its control.

In the second use, the complete outcome of a people or system is at issue: after explanation and warning, the whole body of it reaches collapse.

Yet these two levels are not completely separate. Final tatbir may occur through human agents, and material destruction may lead to political and social collapse.

So the distinction can be stated this way: human tatbir is the direct and limited action of agents in history; tatbir attributed to The God is the attribution of the complete outcome and law of consequence to the final reference point.

Similar Uses

Breaking a body and collapsing a system: In material use, a unified body is shattered. In social and religious use, the order and relation that made the parts into one body disappears. In both, the core is the same: the destruction of continuity and structural form.

Tabar and tatbir: Tabar is more the state or outcome of destruction:

إِلَّا تَبَارًا

Except destruction.

Tatbir is the intense act of shattering:

تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا

We shattered completely.

Mutabbar and batil: Mutabbar means doomed to be shattered and destroyed. Batil means lacking truth and validity. Something may be batil yet still socially exist. Being mutabbar states its structural outcome.

Material tatbir and structural tatbir: Destroying buildings and symbols of power is material tatbir. Collapsing order, authority, bonds, and the reproductive power of a system is structural tatbir.

These two can come together, but they are not identical. Destroying a few buildings does not always mean the complete collapse of a system, and the collapse of a system may occur without every building being shattered.

Destruction of individuals and destruction of a reproducing system: In Noah's supplication, the society is one that transmits misguidance to the next generation. Tabar in this context can include the ending of that society's ability to continue and reproduce itself.

Still, this structural meaning should not erase the direct reference of the verses to individuals and generations.

Cracking and complete tatbir: Not every crack, break, or damage is tatbir. Tatbir occurs when the body can no longer preserve its former form and function.

Grammar and Structure

Emphatic verbal noun:

تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا

Al-Furqan 25:39

يُتَبِّرُوا مَا عَلَوْا تَتْبِيرًا

Al-Isra 17:7

The verbal noun after a verb from the same root emphasizes intensity, comprehensiveness, and certainty.

In English this can be reflected as: We shattered completely; they shattered utterly; the structure was made to collapse entirely.

The structure ma ʿalaw:

مَا عَلَوْا

Here ما points to what the attacking force gained mastery over.

Possible equivalents include: whatever they overpowered; what they brought under control; whatever they gained access over.

“Whatever they rose above” may preserve the literal image, but in English it can sound spatially awkward or value-laden. “Overpowered” is clearer.

The structure mutabbar ma hum fihi:

مُتَبَّرٌ مَا هُمْ فِيهِ

Mutabbar appears at the beginning of the sentence, foregrounding the judgment: what they are in, or what they insist upon, is doomed to destruction.

Suitable equivalents include: what they are in is condemned to be shattered; what they insist upon is doomed to collapse; the structure they inhabit will not endure.

The structure la tazid... illa tabara:

وَلَا تَزِدِ الظَّالِمِينَ إِلَّا تَبَارًا

إلا restricts the result of increase: nothing should be increased for them except tabar.

Possible equivalents: increase the wrongdoers in nothing except destruction; increase only the collapse of the wrongdoers; bring their outcome only toward greater destruction.

Nearby Roots

T B R and T B B: These two roots are close in sound and outcome, but their images differ. T B B indicates failure, loss, and the fruitlessness of effort or result. T B R indicates shattering and disintegrating the body and structure itself.

The difference can be pictured this way: in tabab, the line of function and result reaches a dead end. In tatbir, the vessel and body of the structure shatter and collapse. In tabab, the tool may still apparently exist but produce no result. In tatbir, the structure itself loses the ability to remain in its previous form.

T B R and K S R: Kasr indicates breaking, but not every breaking is total destruction. Kasr: something broke. Tatbir: it broke in such a way that its form and function disappeared.

T B R and F T T: Fatt emphasizes crushing and making something into tiny pieces. Tatbir includes fragmentation, but also the complete destruction of the structure and its ability to continue.

T B R and D M R: Damar indicates extensive ruin and severe destruction. Tatbir highlights the shattering and disintegration of the body; damar focuses on broad devastation and elimination.

T B R and H L K: Halak indicates the perishing of a being or community. Tatbir shows more of the manner of collapse: breaking, shattering, and loss of structure.

T B R and B Ṭ L: Batil indicates invalidity and falsehood. Tatbir indicates structural destruction. In Al-A'raf 7:139, batil means wrong in truth and validity; mutabbar means doomed to destruction in structure and duration.

T B R and Q Ṭ ʿ: Qatʿ indicates cutting a bond and ending connection. Tatbir focuses on shattering the whole body. Bonds may be cut in tatbir, but that is only part of the act.

T B R and Ṣ D ʿ: Ṣadʿ indicates splitting and cracking. Tasaḍḍuʿ may be the beginning of collapse, but tatbir goes beyond a crack and indicates the disappearance of structure.

T B R and Ḥ Ṭ M: Ḥaṭm indicates breaking and crushing through pressure or impact. Ḥaṭm preserves the image of blow and crushing more directly; tatbir emphasizes complete destruction of the body and its structural outcome.

T B R and N Q Ḍ: Naqḍ indicates undoing something previously tied or made firm. Tatbir focuses on shattering and destroying that structure. Naqḍ: the bonds were undone. Tatbir: the body broke apart and its function disappeared.

T B R and ʿ L W: Uluww indicates rising, domination, or arrogant elevation. In Al-Isra 17:7, uluww relates to the agents' domination and gaining access to positions; tatbir is the act that occurs after this domination.

In other contexts, arrogant uluww can create oppressive structures. Such structures may eventually be exposed to collapse, but this relation must be understood from the whole set of verses, not only from the phrase ما علوا.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For تَبَّرَ: shattered; broke apart; crushed and disintegrated; collapsed its structure; destroyed completely; tore its body apart.

For تَتْبِير: complete shattering; crushing and disintegration; collapsing the structure; complete destruction; removing the body and function.

For تَبَار: destruction; ruin; collapse; destructive outcome; complete disappearance.

For مُتَبَّر: doomed to be shattered; condemned to destruction; structurally collapsible; destroyed; unstable and disintegrating.

For مُتَبَّرٌ مَا هُمْ فِيهِ: what they are in is doomed to be shattered; what they insist upon is doomed to collapse; the structure they inhabit will not endure; their condition is condemned to collapse.

For وَلِيُتَبِّرُوا مَا عَلَوْا تَتْبِيرًا: and so that they may shatter completely whatever they overpowered; and so that they may collapse the structure of whatever they brought under control; and whatever they gained access over, they crush and disintegrate.

For وَكُلًّا تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا: and We crushed and disintegrated the structure of each completely; and each We shattered utterly; and the body of each We collapsed completely.

For وَلَا تَزِدِ الظَّالِمِينَ إِلَّا تَبَارًا: and increase the wrongdoers in nothing except destruction; and increase only the collapse of the wrongdoers; and for the wrongdoers increase nothing except a destructive outcome.

Summary

The root ت ب ر indicates breaking, shattering, and disintegrating a body until its structure, continuity, and function disappear. Its material image is a unified object turned into scattered pieces, no longer holding its previous form.

In the Qur'an, this root is used for the shattering of false structures, the destruction of what comes under domination after overpowering, the complete ruin of denying communities, and the ending of an arrangement that reproduces wrongdoing and misguidance.

Human tatbir can refer to the direct destruction of buildings and symbols of power. Tatbir attributed to The God states the complete and decisive outcome of a people or structure, even if that outcome occurs through human and historical causes.

T B R is close to T B B, kasr, fatt, damar, halak, batil, qatʿ, ṣadʿ, and ḥaṭm, but it is identical with none of them. In tabab, the benefit and result of effort reach a dead end; in tatbir, the body and vessel of the structure itself are shattered and lose the power to continue.
