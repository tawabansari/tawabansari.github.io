1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ر أ ي moves in the semantic field of seeing, perceiving, and something becoming clear to the viewer. This seeing sometimes happens through the eye and direct observation, and sometimes relates to awareness, knowledge, mental perception, or the formation of a judgment.

In the Qur'an, derivatives of this root are used for seeing a person or a scene, observing a sign, seeing in a dream, receiving something with the fu'ad, showing something to another, an attention-awakening question, the formation of an opinion, and doing something so that it may be seen.

Short meaning:

Seeing or perceiving something that becomes clear to the eye, awareness, or perception of the viewer.

The breadth of this root should not make all its uses translated in one way. Ra'a in one verse may be direct seeing with the eye, in another verse knowing or perceiving, and in a structure like ara'ayta the opening of a reflective and attention-directing question. The exact meaning of each use is determined by syntax and context.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

رَأَىٰ، يَرَىٰ and its inflected forms

This is the simple verb of the root. It may indicate seeing with the eye, seeing in a dream, mental perception, awareness, or judgment. Forms such as رَأَيْتُ, رَأَيْنَا, تَرَىٰ, تَرَوْنَ, يَرَوْنَ, and أَلَمْ تَرَ are built from this verb.

The verb alone does not determine whether the seeing is bodily or mental. This difference is understood from the subject, object, sentence structure, and context.

رَأْي

The noun of this root. In the Qur'an it points to opinion, impression, or judgment. In the expression بَادِيَ الرَّأْيِ, ra'y relates to a first impression or initial judgment.

رِئْي

This form appears in the Qur'an in أَحْسَنُ أَثَاثًا وَرِئْيًا and concerns appearance, sightly aspect, or what seems better in the viewer's eyes. It should not be confused with رَأْي meaning opinion and judgment.

رُؤْيَا

What is seen in a dream. This word appears in the story of Yusuf, the dream of the king, the dream of Ibrahim, and other uses concerning seeing in sleep.

أَرَىٰ، يُرِي and their inflected forms

Form IV of this root. It usually means to show, to make something visible, or to make it possible for someone else to see something, as in أَرِنِي, “show me,” and نُرِي إِبْرَاهِيمَ, “We show Ibrahim.”

Still, Form IV must be examined in its own structure in every verse. One should not build an added theory beyond the text from the verbal pattern alone.

رَاءَىٰ، يُرَاؤُونَ and رِئَاء

This form concerns an act that a person places before the view of others so that people may see him or his deed. In the Qur'an, this derivative appears in contexts condemning spending, prayer, or conduct directed toward gaining people's gaze.

تَرَاءَىٰ

Form VI. It points to two sides coming into one another's field of view, or each group becoming visible to the other.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of R A Y can be described as: seeing or perceiving what has become clear to the viewer. In this definition, the viewer is not always only the bodily eye, and becoming clear is not always limited to external light and image.

In bodily seeing, a person, object, or event enters the field of observation:

فَلَمَّا رَآهَا تَهْتَزُّ كَأَنَّهَا جَانٌّ

So when he saw it moving as though it were a jann...

An-Naml 27:10

In seeing related to dream, the person receives a visual experience in sleep:

إِنِّي أَرَىٰ فِي الْمَنَامِ أَنِّي أَذْبَحُكَ

I see in sleep that I am slaughtering you.

As-Saffat 37:102

In mental or awareness-giving use, seeing is not limited to direct eye-witnessing:

أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِعَادٍ

Did you not see how your Lord dealt with 'Ad?

Al-Fajr 89:6

The addressee did not necessarily see that historical event with his eyes, but its report and sign are presented for his knowledge and perception.

Therefore, becoming clear may result from direct observation, awareness of an event, reception in a dream, or understanding a truth. Still, this core should not be defined through modern scientific terms such as data recording, detector, sensor, or processor. Such terms may be used to create a modern example, but they are not part of the lexical meaning of the root.

5. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, ra'a is used both for seeing with the eye and for knowing, perceiving, and holding an opinion. Lexical sources also distinguish these uses through syntax.

When the verb means seeing with the eye, it usually takes one object, because a person sees something:

رَأَيْتُ الشَّيْءَ

I saw the thing.

When it means knowing, finding, or considering something to be a certain way, it may take two objects, because the person finds someone or something to have a quality or state:

رَأَيْتُهُ صَادِقًا

I considered him truthful.

The noun ra'y also arises from this field. Ra'y is what a person sees or reaches concerning a matter; thus it may refer to opinion, impression, strategy, or judgment.

In lexical sources, seeing with the “eye” and seeing with the “heart” are both reported within the field of this root. This shows that extension from sensory observation to mental perception is not merely a late interpretive invention. Yet the quality of each kind of perception must be understood from the context of the sentence.

A detailed claim about the place of “people of ra'y” in pre-Islamic tribal systems or a direct link to later legal schools is not needed for defining the root, and without independent historical study it should not enter a linguistic article.

6. History of Understanding and Semantic Restriction

One mistake in understanding this root is restricting all its forms to bodily seeing. Such restriction makes Persian translation in structures like أَلَمْ تَرَ or أَرَأَيْتَ sound strange or misleading, because the addressee did not see many of the mentioned events with his own eyes.

Another mistake is that every non-bodily use is immediately called “seeing with the heart” or “inner unveiling.” Indirect seeing may simply refer to awareness, receiving a report, understanding, judgment, or attention to a clear result. From the fact that a use is not eye-based, one cannot by itself infer a supernatural experience.

The issue of “seeing The God” also became a disputed topic in theological traditions. A root article, however, should not impose a specific theological theory on the word itself. The verb ra'a has capacity for bodily seeing and non-bodily perception, but determining the meaning of a verse about The God requires full contextual study and the Qur'anic principles concerning His incomparability.

On the other hand, riya' is sometimes equated only with one specific act of worship, while Qur'anic usage is not limited to prayer. Spending and going out to be seen by people are also expressed with this root. The common axis is placing an act before people's gaze and directing it toward being seen.

7. Methodological Note

Several levels must be kept distinct when studying this root.

Direct Meaning

Seeing, perceiving, or something becoming clear to the viewer.

Type of Perception in Context

Seeing with the eye, seeing in a dream, knowing, becoming aware, forming a judgment, or inner reception.

Interpretive Conclusion

Determining the precise nature of the experience, the thing seen, or the relation between human perception and the unseen.

For example, the phrase مَا كَذَبَ الْفُؤَادُ مَا رَأَىٰ shows that the fu'ad has a crucial role in confirming or receiving what was seen. But the verse by itself does not speak of a non-material sensor, a heavenly frequency, a central processor, or a special physical mechanism. Such expressions are theories outside the text and should not enter the translation or definition of the root.

Likewise, ru'ya in Qur'anic usage is related to seeing in sleep, but from this one cannot conclude that every dream is a direct reception of information from higher layers of existence. The Qur'an speaks of meaningful dreams, but it does not present a general theory about the mechanism of all dreams.

In comparing roots, absolute boundaries should also be avoided. R A Y, N Z R, B S R, and SH H D have distinct fields, but in some contexts their uses may come close to one another.

8. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

Direct Seeing of Persons and Things

One of the clearest uses of this root is direct observation of something before the eyes:

فَلَمَّا رَأَىٰ قَمِيصَهُ قُدَّ مِنْ دُبُرٍ قَالَ إِنَّهُ مِنْ كَيْدِكُنَّ

So when he saw his shirt torn from behind, he said: This is from your scheming.

Yusuf 12:28

In this verse, seeing the shirt and its condition becomes the premise for a judgment. The verb ra'a itself points to observation, while the conclusion appears afterward in the speaker's words. Therefore, “judging” should not be inserted into the base translation of the verb.

Showing Something to Another

The Qur'an says about Ibrahim:

وَكَذَٰلِكَ نُرِي إِبْرَاهِيمَ مَلَكُوتَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَلِيَكُونَ مِنَ الْمُوقِنِينَ

And thus We were showing Ibrahim the dominion of the heavens and the earth, so that he would be among those with certainty.

Al-Anam 6:75

Nuri is Form IV. The God is the subject who shows, and Ibrahim is the receiver. The verse shows that this displaying is connected with Ibrahim reaching certainty, but it does not explain the material or non-material mechanics of that showing.

The same form appears in Musa's request:

رَبِّ أَرِنِي أَنْظُرْ إِلَيْكَ قَالَ لَنْ تَرَانِي

My Lord, show me, that I may look toward You. He said: You will not see Me.

Al-A'raf 7:143

Here, arini is a request for showing, anzur expresses turning attention and looking, and tarani negates the realization of seeing. The pairing of R A Y and N Z R is important, but this verse by itself does not permit attributing body, direction, or created form to The God.

“Alam Tara” and Presenting a Reality for Awareness

The Qur'an often says:

أَلَمْ تَرَ

Its literal translation is “Did you not see?” But this structure is not always about direct eye-witnessing.

أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى الَّذِي حَاجَّ إِبْرَاهِيمَ فِي رَبِّهِ

Did you not see the one who disputed with Ibrahim about his Lord?

Al-Baqarah 2:258

And:

أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِأَصْحَابِ الْفِيلِ

Did you not see how your Lord dealt with the companions of the elephant?

Al-Fil 105:1

In such structures, seeing may occur through awareness of a report, observation of traces, reception of Qur'anic speech, or the result becoming clear. “Did you not process?” is not suitable, because it imports a technical term into the text that is not in the word. According to context, the Qur'anic structure “Did you not see?” can be preserved, and in explanation the meaning “Did you not become aware?” or “Did you not pay attention?” can be clarified.

“Ara'ayta” as a Questioning and Attention-Directing Structure

أَرَأَيْتَ الَّذِي يُكَذِّبُ بِالدِّينِ

Have you seen the one who denies the judgment?

Al-Ma'un 107:1

أَرَأَيْتَ الَّذِي يَنْهَىٰ عَبْدًا إِذَا صَلَّىٰ

Have you seen the one who forbids a servant when he performs salat?

Al-Alaq 96:9-10

In this structure, the question is not always requesting a report of a past observation. The expression directs the addressee toward a person, behavior, or situation and prepares the ground for judgment about it. In structures like ara'aytakum, translations such as “tell me” may convey the speech function, but the link with seeing and perceiving should not be erased completely.

Seeing in a Dream

يَا بُنَيَّ لَا تَقْصُصْ رُؤْيَاكَ عَلَىٰ إِخْوَتِكَ

My son, do not relate your dream-vision to your brothers.

Yusuf 12:5

إِنِّي أَرَىٰ سَبْعَ بَقَرَاتٍ سِمَانٍ

I see seven fat cows.

Yusuf 12:43

Although the simple verb ara is used, the story context makes clear that the subject is seeing in a dream.

يَا بُنَيَّ إِنِّي أَرَىٰ فِي الْمَنَامِ أَنِّي أَذْبَحُكَ

My son, I see in sleep that I am slaughtering you.

As-Saffat 37:102

Here the phrase fi al-manam explicitly specifies the type of seeing. Thus the difference between seeing while awake and seeing in sleep is not always known from the form of the verb alone; sometimes the contextual clue determines it.

Seeing and the Fu'ad

مَا كَذَبَ الْفُؤَادُ مَا رَأَىٰ

The fu'ad did not deny what he saw.

An-Najm 53:11

The structure requires care. The expression can indicate that the fu'ad did not reject or treat as false what had been seen. The verse highlights the role of the fu'ad in confirming and receiving the experience, but it does not explicitly say that the fu'ad acted like an independent eye or by what mechanism it saw.

مَا زَاغَ الْبَصَرُ وَمَا طَغَىٰ

The sight did not swerve and did not transgress.

An-Najm 53:17

The pairing of fu'ad and basar shows that inner reception and seeing with basar are both important in describing this experience. Yet the precise quality of the experience and the object of ma ra'a must be studied from the whole context of the surah and should not be filled with modern technical terms.

Ra'y as Impression or Judgment

وَمَا نَرَاكَ اتَّبَعَكَ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ هُمْ أَرَاذِلُنَا بَادِيَ الرَّأْيِ

And we do not see that any have followed you except those who are the lowest among us, in the first appearance of opinion.

Hud 11:27

The construction badiya al-ra'y requires grammatical and interpretive care. It may refer to an opinion appearing at first glance, or to following without full reflection. A fixed translation such as “shallow and hasty opinion” may import more meaning than the phrase itself. It is better to keep the base translation short and discuss syntactic possibilities separately.

Ri'y as Visible Appearance

كَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا قَبْلَهُمْ مِنْ قَرْنٍ هُمْ أَحْسَنُ أَثَاثًا وَرِئْيًا

How many generations before them We destroyed who were better in furnishings and visible appearance.

Maryam 19:74

Ri'yan in this verse is not about opinion or belief; it points to what is seen, visible appearance, or sightly aspect. Its written similarity with ra'y should not cause the two uses to be mixed.

Riya' and Acting to Be Seen

كَالَّذِي يُنْفِقُ مَالَهُ رِئَاءَ النَّاسِ

Like one who spends his wealth to be seen by people.

Al-Baqarah 2:264

الَّذِينَ هُمْ يُرَاؤُونَ

Those who show themselves to people.

Al-Ma'un 107:6

وَلَا تَكُونُوا كَالَّذِينَ خَرَجُوا مِنْ دِيَارِهِمْ بَطَرًا وَرِئَاءَ النَّاسِ

And do not be like those who came out from their homes arrogantly and to be seen by people.

Al-Anfal 8:47

These verses show that riya' is not limited to prayer or charity. A person directs his act toward the gaze of people so that it appears before them. The Qur'an does not speak here of a short circuit of energy or recording the act in people's database. The ruin of the act in the context of Al-Baqarah is related to lack of faith, harm, reproach, and wrong direction, not to a proven physical law of energy transfer.

Coming into One Another's Field of View

فَلَمَّا تَرَاءَى الْجَمْعَانِ قَالَ أَصْحَابُ مُوسَىٰ إِنَّا لَمُدْرَكُونَ

So when the two groups came into one another's view, the companions of Musa said: We will surely be overtaken.

Ash-Shu'ara 26:61

Tara'a shows that the two groups entered one another's field of view. The meaning of reciprocal seeing is understood from the structure and the relation of the two groups. Expressions such as “sensor opposition” are neither natural translation nor required by the verbal pattern.

9. Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

The Simple Verb رَأَىٰ

The simple verb has the broadest use in this root. It can express direct seeing, seeing in a dream, perceiving a situation, or forming a judgment. In translation, not all cases should be rendered “observe,” and not all should be rendered “perceive.”

رَآهَا تَهْتَزُّ

He saw it moving.

أَرَأَيْتَ مَنِ اتَّخَذَ إِلَٰهَهُ هَوَاهُ

Have you seen the one who took his desire as his god?

In the second example, the sentence calls the addressee to recognize and examine a type of human being, not merely to report an eye-witnessed scene.

رُؤْيَا

Ru'ya in the Qur'an is specific to what is seen in sleep. Yet the verb ra'a itself can also be used in a dream context. Therefore, the boundary between sleep and waking should not be determined only by the presence or absence of the noun ru'ya.

The dreams of Yusuf, the king, and Ibrahim have meaning and consequences in the Qur'anic narrative, but from these examples one cannot issue a general ruling about the origin and nature of all human dreams.

رَأْي

Ra'y points to a view or impression a person has about a matter. This meaning is not far from the field of seeing, because ra'y is what a person “sees” in a subject. In English, “opinion,” “view,” “impression,” or “judgment” may be the more natural translation according to context.

رِئْي

Ri'y in Maryam 19:74 concerns visible appearance and sightly aspect. This form must be kept separate from ra'y in the cards and article, because the first concerns what comes before the eye and the second concerns opinion and judgment.

أَرَىٰ

Form IV usually creates a relation between the one who shows, the thing shown, and the one who sees it. Sometimes a pronoun carries one of these elements, as in أَرِنِي, “show me,” or:

لِنُرِيَكَ مِنْ آيَاتِنَا الْكُبْرَىٰ

So that We may show you some of Our greatest signs.

Ta-Ha 20:23

Form IV has the capacity of “showing,” but context determines the quality, means, and type of this showing.

رِئَاء and يُرَاؤُونَ

In this form, a person does an act in such a way that it is seen. Its negative force comes from the Qur'anic contexts and from the direction of the act toward people's gaze.

One cannot extract all ethical elements from Form III alone. This form by itself does not prove that two sides are always equally participating in an action. The condemned meaning of riya' is understood from its actual uses in the verses.

تَرَاءَىٰ

This form is used in the Qur'an for two groups becoming visible to one another. Form VI in this context carries a kind of reciprocal relation between the two sides, but one should not produce more meaning from the verbal pattern than it can carry.

10. Grammatical and Structural Notes

One Object and Two Objects

When ra'a means seeing with the eye, it usually takes one object:

رَأَيْتُهُ

I saw him.

When it means knowing, finding, or considering, it may be counted among verbs of the heart and take two objects:

رَأَيْتُهُ صَادِقًا

I considered him truthful.

This rule is useful in identifying meaning, but it must not mechanically replace context. Sometimes the sentence structure is more complex, or the objects come as clauses and pronouns.

Ra'a with Ila

In structures like أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى الَّذِي حَاجَّ إِبْرَاهِيمَ, the particle ila directs attention toward a person or subject. A literal rendering “Did you not see toward the one...” is not natural English, so one may say “Have you not considered the one...” or preserve the simpler “Did you not see the one...” according to context.

Ara'ayta and Ara'aytakum

These structures in the Qur'an often serve to draw attention, pose a case, request judgment, or make the addressee think:

قُلْ أَرَأَيْتُمْ إِنْ أَتَاكُمْ عَذَابُ اللَّهِ

Say: Have you considered, if the punishment of The God comes to you...

Al-Anam 6:40

“Tell me” can convey the conversational function, but it is not always a complete word-for-word equivalent. Translation should preserve both the attention-directing function and natural English.

The Verb Ra'a in Dream Context

The simple verb can be used for sleep without a special verbal form. Clues such as fi al-manam, the word ru'ya, or the story setting specify that the seeing is in a dream.

Thus the claim that “ru'yah is always waking vision and ru'ya is always dream” is only useful for distinguishing the two nouns, not for saying that every verb ra'a without the noun ru'ya refers to waking sight.

The Relation of Fu'ad and Ma Ra'a

In مَا كَذَبَ الْفُؤَادُ مَا رَأَىٰ, the hidden subject of ra'a must be studied in relation to the context. The translation “The fu'ad did not deny what he saw” preserves the Arabic structure more closely, but one should not immediately conclude from it that the fu'ad is grammatically the subject of ra'a. The fu'ad is the subject of kadhaba, and ma ra'a is the object of confirmation or denial.

This grammatical distinction matters because it prevents an interpretive reading from being presented as the certain grammar of the verse.

11. R A Y Compared with N Z R

The root N Z R often relates to looking, paying attention, waiting, or turning one's gaze toward something. The root R A Y more strongly indicates the realization of seeing or perception.

This difference is clear in some verses:

وَتَرَاهُمْ يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَيْكَ وَهُمْ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ

And you see them looking toward you, while they do not perceive.

Al-A'raf 7:198

Here yanzuruna ilayka expresses directing the gaze toward the addressee, while la yubsirun negates effective seeing or discernment. The verse shows that directing the gaze does not necessarily lead to true seeing and perception.

Still, the difference between the two roots should not be turned into a theory about aligning sensors and photon contact. The Qur'an speaks about the direction of looking and the realization or failure of perception, not about the details of light physics.

Also, it cannot be said that nazar is always only a prelude to ra'y. This root has other Qur'anic meanings such as waiting and granting respite. The comparison must remain limited to contexts of seeing.

12. R A Y Compared with B S R

The root B S R is connected with seeing, eyesight, discernment, and insight. Basar can refer to the power of sight, the eye itself, or the act of seeing; basir is one who sees or is aware of something.

By contrast, ra'a often expresses the occurrence of a particular seeing or perception:

رَأَيْتُهُ

I saw him.

فَكَشَفْنَا عَنْكَ غِطَاءَكَ فَبَصَرُكَ الْيَوْمَ حَدِيدٌ

So We removed your cover from you, and your sight today is sharp.

Qaf 50:22

In this verse, basar relates to the state and power of sight.

Even so, the boundary should not be made absolute. Verbs and nouns from B S R can also indicate realized seeing and knowledge. The difference is tendential and contextual: ra'a more often highlights the event of seeing or perception, while basar is more closely connected with sight, the power of discernment, and clear-sightedness.

13. R A Y Compared with SH H D

The root SH H D is connected with being present, being a witness, being aware, and giving testimony. Seeing can be one path to testimony, but not every seeing is testimony, and not every testimony necessarily rests only on eye-witnessing.

Ra'a emphasizes the seeing or perception itself:

رَأَيْتُهُ

I saw him.

But shahida can indicate presence and the position of witness:

وَكُنَّا لِحُكْمِهِمْ شَاهِدِينَ

And We were witnesses to their judgment.

Al-Anbiya 21:78

Therefore, translating ra'a as “being witness” may insert more than the text says in some contexts. Conversely, translating shahida only as “seeing” can erase the dimension of presence, awareness, or testimony.

14. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

رَأَىٰ in direct seeing

Base translations: saw, sees, observed. “Saw” is usually the shortest and most natural translation. “Observed” is more formal and should be used only in suitable contexts.

رَأَىٰ in mental perception or judgment

Depending on structure and context: saw, perceived, knew, found, considered. “Received and recorded” should not be chosen as a fixed translation, because “recording” is not in the root itself.

أَلَمْ تَرَ

Suggested bases: Did you not see? Did you not pay attention? Did you not become aware?

The structure “Did you not see?” should be preserved as much as possible, because it keeps the image of seeing and becoming clear. Where English would misunderstand it as bodily seeing, a contextual translation or short explanation may remove ambiguity. “Did you not process?” is not suitable.

أَرَأَيْتَ and أَرَأَيْتُمْ

Depending on context: Have you seen? Have you considered? Have you brought to mind? Tell me, if...?

The translation should reflect the interrogative and attention-directing function of the sentence.

أَرَىٰ / يُرِي

Showed. Shows. Made visible. Brought into view. “Show” is natural and sufficient in most verses.

رُؤْيَا

Dream-vision. What was seen in sleep. Dream. “Ru'ya” is a known term, but explanation should clarify that seeing in sleep is meant.

رَأْي

Opinion. View. Perspective. Impression. Judgment. The choice depends on context.

رِئْي

Visible appearance. Sightly aspect. Splendor. What appears to the eye. This form should not be translated as “opinion” or “belief.”

رِئَاء

To be seen by people. Showing off before people. Riya'. “Riya'” is common, but it may hide the root sense of being seen. In base translation or explanation, “to be seen by people” is clearer.

تَرَاءَىٰ

They saw one another. They came into one another's view. They became visible to one another. “Opposition of sensors” should be discarded.

15. Conclusion

The root R A Y in the Qur'an moves around seeing and perceiving. Something becomes clear to the viewer and enters the field of observation, awareness, or judgment. This becoming clear may happen through the eye, report, reflection, dream, or inner experience.

Final proposed definition:

R A Y: seeing or perceiving something that becomes clear to the eye, awareness, or perception of the viewer.

The simple verb ra'a is not limited to bodily seeing, but not every non-bodily use is inner unveiling or supernatural experience. The type of seeing is determined by context, subject, object, and syntax.

Ru'ya concerns what is seen in sleep, while the verb ra'a itself is used both in sleep and waking. Ra'y points to opinion and impression, while ri'y concerns visible appearance. Mixing these two forms erases an important difference.

Ara usually relates to showing. Riya' and yura'un describe an act directed toward people's gaze. Tara'a shows two sides entering one another's field of view.

Compared with N Z R, R A Y more strongly highlights realized seeing or perception, while N Z R may relate to looking and directing attention. Compared with B S R, ra'a more often shows the event of seeing, while basar is more closely connected with sight, the power of discernment, and clear-sightedness. SH H D also carries the dimension of presence and testimony in addition to awareness.

The verse مَا كَذَبَ الْفُؤَادُ مَا رَأَىٰ highlights the role of the fu'ad in confirming the experience, but it does not explain a physical or non-material mechanism of perception. For this reason, terms such as photon, sensor, frequency, central processor, or information engineering should not enter the lexical definition or base translation of this root.

This root in the Qur'an gives a way to speak about observation, awareness, dream-vision, judgment, showing, visible appearance, and being seen before others. In every case, the structure of the verse itself must be allowed to specify the type of seeing and the boundaries of meaning.
