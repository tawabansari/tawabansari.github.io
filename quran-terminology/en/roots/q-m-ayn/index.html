Qur’anic Root Q M ʿ (ق م ع)

Restraint by force, subduing, and suppression; maces and implements of striking and control

Qur’anic forms

مَقَامِع

Maqāmiʿ

Maces; sole occurrence at Al-Ḥajj 22:21

1. Basic meaning and linguistic background

Three branches should be distinguished in the family ق م ع (Q M ʿ): restraint and subjugation by force; funnels and certain parts of fruit; and troublesome insects and animals’ behavior in driving them away. The Qur’anic usage belongs to the first branch. Grouping usages under one root in a dictionary does not prove a single historical origin for them all.

In classical Arabic, مِقْمَعَة (miqmaʿah) denotes an implement used for striking, restraining, and forcing submission.

Sufficient independent, datable evidence is not available here to establish this branch’s precise pre-Islamic usage. Its Qur’anic occurrence in the plural nevertheless attests the instrument’s name in the language of revelation.

Other branches concern a funnel, the part connecting certain fruits to their stalk, and a kind of troublesome insect. The last branch is also recorded in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period.

Qur’anic form examined: مَقَامِع (maqāmiʿ), maces and implements of striking and restraint. It occurs once, in Al-Ḥajj 22:21.

Non-Qur’anic forms include قَمَعَ (qamaʿa), suppressed or restrained; أَقْمَعَ (aqmaʿa), drove away or subdued; اِنْقَمَعَ (inqamaʿa), withdrew or was subdued; مِقْمَعَة (miqmaʿah), a striking implement; and قِمْع (qimʿ), a funnel. Only maqāmiʿ, the plural of miqmaʿah, occurs in the Qur’an.

2. Material imagery and early Arabic usages

Qamʿ and miqmaʿah

Classical Arabic uses قَمَعَهُ (qamaʿahu) both for striking someone with a miqmaʿah and for subduing or humiliating a person.

Lexicons describe the miqmaʿah as a mace, a rod, or an implement with a curved head used to control elephants; some usages also allow a wooden implement. Iron is therefore not inherent in its meaning: the material is determined by description or context.

Qamʿ is not limited to bodily blows. In nonphysical applications, it expresses stopping another through power, subduing them, or forcing retreat. أَقْمَعْتُ الرَّجُلَ عَنِّي (aqmaʿtu r-rajula ʿannī) means that I drove the man away from me.

Modern Arabic retains this semantic range. Suppressing protests, forcibly preventing people from expressing their views, and putting down opposition are familiar uses.

The funnel and other material usages

قِمْع (qimʿ) and قِمَع (qimaʿ) name a funnel, an implement for pouring liquid into a narrow-mouthed vessel. The cap-like part attached to the end of a date or certain other fruits is also known by this name.

Some lexicographers connect the funnel with something descending into it. This usage does not establish that the sense of suppression and subjugation originated in the image of a funnel.

Insects and the behavior of animals

قَمَع (qamaʿ) can also name a large, troublesome kind of fly that annoys animals in hot weather.

A verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Aws ibn Ḥajar states:

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ أَرْسَلَ مُزْنَةً وَعُفْرُ الظِّبَاءِ فِي الْكِنَاسِ تَقَمَّعُ

“Have you not seen that The God sent a rain-bearing cloud, while the grayish gazelles in their shelters shake their heads?”

The first half of this verse is transmitted with readings including أَرْسَلَ (arsala), sent, and أَنْزَلَ (anzala), sent down. تَقَمَّعُ (taqammaʿu) expresses shaking the head to drive insects away. This witness attributed to Aws concerns insects and animal behavior, not direct evidence for the striking implement miqmaʿah.

Ibn Fāris likewise distinguishes three principal branches: the funnel and liquid descending into it; subduing and humiliating; and a kind of insect. Historical evidence does not suffice to derive all these branches from a single original meaning.

3. The Qur’anic use of maqāmiʿ

The Qur’an uses Q M ʿ in Sūrat al-Ḥajj to describe punishment in the Hereafter.

Al-Ḥajj 22:19 speaks of garments of fire and scalding water poured over their heads. The passage then states:

وَلَهُم مَقٰمِعُ مِن حَديدٍ

Wa-lahum maqāmiʿu min ḥadīd.

“And for them are maces of iron.”

Al-Hajj 22:21

مَقَامِع (maqāmiʿ) is the broken plural of مِقْمَعَة (miqmaʿah), a striking and restraining implement, following the pattern mafāʿil.

Syntactically, لَهُمْ (lahum) is the preposed predicate and مَقَامِعُ (maqāmiʿu) the postponed subject. مِنْ حَدِيدٍ (min ḥadīdin) specifies the material of the implements.

The next verse states:

كُلَّما أَرادوا أَن يَخرُجوا مِنها مِن غَمٍّ أُعيدوا فيها وَذوقوا عَذابَ الحَريقِ

Kullamā arādū an yakhrujū minhā min ghammin uʿīdū fīhā wa-dhūqū ʿadhāba l-ḥarīq.

“Whenever they seek to come out of it, out of anguish, they are returned to it; and [they are told:] Taste the punishment of burning.”

Al-Hajj 22:22

Seeking to leave and being returned in the following verse accords with the restraint expressed by this lexical branch. The sentence does not explicitly say that these maces perform the return. At its end, the third-person report shifts to the direct command “Taste.” The bracketed phrase in the translation makes the speech connection explicit; it has no separate verbal counterpart in the Arabic text.

These verses do not specify the maces’ exact shape or number, how they are used, or who directly delivers the blows. Familiarity with comparable earthly implements helps readers understand the image but does not determine the nature of events in the Hereafter.

4. Comparison with related roots

Q M ʿ (qamʿ) and Q H R (qahr): suppression and dominance

Qahr denotes dominance and mastery and overlaps with qamʿ. In the branch examined here, qamʿ particularly foregrounds restraint and subduing through force. This distinction is not an absolute boundary between the words.

Concerning treatment of an orphan, the Qur’an states:

فَأَمَّا اليَتيمَ فَلا تَقهَر

Fa-ammā l-yatīma fa-lā taqhar.

“As for the orphan, do not oppress him.”

Ad-Duha 93:9

Oppressing an orphan may involve exploiting vulnerability, violating rights, or imposing another’s will, without necessarily inflicting bodily blows.

Qamʿ is also not confined to physical blows, but miqmaʿah specifically names an instrument for applying force and restraint.

Q M ʿ (miqmaʿah) and GH L L (ghull): striking implement and restraining bond

A ghull is a restraining bond, especially for the neck; a miqmaʿah is an implement of striking and control. Both may limit human freedom, but the instruments and methods differ.

Ghāfir 40:71 mentions neck shackles and chains in its description of punishment, whereas Al-Ḥajj 22:21 names iron maces.

Translating maqāmiʿ as “chains” or “iron bonds” erases the distinction between these implements.

Q M ʿ (qamʿ) and Ḍ R B (ḍarb): restraint and striking

Ḍarb has a wide semantic range, one use of which is striking. In the qamʿ branch under discussion, subduing and restraining are prominent. Not every blow, such as striking iron to shape it, constitutes suppression.

5. Translation recommendations for Forqan

“Iron maces” is recommended for the maqāmiʿ of Al-Ḥajj 22:21: “maces” preserves the image of the implement, while “iron” renders the material explicitly named in the verse.

“Iron clubs” and “iron rods for striking and restraint” are also possible equivalents, although the Persian equivalent of “iron maces” is more natural in this prose context.

“Whips” may correspond to certain lexical descriptions, but in Persian the term generally evokes a flexible leather or cord implement, making it less suitable for conveying a hard iron implement here.

“Implements of suppression” conveys the function but weakens the verse’s material imagery.

For non-Qur’anic uses of the family, suitable equivalents include suppressing, restraining, subduing, driving away, and humiliating, according to form and context.

6. Conclusion

In this family’s Qur’anic use, maqāmiʿ are instruments of striking and restraint. Their material comes from the description “iron,” and their connection with confinement from the context. The branches concerning funnels, fruit parts, and insects require separate consideration.

Proposed definition for Forqan: Restraining, suppressing, and subduing by force; in the noun مِقْمَعَة (miqmaʿah) and its plural مَقَامِع (maqāmiʿ), a mace or implement of striking and restraint.
