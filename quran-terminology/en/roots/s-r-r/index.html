1. Introduction to the Root

The root “S R R” is one of the multi-branched roots of Arabic. Its most important meanings include:

  • being covered and hidden;
  • a secret and private matter;
  • the innermost and purest part of something;
  • joy and satisfaction;
  • ease, spaciousness, and a desirable condition;
  • a place of settlement, sitting, or resting;
  • the middle part of the body and the navel;
  • the final part of the month, when the crescent becomes hidden.

Three main branches of this root appear in the Qur'an:

  1. secret, hiding, and private speech;
  2. joy, gladdening, and ease;
  3. surur, meaning couches, thrones, and prepared places for sitting or reclining.

In early root analysis, two concepts are prominent for explaining this family:

something being hidden; and being located in the inside, center, or place of settlement of something.

“Sirr” is something kept away from public sight or awareness. “Sarirah” is a state, thought, or action placed inside a person. “Sarir” is a place upon which a person settles and rests. “Surur” is also a pleasant state that settles inside a person.

These connections are understandable at the level of imagery; but the historical relation among all branches is not equally certain. In particular, the branch of “secret,” the branch of “joy,” and the branch of “couch or place of sitting” should be examined separately in Qur'anic usage.

2. Condensed Meaning of the Root

The meaning of this root in the Qur'an cannot be limited to one English word; but its Qur'anic branches can be summarized as follows:

“S R R” either indicates something that remains inside and has not been made public, or a pleasant state that arises within the human being, or a place upon which a person settles for ease and rest.

The vowel patterns of the words are important for identifying these branches:

  • sirr: secret and hidden matter;
  • surur: joy;
  • surur: couches and places of sitting;
  • sara'ir: inner and hidden matters;
  • sarra': ease and spaciousness;
  • masrur: joyful.

The root “S R R” appears forty-four times in the Qur'an:

  • “tasurru” once;
  • forms of the verb “asarra” eighteen times;
  • “sirr” eleven times;
  • “sara'ir” once;
  • “sarra'” twice;
  • “surur” six times;
  • “surur” once;
  • “masrur” twice;

In meaning, these forms fall into three main fields:

The Field of Hiddenness and Secret

  • asarra;
  • sirr;
  • sara'ir;
  • israr.

The Field of Joy and Ease

  • sarra;
  • surur;
  • masrur;
  • sarra'.

The Field of Sitting Place and Settlement

  • surur, plural of “sarir.”

4. The Material and Historical Image of the Root

4.1. The Covered Interior

The clearest image of the root is something located inside and not visible to others. A statement may remain in the mind or between two people; a decision may be hidden in the self; and an action may be performed away from the view of others.

From this image, the following words are formed:

  • sirr: a hidden matter;
  • sarirah: an inward state or action;
  • asarra: he hid;
  • israr: hiding or speaking privately;
  • musarrah: secret-speaking between two people.

4.2. The Purest and Most Central Part

In early Arabic, “sirr” and “sararah” were also used for the pure, selected, or central part of something. “Sararatu al-wadi” was the best or middle part of a valley. “Surrah” is also the navel, the middle part of the belly.

In this branch, the main concept is “something located in the center or inside.”

“Asarir” was used for the lines of the palm or the folds of the forehead. This usage is probably connected with lines and folds around a central part of the body or surface of the skin.

4.3. The Hiding of the Moon

“Sirar ash-shahr” or “sarar ash-shahr” referred to the final nights of the month, when the crescent becomes hidden in the brightness of the sun and is not seen.

In this usage, the root directly indicates being covered and moving out of sight.

4.4. The Navel and Umbilical Cord

“Surrah” is the part of the body where the trace of the umbilical cord remains. According to early lexical reports, the severed part of the umbilical cord was also named with forms from this same root.

This usage is connected with the center of the body and inward connection, not with the meaning of secret or joy.

4.5. Place of Settlement

“Sarir” is a place upon which a person sits, reclines, or rests. Its plural in the Qur'an is “surur.”

In root analysis, “sarir” has been connected with the idea of settlement and resting: a place where the body is placed and remains in ease.

5. “Sirr”: Secret, Hidden Matter, and Non-Public Action

“Sirr” appears eleven times in the Qur'an and has two major usages:

5.1. Hidden Matter or Content

In this usage, “sirr” is something a person keeps inside or hides from others:

وَهُوَ اللَّهُ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَفِي الْأَرْضِ يَعْلَمُ سِرَّكُمْ وَجَهْرَكُمْ

And He is The God in the heavens and on the earth; He knows your hidden and your open.

Al-An'am 6:3

“Sirrakum” stands opposite “jahrakum.” This contrast shows that “sirr” is not only one specific secret; it includes everything located in the inward and non-public domain of the human being.

The same usage appears in other verses:

يَعْلَمُ سِرَّهُمْ وَنَجْوَاهُمْ

He knows their secret and their private conversation.

At-Tawbah 9:78

and:

أَمْ يَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّا لَا نَسْمَعُ سِرَّهُمْ وَنَجْوَاهُمْ

Or do they think that We do not hear their secret and their private conversation?

Az-Zukhruf 43:80

In these verses, “sirr” and “najwa” are not identical. “Sirr” may be a thought, intention, decision, or hidden content; while “najwa” is usually whispered and private conversation among people.

5.2. Doing an Action Non-Publicly

“Sirrًا” sometimes expresses the manner of an action; that is, something is done away from public announcement:

الَّذِينَ يُنْفِقُونَ أَمْوَالَهُمْ بِاللَّيْلِ وَالنَّهَارِ سِرًّا وَعَلَانِيَةً

Those who spend their wealth night and day, privately and openly.

Al-Baqarah 2:274

The same contrast is repeated in several other verses:

  • Ar-Ra'd 13:22;
  • Ibrahim 14:31;
  • An-Nahl 16:75;
  • Fatir 35:29.

In this usage, “sirrًا” means “privately and non-publicly,” and it stands opposite “alaniyah,” “jahr,” or public announcement.

6. “La Tuwa'iduhunna Sirran”

In Al-Baqarah 2:235 it says:

وَلَٰكِنْ لَا تُوَاعِدُوهُنَّ سِرًّا إِلَّا أَنْ تَقُولُوا قَوْلًا مَعْرُوفًا

But do not make a hidden promise with them, unless you speak a recognized and proper word.

Al-Baqarah 2:235

“Sirrًا” in this structure may have two close roles:

  • a hidden promise or agreement;
  • making a promise in a hidden way, outside the recognized frame.

In early usages, “sirr” was sometimes also used for private and marital relations, because such a relation takes place in private space. However, the structure of the verse itself rests on the contrast between “a hidden promise” and “recognized speech.”

Therefore, the base meaning of the expression is this:

During a time when a formal decision is not yet possible, do not establish a binding and hidden agreement with them; speech must remain within the bounds of recognized and proper expression.

“Sirrًا” here does not merely refer to lowering the voice; rather, it concerns the private and hidden nature of the promise or agreement itself.

7. “Asarra”: Hiding and Making Private

The verb “asarra” appears eighteen times in the Qur'an. Its base meaning is this:

he removed something from the domain of the open and kept it in the domain of the hidden.

Depending on its object and preposition, this verb forms several semantic structures.

7.1. “Asarra” Opposite “A'lana” and “Jahara”

The clearest usage occurs when “asarra” directly stands opposite announcement or open speech:

أَوَلَا يَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا يُسِرُّونَ وَمَا يُعْلِنُونَ

Do they not know that The God knows what they hide and what they make public?

Al-Baqarah 2:77

This contrast also appears in the following verses:

  • An-Nahl 16:19;
  • An-Nahl 16:23;
  • Ya-Sin 36:76;
  • At-Taghabun 64:4.

In Ar-Ra'd 13:10, “asarra al-qawl” stands opposite “jahara bihi”:

سَوَاءٌ مِنْكُمْ مَنْ أَسَرَّ الْقَوْلَ وَمَنْ جَهَرَ بِهِ

Equal among you is whoever hides speech and whoever says it openly.

Ar-Ra'd 13:10

In Al-Mulk 67:13, the same structure appears as an imperative:

وَأَسِرُّوا قَوْلَكُمْ أَوِ اجْهَرُوا بِهِ

Hide your speech or speak it openly.

Al-Mulk 67:13

In these cases, “asarra” has a clear meaning: keeping speech away from public hearing.

7.2. “Asarra Fi Nafsihi”

In the story of Yusuf it says:

فَأَسَرَّهَا يُوسُفُ فِي نَفْسِهِ وَلَمْ يُبْدِهَا لَهُمْ

Yusuf kept it inside himself and did not disclose it to them.

Yusuf 12:77

The expression “fi nafsihi” specifies the place where the speech or reaction is kept. The next clause, “wa lam yubdiha lahum,” makes the meaning of the verb fully clear:

  • “asarraha”: he kept it inside;
  • “lam yubdiha”: he did not disclose it.

This verse is one of the clearest Qur'anic witnesses for the meaning of hiding in the verb “asarra.”

7.3. “Asarra Ila”

When “asarra” comes with “ila,” its meaning is not only hiding; rather, it means saying something to a person in a private setting:

وَإِذْ أَسَرَّ النَّبِيُّ إِلَىٰ بَعْضِ أَزْوَاجِهِ حَدِيثًا

And when the Prophet privately entrusted a statement to one of his wives.

At-Tahrim 66:3

In this structure, the statement is not hidden from the speaker and is not hidden from the addressee; rather, it is kept covered from others.

So:

أَسَرَّ الشَّيْءَ
he hid the thing.

But:

أَسَرَّ إِلَى فُلَانٍ حَدِيثًا
he privately entrusted a statement to so-and-so.

In Al-Mumtahanah 60:1 it also says:

تُسِرُّونَ إِلَيْهِمْ بِالْمَوَدَّةِ

you secretly convey affection toward them.

Al-Mumtahanah 60:1

“Ilayhim” specifies the direction of the relation, and “bil-mawaddah” shows its ground or motive. Here, hiddenness concerns the relation or transfer of information and affection.

7.4. “Asarru An-Najwa”

In Ta-Ha 20:62 and Al-Anbiya' 21:3 it says:

وَأَسَرُّوا النَّجْوَى They kept their private conversation hidden. Ta-Ha 20:62; Al-Anbiya' 21:3

“Najwa” itself indicates private conversation; “asarru” makes the manner of doing it more emphatic. They did not merely converse among themselves; they tried to keep the content of that conversation covered from others.

So “asarru an-najwa” is not meaningless repetition:

  • “najwa” specifies the type of conversation;
  • “asarru” expresses the will to keep it hidden.

7.5. “Asarruhu Bida'ah”

In the story of Yusuf it says:

وَأَسَرُّوهُ بِضَاعَةً

And they hid him as merchandise.

Yusuf 12:19

The caravan people found Yusuf, but they did not disclose his situation and placed him among trade goods. “Bida'ah” expresses the state or position they assigned to him: they kept and hid him as if he were merchandise.

The meaning of the verb here is not private speech, but hiding a person and the truth of his situation.

8. “Asarru An-Nadamah”: Did They Hide It or Show It?

The exact phrase “asarru an-nadamah” appears in two verses:

  • Yunus 10:54;
  • Saba' 34:33.

In Yunus it says:

وَأَسَرُّوا النَّدَامَةَ لَمَّا رَأَوُا الْعَذَابَ

And they kept regret inside when they saw the punishment.

Yunus 10:54

In Al-Ma'idah 5:52, a related but grammatically different structure appears:

فَيُصْبِحُوا عَلَىٰ مَا أَسَرُّوا فِي أَنْفُسِهِمْ نَادِمِينَ

So they become regretful over what they had hidden inside themselves.

Al-Ma'idah 5:52

In this verse, the object of “asarru” is not the word “an-nadamah,” but “ma”: what they had hidden inside themselves. “Nadimin” expresses their later state. Therefore, Al-Ma'idah 5:52 is not another witness for the exact structure “asarru an-nadamah”; its relation to the other two verses is that what was hidden in the self finally leads to regret.

In early lexicons, there is a report about the verb “asarra” that treats it as one of the words with two opposite meanings:

  • he hid;
  • he revealed.

On this basis, some understood “asarru an-nadamah” as “they revealed regret.”

But this analysis was also opposed from the early period. Against it, it was said that the evidence brought for the meaning “to reveal” either had another form or had been read incorrectly. In addition, almost all clear Qur'anic usages of “asarra” mean hiding or making private.

As a result, the meaning more consistent with the Qur'anic network of the root is this:

They kept regret inside themselves and could not or would not make it visible.

“Israr an-nadamah” may express swallowing, hiding, or becoming inwardly trapped by regret. Even if the effects of regret are seen on their faces, the verb itself indicates keeping it inside.

9. “Israr”: Hiding or Speaking Privately

“Israr” is the verbal noun of “asarra” and appears twice in the Qur'an.

In Nuh 71:9 it says:

ثُمَّ إِنِّي أَعْلَنْتُ لَهُمْ وَأَسْرَرْتُ لَهُمْ إِسْرَارًا

Then I announced to them publicly, and I spoke to them privately in private speech.

Nuh 71:9

“Israran” is an emphatic verbal noun and highlights the private mode of invitation. The contrast in the verse is between two methods of communication:

  • “a'lantu lahum”: public and open address;
  • “asrartu lahum israran”: private speech away from the gathering.

In Muhammad 47:26 it says:

وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ إِسْرَارَهُمْ

And The God knows their hidden conduct or their secrets.

Muhammad 47:26

“Israrahum” may indicate both the act of hiding and their hidden content. The context concerns a statement that a group secretly shared with opponents of revelation; therefore, both their hidden behavior and the content of that relation are present in the word.

10. “Sara'ir”: Inner and Hidden Matters

“Sara'ir” is the plural of “sarirah” and appears once in the Qur'an:

يَوْمَ تُبْلَى السَّرَائِرُ

The day when hidden inner matters are tested and made manifest.

At-Tariq 86:9

“Sarirah” is not only a spoken secret. This word may include:

  • intention;
  • motive;
  • inward belief;
  • hidden decision;
  • an action done away from the gaze of others;
  • a truth that a person’s outward appearance has covered.

The verb “tubla” is from the root “B L W” and indicates testing, examining, and the real quality of something becoming manifest. Therefore, the verse does not merely say that secrets are announced; rather, it says inner matters are tested and their truth becomes visible.

The difference between “sirr” and “sarirah” can be expressed this way:

  • “sirr” is the hidden matter itself;
  • “sarirah” is the state, intention, trait, or action hidden inside a person.

11. “Tasurru”: Gladdening

The simple verb “sarra” appears in the Qur'an only once, in the form “tasurru”:

بَقَرَةٌ صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَوْنُهَا تَسُرُّ النَّاظِرِينَ

a yellow cow whose color is pure and striking and gladdens those who look.

Al-Baqarah 2:69

“Tasurru” is a transitive verb:

سَرَّهُ الشَّيْءُ
that thing gladdened him.

The subject of “tasurru” is the cow or its visual description, and “an-nazirin” are those who become pleased from seeing it.

This verb does not merely mean superficial liking; rather, it indicates the arising of a state of satisfaction and joy in the viewer. In the context of the verse, the cause of this joy is the vividness of the color and the visual harmony of the animal.

12. “Surur”: The State of Joy

“Surur” appears once in the Qur'an:

فَوَقَاهُمُ اللَّهُ شَرَّ ذَٰلِكَ الْيَوْمِ وَلَقَّاهُمْ نَضْرَةً وَسُرُورًا

So The God protected them from the evil of that day and met them with freshness of face and joy.

Al-Insan 76:11

In this expression, “nadrah” and “surur” come together:

  • “nadrah” expresses freshness, brightness, and liveliness of outward appearance;
  • “surur” expresses the pleasant inner state.

This pairing creates harmony between outward appearance and inner condition: the effect of the desirable state is visible in the face and also present in the person’s inner experience.

“Surur” is not necessarily loud joy or sudden excitement. It can indicate satisfaction, tranquility, and settled joy.

13. “Masrur”: Joyful

“Masrur” appears twice in Surat Al-Inshiqaq:

وَيَنْقَلِبُ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهِ مَسْرُورًا

and he returns to his people joyful.

Al-Inshiqaq 84:9

and:

إِنَّهُ كَانَ فِي أَهْلِهِ مَسْرُورًا

he had been joyful among his people.

Al-Inshiqaq 84:13

Structurally, “masrur” describes someone for whom a cause of joy has been provided and who is in a state of satisfaction.

The two usages of this word in one surah differ in context:

In the first verse, joy follows an easy account and a desirable return.

In the second verse, the person was joyful among his family in the former life, but he thought he would never be returned. Therefore, the word “masrur” does not carry fixed positive or negative moral judgment in itself. Its value is determined by the cause of the joy and its place in the context.

14. “Sarra'”: Ease, Spaciousness, and Desirable Condition

“Sarra'” appears twice in the Qur'an, both times opposite “darra'.”

In Al 'Imran 3:134:

الَّذِينَ يُنْفِقُونَ فِي السَّرَّاءِ وَالضَّرَّاءِ

those who spend in ease and hardship.

Al 'Imran 3:134

And in Al-A'raf 7:95:

قَدْ مَسَّ آبَاءَنَا الضَّرَّاءُ وَالسَّرَّاءُ

hardship and ease touched our fathers as well.

Al-A'raf 7:95

“Sarra'” is not only psychological joy; it also includes desirable outward conditions:

  • welfare;
  • spaciousness;
  • health;
  • ease;
  • blessing and opening.

Opposite it, “darra'” indicates harmful and difficult conditions:

  • constriction;
  • harm;
  • illness;
  • poverty;
  • affliction.

Therefore, “sarra' and darra'” are a semantic pair for two opposite states of life: opening and constriction.

15. “Surur”: Couches and Places of Sitting

“Surur” is the plural of “sarir” and appears six times in the Qur'an:

  • Al-Hijr 15:47;
  • As-Saffat 37:44;
  • Az-Zukhruf 43:34;
  • At-Tur 52:20;
  • Al-Waqi'ah 56:15;
  • Al-Ghashiyah 88:13.

“Sarir” is a raised or prepared place for sitting, resting, or reclining. Depending on context, it may be translated as “couches,” “adorned seats,” “reclining places,” or “raised beds.”

15.1. Sururin Mutaqabilin

In Al-Hijr 15:47 it says:

إِخْوَانًا عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مُتَقَابِلِينَ

as brothers upon couches facing one another.

Al-Hijr 15:47

The same expression appears in As-Saffat 37:44:

عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مُتَقَابِلِينَ

upon couches facing one another.

As-Saffat 37:44

“Mutaqabilin” expresses the social arrangement of the seats. People are not described as turning their backs to each other or as being in a hierarchy; rather, they are facing one another.

15.2. Sururan 'Alayha Yattaki'un

In Az-Zukhruf 43:34:

وَسُرُرًا عَلَيْهَا يَتَّكِئُونَ

and couches upon which they recline.

Az-Zukhruf 43:34

The verb “yattaki'un” shows that “surur” are not simple chairs, but places suited for ease and reclining.

15.3. Sururin Masfufah

In At-Tur 52:20:

مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مَصْفُوفَةٍ

reclining upon couches arranged in rows.

At-Tur 52:20

“Masfufah” expresses order and the side-by-side arrangement of these places.

15.4. Sururin Mawdunah

In Al-Waqi'ah 56:15:

عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مَوْضُونَةٍ

upon woven and firmly adorned couches.

Al-Waqi'ah 56:15

“Mawdunah” indicates a woven, interlaced, firm, or adorned construction. The image is not of a simple and temporary seat, but a couch made with order and refinement.

15.5. Sururun Marfu'ah

In Al-Ghashiyah 88:13:

فِيهَا سُرُرٌ مَرْفُوعَةٌ

in it are raised couches.

Al-Ghashiyah 88:13

“Marfu'ah” indicates the height and elevated status of these couches.

15.6. The Relation of “Surur” with Joy

The written similarity between “surur” and “surur” may create the impression that “sarir” is directly derived from joy. There is a natural relation between a couch of ease and joy, but the closer root explanation for “sarir” is the concept of settlement and resting.

Therefore:

  • surur: plural of sarir, places of sitting and settlement;
  • surur: joy and satisfaction.

These two forms share the root, but in the Qur'an they have two independent meanings.

16. Difference from Nearby Roots

S R R (Sirr) and Kh F Y (Khafiy)

“Kh F Y” indicates not being seen, not being apparent, or being difficult to perceive. Something may be hidden without anyone intending to hide it.

“Sirr” is more connected with inward content, a private matter, or something kept in the non-public domain.

So every “sirr” is hidden, but not every hidden thing is necessarily a secret.

S R R (Sirr) and K T M (Katama)

“Katama” indicates covering and holding information back from appearance. In it, the act of suppressing or preventing disclosure is prominent.

“Asarra” can mean both hiding and privately sharing something with someone. Therefore, its field is broader than “katm.”

S R R (Sirr) and N J W (Najw)

“Najwa” is usually a private conversation among two or more persons.

“Sirr” can be content that has not been told to anyone and remains only inside one person.

In the expression “sirrahum wa najwahum”:

  • “sirr” includes their hidden matters;
  • “najwa” includes their private conversations.

S R R (Sirr) and ʿ L N (ʿAlan)

“ʿAlan” and “ʿalaniyah” indicate being open in the public domain. This root is one of the clearest opposites of “sirr” in the Qur'an:

سِرًّا وَعَلَانِيَةً
privately and openly.

S R R (Sirr) and J H R (Jahar)

“Jahar” more specifically indicates making speech open, saying it loudly or audibly, and bringing it out of hiddenness.

For this reason, the Qur'an says:

أَسَرَّ الْقَوْلَ وَمَنْ جَهَرَ بِهِ
he hid the speech, or he spoke it openly.

S R R (Surur) and F R Ḥ (Farah)

“Surur” is a state of joy and satisfaction. “Farah” also indicates joy, but more often it may show the visible reaction of a person to the arrival of something desired.

“Farah,” depending on its object and context, may be praised or blamed; “surur” also has no fixed moral judgment in its lexical meaning, as Surat Al-Inshiqaq uses it for two different situations.

S R R (Surur) and B Sh R (Bashara)

The branch of glad tidings from the root “B Sh R” is connected with the effect of good news on the outward skin and face of the human being; good news opens the face.

“Surur” expresses the state of joy and satisfaction itself. In Al-Insan 76:11, “nadrah” places the outward manifestation and “surur” the inner state side by side.

S R R (Surur) and ʾ R K (Arikah)

“Surur” is a more general word for couches and raised places of sitting or reclining.

“Ara'ik” usually expresses adorned places prepared for ease. The two are close in some contexts, but “surur” more directly describes the structures of the couches and their arrangement.

18. Conclusion

The root “S R R” in the Qur'an has three major semantic fields:

First, something that remains inside or outside the public domain:

sirr, sara'ir, asarra, and israr.

Second, a state of satisfaction, ease, and joy:

tasurru, surur, masrur, and sarra'.

Third, a material place of sitting, reclining, and settlement:

surur.

In the first branch, the movement of meaning is from “open” to “inside and hidden.” In the second branch, a pleasant state arises and settles in the human self. In the third branch, the body is placed upon a setting for ease and rest.

The resulting meaning of the root can be expressed as follows:

“S R R” is a family of meanings related to inwardness, hiddenness, center, settlement, and satisfaction; but each derivative reveals its precise meaning only in its morphological structure and context.
