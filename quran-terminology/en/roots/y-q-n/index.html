The root Y Q N in the Qur’an

The root ي ق ن (Y Q N) in the Qur’an moves around the meaning of firmness, settling, and steadiness in knowing. Certainty is not knowledge that wanders between possibilities. Certainty is a stage of understanding that has come out of conjecture, imitation, doubt, and anxiety, and has reached firmness.

In some lexical explanations, the image of calm and unmoving water is used to approach the sensory feel of this root: water that, after movement and turbulence, settles in its place and becomes still. This image can help us understand yaqīn. Certainty in human awareness is like the settling of the turbulence of doubts, the sinking of the dust of confusion, and the appearance of the clarity of truth within the soul.

But certainty in the Qur’an is not merely an abstract perception. If certainty is true, it must change the human being’s direction. Whoever has certainty in the akhirah does not see life only at the surface of dunya. Whoever has certainty in God’s signs does not leave the signs without effect. And whoever has inward certainty of the truth but denies it out of oppression and arrogance is not, in the Qur’anic view, suffering from a simple knowledge problem; his problem is moral and existential.

Central meaning of the root

The central meaning of ي ق ن (Y Q N) may be stated as this: knowledge reaching a firmness that doubt, conjecture, and anxiety cannot shake.

From this meaning, the different branches of the root appear. يُوقِنُونَ (yūqinūn) are those who, in relation to a truth, have reached firm knowledge. اسْتَيْقَنَ (istayqana) means that a matter has reached a degree of clarity and certainty within a person. يَقِين (yaqīn) is this state of firmness and certainty itself. مُوقِن (mūqin) is someone standing within this firmness of knowledge.

So certainty in the Qur’an is not merely belief. Belief may be weak, inherited, emotional, or imitative. Certainty is knowledge established upon signs, insight, experience, reflection, or a clear encounter with truth.

Why certainty is not conjecture

The Qur’an repeatedly distinguishes between يقين (yaqīn) and ظن (ẓann). Ẓann is conjecture, probability, or a judgment that has not yet reached firmness and certainty. Yaqīn is knowledge that has passed beyond that stage.

In An-Nisa 4:157, regarding the matter of Jesus, the Qur’an says that they have no knowledge of it and follow only conjecture. Then it says: وَمَا قَتَلُوهُ يَقِينًا (wa-mā qatalūhu yaqīnan). This structure shows that the issue is not only historical reporting; the Qur’an distinguishes between knowledge, conjecture, and certainty. They do not possess certain knowledge in this matter, and their speech rests upon conjecture.

From here it becomes clear that certainty in the Qur’an is not anti-investigation. It is the result of passing beyond conjecture. One who insists on inherited belief without evidence is not mūqin. He may only be trapped in habit, partisanship, or imitation. Qur’anic certainty must be joined with the clarification of truth, seeing the signs, understanding the signs, and moral responsibility.

Yūqinūn: having certainty and living with conviction

The form يُوقِنُونَ (yūqinūn) is from Form IV and appears several times in the Qur’an. It is usually used for those who have reached certainty regarding the akhirah, God’s signs, God’s judgment, or meeting the Lord.

At the beginning of Al-Baqarah, those who receive guidance are described with the phrase وَبِالْآخِرَةِ هُمْ يُوقِنُونَ (wa-bi-al-ākhirati hum yūqinūn). This does not only say that they believe in the akhirah. Its deeper meaning is that the akhirah is present in their awareness as a firm reality. They see life in such a way that final outcome, account, return, and responsibility are real.

The same theme appears in An-Naml and Luqman, where establishing salat, giving zakat, and certainty in the akhirah are placed together. This connection shows that certainty in the akhirah must affect human action. In the Qur’anic structure, certainty in the akhirah is a force that moves action in dunya. Weakness in tawhidic connection, neglect of salat, or irresponsibility regarding zakat and people’s rights is not merely lack of information; it is a sign of trembling in certainty.

In other verses, يُوقِنُونَ (yūqinūn) is connected with God’s signs. This shows that Qur’anic certainty is not separated from signs. By seeing, reflecting, and taking seriously the signs in horizons, selves, history, revelation, and the order of creation, the human being comes closer to firm knowledge.

Tūqinūn: a call to certainty about meeting the Lord

The form تُوقِنُونَ (tūqinūn) appears in Ar-Raʿd 13:2, where after speaking of the heavens, the governance of the command, and the detailed explanation of signs, the aim is stated as: لَعَلَّكُمْ بِلِقَاءِ رَبِّكُمْ تُوقِنُونَ (laʿallakum bi-liqā’i rabbikum tūqinūn).

This verse shows that certainty about meeting the Lord is not attained only through religious claim. The Qur’an presents the signs of creation, governance, order, and the detailed unfolding of signs so that the human being may come near certainty.

Meeting the Lord means the human being’s final encounter with his Lord: a truth that removes worldly life from meaninglessness. If the human being has certainty in this meeting, his choices do not remain weightless. He understands that life is not without account, and that no action, intention, or orientation is wasted.

Istayqanathā: inward certainty with outward denial

One of the most important uses of the root ي ق ن (Y Q N) in the Qur’an is An-Naml 27:14:

وَجَحَدُوا بِهَا وَاسْتَيْقَنَتْهَا أَنْفُسُهُمْ ظُلْمًا وَعُلُوًّا
wa-jaḥadū bihā wa-istayqanathā anfusuhum ẓulman wa-ʿuluwwā

They denied them, while their own souls had become certain of them, out of oppression and arrogance.

This is one of the deepest Qur’anic verses about the relationship between knowledge and ethics. The Qur’an shows that denial is not always from not knowing. Sometimes a human being recognizes the truth inwardly, but because of oppression, arrogance, interest, power, social position, or fear that his mental system will collapse, he denies it.

So اسْتَيْقَنَتْهَا (istayqanathā) here does not merely mean that they believed. It means that the signs had become clear and settled within their souls. But inward certainty, when not joined with right ethics, does not lead to salvation. Knowing the truth is not enough; submission to truth is also necessary.

This verse is important for understanding many historical and religious denials. Sometimes the human being asks for evidence, but when evidence comes, he still refuses. In that case, his problem is not lack of signs; his problem is inward orientation and ethics.

Liyastayqina: reaching certainty after a test and sign

The form لِيَسْتَيْقِنَ (liyastayqina) appears in Al-Muddaththir 74:31. In this verse, the number of the guardians of the Fire and the reactions of different groups are mentioned. One of the outcomes of placing this number is that the People of the Book may reach certainty.

This verse shows that sometimes a sign or test reveals people. For some, the sign increases certainty; for some, it causes slipping and objection; and for some, it exposes disease in the heart.

So certainty in the Qur’an does not always appear in a calm and test-free space. Sometimes tests, ambiguities, numbers, signs, or weighty Qur’anic statements separate people from one another. One who seeks truth comes nearer to certainty through the right sign; but one whose heart contains disease turns the same sign into an excuse for denial.

Yaqīn: certainty and firmness

يَقِين (yaqīn) is the noun of this root and means certainty, definiteness, and knowledge that has reached firmness.

In Al-Hijr 15:99, it says: وَاعْبُدْ رَبَّكَ حَتَّىٰ يَأْتِيَكَ الْيَقِينُ (wa-uʿbud rabbaka ḥattā ya’tiyaka al-yaqīn). This expression has usually been understood as death, and this reading is semantically defensible, because death is the most certain event that comes to the human being and marks the end of the stage of action in dunya.

But one must be careful: the root meaning of الْيَقِين (al-yaqīn) is not death. Its root meaning is certainty and the certain matter. In this verse, death may be a clear instance of that certain matter. So the root ي ق ن (Y Q N) should not be reduced to the meaning of death.

In Al-Muddaththir 74:47, the words of the misguided are also reported: حَتَّىٰ أَتَانَا الْيَقِينُ (ḥattā atānā al-yaqīn). They remained in heedlessness and denial until certainty came to them. Here too, certainty may point to death or to certain encounter with truth: the stage where conjecture, argument, and escape no longer benefit.

Yaqīnan: denying certainty against conjecture

The form يَقِينًا (yaqīnan) appears in An-Nisa 4:157. This verse concerns the claim of killing Jesus and says that they have no knowledge of this matter, follow only conjecture, and did not kill him yaqīnan.

This verse must be read carefully. Before bringing يَقِينًا (yaqīnan), the Qur’an raises the issue of knowledge and conjecture. So the emphasis is that their claim is not established upon certainty. They follow conjecture and do not have certainty.

In translation, rendering يَقِينًا (yaqīnan) simply as “certainly” transfers part of the meaning, but its connection with the negation of knowledge and following conjecture must not be lost. This verse is one of the Qur’an’s important examples in distinguishing report, conjecture, claim, and certainty.

Mūqinīn / mūqinūn: those who have certainty

مُوقِنِين (mūqinīn) and مُوقِنُونَ (mūqinūn) are active participles of Form IV and refer to those who have reached certainty: possessors of firm and unshaken belief.

In Al-Anʿam 6:75, God shows Abraham the dominion of the heavens and the earth so that he may be among the mūqinīn. This verse shows that Abrahamic certainty is connected with seeing the signs of dominion and understanding the deeper layers of creation.

In other verses, the earth and its signs are presented for the mūqinīn. This means that a mūqin is not someone who closes his eyes to the world and only claims. A mūqin is someone who sees the signs, understands them, and moves from the scattered surface of events toward order, lordship, and truth.

But the Qur’an also shows that certainty can come late, after punishment or final truth has become visible. In As-Sajdah 32:12, the criminals say: رَبَّنَا أَبْصَرْنَا وَسَمِعْنَا ... إِنَّا مُوقِنُونَ (rabbanā abṣarnā wa-samiʿnā ... innā mūqinūn). This certainty comes after seeing the truth; but at that stage, they ask to return to dunya. This shows that certainty after the end of opportunity is not the same as path-making certainty in dunya.

Mustayqinīn: those who have reached certainty in a negative context

مُسْتَيْقِنِين (mustayqinīn) appears in the Qur’an only in Al-Jathiyah 45:32, where a group says: إِنْ نَظُنُّ إِلَّا ظَنًّا وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمُسْتَيْقِنِينَ (in naẓunnu illā ẓannan wa-mā naḥnu bi-mustayqinīn).

This sentence is very important. They say: we do not do anything except conjecture, and we are not among those who have reached certainty. This confession shows that there is a distance between conjecture and istīqān. A human being may remain in conjecture about the akhirah, account, or divine promise, and fail to reach certainty.

But the Qur’an does not present this state as healthy or final. The human being must move from conjecture toward certainty, especially when signs, insights, revelation, signs of creation, and the moral logic of account stand before him.

ʿIlma al-yaqīn: certain knowledge

The expression عِلْمَ الْيَقِين (ʿilma al-yaqīn) appears in At-Takathur 102:5: كَلَّا لَوْ تَعْلَمُونَ عِلْمَ الْيَقِينِ (kallā law taʿlamūna ʿilma al-yaqīn).

This expression points to knowledge that has reached the level of certainty: knowledge that pulls the human being out of distraction, rivalry in increase, heedlessness, and blind competition. If the human being knew the truth with ʿilm al-yaqīn, he would not see dunya as the end of everything and would not be lost in the race of increase.

عِلْمَ الْيَقِين (ʿilma al-yaqīn) is certain knowledge: knowing that arises from deep reflection on signs, evidence, and the awakening of reason and conscience. A human being may have heard something, but that hearing may not yet have moved his soul. ʿIlm al-yaqīn is when knowing becomes firm and clear, and the human being can no longer pretend not to know.

ʿAyna al-yaqīn: seeing the certain reality

The expression عَيْنَ الْيَقِين (ʿayna al-yaqīn) appears in At-Takathur 102:7: ثُمَّ لَتَرَوُنَّهَا عَيْنَ الْيَقِينِ (thumma la-tarawunnahā ʿayna al-yaqīn).

This expression points to direct encounter with reality: where the human being no longer knows only through report or inference, but sees the truth and faces it.

In the context of At-Takathur, people are distracted by rivalry in increase until they reach the graves. Then the warning comes: if they had knowledge of certainty, they would not do this; and eventually they will see Jahim with a seeing of ʿayna al-yaqīn.

So عَيْنَ الْيَقِين (ʿayna al-yaqīn) is the stage where truth changes from warning and report into vision and direct encounter. This seeing may come too late for guidance if the human being did not benefit from signs and warnings before it.

Ḥaqqu al-yaqīn: certain and realized truth

The expression حَقُّ الْيَقِين (ḥaqqu al-yaqīn) appears in Al-Waqiʿah 56:95 and Al-Haqqah 69:51. This expression shows that the Qur’an’s speech about ultimate reality is not conjecture, an empty symbol, or a baseless threat; it is the truth of certainty.

حَقُّ الْيَقِين (ḥaqqu al-yaqīn) should not be carelessly handed over to later mystical systems and loaded with concepts the Qur’anic text does not clearly state. From the Qur’an itself, we can say that ḥaqqu al-yaqīn means a truth whose certainty and realization are established: a truth that leaves no room for playing with conjecture, denial, or evasive interpretation.

In Al-Haqqah, after presenting outcomes and the truthfulness of revelation, the Qur’an says: وَإِنَّهُ لَحَقُّ الْيَقِينِ (wa-innahu la-ḥaqqu al-yaqīn). That is: this message, this report, and this reality are not conjecture or fabrication; they are certain and realized truth.

Certainty and moral responsibility

One of the important points of the root ي ق ن (Y Q N) is that certainty is not only a matter of knowing; it is a matter of responsibility. The Qur’an shows that a human being may have certainty about something and still deny it. An-Naml 27:14 is the clear example.

So the human problem is not always lack of evidence. Sometimes the truth is clear, but the human being does not want to humble himself before it. Certainty, if not joined with truthfulness, humility, and readiness to accept truth, may become proof against the human being.

On the other hand, true certainty makes the human being calm and responsible. Whoever has certainty in the akhirah does not take justice lightly. Whoever has certainty in meeting the Lord does not imagine his action to be without account. Whoever has certainty in God’s signs does not see the world as meaningless and abandoned.

Suggested translation in Forqan

For the root ي ق ن (Y Q N), one fixed translation is not enough. The form of the word and the context of the verse must be seen.

يُوقِنُ / يُوقِنُونَ / تُوقِنُونَ (yūqinu / yūqinūn / tūqinūn): have certainty, reach certainty, know with clear assurance.

اسْتَيْقَنَ / اسْتَيْقَنَتْ / لِيَسْتَيْقِنَ (istayqana / istayqanat / liyastayqina): reached clear certainty, became inwardly certain, so that one may reach certainty.

يَقِين (yaqīn): certainty, definiteness, a fixed and doubtless matter.

يَقِينًا (yaqīnan): certainly, with certainty, not upon conjecture.

مُوقِنِين / مُوقِنُونَ (mūqinīn / mūqinūn): those who have certainty, possessors of firm and unshaken belief.

مُسْتَيْقِنِين (mustayqinīn): those who have reached certainty, those for whom truth has become settled, depending on context.

عِلْمَ الْيَقِين (ʿilma al-yaqīn): certain knowledge, firm knowing.

عَيْنَ الْيَقِين (ʿayna al-yaqīn): seeing the certain reality, clear encounter with truth.

حَقُّ الْيَقِين (ḥaqqu al-yaqīn): certain and realized truth.

Summary

The root ي ق ن (Y Q N) in the Qur’an points to firmness of knowledge, certainty, and the settling of knowing after passing beyond conjecture and doubt.

Certainty in the Qur’an is not merely religious belief or inner feeling. Certainty is connected with signs, insight, reflection, meeting the Lord, the akhirah, and moral responsibility. A mūqin is a human being for whom truth has become firm, and this firmness must affect the direction of his life.

At the same time, the Qur’an warns that inward certainty without humility and justice is not enough. There were people whose souls became certain of the signs, but they denied them out of oppression and arrogance. So certainty, if not joined with submission before truth, does not save the human being.

Thus ي ق ن (Y Q N) clarifies the human being’s relation to conjecture, knowing, claim, firmness, responsibility, and truth. Qur’anic certainty is firm knowledge: knowledge that brings the human being out of heedlessness and makes him responsible before God, the akhirah, the signs, and truth.
