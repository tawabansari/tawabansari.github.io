1. Introduction to the Root and Its Difference from “H R Th”

The root “Z R Ayn” indicates placing seed in a suitable bed and the emergence and growth of a plant from it. This root can express the act of planting, the process of causing to grow, and the plant or product that has grown.

Its summarized difference from “H R Th” is this:

“Harth” places more emphasis on agricultural work, preparing the land, and striving to obtain produce; but “zar'” focuses on seed-placement and especially on the sprouting and growth of the plant until produce appears.

The human being prepares the land, places the seed in it, and cares for the cultivation; but the living of the seed, the coming forth of the sprout, and the formation of the plant are not in his independent control. This distinction is made clear in Al-Waqiah 56:63-64:

أَفَرَأَيْتُمْ مَا تَحْرُثُونَ ۝ أَأَنْتُمْ تَزْرَعُونَهُ أَمْ نَحْنُ الزَّارِعُونَ

“Have you seen what you cultivate? Is it you who make it grow, or are We the growers?”

Al-Waqiah 56:63-64

Even so, in general Arabic usage, “zara'a” is also used for the human act of planting. Therefore the distinction mentioned here is not an absolute wall between the two words; it is the difference in their semantic focus, which is highlighted in Surat Al-Waqiah in a special way.

2. Basic Meaning

The base form of this root is:

زَرَعَ ـ يَزْرَعُ ـ زَرْعًا وَزِرَاعَةً

In the simplest usage:

زَرَعَ الْحَبَّ

That is, he scattered or placed the seed in the earth so that it would grow.

In lexical reports, the core of this root is also defined with “making something grow and develop.” For this reason, “zara'a” does not convey only the movement of the hand when scattering seed; it can include the whole process that turns the seed into a grown plant.

The semantic path of the root is this:

seed-placement ← sprouting ← growth ← cultivated plant ← product

Therefore “zar'” can indicate any of the following:

  • the act of planting;
  • the process of causing growth;
  • the plant that has grown from seed;
  • a collection of cultivated plants;
  • a usable or harvestable product.

Its exact meaning must be recognized from the verbs, descriptions, and nouns that accompany it.

3. The Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is a seed that is placed in soil and, after receiving water, comes out of its closed and hidden state.

This process has several stages:

  1. the seed is scattered or placed in the earth;
  2. water and suitable conditions reach it;
  3. the seed splits;
  4. the root goes into the soil;
  5. the sprout comes out of the earth;
  6. stems and secondary branches appear;
  7. the plant gains strength and becomes firm;
  8. the product reaches the stage of use.

In this image, one part of the work belongs to the farmer’s action, and another part occurs within the living structure of the seed and plant. This link between “human action” and “the realization of a life that the human being does not independently create” is one of the most important Qur'anic features of this root.

4. Branches of Meaning

4.1. Scattering or Placing Seed

“Zara'a” can indicate the human act of placing seed in the earth:

تَزْرَعُونَ سَبْعَ سِنِينَ دَأَبًا

“You cultivate for seven years continuously.”

4.2. Causing to Grow and Nurturing

This root can indicate growing the seed and bringing it to the stage of a plant:

أَأَنْتُمْ تَزْرَعُونَهُ أَمْ نَحْنُ الزَّارِعُونَ

In this verse, the matter is not merely scattering seed, but the realization of its sprouting and growth.

4.3. The Cultivated Plant

“Zar'” is said of the plant itself or a collection of plants that have grown from seed:

ثُمَّ يُخْرِجُ بِهِ زَرْعًا مُخْتَلِفًا أَلْوَانُهُ

“Then by means of it He brings forth cultivation of diverse colors.”

4.4. Cultivation and Product

This word can indicate products such as grains and other harvestable plants. Its use for wheat, barley, and similar products is known, but the meaning of the root is not limited to them.

4.5. Cultivated Fields and Productive Resources

The plural form “zuru'” can indicate several cultivations, different kinds of produce, or a group of lands under cultivation:

وَزُرُوعٍ وَنَخْلٍ

“And cultivated fields and palms.”

4.6. Metaphorical Growth

Anything that begins like a seed from a small and hidden state, grows, and becomes a visible and firm structure can be compared with “zar'.” This extension is seen in Al-Fath 48:29.

5. Known Derivatives

Zar'

It is both a verbal noun and a noun. It can mean planting, causing to grow, cultivation, the cultivated plant, or the product.

Zuru'

The plural of “zar',” meaning crops, products, or multiple cultivated fields.

Zari'

An active participle: one who plants or causes to grow.

Zurra'

The broken plural of “zari',” usually indicating farmers and those who work with planting and nurturing plants.

Zira'ah

The act, craft, or profession of cultivating and nurturing plants.

Mazru'

Something planted or grown.

Mazra'ah

A place where seed is planted and plant products are grown.

Zari'ah

It can indicate seed prepared for planting or cultivated land.

Izdara'a

It means he cultivated for himself or assigned land for cultivation. This word arose from the pattern “ifta'ala.” Its structural base was “iztara'a,” and the “t” changed to “d” next to “z”; therefore the common form became “izdara'a.” This derivative does not occur in the Qur'an.

In the Qur'an, the following forms of this root occur:

  • “زَرْع”;
  • “زُرُوع”;
  • “تَزْرَعُونَ”;
  • “تَزْرَعُونَهُ”;
  • “الزُّرَّاع”;
  • “الزَّارِعُونَ”.

The root “Z R Ayn” appears fourteen times in the Qur'an:

  • twice in the form of the present verb;
  • eight times in the singular form “zar'”;
  • twice in the plural form “zuru'”;
  • once in the form “az-zurra'”;
  • once in the form “az-zari'un.”

These usages occur in Yusuf 12:47, Al-An'am 6:141, Ar-Ra'd 13:4, Ibrahim 14:37, An-Nahl 16:11, Al-Kahf 18:32, Ash-Shu'ara 26:148, As-Sajdah 32:27, Az-Zumar 39:21, Ad-Dukhan 44:26, Al-Fath 48:29, and Al-Waqiah 56:64.

The Qur'anic usages begin from the human act of planting, reach the sprouting and growth of the plant, and then include cultivation, product, cultivated field, and the parable of the growth of a group.

7. “Tazra'una Sab'a Sinin Da'aban”

In Yusuf 12:47 it says:

قَالَ تَزْرَعُونَ سَبْعَ سِنِينَ دَأَبًا فَمَا حَصَدْتُمْ فَذَرُوهُ فِي سُنْبُلِهِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِمَّا تَأْكُلُونَ

“He said: You will cultivate for seven years continuously; then whatever you harvest, leave it in its ear, except a little from which you eat.”

Yusuf 12:47

“Tazra'una” in this verse refers to sustained and planned agricultural activity. The word “da'aban” highlights the continuity and regular pursuit of this work.

Several agricultural stages are placed together in one sentence:

  • “tazra'una”: you plant and nurture the product;
  • “hasadtum”: you harvest the ripened product;
  • “fadharuhu fi sunbulihi”: leave it in its ear for storage;
  • “ta'kulun”: you consume part of it.

Therefore “zar'” comes before “hasd.” Agriculture is the stage of creating and nurturing the product, while harvesting is the end of the growth period and the beginning of gathering it.

“Tazra'una” in this verse is not limited only to the moment of scattering seed, because the speech is about a complete agricultural program across seven years.

8. The Contrast Between “Harth” and “Zar'” in Surat Al-Waqiah

Al-Waqiah 56:63-64 are the most important Qur'anic witness for recognizing the difference between these two roots:

أَفَرَأَيْتُمْ مَا تَحْرُثُونَ ۝ أَأَنْتُمْ تَزْرَعُونَهُ أَمْ نَحْنُ الزَّارِعُونَ

“Have you seen what you cultivate? Is it you who make it grow, or are We the growers?”

Al-Waqiah 56:63-64

8.1. “Ma Tahruthun”

“Harth” emphasizes the set of works that the human being performs in order to produce a crop:

  • preparing the land;
  • turning over the soil;
  • placing seed;
  • watering;
  • caring for the cultivation;
  • directing the land toward the intended product.

So “ma tahruthun” is what the human being works upon agriculturally and prepares in order to obtain produce.

8.2. “Tazra'unahu”

“Tazra'unahu” in this contrast emphasizes the stage in which the seed becomes a living and growing being.

The human being can select the seed and place it in the soil, but he cannot independently:

  • activate the hidden life in the seed;
  • split its shell;
  • bring root and sprout out of it;
  • build the structure of the plant stage by stage;
  • keep it alive until it reaches product.

For this reason, translating “Is it you who plant it?” does not show the full contrast, because the human being, in the ordinary sense, really does plant the seed. The question of the verse is about causing growth and the realization of growing.

The more precise translation in this context is:

“Is it you who make it grow, or are We the growers?”

8.3. The Difference Between “Tazra'unahu” and “Az-Zari'un”

Grammatically, both sides of the question begin with an independent pronoun:

  • “a-antum tazra'unahu”;
  • “am nahnu az-zari'un.”

Therefore the precise difference is not merely a shift from a verbal sentence to a nominal sentence, because “antum tazra'unahu” also begins with “antum,” and the verbal sentence “tazra'unahu” is its predicate.

The main difference is in the type of predicate:

  • in the first part, the predicate is a present verb: “tazra'unahu”;
  • in the second part, the predicate is a definite active participle: “az-zari'un.”

The present verb places the action being claimed before the audience: “Do you perform this act?” But the active participle highlights the station and relation of the doer: “Are We the growers?”

This shift moves the question from performing a punctual act to identifying the doer to whom growing is truly attributed. Even so, from this structure alone one cannot extract an absolute rule about permanence or essentiality; the main emphasis is on the true relation of causing growth.

9. “Az-Zar'a Mukhtalifan Ukuluhu”

In Al-An'am 6:141 it says:

وَالنَّخْلَ وَالزَّرْعَ مُخْتَلِفًا أُكُلُهُ وَالزَّيْتُونَ وَالرُّمَّانَ مُتَشَابِهًا وَغَيْرَ مُتَشَابِهٍ

“And the palms and the cultivation, its edible yield diverse, and the olive and the pomegranate, similar and dissimilar.”

Al-An'am 6:141

“Az-zar'” here is the cultivated plants themselves or the cultivated products. The phrase “mukhtalifan ukuluhu” shows that the verse is looking at the edible yield of the cultivation.

“Ukul” indicates what is eaten or the consumable product. Therefore “zar'” is a collection of cultivated plants that produce different edible things.

The separation of “az-zar'” from palms, olives, and pomegranates shows that this word in this context refers more to field crops and agricultural plants, while fruit trees are named separately.

10. Earth, Water, and the Diversity of Cultivations

10.1. Neighboring Pieces of Land

In Ar-Ra'd 13:4 it says:

وَفِي الْأَرْضِ قِطَعٌ مُتَجَاوِرَاتٌ وَجَنَّاتٌ مِنْ أَعْنَابٍ وَزَرْعٌ وَنَخِيلٌ صِنْوَانٌ وَغَيْرُ صِنْوَانٍ يُسْقَىٰ بِمَاءٍ وَاحِدٍ وَنُفَضِّلُ بَعْضَهَا عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ فِي الْأُكُلِ

“And in the earth there are neighboring pieces, gardens of grapes, cultivation, and palms, clustered and unclustered, watered with one water, yet We make some of them exceed others in edible yield.”

Ar-Ra'd 13:4

“Zar'” in this verse is one of the productive coverings of the earth. The lands are beside one another and their water is one, but they do not produce identical plants and products.

“Zar'” here is a living and growing thing, not merely land upon which the human being has worked.

10.2. A Valley Without Cultivation

In Ibrahim 14:37 it says:

رَبَّنَا إِنِّي أَسْكَنْتُ مِنْ ذُرِّيَّتِي بِوَادٍ غَيْرِ ذِي زَرْعٍ عِنْدَ بَيْتِكَ الْمُحَرَّمِ

“Our Lord, I have settled some of my offspring in a valley without cultivation, near Your protected House.”

Ibrahim 14:37

“Ghayri dhi zar'” means a valley that has no cultivation or agricultural product. The phrase does not merely say that at that moment no one had scattered seed there; rather, it introduces the absence of cultivation and productive agricultural resources as one of the characteristics of the environment.

10.3. Causing Cultivation to Grow with Water

In An-Nahl 16:11 it says:

يُنْبِتُ لَكُمْ بِهِ الزَّرْعَ وَالزَّيْتُونَ وَالنَّخِيلَ وَالْأَعْنَابَ وَمِنْ كُلِّ الثَّمَرَاتِ

“By means of it He grows for you the cultivation, the olive, the palms, the grapes, and from every kind of fruit.”

An-Nahl 16:11

The verb “yunbitu” is from the root “N B T,” and “az-zar'” is what is caused to grow. Therefore “zar'” here is the plant itself or the cultivated product, not the act of planting.

10.4. Cultivation Between Two Gardens

In Al-Kahf 18:32 it says:

وَحَفَفْنَاهُمَا بِنَخْلٍ وَجَعَلْنَا بَيْنَهُمَا زَرْعًا

“We surrounded the two of them with palms and placed cultivation between them.”

Al-Kahf 18:32

Three kinds of land use are seen in this image:

  • gardens of grapes;
  • palms around them;
  • cultivation in the space between the two gardens.

“Zar'an” here refers to field products grown in the space between the gardens.

11. Cultivation in Dry Land

In As-Sajdah 32:27 it says:

أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا أَنَّا نَسُوقُ الْمَاءَ إِلَى الْأَرْضِ الْجُرُزِ فَنُخْرِجُ بِهِ زَرْعًا تَأْكُلُ مِنْهُ أَنْعَامُهُمْ وَأَنْفُسُهُمْ

“Have they not seen that We drive the water toward barren land and by means of it bring forth cultivation from which their livestock and they themselves eat?”

As-Sajdah 32:27

The verse creates a contrast between two states:

  • “al-ard al-juruz”: dry land lacking growth;
  • “nukhriju bihi zar'an”: bringing forth cultivation by means of water.

The verb “nukhriju” highlights the plant’s coming out of the earth, and “zar'an” is the name of what has come out and grown.

The resulting cultivation is food for both livestock and human beings. Therefore “zar'” in this verse is a general title for plant products that can feed living beings.

12. The Complete Cycle of Agriculture

In Az-Zumar 39:21 it says:

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ أَنْزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَسَلَكَهُ يَنَابِيعَ فِي الْأَرْضِ ثُمَّ يُخْرِجُ بِهِ زَرْعًا مُخْتَلِفًا أَلْوَانُهُ ثُمَّ يَهِيجُ فَتَرَاهُ مُصْفَرًّا ثُمَّ يَجْعَلُهُ حُطَامًا

“Have you not seen that The God sent down water from the sky and caused it to enter as springs in the earth; then by means of it He brings forth cultivation of diverse colors; then it dries, and you see it yellowed; then He makes it broken debris?”

Az-Zumar 39:21

The verse describes the agricultural cycle in several stages:

  1. the sending down of water;
  2. the entering and flowing of water in the earth;
  3. the coming forth of cultivation;
  4. the diversity of the plants’ colors;
  5. the plant’s reaching the stage of dryness;
  6. yellowing;
  7. becoming broken remains.

“Zar'” here is a living plant that comes out of the earth, passes through its period of growth, and then moves toward dryness and collapse.

This usage shows that “zar'” is not only the product ready for harvest; the plant is called “zar'” from the time of its appearance and growth as well.

13. The Plural Form “Zuru'”

The form “zuru'” appears twice in the Qur'an.

In Ash-Shu'ara 26:148:

وَزُرُوعٍ وَنَخْلٍ طَلْعُهَا هَضِيمٌ

“And cultivated fields and palms whose spathes are tender and clustered.”

Ash-Shu'ara 26:148

And in Ad-Dukhan 44:26:

وَزُرُوعٍ وَمَقَامٍ كَرِيمٍ

“And cultivated fields and a noble station.”

Ad-Dukhan 44:26

The plural form can indicate:

  • a plurality of lands under cultivation;
  • different kinds of products;
  • the breadth of agricultural production;
  • a collection of productive resources.

In the verses of Ad-Dukhan, “zuru'” appears beside gardens and springs as part of the structure of a flourishing land. In the verse of Ash-Shu'ara as well, alongside palms, it shows the abundance and diversity of agricultural resources.

14. The Parable of Growth in Al-Fath 48:29

In Al-Fath 48:29 it says:

وَمَثَلُهُمْ فِي الْإِنْجِيلِ كَزَرْعٍ أَخْرَجَ شَطْأَهُ فَآزَرَهُ فَاسْتَغْلَظَ فَاسْتَوَىٰ عَلَىٰ سُوقِهِ يُعْجِبُ الزُّرَّاعَ

“And their description in the Gospel is like cultivation that brought forth its fresh shoots and side stems; then it strengthened it, then it became thick, then it stood firm upon its stalks, pleasing the cultivators.”

Al-Fath 48:29

This verse gives the most complete Qur'anic description of the growth of “zar'.”

14.1. “Ka-Zar'in”

“Zar'” here is “cultivation” or “the cultivated plant,” not merely seed, because the continuation of the verse speaks of a plant that has produced shoots, become thick, and stood upon its stalk.

14.2. “Akhraja Shat'ahu”

“Shat'” refers to shoots or secondary stems that appear beside the main stem.

In the agricultural science of grains, the emergence of secondary stems from the lower part of the plant is called tillering. In plants such as wheat and barley, after greening, secondary stems or tillers appear from the part near the soil surface and make the plant more dense.

The term “tillering” is a scientific description for this same observable stage; the meaning of “shat'” also returns to fresh growths that come out from beside the main plant.

14.3. “Fa-Azarahu”

This part indicates the plant gaining strength and being supported. The fresh stems stand beside the main stem and make the whole plant stronger.

14.4. “Fa-Staghlaza”

The plant comes out of its initial thinness and vulnerability and becomes thick and dense. The increase of secondary stems also helps the plant become fuller and more stable.

14.5. “Fa-Stawa Ala Suqihi”

The cultivation stands upright on its stalks. “Suq” in this phrase is the plural of “saq,” meaning the stalks of the plant. In this structure, this word has no semantic connection with “suq” meaning marketplace, even though the two look the same in unvowelled writing.

At this stage, the plant is no longer a weak sprout; its stalks have grown and have the strength to hold the plant and the ears.

14.6. “Yu'jibu az-Zurra'”

“Az-zurra'” are cultivators who know the stages of plant growth and observe the outcome of cultivation. Their wonder is at a plant that began from a small seed, became multi-stemmed, gained strength, and stood upon its stalks.

The stages of the parable are these:

the emergence of fresh growths ← increase and support ← thickening ← standing firm upon stalks

The structure of the parable shows that the increase of parts did not lead to dispersion and weakness; the fresh stems made the whole plant stronger.

15. The Link Between Seed and Lineage

In some Arabic usages outside the Qur'an, child or lineage has been compared to “zar'.” The basis of this semantic extension is clear:

  • beginning from a small origin;
  • being placed in a suitable bed;
  • gradual growth;
  • the emergence of a living being;
  • the continuation of lineage.

This connection is not limited to Arabic. In some related Semitic languages, words cognate with “Z R Ayn” have been used for both “seed” and “lineage or offspring.” For example, in Hebrew the cognate word “zera'” can convey both meanings.

This shared usage shows that the conceptual link between seed and lineage has an ancient background in the family of Semitic languages. Even so, the Qur'anic derivatives of “Z R Ayn” are not used directly for child or lineage, and the Qur'anic usages of this root mainly remain in the field of plant, agriculture, and the parable of growth.

16. Difference from Nearby Words

“Zar'” and “Harth”

“Harth” highlights work upon the land and effort to make produce possible; “zar'” emphasizes seed-placement, sprouting, and plant growth. The detail of this difference is seen in Al-Waqiah 56:63-64.

“Zar'” and “Badhr”

“Badhr” focuses on scattering and dispersing seed. “Zar'” can include seed-placement, but it is not limited to it and also includes sprouting and the product that results.

A seed may be scattered but not grow; in that case, “badhr” has taken place, but “zar'” in the sense of a grown product has not been realized.

“Zar'” and “Nabat”

“Nabata” indicates coming out and growing from the earth. “Zar'” preserves, in addition to growth, the plant’s relation with planting and purposeful nurturing.

In An-Nahl 16:11, “yunbitu” expresses the act of causing to grow, and “az-zar'” is what is caused to grow.

“Zar'” and “Ghars”

“Ghars” more often indicates planting a sapling, tree, or plant that has a visible existence at the time of transfer. “Zar'” is usually connected with seed and the hidden beginning of growth.

“Zar'” and “Hasd”

“Zar'” is the stage of planting and nurturing the product; “hasd” is cutting and gathering the product after it has ripened. In the story of Yusuf, first “tazra'una” comes, and then “hasadtum.”

“Zar'” and “Thamar”

“Zar'” is the plant or product that has come about from planting and growth. “Thamar” emphasizes the final fruit or yield of the plant, or the usable result.

17. Translation Suggestions and Conclusion

“Zar'” cannot be translated in every verse with one Persian equivalent. The translation must be chosen according to the role of the word in the sentence:

  • “تَزْرَعُونَ سَبْعَ سِنِينَ”: “you cultivate for seven years”;
  • “أَأَنْتُمْ تَزْرَعُونَهُ”: “is it you who make it grow?”;
  • “الزَّرْعَ مُخْتَلِفًا أُكُلُهُ”: “cultivations with diverse edible yields”;
  • “بِوَادٍ غَيْرِ ذِي زَرْعٍ”: “in a valley without cultivation”;
  • “يُنْبِتُ لَكُمْ بِهِ الزَّرْعَ”: “by means of it He grows cultivation for you”;
  • “وَجَعَلْنَا بَيْنَهُمَا زَرْعًا”: “We placed cultivation between the two of them”;
  • “وَزُرُوعٍ وَنَخْلٍ”: “cultivated fields and palms”;
  • “فَنُخْرِجُ بِهِ زَرْعًا”: “by means of it We bring forth cultivation”;
  • “كَزَرْعٍ أَخْرَجَ شَطْأَهُ”: “like cultivation that brought forth its fresh growths”;
  • “يُعْجِبُ الزُّرَّاعَ”: “it pleases the cultivators”;
  • “أَمْ نَحْنُ الزَّارِعُونَ”: “or are We the growers?”

Its main equivalents in English are:

  • to plant;
  • to cultivate;
  • to cause to grow;
  • cultivation;
  • cultivated plant;
  • product;
  • cultivated field.

Overall, the root “Z R Ayn” expresses a process that begins from seed and continues through sprouting, development, and the appearance of a firm plant:

seed-placement ← sprouting ← growing ← becoming firm ← product

The stable components of this root are:

seed, the beginning of life, sprouting, growth, manifestation, and reaching a living and firm structure.
