Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root J AYN L (J ʿ L)

Placing, appointing, making into a role, establishing within an order, and assigning a thing to a state or function

Important Forms from This Root

جَعَلَ / يَجْعَلُ (jaʿala / yajʿalu)

jaʿala / yajʿalu; placed, made, appointed, established, assigned a role or state.

جَعْل (jaʿl)

jaʿl; placing, making, assigning, appointing, establishing.

جَاعِل (jāʿil)

jāʿil; one who places, appoints, assigns, or makes something hold a role.

مَجْعُول / جُعِلَ (majʿūl / juʿila)

majʿūl / juʿila; made, appointed, assigned; it was made or placed.

جُعْل (juʿl)

juʿl; a wage, reward, or compensation set for a task, in lexical usage.

جُعَل (juʿal)

juʿal; a known insect name in Arabic; its relation to the root field should be handled carefully.

Central Meaning

The root J AYN L (J ʿ L) means placing, putting, making, appointing, establishing, or assigning something to a particular state, role, position, or relation.

This root is very broad in the Qur’an. Sometimes it means “to place.” Sometimes it means “to make into,” “to appoint,” “to establish within the created order,” or even “to regard” and “to assign by claim or belief.”

So جَعَلَ (jaʿala) is not simply a weak English “make.” It often shows that something is being placed within a role, state, function, rank, or relation.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of J AYN L (J ʿ L) is the creation of a new relation between a thing and a state, role, or position. The focus is not always on bringing the raw substance into existence. Often the focus is on assigning a thing a function or placing it within a meaningful order.

A thing may be made “light.” A human being may be appointed as “successor.” A natural phenomenon may be made a “sign.” Mountains may be placed as stabilizers. Night may be made a covering. These are not merely acts of producing raw matter. They are acts of assigning role, function, position, or relation.

For this reason, جَعَلَ (jaʿala) differs from khalaqa. Khalaqa is closer to creating, measuring, forming, and bringing a thing into its created structure. Jaʿala is closer to placing, assigning, appointing, making into a role, or establishing something within a particular order.

In some contexts, jaʿala comes close to creating, especially when it has one object and speaks about the created order. Even there, the color of “establishing in an order” or “assigning a state” often remains.

Material Image

The first material image is a hand placing something in a specific position or giving it a new role. A stone is on the ground; someone makes it a road marker. A piece of cloth exists; someone makes it a covering. A lamp is in a room; someone makes it a source of light. A human being lives on earth; The God places him in the position of responsibility and succession.

The second image is a change of function. A raw thing exists, but by a new act it enters a useful system. Leather becomes a sandal. Night is understood in the role of covering. Mountains are placed in the role of stabilizing the earth.

The third image is the beginning of a process. In Arabic, جَعَلَ يَفْعَلُ (jaʿala yafʿalu) can mean “he began to do.” The subject places himself into the course of an action and starts moving within that process.

The fourth image is setting something in return for work, as in جُعْل (juʿl), a wage or compensation fixed for a task. Something is placed in the role of payment, reward, or exchange.

In all these images, something is placed into a position, state, relation, or function, and because of that placement its meaning or use changes. Jaʿl is therefore not only physical putting; it is the assignment of position, function, role, or status.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, جَعَلَ (jaʿala) is used for placing, making, appointing, setting, establishing, and sometimes beginning to do something. It is a flexible root because it can build a relation between a thing and a new state.

Arabic may use this root when something is made a sign, a wage is set, a person is appointed to responsibility, or a thing is turned into a different state. The usage “he began to do such-and-such” also fits the same field, because the person enters a new course of action.

جُعْل (juʿl) is a wage or compensation set for a task: something placed in the position of payment. Some lexical items such as جُعَل (juʿal), the name of an insect, should be treated cautiously and not made the root’s central proof without precise lexical support.

Qur’anic Usage

Placing in a Role

The Qur’an says: إِنِّي جَاعِلٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً (innī jāʿilun fī al-arḍi khalīfah); I am placing upon the earth a successor / responsible vicegerent 2:30.

Here, جَاعِل (jāʿil) does not only mean that the human being is created. The human being is placed on earth in a role of responsibility, succession, and practical trust. The point is position and function, not merely biological existence.

Establishing within Creation

The Qur’an says: وَجَعَلَ الظُّلُمَاتِ وَالنُّورَ (wajaʿala al-ẓulumāti wa-al-nūra); and He established the darknesses and the light 6:1.

Here, jaʿala has one object structure. Darknesses and light are established within the created order. This usage comes near creation, but it still carries the sense of setting phenomena within the whole order of creation.

Assigning Function

Sometimes jaʿala has two objects. In that structure, the second object shows the role, state, or function being assigned. The Qur’an says: وَجَعَلْنَا اللَّيْلَ لِبَاسًا (wajaʿalnā al-layla libāsā); and We made the night a covering 78:10.

The night is not being destroyed and recreated. Rather, its role is shown: night functions as a covering. The first object is “night,” and the second object is “covering.” This structure is often the key to understanding jaʿala.

Making a Sign

The Qur’an says: وَجَعَلْنَا اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ آيَتَيْنِ (wajaʿalnā al-layla wa-al-nahāra āyatayn); and We made the night and the day two signs 17:12.

Night and day are not only natural phenomena. They are placed in the role of signs. Jaʿala here gives a sign-function to a natural reality.

Natural Order

The Qur’an speaks of making mountains, roads, earth, sky, night, day, pairs, and other structures within the natural order. For example: وَجَعَلْنَا فِي الْأَرْضِ رَوَاسِيَ (wajaʿalnā fī al-arḍi rawāsiya); and We placed firm stabilizers in the earth 21:31.

Here, mountains are not meaningless objects. They are placed within the earth with a stabilizing role. Jaʿala shows structure and function within the created order.

Appointment and Public Role

Jaʿala can also refer to appointment, rank, or responsibility. The Qur’an says to Abraham: إِنِّي جَاعِلُكَ لِلنَّاسِ إِمَامًا (innī jāʿiluka lil-nāsi imāmā); I am appointing you as an imam for the people 2:124.

Here, Abraham is placed in a role of guidance and leadership. Jaʿala is not merely a physical placing; it is an appointment into a public and spiritual function.

False Assignment and Shirk

Human beings can also “make” or “assign” things falsely. The Qur’an says: وَجَعَلُوا لِلَّهِ شُرَكَاءَ (wajaʿalū lillāhi shurakāʾa); and they assigned partners to The God 6:100.

This does not mean that humans create real partners for The God. It means they assign, imagine, or place something in a position that does not belong to it. Here jaʿala becomes a dangerous act of false relation-making: something is seated in a rank that is not its right. This comes close to injustice and shirk, because the rightful order is displaced.

Beginning a Process

In Arabic, jaʿala may also combine with a present-tense verb to mean beginning an action. In that construction, the subject places himself into a new course of action. This is not separate from the root’s core meaning: the actor enters a new state or process.

One-Object and Two-Object Jaʿala

One important key to translating جَعَلَ (jaʿala) is grammar. When jaʿala takes one object, it often means establishing, bringing into an order, or setting a phenomenon within the created structure. An example is وَجَعَلَ الظُّلُمَاتِ وَالنُّورَ (wajaʿala al-ẓulumāti wa-al-nūra) in 6:1.

When jaʿala takes two objects, the second object usually shows the role, state, rank, or function assigned to the first. In وَجَعَلْنَا اللَّيْلَ لِبَاسًا (wajaʿalnā al-layla libāsā) in 78:10, the night is the first object and covering is the second. The verse is teaching the role of night.

The same pattern appears in إِنِّي جَاعِلُكَ لِلنَّاسِ إِمَامًا (innī jāʿiluka lil-nāsi imāmā) in 2:124. Abraham is placed in the role of imam for the people.

Jaʿala and Khalaqa

In the Qur’an, khalaqa and jaʿala may appear near each other, but they are not identical. Khalaqa is closer to creating, measuring, and bringing the basic structure of a thing into existence. Jaʿala is closer to assigning a role, placing in a state, appointing, or giving function within an order.

A thing may be created and then made into a role. The human being is created, but is placed as khalīfah on earth. Night and day exist, but they are made signs. In some contexts jaʿala can come near the meaning of creation, especially with one object, but the distinction should not be erased automatically.

Difference from Nearby Roots

KH L Q is creating, measuring, and bringing the basic structure of a thing into being. Jaʿala is more about placing, assigning a role, or making something function in a certain way.

W Ḍ ʿ is putting down, placing, setting, or laying. Waḍʿ is closer to physical placing; jaʿala is wider and can create meaning, role, or status.

Ṣ Y R is becoming, turning into, and reaching a final state. Ṣayrūrah emphasizes the thing’s transformation into a state; jaʿala emphasizes the act of placing or making by an agent.

Ḥ W L is change, transfer, and shift of state or direction. Jaʿala may create a new state, but ḥawl emphasizes change and turning. N Ṣ B is setting up or appointing, often formally; jaʿala is broader. Q R R is settling and becoming established; jaʿala can place something in a state, while qarār emphasizes stability. S W Y is arranging, leveling, proportioning, and making even; jaʿala may come after taswiyah, but taswiyah emphasizes ordered proportion. B D L is replacing one thing with another; jaʿala may assign a role without replacement.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, جَعَلَ (jaʿala) should not always be translated with one English word. In contexts of role and position, “placed” or “appointed” may be best. In contexts of changed state, “made into” or “rendered” may fit. In contexts of public rank, “appointed” or “placed in the role of” is often clearer.

When signs are involved, “made as a sign” or “placed in the role of a sign” may be more precise. In contexts of shirk, “assigned” is often helpful, because the text is not saying that humans created real partners for The God; it is saying they placed something falsely in that rank. With one-object jaʿala, “established” may sometimes be better than “made.” With two-object jaʿala, the second object must be read as the assigned role, function, status, or state.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

جَعَلَ (jaʿala): placed, made, appointed, assigned, established, made into a state or role.

يَجْعَلُ (yajʿalu): places, makes, appoints, assigns, establishes.

جَعْل (jaʿl): placing, making, assignment, appointment, establishment.

جَاعِل (jāʿil): one who places, appoints, assigns, or establishes.

مَجْعُول / جُعِلَ (majʿūl / juʿila): made, appointed, assigned; it was made or placed.

جُعْل (juʿl): wage set for a task, compensation, appointed reward.

إِنِّي جَاعِلٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً (innī jāʿilun fī al-arḍi khalīfah): I am placing upon the earth a khalīfah; or I am placing a being on earth in the role of responsibility and succession.

وَجَعَلَ الظُّلُمَاتِ وَالنُّورَ (wajaʿala al-ẓulumāti wa-al-nūra): He established the darknesses and the light within the created order.

وَجَعَلْنَا اللَّيْلَ لِبَاسًا (wajaʿalnā al-layla libāsā): We made the night a covering; or We placed the night in the role of covering.

وَجَعَلُوا لِلَّهِ شُرَكَاءَ (wajaʿalū lillāhi shurakāʾa): they assigned partners to The God; or they seated beings in the rank of partners for The God.

Linguistic Summary

The root J AYN L (J ʿ L) denotes placing, making, appointing, assigning a role, establishing something within an order, and setting a thing in a particular state.

Materially, jaʿl is the act of placing something into a position or giving it a function. Structurally, it means that a thing, person, phenomenon, or concept is assigned a role: night is made a covering, night and day are made signs, the human being is placed as khalīfah, and Abraham is appointed as imam.

Its difference from khalaqa is that khalaqa is more about creating and measuring the basic structure of a thing, while jaʿala is more about role, position, state, and relation within an order. Its difference from waḍaʿa is that waḍaʿa is closer to putting down or laying, while jaʿala can also create meaning, function, and rank.

The central image is this: something is placed into a particular role, state, position, or relation, and from that point its meaning or function changes.
