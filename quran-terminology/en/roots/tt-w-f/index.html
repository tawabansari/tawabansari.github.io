The Qur’anic Root Ṭ W F

Going around, surrounding, circulation, and a group within a whole

The root ط و ف occurs 41 times in the Qur’an in six forms: eight occurrences of the simple verb طَافَ / يَطُوفُ, two of يَطَّوَّفَ, twenty-four of طَائِفَة, one of طَوَّافُون, two of طُوفَان, and four of طَائِف.

The concrete image in this family is moving around something or circulating in its surroundings. This appears in pre-Qur’anic poetry concerning the House and in Qur’anic passages about going around the House or moving among people. Alongside these verbs, طائفة names a group or part of a larger body, while طوفان names an overwhelming event. The historical formation of these two nouns requires consideration separately from the verb’s clear meaning.

Ṭawf does not always describe a complete, precise circle. Sometimes the movement is around a center, sometimes among people or between two places, and sometimes something passes over a person or place. The construction and the complement of the verb identify these differences.

An initial definition is:

Ṭ W F: in its verbal uses, going around something, circulating around or among people, or passing over a person or place. Ṭāʾif and ṭawwāf are connected with this field of movement and circulation. In the Qur’an, ṭāʾifah denotes a group or part of a larger body, and ṭūfān an overwhelming event; these nouns are understood through their own uses.

Early Arabic: going around a center

Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā, a pre-Qur’anic poet, says in his Muʿallaqah:

فَأَقْسَمْتُ بِالْبَيْتِ الَّذِي طَافَ حَوْلَهُ
رِجَالٌ بَنَوْهُ مِنْ قُرَيْشٍ وَجُرْهُمِ

“I swore by the House around which men of Quraysh and Jurhum, who built it, had gone.”

In طَافَ حَوْلَهُ, حَوْلَهُ explicitly identifies the path: the men went around the House. This attests the use of ṭawāf around the House in pre-Qur’anic poetry. Source: Zuhayr’s Muʿallaqah, beginning “أمن أم أوفى,” the verse “فأقسمت بالبيت…”.

In a verse attributed to Al-Aʿshā, a poet close to the period of revelation, a she-camel’s movement and energy are described through an image of طائف الجن. Early transmitted versions give the verse as follows:

وَتُصْبِحُ عَنْ غِبِّ السُّرَى وَكَأَنَّمَا
أَلَمَّ بِهَا مِنْ طَائِفِ الْجِنِّ أَوْلَقُ

“After traveling through the night, she comes to morning as though a frenzy from a visitation of the jinn had touched her.” The pronoun in بها refers to the she-camel; أَوْلَق expresses frenzy in this poetic image. طائف الجن is something that reaches or passes over her. The simile describes the intensity and energy of the camel’s movement. It is attributed to Al-Aʿshā, but its exact date of composition has not been established here. Source of transmission: Maqāyīs al-Lughah, entry طوف; this source is used here to transmit the verse.

The two examples attest both movement around a center and the image of something reaching or passing over another being. Their meanings are related, but these two verses alone do not establish the historical order in which the uses developed.

Going around the House and moving about at Ṣafā and Marwah

The form الطَّائِفِينَ refers directly to the House in two verses. Al-Ḥajj states:

وَإِذْ بَوَّأْنَا لِإِبْرَاهِيمَ مَكَانَ الْبَيْتِ أَنْ لَا تُشْرِكْ بِي شَيْئًا وَطَهِّرْ بَيْتِيَ لِلطَّائِفِينَ وَالْقَائِمِينَ وَالرُّكَّعِ السُّجُودِ

“And when We assigned to Abraham the place of the House: ‘Associate nothing with Me, and purify My House for those who go around it, those who stand, and those who bow and prostrate.’”

Al-Ḥajj 22:26

الطائفين here denotes those who go around the House. The same form occurs in Al-Baqarah 2:125.

The use concerning Ṣafā and Marwah also shows why ṭawf should not be defined solely as a complete circle:

إِنَّ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةَ مِنْ شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ فَمَنْ حَجَّ الْبَيْتَ أَوِ اعْتَمَرَ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِ أَنْ يَطَّوَّفَ بِهِمَا وَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ شَاكِرٌ عَلِيمٌ

“Ṣafā and Marwah are among the signs of The God. Whoever performs pilgrimage to the House or ʿumrah incurs no blame by moving about at the two of them. Whoever voluntarily does good—The God is appreciative, knowing.”

Al-Baqarah 2:158

يَطَّوَّفَ is form V: the tāʾ in يَتَطَوَّفَ has assimilated into the ṭāʾ. بِهِمَا refers to Ṣafā and Marwah. “Moving about at the two of them” expresses this connection; the verb alone does not specify the number of movements or the precise geometry of the route.

Al-Ḥajj 22:29 also states:

وَلْيَطَّوَّفُوا بِالْبَيْتِ الْعَتِيقِ

“And let them go around the ancient House.” Here the verb’s complement is the House; the imperative lām expresses the command.

The preposition and complement help identify the course of movement: بِالْبَيْتِ connects it with the House, while بِهِمَا connects it with the two signs, Ṣafā and Marwah.

Circulating among people: attendance and everyday movement

In several passages concerning the Hereafter, ṭawf describes those who circulate among the people of the Garden:

وَيَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ وِلْدَانٌ مُخَلَّدُونَ إِذَا رَأَيْتَهُمْ حَسِبْتَهُمْ لُؤْلُؤًا مَنْثُورًا

“Everlasting youths circulate among them. When you see them, you would think them scattered pearls.”

Al-Insān 76:19

Al-Ṭūr 52:24 likewise has يَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ غِلْمَانٌ لَهُمْ, and Al-Wāqiʿah 56:17 has يَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ وِلْدَانٌ مُخَلَّدُونَ.

In these verses, young attendants circulate among the people of the Garden. Their service is understood from the context of cups and drink; the verb itself expresses going around or circulating. A passive construction occurs in the same setting:

يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِكَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ

“A cup from a flowing spring is passed around among them.” — Al-Ṣāffāt 37:45

And:

وَيُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِآنِيَةٍ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ

“And vessels of silver are passed around among them.” — Al-Insān 76:15

Cups and vessels are circulated among the company and offered to them. The Qur’anic verbal forms illustrate this range of use.

Elsewhere, the people undergoing punishment are described as follows:

يَطُوفُونَ بَيْنَهَا وَبَيْنَ حَمِيمٍ آنٍ

“They go back and forth between it and scalding water.”

Al-Raḥmān 55:44

Here بَيْنَهَا وَبَيْنَ identifies the two sides of the movement. The pronoun ها refers to the Hell mentioned in the preceding verse. The verb describes going back and forth between it and scalding water.

The same field of meaning appears in everyday life:

طَوَّافُونَ عَلَيْكُمْ بَعْضُكُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ

Al-Nūr 24:58

The verse concerns seeking permission by those under the addressees’ authority and by children who have not reached puberty. After specifying three private times, طَوَّافُونَ عَلَيْكُمْ بَعْضُكُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ refers to their usual circulation and that of the household. In this context, ṭawwāfūn therefore denotes people who frequently move among others; it is not an exclusive title for servants as a class.

طَوَّافُونَ is the plural of طَوَّاف, on the pattern فَعَّال. The pattern’s sense of frequency or repetition fits the everyday movement described in the verse.

Ṭāʾif: something that passes over a person or place

The active participle طَائِف has four occurrences when its plural forms are included: twice as الطَّائِفِينَ for those going around the House, and twice as طَائِفٌ, in Al-Aʿrāf 7:201 and Al-Qalam 68:19. Four occurrences does not mean four instances of the singular form.

The account of the garden owners states:

فَطَافَ عَلَيْهَا طَائِفٌ مِنْ رَبِّكَ وَهُمْ نَائِمُونَ ۝ فَأَصْبَحَتْ كَالصَّرِيمِ

“Then, while they slept, a visitation from your Lord passed over it; by morning it was like a reaped field.”

Al-Qalam 68:19–20

Ṭāʾif does not identify precisely what came upon the garden; its destructive result appears in the next verse. The translation above renders كَالصَّرِيمِ as “like a reaped field.” Darkness or blackness is another proposed understanding of ṣarīm; the context makes clear that the garden has lost its produce. The verse does not explicitly name fire, wind, or a particular lightning strike.

Al-Aʿrāf states:

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا إِذَا مَسَّهُمْ طَائِفٌ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ تَذَكَّرُوا فَإِذَا هُمْ مُبْصِرُونَ

“Those who are mindful, when something from Satan touches them, remember, and then they see clearly.”

Al-Aʿrāf 7:201

“Something from Satan” is an explanatory rendering of طَائِفٌ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ. Ṭāʾif carries the image of something coming upon or passing over a person; its relation to Satan and the response of remembering and seeing clearly come from the context. “A temptation” may provide a contextual explanation, but brief duration or harmlessness does not follow from the active participle itself.

Alongside طَائِفٌ, a reading طَيْفٌ is also transmitted for Al-Aʿrāf 7:201. This article uses the reading ṭāʾif. The two forms matter for historical linguistics, but do not by themselves establish either the historical identity or independence of Ṭ W F and Ṭ Y F.

Ṭāʾifah: part of a larger body, without a fixed number

The most frequent form of this root in the Qur’an is طَائِفَة, with 24 occurrences.

In these occurrences it denotes a group or part of a larger body.

One particularly clear verse is:

وَمَا كَانَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ لِيَنْفِرُوا كَافَّةً فَلَوْلَا نَفَرَ مِنْ كُلِّ فِرْقَةٍ مِنْهُمْ طَائِفَةٌ لِيَتَفَقَّهُوا فِي الدِّينِ وَلِيُنْذِرُوا قَوْمَهُمْ إِذَا رَجَعُوا إِلَيْهِمْ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَحْذَرُونَ

“It is not for the believers to go forth all together. Why, then, should a contingent from every division of them not go forth, to gain a deep understanding of the dīn and warn their people when they return to them, so that they may be wary?”

Al-Tawbah 9:122

مِنْ كُلِّ فِرْقَةٍ مِنْهُمْ طَائِفَةٌ is especially revealing. Here ṭāʾifah is a part drawn from a larger body.

Al-Ḥujurāt states:

وَإِنْ طَائِفَتَانِ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ اقْتَتَلُوا فَأَصْلِحُوا بَيْنَهُمَا فَإِنْ بَغَتْ إِحْدَاهُمَا عَلَى الْأُخْرَىٰ فَقَاتِلُوا الَّتِي تَبْغِي حَتَّىٰ تَفِيءَ إِلَىٰ أَمْرِ اللَّهِ فَإِنْ فَاءَتْ فَأَصْلِحُوا بَيْنَهُمَا بِالْعَدْلِ وَأَقْسِطُوا إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُقْسِطِينَ

“If two groups of believers fight, reconcile them. If one of them transgresses against the other, fight the one that transgresses until it returns to the command of The God. If it returns, reconcile them justly and act equitably. The God loves those who act equitably.”

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:9

Here طائفتان clearly denotes two groups.

In Al-Tawbah 9:122, ṭāʾifah is used in relation to firqah; in Al-Ḥujurāt 49:9, ṭāʾifatān is used in relation to the body of believers. Neither construction supplies a fixed number within the word itself. Determining the number of people, if necessary for a particular question, requires other information.

Depending on the construction, suitable translations are:

“A group,”
“some of them,”
or “a contingent.”

A specific number should not be extracted from ṭāʾifah alone.

In Qur’anic usage, ṭāʾifah is an established word for a group or part of a larger body. Deriving its historical origin from an image of forming a circle requires independent evidence; its established group sense governs translation of these verses.

Ṭūfān: an overwhelming event and evidence from neighboring languages

طُوفَان occurs twice in the Qur’an.

In Noah’s account:

وَلَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا نُوحًا إِلَىٰ قَوْمِهِ فَلَبِثَ فِيهِمْ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ إِلَّا خَمْسِينَ عَامًا فَأَخَذَهُمُ الطُّوفَانُ وَهُمْ ظَالِمُونَ

“We sent Noah to his people, and he remained among them a thousand years less fifty. Then the overwhelming flood seized them while they were doing wrong.”

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:14

And in Pharaoh’s account:

فَأَرْسَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمُ الطُّوفَانَ وَالْجَرَادَ وَالْقُمَّلَ وَالضَّفَادِعَ وَالدَّمَ آيَاتٍ مُفَصَّلَاتٍ فَاسْتَكْبَرُوا وَكَانُوا قَوْمًا مُجْرِمِينَ

“We sent upon them the ṭūfān, locusts, lice, frogs, and blood as distinct signs. Yet they acted arrogantly and were a guilty people.”

Al-Aʿrāf 7:133

In Noah’s account, the Qur’an itself establishes the watery setting: Hūd 11:42 describes the vessel moving through waves like mountains, and Al-Qamar 54:11–12 describes water from the sky and the earth bursting with springs. “An overwhelming flood” for ṭūfān in Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:14 therefore has support within the Qur’an. In Al-Aʿrāf 7:133, ṭūfān is one of the signs sent, but its nature and details are not explained in that sentence.

Aramaic and Syriac evidence includes the closely resembling ṭawpānā / ṭōpānā, “deluge.” The Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon (CAL) cites, among other examples, ܡܝܐ ܕܛܘܦܢܐ, “the waters of the flood,” from the fourth-century writings of Aphrahat. This is pre-Qur’anic evidence for a similar word in a neighboring language, not an interpretation of the Arabic verse.

This attestation permits historical comparison, but similarity in form and meaning alone cannot establish either a direction of borrowing or a common origin. A possible semantic connection between ṭūfān and the Arabic family of going around and surrounding is likewise distinct from proving the word’s history.

English “storm” often suggests wind and atmospheric disturbance. In Noah’s account, “an overwhelming flood” expresses the watery setting of the Qur’anic narrative more clearly.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:133 does not itself explain the precise nature of the ṭūfān, so later narrative details should not be inserted into its translation. In Noah’s account, the wider Qur’anic context is consistent with flooding and drowning.

Related forms and the distinct meaning of each derivative

The uses examined here can be set alongside one another as follows:

طاف: went around something.

طائف: one who goes around, or something that comes upon or passes over a person or place.

طوّافون: people who frequently circulate around and among others.

طوفان: an overwhelming event; in Noah’s account, a flood that engulfs his people.

طائفة: a group or part of a larger body. Its Qur’anic meaning is directly recognizable in constructions such as مِنْ كُلِّ فِرْقَةٍ مِنْهُمْ طَائِفَةٌ. Understanding it does not require imagining every group as a circle in motion.

For ṭūfān, Aramaic and Syriac evidence calls for historical comparison; the word’s history cannot be settled simply by its resemblance to the Arabic verb ṭāfa.

A root’s core meaning can illuminate its family, but cannot replace examination of each derivative.

Ṭ W F and related expressions

Ṭawf and dawr

“Going around” and “turning” overlap in English. For a comparison within Arabic, Al-Aḥzāb 33:19 says تَدُورُ أَعْيُنُهُمْ, “their eyes roll.” This use of D W R describes the eyes’ revolving movement, whereas the following examples of Ṭ W F describe circulation in relation to people or places:

The attendants يَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ: circulate among them.

The people undergoing punishment يَطُوفُونَ بَيْنَهَا وَبَيْنَ حَمِيمٍ: go back and forth between two places.

For Ṣafā and Marwah, يَطَّوَّفَ بِهِمَا connects movement with two points.

These examples guide translation: with the House, “go around it”; with people, “circulate among them”; and with two places, “go back and forth between them.” These examples alone do not establish an absolute boundary between all uses of Ṭ W F and D W R.

Ṭūfān and sayl

The Qur’an also uses سيل for flooding, as in سَيْلَ الْعَرِمِ in Sabaʾ 34:16.

Sayl occurs in Al-Raʿd 13:17 alongside the flowing of valleys: فَاحْتَمَلَ السَّيْلُ زَبَدًا رَابِيًا, “the torrent carried rising foam.” This sentence depicts flowing water carrying foam; ṭūfān in Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:14 names the event that seizes Noah’s people. That contextual difference is clear, but does not by itself establish an absolute dictionary boundary between the words.

The possibility that two words refer to the same event does not make them completely synonymous.

Translation choices for Forqan

A single fixed translation does not suit every occurrence of طاف / يطوف.

With the House, a clear rendering is:

“Go around it.”

يَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ وِلْدَانٌ

“Youths circulate among and around them.”

يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِكَأْسٍ

“A cup is passed around among them,” or:

“A cup is circulated for them.”

يَطُوفُونَ بَيْنَهَا وَبَيْنَ حَمِيمٍ آنٍ

“They go back and forth between it and scalding water.”

For الطائفين in relation to the House:

“Those who go around the House.”

This is clearer for an unfamiliar reader than leaving ṭāʾifīn untranslated.

For طَوَّافُونَ عَلَيْكُمْ:

“They frequently move about among you.”

For طَائِفَة:

“A group”
or “some of them.”

The choice depends on the construction. No fixed number should be inserted into the translation.

For طَائِفٌ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ, “something from Satan that touches a person” is an explanatory rendering. “A temptation” may be discussed in explaining the context; qualifiers such as “fleeting,” in the sense of brief duration, are not established by the form ṭāʾif alone.

For طُوفَان in Noah’s account:

“An overwhelming flood.”

This conveys the context more precisely than “a storm.”

In Al-Aʿrāf 7:133, since the verse does not spell out the event’s nature, retaining ṭūfān, with an explanation such as “an overwhelming event or inundation,” is more cautious. Its precise nature should not be imported into the translation from external narratives.

Conclusion

The clearest image in the verbs of Ṭ W F is going around and circulating in the surroundings. Zuhayr’s verse makes the movement explicit through حَوْلَهُ, while Qur’anic passages express it through different complements and constructions.

The Qur’an shows that this movement is not always a complete circle. Attendants circulate among the people of the Garden, those undergoing punishment move between Hell and scalding water, vessels are passed around a company, and at Ṣafā and Marwah the movement concerns two signs.

Ṭāʾif may denote someone going around the House, or something that passes over a person or place. The nature and duration of its effect must be understood from context. The reading ṭayf in Al-Aʿrāf 7:201 also raises a historical question that the meaning of this verse alone cannot settle.

Ṭāʾifah, the root’s most frequent Qur’anic form, denotes “a group” or “part of a larger body” without a fixed numerical size. This established meaning should not be turned back into circular movement in every verse.

Ṭūfān in Noah’s account is connected with flooding and drowning. Pre-Qur’anic Aramaic and Syriac attestation provides a basis for historical comparison without by itself proving a direction of transmission.

Ṭ W F: in its verbs, going around, circulating, and passing over a person or place. Ṭāʾif and ṭawwāf are connected with this field of movement; ṭāʾifah denotes a group or part of a larger body, and ṭūfān names an overwhelming event. Each form is understood through its construction and Qur’anic use. Semantic relationships among forms should be kept distinct from definite claims about their historical derivation.
