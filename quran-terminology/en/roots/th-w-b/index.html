The Qur’anic Root TH W B

Thāba: returning and the return of an outcome

The root ث و ب, TH W B, has several important Qur’anic branches: ثَوَاب, thawāb, and مَثُوبَة, mathūbah, concern recompense and the outcome of action; أَثَابَ / ثُوِّبَ, athāba / thuwwiba, concern delivering or returning that outcome; مَثَابَة, mathābah, denotes a place of return; and ثِيَاب, thiyāb, means clothing.

“Reward” commonly suggests a favorable return for something good. The Qur’anic family has a wider range. The same family used for gardens, victory, and good reward also occurs concerning grief and even adverse recompense.

The clear core of the root’s main branch is returning and coming back.

In Arabic, ثَابَ يَثُوبُ, thāba yathūbu, means to return after departing. Of people, it could also describe coming and gathering again; of water, returning and collecting after being drawn away.

This field remains particularly clear in two Qur’anic forms:

مَثَابَة, mathābah: a place to which people return;

And:

ثَوَاب, thawāb: what comes back to a person as a consequence of action.

Lexical accounts of thawāb emphasize recompense or an outcome returning to the agent following an action. Its positive use nevertheless predominates in Arabic too. The scope of the whole family should not be confused with the frequency and semantic tendency of each individual form.

Arabic lexicons also place ثِيَاب, thiyāb, under this root. However, the precise historical connection between clothing and returning is less clear than the relationship between thawāb and mathābah. Explanations such as clothing repeatedly returning to the body, or threads returning in weaving, have been proposed. None should be treated as the word’s established origin without sufficient evidence.

It is therefore better to distinguish two levels within this family: the clear core of returning in thāba, mathābah, and thawāb; and the established clothing branch, thiyāb, whose precise historical relationship to that core remains more open.

Mathābah, thawāb, and athāba: morphological structure and meaning

The structures of these forms accord with their semantic field.

ثَوَاب, thawāb, follows the فَعَال, faʿāl, pattern. In the Qur’an, this noun describes what reaches a person as a consequence of action.

مَثَابَة, mathābah, is formed on the مَفْعَلَة, mafʿalah, pattern from ثَابَ يَثُوبُ, thāba yathūbu. Here its analysis as a noun of place accords with “a place of return.” Before the changes affecting the weak root consonant, its underlying structure contained the root’s wāw. The sources also discuss an analysis as a mīm-prefixed verbal noun. The pattern alone therefore does not conclusively establish a noun of place; structure and context together support the meaning “a place of return.”

Al-Baqarah states:

وَإِذْ جَعَلْنَا الْبَيْتَ مَثَابَةً لِلنَّاسِ وَأَمْنًا

Wa-idh jaʿalnā l-bayta mathābatan li-n-nāsi wa-amnā.

“When We made the House a place of return for people and a place of security.”

Al-Baqarah 2:125

Mathābah is more than simply “a gathering place.” People’s gathering may result from their repeated return, but the word is closer to a place to which people repeatedly come back.

أَثَابَ, athāba, belongs to form IV, traditionally called ifʿāl. Arabic uses it for returning something and for recompensing. The pattern is compatible with conveying an outcome to someone else, but the pattern alone should not generate an entire theory of thawāb. Actual Qur’anic usage matters more.

These three forms establish a clear relationship:

ثَابَ, thāba — to return; مَثَابَة, mathābah — a place of return; ثَوَاب / أَثَابَ, thawāb / athāba — the outcome or recompense that reaches a person following an action.

The thawāb family is not confined to favorable reward

The Qur’anic uses of the noun ثَوَاب, thawāb, belong to the field of reward, desirable benefit, and a good outcome. This article’s explicit examples of an adverse outcome involve the related forms athāba, mathūbah, and thuwwiba. That distinction must remain part of the argument.

Āl ʿImrān states:

وَمَنْ يُرِدْ ثَوَابَ الدُّنْيَا نُؤْتِهِ مِنْهَا ۚ وَمَنْ يُرِدْ ثَوَابَ الْآخِرَةِ نُؤْتِهِ مِنْهَا

Wa-man yurid thawāba d-dunyā nuʾtihi minhā wa-man yurid thawāba l-ākhirati nuʾtihi minhā.

“Whoever desires the reward of this world, We give him of it; and whoever desires the reward of the Hereafter, We give him of it.”

Āl ʿImrān 3:145

A few ayahs later:

فَآتَاهُمُ اللَّهُ ثَوَابَ الدُّنْيَا وَحُسْنَ ثَوَابِ الْآخِرَةِ

Fa-ātāhumu llāhu thawāba d-dunyā wa-ḥusna thawābi l-ākhirah.

“The God gave them the reward of this world and the excellence of the reward of the Hereafter.”

Āl ʿImrān 3:148

These ayahs establish two points. First, thawāb does not concern the Hereafter alone: there is also the reward of this world. Second, the Qur’an can say:

حُسْنَ ثَوَابِ الْآخِرَةِ

Ḥusna thawābi l-ākhirah.

That is, “the excellence of the reward of the Hereafter.”

Other ayahs also state:

ثَوَابًا مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ ۗ وَاللَّهُ عِنْدَهُ حُسْنُ الثَّوَابِ

Thawāban min ʿindi llāhi wa-llāhu ʿindahu ḥusnu th-thawāb.

Āl ʿImrān 3:195

And:

هُوَ خَيْرٌ ثَوَابًا وَخَيْرٌ عُقْبًا

Huwa khayrun thawāban wa-khayrun ʿuqbā.

Al-Kahf 18:44

“Reward” is a natural translation in such contexts because the outcome itself is favorable.

Āl ʿImrān 3:153, however, makes the family’s semantic range particularly clear:

إِذْ تُصْعِدُونَ وَلَا تَلْوُونَ عَلَىٰ أَحَدٍ وَالرَّسُولُ يَدْعُوكُمْ فِي أُخْرَاكُمْ فَأَثَابَكُمْ غَمًّا بِغَمٍّ

Idh tuṣʿidūna wa-lā talwūna ʿalā aḥadin wa-r-rasūlu yadʿūkum fī ukhrākum fa-athābakum ghamman bi-ghamm.

“When you were moving away, turning toward no one, while the Messenger called you from behind, He repaid you with grief for grief.”

Āl ʿImrān 3:153

What reaches them through أَثَابَكُمْ, athābakum, “He repaid you,” is:

غَمًّا

Ghammā.

Grief.

In this use, an outcome or recompense reaches a person, and that outcome is grief. The example shows that the family is not confined to pleasant reward; it does not by itself establish that every form lacks positive associations in every use. Some analyses treat the negative use as semantic extension; others understand it as figurative or as a rhetorical correspondence. غَمًّا بِغَمٍّ, ghamman bi-ghammin, has also been understood both as “grief for grief” and as “grief upon grief.” Establishing the relationship between the two instances of grief concerns context and sentence structure, not the meaning of athāba itself.

By contrast, the same verb occurs concerning gardens:

فَأَثَابَهُمُ اللَّهُ بِمَا قَالُوا جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ

Fa-athābahumu llāhu bi-mā qālū jannātin tajrī min taḥtihā l-anhār.

“The God rewarded them for what they said with gardens beneath which rivers flow.”

Al-Māʾidah 5:85

And concerning an opening or victory:

وَأَثَابَهُمْ فَتْحًا قَرِيبًا

Wa-athābahum fatḥan qarībā.

“He rewarded them with a near opening.”

Al-Fatḥ 48:18

The verb is the same, but what reaches a person can be grief, gardens, or an opening.

The same point appears in مَثُوبَة, mathūbah.

Al-Baqarah states:

وَلَوْ أَنَّهُمْ آمَنُوا وَاتَّقَوْا لَمَثُوبَةٌ مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ خَيْرٌ

Wa-law annahum āmanū wa-ttaqaw la-mathūbatun min ʿindi llāhi khayr.

“Had they believed and practiced taqwa, recompense from The God would have been better.”

Al-Baqarah 2:103

Al-Māʾidah, however, states:

قُلْ هَلْ أُنَبِّئُكُمْ بِشَرٍّ مِنْ ذَٰلِكَ مَثُوبَةً عِنْدَ اللَّهِ

Qul hal unabbiʾukum bi-sharrin min dhālika mathūbatan ʿinda llāh.

“Say: ‘Shall I tell you of something worse than that in recompense with The God?’”

Al-Māʾidah 5:60

Here مَثُوبَة, mathūbah, appears with شَرّ, sharr, “worse.”

Al-Muṭaffifīn also uses ثُوِّبَ, thuwwiba, the passive past-tense form of form II, traditionally called tafʿīl:

هَلْ ثُوِّبَ الْكُفَّارُ مَا كَانُوا يَفْعَلُونَ

Hal thuwwiba l-kuffāru mā kānū yafʿalūn.

“Have those who rejected been repaid for what they used to do?”

Al-Muṭaffifīn 83:36

Taken together, these examples prevent us from defining the entire TH W B family as “favorable reward.” At the same time, the noun thawāb’s positive tendency and the possibility of rhetorical usage in the negative examples must be preserved.

A more precise account is:

Within this family, recompense is something returning to the agent following an action. This branch can include an adverse outcome, although thawāb is more commonly associated with favorable reward.

“Reward” captures the positive tendency common in Arabic, but is insufficient as a translation for every Qur’anic form in this family.

Thiyāb: clothing and its figurative possibilities

Another branch of the family is ثِيَاب, thiyāb, the plural of thawb, meaning clothing or garments.

The Qur’anic uses in this sense are clear:

أَلَا حِينَ يَسْتَغْشُونَ ثِيَابَهُمْ يَعْلَمُ مَا يُسِرُّونَ وَمَا يُعْلِنُونَ

Alā ḥīna yastaghshūna thiyābahum yaʿlamu mā yusirrūna wa-mā yuʿlinūn.

“Indeed, when they cover themselves with their garments, He knows what they conceal and what they reveal.”

Hūd 11:5

In Nūḥ:

جَعَلُوا أَصَابِعَهُمْ فِي آذَانِهِمْ وَاسْتَغْشَوْا ثِيَابَهُمْ

Jaʿalū aṣābiʿahum fī ādhānihim wa-staghshaw thiyābahum.

“They put their fingers in their ears and covered themselves with their garments.”

Nūḥ 71:7

In Al-Nūr:

وَحِينَ تَضَعُونَ ثِيَابَكُمْ مِنَ الظَّهِيرَةِ

Wa-ḥīna taḍaʿūna thiyābakum mina ẓ-ẓahīrah.

“And when you put aside your garments at midday.”

Al-Nūr 24:58

Concerning the people of the Garden:

وَيَلْبَسُونَ ثِيَابًا خُضْرًا مِنْ سُنْدُسٍ وَإِسْتَبْرَقٍ

Wa-yalbasūna thiyāban khuḍran min sundusin wa-istabraq.

“They wear green garments of fine silk and brocade.”

Al-Kahf 18:31

And concerning the people of the Fire:

قُطِّعَتْ لَهُمْ ثِيَابٌ مِنْ نَارٍ

Quṭṭiʿat lahum thiyābun min nār.

“Garments of fire have been cut out for them.”

Al-Ḥajj 22:19

In these uses, thiyāb clearly means clothing. There is no need to insert an image of returning into every occurrence merely to preserve a shared root meaning.

The precise historical relationship between thawb and thāba also calls for caution. Their classification under one lexical root does not, by itself, establish that a historical development from “returning” to “clothing” can be reconstructed with certainty.

Wa-thiyābaka fa-ṭahhir

Al-Muddaththir states:

وَثِيَابَكَ فَطَهِّرْ

Wa-thiyābaka fa-ṭahhir.

“Purify your garments.”

Al-Muddaththir 74:4

The direct meaning of ثياب, thiyāb, is clothing. Older Arabic nevertheless shows that clothing could also acquire figurative possibilities in descriptions of a person and their condition.

A poem attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays states:

ثِيَابُ بَنِي عَوْفٍ طَهَارَى نَقِيَّةٌ
وَأَوْجُهُهُمْ عِنْدَ الْمَشَاهِدِ غُرَّانُ

Thiyābu banī ʿAwfin ṭahārā naqiyyatun / wa-awjuhuhum ʿinda l-mashāhidi ghurrān.

“The garments of Banū ʿAwf are pure and clean, and their faces shine in public gatherings.”

In such praise, clean clothing can extend beyond washed fabric to convey purity of reputation, conduct, or condition.

This linguistic possibility matters for وَثِيَابَكَ فَطَهِّرْ, wa-thiyābaka fa-ṭahhir, but two extremes should be avoided.

If thiyāb is translated from the outset only as “morals,” “heart,” or “deeds,” its direct meaning has been removed.

The existence of this figurative possibility does not, by itself, prove that the ayah must mean something other than actual garments. An explanation of the ayah should nevertheless acknowledge that linguistic possibility.

The basic translation is better retained as:

“Purify your garments.”

The possible extension of the image to purity of condition and conduct can then be examined in the explanation.

This approach preserves both the Qur’an’s wording and the possibilities of the Arabic language.

TH W B (thawāb), J Z Y (jazāʾ), and A J R (ajr): reward, recompense, and compensation

Thawāb, jazāʾ, and ajr concern the outcome of actions in many ayahs, and all three may sometimes be translated as “reward.” Their perspectives are not identical, however.

In ث و ب, TH W B (ثَوَاب, thawāb), the background of returning is prominent. Mathābah is a place of return, and thawāb is what reaches a person following an action.

ج ز ي, J Z Y (جَزَاء, jazāʾ), emphasizes the response to an action or its correspondence with a fitting outcome, which can explicitly be favorable or unfavorable.

أ ج ر, A J R (أَجْر, ajr), is closer to compensation or payment given for work. The details of its semantic field require examination of that root in its own right.

As general tendencies:

Thawāb — the return of an action’s outcome; jazāʾ — a fitting response to an action; ajr — compensation or payment given for an action.

These are not absolute boundaries. Different ayahs may describe a single outcome from more than one perspective, and “reward” may sometimes translate all three.

The important point is that the positive tendency of thawāb should not lead us to assume that every use within the family is positive. Expressions such as:

فَأَثَابَكُمْ غَمًّا بِغَمٍّ

Fa-athābakum ghamman bi-ghamm.

And:

بِشَرٍّ مِنْ ذَٰلِكَ مَثُوبَةً

Bi-sharrin min dhālika mathūbah.

Clearly show that the TH W B family also encompasses an unpleasant outcome.

Translation proposals for Forqan

ثَوَاب, thawāb, and its family should not be rendered mechanically with the same positive-sounding “reward” in every ayah. In English, “reward” ordinarily suggests a favorable return, often for a good act.

In positive contexts, “reward” is natural. For example, ثَوَابَ الْآخِرَةِ, thawāba al-ākhirah, can be “the reward of the Hereafter,” and حُسْنُ الثَّوَابِ, ḥusnu al-thawāb, “the excellence of the reward.”

In a general definition of the root, “recompense,” “the return on an action,” or “an outcome that comes back to someone following an action” better preserves the family’s range.

أَثَابَ, athāba, must likewise be translated according to its object and context. In فَأَثَابَهُمُ اللَّهُ ... جَنَّاتٍ, fa-athābahumu llāhu … jannātin, “The God rewarded them” is natural. In فَأَثَابَكُمْ غَمًّا بِغَمٍّ, fa-athābakum ghamman bi-ghammin, “He repaid you with grief for grief” better suits the adverse context. “Grief upon grief” is another analysis of the relationship between the two instances of grief; the difference comes from the preposition bi- and the context, not from athāba itself.

مَثُوبَة, mathūbah, can be “reward” in a positive context. In Al-Māʾidah 5:60, “recompense” is preferable so that a positive English association is not imposed on the ayah.

For مَثَابَة, mathābah, in Al-Baqarah 2:125, “a place of return” or “a place to which people continually return” better preserves the word’s core than “a gathering center.”

ثِيَاب, thiyāb, means “garments” in the Qur’an. In وَثِيَابَكَ فَطَهِّرْ, wa-thiyābaka fa-ṭahhir, the direct translation “purify your garments” is retained, while its figurative possibilities are examined in the explanation.

Conclusion

The clear core of TH W B’s main branch is returning and coming back. Analyzed as a noun of place, مَثَابَة, mathābah, in Al-Baqarah 2:125 denotes a place to which people return; an analysis as a mīm-prefixed verbal noun has also been proposed. ثَوَاب, thawāb, on the faʿāl pattern, and أَثَابَ, athāba, in form IV, concern an outcome or recompense reaching a person following an action.

The TH W B family is not confined to favorable reward. The Qur’an speaks of good reward, gardens, and an opening, but also uses related forms for “grief for grief,” adverse mathūbah, and the recompense of those who reject. This breadth is compatible with the noun thawāb’s positive tendency. The role of semantic extension or rhetorical expression in negative uses should not be overlooked.

The clothing sense of ثياب, thiyāb, is established in the Qur’an. Clothing’s figurative possibilities for condition, reputation, and conduct are attested in older Arabic and may inform the understanding of wa-thiyābaka fa-ṭahhir. They should not, however, remove the direct meaning of clothing from the translation. The precise historical relationship between this branch and returning should remain no more certain than the evidence allows.

A concise definition of the root can be stated as follows:

TH W B: in its main branch, returning and coming back. Within this field, mathābah is explained as a place of return, and thawāb / ithābah as recompense or an outcome reaching the agent following an action. The recompense family is not confined to a positive outcome, although thawāb tends toward favorable reward. Thiyāb is the clothing branch of this family, but the precise historical link to returning should remain open and cautiously assessed.

Mathūbah in Al-Baqarah 2:103
