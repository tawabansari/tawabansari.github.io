Principal meanings: mountain; a state, occasion, or stage; and, in some lexical uses, a limit or extent.

1. The root in early Arabic

The triliteral root ط و ر has several semantic branches in early Arabic. Two occur in the Quran: طُور, “mountain,” and طَوْر, “state, occasion, or stage.” They look identical without vowel marks, but differ in pronunciation and usage.

طَوْر as a state or occasion: in pre-Islamic Arabic, طَوْر could denote a particular occasion or condition. طَوْرًا بَعْدَ طَوْرٍ could mean one occasion after another or one state after another. أَطْوَار accordingly denotes various conditions or states.

A witness occurs in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī:

تَنَاذَرَهَا الرَّاقُونَ مِنْ سُوءِ سَمِّهَا

tanādharahā al-rāqūna min sūʾi sammihā

تُطَلِّقُهُ طَوْرًا وَطَوْرًا تُرَاجِعُ

tuṭalliquhu ṭawran wa-ṭawran turājiʿu

Translation: The incantation healers warned one another of the serpent's dangerous venom; at one time it released the sufferer, and at another it returned.

The poet describes a snakebite victim whose suffering subsides and then intensifies again. طَوْرًا وَطَوْرًا denotes alternating occasions or states, rather than two necessarily progressive stages.

The verse belongs to Al-Nābighah's well-known poem expressing fear of Al-Nuʿmān's threat and is cited in early literary sources. Its attribution supports a pre-Quranic use, although the precise date of composition is not independently established.

The semantic result is clear: طَوْر could denote a particular occasion or condition among different conditions. These might be successive, alternating, or diverse. The word does not inherently signify improvement or evolution.

طُور as mountain: another branch is طُور, “mountain.” Some early lexicographers and commentators associate it with Syriac. Arabic sources use it both for a mountain and for the familiar mountain associated with Moses.

This linguistic association should be distinguished from a demonstrated history of borrowing. A similar form in another language alone does not prove that طُور, “mountain,” and طَوْر, “state or occasion,” developed from one original meaning.

The Quran itself clearly attests the mountain sense in the language of revelation. The poetic evidence examined here establishes the occasion sense rather than the mountain sense; it does not justify deriving either meaning from the other or declaring that no pre-Islamic mountain usage existed.

2. Lexical forms and semantic branches

طَوْر and أَطْوَار: states, occasions, and stages

In this branch, طَوْر denotes a particular state or occasion, with أَطْوَار as its plural.

النَّاسُ أَطْوَارٌ means that people have various conditions, characteristics, or situations. أطوار therefore need not denote successive stages of one individual's life; it can describe differences among people.

When something changes over time, طَوْر can denote one stage in that change. Childhood, youth, and old age, for example, are distinct conditions of human life.

The stage sense emerges from the relationship between conditions and time. طَوْر primarily expresses a particular state or occasion.

طُور: mountain

In the geographical branch, طُور means a mountain. Its Quranic uses are commonly understood as referring to the familiar mountain associated with Moses.

In طُورِ سَيْنَاءَ and طُورِ سِينِينَ, طور supplies the mountain designation, while the following word identifies it more specifically.

Some lexical explanations restrict طور to a tree-bearing mountain, while others define it simply as a mountain. Such qualifications should not be treated as necessary in every use. Holiness and association with revelation likewise require the name or context, rather than following from the independent lexical meaning alone.

طَوْر and طَوَار: limit and extent

Arabic dictionaries also record طَوْر for a limit, measure, or extent.

عَدَا طَوْرَهُ means that someone overstepped their limit or proper measure.

طَوَار can denote an extension or adjoining area, as in طَوَارُ الدَّارِ, the space extending alongside a house.

These uses concern a defined limit. A semantic relationship between a particular condition and its boundaries is conceivable, but it does not establish that all meanings originated in “boundary.”

طَارَ يَطُورُ: circling and approaching

Classical Arabic also records طَارَ يَطُورُ for moving around something or approaching it.

لَا أَطُورُ بِهِ means “I do not approach him/it,” while يَطُورُ بِفُلَانٍ may describe circling around or coming near someone.

This is distinct from طَارَ يَطِيرُ, from ط ي ر, “to fly.” An identical past-tense form does not establish an identical root or meaning.

Modern تَطَوُّر, used for transformation, development, or gradual change, belongs to this word family. Its modern scientific and philosophical uses should not be projected onto all historical derivatives or Quranic أطوار without evidence.

3. Quranic occurrences

The root occurs eleven times: ten as طُور, “mountain,” and once as أَطْوَارًا, “various states or stages.”

These uses are considered in two distinct branches.

A. طُور: the mountain associated with Moses

1. The mountain and the covenant with the Children of Israel

وَإِذْ أَخَذْنَا مِيثَاقَكُمْ وَرَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ خُذُوا مَا آتَيْنَاكُمْ بِقُوَّةٍ وَاذْكُرُوا مَا فِيهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ

wa-idh akhadhnā mīthāqakum wa-rafaʿnā fawqakumu al-ṭūra khudhū mā ātaynākum bi-quwwatin wa-udhkurū mā fīhi laʿallakum tattaqūn

Al-Baqarah 2:63

And when We took your covenant and raised the Mount above you: Hold firmly to what We have given you, and remember what it contains, so that you may be mindful.

Al-Baqarah 2:63

الطُّور here is the mountain mentioned in the covenant episode.

The expression recurs in Al-Baqarah 2:93. Al-Nisāʾ 4:154 likewise speaks of raising the Mount above them in connection with their covenant.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:171 uses الْجَبَلَ for a similar event. This connection confirms the mountain sense in this context and its overlap with جبل.

رَفَعْنَا and فَوْقَكُمُ express raising and being above. Those meanings do not come from طور itself: the noun names the mountain, while the surrounding wording specifies what happens to it.

2. The mountain and the call to Moses

وَنَادَيْنَاهُ مِنْ جَانِبِ الطُّورِ الْأَيْمَنِ وَقَرَّبْنَاهُ نَجِيًّا

wa-nādaynāhu min jānibi al-ṭūri al-aymani wa-qarrabnāhu najiyyā

Maryam 19:52

And We called him from the right side of the Mount and brought him near in intimate converse.

Maryam 19:52

الطُّور identifies the mountain associated with the divine call to Moses.

جَانِبِ الطُّورِ الْأَيْمَنِ also appears in Ṭā Hā 20:80, which speaks of saving the Children of Israel and appointing a meeting on the Mount's right side.

In Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:29, Moses sees a fire on the side of the Mount while travelling with his family. Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:46 also refers to the mountain and the divine call.

In these passages, طور is a geographical place associated with events concerning Moses. Calling, appointment, nearness, and converse are expressed by other words.

الْأَيْمَن identifies a particular side or direction; it is not part of the root meaning of ط و ر.

3. Mount Sinai and the tree growing from it

وَشَجَرَةً تَخْرُجُ مِنْ طُورِ سَيْنَاءَ تَنْبُتُ بِالدُّهْنِ وَصِبْغٍ لِلْآكِلِينَ

wa-shajaratan takhruju min ṭūri saynāʾa tanbutu bi-l-duhni wa-ṣibghin li-l-ākilīn

Al-Muʾminūn 23:20

And a tree that grows from Mount Sinai, producing oil and a relish for those who eat.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:20

The verse occurs among descriptions of rain, plants, gardens, and edible blessings.

طُورِ سَيْنَاءَ designates the place associated with the tree. طور means mountain, and سيناء identifies it.

The description of oil and a food accompaniment is commonly understood to refer to the olive tree. The word “olive” itself does not appear in this verse; the identification comes from the description and other evidence.

طور thus appears not only in accounts of the divine call or the covenant, but also in the description of a natural blessing.

4. The Mount in oaths

وَالطُّورِ

wa-l-ṭūr

Al-Ṭūr 52:1

By the Mount.

Al-Ṭūr 52:1

The surah opens by placing the mountain among other subjects of an oath.

وَ functions as an oath particle. الطُّورِ is commonly understood here as the mountain associated with Moses, although the verse itself supplies no further name. Swearing by a mountain does not change its lexical meaning into “holy” or “place of revelation”; that significance is established through context and interpretation.

وَطُورِ سِينِينَ

wa-ṭūri sīnīn

Al-Tīn 95:2

And by Mount Sīnīn.

Al-Tīn 95:2

Here too, طور occurs in a geographical construction.

سِينِينَ differs in spelling from سَيْنَاءَ. Both expressions are commonly associated with the same mountain or region connected with Moses, although several lexical explanations have been proposed for the names themselves.

For ط و ر, the relevant point is طُور as mountain. Proposed meanings of Sīnīn or Sīnāʾ should not be attributed to this root.

The ten mountain occurrences are Al-Baqarah 2:63, Al-Baqarah 2:93, Al-Nisāʾ 4:154, Maryam 19:52, Ṭā Hā 20:80, Al-Muʾminūn 23:20, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:29, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:46, Al-Ṭūr 52:1, and Al-Tīn 95:2.

All belong to the mountain branch. The surrounding narratives or familiar interpretation connect them with the known location.

B. أَطْوَارًا: states and stages of human creation

وَقَدْ خَلَقَكُمْ أَطْوَارًا

wa-qad khalaqakum aṭwārā

Nūḥ 71:14

While He has created you in various states and stages.

Nūḥ 71:14

This is the sole Quranic occurrence of أَطْوَار.

أَطْوَارًا is the plural of طَوْر and describes the varied conditions of humans in their creation.

The phrase occurs in Noah's address to his people. Nūḥ 71:13 asks why they do not accord The God due greatness; the following reference to human creation in varied conditions directs attention to divine power and ordering. وقار here concerns greatness and reverence, not merely personal composure.

The following verses discuss the heavens, moon, and sun, then humans being brought forth from the earth and returned to it, including Nūḥ 71:17 and Nūḥ 71:18.

What exactly does أَطْوَارًا convey?

Lexically, أَطْوَارًا denotes diverse states, forms, or stages. With خَلَقَكُمْ, it can refer to differing human conditions through creation and growth.

Other passages, including Al-Ḥajj 22:5 and Ghāfir 40:67, describe stages of human formation and life. Successive changes in human creation are therefore a clear instance of أطوار.

Nūḥ 71:14 does not itself name these stages. Restricting its translation to embryonic stages or inserting a specific sequence of sperm-drop, clinging substance, and lump goes beyond this verse's wording.

The lexical range is not limited to temporal changes: differences in people's forms, conditions, and characteristics also belong to it. In the context of خَلَقَكُمْ, stages and conditions of human creation nevertheless form a natural reading supported by other Quranic passages.

Does أَطْوَارًا mean evolution?

The modern Persian term for evolution can mean gradual perfection, biological development, or a particular scientific theory of the transformation of living organisms.

أَطْوَار alone does not require any of these specialized meanings.

Something can move from one condition to another without the later condition being better in every respect. In Al-Nābighah's poem, طَوْرًا وَطَوْرًا describes alternating pain and relief, not progress or evolution.

“In various states and stages” therefore respects the lexical scope of Nūḥ 71:14 without imposing a modern technical meaning. Its relationship to scientific theories is a separate question from the definition of the word.

4. Morphological and semantic observations

Vowel differences matter in this family.

طُور, with ḍammah on the first consonant, is the established mountain form and appears ten times with this meaning.

طَوْر, with fatḥah followed by a silent wāw, denotes an occasion, condition, or stage; its plural is أَطْوَار.

In Nūḥ 71:14, أَطْوَارًا is indefinite and accusative, describing varied conditions of human creation. It should not be read here as a plural of طُور, “mountain”: the context calls for conditions or stages.

The mountain occurrences have accusative or genitive endings according to their grammatical role, while retaining the base form طُور. الطُّورَ and الطُّورِ differ in case, not root meaning.

In طُورِ سَيْنَاءَ, طور is the first noun in a genitive construction and سيناء the dependent noun. طُورِ سِينِينَ has the same construction. These identify a location; Sīnāʾ and Sīnīn are not additional derivatives of ط و ر.

5. Related roots

Ṭ W R and M R R: condition and passing

م ر ر, in its passing and movement uses, emphasizes something moving or elapsing. مَرَّة can denote one occurrence or occasion.

طَوْر in the branch under discussion can identify the particular state or condition itself.

Saying that someone did something twice counts occurrences. Speaking of the أطوار of their life emphasizes its differing states and circumstances.

The two semantic fields overlap without being identical.

Ṭ W R and J B L: mountain and the identified mountain

جَبَل is the general Arabic word for mountain and is used for various mountains in the Quran.

طُور also means mountain, but its Quranic usage is associated with the familiar mountain and events concerning Moses.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:171 uses الجبل, while Al-Baqarah 2:63 uses الطور in the covenant narrative. Both carry the mountain meaning.

The distinction here is chiefly between the general noun جبل and الطور referring to the familiar mountain. It does not establish “holy mountain” as the necessary lexical meaning of طور.

Ṭ W R and Ṭ W D: mountain and an immense mountain

ط و د also concerns mountains. طَوْد characteristically denotes a tall, immense mountain.

In Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:63, the separated water is compared to الطَّوْدِ الْعَظِيمِ. Magnitude and height dominate that image.

طُور in Quranic usage chiefly functions as a mountain designation or an identified geographical reference.

طود is suited to emphasizing a mountain's scale, whereas طور chiefly identifies a particular mountain in these contexts. This does not imply that the Mount cannot itself be immense.

Ṭ W R and N SH ʾ: condition and coming into being

ن ش أ concerns coming into being, emergence, and growth.

النَّشْأَةَ الْأُولَىٰ in Al-Wāqiʿah 56:62 concerns humans' first coming into being.

أَطْوَارًا describes the diverse conditions something can have during creation or life.

نشأة can emphasize origination or a phase of emergence, whereas أطوار emphasizes the plurality and diversity of conditions. They may be related in discussing creation without being synonyms.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

For طُور in the Moses and Children of Israel passages, retaining “Ṭūr” is possible because it identifies the familiar mountain. “Mount Ṭūr” at first occurrence can make its geographical meaning clear; English may also use “the Mount.”

طُورِ سَيْنَاءَ can be rendered “Mount Sinai” or “Ṭūr Sīnāʾ.” For طُورِ سِينِينَ, “Mount Sīnīn” preserves the Quranic name, leaving its association with Sinai to the lexical discussion.

For non-Quranic طَوْر, “occasion,” “state,” “condition,” or “stage” may fit the context.

For أَطْوَارًا in Nūḥ 71:14, the proposed translation is:

“While He has created you in various states and stages.”

This preserves the word's range without restricting it to one instance, such as embryonic stages.

Brief root meaning: two principal branches: mountain; state, occasion, and stage. Other lexical uses include limit and extent.

Meaning in the Moses passages: the Mount, the familiar mountain associated with events concerning Moses and the Children of Israel.

Meaning in Nūḥ 71:14: the various states and stages of human creation.

7. Conclusion

ط و ر has several semantic branches in Arabic that cannot confidently be reduced to one historically demonstrated original meaning.

طَوْر as a state or occasion is attested in poetry attributed to Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī. It can denote one occurrence of a condition or a state distinct from another without requiring progress or evolution.

طُور as mountain is associated in the Quran with the familiar mountain connected with Moses. Early sources also note an association with Syriac, but this should not become a claim that all meanings of ط و ر are certainly derived from one another.

The Quran uses طُور ten times for mountain and أَطْوَارًا once for the conditions and stages of human creation. Modern biological evolution and holiness as an inseparable lexical property of mountain are not directly entailed by the root.

Final definition: the principal uses divide into طُور, “mountain,” and طَوْر, a particular state, occasion, or stage among diverse conditions. In the Quran, the first concerns the familiar mountain associated with Moses; the second, in the plural أَطْوَار, describes the various states and stages of human creation.
