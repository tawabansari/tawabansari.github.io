Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root SH Y A

To will, want, or have volition; and in Qur’anic usage, for a matter to enter the field of will, possibility, and realization, especially in relation to The God

Important Forms from This Root

شَاءَ / يَشَاءُ (shāʾa / yashāʾu)

shāʾa / yashāʾu; willed, wants, wills, or places within will and possibility, depending on human or divine context.

تَشَاءُونَ (tashāʾūn)

tashāʾūn; you will, you want, you choose.

شِئْتَ / شَاءُوا (shiʾta / shāʾū)

shiʾta; you willed. Shāʾū; they willed.

مَشِيئَة (mashīʾah)

mashīʾah; will, divine willing, or the governing will over possibility and realization.

Method Note

The root SH Y A is one of the Qur’an’s sensitive roots because it touches will, human choice, guidance, misguidance, the future, and the relation between the human being and The God. If it is read superficially, one may fall into absolute determinism. If it is separated from the Qur’an’s tawḥīd, one may imagine human independence from The God. So human willing, divine will, permission, command, measure, and moral approval must be distinguished.

When شَاءَ (shāʾa) is attributed to the human being, it points to wanting and choosing. The human being wills, chooses a path, and remains responsible. But when shāʾa is attributed to The God, it is not merely a psychological desire. It is the governing will over possibility and realization. Nothing happens independently of The God or outside His knowledge, power, and way.

Divine will in the order of existence must not be confused with The God’s moral approval. The God may allow a bad choice to occur within the field of testing, but He does not approve that choice. Denial, wrongdoing, and corruption occur in the world, but the Qur’an does not describe them as true, good, or beloved to The God.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of SH Y A is willing and placing a matter in the direction of intent. When a human being wants something, that thing enters the field of attention, intention, and choice. But between wanting and realization there is distance, because the human being does not control all causes, time, ability, and outcome.

In relation to The God, شَاءَ (shāʾa) comes close to the will governing the field of realization. Nothing occurs in the world unless it has a path of realization within The God’s knowledge, power, and will. Yet this divine order also contains the field of human choice. Human willing is real, but it is not independent or self-sufficient apart from The God.

Material Image

The material image may be seen in the moment a person decides to do something. Before the decision, the act is at the edge of attention. When will appears, the mind, attention, and movement turn toward that act. Still, wanting alone is not enough. A person may want and be unable; want and not find the time; want and lack the necessary causes.

At the level of creation and realization, divine will is like the opening of the field of possibility for an event. This image is close to permission, but not identical. Permission shows the opening of a path for effect; mashīʾah, divine will, shows the governing will over possibility and direction of realization.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, شَاءَ (shāʾa) is a verb of willing and wanting. مَشِيئَة (mashīʾah) is its verbal noun. The word shares a field with أَرَادَ (arāda), to intend or want, but in Qur’anic usage, especially when applied to The God, it is strongly linked to possibility, realization, and sovereignty over the outcome.

The relation between شَاءَ (shāʾa) and شَيْء (shayʾ), “thing,” should be handled carefully. Their letters are close, and some lexical discussions mention the relation between them, but this entry does not need to build a philosophical argument from “willing” to “thingness.” For Forqan’s method, it is enough to study shāʾa in the field of will, intent, and possible realization, while shayʾ should be studied separately in its own place.

Qur’anic Usage

In Shāʾa Allāh and the Future’s Dependence on The God’s Will

In وَلَا تَقُولَنَّ لِشَيْءٍ إِنِّي فَاعِلٌ ذَٰلِكَ غَدًا إِلَّا أَن يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ (walā taqūlanna lishayʾin innī fāʿilun dhālika ghadan illā an yashāʾa allāh), the Messenger is taught not to speak of doing something tomorrow without connecting it to The God’s will 18:23 and 18:24. This is not only etiquette of speech. It is a reminder of the human position before the future.

The human being plans, but does not own the future. Health, opportunity, time, tools, conditions, and outcome are not under his absolute control. إِن شَاءَ اللَّهُ (in shāʾa allāh) means: I intend it, but its realization depends on The God’s will and opening of possibility.

Human Will Within The God’s Will

In وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَن يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ (wamā tashāʾūna illā an yashāʾa allāhu rabbu al-ʿālamīn), human will is not denied; it is placed within The God’s will 81:29. The verse does not say that you have no will. It says your will is not independent of the will of the Lord of the worlds.

This verse prevents two errors. The first is imagining human independence from The God. The second is imagining that because human will depends on The God, the human being has no responsibility. The Qur’an rejects both. Human will is real, but it operates within a greater order.

Human Will Toward the Straight Path

The verse before it says: لِمَن شَاءَ مِنكُمْ أَن يَسْتَقِيمَ (liman shāʾa minkum an yastaqīm); for whoever among you wills to be upright 81:28. This shows that guidance is not forced upon the human being. A person must will, respond, and choose direction.

The pairing of these two verses is essential. First: for whoever wills to be upright. Then: you do not will unless The God wills. Together, they place human responsibility within divine will. They do not create absolute compulsion, and they do not affirm absolute human independence.

Law Shāʾa Allāh and Other Possible Arrangements

The expression لَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ (law shāʾa allāh) appears often in the Qur’an. For example: وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَجَعَلَكُمْ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً وَلَٰكِن لِّيَبْلُوَكُمْ فِي مَا آتَاكُمْ (walaw shāʾa allāhu lajaʿalakum ummatan wāḥidatan walākin liyabluwakum fī mā ātākum); if The God had willed, He would have made you one community, but He tests you in what He has given you 5:48.

This verse shows that the present form of the world was not the only possible arrangement. The God could have placed another structure. But His will placed human beings in a field of testing, differing capacities, and racing toward good. Difference is not outside His power; it becomes meaningful within the wisdom of testing.

Divine Will, Guidance, and Misguidance

Many verses say that The God guides whom He wills and lets go astray whom He wills, such as يَهْدِي مَن يَشَاءُ وَيُضِلُّ مَن يَشَاءُ (yahdī man yashāʾu wayuḍillu man yashāʾ). If read apart from the whole Qur’an, such verses may be misunderstood as arbitrary determinism. But the Qur’an itself clarifies that misguidance is not without cause or justice.

The God leaves in misguidance, or increases in misguidance, the defiantly corrupt, the wrongdoers, the stubborn deniers, and those who reject truth. Therefore, مَن يَشَاءُ (man yashāʾ) in these contexts does not mean ruleless desire. Divine will is joined to knowledge, justice, wisdom, and The God’s ways.

Divine Will and the Rejection of Forced Faith

In وَلَوْ شَاءَ رَبُّكَ لَآمَنَ مَن فِي الْأَرْضِ كُلُّهُمْ جَمِيعًا أَفَأَنتَ تُكْرِهُ النَّاسَ حَتَّىٰ يَكُونُوا مُؤْمِنِينَ (walaw shāʾa rabbuka la-āmana man fī al-arḍi kulluhum jamīʿan afa-anta tukrihu al-nāsa ḥattā yakūnū muʾminīn), the Qur’an says that if your Lord had willed, everyone on earth would have believed altogether; then it asks whether you would compel people until they become believers 10:99.

This verse is one of the keys to understanding divine will. The God could have made faith compulsory, but He did not. His will placed faith in the field of invitation, understanding, choice, and response, not coercion. Divine will in the Qur’an is therefore tied to the freedom of human testing.

Will and Creation

In يَخْلُقُ مَا يَشَاءُ (yakhluqu mā yashāʾ), The God creates what He wills 3:47. Here will is tied to creation. The God is not subject to the will of any other being in creation. He governs the possibilities of existence and creates what He wills within His knowledge and wisdom.

In this context, شَاءَ (shāʾa) is not human wanting. It is Creatorly will: the coming-into-existence of something depends on The God’s will and power, not on an external necessity above Him.

Will, Provision, Mercy, and Favor

The Qur’an repeatedly says that The God provides for whom He wills, gives mercy, forgives, or opens His favor to whom He wills. These expressions must be read alongside The God’s justice and wisdom. مَن يَشَاءُ (man yashāʾ) means that The God is not bound by outward human measures and expectations in giving and opening, but this does not mean rulelessness or injustice.

Divine will in these contexts reminds the human being that he does not own provision, mercy, or outcome absolutely. The God knows capacities, times, causes, and consequences, and He gives according to what He knows.

The Difference Between Divine Will and Moral Approval

One of the most important points in this root is that something occurring by The God’s will does not mean The God morally approves of it. Wrongdoing, denial, association, and corruption occur in the world, but the Qur’an does not approve of them and commands against them.

The verse وَلَا يَرْضَىٰ لِعِبَادِهِ الْكُفْرَ (walā yarḍā liʿibādihi al-kufr) makes the distinction clear 39:7. If someone chooses denial, that choice has been allowed to occur within the field of testing, but The God does not approve it for His servants. Divine will in the order of occurrence must be distinguished from moral approval.

Difference from Nearby Words

Irādah is will, intent, and directional wanting. It often shares a field with mashīʾah, but in Qur’anic usage, especially regarding The God, mashīʾah is more strongly connected to possibility, realization, and authority over the outcome.

Idhn is permission: the opening of a path for effect or occurrence. Something acts only within The God’s permission. Shāʾa points to the governing will over possibility. Amr is command; it can be legislative, such as the command to justice, or creative, such as كُن (kun), “Be.”

Qadar is measuring, determining, and structuring the terms of realization. Mashīʾah is related to qadar but is not identical to it: mashīʾah is governing will, while qadar is measure and structure. Riḍā is moral approval and pleasure, and it must be carefully distinguished from divine will.

Translation Method

When translating شَاءَ (shāʾa), “willed” or “wanted” is often correct, but not always enough. For human beings, “wanted” or “willed” is natural. For The God, depending on context, one may say “willed,” “placed within His will,” or “brought within His governing will.” Still, translation should avoid heavy philosophical language that takes the text away from the Qur’an’s directness.

For إِن شَاءَ اللَّهُ (in shāʾa allāh), the meaning should remain alive in the reader’s mind: “if The God wills” means if the matter is placed within The God’s will and opening of possibility. For وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَن يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ (wamā tashāʾūna illā an yashāʾa allāh), both human willing and its dependence on The God must be preserved.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

إِن شَاءَ اللَّهُ (in shāʾa allāh): if The God wills; if it is within The God’s will.

وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَن يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ (wamā tashāʾūna illā an yashāʾa allāh): you do not will unless The God wills; your willing is not outside The God’s will.

لِمَن شَاءَ مِنكُمْ أَن يَسْتَقِيمَ (liman shāʾa minkum an yastaqīm): for whoever among you wills to take the straight and upright path.

وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَجَعَلَكُمْ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً (walaw shāʾa allāhu lajaʿalakum ummatan wāḥidah): if The God had willed, He would have made you one community.

وَلَوْ شَاءَ رَبُّكَ لَآمَنَ مَن فِي الْأَرْضِ كُلُّهُمْ جَمِيعًا (walaw shāʾa rabbuka la-āmana man fī al-arḍi kulluhum jamīʿā): if your Lord had willed, everyone on earth would have believed altogether.

يَخْلُقُ مَا يَشَاءُ (yakhluqu mā yashāʾ): He creates what He wills; He creates what He wills within His will.

يَهْدِي مَن يَشَاءُ (yahdī man yashāʾ): He guides whom He wills. Forqan note: this will is joined in the Qur’an to divine knowledge, justice, wisdom, and ways.

Summary

The root SH Y A in the Qur’an is the language of will and divine willing. The human being wills and remains responsible, but his will is not independent of The God. The God wills, and His will governs the field of possibility, realization, guidance, provision, creation, and the future. Yet divine will must not be confused with moral approval. The God may allow certain bad choices to occur within the field of testing, but He does not approve them or command them.

In Forqan’s reading, شَاءَ (shāʾa) means that a matter enters the field of will. For the human being, this will is limited, responsible, and dependent. For The God, mashīʾah is the governing will over possibility and realization: a will that does not erase human choice, but places it within a larger order of knowledge, justice, wisdom, and testing.
