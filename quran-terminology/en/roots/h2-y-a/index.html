Root ه ي أ — Form and preparation

Qur’anic and lexical forms

هَيِّئْ / يُهَيِّئْ
(hayyiʾ / yuhayyiʾ)
Preparing and providing

هَيْئَة
(hayʾah)
Form and shape

From a thing’s shape and condition to arranging suitable circumstances for an undertaking

Arabic ه ي أ concerns form, shape, condition, and arrangement. Its verb hayyaʾa means preparing, organizing, and providing suitable conditions.

The Qur’an uses two clear branches: hayʾah describes the form made from clay in Jesus’s story; hayyiʾ and yuhayyiʾ concern arranging favorable circumstances in the story of the Companions of the Cave.

The senses are related: something has a particular condition, and preparing it can mean placing it in a condition suitable for a purpose.

Having a form and preparing something remain different. One describes a condition; the other an action arranging suitable conditions.

1. Early Arabic meaning

Classical hayʾah denotes form, shape, state, and manner.

It can describe human appearance, objects’ shapes, and their arrangement.

Ḥasanu al-hayʾati describes someone well presented and pleasing in appearance.

Hayʾatu al-shayʾi denotes a thing’s form or condition.

Hayʾah does not inherently mean beauty. A shape can be beautiful or ugly, orderly or disorderly.

Beauty, elegance, or unattractiveness comes from qualifications and context.

Hayyaʾa: preparing and arranging

Hayyaʾa al-amra means preparing, organizing, and making an undertaking ready.

Hayyaʾa al-shayʾa likewise concerns preparing something for a purpose.

One may prepare accommodation, assemble equipment, or arrange a journey.

Preparation is not merely outward reshaping: an object may remain unchanged while the means and circumstances for using it become available.

A room may already exist but be unsuitable for living. Water, lighting, bedding, and essentials prepare it for habitation.

Its structure already exists; its condition and usability change.

This illustrates the relationship between hayʾah and tahyiʾah without establishing which meaning historically came first.

Tahayyaʾa: becoming ready

Tahayyaʾa li-al-amri means becoming ready for an undertaking.

It differs from hayyaʾa in the direction of the action.

In hayyaʾa, an agent prepares something.

In tahayyaʾa, the person or thing becomes ready.

Someone may prepare travel equipment and then get ready to depart.

The actions are related but differ in their formation and attribution.

Classical sources also record the simple verb hāʾa for becoming ready or adopting a suitable condition.

Hāʾa li-al-amri and tahayyaʾa lahu overlap in some uses.

Hāʾa ilā al-amri also occurs for longing and turning toward something. It can relate to readiness and inclination but should not define every derivative.

Historical evidence and its limits

Early lexicons clearly recognize hayʾah as form or condition and tahyiʾah as preparation and arrangement.

This review has not established an independently datable poetic example for these principal branches that can confidently be placed before revelation.

Lexicons quote verses containing similar forms such as hīʾ, sometimes a call to food and drink or an expression of regret. Similar letters do not establish the meanings of hayʾah and tahyiʾah.

Some quotations also have disputed readings or attributions.

They cannot establish that pre-Islamic Arabic employed every later recorded sense in precisely the same way.

Both principal uses are verifiable in the Qur’an itself. The stories of Jesus and the Cave use preparation and form during the period of revelation.

Later lexicons record a wider range, but later documentation alone does not prove pre-Qur’anic antiquity.

2. Qur’anic forms

ه ي أ occurs four times, in two morphological families:

  • وَهَيِّئْ — al-Kahf 18:10: Form II imperative, “prepare/provide.”
  • وَيُهَيِّئْ — al-Kahf 18:16: Form II imperfect, “provide/make ready.”
  • كَهَيْئَةِ — Āl ʿImrān 3:49: the noun hayʾah with comparative ka-, “like the form of.”
  • كَهَيْئَةِ — al-Māʾidah 5:110: the same construction describing a bird’s form.

Hayyiʾ and yuhayyiʾ belong to one verb, differing in conjugation and sentence structure.

Hayʾah has the same lexical form in both verses about Jesus.

Forms such as هَيَّأَ, تَهَيَّأَ, تَهْيِئَة, تَهَيُّؤ, مُهَيَّأ, and مُتَهَيِّئ exist in Arabic but do not occur in these exact forms in the Qur’an.

Persian heyʾat can mean a group, association, or institution. This administrative sense must not be imposed on Qur’anic hayʾah.

The later scientific term ʿilm al-hayʾah concerns astronomy and celestial configurations. It is not the direct sense in Jesus’s story either.

3. Hayyiʾ: providing a right course for the Companions of the Cave

Al-Kahf 18:10

«إِذْ أَوَى الْفِتْيَةُ إِلَى الْكَهْفِ فَقَالُوا رَبَّنَا آتِنَا مِنْ لَدُنْكَ رَحْمَةً وَهَيِّئْ لَنَا مِنْ أَمْرِنَا رَشَدًا»

Idh awā al-fityatu ilā al-kahfi fa-qālū rabbanā ātinā min ladunka raḥmatan wa-hayyiʾ lanā min amrinā rashadā.

“When the young men took refuge in the cave, they said: Our Lord, grant us mercy from Yourself and provide us a right course in our affair.”

Wa-hayyiʾ is a Form II imperative.

Here the imperative is a prayer: the youths ask their Lord to provide suitable circumstances and a right course.

Lanā indicates that the provision is requested for their benefit.

Min amrinā concerns the situation and affair in which they find themselves.

Rashadan, from ر ش د, concerns right guidance and a sound course.

That meaning is not part of ه ي أ itself. Hayyiʾ expresses provision; rashad specifies the requested course or outcome.

Providing and guiding

They do not merely say “guide us”; they ask for a right course to be provided in their affair.

This may include favorable conditions, sound decision-making, an opening for escape, or means of leaving danger.

Someone may recognize that remaining in danger is harmful without yet having a safe way out.

Recognizing danger and deciding to leave differ from having the means to leave and reach safety.

Hayyiʾ lanā in such a situation concerns suitable conditions and means.

These possible illustrations are not all lexical components of the verb. The verse concerns youths seeking refuge and asking God for mercy and a sound arrangement of their affairs.

Hayyiʾ provides the suitable course; rashad specifies its right direction.

The youths’ own action

At the verse’s beginning, they have taken refuge in the cave.

They have acted to leave their previous situation rather than merely waiting for conditions to change.

They then ask God to arrange their affair toward a sound outcome.

The story therefore combines seeking suitable circumstances with practical human action.

People can strive to preserve life, faith, and freedom while asking their Lord to provide a right course.

The root itself does not guarantee that anyone who acts will obtain precisely their imagined outcome. Each situation requires independent evidence.

4. Yuhayyiʾ: providing ease and suitable means

Al-Kahf 18:16

«وَإِذِ اعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ إِلَّا اللَّهَ فَأْوُوا إِلَى الْكَهْفِ يَنْشُرْ لَكُمْ رَبُّكُمْ مِنْ رَحْمَتِهِ وَيُهَيِّئْ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَمْرِكُمْ مِرْفَقًا»

Wa-idhi iʿtazaltumūhum wa-mā yaʿbudūna illā Allāha fa-ʾwū ilā al-kahfi yanshur lakum rabbukum min raḥmatihi wa-yuhayyiʾ lakum min amrikum mirfaqā.

“When you have withdrawn from them and what they worship apart from The God, take refuge in the cave: your Lord will spread His mercy for you and provide ease for you in your affair.”

Wa-yuhayyiʾ is a Form II imperfect verb.

Attributed to the Lord, it follows the command to seek refuge in the cave, connecting that action with the provision of ease.

Lakum identifies the beneficiaries.

Min amrikum relates it to their circumstances and affair.

Mirfaqan, from ر ف ق, concerns ease, relief, and means that reduce difficulty.

Yuhayyiʾ expresses provision; mirfaq identifies what is provided.

Rashad and mirfaq

In al-Kahf 18:10 the youths request rashadan.

Al-Kahf 18:16 speaks of providing mirfaqan.

These are different words.

Rashad concerns a right course, decision, or result.

Mirfaq concerns ease, relief, and suitable means.

One may know the right path yet need resources and relief to follow it.

A family may rightly decide to leave danger. Shelter, food, security, and the necessities for continuing life are a separate matter.

Rashad can illustrate the decision’s sound direction; mirfaq the means enabling or easing its continuation.

Both verses use ه ي أ because both concern appropriate provision.

The root is not restricted to guidance or comfort. Its object specifies what is provided.

Providing and facilitating

Arabic sometimes explains hayyaʾa al-amra through yassarahu: making something easier or practicable.

The semantic proximity is natural.

Suitable circumstances can ease an undertaking.

Providing and making easy are not always identical, however.

One may provide equipment for a difficult task while the task remains demanding.

Preparing climbing equipment does not eliminate the difficulty of ascending a mountain, though it improves readiness.

“Provides” or “prepares” thus preserves a broader sense. “Makes easy” may convey a contextual result but should not replace the basic meaning everywhere.

5. Hayʾah: a bird’s form in Jesus’s story

Āl ʿImrān 3:49

«وَرَسُولًا إِلَىٰ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ أَنِّي قَدْ جِئْتُكُمْ بِآيَةٍ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ أَنِّي أَخْلُقُ لَكُمْ مِنَ الطِّينِ كَهَيْئَةِ الطَّيْرِ فَأَنْفُخُ فِيهِ فَيَكُونُ طَيْرًا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ وَأُبْرِئُ الْأَكْمَهَ وَالْأَبْرَصَ وَأُحْيِي الْمَوْتَىٰ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ وَأُنَبِّئُكُمْ بِمَا تَأْكُلُونَ وَمَا تَدَّخِرُونَ فِي بُيُوتِكُمْ إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لَكُمْ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ مُؤْمِنِينَ»

Wa-rasūlan ilā banī Isrāʾīla annī qad jiʾtukum bi-āyatin min rabbikum annī akhluqu lakum mina al-ṭīni ka-hayʾati al-ṭayri fa-anfukhu fīhi fa-yakūnu ṭayran bi-idhni Allāhi wa-ubriʾu al-akmaha wa-al-abraṣa wa-uḥyī al-mawtā bi-idhni Allāhi wa-unabbiʾukum bi-mā taʾkulūna wa-mā taddakhirūna fī buyūtikum inna fī dhālika la-āyatan lakum in kuntum muʾminīn.

“And a Messenger to the Children of Israel: I have brought you a sign from your Lord. I fashion for you from clay something like the form of a bird, then breathe into it, and it becomes a bird by permission of The God. I heal the congenitally blind and the person with leprosy, and give life to the dead by permission of The God. I inform you of what you eat and store in your homes. In that is a sign for you, if you are believers.”

Ka-hayʾati al-ṭayri means “like the form of a bird.”

The initial kāf expresses comparison.

Hayʾah specifies shape and form.

Al-ṭayr identifies what the fashioned form resembles.

The phrase thus concerns something shaped like a bird.

Form and a living being

People can fashion a bird-shaped object from clay, wood, or stone.

It may have a head, body, wings, and other outward features without resemblance alone making it alive.

The verse likewise distinguishes these matters.

First Jesus speaks of fashioning something bird-shaped from clay.

Then he breathes into it and it becomes a bird by God’s permission.

Shaping and becoming a bird are distinguishable stages.

Hayʾah conveys form; becoming alive follows from the whole sentence and bi-idhni Allāh.

Hayʾah should not be translated directly as life, soul, spirit, or the complete creation of a living being.

Nor should Jesus’s attributed action be erased. The Qur’an states that he fashions clay into a bird’s form and breathes into it.

But the action remains qualified by divine permission, so it should not imply independent power to create or give life apart from God.

The role of akhluqu

Akhluqu belongs to خ ل ق.

Here it concerns making and shaping something from clay.

Min al-ṭīn specifies the material.

Ka-hayʾati al-ṭayr specifies the resulting form.

These three components do not mean the same thing.

Akhluqu expresses fashioning, min al-ṭīn the material, and ka-hayʾati al-ṭayr the shape.

The entire process of making, breathing, and becoming a bird cannot be derived from ه ي أ alone.

Fīhi has a masculine pronoun. Its antecedent has several grammatical analyses, including the fashioned object or its material.

The sentence structure must be examined rather than assigning a masculine pronoun directly to feminine hayʾah without explanation.

6. Hayʾati al-ṭayr in al-Māʾidah

Al-Māʾidah 5:110

«إِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ اذْكُرْ نِعْمَتِي عَلَيْكَ وَعَلَىٰ وَالِدَتِكَ إِذْ أَيَّدْتُكَ بِرُوحِ الْقُدُسِ تُكَلِّمُ النَّاسَ فِي الْمَهْدِ وَكَهْلًا وَإِذْ عَلَّمْتُكَ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ وَالتَّوْرَاةَ وَالْإِنْجِيلَ وَإِذْ تَخْلُقُ مِنَ الطِّينِ كَهَيْئَةِ الطَّيْرِ بِإِذْنِي فَتَنْفُخُ فِيهَا فَتَكُونُ طَيْرًا بِإِذْنِي وَتُبْرِئُ الْأَكْمَهَ وَالْأَبْرَصَ بِإِذْنِي وَإِذْ تُخْرِجُ الْمَوْتَىٰ بِإِذْنِي وَإِذْ كَفَفْتُ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ عَنْكَ إِذْ جِئْتَهُمْ بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ فَقَالَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْهُمْ إِنْ هَٰذَا إِلَّا سِحْرٌ مُبِينٌ»

Idh qāla Allāhu yā ʿĪsā ibna Maryama udhkur niʿmatī ʿalayka wa-ʿalā wālidatika idh ayyadtuka bi-rūḥi al-qudusi tukallimu al-nāsa fī al-mahdi wa-kahlan wa-idh ʿallamtuka al-kitāba wa-al-ḥikmata wa-al-tawrāta wa-al-injīla wa-idh takhluqu mina al-ṭīni ka-hayʾati al-ṭayri bi-idhnī fa-tanfukhu fīhā fa-takūnu ṭayran bi-idhnī wa-tubriʾu al-akmaha wa-al-abraṣa bi-idhnī wa-idh tukhriju al-mawtā bi-idhnī wa-idh kafaftu banī Isrāʾīla ʿanka idh jiʾtahum bi-al-bayyināti fa-qāla alladhīna kafarū minhum in hādhā illā siḥrun mubīn.

“When The God said: Jesus, son of Mary, remember My favor upon you and your mother, when I strengthened you with Spirit of The Holy, speaking to people in the cradle and in adulthood; when I taught you the Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel; when you fashioned from clay something like the form of a bird by My permission, then breathed into it and it became a bird by My permission; when you healed the congenitally blind and the person with leprosy by My permission; when you brought forth the dead by My permission; and when I restrained the Children of Israel from you as you brought them clear signs, and those among them who disbelieved said: This is nothing but clear magic.”

Ka-hayʾati al-ṭayr retains the sense of a bird’s form.

There are nevertheless differences between the verses.

In Āl ʿImrān 3:49, Jesus speaks and identifies the signs brought to the Children of Israel.

In al-Māʾidah 5:110, God reminds Jesus of those favors and signs.

Verbs and pronouns change accordingly.

In Āl ʿImrān, Jesus says akhluqu and anfukhu.

In al-Māʾidah, God addresses him with takhluqu and tanfukhu.

Āl ʿImrān uses bi-idhni Allāh.

In al-Māʾidah, God says bi-idhnī.

These differences follow the change of speaker and addressee; they do not give hayʾah two separate meanings.

Fīhi and fīhā

Āl ʿImrān 3:49 uses masculine fīhi.

Al-Māʾidah 5:110 uses feminine fīhā.

The feminine pronoun can refer to hayʾah: breathing into the fashioned form.

This grammatical difference does not change the event’s nature, but requires reading each verse’s syntax independently.

An identical verbal antecedent cannot be assumed without considering grammatical gender.

Hayʾah and the bird’s life

Both verses place shaping clay and becoming a bird in one scene.

Hayʾah itself expresses only form.

Becoming a bird after breathing is understood from fa-yakūnu or fa-takūnu and divine permission.

Outward resemblance and a living being’s emergence are lexically distinct matters.

The verses do not independently supply details about the bird’s species, biology, the nature of breathing, or the physical mechanism of clay becoming alive.

The Qur’an presents the event as a sign of Jesus’s mission as a Messenger, within divine permission. Studying ه ي أ should clarify form without replacing separate study of creation, life, and divine signs.

7. Hayʾah and tahyiʾah

Form and provision may initially seem far apart.

Their relationship can be explained through condition and arrangement.

A thing or undertaking has a particular condition.

If unsuitable for a purpose, it can be changed and arranged.

Shaping an unformed piece of clay into a bird gives it a defined material form.

Providing a person escaping danger with a route, shelter, and necessities prepares the conditions for their undertaking.

The first concerns material form.

The second concerns an undertaking’s circumstances.

This illustrates the family’s semantic relationship without proving that every use historically arose from shaping clay or preparing household equipment.

Both branches occur in the Qur’an and each is understood through its own construction.

8. Neighboring roots

ه ي أ and ع د د: providing and preparing

Aʿadda concerns preparing and having something ready for a use or event.

Hayyaʾa can likewise mean preparing and organizing.

The verbs overlap widely.

In al-Kahf 18:10 and al-Kahf 18:16, hayyaʾa emphasizes appropriate provision for an affair.

Qur’anic aʿadda can denote something prepared for future use.

For example, aʿadda Allāhu describes something prepared for a group.

This is a tendency, not an absolute boundary: neither verb is exclusively material or nonmaterial.

ه ي أ and ي س ر: preparing and easing

Yassara concerns making easy and providing ease.

Hayyaʾa can have a similar result because preparation can reduce difficulty.

The concepts are not identical.

All necessary equipment may be ready while the work still requires great effort.

Hayyaʾa emphasizes suitable preparation; yassara ease.

Both aspects may feature in al-Kahf 18:16, but preparation should not be reduced to ease alone.

ه ي أ and ص و ر: shape and form

Ṣūrah concerns a thing’s shape and form.

Hayʾah can express outward shape as well.

They are close in the context of fashioning a bird from clay.

Hayʾah also has a broader range, including condition and arrangement.

Ṣūrah emphasizes form and features presenting something in a defined configuration.

The distinction is not absolute; many uses overlap.

“A bird’s form” is a natural equivalent for hayʾati al-ṭayr in Āl ʿImrān 3:49 and al-Māʾidah 5:110.

ه ي أ and خ ل ق: form and making

These roots occur together in Jesus’s story.

Akhluqu and takhluqu express making and shaping.

Ka-hayʾati al-ṭayr specifies what the product resembles.

Khalq and hayʾah therefore complement each other here.

Their combination does not establish that khalq always means merely shaping existing matter, or that hayʾah alone means creation.

The broader meaning of khalq requires all its uses to be considered.

ه ي أ and ر ش د: provision and a right course

Both families occur in al-Kahf 18:10.

Hayyiʾ means “provide.”

Rashadan expresses a right course or result.

Preparation is the requested action; rashad its desired direction.

Translating hayyiʾ as “guide” may merge the verb with its object.

“Provide a right course in our affair” keeps their contributions clearer.

ه ي أ and ر ف ق: provision and ease

Al-Kahf 18:16 displays the same distinction.

Yuhayyiʾ expresses providing.

Mirfaqan denotes ease, relief, or suitable means.

Verb and object should be understood separately.

Hayyaʾa itself does not mean welfare, though the thing provided here concerns ease and relief.

9. Challenges of translation

One problem is translating every derivative as preparation.

That fits the Cave’s verbs but not hayʾati al-ṭayr.

Conversely, restricting the family to shape obscures the Cave verses’ provision of suitable conditions.

Another problem is equating hayʾah itself with creating a living being.

The Qur’an expresses shaping, breathing, and becoming a bird through separate verbs and qualifications.

Nor does preparation always mean guaranteed ease or a guaranteed result.

Conditions may be prepared while the outcome remains dependent on other factors.

Suggested translations for Forqan

وَهَيِّئْ — al-Kahf 18:10

“And provide.”

In the complete construction:

“And provide us a right course in our affair.”

وَيُهَيِّئْ — al-Kahf 18:16

“And He will provide.”

In the complete construction:

“And He will provide ease for you in your affair.”

كَهَيْئَةِ الطَّيْرِ — Āl ʿImrān 3:49

“Like the form of a bird.”

In context:

“I fashion from clay something like a bird’s form.”

كَهَيْئَةِ الطَّيْرِ — al-Māʾidah 5:110

“Like the form of a bird.”

In context:

“You fashioned from clay something like a bird’s form.”

Non-Qur’anic equivalents likewise follow the subject.

Hayyaʾa al-amra means “prepared and organized the affair.”

Tahayyaʾa li-al-safari means “got ready to travel.”

Ḥasanu al-hayʾati means “well presented” or “well groomed.”

Hayʾatu al-shayʾi means “the thing’s form or condition.”

These equivalents belong to one family but should not all be replaced with one fixed phrase.

10. Conclusion

ه ي أ concerns form, condition, and arrangement, as well as preparation and suitable provision.

Classical hayʾah denotes shape, state, and quality; tahyiʾah denotes preparation.

The evidence does not establish either branch’s historical priority. Their semantic relationship is explicable without claiming certain derivation of one from the other.

The Qur’an contains four occurrences in two morphological families.

Al-Kahf 18:10 and al-Kahf 18:16 use Form II verbs for providing a right course and ease.

Āl ʿImrān 3:49 and al-Māʾidah 5:110 use hayʾah for the bird’s form Jesus fashions from clay.

Final definition for Forqan: The noun branch of ه ي أ denotes form and condition. The verb hayyaʾa concerns preparing and organizing something or providing circumstances suitable for a purpose. Each derivative must be read through its construction and context: having a shape, preparing, and achieving a result are different.

The Cave story concerns a right course and suitable means; Jesus’s story distinguishes a bird’s form from its becoming alive.

Both branches emphasize condition and arrangement from different directions: the state something has, and the action of placing it in a suitable state.
