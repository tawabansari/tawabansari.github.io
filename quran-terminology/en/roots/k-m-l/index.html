1. Introducing the root and its Qur’anic forms

The three-letter Arabic root ك م ل concerns something being complete, reaching its prescribed measure, and possessing everything needed for it to count as complete. It is used for numbers, durations, work, responsibilities, and matters with specified parts or conditions.

Qur’anic completeness does not always mean moral superiority or the highest possible rank. Sometimes a count is complete, such as ten fasting days. Sometimes a specified duration is whole, such as two full years of nursing. Elsewhere a responsibility or order is completed, such as the fasting count or dīn for revelation’s audience.

The root occurs five times, in five verses across three surahs. Its forms are:

Quranic Arabic Corpus: five occurrences, two Form IV verbs and three participial forms

  • كَمَلَ / كَمُلَ / كَمِلَ: to become complete or attain wholeness. Used in the Qur’an: no, in these verbal forms.
  • أَكْمَلَ / يُكْمِلُ: to complete or bring to a prescribed measure. Used in the Qur’an: yes, as أَكْمَلْتُ in Al-Māʾidah 5:3 and وَلِتُكْمِلُوا in Al-Baqarah 2:185.
  • كَامِل: complete, possessing the entire necessary amount or parts. Used in the Qur’an: yes, as كَامِلَيْنِ in Al-Baqarah 2:233, كَامِلَةٌ in Al-Baqarah 2:196, and كَامِلَةً in Al-Naḥl 16:25.
  • كَمَال: completeness or wholeness. Used in the Qur’an: no, in this nominal form.
  • تَكْمِيل: bringing to completion. Used in the Qur’an: no, in this verbal-noun form.
  • كَمَّلَ / يُكَمِّلُ: to complete or bring to fullness. Used in the Qur’an: no.
  • تَكَامَلَ: to attain completeness or become complete. Used in the Qur’an: no.
  • تَكَمَّلَ: to become complete. Used in the Qur’an: no.
  • اِسْتَكْمَلَ: to complete or obtain the whole necessary amount or parts. Used in the Qur’an: no.
  • كَمِيل: complete; a lexical form with limited usage. Used in the Qur’an: no.

No independent kamāl verbal noun occurs in the Qur’an. Neither Form I nor Form II occurs in its text.

Qur’anic study therefore rests on two main branches: akmala, expressing bringing something to completion, and the descriptive kāmil, expressing its complete state.

2. The linguistic core and older Arabic usage

What does being complete mean?

Arabic كَمَلَ الشَّيْءُ and كَمُلَ الشَّيْءُ describe something attaining wholeness. أَكْمَلْتُهُ is used when someone completes it.

Classical lexical accounts place al-kamāl close to al-tamām. Two important explanations describe something being whole, and all the parts making up a whole being present.

A defensible core is therefore: something reaching its own measure of wholeness, with what is necessary for completeness present.

This raises an important question: what determines that measure?

The answer differs. Sometimes a number is specified, such as ten days; sometimes a duration, such as two years; sometimes a set of matters must be completed according to a purpose or criterion.

If an undertaking’s duration is ten days, performing it for nine does not complete the duration, however excellent those nine days were. Conversely, once all ten days have passed, the duration is complete; that does not establish that the performance’s quality was excellent in every respect.

Completeness is therefore not always identical with being best. A set can be complete in number or parts while questions of quality, value, and outcome remain.

Recorded Arabic uses

Older Arabic uses كَمَلَ الْعَدَدُ for a count reaching its prescribed amount. كَمَّلْتُ لَهُ عَدَدَ حَقِّهِ also concerns fully making up the amount or count of someone’s entitlement.

These uses concern completeness of amount rather than moral worth or beauty.

A verse attributed in literary and lexical sources to Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī reads:

فَكَمَّلَتْ مِائَةً فِيهَا حَمَامَتُهَا
وَأَسْرَعَتْ حِسْبَةً فِي ذَلِكَ الْعَدَدِ

fa-kammalat miʾatan fīhā ḥamāmatuhā / wa-asraʿat ḥisbatan fī dhālika al-ʿadadi

She made up a hundred, including her own pigeon, and was quick in reckoning that number.

With the doubled mīm in فَكَمَّلَتْ, the verb is Form II: completing the count to a hundred by including her own pigeon. The numeral supplies the criterion of completeness, not moral worth or superior rank.

Al-Baghdādī’s Khizānat al-Adab attributes the verse to Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī, and Lisān al-ʿArab also records the doubled verb. Identifying the poet and vocalization should therefore go beyond an abbreviated “Al-Nābighah” citation in one dictionary; transmitted attribution and the precise date of composition are separate questions.

Khizānat al-Adab: Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī and the doubled verb fa-kammalat

Another lexical witness uses كَمِيل instead of كَامِل in an expression about thirty full years having passed. The family was therefore not restricted to the familiar kāmil form.

أَعْطَاهُ الْمَالَ كَمَلًا means giving the entire amount of money, without diminishing what is intended.

These examples show completeness concerning a count, quantity, duration, or set.

Lisān al-ʿArab, al-Ṣiḥāḥ, and Asās al-Balāghah: wholeness and lexical forms

Does the root have a distinct physical image?

Unlike roots with an evident connection to a bodily movement, implement, or tangible phenomenon, ك م ل does not permit a single physical, pre-Qur’anic image to be established confidently.

Filling a container, constructing a building, or assembling an object’s parts can illustrate the meaning, but none should be identified without independent evidence as its historical origin.

The principal core in the evidence examined is wholeness and completeness. Physical images are intelligible instances, not necessarily its origin.

3. Complete counts and durations

Al-Baqarah 2:196: تِلْكَ عَشَرَةٌ كَامِلَةٌ

Verse text

وَأَتِمُّوا الْحَجَّ وَالْعُمْرَةَ لِلَّهِ فَإِنْ أُحْصِرْتُمْ فَمَا اسْتَيْسَرَ مِنَ الْهَدْيِ وَلَا تَحْلِقُوا رُءُوسَكُمْ حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ الْهَدْيُ مَحِلَّهُ فَمَنْ كَانَ مِنْكُمْ مَرِيضًا أَوْ بِهِ أَذًى مِنْ رَأْسِهِ فَفِدْيَةٌ مِنْ صِيَامٍ أَوْ صَدَقَةٍ أَوْ نُسُكٍ فَإِذَا أَمِنْتُمْ فَمَنْ تَمَتَّعَ بِالْعُمْرَةِ إِلَى الْحَجِّ فَمَا اسْتَيْسَرَ مِنَ الْهَدْيِ فَمَنْ لَمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ فِي الْحَجِّ وَسَبْعَةٍ إِذَا رَجَعْتُمْ تِلْكَ عَشَرَةٌ كَامِلَةٌ ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ أَهْلُهُ حَاضِرِي الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ شَدِيدُ الْعِقَابِ

wa-atimmū al-ḥajja wa-al-ʿumrata li-Allāhi fa-in uḥṣirtum fa-mā istaysara mina al-hadyi wa-lā taḥliqū ruʾūsakum ḥattā yablugha al-hadyu maḥillahu fa-man kāna minkum marīḍan aw bihi adhan min raʾsihi fa-fidyatun min ṣiyāmin aw ṣadaqatin aw nusukin fa-idhā amintum fa-man tamattaʿa bi-al-ʿumrati ilā al-ḥajji fa-mā istaysara mina al-hadyi fa-man lam yajid fa-ṣiyāmu thalāthati ayyāmin fī al-ḥajji wa-sabʿatin idhā rajaʿtum tilka ʿasharatun kāmilatun dhālika li-man lam yakun ahluhu ḥāḍirī al-masjidi al-ḥarāmi wa-ittaqū Allāha wa-iʿlamū anna Allāha shadīdu al-ʿiqābi

English translation

Complete pilgrimage and the visitation for The God. If you are prevented, then whatever offering is feasible; do not shave your heads until the offering reaches its appointed place. Whoever among you is ill or has an ailment of the head, then a ransom of fasting, charity, or sacrifice. When you are secure, whoever benefits from the visitation until pilgrimage, then whatever offering is feasible; whoever cannot find one shall fast three days during pilgrimage and seven when you return. Those are ten complete days. This is for someone whose family is not present at the inviolable mosque. Be mindful of The God, and know that The God is severe in requital.

Usage analysis

كَامِلَةٌ describes عَشَرَةٌ. Two quantities precede it: three days during pilgrimage and seven after returning.

تِلْكَ عَشَرَةٌ كَامِلَةٌ identifies them together as a complete total.

Completeness concerns the count of days. Three and seven make ten, the full prescribed amount under discussion.

Describing ten as kāmilah is not pointless repetition: it emphasizes fulfillment of the prescribed amount and makes clear that the two parts together constitute one counted obligation.

No meaning beyond context should be extracted. The verse does not say that these ten fasting days confer absolute moral perfection, or that those days are inherently superior to all others.

Nor does kāmilah add another ten to the three and seven. The expression concerns the completeness of that same total.

The opening وَأَتِمُّوا الْحَجَّ وَالْعُمْرَةَ also matters. Atimmū and kāmilah occur within one verse about fulfilling pilgrimage-related responsibilities.

This will matter when examining ك م ل and ت م م.

Al-Baqarah 2:233: حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ

Verse text

وَالْوَالِدَاتُ يُرْضِعْنَ أَوْلَادَهُنَّ حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ وَعَلَى الْمَوْلُودِ لَهُ رِزْقُهُنَّ وَكِسْوَتُهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ لَا تُكَلَّفُ نَفْسٌ إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا لَا تُضَارَّ وَالِدَةٌ بِوَلَدِهَا وَلَا مَوْلُودٌ لَهُ بِوَلَدِهِ وَعَلَى الْوَارِثِ مِثْلُ ذَٰلِكَ فَإِنْ أَرَادَا فِصَالًا عَنْ تَرَاضٍ مِنْهُمَا وَتَشَاوُرٍ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا وَإِنْ أَرَدْتُمْ أَنْ تَسْتَرْضِعُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا سَلَّمْتُمْ مَا آتَيْتُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ

wa-al-wālidātu yurḍiʿna awlādahunna ḥawlayni kāmilayni li-man arāda an yutimma al-raḍāʿata wa-ʿalā al-mawlūdi lahu rizquhunna wa-kiswatuhunna bi-al-maʿrūfi lā tukallafu nafsun illā wusʿahā lā tuḍārra wālidatun bi-waladihā wa-lā mawlūdun lahu bi-waladihi wa-ʿalā al-wārithi mithlu dhālika fa-in arādā fiṣālan ʿan tarāḍin minhumā wa-tashāwurin fa-lā junāḥa ʿalayhimā wa-in aradtum an tastarḍiʿū awlādakum fa-lā junāḥa ʿalaykum idhā sallamtum mā ātaytum bi-al-maʿrūfi wa-ittaqū Allāha wa-iʿlamū anna Allāha bi-mā taʿmalūna baṣīrun

English translation

Mothers nurse their children for two full years, for whoever wishes to complete nursing. The child’s father is responsible for their food and clothing in an accepted manner. No person is charged beyond her capacity. No mother shall be harmed because of her child, nor a father because of his child; the heir has a corresponding responsibility. If the two wish to wean by mutual consent and consultation, there is no blame upon them. If you wish to have your children nursed by another, there is no blame upon you when you deliver the agreed payment in an accepted manner. Be mindful of The God, and know that The God sees what you do.

Usage analysis

كَامِلَيْنِ describes حَوْلَيْنِ, expressing two years’ completeness.

Number and duration supply the criterion. Two full years differ from one year and part of the second.

Kāmilayn clarifies the duration’s boundary and avoids ambiguity from approximate time expressions.

The next expression qualifies its scope: لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ, for whoever wishes to complete nursing.

Two full years are therefore the stated duration for completing nursing. The verse then also allows weaning by both parties’ consent and consultation.

Kāmilayn should not become an unconditional requirement that every mother nurse every child for two years. The stated duration’s completeness and conditions for earlier weaning are distinct questions.

The same verse mentions وُسْعَهَا and prohibits harm. The complete period thus belongs to a context attentive to parents’ and children’s rights and responsibilities.

The coexistence of two roots matters: كَامِلَيْنِ belongs to ك م ل, and يُتِمَّ to ت م م.

Both concern completion of nursing here. They do not support an absolute boundary making one exclusively quality and the other exclusively quantity.

Kāmilayn describes two years; yutimma concerns bringing nursing to completion. Their grammatical functions differ, while their semantic fields overlap.

4. Akmala: completing a specified amount or whole

Al-Baqarah 2:185: completing the fasting count

Verse text

شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ الَّذِي أُنْزِلَ فِيهِ الْقُرْآنُ هُدًى لِلنَّاسِ وَبَيِّنَاتٍ مِنَ الْهُدَى وَالْفُرْقَانِ فَمَنْ شَهِدَ مِنْكُمُ الشَّهْرَ فَلْيَصُمْهُ وَمَنْ كَانَ مَرِيضًا أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ فَعِدَّةٌ مِنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ بِكُمُ الْيُسْرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ الْعُسْرَ وَلِتُكْمِلُوا الْعِدَّةَ وَلِتُكَبِّرُوا اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَاكُمْ وَلَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

shahru ramaḍāna alladhī unzila fīhi al-qurʾānu hudan li-al-nāsi wa-bayyinātin mina al-hudā wa-al-furqāni fa-man shahida minkumu al-shahra fa-l-yaṣumhu wa-man kāna marīḍan aw ʿalā safarin fa-ʿiddatun min ayyāmin ukhara yurīdu Allāhu bikumu al-yusra wa-lā yurīdu bikumu al-ʿusra wa-li-tukmilū al-ʿiddata wa-li-tukabbirū Allāha ʿalā mā hadākum wa-laʿallakum tashkurūna

English translation

The month of Ramadan is that in which the Qur’an was sent down, as guidance for people and clear signs of guidance and discernment. Whoever among you is present for the month shall fast it; whoever is ill or on a journey, then a number of other days. The God intends ease for you and does not intend hardship for you, so that you may complete the number and magnify The God for having guided you, and so that you may give thanks.

Usage analysis

وَلِتُكْمِلُوا is a Form IV imperfect in the subjunctive. الْعِدَّةَ is its object: a specified number or count to be completed.

The thing completed is the fasting count itself.

The preceding context concerns Ramadan fasting and those who are ill or traveling. For them, completing the necessary number on other days is specified.

The statement that The God intends ease rather than hardship is followed by completing the count.

Completing a responsibility is therefore compatible with attending to actual human circumstances.

An ill or traveling person can complete the necessary number on other days within the arrangement stated. Completion does not necessarily require every day to occur at one time or under identical conditions.

Conversely, وَلِتُكْمِلُوا الْعِدَّةَ does not authorize arbitrarily increasing or reducing the prescribed number. Completeness concerns fulfilling the entire specified measure.

Akmala here does not mean raising fasting’s moral quality to an unspecified rank. Completing the ʿiddah concerns a count, though fasting may have further purposes and effects.

Mindfulness is stated as fasting’s purpose in Al-Baqarah 2:183; the lexical meaning of وَلِتُكْمِلُوا in Al-Baqarah 2:185 remains completing the count. The distinction matters for fasting’s relationship with mindfulness.

Fasting’s formative purpose should therefore not replace the verb’s specified object.

Completing compared with merely doing

Performing part of a responsibility differs from completing its entire amount.

Someone may have performed part correctly without everything prescribed having been fulfilled. One can describe a part as correctly done, but cannot call the whole responsibility complete.

Conversely, a completed count alone does not prove that every moral or spiritual aspect was fulfilled in the best manner.

Completing the fasting count in Al-Baqarah 2:185 is therefore a specified matter; sincerity, mindfulness, and fasting’s effects upon conduct are related but distinct questions.

5. Al-Māʾidah 5:3: completing dīn and completing favor

Verse text

حُرِّمَتْ عَلَيْكُمُ الْمَيْتَةُ وَالدَّمُ وَلَحْمُ الْخِنْزِيرِ وَمَا أُهِلَّ لِغَيْرِ اللَّهِ بِهِ وَالْمُنْخَنِقَةُ وَالْمَوْقُوذَةُ وَالْمُتَرَدِّيَةُ وَالنَّطِيحَةُ وَمَا أَكَلَ السَّبُعُ إِلَّا مَا ذَكَّيْتُمْ وَمَا ذُبِحَ عَلَى النُّصُبِ وَأَنْ تَسْتَقْسِمُوا بِالْأَزْلَامِ ذَٰلِكُمْ فِسْقٌ الْيَوْمَ يَئِسَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْ دِينِكُمْ فَلَا تَخْشَوْهُمْ وَاخْشَوْنِ الْيَوْمَ أَكْمَلْتُ لَكُمْ دِينَكُمْ وَأَتْمَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ نِعْمَتِي وَرَضِيتُ لَكُمُ الْإِسْلَامَ دِينًا فَمَنِ اضْطُرَّ فِي مَخْمَصَةٍ غَيْرَ مُتَجَانِفٍ لِإِثْمٍ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ

ḥurrimat ʿalaykumu al-maytatu wa-al-damu wa-laḥmu al-khinzīri wa-mā uhilla li-ghayri Allāhi bihi wa-al-munkhaniqatu wa-al-mawqūdhatu wa-al-mutaraddiyatu wa-al-naṭīḥatu wa-mā akala al-sabuʿu illā mā dhakkaytum wa-mā dhubiḥa ʿalā al-nuṣubi wa-an tastaqsimū bi-al-azlāmi dhālikum fisqun al-yawma yaʾisa alladhīna kafarū min dīnikum fa-lā takhshawhum wa-ikhshawni al-yawma akmaltu lakum dīnakum wa-atmamtu ʿalaykum niʿmatī wa-raḍītu lakumu al-islāma dīnan fa-mani uḍṭurra fī makhmaṣatin ghayra mutajānifin li-ithmin fa-inna Allāha ghafūrun raḥīmun

English translation

Forbidden to you are carrion, blood, swine flesh, that over which an utterance has been raised for other than The God, the strangled, the beaten, the fallen, the gored, and what a predator has eaten, except what you properly slaughter; also what is slaughtered upon erected stones, and determining shares by divining arrows. These are transgression. Today those who disbelieve have despaired of your dīn, so do not fear them; be mindful of Me. Today I have completed your dīn for you, completed My favor upon you, and approved submission as dīn for you. Whoever is compelled by severe hunger without inclining toward sin, The God is indeed forgiving and merciful.

What has been completed?

The central expression is:

الْيَوْمَ أَكْمَلْتُ لَكُمْ دِينَكُمْ.

The agent is The God. أَكْمَلْتُ is a Form IV perfect verb, with دِينَكُمْ as its object. لَكُمْ relates dīn’s completion to the audience.

Unlike Al-Baqarah 2:185, the thing completed is not a numerical count but dīn.

The expression cannot therefore be reduced to completing a number of verses, prescriptions, or a mathematical quantity. It concerns completion of what the Qur’an calls “your dīn.”

This is dīn reaching the completeness The God has prescribed for this address’s audience.

Several points matter here.

First, something becoming complete at a particular stage does not necessarily make every earlier stage defective and worthless.

An undertaking may be sound and valid according to each stage’s purpose before ultimately reaching its prescribed fullness. Final completion does not make every previous stage wrong.

The Qur’an presents Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and their true followers before Qur’anic revelation as having a real relationship with The God and His dīn. Al-yawma akmaltu therefore does not establish that the very dīn of The God was previously defective or invalid.

Second, the pronouns matter: لَكُمْ, دِينَكُمْ, and عَلَيْكُمْ. The address speaks to a particular community.

Al-Māʾidah 5:48 also mentions شِرْعَةً وَمِنْهَاجًا for differing audiences. Completion for this verse’s audience should not become, without examination, a claim that all communities’ prescriptions and rites throughout history were made identical.

The separate study “Dīn, Islām, and Sharīʿah in the Qur’an” examines this distinction more fully.

Does akmaltu eliminate the need for understanding and inquiry?

A complete message differs from everyone’s complete understanding of it.

The verse says that The God completed the audience’s dīn, not that everyone later claiming to follow it would understand every verse correctly.

Completion should therefore not be extended to interpreters’ infallibility, every historical tradition’s perfection, or the correctness of all later prescriptions advanced in religion’s name.

Nor does completion itself answer every question about interpretation, applying prescriptions to new circumstances, or the authority of later religious claims. Those require separate inquiry.

Conversely, the explicit meaning of completion should not be narrowed until the verse says nothing about dīn’s wholeness.

Akmaltu lakum dīnakum makes a real declaration of completion for the audience. Its limits and implications require the Qur’an together, without adding unsupported meanings or diminishing the verb’s sense.

Akmaltu and atmamtu

The same sentence continues:

وَأَتْمَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ نِعْمَتِي.

أَتْمَمْتُ belongs to ت م م, concerning becoming whole, reaching completion, and the necessary amount or parts being present.

Arabic kamāl and tamām are close in many uses. Some lexical analyses distinguish them, for example associating kamāl more with all a whole’s parts and conditions being present, and tamām with reaching an endpoint or prescribed limit.

Such distinctions can help in particular contexts but should not become absolute rules.

Al-Baqarah 2:233 uses حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ and أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ concerning one nursing period. Al-Baqarah 2:196 places أَتِمُّوا الْحَجَّ alongside عَشَرَةٌ كَامِلَةٌ.

Kamāl therefore cannot always mean exclusively superior quality while tamām exclusively means fulfilling quantity.

The first evident difference in Al-Māʾidah 5:3 is the verbs’ objects: dīn is completed and favor is brought to fullness.

لَكُمْ accompanies dīn’s completion, whereas عَلَيْكُمْ accompanies favor’s completion. This structural difference is present, but prepositions alone do not establish every detail of a theological theory of kamāl and tamām.

The two verbs can be understood as connected aspects of a major declaration: dīn’s fullness for the audience and divine favor being completed upon them.

The evidence nevertheless does not establish an absolute rule that kamāl always admits no increase in any respect, whereas tamām always allows further addition.

Lisān al-ʿArab and Tāj al-ʿArūs: overlapping kamāl and tamām and differing analyses

Dīn’s completion and the whole verse

Before declaring dīn’s completion, Al-Māʾidah 5:3 lists prohibited matters. It subsequently addresses compulsion through severe hunger.

Completion therefore occurs within a context containing prescriptions, boundaries, mindfulness of The God, and actual human circumstances.

This proximity does not reduce akmaltu to completing a list of prohibited foods. The verb’s object is dīn, not a food list.

Conversely, placing the declaration among prescriptions does not make dīn an entirely abstract concept detached from practical responsibilities.

The verse concerns wholeness The God has given the audience’s dīn. Its details require examining dīn, submission, prescriptions, guidance, and Qur’anic responsibilities.

6. Kāmilatan: bearing one’s responsibility in full at the account

Al-Naḥl 16:25

Verse text

لِيَحْمِلُوا أَوْزَارَهُمْ كَامِلَةً يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ وَمِنْ أَوْزَارِ الَّذِينَ يُضِلُّونَهُمْ بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ أَلَا سَاءَ مَا يَزِرُونَ

li-yaḥmilū awzārahum kāmilatan yawma al-qiyāmati wa-min awzāri alladhīna yuḍillūnahum bi-ghayri ʿilmin alā sāʾa mā yazirūna

English translation

So that they may bear their own burdens in full on the Day of Resurrection, and some of the burdens of those they mislead without knowledge. How evil is what they bear.

Usage analysis

كَامِلَةً relates to أَوْزَارَهُمْ.

Awzār is a plural of wizr, here denoting the burden of wrongdoing and responsibility resulting from human conduct.

Kāmilah indicates that their own burdens remain fully upon them.

The verse adds that they also bear some burdens of those they mislead.

The construction distinguishes full responsibility for one’s own deeds from responsibility arising through misleading others.

Kāmilah should not imply that these people take all responsibility away from those misled and bear it alone. مِنْ in وَمِنْ أَوْزَارِ الَّذِينَ يُضِلُّونَهُمْ matters and must be preserved when assessing the relationship.

Nor does kāmilah establish that anyone saying something incorrect necessarily shares all another’s responsibility regardless of knowledge, intention, consequences, or an actual connection with misleading them.

The subject is those misleading others. Their responsibility’s scope requires examination of this conduct and other verses.

What ك م ل makes clear is that their own burdens are complete, not described as deficient or merely partial.

“Complete” therefore does not always describe something desirable in the Qur’an.

Two full nursing years, ten full fasting days, and a full burden of wrongdoing can all be called complete, despite differing moral values.

Thus completeness of amount or realization is not identical with moral goodness.

This is a fundamental boundary. Value follows from the subject and context, not merely the adjective complete.

7. Neighboring roots and grammatical points

K M L (كَمَلَ) compared with T M M (تَمَّ)

The nearest root is ت م م.

Both concern wholeness and completion and can approach one another in particular uses.

No fixed formula fully explains their difference, such as tamām always being quantity and kamāl always quality.

Al-Baqarah 2:196 and Al-Baqarah 2:233 do not support that rigid division. Both roots occur there concerning amount and duration.

Each verse requires examining the selected verb or adjective, its object, and its grammatical role.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:3, akmaltu concerns dīn and atmamtu favor. The different objects are real, but do not establish an absolute separation of the roots.

Study of T M M

K M L (كَمَلَ) compared with J M ʿ (جَمَعَ)

Gathering concerns bringing parts or people together, while completeness concerns a state of wholeness.

Gathering parts can precede a whole’s completion, but not every gathering is complete.

If eight of ten necessary parts are present, parts have been gathered, yet the whole remains incomplete.

Conversely, a whole complete according to its prescribed criterion may have had its parts supplied gradually.

Gathering and completeness are therefore connected but not synonymous.

Study of J M ʿ

K M L (كَمَلَ) compared with W F Y (وَفَى)

و ف ي concerns fulfillment, fully rendering an entitlement, and realizing something without deficiency.

It can approach completeness, especially in fully paying someone’s due or fulfilling an obligation.

Qur’anic fulfillment can nevertheless emphasize discharging an obligation and delivering an entitlement to its owner, while completeness focuses on an amount’s or whole’s fullness.

A complete count can therefore relate to fulfilling a covenant, but the count itself is not necessarily a covenant.

Study of W F Y

K M L (كَمَلَ) compared with B L GH (بَلَغَ)

Balagha concerns reaching a destination, limit, or stage.

Reaching a prescribed limit may complete something, but not every arrival is completion.

Reaching a developmental stage, for example, does not necessarily end all further development.

Bulūgh thus foregrounds reaching, while kamāl foregrounds wholeness. This is a tendency requiring assessment in each use.

Study of B L GH

Morphological structure and grammatical roles

The two Qur’anic verbal forms, أَكْمَلْتُ and وَلِتُكْمِلُوا, belong to Form IV.

Both have an agent completing something. In Al-Māʾidah 5:3, The God completes dīn; in Al-Baqarah 2:185, the audience is to complete the count.

كَامِلَيْنِ and كَامِلَة, by contrast, are active-participle forms functioning descriptively or circumstantially.

In Al-Baqarah 2:233, كَامِلَيْنِ agrees with حَوْلَيْنِ in number, gender, and case.

In Al-Baqarah 2:196, كَامِلَةٌ describes عَشَرَةٌ; in Al-Naḥl 16:25, كَامِلَةً expresses the completeness of awzārahum.

The feminine singular kāmilah in Al-Naḥl 16:25 accords with the nonhuman plural awzār. Its grammatical gender implies no particular moral or semantic difference.

Form IV also does not make completion necessarily sudden or instantaneous. The verb expresses completing; its temporal process requires context.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

Persian kāmil, kāmil kardan, and be-kamāl resāndan naturally correspond to “complete,” “complete something,” and “bring to completion.” The final choice must follow the verb’s object and context.

In Al-Baqarah 2:185, “so that you may complete the count” appropriately renders وَلِتُكْمِلُوا الْعِدَّةَ. Adding moral or spiritual perfection would change the direct meaning.

تِلْكَ عَشَرَةٌ كَامِلَةٌ in Al-Baqarah 2:196 is close to “those are ten complete days” or “those ten days are full and complete.” The first is shorter and more natural.

حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ in Al-Baqarah 2:233 is “two full years.” Kāmilayn should not be omitted: it specifies the duration’s boundary.

أَكْمَلْتُ لَكُمْ دِينَكُمْ in Al-Māʾidah 5:3 can be “I have completed your dīn for you.” “Brought to perfection” is linguistically defensible, but may convey philosophical or evaluative implications for some readers beyond what the verb alone specifies.

أَوْزَارَهُمْ كَامِلَةً in Al-Naḥl 16:25 is close to “their own burdens in full.” Complete must not become moral perfection or superiority here.

Completeness must be judged according to its subject. A count, duration, dīn, and burden of responsibility belong to one family of meanings without necessarily sharing one evaluation or outcome.

9. Final synthesis

Qur’anic ك م ل concerns being complete and completing something: attaining the measure of wholeness prescribed or recognized for it.

It occurs in five verses through two principal branches: Form IV and the descriptive kāmil.

Kāmilah in Al-Baqarah 2:196 describes the full ten-day count; kāmilayn in Al-Baqarah 2:233, two years’ complete duration; li-tukmilū in Al-Baqarah 2:185, completing the fasting count.

Al-Māʾidah 5:3 concerns completing dīn, something broader than a specified number or duration. The God announces its completion for the audience, but the verb alone does not establish every detail of legal history, interpretive authority, or perfect human understanding.

In Al-Naḥl 16:25, a full burden of wrongdoing shows that kamāl is not inherently praise. Something may be complete in amount and realization while morally undesirable.

The relationship with ت م م is close. Both concern wholeness, and two verses place them together in one setting with related subjects. An absolute division into entirely separate meanings does not fit the Qur’anic evidence.

Final definition: ك م ل concerns something attaining its prescribed or necessary wholeness, or being brought to that measure. This may concern a count, duration, parts, responsibility, or a broader whole.

The criterion follows from the subject and context, not an assumption that completeness invariably means moral superiority, the best possible quality, or absolute independence from every change and explanation.

Study should therefore distinguish three questions: what has been completed, what criterion determines its completeness, and what can be inferred from its completion.

Especially for أَكْمَلْتُ لَكُمْ دِينَكُمْ, this prevents a clear lexical meaning becoming entangled with interpretive and historical claims requiring independent reasoning.
