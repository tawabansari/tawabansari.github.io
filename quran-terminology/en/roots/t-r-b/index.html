Qur'anic Root

Qur'anic Root T R B

Dust, nearness to dust, severe poverty, equal age or stage, and the special anatomical branch of tara'ib.

Important Forms

Nounturab
تُرَاب

Dust, earth-material, and the scattered material of the ground.
Pluralatrab
أَتْرَاب

Same age, same stage, or near in developmental rank.
Statematrabah
مَتْرَبَة

Being close to, stuck to, or fallen upon dust; an image of severe poverty.

Core Meaning

The root ت ر ب, in its main branch, indicates dust, earth-material, the surface matter of the ground, and the body's return to an earthly and scattered condition.

In مَتْرَبَة, nearness to dust becomes an image of severe poverty and exposure. In the branch of أَتْرَاب, the word indicates people being of the same age, stage, or rank of life. تَرَائِب is also the name of parts of the upper chest and the place where a necklace rests, and it should be studied as a special anatomical branch.

The general field of this family may be summarized as dust and the ground surface; nearness to or falling upon dust; and, in the branch of atrab, people standing in a close rank and stage.

Important Forms

تُرَاب is a noun meaning dust and the soft, scattered material of the earth.

خَلَقَهُ مِنْ تُرَابٍ

He created him from dust.

Al Imran 3:59

Turab may be the first material of creation, the state of the body after disintegration, or the material to which a person wishes he had returned.

أَتْرَاب is the plural of tirb in the sense of same age, same year, or same stage.

وَكَوَاعِبَ أَتْرَابًا

And full-grown companions of equal age.

Al-Naba 78:33

عُرُبًا أَتْرَابًا

Delightful and of equal age.

Al-Waqi'ah 56:37

Atrab most directly indicates equality or nearness in age and life-stage. “Equal in rank” and “same-stage” can explain the field of the word, but for direct translation “same age” or “equal age” is clearer.

مَتْرَبَة is a noun for the state of being near to, stuck to, or fallen upon dust.

أَوْ مِسْكِينًا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍ

Or a needy person of dust-closeness.

Al-Balad 90:16

Dha matrabah is a needy person whose severe poverty and lack of shelter have brought him close to dust, as though no covering, place, or protection remains between his body and the ground.

تَرَائِب is the plural of taribah and occurs once in the Qur'an:

يَخْرُجُ مِنْ بَيْنِ الصُّلْبِ وَالتَّرَائِبِ

It comes out from between the backbone and the tara'ib.

Al-Tariq 86:7

In old Arabic, tara'ib was used for parts of the upper chest, the front bones of the chest, and the place where a necklace rests. A line attributed to Imru' al-Qays also uses “tara'ibuha” in describing this area of the body; this witness shows that the anatomical meaning of the word was known in Arabic before later exegetical debates.

Even so, this witness only clarifies the lexical field of tara'ib. By itself it does not solve all the grammatical and biological questions of the verse in Al-Tariq.

تَرِبَ is a lexical verb: to become dust-covered, to stick to dust, or to be brought near the ground through severe poverty. This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

أَتْرَبَ is reported in lexical usage for covering with dust, throwing dust, or in some constructions for abundance of wealth. This is not a Qur'anic form and should not be imposed on Qur'anic usage without evidence.

Linguistic Note

The first axis of T R B is dust as earthly, scattered material close to the lowest surface of human life.

Dust is underfoot and close to the body. It has no fixed or fashioned form. It can take shape with water. The human body is nourished from earthly material, and after death the body returns to it.

In the axis of atrab, the meaning of being of the same age and same level appears. Some popular explanations connect the word to children of the same age playing in dust, but such a story cannot be treated as the certain origin of the word.

With caution, one may see a possible image-link between the two branches: dust is a spread and level surface, and a tirb is someone standing with another in one age-level. In both, there is an image of being on the same level.

But this link should not be stated as a certain etymology. Dust in nature is not always perfectly level and without raised parts, and atrab does not mean absolute equality. The more reliable core in atrab is same age and same stage.

Material Image

The main image is dust on the surface of the ground: scattered material without an independent fixed form, yet capable of being the material of beginning, growth, and reconstitution.

When the human body becomes turab, the organized bodily form disappears and its material is scattered into the earth. This is why deniers of resurrection imagine becoming dust as the end of any possibility of re-creation.

In matrabah, the human body is so close to the ground that dust displays the person's social condition. The person is not merely needy but grounded, exposed, and without shelter.

In atrab, the material image of dust is not directly present, but the idea of same-levelness can help one understand same-age status: people placed in one stage of growth and time.

In tara'ib, the word points to an area of the body, and the meanings of dust or equal age should not be imposed on it.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, turab was the common word for dust and the surface material of the earth. Turbah could express dust itself, the material of the ground, a piece of land, or the quality of soil.

The verb tariba was used for becoming dust-covered and coming close to dust. From this image arose usages about severe poverty: an exposed human being seems to have fallen upon dust, with nothing between him and the ground.

In the branch of atrab, tirb was used for one who is of the same age and rank. The core of this usage is equality in stage of life, not absolute equality in every feature.

In the branch of tara'ib, old Arabic usage connects it with the chest and the place of the necklace. The witness attributed to Imru' al-Qays supports this same field.

One should not infer from the presence of these forms in one family that all of them arose from one simple and certain material image. Lexical families sometimes have branches whose historical relation is clearer than their present pictorial relation.

Adam Created from Dust

إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَىٰ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ آدَمَ خَلَقَهُ مِنْ تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ قَالَ لَهُ كُنْ فَيَكُونُ

The example of Isa with The God is like the example of Adam: He created him from dust, then said to him, “Be,” and he becomes.

Al Imran 3:59

Turab here is the first material of Adam's creation. The verse uses this example to show that creation is not dependent on the usual pattern of birth. The absence of a father for Isa does not remove him from being created; Adam was created in a still more unusual way, from turab.

Dust in the Chain of Human Creation

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ فِي رَيْبٍ مِنَ الْبَعْثِ فَإِنَّا خَلَقْنَاكُمْ مِنْ تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ مِنْ نُطْفَةٍ

O people, if you are in doubt about being raised, then We created you from dust, then from a drop.

Al-Hajj 22:5

Turab is the beginning of a chain that then reaches biological stages of the body. This structure can include both the first creation of the human being from dust and the earthly origin of the materials of the human body. The verse does not explain the chemical details by which dust becomes the body of each individual; its aim is to remind of the transformation of matter from a simple and scattered condition into an organized human being.

Turab and Tin in Material Stages

In some verses, human creation is described with turab:

خَلَقَهُ مِنْ تُرَابٍ

He created him from dust.

Al Imran 3:59

In other verses, tin is mentioned:

إِنَّا خَلَقْنَاهُمْ مِنْ طِينٍ لَازِبٍ

We created them from sticky clay.

Al-Saffat 37:11

Turab and tin are not necessarily two contradictory accounts. Turab is the dusty material in a dry, scattered, and not-yet-formed state. Tin is dust mixed with water, ready for adhesion, cohesion, and taking form. In material imagery, turab can be the stage of scattered material and tin the stage of combination and formability.

Yet the Qur'an in these verses does not present a complete scientific sequence of geological or biological stages. The difference between the two words shows more about the quality of the material than about a precise and certain timeline.

Becoming Dust and Denying Resurrection

أَإِذَا كُنَّا تُرَابًا أَإِنَّا لَفِي خَلْقٍ جَدِيدٍ

When we have become dust, will we be in a new creation?

Al-Ra'd 13:5

The human being who denies resurrection sees becoming dust as the complete loss of bodily form and particularity. The question is how scattered material, no longer bearing human form, can become a new creation again.

Bones and Dust

أَإِذَا كُنَّا عِظَامًا وَتُرَابًا أَإِنَّا لَمَبْعُوثُونَ خَلْقًا جَدِيدًا

When we have become bones and dust, will we be raised as a new creation?

Al-Isra 17:49

Bones still preserve part of the body's structure. Turab is the stage in which even that structure has disappeared and the material has mixed with the earth. The phrase “bones and dust” highlights the intensity of bodily disintegration.

The Wish to Be Dust

وَيَقُولُ الْكَافِرُ يَا لَيْتَنِي كُنْتُ تُرَابًا

And the coverer of truth says, “Would that I were dust.”

Al-Naba 78:40

Turab here is matter without particularity and without accountability. The person wishes he had not appeared in that scene as a responsible and answerable being. The direct meaning of the verse remains “Would that I were dust.” Readings such as “Would that I had never become an answerable person” are explanatory, not literal translation.

Turab and Atrab in Surah Al-Naba

Earlier in the same surah, about the blessing of the mindful, it says:

وَكَوَاعِبَ أَتْرَابًا

And full-grown companions of equal age.

Al-Naba 78:33

And at the end it says:

يَا لَيْتَنِي كُنْتُ تُرَابًا

Would that I were dust.

Al-Naba 78:40

The presence of atrab and turab in one surah is noteworthy in sound and image. In atrab, persons are presented in a state of particularity, companionship, and same-stage presence. In turab, the condemned person wishes to become matter without particularity so as to be removed from answerability.

But this contrast should not be presented as a certain technical root-play. The Qur'an does not explicitly say that the two words were chosen to create such a technical contrast. What can be said is that the structure of the surah images two conditions: particularity and companionship in success; and the wish to erase particularity before account.

The Dust-Dwelling Needy Person

أَوْ مِسْكِينًا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍ

Or a needy person of dust-closeness.

Al-Balad 90:16

Matrabah expresses the intensity of need through an image. The person is close to the ground and deprived of shelter, bedding, and means.

This phrase does not make poverty a moral fault of the needy person. On the contrary, feeding such a person is presented as part of crossing the difficult pass.

An explanatory rendering such as “a needy person grounded and exposed” can clarify the severity of the condition, but in direct translation, “a dust-dwelling needy person” better preserves the image of the word.

Atrab as Same Age and Same Stage

وَكَوَاعِبَ أَتْرَابًا

And full-grown companions of equal age.

Al-Naba 78:33

Atrab describes a group that is close and level with one another in age or stage of growth. The word by itself does not determine the exact age, their relation to others, or the physical details.

إِنَّا أَنْشَأْنَاهُنَّ إِنْشَاءً ۝ فَجَعَلْنَاهُنَّ أَبْكَارًا ۝ عُرُبًا أَتْرَابًا

We brought them forth in a special bringing forth, and made them fresh, delightful, and of equal age.

Al-Waqi'ah 56:35-37

Atrab here indicates same-age and same-stage quality within the group. “Companions of equal age and harmony” may communicate a broader field, but “harmony” is not obtained directly from atrab alone; it is part of contextual reflection.

Tara'ib in the Description of Creation

فَلْيَنْظُرِ الْإِنْسَانُ مِمَّ خُلِقَ ۝ خُلِقَ مِنْ مَاءٍ دَافِقٍ ۝ يَخْرُجُ مِنْ بَيْنِ الصُّلْبِ وَالتَّرَائِبِ

So let the human being look at what he was created from: he was created from a gushing water that comes out from between the backbone and the tara'ib.

Al-Tariq 86:5-7

Lexically, tara'ib is close to the chest area and the front bones of the chest. The old poetic witness also supports this anatomical usage.

But several questions must remain separate: Which exact bones or area does tara'ib include? Do sulb and tara'ib belong to one person, or do they show two sides of creation? Is the subject of yakhruju the water or the human being in formation? What is the relation of the verse to present biological knowledge? These questions do not receive a decisive answer from the root T R B itself and should be studied in an independent article on the verse.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

Turab as origin and turab as return: In creation, turab is the first material. In death, turab is the disintegrated state of the body. In both, earthly material is present, but the direction differs: from scattered material to human form; and from human form to scattered material.

Turab and tin: Turab is dry and scattered dust. Tin is dust mixed with water, adhesive and formable. This distinction can show two material qualities: turab as formlessness and scattering; tin as mixture, adhesion, and readiness to take form. But without explicit evidence, one should not build from this a complete and certain scientific sequence.

Turab and ard: Ard is the earth as expanse, place, and domain. Turab is the dusty material of the earth. Ard is where the human being lives; turab is a material that forms part of the earth.

Turab and sa'id: Sa'id is not necessarily a synonym of turab. Sa'id points to the upper and visible surface of the earth and can include dust, stone, or a clean surface of the ground:

فَتَيَمَّمُوا صَعِيدًا طَيِّبًا

Then seek a clean surface of the earth.

Al-Nisa 4:43

The general difference is this: turab is the material kind of dust; sa'id is the position and visible surface of the ground. Therefore limiting sa'id to soft dust does not preserve the whole field of the word.

Matrabah and poverty: Faqr is the general word for need. Matrabah is a more severe image: a needy person fallen upon dust or brought near it, with no shelter or means. Not every poor person is necessarily dha matrabah; this expression highlights the severity and ground-level condition of poverty.

Atrab and complete equality: Atrab indicates same-age and same-stage status, not absolute equality in every feature. Two people may be atrab while not equal in knowledge, ability, height, or social position.

Tara'ib and turab: The formal similarity between tara'ib and turab should not lead to translating tara'ib as “dusts” or earthly material. The context of Al-Tariq and old Arabic usage show that tara'ib is the name of an area of the body.

Grammar and Structure

Min turab:

خَلَقَهُ مِنْ تُرَابٍ

He created him from dust.

Al Imran 3:59

The particle min shows origin or first material. With this structure, the verse does not explain the details of how dust became human.

Kunna turaban:

أَإِذَا كُنَّا تُرَابًا

When we have become dust?

Turab is the predicate of kunna. The speakers are not saying only that they will be under dust; they say their body becomes dust.

Dha matrabah: مِسْكِينًا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍ. Dha means one possessing or having, but a literal “one having matrabah” is not clear in English. Natural equivalents include: dust-dwelling needy person; needy person fallen upon dust; grounded and exposed needy person.

Atraban: In كَوَاعِبَ أَتْرَابًا and عُرُبًا أَتْرَابًا, atrab describes the preceding plural. Suitable equivalents: same age, equal age, same stage. “Same level” can help explain the semantic field, but in direct translation it may obscure the age-relation.

Min bayni al-sulb wa al-tara'ib: Min shows a point of emergence, and bayn shows the interval or region between the two named sides. But this structure alone does not settle the exact reference of the two regions or their biological relation.

Nearby Roots

T R B and A R D (ard): Ard is earth as place, expanse, and domain. Turab is its dusty material.

T R B and T Y N (tin): Tin is dust mixed with water and formable. Turab is dry and scattered dust.

T R B and S A D (sa'id): Sa'id is the visible upper surface of the earth. Turab is the material kind of dust. The surface of sa'id may be dusty, stony, or another kind of earth-surface.

T R B and Th R Y (thara): Thara is close to soft and moist soil or the lower layer of soil. Turab is the more general word for dust.

T R B and Gh B R (ghubar): Ghubar is fine dusty particles dispersed in the air or settling on things. Turab is dust as material on the ground; ghubar is its airborne or settled-particle state.

T R B and R M L (raml): Raml is sand and larger, less adhesive grains. Turab has a broader range, and not every turab is raml.

T R B and F Q R (faqr): Faqr is need and lack of means. Matrabah shows the dusty and severe image of that need.

T R B and S W Y (sawa): Sawa indicates making level, making equal, and reaching a balanced condition. Atrab relates to people being level with one another, especially in age.

T R B and A D L ('adl): 'Adl indicates measured equality and putting each thing in its proper place. Atrab carries no judgment of justice; it only states nearness in age or stage.

T R B and Q R N (qarin): Qarin indicates companionship and close joining. Tirb emphasizes same age and same stage. Two qarins are not necessarily the same age.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For تُرَاب: dust; earth-material; dust of the ground; scattered dust.

For مِنْ تُرَابٍ: from dust; from earth-material; with an origin from dust.

For أَإِذَا كُنَّا تُرَابًا: When we have become dust? After we have turned into dust? When our body has become dust?

For يَا لَيْتَنِي كُنْتُ تُرَابًا: Would that I were dust. For explanation: Would that I had remained in the unparticular state of dust; would that I had not appeared as an answerable person.

For مَتْرَبَة: dust-dwelling; fallen upon dust; stuck to the ground; exposure and severe poverty.

For مِسْكِينًا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍ: a dust-dwelling needy person; a needy person fallen upon dust; a grounded and exposed needy person.

For أَتْرَاب: same age; equal age; same stage; equal in age-rank.

For كَوَاعِبَ أَتْرَابًا: full-grown companions of equal age; mature ones of the same age; companions in one age-stage. The meaning of kawa'ib should be studied separately under the root K A B.

For عُرُبًا أَتْرَابًا: delightful and of equal age; companions of equal age and harmony; loving and same-stage. “Harmony” is contextual explanation and should not be treated as the direct meaning of atrab alone.

For تَرَائِب: tara'ib; upper chest bones; the front chest area; the place of the necklace on the chest. For a first translation, preserving “tara'ib” with a cautious lexical note is safer.

Summary

The root ت ر ب, in its main axis, indicates dust, earthly material, and the body's nearness or return to the surface of the ground. Turab is the material to which human creation is tied; the body returns to it after death; and deniers of resurrection imagine scattering into it as the end of any possibility of creation.

In matrabah, dust becomes the image of severe poverty and exposure: a human being fallen to the ground with no shelter or covering between the body and dust.

In atrab, the root expands to same age and same stage. One may see a possible image of same-levelness between dust and atrab, but this relation should not be presented as certain etymology.

In Surah Al-Naba, atrab appears in the image of companionship and success, while turab appears in the wish to erase the person before account. This contrast is notable in the structure of the surah, but one should not give it more certainty than the text itself gives.

Tara'ib is an anatomical branch that in old Arabic referred to the upper chest area and the place of the necklace. This word should not be reduced to dust or same age.

T R B is close to ard, tin, sa'id, thara, ghubar, raml, faqr, sawa, and 'adl, but it is not identical with any of them. The distinct core of its main branch is dust as earthly material in a scattered and unformed state, while the core of the atrab branch is same age and people standing in a close stage.
