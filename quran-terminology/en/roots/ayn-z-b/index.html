Core meaning: moving or remaining away from a place, person, or defined sphere; being outside someone's presence, reach, or awareness.

1. Introduction and meanings in early Arabic

The triliteral Arabic root ع ز ب concerns moving away, becoming distant, withdrawing, and being absent. The distance may be spatial, as when someone leaves their family or livestock goes to distant pasture. It may also concern something beyond a person's reach or awareness.

Early Arabic uses عَزَبَ for becoming distant or absent, عُزُوب for distance and absence, and عَازِب for someone or something remote or away from its usual place.

One familiar pastoral expression is الْكَلَأُ الْعَازِبُ: pasture far from people's dwelling place, which requires a journey to reach.

إِبِلٌ عَازِبَةٌ and إِبِلٌ عَزِيبٌ can describe camels remaining at distant pasture rather than returning to their owners' settlement at night. Some related uses concern animals returning only at night; the common feature is distant grazing, not one fixed timetable.

In such uses, the root does not merely denote movement. Movement can take people or animals from one location to another, but this root emphasizes distance from the usual sphere of presence or access.

The same semantic field includes separation from family, a spouse's absence, land without inhabitants, and something passing out of mind or awareness.

The root's basic meaning can therefore be defined as follows:

ع ز ب expresses moving or remaining away from a person, place, or defined sphere, with presence, access, or awareness consequently absent or limited.

Remaining hidden and being forgotten are possible consequences of that distance, not invariable components of every use.

2. Early poetic evidence and its dating

An early witness occurs in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic poet al-Nabighah al-Dhubyani:

شُعَبُ الْعِلَافِيَّاتِ بَيْنَ فُرُوجِهِمْ
وَالْمُحْصَنَاتُ عَوَازِبُ الْأَطْهَارِ

shuʿabu al-ʿilāfiyyāti bayna furūjihim / wa-l-muḥṣanātu ʿawāzibu al-aṭhāri

“The branches of the 'Allafi saddles are between their legs, while their married women remain apart from their husbands during their periods of purity.”

The verse depicts men away on journeys or campaigns, with their wives remaining in their absence.

عَوَازِبُ الْأَطْهَارِ concerns husbands being absent from their wives during periods of purity. Here the root does not mean being unmarried: the women are الْمُحْصَنَات, understood here as married women. The point is their husbands' distance and absence.

This witness matters because it shows that the root can express temporary absence rather than necessarily permanent separation.

Another witness is found in poetry attributed to al-A'sha:

كِلَانَا يُرَائِي أَنَّهُ غَيْرُ ظَالِمٍ
فَأَعْزَبْتُ حِلْمِي بَلْ هُوَ الْيَوْمَ أَعْزَبَا

kilānā yurāʾī annahu ghayru ẓālimin / fa-aʿzabtu ḥilmī bal huwa al-yawma aʿzabā

“Each of us presents himself as doing no wrong; I set aside my forbearance—rather, today it has departed from me.”

The distance here is not spatial. حِلْم, forbearance, is something that has left a person or that the person has put aside. The verse illustrates both the causative and intransitive uses of أَعْزَبَ; its wording also varies between transmitted versions.

This use shows that the root can express the loss or absence of nonmaterial qualities such as forbearance and self-restraint.

Al-A'sha lived into the period of Islam's emergence, but this study has not independently established when this particular verse was composed. It is evidence for early usage, without being treated as a conclusively dated pre-Quranic witness.

Taken together, lexical and poetic evidence establishes distance and absence as familiar meanings of this family. The Quran employs the same semantic field in discussing The God's knowledge, with its own subject and context.

3. Lexical and Quranic forms

Important forms include عَزَبَ, يَعْزُبُ, عُزُوب, عَازِب, عَزَب, عَزِيب, عُزُوبَة, أَعْزَبَ, عَزَّبَ, and مِعْزَابَة.

They have different but related meanings in their various uses.

عَزَبَ and يَعْزُبُ: To become distant or absent, or remain beyond reach or awareness.

عُزُوب: Distance, absence, and separation.

عَازِب: Remote or absent; also, in a social use, someone without a spouse.

عَزَب and عُزُوبَة: In the family context, being without a spouse.

عَزِيب: Among other uses, livestock remaining at distant pasture.

أَعْزَبَ and عَزَّبَ: In some constructions, to send away or take livestock to distant pasture.

مِعْزَابَة: Someone remaining away from family and their usual dwelling; also used for someone long without a spouse.

Not all these forms occur in the Quran.

The Quran uses only the imperfect verb from this root, twice: in Yunus 10:61 and Saba 34:3. The reading followed here has يَعْزُبُ in both places.

It is a third-person masculine singular imperfect of the basic verbal form. In both verses it is negated and used with عَنْ.

Arabic recognizes both يَعْزُبُ and يَعْزِبُ. The vowel variation is also recorded in Quranic readings and does not change the basic sense of being distant or absent.

4. يَعْزُبُ: remaining beyond awareness or reach

In material uses, distance is generally measured relative to a place or person.

If camels are said to be far from the settlement, that distance is relative to the settlement. They still exist elsewhere: becoming distant does not mean being destroyed or ceasing to exist.

Likewise, being away from one's family does not mean having no family.

This matters for the Quranic use, where يَعْزُبُ is accompanied by عَنْ.

عَزَبَ عَنْ expresses distance or absence relative to the person or sphere concerned. Applied to The God's knowledge, it concerns the possibility of something remaining beyond His awareness.

Both Quranic verses deny that possibility.

The contextual sense is thus not merely spatial distance but remaining outside awareness. It emerges from the verb, preposition, negation, and subject of the verse together.

The root does not necessarily denote deliberate concealment. Something may be beyond a person's sight or reach without anyone intentionally hiding it.

It should therefore not be equated with concealing the truth deliberately.

5. Yunus 10:61: no action escapes The God's witnessing

The first occurrence in Quranic order is in Yunus:

وَمَا تَكُونُ فِي شَأْنٍ وَمَا تَتْلُو مِنْهُ مِنْ قُرْآنٍ وَلَا تَعْمَلُونَ مِنْ عَمَلٍ إِلَّا كُنَّا عَلَيْكُمْ شُهُودًا إِذْ تُفِيضُونَ فِيهِ ۚ وَمَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْ رَبِّكَ مِنْ مِثْقَالِ ذَرَّةٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي السَّمَاءِ وَلَا أَصْغَرَ مِنْ ذَٰلِكَ وَلَا أَكْبَرَ إِلَّا فِي كِتَابٍ مُبِينٍ

wa-mā takūnu fī shaʾnin wa-mā tatlū minhu min qurʾānin wa-lā taʿmalūna min ʿamalin illā kunnā ʿalaykum shuhūdan idh tufīḍūna fīhi wa-mā yaʿzubu ʿan rabbika min mithqāli dharratin fī al-arḍi wa-lā fī al-samāʾi wa-lā aṣghara min dhālika wa-lā akbara illā fī kitābin mubīn

Yūnus 10:61

Yunus 10:61

“You are engaged in no matter, and recite from it no portion of Quran, and you all perform no deed, without Us being witnesses over you as you engage in it. Not the weight of a speck in the earth or the sky remains hidden from your Lord; nor is there anything smaller or greater than that but it is in a clear record.”

The opening concerns human activities. The singular verbs تَكُونُ and تَتْلُو address one person; تَعْمَلُونَ and عَلَيْكُمْ then extend the address to a plural audience.

The God states that He witnesses their involvement in their activities.

Then comes وَمَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْ رَبِّكَ.

The sequence matters: the verse begins with witnessing actions, then extends the scope of divine knowledge to the smallest and largest things.

مِنْ مِثْقَالِ ذَرَّةٍ concerns something weighing as little as a speck. In this negative construction, مِنْ emphasizes the comprehensiveness of the negation: even that amount is not excluded.

The verse adds وَلَا أَصْغَرَ مِنْ ذَٰلِكَ وَلَا أَكْبَرَ. It therefore includes both smaller and greater things, not only the very small.

فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي السَّمَاءِ extends the spatial scope. Location or distance does not put anything in earth or sky outside The God's knowledge.

Here يَعْزُبُ conveys remaining beyond awareness, with that possibility categorically denied.

The verse concerns divine knowledge; it does not define a bodily or spatial boundary around The God that things could approach or leave.

“Remains hidden from your Lord,” when negated, is therefore natural and appropriate to this context.

The meaning also connects with the preceding verses. Yunus 10:59 and Yunus 10:60 concern declaring things lawful or forbidden without The God's permission and inventing lies about Him. Yunus 10:61 recalls that no action or speech escapes His witnessing and knowledge.

The lexical meaning should not, however, be confined to false attribution, moral judgment, or recording deeds. Those are contextual subjects; the verb itself concerns being distant or remaining hidden.

6. Saba 34:3: nothing escapes knowledge of the unseen

The root's other Quranic occurrence is in Saba:

وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لَا تَأْتِينَا السَّاعَةُ ۖ قُلْ بَلَىٰ وَرَبِّي لَتَأْتِيَنَّكُمْ عَالِمِ الْغَيْبِ ۖ لَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْهُ مِثْقَالُ ذَرَّةٍ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَلَا فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا أَصْغَرُ مِنْ ذَٰلِكَ وَلَا أَكْبَرُ إِلَّا فِي كِتَابٍ مُبِينٍ

wa-qāla alladhīna kafarū lā taʾtīnā al-sāʿatu qul balā wa-rabbī la-taʾtiyannakum ʿālimi al-ghaybi lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍi wa-lā aṣgharu min dhālika wa-lā akbaru illā fī kitābin mubīn

Sabaʾ 34:3

Saba 34:3

“Those who disbelieved said, ‘The Hour will not come upon us.’ Say, ‘Indeed, by my Lord, it will certainly come upon you—the Knower of the unseen. Not the weight of a speck in the heavens or the earth remains hidden from Him; nor is there anything smaller or greater than that but it is in a clear record.’”

The principal subject is a response to those denying the coming of the resurrection.

The reply first affirms the certainty of the Hour, then describes The God as عَالِمِ الْغَيْبِ, the Knower of the unseen.

Immediately after that description comes لَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْهُ مِثْقَالُ ذَرَّةٍ.

This connection again places يَعْزُبُ in the field of remaining outside knowledge or awareness.

The unseen is what is hidden from ordinary human sight and awareness. That hiddenness imposes no limit on divine knowledge.

لَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْهُ thus emphasizes that nothing, however small, lies beyond His knowledge.

The preceding verse, Saba 34:2, describes The God's knowledge of what enters the earth, emerges from it, descends from the sky, and ascends into it.

Saba 34:3 continues that description of comprehensive knowledge, now in connection with denial of the resurrection.

The next verse, Saba 34:4, speaks of rewarding those who believe and do righteous deeds.

Divine knowledge is therefore discussed in the context of resurrection and recompense. عزب by itself does not mean accounting or reward; those ideas come from the passage as a whole.

Differences between the two constructions

Both verses deny that anything can remain hidden from divine knowledge, but their constructions differ.

Yunus 10:61 reads:

وَمَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْ رَبِّكَ مِنْ مِثْقَالِ ذَرَّةٍ

wa-mā yaʿzubu ʿan rabbika min mithqāli dharrah

Yūnus 10:61

Saba 34:3 reads:

لَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْهُ مِثْقَالُ ذَرَّةٍ

lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharrah

Sabaʾ 34:3

In Yunus, مَا supplies the negation, and مِنْ before مِثْقَالِ emphasizes its comprehensiveness. مِثْقَالِ is genitive in form after مِنْ but occupies the subject's syntactic position.

In Saba, لَا supplies the negation, and مِثْقَالُ is the subject without that preceding مِنْ.

Yunus also mentions the earth before the sky, whereas Saba mentions the heavens before the earth.

These differences do not change the meaning of يَعْزُبُ. Both verses express the same principle: nothing remains distant or hidden from The God's knowledge.

7. Distinctions from related roots

ع ز ب (ʿazaba) and غ ي ب (ghāba)

غيب concerns absence or being hidden from sight and awareness.

عزب in its basic uses emphasizes distance from a sphere of presence or access.

A camel that has gone to distant pasture, for instance, is called عَازِب because it is away from its usual location.

Something can be absent from view without spatial remoteness being the cause: it may be hidden behind a barrier.

The roots overlap in some uses but do not always emphasize precisely the same feature.

The combination of عَالِمِ الْغَيْبِ and لَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْهُ in Saba 34:3 makes their relationship clear: nothing can remain absent from or beyond divine knowledge.

ع ز ب (ʿazaba) and خ ف ي (khafiya)

In the concealment branch of خ ف ي, the meaning concerns being hidden or not manifest.

عزب primarily concerns moving or remaining away.

Something may be close yet hidden by its small size or a covering. Conversely, something may be visible but situated far away.

The Quranic uses bring these semantic fields together by denying that anything lies beyond awareness.

“Remains hidden,” under negation, is therefore suitable for both مَا يَعْزُبُ and لَا يَعْزُبُ in the verses examined, although it does not express every historical use of the root.

ع ز ب (ʿazaba) and ب ع د (baʿuda)

ب ع د concerns distance in space, time, or relationships between people and things.

عزب also denotes distance, often emphasizing separation from one's usual location, family, nearby pasture, or sphere of access.

This is a tendency, not an absolute rule applying to every Arabic sentence.

الْكَلَأُ الْعَازِبُ, for example, is pasture more difficult to reach because of distance. Yet not every distant thing is necessarily described as عَازِب.

ع ز ب (ʿazaba) and ع ز ل (ʿazala)

These roots differ in one consonant, and both can concern separation and distance.

عزل commonly denotes separating, setting aside, or removing something from a group or position.

عزب can describe distance or absence without anyone necessarily causing the separation deliberately.

Livestock going to distant pasture can be described with عزب, whereas removing someone from office or separating an item from a collection belongs to the semantic field of عزل.

This distinction must also be assessed through verbal construction and context; it is not an absolute boundary between every form of the roots.

ع ز ب (ʿazaba) and ن س ي (nasiya)

In the forgetting branch, نسيان concerns something previously known or expected to be remembered passing from memory. This does not exhaust every use of ن س ي.

عزب does not necessarily mean forgetting.

Something may lie beyond a person's reach or knowledge without that person having known and then forgotten it.

The Quranic denial of عزب is compatible with denying forgetfulness of The God, but the verb should not simply be translated as “not forgetting.”

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

In both Quranic uses, a rendering denying that anything “remains hidden” is natural and accurate because the context concerns comprehensive divine knowledge.

يَعْزُبُ — Yunus 10:61

In the phrase:

وَمَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْ رَبِّكَ مِنْ مِثْقَالِ ذَرَّةٍ

wa-mā yaʿzubu ʿan rabbika min mithqāli dharrah

Yūnus 10:61

Suggested translation:

“Not even the weight of a speck remains hidden from your Lord.”

يَعْزُبُ — Saba 34:3

In the phrase:

لَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْهُ مِثْقَالُ ذَرَّةٍ

lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharrah

Sabaʾ 34:3

Suggested translation:

“Not the weight of a speck remains hidden from Him.”

“Does not remain distant from Him” is closer to the spatial image but may suggest physical distance from The God. The rendering with “hidden” is preferable in these contexts.

“Does not lie beyond His knowledge” can clarify the contextual sense, but the word “knowledge” is not present in لَا يَعْزُبُ عَنْهُ itself. The explanation can therefore remain in the word study rather than being added unnecessarily to a direct translation.

Suggested wording for a Forqan word box:

Word: يَعْزُبُ

Root: ع ز ب

Word type: Third-person masculine singular imperfect, basic verbal form.

Brief root meaning: Moving away, remaining distant, or passing beyond reach or awareness.

Meaning in this verse: Remains hidden; under negation, “does not remain hidden.”

Conclusion

ع ز ب denotes becoming distant, moving away, and remaining absent. Early pastoral uses, separation from family, being without a spouse, and the loss of qualities such as forbearance show its application to material and nonmaterial matters.

The Quran uses only the imperfect verb from this root, with both occurrences negated. In the reading followed here, it is يَعْزُبُ.

In Yunus 10:61 it appears in the context of The God's witnessing human actions and knowing the smallest and largest things.

In Saba 34:3 it answers denial of the resurrection in connection with عَالِمِ الْغَيْبِ, the Knower of the unseen.

In both verses, the sense of distance and absence applies to knowledge and awareness, and the Quran denies that anything can stand outside The God's knowledge.

The root's core is moving or remaining beyond a sphere of presence, access, or awareness. In its two Quranic uses, remaining hidden from divine knowledge is categorically denied.
