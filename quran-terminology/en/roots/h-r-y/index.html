Taḥarrī: seeking and choosing what is considered more fitting or correct

1. Introducing the root and its basic meaning

The three-letter Arabic root Ḥ R Y has several semantic branches. The branch most important for understanding the Qur’an concerns being worthy, aiming for something, and seeking what is considered more fitting or correct.

Within this family, حَرِيّ (ḥariyy) means worthy, fitting, or appropriate. The verb تَحَرَّى (taḥarrā) means to seek something deliberately and attentively, turn toward it, or make an effort to find or choose it.

Taḥarrī differs from simple wanting in that it usually involves attention and selection. A person does not merely want something, but seeks to find or choose what they consider more suitable, correct, or fitting.

Nevertheless, taḥarrā does not itself guarantee that the seeker has found the truth or made an error-free choice. Intention and effort are conveyed by the verb; whether the chosen object is correct must be assessed from the object itself and the sentence’s context.

The root occurs only once in the Qur’an, in Al-Jinn 72:14, as تَحَرَّوْا:

فَمَنْ أَسْلَمَ فَأُولَئِكَ تَحَرَّوْا رَشَدًا

fa-man aslama fa-ulāʾika taḥarraw rashadan

“So whoever submits: those are the ones who have deliberately and earnestly sought sound guidance.”

In this phrase, تَحَرَّوْا expresses seeking and deliberately turning toward something. رَشَدًا identifies what they have turned toward.

The meaning of rushd, sound guidance, should therefore not be included in the lexical definition of Ḥ R Y. It comes from the separate root R SH D.

2. Lexical forms and semantic branches

Ḥariyy, ḥarā, and aḥrā: worthiness and suitability

حَرِيّ means worthy, fitting, or appropriate in Arabic.

هُوَ حَرِيٌّ بِذَلِكَ means that someone is fitting or deserving for the matter in question. حَرِيٌّ أَنْ يَفْعَلَ may also express that an action is expected of someone or is appropriate for them.

حَرًى is also used in this semantic field. In some uses, it remains unchanged for singular and plural, masculine and feminine, whereas ḥariyy can inflect according to Arabic adjectival patterns.

أَحْرَى (aḥrā) is the family’s comparative form, meaning more worthy, fitting, or suitable.

For example, saying that one action is aḥrā than another may mean that performing it is more suitable or fitting.

Suitability here does not always mean moral virtue. The subject may be an action’s appropriateness to circumstances, the likelihood of an event, or someone’s worthiness to receive something.

Ḥariyy should therefore not always be restricted to deserving divine reward or possessing a moral right.

Taḥarrā: seeking and aiming

تَحَرَّى is a form V verb, with present يَتَحَرَّى. Its definite verbal noun is التَّحَرِّي; the indefinite nominative and genitive form is تَحَرٍّ, while the accusative is تَحَرِّيًا.

The verb has several closely related Arabic uses.

In تَحَرَّى الشَّيْءَ, someone has deliberately and attentively sought something or turned toward it.

In تَحَرَّى الأَمْرَ, someone has attended to an affair or subject, aimed for it, or sought to choose it.

In تَحَرَّى فِي الْأُمُورِ, seeking the better or more suitable option is prominent.

In تَحَرَّى عَنِ الشَّيْءِ, especially in modern usage, the sense is searching, investigating, or examining in order to find facts or information.

Taḥarrī may therefore approach intending, considered searching, examination, or selection in different contexts. These uses should not all be imported into every sentence.

Lisān al-ʿArab and Tāj al-ʿArūs: suitability and taḥarrī

Ḥarā yaḥrī: diminishing and decreasing

Dictionaries record another form in this family: حَرَى الشَّيْءُ يَحْرِي حَرْيًا, meaning that something diminished or decreased.

Lexical explanations mention, for example, the moon’s gradual waning or a body’s diminution.

This branch differs clearly from being fitting and undertaking taḥarrī.

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris divides Ḥ R followed by a weak final consonant into three bases: a kind of heat, proximity and intention, and returning. When explaining the third, he connects ḥarā yaḥrī with returning and diminishing.

Collecting these words in one broad lexical entry does not, by itself, prove that they all developed from a single original image.

In particular, words such as حَرَاوَة (ḥarāwah), associated with pungency and a burning sensation and also recorded in forms with wāw, should not become the basic meaning of taḥarrā without independent historical research.

For the Qur’anic Ḥ R Y, it is therefore preferable to distinguish intention and suitability from diminution and other recorded uses.

Taḥarrī as remaining in a particular place

Early Arabic also records تَحَرَّى بِالْمَكَانِ as lingering or remaining in a place.

This shows that the verb does not always mean searching for something lost. Here a particular place and remaining there are in view.

Its connection with intending or turning toward a particular place can be examined, but should not be treated as decisive evidence that every semantic branch has the same historical origin.

3. Early Arabic evidence

Ibn Fāris’s analysis

Under Ḥ R W/Y in Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris examines a broad family of words with ḥāʾ, rāʾ, and a weak final consonant.

In the proximity and intention branch, he discusses ḥariyy as fitting or worthy and connects يَتَحَرَّى الْأَمْرَ with aiming for the affair.

This explanation matters for understanding the relationship between ḥariyy and taḥarrā.

When something is considered more fitting or suitable, a person may choose it to perform or obtain. There is thus a semantic relationship between suitability and considered selection.

The relationship fits the recorded uses, but should not be made into a certain account of the root’s earliest development.

Ibn Fāris, Maqāyīs al-Lughah: three bases under Ḥ R W/Y

A poetic witness attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays

In a rain description attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Imruʾ al-Qays, we read:

دِيمَةٌ هَطْلَاءُ فِيهَا وَطَفٌ
طَبَقُ الْأَرْضِ تَحَرَّى وَتَدُرُّ

dīmatun haṭlāʾu fīhā waṭafun / ṭabaqu al-arḍi taḥarrā wa-tadurru

Explanatory translation: “Steady, pouring rain, with trailing cloud covering the earth; it aims for the place and pours water continuously.”

The verse is transmitted in early sources, including Al-Jāḥiẓ’s Kitāb al-Ḥayawān and later dictionaries.

Here تَحَرَّى concerns aiming for and turning toward a particular place. The commentary understands it as تَتَحَرَّى: a feminine present-tense form with one of the two initial tāʾs omitted, rather than the identical-looking singular past taḥarrā. The rain or cloud is pictured as if it targets a place, stays over it, and rains upon it.

Ibn Qutaybah, Al-Shiʿr wa-l-Shuʿarāʾ: Imruʾ al-Qays’s witness and tataḥarrā

The use matters because taḥarrā here is not a search for a moral or religious truth. The verb concerns aiming for a place upon which rain falls.

Taḥarrī in Arabic is therefore broader than seeking truth. Depending on context, it may also express a purposeful turn toward a place or subject.

Its attribution to a pre-Islamic poet is important evidence for the verb’s history. Nevertheless, the poem reaches us through later transmission and compilation, so historical caution about the precise age of its wording remains appropriate.

Al-Jāḥiẓ, Kitāb al-Ḥayawān: the rain witness

A poetic witness from Labīd

Lisān al-ʿArab quotes a verse by Labīd to explain ḥariyy:

مِنْ حَيَاةٍ قَدْ سَئِمْنَا طُولَهَا
وَحَرِيٌّ طُولُ عَيْشٍ أَنْ يُمَلَّ

min ḥayātin qad saʾimnā ṭūlahā / wa-ḥariyyun ṭūlu ʿayshin an yumalla

“Of a life whose length has wearied us; and a long life is indeed apt to become wearisome.”

Here ḥariyy means fitting or apt, rather than seeking.

The poet says that a long life may reach a point at which becoming wearisome is to be expected.

The witness clarifies the distinction between two important uses: ḥariyy describes suitability, whereas taḥarrā is a verb of aiming and seeking.

Labīd lived through the transition from the pre-Islamic into the Islamic period. The verse is therefore evidence of early Arabic, but the lexical quotation alone does not establish exactly when it was composed.

Lisān al-ʿArab: Labīd’s witness for ḥariyy

4. The root’s sole Qur’anic occurrence

Al-Jinn 72:14

وَأَنَّا مِنَّا الْمُسْلِمُونَ وَمِنَّا الْقَاسِطُونَ فَمَنْ أَسْلَمَ فَأُولَئِكَ تَحَرَّوْا رَشَدًا

wa-annā minnā al-muslimūna wa-minnā al-qāsiṭūna fa-man aslama fa-ulāʾika taḥarraw rashadan

Suggested translation: “And among us are those who submit, and among us are the unjust and those who deviate; so whoever submits: those are the ones who have deliberately and earnestly sought sound guidance.”

This is the only Qur’anic verse containing تَحَرَّوْا from Ḥ R Y.

The verse belongs to the speech of jinn who have listened to the Qur’an. At the surah’s beginning, they describe hearing a wondrous Qur’an that guides toward sound guidance, as in Al-Jinn 72:1 and Al-Jinn 72:2.

In Al-Jinn 72:11, they also say that some among them are righteous and others fall below that standing, and that they have followed different ways.

Al-Jinn 72:14 then distinguishes between الْمُسْلِمُونَ and الْقَاسِطُونَ.

Al-muslimūn belongs to S L M, and al-qāsiṭūn to Q S Ṭ. In this form, qāsiṭūn denotes the unjust or those who deviate from what is right. It differs from مُقْسِطُونَ, those who act justly.

In the verse’s second part, فَمَنْ أَسْلَمَ is a conditional clause. Its response begins with فَأُولَئِكَ, and تَحَرَّوْا رَشَدًا describes the action of those who submit.

Morphologically, تَحَرَّوْا is a third-person masculine plural past-tense verb of form V. Its singular is تَحَرَّى, and the final wāw in taḥarraw is the plural subject pronoun. This is the plural wāw, not the root’s final consonant: the weak verb’s final yāʾ is deleted before its attachment.

رَشَدًا is the verb’s accusative object, specifying what has been sought or aimed for.

The meaning of taḥarraw rashadan

The phrase describes those who have sought sound guidance and turned toward it.

Taḥarrī is not confined to intellectual or theoretical investigation.

A person may investigate a path, recognize it as correct, and then decide to choose it. Conversely, someone may speak extensively about truth and acquire considerable information, yet choose another path in practice.

Taḥarraw may express deliberate intention and choice. In this verse, where it accompanies فَمَنْ أَسْلَمَ, seeking sound guidance is connected with practically turning toward it.

Nevertheless, a complete theory of cognition, decision, and action should not be extracted from taḥarraw alone. The verse makes clear that this group’s submission belongs to a course of seeking sound guidance.

Although taḥarraw has past-tense form, the sentence does not mean that a person must first submit and only afterwards receive permission to seek truth. The conditional relationship does not establish the temporal order of every stage of seeking and submission.

The sentence naturally means that those who submit have, in this choice, sought and aimed for sound guidance.

Does taḥarrī always lead to truth?

Lexically, taḥarrā expresses purposeful effort to find or choose something. The verb alone does not establish that every choice made through such effort is necessarily correct.

For example, someone may carefully choose what they think best among several paths while lacking complete information. Their effort and intention are real, but whether the choice is correct requires an independent criterion.

In Al-Jinn 72:14, the object sought is رَشَدًا, and the context contrasts it with the path of the unjust. This use’s positive direction therefore comes from sound guidance, submission, and the verse’s contrast.

If the same verb is used elsewhere for seeking personal benefit or choosing a different destination, it need not carry the same religious or moral value.

Taḥarrī thus names seeking and aiming; rushd specifies the action’s desirable value and direction in this verse.

The relationship with nearby verses

In Al-Jinn 72:2, the speakers describe a Qur’an that guides toward sound guidance.

In Al-Jinn 72:13, they speak of hearing guidance and believing in it.

In Al-Jinn 72:14, they distinguish those who submit from the unjust and describe the former as seekers of sound guidance.

Al-Jinn 72:15 describes the outcome for the unjust.

This continuity shows that rushd is not incidental or isolated in their speech. It is connected with hearing guidance, belief, and choosing a path.

The words must nevertheless remain distinct. Guidance belongs to H D Y; belief, to ʾ M N; submission, to S L M; and rushd, to R SH D.

The concepts are related in the verses, but none is the lexical definition of Ḥ R Y.

5. Taḥarrī and related words

Taḥarrī and ṭalab

طَلَب (ṭalab) means wanting or seeking something in Arabic and has a broad range.

A person may seek wealth, food, knowledge, power, or something else.

Taḥarrī also concerns seeking, but many of its uses emphasize choice, a definite aim, and effort to find what is considered more suitable.

The distinction should not be absolute: ṭalab too may involve care, seriousness, and selection in an appropriate context.

Taḥarrī and qaṣd

قَصْد (qaṣd) concerns turning toward a direction or goal.

Intention is also present in taḥarrī, but is often accompanied by attention to what someone seeks to find or choose.

When someone aims for a destination, the simple orientation may be the subject. In taḥarrī, choosing or seeking the goal becomes more prominent.

This is a distinction in usage, rather than a fixed boundary governing every Arabic sentence.

Taḥarrī and tawakhkhī

تَوَخَّى (tawakhkhā) is among the closest words in meaning to taḥarrā.

Lexicographers explain both through intending and turning toward something. In many sentences they can be very close in meaning.

Some uses emphasize a precise direction or goal in tawakhkhī and seeking or choosing what is considered more suitable in taḥarrī.

This difference too should not become an unchangeable rule.

Taḥarrī and investigation

In contemporary Persian, taḥarrī commonly appears in expressions such as seeking truth, investigating a crime, or investigations.

These uses emphasize examination, searching for information, and discovering facts.

The meaning is connected with part of taḥarrā’s lexical field, but is insufficient for translating Al-Jinn 72:14.

Translating تَحَرَّوْا رَشَدًا merely as “they investigated sound guidance” may understate the intention and choice involved in the verse’s context.

Conversely, translating it only as “they attained sound guidance” removes seeking and purposeful choice.

It is therefore preferable for the Persian translation to retain both principal aspects as far as possible: seeking sound guidance and turning toward it.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

The different forms and semantic branches of Ḥ R Y must be distinguished in translation.

حَرِيّ (ḥariyy): worthy, fitting, appropriate.

أَحْرَى (aḥrā): more worthy, fitting, or suitable.

تَحَرَّى (taḥarrā): deliberately sought, pursued, aimed for, or chose with consideration, according to context.

تَحَرٍّ (taḥarrin): purposeful searching, considered examination, or effort to find and choose something.

حَرَى يَحْرِي (ḥarā yaḥrī): diminished or decreased; a different branch of the family’s recorded uses.

For the sole Qur’anic use, تَحَرَّوْا رَشَدًا in Al-Jinn 72:14, several renderings merit consideration:

“They have sought sound guidance” is brief and close to the Arabic construction, but may leave intention and selection less explicit.

“They have deliberately and earnestly sought sound guidance” makes those aspects clearer while remaining close to the verb’s meaning.

“They have chosen sound guidance” suits the context of submission, but by itself may understate seeking.

“They have attained sound guidance” expresses a positive result but replaces the act of seeking with its outcome, and is therefore not the most precise direct translation.

The proposed rendering of the Qur’anic phrase is therefore:

“So whoever submits: those are the ones who have deliberately and earnestly sought sound guidance.”

7. Conclusion

Ḥ R Y has several recorded Arabic semantic branches. The branch involving ḥariyy and taḥarrā concerns suitability, intention, and seeking what is considered more suitable or fitting. Ḥarā yaḥrī concerns diminishing and decrease, and should not be mixed into taḥarrī without reason.

Early Arabic evidence shows that taḥarrā was not limited to seeking religious truth. In the poem attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays, it concerns rain aiming for and falling upon a place. By contrast, ḥariyy in Labīd’s poetry means fitting or apt.

The Qur’an uses only تَحَرَّوْا from this root, together with رَشَدًا.

The phrase expresses seeking and aiming for sound guidance. Its desirable direction comes from rushd and the verse’s context, not from taḥarrī necessarily ending in truth and goodness in every use.

Al-Jinn 72:14 presents those who submit as people who have sought sound guidance and turned toward it. The verb thus fits deliberate, purposeful choice without requiring the entire meanings of belief, guidance, or rushd to be placed inside the root’s definition.

A short root definition for Forqan:

Brief root meaning: In the branch involving ḥariyy and taḥarrā, Ḥ R Y concerns suitability, intention, and purposeful seeking. Taḥarrā means seeking or aiming for something attentively and earnestly, particularly what is considered more suitable or fitting. Qur’anic تَحَرَّوْا رَشَدًا expresses seeking sound guidance and purposefully turning toward it.
