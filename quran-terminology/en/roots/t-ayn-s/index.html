Qur'anic Root T AYN S

Stumbling, falling face-down, being overthrown, and losing the ability to recover one's stable path.

Core Meaning

The root ت ع س appears in the Qur'an through the word ta'san. This root occurs in only one Qur'anic expression:

فَتَعْسًا لَهُمْ وَأَضَلَّ أَعْمَالَهُمْ

So ruinous downfall is for them, and He made their deeds lose their way and result.

Muhammad 47:8

T AYN S is originally connected with slipping, falling face-down, being overthrown, and being unable to recover balance. From this material image, meanings such as failure, ill outcome, and being deprived of success develop.

The root T AYN S indicates slipping, being overthrown, falling with the face or hands to the ground, and remaining in a fallen state.

Ta's is not merely a passing fall. At its core is a fall that cuts off motion and makes rising again, continuing, and reaching the destination difficult or impossible.

The semantic core may be summarized as a slipping and overthrowing that removes the person from stable standing and blocks the path of rising and success.

Important Forms

تَعِسَ is a form I past verb: he slipped and fell; fell face-down; was overthrown; became unsuccessful and ill-fated. This verbal form does not occur in the Qur'an.

يَتْعَسُ is the imperfect verb: he slips, falls, or comes to ruinous failure. This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

تَعْس is the verbal noun of the root: slipping, falling, ruinous downfall, failure, and ill outcome. This unaccusative form does not occur in the Qur'an.

تَعْسًا is the accusative verbal noun and is used in a curse-like, declarative, or result structure:

فَتَعْسًا لَهُمْ

So ruinous downfall is for them.

Muhammad 47:8

This is the only clear Qur'anic form of the root.

أَتْعَسَ is a form IV verb: to cast someone down, bring someone to ruinous downfall, or make someone fail. This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

مَتْعُوس is a lexical passive participle: fallen, ruined, or ill-fated. This form also does not occur in the Qur'an.

Linguistic Note

The core of T AYN S is movement from stability into a fallen low state.

In an ordinary state, a human being stands on his feet, has balance, and can move forward on a path. When his foot slips and he falls on his face or hands, his movement is cut off. If he can rise again, the fall is a passing incident; but in ta's, the image of a fall is present in which recovery is difficult or impossible.

Several elements join in this root: moving along a path, losing balance, falling downward, the body striking the ground, stopping movement, and the way forward being closed.

But the Qur'anic use of this root is not about an unintended natural slip. Surah Muhammad attaches ta's to those who knowingly disliked what The God sent down:

ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ كَرِهُوا مَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ

That is because they disliked what The God sent down.

Muhammad 47:9

So here the person has not merely struck an external obstacle. He has chosen a path that is incompatible with truth. Ta's is the outcome of motion on a path that has neither stability nor a right destination.

The difference may be stated like this: in 'ithar, the foot may unintentionally hit an obstacle. In Qur'anic ta's, a wrong direction and persistence upon it bring the motion to overthrow.

This does not mean that the root T AYN S itself means “moral choice.” Choice and responsibility come from the context of the surah; the lexical core remains slipping and falling.

Material Image

The material image of T AYN S is a human being or animal that loses balance while moving, falls face-down, and can no longer continue the path in the previous way.

The stages of the image are: standing and moving; the foot slipping; balance being lost; overthrow; the face or hands striking the ground; movement stopping; and difficulty rising.

This image is not identical with falling from a height. In falling from a height, the axis is descent from an elevated place. In ta's, the axis is slipping in the path and being thrown down while moving.

Ta's also differs from simple weakness. A person may become weak and still remain standing. In ta's, actual stability is gone and the body, or the path, has fallen to the ground.

At a non-material level, the same image is used for someone who has action and movement, but whose direction is so wrong that the movement does not become progress; rather, it overthrows him.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, ta's was used for slipping, falling face-down, ruinous downfall, and failure.

In curse-like structures, one would say ta'san lahu, meaning may falling, ruinous downfall, or ill outcome be upon him.

Expressions of the type “ta'isa fa-la int'asha” also clarify this field: he fell and could no longer lift himself or rise again. The axis of this usage is not only contact with the ground; the inability to regain a stable state is also part of the image.

From this material image, the word expands into the fields of failure, the closing of the way to success, being deprived of good, ill outcome, and destruction.

But “misery” alone is not a precise equivalent, because it removes the root's bodily and motion-based image.

Qur'anic Use

وَالَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا فَتَعْسًا لَهُمْ وَأَضَلَّ أَعْمَالَهُمْ

And those who covered truth, so ruinous downfall is for them, and He made their deeds lose their way and result.

Muhammad 47:8

Ta'san in this verse stands between two levels: the state of the people themselves, and the state of their deeds.

They themselves reach ta's, meaning they are overthrown and fail. Their deeds also lose their way and result.

So the verse states two things that are distinct but connected: ta's, the falling of the doer; and idlal of deeds, the loss of the deed's direction and destination.

This distinction matters. The Qur'an has not merely placed two synonyms beside each other. One concerns the actor; the other concerns what the actor produced.

Ta's: Curse or Announced Outcome

The structure fa-ta'san lahum can have a curse-like tone: “may ruinous downfall be upon them.” At the same time, it can announce an outcome: “ruinous downfall is their share.”

These two layers are not completely separate in language. The phrase expresses both condemnation and the outcome of the path they chose.

Translating it as “death to them” is too narrow, because ta's is not only death. Translating it as “misery for them” also hides the image of slipping and overthrow.

T AYN S and Choosing the Wrong Path

ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ كَرِهُوا مَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ

That is because they disliked what The God sent down.

Muhammad 47:9

So ta's is not accidental bad luck. They took a position against what had been sent down and disliked it.

The image may be understood like this: a way of stability and guidance was offered; they turned away from it; they continued another path; and that path ended in overthrow.

In a natural stumble, an external obstacle may make the human foot slip. In this context, the chosen direction itself creates the ground of the fall.

But one should not say that the person “intentionally wants to fall.” He has another goal, but he chooses a path whose structural outcome is ta's.

Contrast With Firm Feet

In the same surah, regarding those who have faith, it says:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنْ تَنْصُرُوا اللَّهَ يَنْصُرْكُمْ وَيُثَبِّتْ أَقْدَامَكُمْ

O you who have faith, if you support The God, He will support you and make your feet firm.

Muhammad 47:7

Immediately after that, regarding those who covered truth, it says:

فَتَعْسًا لَهُمْ

So ruinous downfall is for them.

Muhammad 47:8

These two verses build a clear movement contrast: firming the feet, against ta's and overthrow.

On one side, the feet are made firm and the possibility of continuing the path remains. On the other side, movement reaches slipping and falling.

This contrast strengthens the material field of T AYN S. Ta's is not merely sadness or psychological defeat; it stands opposite to standing and moving with firm feet.

However, the word “istiqamah” itself does not occur in this verse. The direct contrast is between yuthabbit aqdamakum and ta'san lahum.

T AYN S and the Deeds Losing Their Way

وَأَضَلَّ أَعْمَالَهُمْ

And He made their deeds lose their way and result.

Muhammad 47:8

The idlal of deeds means that their efforts do not find a way to the desired result.

An action may outwardly be large, costly, or organized, but still lose the true destination.

The relationship between the two parts is this: the doer is overthrown; the deed also exits the path of result.

At the image level: the foot slips, and the path also does not lead to the destination.

T AYN S, Idlal, and Ihbat

The next verse says:

فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَالَهُمْ

So He made their deeds come to nothing.

Muhammad 47:9

In these two verses, three nearby fields stand beside one another: ta's, idlal of deeds, and ihbat of deeds.

Ta's: فَتَعْسًا لَهُمْ concerns the people themselves: the overthrow and ruinous downfall of the doer.

Idlal of deeds: وَأَضَلَّ أَعْمَالَهُمْ concerns the direction and destination of the deeds: the deeds lose their way and do not reach the right result.

Ihbat of deeds: فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَالَهُمْ concerns the effect and value of the deed: the deed becomes fruitless and loses its effect.

The difference may be summarized like this: in ta's, the actor falls; in idlal, the deed loses the path to the destination; in ihbat, the effect and outcome of the deed collapses.

These three words do not repeat one another. They show three layers of one collapse: the fall of the person, the loss of the effort's path, and the disappearance of the effort's effect.

Still, the phrase “the inner content of the deed becomes void” is explanatory. The core of H B T should be studied in its own root article.

Much Motion on a Wrong Path

Ta's shows that motion alone is not a sign of guidance. A person may strive intensely, but if the direction of motion is wrong, greater speed does not necessarily bring him closer to the destination.

Sometimes more movement on a wrong path brings the overthrow closer.

This sentence is a reflection on the image of the root, not the direct lexical meaning of ta's.

Similar Uses

'Ithar and ta's: 'A-th-r indicates the foot catching on an obstacle and slipping. Ta's emphasizes the more severe outcome: the person slips, is overthrown, and rising or continuing is disrupted. 'Ithar is the incident of stumbling; ta's is stumbling with a ruinous outcome. 'Ithar may be unintended and passing. Ta's in the Qur'anic context is the outcome of a path on which the person knowingly stands.

Ta's and suqut: Suqut indicates falling from a place or height. Ta's is closer to slipping while moving and falling face-down. Suqut means falling downward; ta's means slipping and being overthrown together with failure.

Ta's and kabb: Kabb indicates throwing face-down or falling upon the face. Ta's can lead to kabb, but beyond the bodily posture, it also includes the inability to continue and the ill outcome. Kabb shows the form of falling; ta's shows the slipping and its ruinous result.

Ta's and naks: Naks indicates being inverted or returned to a lower state. Ta's focuses on slipping and falling in the path; naks on the reversal of direction or condition.

Ta's and halak: Halak indicates perishing and the end of remaining. Ta's can lead to destruction, but its main image is overthrow. Halak means he perished; ta's means he fell and lost the way to success.

Ta's and tabab: Tabab indicates the fruitlessness of effort and collapse of benefit and result. Ta's focuses on the fall of the one walking the path. Tabab means the result and function reached dead end; ta's means the person on the path slipped and was overthrown.

Ta's and khusran: Khusran indicates losing capital, gain, or opportunity. Ta's emphasizes the bodily and motion-based state of falling. Khusran means he lost something; ta's means he himself fell on the path.

Ta's and fashal: Fashal indicates weakness, loss of force, and inability to continue. Ta's goes beyond weakness and reaches overthrow. Fashal means firmness weakened; ta's means motion ended in falling.

Ta's and dalal: Dalal indicates losing the way. Ta's emphasizes the person falling in the path. In Muhammad 47:8, both fields stand together: ta's for the people and idlal for the deeds.

Ta's and habt: Habt indicates the loss of effect and outcome of an action. Ta's is the state of the doer; habt is the state of the deed's effect. Ta's means the person came to ruinous downfall; habt means the deed lost its effect.

Grammar and Structure

In فَتَعْسًا لَهُمْ, ta'san is an accusative verbal noun and its verb is omitted. This structure can carry a curse-like or declarative meaning: ruinous downfall be upon them; ruinous downfall is for them; may they be overthrown and fail.

The omission of the verb makes the phrase short, decisive, and heavy.

The fa in fa-ta'san connects ta's to the previous description:

وَالَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا فَتَعْسًا لَهُمْ

And those who covered truth, so ruinous downfall is for them.

Muhammad 47:8

Ta's is not an outcome separated from their conduct; it is connected to their covering and prior direction.

The lam in lahum assigns the result to them: ruinous downfall for them; overthrow as their share; failure upon them.

In English, “upon them” carries the curse-like tone, while “is their share” highlights the announced outcome.

The waw in وَأَضَلَّ أَعْمَالَهُمْ links the condition of the people themselves with the condition of their deeds: they are overthrown, and their deeds also lose their path and result.

The fa in فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَالَهُمْ connects the ihbat of the deeds to the preceding cause:

كَرِهُوا مَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ

They disliked what The God sent down.

Muhammad 47:9

Disliking what was sent down leads to the deeds losing their effect. This relation comes from the context of the verse, and ihbat should not be treated as an accidental event.

Nearby Roots

T AYN S and A TH R: A TH R indicates the foot slipping and striking an obstacle. Ta's emphasizes overthrow and inability to recover afterward.

T AYN S and S Q T: Suqut is falling down. Ta's is slipping and falling face-down together with failure.

T AYN S and K B B: Kabb is throwing down upon the face. Ta's emphasizes the outcome of this falling and the closing of the way forward.

T AYN S and N K S: Naks is being inverted or returned downward. Ta's is slipping and being overthrown while on the path.

T AYN S and H L K: Halak is perishing. Ta's is falling and becoming ruined, though it may lead to destruction.

T AYN S and T B B: Tabab is the fruitlessness of the result. Ta's is the overthrow of the person who strives.

T AYN S and KH S R: Khusran is losing gain and capital. Ta's is the person himself falling.

T AYN S and F SH L: Fashal is loss of strength and firmness. Ta's is the stage where that instability has ended in falling.

T AYN S and D L L: Dalal is losing the way. Ta's is falling in the way.

T AYN S and H B T: Habt is the loss of the deed's effect. Ta's is the ruinous downfall of the doer.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For تَعِسَ: he slipped and fell; fell face-down; was overthrown; came to ruinous downfall; failed and came to an ill outcome.

For تَعْس: slipping and overthrow; ruinous downfall; fallen state; failure; ill outcome.

For تَعْسًا لَهُ: ruinous downfall be upon him; overthrow is his share; falling and failure are for him; may he be overthrown and fail.

For فَتَعْسًا لَهُمْ: so ruinous downfall be upon them; so overthrow and failure be upon them; so ruinous downfall is their share; so falling and ill outcome are for them.

For فَتَعْسًا لَهُمْ وَأَضَلَّ أَعْمَالَهُمْ, a close translation: “So ruinous downfall is for them, and He made their deeds lose their way and result.”

For a clearer translation: “So overthrow and failure be upon them, and The God made their deeds leave the path and result.” Or: “So ruinous downfall is their share, and their efforts lost the way to their destination.”

In the standard translation, it is better to clarify the subject of adalla from the context, while keeping clear that this is an added clarification in English.

Summary

The root ت ع س indicates slipping, falling face-down, being overthrown, and being unable to recover stable standing. Its material image is a human being or animal whose foot slips while moving, who falls to the ground, and whose path forward is closed.

In Surah Muhammad, ta's stands opposite the firming of the feet. Those who have faith continue with firm feet, while those who dislike what The God sent down reach ruinous downfall. Therefore ta's in this context is not an accidental stumble; it is the outcome of movement on a path that has become incompatible with truth.

The verses place three layers of collapse beside one another: ta's, the doer is overthrown; idlal of deeds, his effort loses its path and destination; and ihbat of deeds, the effect and outcome of the effort vanish.

T AYN S is close to 'ithar, suqut, kabb, naks, halak, tabab, khusran, fashal, dalal, and habt, but it is not identical with any of them. Its specific core is a slipping and overthrow that removes the person from stability and takes away the possibility of rising, continuing, and reaching success.
