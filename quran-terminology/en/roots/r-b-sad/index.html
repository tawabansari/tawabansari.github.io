Waiting and expectation, pausing until a particular time or event, watching for an opportunity, and awaiting others’ outcomes in the Qur’an

In early Arabic, R B Ṣ refers to waiting and pausing in expectation of an event or a particular time. Sometimes the expectation concerns a useful opportunity, sometimes the removal of an obstacle, and sometimes an event that would harm someone else.

In the Qur’an, the root occurs in two very different settings: waiting for a specified period, as in provisions concerning marital relationships and the end of marriage; and waiting for circumstances to change, for others to win or lose, for someone to die, or for the outcome of a confrontation to become clear.

Tarabbuṣ therefore does not inherently mean plotting, hostile ambush, inactivity, or postponing a duty. Such meanings may arise from particular contexts, while elsewhere waiting may itself form part of a clear, legitimate instruction.

The central meaning of this root can be expressed as follows:

الرَّاءُ وَالْبَاءُ وَالصَّادُ أَصْلٌ وَاحِدٌ يَدُلُّ عَلَى الِانْتِظَارِ.

al-rāʾu wa-al-bāʾu wa-al-ṣādu aṣlun wāḥidun yadullu ʿalā al-intiẓār

That is: “Rāʾ, bāʾ, and ṣād form one root indicating waiting.”

تَرَبَّصْتُ بِهِ and رُبْصَة are uses within this family.

1. Qur’anic frequency and forms

R B Ṣ occurs 17 times in 12 verses across seven surahs. Every occurrence belongs to the Form V family, in three grammatical forms:

Active participle مُتَرَبِّص3
Total17

The occurrences are in Al-Baqarah 2:226, Al-Baqarah 2:228, Al-Baqarah 2:234; Al-Nisāʾ 4:141; Al-Tawbah 9:24, Al-Tawbah 9:52, Al-Tawbah 9:98; Ṭā Hā 20:135; Al-Muʾminūn 23:25; Al-Ṭūr 52:30, Al-Ṭūr 52:31; and Al-Ḥadīd 57:14.

The basic verb رَبَصَ and the noun رُبْصَة, although attested in Arabic, do not occur in the Qur’an. مُحْتَكِر belongs to the separate root Ḥ K R and explains one economic use of mutarabbiṣ; it is not a derivative of R B Ṣ.

2. Basic meaning and lexical family

رَبَصَ and تَرَبَّصَ: waiting with a pause

A short Arabic definition of tarabbuṣ is:

التَّرَبُّصُ: الْانْتِظَارُ.

al-tarabbuṣu: al-intiẓār

That is: “Tarabbuṣ means waiting.”

Both the basic verb and Form V can describe waiting for good or harm to befall another person.

This range is attested in Arabic usage; expectation in this family is therefore not restricted from the outset to an unwelcome event.

Tarabbuṣ also describes goods whose rise or fall in price someone awaits, or an event whose occurrence or cessation someone expects.

Three elements can be identified in these uses: a situation exists now, someone expects a change or something to arrive, and that person pauses in deciding or acting until then.

These elements must not, however, always be interpreted as deliberately postponing a duty. Waiting may itself be what the person is required to do.

رَبَصَ يَرْبُصُ: the basic verb

The basic verb رَبَصَ, with the verbal noun رَبْص, is attested in Arabic dictionaries.

رَبَصَ بِالشَّيْءِ means to wait for something or pause in expectation of an event concerning it.

رَبَصَنِي أَمْرٌ is also attested in Arabic: a matter kept me waiting.

In this construction, the event itself can be what causes someone to wait.

The basic verb does not occur in the Qur’an. All Qur’anic verbal occurrences belong to Form V.

تَرَبَّصَ يَتَرَبَّصُ: remaining expectant

تَرَبَّصَ is Form V, and its verbal noun is تَرَبُّص.

The verb can take the preposition بِـ, as in تَرَبَّصَ بِهِ: he waited for something to happen concerning that person.

It can also take a direct object, as in تَرَبَّصَ الْأَمْرَ: he awaited that event.

Consider the difference between these two expressions:

تَرَبَّصْتُ الْأَمْرَ: I awaited that event.

تَرَبَّصْتُ بِفُلَانٍ أَمْرًا: I waited for that event to happen to so-and-so.

Both involve waiting, but the second construction also identifies the person to whom the anticipated event would happen.

This distinction matters for understanding يَتَرَبَّصُونَ بِكُمْ and نَتَرَبَّصُ بِهِ رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ in the Qur’an.

رُبْصَة: waiting and a period of delay

رُبْصَة is a noun associated with waiting and pausing.

An expression concerning waiting to sell goods is:

لِي فِي مَتَاعِي رُبْصَةٌ

lī fī matāʿī rubṣatun

That is: “I have a period of waiting concerning my goods.”

This can describe a seller who holds goods until a suitable time to sell arrives.

لِي بِالْبَصْرَةِ رُبْصَةٌ is also attested: someone has a period of delay or waiting in Basra.

In ordinary usage, rubṣah thus refers to delay or a waiting period without necessarily specifying its cause.

رُبْصَة in marital relationships

Another use of this word is: أَقَامَتِ الْمَرْأَةُ رُبْصَتَهَا فِي بَيْتِ زَوْجِهَا.

This expression concerns a waiting period assigned in a particular legal situation to clarify whether a marital relationship would continue.

The usage shows that rubṣah could also denote a specified waiting period within a family relationship.

This lexical report is not itself a Qur’anic command. Details of the ruling recorded in a dictionary must not be transferred to verses about the waiting period after marriage.

مُتَرَبِّص: one who waits

مُتَرَبِّص is the active participle of Form V.

Generally, it describes someone awaiting an event or a change in circumstances.

In one economic use, mutarabbiṣ is explained as follows:

وَالْمُتَرَبِّصُ: الْمُحْتَكِرُ.

wa-al-mutarabbiṣu: al-muḥtakir

That is: “The mutarabbiṣ is the hoarder.”

This explanation must be understood within its economic setting.

A hoarder holds goods to benefit from changing market conditions and may therefore be called a mutarabbiṣ in Arabic.

But not every mutarabbiṣ is a hoarder, just as not all waiting is hoarding.

The Qur’an itself uses مُتَرَبِّص in Ṭā Hā 20:135 and Al-Ṭūr 52:31 without any reference to goods or markets.

Less frequent uses

A use of رُبْصَة concerning color is also attested and has been compared with رُبْشَة.

This branch has no obvious semantic connection with waiting. The available evidence is also insufficient to establish a shared historical origin with tarabbuṣ.

Later sources also record uses of رِبْص involving irrigated land and cultivation, but their historical status and precise relationship to R B Ṣ are less clear than the meaning of waiting.

These uses should not enter the root’s central definition or its Qur’anic meaning.

Despite differences among some forms, early dictionaries show substantial agreement on the family’s central meaning: waiting and pausing in expectation of something.

3. Early Arabic and poetic evidence

Waiting for a relationship’s circumstances to change

One of the principal poetic witnesses for this root is:

تَرَبَّصْ بِهَا رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ لَعَلَّهَا
تُطَلَّقُ يَوْمًا أَوْ يَمُوتُ حَلِيلُهَا

tarabbaṣ bihā rayba al-manūni laʿallahā / tuṭallaqu yawman aw yamūtu ḥalīluhā

Meaning: “Await the vicissitudes of time concerning her; perhaps one day she will be divorced, or her husband will die.”

Here, someone awaits an event that would change a woman’s marital circumstances.

Two lexical points matter.

First, تَرَبَّصْ does not mean directly acting to divorce or kill someone. The person waits for an event to happen.

Second, رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ names what is expected to occur. Tarabbuṣ does not itself denote death or a calamity; another expression identifies that event.

The verse has been preserved in different literary and exegetical transmissions.

It is attributed to Farāḍ ibn ʿUtaybah al-Azdī, a poet assigned to the pre-Islamic period. Another transmission ends أَوْ يَمُوتَ حَمِيمُهَا.

The second hemistich has different transmitted readings. These textual differences must therefore be considered when examining it.

Literary transmission supports its attribution to a pre-Islamic poet, but the differences in wording and transmission must be retained. No single reading can be presented without explanation as its certain and sole text.

The grammatical importance of this witness

This poetic verse has also been used to explain the construction of tarabbaṣa.

The point is that the verb can take a direct object in addition to a prepositional phrase.

In تَرَبَّصْ بِهَا رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ, بها identifies the person concerned, while ريب المنون identifies the expected event.

The same distinction appears in Al-Ṭūr 52:30:

نَتَرَبَّصُ بِهِ رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ

natarabbaṣu bihi rayba al-manūn

“We await the vicissitudes of death and time concerning him.”

Early poetry thus illustrates both the meaning of waiting and a grammatical construction that helps explain the Qur’anic expression.

Waiting for goods to rise in price

An attested expression concerning waiting for a rise in price is:

تَرَبَّصَ بِسِلْعَتِهِ الْغَلَاءَ

tarabbaṣa bi-silʿatihi al-ghalāʾ

That is: “He held his goods and waited for their price to rise.”

Here, the seller awaits an external event that could benefit him.

This example differs from the expectation in the preceding poem. There, someone awaits a change in a relationship; here, a change in the price of goods.

In both, however, someone expects something to happen and holds an action or decision in abeyance until then.

This is an expression recorded in a dictionary, not a pre-Islamic poetic verse of known date. The authority and age of these two types of witness must not be presented as identical.

What the evidence shows

The linguistic evidence shows that tarabbuṣ can concern an unwelcome event, a change in family circumstances, a suitable economic opportunity, or other anticipated events.

None of these establishes that the root’s earliest meaning was necessarily “lying in wait to cause harm” or “awaiting someone’s death.”

These are particular uses that depend on the situation.

The more stable and general meaning is waiting for something to occur or change.

4. Three uses in family provisions

Al-Baqarah 2:226: a four-month wait

لِلَّذِينَ يُؤْلُونَ مِنْ نِسَائِهِمْ تَرَبُّصُ أَرْبَعَةِ أَشْهُرٍ فَإِنْ فَاءُوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ

li-alladhīna yuʾlūna min nisāʾihim tarabbuṣu arbaʿati ashhurin fa-in fāʾū fa-inna allāha ghafūrun raḥīm

“For those who swear abstinence from their wives, there is a wait of four months; then, if they return, The God is forgiving and merciful.”

Here تَرَبُّصُ is a nominative verbal noun in a genitive construction with أَرْبَعَةِ أَشْهُرٍ.

The duration of the wait is thus stated expressly: four months.

Īlāʾ belongs to the separate root A L W and here concerns a man’s oath to abstain from marital intercourse with his wife.

Tarabbuṣ itself, however, does not mean taking an oath or abstaining from marital intercourse. That subject comes from يُؤْلُونَ مِنْ نِسَائِهِمْ.

This verse and Al-Baqarah 2:227 address returning and deciding on divorce in two separate statements.

The four-month wait therefore belongs to a situation in which the continuation of the relationship must be clarified.

This does not mean that R B Ṣ itself denotes a divorce deadline or an opportunity reserved for men. The root means waiting; the verses specify its legal subject.

Al-Baqarah 2:228: waiting by divorced women

وَالْمُطَلَّقَاتُ يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنْفُسِهِنَّ ثَلَاثَةَ قُرُوءٍ

wa-al-muṭallaqātu yatarabbaṣna bi-anfusihinna thalāthata qurūʾ

“And divorced women shall wait concerning themselves for three qurūʾ.”

يَتَرَبَّصْنَ is a third-person feminine plural imperfect verb of Form V.

The final nūn is the feminine subject pronoun.

بِأَنْفُسِهِنَّ refers to the women themselves, while ثَلَاثَةَ قُرُوءٍ states the duration of the wait.

Here, the waiting concerns the women’s own circumstances after divorce, rather than an event that would harm someone else.

قُرُوء belongs to a separate root, and its precise linguistic and exegetical meaning is disputed.

One disputed meaning of qurūʾ should not be assumed certain in advance to explain tarabbuṣ.

The rest of the verse addresses not concealing what is in the wombs, returning when reconciliation is intended, and reciprocal rights.

This continuity shows that waiting here forms part of regulating family and legal circumstances.

يَتَرَبَّصْنَ itself, however, does not mean pregnancy, menstruation, resumption of marriage, or reconciliation. Separate words express each subject.

Al-Baqarah 2:234: waiting after a husband’s death

وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنْكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنْفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا

wa-alladhīna yutawaffawna minkum wa-yadharūna azwājan yatarabbaṣna bi-anfusihinna arbaʿata ashhurin wa-ʿashrā

“And those among you who die and leave wives behind—the women shall wait concerning themselves for four months and ten [days].”

The verbal form يَتَرَبَّصْنَ occurs here too.

The subject of waiting differs, however, from Al-Baqarah 2:228.

Al-Baqarah 2:228 concerned divorce; here, the subject is the husband’s death.

The period also differs: four months and ten. In the usual grammatical explanation, ʿashr implies nights, with their days included in the period. Hence the rendering “four months and ten days.”

The different durations must therefore not be included in the definition of tarabbuṣ.

Tarabbuṣ itself means waiting; the sentence specifies the period and what the wait concerns.

Al-Baqarah 2:240 should also be studied separately here. That verse speaks of a bequest and provision for wives for up to a year, without expelling them.

Forqan’s published article “Naskh in the Qur’an” also examines the different subjects of the two verses: waiting four months and ten in Al-Baqarah 2:234, and a bequest and maintenance for up to a year in Al-Baqarah 2:240.

Naskh in the Qur’an: Forqan study

These two periods should not be assumed to govern a single requirement merely because both occur within a family-related subject.

What the three verses show

The three uses in Al-Baqarah show that tarabbuṣ can form part of a specified, legitimate instruction.

Al-Baqarah 2:226 specifies four months in the situation of īlāʾ.

In Al-Baqarah 2:228, divorced women wait for a specified period.

Al-Baqarah 2:234 concerns waiting after a husband’s death.

None of these three uses inherently conveys plotting or waiting to cause harm.

Nor does any justify translating tarabbuṣ throughout the Qur’an as “women’s waiting period,” because the root’s other uses occur in entirely different settings.

5. Awaiting opportunities and changing circumstances in social confrontations

Al-Nisāʾ 4:141: waiting to benefit from others’ outcomes

الَّذِينَ يَتَرَبَّصُونَ بِكُمْ فَإِنْ كَانَ لَكُمْ فَتْحٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ قَالُوا أَلَمْ نَكُنْ مَعَكُمْ وَإِنْ كَانَ لِلْكَافِرِينَ نَصِيبٌ قَالُوا أَلَمْ نَسْتَحْوِذْ عَلَيْكُمْ وَنَمْنَعْكُمْ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ

alladhīna yatarabbaṣūna bikum fa-in kāna lakum fatḥun mina allāhi qālū a-lam nakun maʿakum wa-in kāna li-al-kāfirīna naṣībun qālū a-lam nastaḥwidh ʿalaykum wa-namnaʿkum mina al-muʾminīn

“Those who wait concerning you: if you receive an opening from The God, they say, ‘Were we not with you?’ And if the disbelievers gain a share, they say, ‘Did we not gain mastery over you and protect you from the believers?’”

يَتَرَبَّصُونَ بِكُمْ describes a group waiting for the outcome of a confrontation to become clear.

The following words show that they want to use either result for their own interests.

If the believers succeed, they recall their association with them.

If the other side gains a share, they also claim a role and an association before that side.

Waiting in this context is therefore accompanied by opportunism and changing position according to the outcome.

The verb يَتَرَبَّصُونَ itself, however, does not mean hypocrisy or opportunism. Those characteristics emerge from the behavior subsequently described.

The distinction matters: someone can await an event’s outcome without seeking to align themselves for advantage with whichever side wins.

Al-Tawbah 9:24: waiting as a warning

After naming family, wealth, trade, and homes that people love, Al-Tawbah 9:24 says:

فَتَرَبَّصُوا حَتَّىٰ يَأْتِيَ اللَّهُ بِأَمْرِهِ

fa-tarabbaṣū ḥattā yaʾtiya allāhu bi-amrih

“Then wait until The God brings His command.”

فَتَرَبَّصُوا is a second-person plural imperative.

Not every imperative in the Qur’an, however, necessarily encourages a commendable action.

Here it serves as a warning: if family and economic attachments are dearer than The God, His messenger, and striving in His way, the addressees will face the consequences of their choice.

فَتَرَبَّصُوا therefore cannot be read without context as advice to exercise patience or remain idle.

The word itself means “wait,” while the complete sentence gives the warning its force.

أَمْرِهِ belongs to another root. Determining which event or consequence the divine command denotes here is separate from defining tarabbuṣ.

Al-Tawbah 9:52: two expectations with different outcomes

قُلْ هَلْ تَرَبَّصُونَ بِنَا إِلَّا إِحْدَى الْحُسْنَيَيْنِ وَنَحْنُ نَتَرَبَّصُ بِكُمْ أَنْ يُصِيبَكُمُ اللَّهُ بِعَذَابٍ مِنْ عِنْدِهِ أَوْ بِأَيْدِينَا فَتَرَبَّصُوا إِنَّا مَعَكُمْ مُتَرَبِّصُونَ

qul hal tarabbaṣūna binā illā iḥdā al-ḥusnayayni wa-naḥnu natarabbaṣu bikum an yuṣībakumu allāhu bi-ʿadhābin min ʿindihi aw bi-aydīnā fa-tarabbaṣū innā maʿakum mutarabbiṣūn

“Say: ‘Do you await anything concerning us except one of the two good outcomes? We await concerning you that The God afflict you with punishment from Himself or at our hands. So wait; we too are waiting with you.’”

This verse contains four occurrences of R B Ṣ:

تَرَبَّصُونَ, نَتَرَبَّصُ, فَتَرَبَّصُوا, and مُتَرَبِّصُونَ.

In one sentence, both sides wait, but what they await and how they assess the outcome differ.

The opponents await an event affecting the believers, while the Qur’anic reply presents what is expected for the believers as one of “two good outcomes.”

In turn, the believers also await a consequence reaching the other side.

This is particularly clear evidence for a lexical principle: tarabbuṣ alone does not determine whether the anticipated event is good or bad.

The same root is used for both sides here.

The ethical and contextual difference comes from what is awaited and the verse’s other components.

Early interpretation usually explains إِحْدَى الْحُسْنَيَيْنِ as victory or being killed in The God’s way. That explanation concerns the context; tarabbuṣ itself does not mean victory or martyrdom.

Al-Tawbah 9:98: expecting adversity for another

وَمِنَ الْأَعْرَابِ مَنْ يَتَّخِذُ مَا يُنْفِقُ مَغْرَمًا وَيَتَرَبَّصُ بِكُمُ الدَّوَائِرَ

wa-mina al-aʿrābi man yattakhidhu mā yunfiqu maghraman wa-yatarabbaṣu bikumu al-dawāʾir

“Among the desert dwellers are those who regard what they spend as a loss and await turns of fortune concerning you.”

يَتَرَبَّصُ occurs here with الدَّوَائِرَ.

Dawāʾir belongs to D W R and, in such a setting, concerns adverse events and turns of fortune.

The verse continues:

عَلَيْهِمْ دَائِرَةُ السَّوْءِ

ʿalayhim dāʾiratu al-sawʾ

“May the turn of evil be upon them.”

This connection shows that the person criticized awaits unfavorable circumstances for the believers.

Tarabbuṣ therefore acquires a hostile implication here because of what the person expects to happen, rather than because the root is inherently hostile.

This use can be compared with Al-Nisāʾ 4:141. In both, waiting concerns a social situation and the outcome of a confrontation.

Al-Nisāʾ emphasizes opportunism between two sides, while Al-Tawbah 9:98 emphasizes expecting an unwelcome event for the other side.

6. Awaiting the outcome of the prophets’ call

Ṭā Hā 20:135: each side waits

قُلْ كُلٌّ مُتَرَبِّصٌ فَتَرَبَّصُوا فَسَتَعْلَمُونَ مَنْ أَصْحَابُ الصِّرَاطِ السَّوِيِّ وَمَنِ اهْتَدَىٰ

qul kullun mutarabbiṣun fa-tarabbaṣū fa-sa-taʿlamūna man aṣḥābu al-ṣirāṭi al-sawiyyi wa-mani ihtadā

“Say: ‘Each is waiting, so wait. Then you will know who follows the even path and who has found guidance.’”

Two forms of the root occur here: مُتَرَبِّصٌ and فَتَرَبَّصُوا.

مُتَرَبِّص is an active participle describing someone awaiting an event or outcome.

فَتَرَبَّصُوا is an imperative.

The following words clarify what is awaited: the outcome will become apparent, revealing who is on the right path.

Tarabbuṣ here does not necessarily mean lying in ambush to destroy the other side.

Nor does mutarabbiṣ itself mean guided or misguided. Each side may await an outcome, but the truth or error of its path is determined by another criterion.

This verse also shows that waiting is not itself a measure of truth.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:25: waiting for the prophet’s presence to end

Noah’s opponents say:

إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا رَجُلٌ بِهِ جِنَّةٌ فَتَرَبَّصُوا بِهِ حَتَّىٰ حِينٍ

in huwa illā rajulun bihi jinnatun fa-tarabbaṣū bihi ḥattā ḥīn

“He is only a man afflicted with madness; so wait concerning him for a time.”

The opponents first make an accusation against the prophet, then call on others to wait concerning him.

حَتَّىٰ حِينٍ indicates that the wait should continue for a time.

The expression itself, however, does not specify how many days, months, or years that means.

Interpretations have proposed waiting for his circumstances to change, for his call to end, or for his death.

These fit the hostile tone of the speech, but تَرَبَّصُوا itself does not mean death or destruction.

Nor can it establish that the opponents necessarily devised a practical plan to kill him. The text reports an accusation and waiting; further details require independent evidence.

Al-Ṭūr 52:30: awaiting رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ

أَمْ يَقُولُونَ شَاعِرٌ نَتَرَبَّصُ بِهِ رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ

am yaqūlūna shāʿirun natarabbaṣu bihi rayba al-manūn

“Or do they say, ‘A poet—we await the vicissitudes of death and time concerning him’?”

Here the opponents call the prophet a poet and await رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ concerning him.

Early interpretation explains this phrase as death or the adverse vicissitudes of time.

A natural reading is therefore that they hope the passage of time or some event will end the prophet’s presence and call.

Tarabbuṣ does not itself denote death. If ريب المنون is removed, the verb retains only the meaning of waiting.

The same construction occurred in the poetic witness quoted earlier.

The connection shows that the Qur’anic expression uses a known Arabic construction in which someone can await an event befalling another person.

Al-Ṭūr 52:31: the reply to the opponents’ expectation

The next verse says:

قُلْ تَرَبَّصُوا فَإِنِّي مَعَكُمْ مِنَ الْمُتَرَبِّصِينَ

qul tarabbaṣū fa-innī maʿakum mina al-mutarabbiṣīn

“Say: ‘Wait; I too am among those waiting with you.’”

Here تَرَبَّصُوا is an imperative, while الْمُتَرَبِّصِينَ is a sound masculine plural active participle in the genitive case.

The reply uses the same root that the opponents used in the preceding verse.

They say they await the end of the prophet’s call; the reply tells them to wait, since the actual outcome will become clear.

The same verb and active participle apply to both sides, but their aims in waiting differ.

This use does not establish that the prophet likewise wished for their death or an adverse event in precisely the same way.

Sharing the word “waiting” does not mean sharing an intention or goal.

7. Al-Ḥadīd 57:14: waiting and postponing a decision

يُنَادُونَهُمْ أَلَمْ نَكُنْ مَعَكُمْ قَالُوا بَلَىٰ وَلَٰكِنَّكُمْ فَتَنْتُمْ أَنْفُسَكُمْ وَتَرَبَّصْتُمْ وَارْتَبْتُمْ وَغَرَّتْكُمُ الْأَمَانِيُّ حَتَّىٰ جَاءَ أَمْرُ اللَّهِ وَغَرَّكُمْ بِاللَّهِ الْغَرُورُ

yunādūnahum a-lam nakun maʿakum qālū balā wa-lākinnakum fatantum anfusakum wa-tarabbaṣtum wa-irtabtum wa-gharratkumu al-amāniyyu ḥattā jāʾa amru allāhi wa-gharrakum bi-allāhi al-gharūr

“They call to them, ‘Were we not with you?’ They say, ‘Yes; but you put yourselves to trial, waited, and doubted, and wishes deceived you until The God’s command came; and the deceiver deceived you concerning The God.’”

وَتَرَبَّصْتُمْ is a second-person masculine plural past verb.

Unlike expressions such as نَتَرَبَّصُ بِهِ رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ, however, the object of waiting is not expressly stated after the verb.

This omission has allowed different interpretations.

First reading: awaiting an event harmful to the believers

In one exegetical reading, the meaning is waiting concerning the truth and its followers.

Other explanations connect this expectation with hoping for the prophet’s death or an adverse event affecting the believers.

This reading relates to hostile uses of tarabbuṣ in Al-Tawbah 9:98 and Al-Ṭūr 52:30.

But the verb without a complement cannot establish what is awaited on its own.

Second reading: postponing repentance and the right decision

Another exegetical reading is also attested: the addressees kept postponing acceptance of the truth or repentance from one time to another.

Here, tarabbuṣ is not waiting for another’s death but a delay that prevents someone from taking the necessary decision.

This meaning also has an intelligible connection with the verse’s continuation:

وَارْتَبْتُمْ: and you fell into doubt.

وَغَرَّتْكُمُ الْأَمَانِيُّ: and wishes deceived you.

حَتَّىٰ جَاءَ أَمْرُ اللَّهِ: until The God’s command came.

This sequence can describe people who continually put off deciding and, through groundless hopes, lose the opportunity to act.

Assessment

Both readings are lexically possible.

In the first, someone awaits an external event.

In the second, pausing puts a decision or action off into the future.

They should not, however, be merged without explanation into one definite meaning.

The second reading is significant in relation to وَارْتَبْتُمْ, وَغَرَّتْكُمُ الْأَمَانِيُّ, and حَتَّىٰ جَاءَ أَمْرُ اللَّهِ: the verse describes doubt and deceptive wishes continuing until the opportunity ends.

Nevertheless, no contextual indicator eliminates the first reading altogether.

In direct translation, وَتَرَبَّصْتُمْ is better rendered “and you waited” or “and you delayed,” with its object examined in the verse’s reflection.

A further distinction is necessary: tarabbuṣ is not irtiyāb. The former concerns waiting and delay, the latter doubt and uncertainty. The Qur’an expresses them through two separate verbs.

8. Tarabbuṣ compared with expectation, patience, and related concepts

R B Ṣ and N Ẓ R: tarabbuṣ and intiẓār

Intiẓār belongs to N Ẓ R.

Tarabbuṣ is repeatedly explained through intiẓār in Arabic lexicography, so the words clearly overlap in meaning.

Naẓar has a broader Arabic range: looking and paying attention, examining, granting time, and waiting.

The principal uses of tarabbuṣ are more specifically associated with waiting and pausing.

For example, اُنْظُرْنِي can request a reprieve, and اُنْظُرُوا can mean “look.” تَرَبَّصُوا usually expresses waiting rather than seeing with the eyes.

The Qur’an uses both families to express waiting.

This proximity does not make them the same root.

Study of N Ẓ R

R B Ṣ and Ṣ B R: waiting and steadfastness

Ṣabr belongs to Ṣ B R and concerns restraint, steadfastness, and holding oneself to a course or conduct.

Tarabbuṣ concerns waiting and pausing until an event or time arrives.

Someone may exercise patience while waiting, but the two behaviors are not necessarily identical.

For example, someone may wait for a specified period with steadfastness and respect for rights.

Someone else may await another’s defeat out of envy, hostility, or opportunism.

Not all tarabbuṣ is commendable patience, just as not all patience means waiting for an event.

This distinction is clear when comparing the uses in Al-Baqarah with verses about the prophets’ opponents.

Study of Ṣ B R

R B Ṣ and L B TH: waiting and remaining

لَبِثَ belongs to L B TH and concerns remaining or spending time in a state or place.

Tarabbuṣ, by contrast, foregrounds expectation.

Someone may stay in a place for a time without waiting for anything.

Waiting may involve remaining in a place, but it is not necessarily restricted to that.

For example, divorced women wait a specified period in Al-Baqarah 2:228; يَتَرَبَّصْنَ itself does not establish that they must remain physically motionless in one spot throughout it.

Any such requirements would need independent evidence.

R B Ṣ and M K TH: waiting and pausing

مَكَثَ concerns remaining or staying in a state or place.

Some dictionaries explain tarabbuṣ through makth.

Makth may simply indicate a pause, whereas tarabbuṣ generally includes expectation concerning a time or event.

This is a tendency rather than an absolute distinction: actual uses of both roots can overlap.

R B Ṣ and R Ṣ D: waiting and watching

رَصَدَ belongs to R Ṣ D and concerns watching, monitoring, and being ready to observe something or for it to occur.

In Al-Jinn 72:27, رَصَدًا concerns guarding and watching.

In some uses, someone watching for an opportunity can also be engaged in tarabbuṣ.

The two words are not identical, however.

Raṣd can emphasize active monitoring and keeping something under observation, while tarabbuṣ emphasizes waiting and pausing.

Automatically translating mutarabbiṣ as “one lying in ambush” in every verse is therefore unsound.

That implication can be examined in the opportunistic setting of Al-Nisāʾ 4:141, but divorced women’s waiting in Al-Baqarah 2:228 has nothing to do with ambush.

R B Ṣ and R Y B: waiting and doubt

Rayb belongs to R Y B and in Qur’anic usage often concerns doubt and uncertainty.

Al-Ṭūr 52:30 contains رَيْبَ الْمَنُونِ, explained as adverse vicissitudes of time or death.

Tarabbuṣ in the same verse retains the meaning of waiting.

In Al-Ḥadīd 57:14, وَتَرَبَّصْتُمْ and وَارْتَبْتُمْ also occur side by side.

Neither association should lead to defining tarabbuṣ as doubt.

Someone may doubt something and consequently wait, but doubt and waiting are different states.

Study of R Y B

R B Ṣ and A J L: waiting and a term’s end

Ajal concerns a term’s end or a specified time.

Al-Baqarah 2:234 says:

فَإِذَا بَلَغْنَ أَجَلَهُنَّ

fa-idhā balaghna ajalahunna

“Then, when they reach the end of their term.”

This expression shows that the waiting period has an end.

Tarabbuṣ itself does not mean that endpoint; it expresses waiting during the period.

Neither the length nor the endpoint of a deadline can therefore be derived from R B Ṣ alone.

Study of A J L

9. Semantic assessment of all Qur’anic uses

Three principal groups can be identified across the 17 occurrences.

Contextual groupVersesProminent feature
Waiting for a prescribed periodAl-Baqarah 2:226, Al-Baqarah 2:228, Al-Baqarah 2:234Waiting within legal and family circumstances
Awaiting an opportunity or someone else’s outcomeAl-Nisāʾ 4:141, Al-Tawbah 9:52, Al-Tawbah 9:98, Al-Muʾminūn 23:25, Al-Ṭūr 52:30, Al-Ṭūr 52:31Expecting changed circumstances or another person’s outcome
Warning, awaiting a result, or delaying a decisionAl-Tawbah 9:24, Ṭā Hā 20:135, Al-Ḥadīd 57:14Awaiting an outcome, sometimes accompanied by postponing action

This grouping rests on context; it does not mean that R B Ṣ acquires an entirely separate lexical meaning in each group.

An element of waiting or pausing remains throughout.

The differences concern what is awaited, the addressee’s circumstances, the period specified, and the ethical assessment of the behavior.

For example, in Al-Baqarah 2:234, waiting forms part of regulating family circumstances.

In Al-Tawbah 9:98, someone awaits an adverse event for another.

In Ṭā Hā 20:135, waiting concerns the outcome that will reveal the right path.

In Al-Ḥadīd 57:14, waiting can occur in the censure of people who failed to reach the right decision in time.

No single occurrence can therefore determine the meaning of all the others.

10. Translation proposals for Forqan

The general equivalent for R B Ṣ is “to wait.”

“To remain expectant” is also suitable and, in some verses, better conveys actively following an outcome.

“To delay” can be used where postponing action is prominent, but it should not replace “to wait” without regard to context.

In Al-Baqarah 2:226, tarabbuṣ is a noun, naturally rendered “a period of waiting.”

In Al-Baqarah 2:228 and Al-Baqarah 2:234, the waiting concerns the women’s own circumstances, so بِأَنْفُسِهِنَّ is best retained in translation.

In Al-Tawbah 9:52, the shared root in both sides’ speech should be preserved: repeated tarabbuṣ forms part of the verse’s verbal contrast.

For Al-Ḥadīd 57:14, literal translation should likewise avoid fixing the object of waiting without sufficient evidence. “And you waited” or “and you delayed” can provide the basis, while the disagreement over the object is discussed in reflection.

Renderings such as “they plotted,” “they lay in ambush,” “they wished for death,” “they abandoned their duty,” or “they were hoarders” must not replace the word in every setting.

These behaviors may arise in particular contexts, but they are not the root’s direct meaning.

11. Conclusion

In early Arabic, R B Ṣ concerns waiting and pausing in expectation of something happening.

Waiting can be regarded as this family’s central meaning.

Tarabbuṣ, rabṣ, and rubṣah are defined within this same field in Arabic dictionaries. Lexical evidence shows that the expectation can concern good or harm, a change in goods’ prices, removal of an obstacle, a specified period elapsing, or an event affecting another person.

The root occurs 17 times in 12 Qur’anic verses.

The three uses in Al-Baqarah concern regulating family relationships and specified periods.

In Al-Nisāʾ and Al-Tawbah, tarabbuṣ is sometimes associated with opportunism, awaiting the other side’s defeat, and expecting turns of fortune.

In Ṭā Hā and Al-Ṭūr, opposing sides wait, each expecting an outcome concerning the other or the result to become clear.

In Al-Ḥadīd 57:14, waiting occurs beside doubt and deceptive wishes. Exegetical tradition considers both awaiting an event harmful to the believers and postponing acceptance of the truth or repentance.

The principal finding is that tarabbuṣ itself is not necessarily either good or bad.

It may be a legitimate, specified wait; opportunism and hope for harm to others; or a delay that costs someone the opportunity to act.

These differences come from context and the object of expectation, rather than arbitrary changes to the root’s meaning.

Proposed final definition for Forqan:

R B Ṣ refers to waiting and pausing in expectation of a time arriving, an event occurring, or circumstances changing. The wait may concern good or harm, the end of a specified period, or a suitable opportunity. In the Qur’an, tarabbuṣ concerns waiting within family-related periods, awaiting others’ outcomes, and sometimes delaying a decision or action. The root itself, however, determines neither the ethical value nor the precise object of expectation.
