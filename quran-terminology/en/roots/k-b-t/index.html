The Qur’anic Root K B T (ك ب ت)

Breaking resistance, subduing, and frustrating an opponent in humiliation

Qur’anic forms

كُبِتُوا

1. Meaning in early Arabic

ك ب ت concerns casting down, crushing, preventing someone from attaining a goal, and humiliating.

Early Arabic lexicons use كَبَتَ both for casting someone face down and for defeating an enemy so that the enemy fails to attain the intended goal.

Classical expressions include:

  • كَبَتَهُ لِوَجْهِهِ: he cast him onto his face.
  • كَبَتَ اللَّهُ الْعَدُوَّ: The God defeated and humbled the enemy.
  • كَبَتَهُ فَانْكَبَتَ: he subdued him, and he was subdued.

Kitab al-ʿAyn records casting face down, humiliation, and failure to attain what is sought. Ibn Faris likewise locates the core in humbling someone and turning that person back from an objective.

There is insufficient independently datable evidence here to establish its precise pre-Islamic use. The Qur’an itself, however, attests that defeat, humiliation, and frustrated return were intelligible meanings at the time of revelation.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important Arabic forms include:

  • كَبَتَ ـ يَكْبِتُ: subdued, humbled, or defeated.
  • كَبْت: subduing, restraining, and humiliating.
  • كُبِتَ: was subdued or humbled.
  • كَابِت: one who subdues.
  • مَكْبُوت: subdued or humiliated; in some uses, severely distressed.
  • اِنْكَبَتَ: was subdued or defeated.
  • كُبَات: a noun recorded for the sense of kabt.

Forms examined in the Qur’an:

يَكْبِتَهُمْ ـ كُبِتُوا ـ كُبِتَ

All belong to the basic triliteral verb. The verbal noun كَبْت and the participles كَابِت and مَكْبُوت do not occur in the Qur’an.

3. Semantic core and physical image

One recorded physical image is casting someone face down. A person standing in resistance is thrown down and loses the ability to continue resisting.

In hostility and confrontation, this approaches breaking resistance, preventing attainment of a goal, and humiliating the opponent.

For example, a group enters a confrontation to overcome another, but its resistance is broken and it returns without achieving its purpose. Such a situation fits كَبْت.

Kabt is not limited to an inward feeling of grief or shame. Its Qur’anic uses emphasize the defeat and humiliation of those who have risen in opposition.

Some early lexicographers proposed a connection between كَبْت and كَبْد, explaining it through grief and anger culturally associated with the liver. This etymological proposal is uncertain and does not justify treating ك ب ت and ك ب د as one root.

4. Qur’anic uses

يَكْبِتَهُمْ: subduing and turning back frustrated

This verse concerns military confrontation and aid to the believers. The preceding verse is also relevant:

Ali ʿImran 3:127 distinguishes two possibilities with أَوْ: eliminating a portion of the opponents or subduing them so they return frustrated.

Thus kabt need not mean killing or annihilating an enemy. A force may suffer defeat, fail to achieve its objective, and retreat without every member being killed.

Three words express different aspects and stages of this outcome:

  • يَكْبِتَهُمْ: He subdues them.
  • يَنْقَلِبُوا: they return.
  • خَائِبِينَ: having failed to attain their goal.

Kabt is the action inflicted on them; return is their subsequent movement; khaybah describes their failure to attain the objective.

This combination is among the clearest Qur’anic evidence for the meaning of ك ب ت.

كُبِتُوا and كُبِتَ: opponents being subdued

Both كُبِتُوا and كُبِتَ are passive perfect forms of the same verb.

يُحَادُّونَ, Form III of ح د د, expresses opposition and taking a stand against another. Here it describes opposition to The God and His Messenger.

The Qur’an compares their outcome with that of earlier opponents. كَمَا connects the groups, presenting defeat and humiliation as not confined to one historical period.

كُبِتُوا can describe a defeat already experienced or a certain outcome expressed through a perfect verb. Arabic can employ the perfect to convey the certainty of a future event.

Either reading retains the fundamental meaning: opponents being defeated and humbled.

Later in the same surah:

These verses help explain كُبِتُوا in Al-Mujadilah 58:5. One speaks of the opponents’ abasement; the other of the victory of The God and His Messengers.

The lexical associations of defeat, being overcome, and humiliation therefore accord with the Qur’an’s own semantic connections.

The form and timing of that victory must be understood from context and other verses. Kabt alone does not specify one punishment or manner of defeat.

5. Morphology and syntax

يَكْبِتَهُمْ in Ali ʿImran 3:127 is an active imperfect verb in the subjunctive, coordinated by أَوْ with يَقْطَعَ. The attached هُمْ is its object.

فَيَنْقَلِبُوا expresses the resulting return; خَائِبِينَ is a plural active participle functioning as a circumstantial accusative.

Both كُبِتُوا and كُبِتَ in Al-Mujadilah 58:5 are passive.

In the first form, the plural suffix refers to the group introduced at the start of the verse. In the second, الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِهِمْ is the passive subject; the preceding verb appears in the singular.

The active and passive constructions highlight two aspects of the same concept: the act of subduing in Ali ʿImran 3:127, and the outcome for those subdued in Al-Mujadilah 58:5.

6. The later psychological use of kabt

Modern Arabic uses الْكَبْتُ النَّفْسِيُّ as a psychological term for repression: certain unwelcome desires, thoughts, memories, or emotions are kept outside awareness.

Ordinary speech also uses كَبْتُ الْمَشَاعِرِ for suppressing feelings.

These later uses are related to restraint and suppression, but the technical psychological sense should not be imposed on the Qur’an.

None of the verses using this root discusses unconscious mechanisms or the repression of inner desires and emotions.

7. Comparison with related roots

ك ب ت (كَبْت) and ك ب ب (كَبّ)

كَبّ concerns overturning and casting face down, as in Al-Naml 27:90 and Al-Mulk 67:22.

Kabt can likewise denote casting down in its recorded physical use, but its Qur’anic uses foreground subjugation, defeat, and humiliation.

The physical images resemble one another, but these are independent roots.

ك ب ت (كَبْت) and خ ي ب (خَيْبَة)

Khaybah means failing to attain a goal or being deprived of an expected result.

Kabt concerns subduing and defeating someone and can cause that failure.

A person may fail through a mistake, adverse circumstances, or inability without being subdued by anyone.

In Ali ʿImran 3:127, however, kabt leads to the opponents’ frustrated return. The verse demonstrates the relationship without making the words exact synonyms.

ك ب ت (كَبْت) and ه ز م (هَزْم)

ه ز م concerns breaking and defeating, especially in military confrontation.

Al-Baqarah 2:251 describes defeating Goliath’s forces by divine permission.

Kabt overlaps with defeat, but its lexical range also emphasizes humiliation and frustrated return.

Hazīmah emphasizes defeat; kabt can convey defeat together with humiliation and failure to attain an objective.

ك ب ت (كَبْت) and ذ ل ل (ذُلّ)

ذ ل ل concerns lowered standing, humiliation, humility, tameness, and reduced resistance.

Abasement can result from defeat, but not all uses of this family are negative. Al-Isra’ 17:24, for example, concerns merciful humility toward parents.

Qur’anic kabt concerns opponents being defeated and subdued, and is distinct from voluntary, merciful humility.

ك ب ت (كَبْت) and غ ل ب (غَلَبَة)

Ghalabah means prevailing over another.

Kabt focuses more on the person whose resistance has been broken and who has been overcome.

Al-Mujadilah 58:5 describes opponents being subdued; Al-Mujadilah 58:21 describes The God and His Messengers prevailing.

These can be two sides of one confrontation: one prevails and the other is subdued.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

Arabic word or expressionSuggested rendering
كَبَتَsubdued; defeated and humbled
كَبْتsubjugation; breaking resistance
يَكْبِتَهُمْthat He subdue them
كُبِتُواthey were subdued
كُبِتَwas subdued
فَيَنْقَلِبُوا خَائِبِينَso they return frustrated

“Subdue” is suitable for these Qur’anic uses. Explanation should clarify defeat, broken resistance, and humiliation, without making physical annihilation or death necessary.

Conclusion

ك ب ت concerns casting face down, breaking resistance, preventing attainment of a goal, and humbling.

Ali ʿImran 3:127 describes opponents subdued so they return frustrated. Al-Mujadilah 58:5 compares the defeat of those opposing The God and His Messenger with earlier opponents’ fate.

Kabt is closely associated with defeat, humiliation, and frustration, but does not mean complete annihilation or merely an inward feeling of grief and despair.

Its Qur’anic focus is subduing and breaking another’s resistance, accompanied by humiliation and frustrated aims.
