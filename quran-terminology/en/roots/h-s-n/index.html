1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ح س ن moves in the field of goodness, beauty, fittingness, desirability, and doing something properly and well. Something called hasan is not ugly, unworthy, improper, or bad within its context; rather, it has a pleasing form, a desirable result, or a worthy quality.

In the Qur'an, this root is not used only for outward beauty. Speech, conduct, judgment, promise, reward, provision, rank, final outcome, the human form, the manner of argument, relation with parents, and the quality of creation can all be described through derivatives of this root.

One of its most important branches is ihsan. In the Qur'an, ihsan has two main directions:

Ihsan within the act itself:

Doing the act well, rightly, and worthily.

Ihsan toward another:

Conveying good, kindness, and worthy conduct to someone else.

These two directions are not separate from each other, but they should not be treated as entirely identical. A person may do something technically well while that act is not a conveyance of good to another. Likewise, intending to help another, without a right and just manner, is not necessarily complete ihsan.

Short meaning:

Being good, beautiful, worthy, and pleasing; and in Form IV, doing something well or conveying good to another.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

The root ح س ن is reported in the Qur'an 194 times and in twelve morphological forms: the simple verb حَسُنَ, the Form IV verb أَحْسَنَ, the comparative form أَحْسَن, the adjective حَسَن, the noun حُسْن, the plural حَسَنات, the form حُسْنَىٰ, the dual حُسْنَيَيْن, the noun حَسَنَة, the verbal noun إِحْسَان, and the active participles مُحْسِن and مُحْسِنَات.

حَسُنَ

The simple verb of the root. It points to being good, fine, or pleasing:

وَحَسُنَ أُولَٰئِكَ رَفِيقًا

And how good they are as companions.

حَسَن

An adjective meaning good, pleasing, desirable, fitting, or beautiful. In the Qur'an it is used for a loan, acceptance, growth, provision, promise, reward, and enjoyment.

حُسْن

The noun or verbal noun of the root. It may point to goodness, beauty, worthy conduct, or a desirable outcome:

وَقُولُوا لِلنَّاسِ حُسْنًا

Speak to people with good speech.

حَسَنَة

The feminine form of hasan, but in the Qur'an it is often used as a noun. According to context, it may refer to a good deed, a blessing, success, a desirable event, or a good result.

حَسَنَات

The plural of hasanah. In the Qur'an it is used both for blessings and desirable events and for good deeds.

أَحْسَنَ / يُحْسِنُ

The Form IV verb. It can carry several structures:

  • he did an act well;
  • he made something good;
  • he did good to another;
  • he brought the quality or state of something into a pleasing form.

إِحْسَان

The verbal noun of Form IV. It points to doing well or conveying good to another.

مُحْسِن

One who acts well or conveys good to others. The exact meaning is specified by the verbs and relationships present in the context.

أَحْسَن

A comparative or superlative form on the pattern af'al. It points to “better,” “more fitting,” “more worthy,” or “best.”

Sometimes it compares between two or more options:

فَحَيُّوا بِأَحْسَنَ مِنْهَا

Then respond with a greeting better than it.

Sometimes it indicates the highest degree:

أَحْسَنَ الْحَدِيثِ

The best speech.

حُسْنَىٰ

The feminine form of ahsan on the pattern fu'la. In the Qur'an it may be a feminine adjective, as in:

الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ

And it may function as a noun pointing to the best reward, the best promise, or the better final outcome:

لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا الْحُسْنَىٰ

حُسْنَيَيْن

The dual form of husna:

إِحْدَى الْحُسْنَيَيْنِ

One of the two good outcomes.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of ح س ن may be expressed as:

Having, or bringing forth, a quality that is good and pleasing in form, act, result, or judgment.

In outward beauty, hasan points to a form and proportion that the eye finds pleasing:

وَلَوْ أَعْجَبَكَ حُسْنُهُنَّ

Even if their beauty impresses you.

In speech, husn points to words that are far from ugliness, harm, and injustice:

وَقُولُوا لِلنَّاسِ حُسْنًا

In conduct, husn points to a worthy and ethical manner:

وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا

In making and creating, husn points to making something well and placing parts and form in a fitting state:

وَصَوَّرَكُمْ فَأَحْسَنَ صُوَرَكُمْ

In result and final return, husn relates to a desirable outcome:

حُسْنُ الْمَآبِ

So this root is not only “beauty,” and it is not only “ethical goodness.” Its field includes outward form, action, relationship, result, and manner.

Raghib al-Isfahani distinguishes between three forms: hasan is used for things and events; hasanah, when it is a noun, is often about an event or act; and husna is more often used for matters and results. He also says that the common use of hasan is known through what the eye finds pleasing, while most Qur'anic uses concern what is recognized as good through insight.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The clear material image of this root may be seen in a form whose parts are harmonious and pleasing to the observer, or in a crafted thing that is shaped properly, proportionately, and without obvious disorder.

The Qur'an speaks of the beauty of the human form:

وَصَوَّرَكُمْ فَأَحْسَنَ صُوَرَكُمْ

And He formed you and made your forms good.

Ghafir 40:64

The same image extends to action. An act whose parts are in their right place, whose aim is rightly chosen, and whose manner of execution is worthy may be called hasan or ahsan.

But this image should not be turned into the claim that the historical core of the root was necessarily “fortress, rock, hardness, or physical solidity.” Such a claim would require independent linguistic evidence and may confuse this root with ح ص ن, which is connected with fortification and protection.

The main Arabic lexical sources place al-hasan opposite al-qubh and report beauty, goodness, pleasing conduct, and desirable quality for it.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, al-husn is the opposite of al-qubh. This contrast shows that husn can include anything pleasing from a visual, ethical, or practical angle.

For outward beauty, expressions such as these were used:

رجل حسن

A good-looking or fine man.

امرأة حسناء

A beautiful woman.

محاسن

Beautiful and pleasing features.

The root was also used in ethics and action:

حسن الخلق

Good character and conduct.

أحسن إليه

He did good to him.

أحسن الشيء

He made the thing good, or did it well.

Lexical usage distinguishes between أحسن إليه and أحسن الشيء. The first is conveying good to another; the second is making something good or doing an affair properly. This distinction matters for understanding Qur'anic ihsan.

Likewise, hasanah stands opposite sayyi'ah, and mahasin stands opposite masawi'. This contrast expands from outward form to conduct, event, and result.

7. History of Understanding and Technical Expansion

In later traditions, ihsan was sometimes introduced as a technical station alongside Islam and iman. This is an expansion within religious tradition, but it must not replace the broader and more varied Qur'anic uses.

In the Qur'an, ihsan is not an abstract rank separated from life. It appears in relation to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, neighbors, spouses, captives, opponents, speech, spending, retaliation, divorce, judgment, and the quality of action.

Ihsan should also not be defined only as “doing more than one's duty.” In some contexts, ihsan is the proper manner of carrying out a basic responsibility, not merely a voluntary act added beyond justice.

For example, ihsan toward parents is an explicit command. The separated right in qisas must be paid bi-ihsan, in a good manner. Releasing a woman must also be bi-ihsan. Thus ihsan can define the required quality of conduct.

The later theological debate over “rational good and ugly” also used the field of this root, but it is a later kalam discussion about the source of moral knowledge. A root article should not confine the verses to the position of one theological school.

What is clear from the Qur'an itself is that the human being is repeatedly called to recognize between hasan and su', ahsan and what is lower than it, hasanah and sayyi'ah, and ma'ruf and munkar. The Qur'an does not isolate intellect, observation of consequence, moral disposition, and revelation from one another in this recognition.

8. Methodological Note

Several levels must be distinguished when studying this root:

Basic meaning:

Being good, beautiful, worthy, and pleasing.

Type of subject:

Form, speech, act, relationship, reward, provision, or final outcome.

Morphological structure:

Hasan, hasanah, ahsan, ihsan, or husna.

Interpretive judgment:

Why an act is better and what the criterion of its superiority is.

Hasan must not be reduced to personal liking. The Qur'an speaks of a person whose bad deed has been adorned for him, so he sees it as hasan:

أَفَمَنْ زُيِّنَ لَهُ سُوءُ عَمَلِهِ فَرَآهُ حَسَنًا

Fatir 35:8

Thus a human being may consider something hasan while in reality it is su'. Personal preference is not the final measure of Qur'anic husn.

On the other hand, hasan should not be defined only as “final benefit.” Something may be beneficial from one angle while the manner of doing it is ugly or unworthy. The Qur'an weighs both result and manner.

In analyzing Form IV, one should also not claim that the pattern if'al always means bringing an act to the highest possible perfection. Form IV has the capacity to make transitive and to bring something into a state, but the exact meaning of ahsan is understood from its actual uses.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

Goodness in This World and the Hereafter

The Qur'an reports the supplication of aware people:

رَبَّنَا آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً وَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ

Our Lord, give us a hasanah in this world and a hasanah in the Hereafter, and guard us from the punishment of the Fire.

Al-Baqarah 2:201

Hasanah here is not an act performed by the human being. It is good, blessing, or a desirable state requested from The God.

The verse leaves the word open and comprehensive. Hasanah in this world should not be restricted to wealth, spouse, health, or one specific blessing.

Hasanah and Sayyi'ah as Events

The Qur'an says:

وَإِنْ تُصِبْهُمْ حَسَنَةٌ يَقُولُوا هَٰذِهِ مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ وَإِنْ تُصِبْهُمْ سَيِّئَةٌ يَقُولُوا هَٰذِهِ مِنْ عِنْدِكَ

If a hasanah reaches them, they say: This is from The God; and if a sayyi'ah reaches them, they say: This is from you.

An-Nisa 4:78

Hasanah here is a pleasant event, success, or blessing, while sayyi'ah is a calamity or unpleasant event.

This use shows that hasanah is not always a “good deed.”

Hasanah as an Act

The Qur'an says:

مَنْ جَاءَ بِالْحَسَنَةِ فَلَهُ عَشْرُ أَمْثَالِهَا

Whoever comes with the hasanah, for him is ten like it.

Al-An'am 6:160

Here hasanah points to a good act or moral achievement.

The verb ja'a shows that the human being comes into judgment with the result of his act.

Hasanat and Sayyi'at

The Qur'an says:

إِنَّ الْحَسَنَاتِ يُذْهِبْنَ السَّيِّئَاتِ

Indeed, the hasanat remove the sayyi'at.

Hud 11:114

Hasanat in this verse are good acts that remove or compensate for the effects of sayyi'at.

This expression should not be turned into a simple accounting transaction in which every good act automatically erases every injustice without repentance, correction, or stopping the wrong. The context is connected with salat, steadiness, and reminder for people of dhikr.

In Al-Furqan 25:70, it also comes:

فَأُولَٰئِكَ يُبَدِّلُ اللَّهُ سَيِّئَاتِهِمْ حَسَنَاتٍ

This change is mentioned after repentance, faith, and righteous action.

The Command to Justice and Ihsan

The Qur'an says:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُ بِالْعَدْلِ وَالْإِحْسَانِ وَإِيتَاءِ ذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ

Indeed, The God commands justice, ihsan, and giving to the near of kin.

An-Nahl 16:90

Justice and ihsan come together, but their pairing alone does not prove that ihsan is always an optional act “beyond justice.”

Justice preserves right and proper measure. Ihsan highlights good quality and conveying good in action. Sometimes ihsan may be more than the minimum right, and sometimes it is the proper way of carrying out that same right.

For example, in qisas the payment of the right must be bi-ihsan; in divorce, releasing a woman must be bi-ihsan. These are not merely decorative matters after justice.

Ihsan toward Parents

The Qur'an says:

وَقَضَىٰ رَبُّكَ أَلَّا تَعْبُدُوا إِلَّا إِيَّاهُ وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا

And your Lord has decreed that you serve none except Him, and toward parents, ihsan.

Al-Isra 17:23

Ihsan in this verse is relationship-oriented. The child must convey good, respect, and worthy conduct to parents.

The continuation of the verse explains some of its expressions: not saying words of contempt, not repelling, speaking noble words, and lowering the wing of mercy.

Thus translating ihsanan only as “performing the act with high quality” is not enough.

Ihsan in Social Relations

The Qur'an says:

وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا وَبِذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْيَتَامَىٰ وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَالْجَارِ ذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ وَالصَّاحِبِ بِالْجَنْبِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ

An-Nisa 4:36

Ihsan in this context includes a network of human relations. Goodness to parents is not separated from other social responsibilities.

This use shows that ihsan is not only an inner quality of worship; it is the form of a human being's relation with others.

Ihsan in Release and Separation

The Qur'an says about separation:

فَإِمْسَاكٌ بِمَعْرُوفٍ أَوْ تَسْرِيحٌ بِإِحْسَانٍ

Then either holding in ma'ruf or releasing with ihsan.

Al-Baqarah 2:229

Ihsan here concerns the manner of ending a relationship. Separation must not become humiliation, harm, revenge, or the violation of right.

In another verse:

فَمَتِّعُوهُنَّ وَسَرِّحُوهُنَّ سَرَاحًا جَمِيلًا

A nearby field is expressed through jamil. This pairing shows that Qur'anic ethics weighs not only the decision but also the manner of executing it.

Paying a Right with Ihsan

In the ruling of qisas, it says:

فَاتِّبَاعٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَأَدَاءٌ إِلَيْهِ بِإِحْسَانٍ

Then following up should be in ma'ruf and payment to him with ihsan.

Al-Baqarah 2:178

If the family of the killed person accepts compensation instead of qisas, neither the claim should be aggressive nor the payment made with delay, humiliation, or deception.

Ihsan here is the quality of carrying out a legal obligation.

Quality-Oriented Ihsan

The Qur'an says:

الَّذِي أَحْسَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقَهُ

The One who made good everything He created.

As-Sajdah 32:7

Ahsana here is not limited to conveying good to an external receiver; it expresses the quality and proportion of creation.

Also:

إِنَّا لَا نُضِيعُ أَجْرَ مَنْ أَحْسَنَ عَمَلًا

We do not waste the reward of one who does the act well.

Al-Kahf 18:30

In this structure, ihsan is directly connected with the quality of the act.

Thinking One Is Doing Well

The Qur'an says about the greatest losers:

وَهُمْ يَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّهُمْ يُحْسِنُونَ صُنْعًا

While they think they are doing the work well.

Al-Kahf 18:104

This verse shows that claiming quality or good intention is not enough for ihsan to be real. A person may think his method is good while his effort is lost and fruitless.

Ihsan must be measured by truth, right direction, and the result of the act.

Goodness to Another

Yusuf says:

وَقَدْ أَحْسَنَ بِي إِذْ أَخْرَجَنِي مِنَ السِّجْنِ

And He did good to me when He brought me out of prison.

Yusuf 12:100

Here ahsana bi is in the field of ahsana ilayya: The God conveyed good to Yusuf.

In Al-Qasas 28:77, it also comes:

وَأَحْسِنْ كَمَا أَحْسَنَ اللَّهُ إِلَيْكَ

And do good, as The God has done good to you.

This structure clearly expresses relationship-oriented ihsan.

The Reward of Ihsan

The Qur'an says:

هَلْ جَزَاءُ الْإِحْسَانِ إِلَّا الْإِحْسَانُ

Is the reward of ihsan anything but ihsan?

Ar-Rahman 55:60

The repetition of the word on both sides of the sentence does not necessarily mean that the two instances are exactly the same in referent. The first ihsan is the human being's good act; the second is the fitting goodness and reward from The God.

In Yunus 10:26, it says:

لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا الْحُسْنَىٰ وَزِيَادَةٌ

For those who did ihsan is al-husna and increase.

Al-husna here is the best reward or better final outcome.

Repelling Bad with What Is Better

The Qur'an says:

ادْفَعْ بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ السَّيِّئَةَ

Repel the bad with that which is better.

Al-Mu'minun 23:96

And:

ادْفَعْ بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ

Fussilat 41:34

The verse does not say that every bad thing must be answered with unlimited softness. Ahsan points to the best and most worthy method of repelling: a method that stops the bad while not itself becoming a new injustice or corruption.

Arguing in the Ahsan Manner

The Qur'an says:

وَجَادِلْهُمْ بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ

An-Nahl 16:125

And about the People of the Book:

وَلَا تُجَادِلُوا أَهْلَ الْكِتَابِ إِلَّا بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا مِنْهُمْ

Al-'Ankabut 29:46

Ahsan here is not only about a pleasant tone. The method of argument must be better in truth, justice, respect, and result.

The exception of those who commit injustice shows that the ahsan manner does not mean ignoring wrongdoing.

Saying What Is Ahsan

The Qur'an says:

وَقُلْ لِعِبَادِي يَقُولُوا الَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ

And say to My servants that they should say what is ahsan.

Al-Isra 17:53

The choice of allati hiya ahsan instead of merely “good speech” shows that the human being must choose the better among possible manners and expressions.

The Best Speech

The Qur'an says:

اللَّهُ نَزَّلَ أَحْسَنَ الْحَدِيثِ كِتَابًا مُتَشَابِهًا مَثَانِيَ

The God sent down the best speech, a book harmonious and recurring.

Az-Zumar 39:23

Ahsan al-hadith describes the Qur'an and points to the superior quality of its speech.

In Yusuf 12:3, it also says:

نَحْنُ نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ أَحْسَنَ الْقَصَصِ

The expression may point to the best manner of narration, the best narration, or the best narrated content. It should not be translated as “the best story in history” without grammatical analysis.

Following the Ahsan of Speech

The Qur'an says:

الَّذِينَ يَسْتَمِعُونَ الْقَوْلَ فَيَتَّبِعُونَ أَحْسَنَهُ

Those who listen to speech and then follow the ahsan of it.

Az-Zumar 39:18

The verse does not consider listening enough. Recognizing and following ahsanahu is required.

This structure may express choosing the best speech from among speeches, or following the best indication and command of the revealed speech. The precise determination depends on context.

The Best Act, Not the Most Acts

The Qur'an describes the purpose of creating death and life as:

لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا

To test you as to which of you is better in act.

Al-Mulk 67:2

The verse does not say akthar 'amalan. The criterion is not only the volume or count of action, but its quality and rightness.

The same expression appears in Hud 11:7 and Al-Kahf 18:7.

Making the Human Form Good

The Qur'an says:

وَصَوَّرَكُمْ فَأَحْسَنَ صُوَرَكُمْ

Ghafir 40:64؛ At-Taghabun 64:3

This verse is about making the human form good. Ahsana here is a verb, not a comparative noun.

One should not conclude from the verse that all individuals are identical according to cultural standards of beauty. The speech concerns the human form and structure as a whole.

The Best Taqwim

The Qur'an says:

لَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا الْإِنْسَانَ فِي أَحْسَنِ تَقْوِيمٍ

We created the human being in the best taqwim.

At-Tin 95:4

Taqwim is close to setting upright, measuring, and balanced structure. Ahsan taqwim is not only facial beauty; it includes human form, capacity, and structure.

A Good Loan

The Qur'an says several times:

مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يُقْرِضُ اللَّهَ قَرْضًا حَسَنًا

Who is the one who will lend The God a good loan?

Al-Baqarah 2:245

Qardan hasanan is a good and worthy loan; in this context, spending done with right intention and manner. It does not imply collecting profit from The God or The God's need for wealth.

Good Acceptance and Good Growth

About Maryam it says:

فَتَقَبَّلَهَا رَبُّهَا بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍ وَأَنْبَتَهَا نَبَاتًا حَسَنًا

Her Lord accepted her with a good acceptance and caused her to grow with a good growth.

Ali 'Imran 3:37

Hasan in this verse describes both the manner of acceptance and the process of growth.

10. Examination of Important Qur'anic Derivatives

حَسَنَة

Hasanah has three major uses:

  • a good act;
  • a blessing or success;
  • a desirable state or result.

In:

مَنْ جَاءَ بِالْحَسَنَةِ

it is a good act.

In:

إِنْ تَمْسَسْكُمْ حَسَنَةٌ تَسُؤْهُمْ

it is a pleasant event or success.

In:

آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً

it is good and a desirable state requested from The God.

Therefore, the fixed translation “good deed” for every case is wrong.

حَسَنَات

In Al-A'raf 7:168:

وَبَلَوْنَاهُمْ بِالْحَسَنَاتِ وَالسَّيِّئَاتِ

hasanat are blessings and pleasant states.

In Hud 11:114:

إِنَّ الْحَسَنَاتِ يُذْهِبْنَ السَّيِّئَاتِ

hasanat are good acts.

The plural, like the singular, must be translated from context.

حُسْنَىٰ

Husna is sometimes the noun of the best reward:

لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا الْحُسْنَىٰ

Sometimes it is a feminine adjective:

كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ الْحُسْنَىٰ

And sometimes it describes the names of The God:

الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ

The fixed translation “Paradise” for all uses imports more meaning than the text gives.

إِحْسَان

Ihsan in the Qur'an has two main directions:

Relationship-Oriented Ihsan

Conveying good to another:

بِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا

أَحْسَنَ اللَّهُ إِلَيْكَ

أَحْسَنَ بِي

Quality-Oriented Ihsan

Doing something well or making something good:

أَحْسَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقَهُ

مَنْ أَحْسَنَ عَمَلًا

يُحْسِنُونَ صُنْعًا

These two fields can meet in the person of the muhsin: his act is right and worthy, and it also brings good to others.

مُحْسِن

Muhsin should not be translated only as “benevolent” or only as “skilled.” Sometimes the context highlights the quality of action, and sometimes conduct toward others.

The Qur'an joins the muhsin with taqwa, patience, spending, pardon, striving, submitting one's face to The God, and social goodness.

أَحْسَن

This form is sometimes a real comparative, comparing one option with another:

بِأَحْسَنَ مِنْهَا

Sometimes it expresses the highest degree:

أَحْسَنَ الْحَدِيثِ

And sometimes, in a question, it denies the existence of a better option:

وَمَنْ أَحْسَنُ مِنَ اللَّهِ حُكْمًا

11. Derivatives Connected with Divine Descriptions

The root ح س ن is related to The God and His acts in several forms in the Qur'an, but not every one of them should be turned into an independent name for The God.

The God as the Doer of أَحْسَنَ

The Qur'an says:

أَحْسَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقَهُ

And:

فَأَحْسَنَ صُوَرَكُمْ

In these verses, ahsana is the act of The God: He made created things and forms good.

أَحْسَنُ الْخَالِقِينَ

The Qur'an says:

فَتَبَارَكَ اللَّهُ أَحْسَنُ الْخَالِقِينَ

Al-Mu'minun 23:14

And:

وَتَذَرُونَ أَحْسَنَ الْخَالِقِينَ

As-Saffat 37:125

Ahsan al-khaliqin is a comparative description of The God. The expression presents The God as better and superior from the angle of creating, but ahsan here is not an independent name separated from the compound.

The Best Judgment

The Qur'an says:

وَمَنْ أَحْسَنُ مِنَ اللَّهِ حُكْمًا

Who is better than The God in judgment?

Al-Ma'idah 5:50

And:

أَلَيْسَ اللَّهُ بِأَحْكَمِ الْحَاكِمِينَ

which is from another root, but completes the field of superior divine judgment.

الأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ

The Qur'an says in several verses:

وَلِلَّهِ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ فَادْعُوهُ بِهَا

Al-A'raf 7:180

لَهُ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ

Ta-Ha 20:8

فَلَهُ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ

Al-Isra 17:110

In this expression, al-husna is the direct adjective of al-asma'. Because al-asma' is a non-rational plural, its adjective appears as a feminine singular.

The meaning of the expression is:

The better and best names belong to The God.

Husna here is not an independent name for the essence of The God; it describes the names of The God.

These verses also do not present a closed numerical list of names. The command fad'uhu biha calls the human being to call The God by His good names.

Definiteness and Indefiniteness

In al-asma' al-husna, both words are definite and form a specific adjectival construction.

But this definiteness does not permit building an independent theory about the essence of The God from the root itself. The article should stay with what grammar and context say: the names of The God are the best names.

12. Grammatical and Structural Notes

حَسُنَ as an Intransitive Verb

In:

وَحَسُنَ أُولَٰئِكَ رَفِيقًا

Hasuna is intransitive and occurs in a praise construction. Rafiqan is a specification that clarifies the angle of goodness:

They are good from the angle of companionship.

In:

حَسُنَتْ مُسْتَقَرًّا وَمُقَامًا

the two specifications explain the angle of praise: resting place and dwelling.

أَحْسَنَ with a Direct Object

In:

أَحْسَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقَهُ

the verb indicates making good.

In:

فَأَحْسَنَ صُوَرَكُمْ

the human forms are the object of the verb.

أَحْسَنَ إِلَى / بِـ

In:

أَحْسَنَ اللَّهُ إِلَيْكَ

the preposition ila shows the direction of good reaching the person.

In:

أَحْسَنَ بِي

the ba' in this structure expresses the same relationship of conveying good, and Arabic also uses it for ahsana ilayya.

بِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا

Ihsanan is an accusative verbal noun that emphasizes an implied command or charge:

Do ihsan toward parents.

The ba' shows the relation of conduct with the parents.

أَحْسَن as a Comparative

Ahsan is on the pattern af'al, and its feminine is husna on the pattern fu'la.

The comparative may appear with min:

أَحْسَنُ مِنْهَا

or be attached to the noun after it:

أَحْسَنَ الْحَدِيثِ

or be the predicate of a question:

وَمَنْ أَحْسَنُ قَوْلًا

بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ

In this structure, allati returns to the manner, quality, or method understood from context, and ahsan is the predicate of hiya.

Natural translation:

With the manner that is better.

حُسْنَىٰ as Adjective and Noun

In:

الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ

it is an adjective.

In:

لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا الْحُسْنَىٰ

it is a noun and points to the best reward or final outcome.

The grammatical role changes the translation.

13. H S N Compared with S W A

The nearest Qur'anic opposite of hasan is the field of su' and sayyi'ah.

Hasanah and sayyi'ah can both be used for an act or for an event:

  • a good act and a bad act;
  • a blessing and a calamity;
  • a desirable result and an unpleasant result.

The Qur'an says:

إِنَّ الْحَسَنَاتِ يُذْهِبْنَ السَّيِّئَاتِ

And:

ادْفَعْ بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ السَّيِّئَةَ

Hasan is not merely the absence of su'. It can be an active quality for repair, a better response, and the making of a desirable result.

14. H S N Compared with KH Y R

Khayr is close to benefit, superiority, desirable choice, and what has a truly better result.

Hasan highlights more the good quality, fittingness, beauty, or pleasingness of the thing or manner itself.

The Qur'an sometimes brings both together:

ذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ وَأَحْسَنُ تَأْوِيلًا

An-Nisa 4:59؛ Al-Isra 17:35

If the two words were entirely synonymous, this pairing would lose its precision.

As a tendency:

Khayr:

Better from the angle of benefit, result, or preference.

Ahsan:

Better from the angle of manner, quality, proportion, or final outcome.

But examples such as “a bitter medicine is khayr but not hasan” should not be turned into a firm rule. The Qur'an may consider something hasan from the angle of result or manner, even if it is not sensually pleasant in the moment.

15. H S N Compared with TT Y B

Tayyib indicates purity, pleasantness, wholesomeness, and fitting desirability in context.

Hasan emphasizes goodness, worthiness, beauty, and desirable quality.

Speech may be both tayyib and hasan, but the angle of the two descriptions differs:

  • tayyib: pure, pleasant, and far from khubth;
  • hasan: good, fitting, and pleasing.

One should not say that tayyib is always “biological health” and hasan only “outward beauty.” Both have ethical and spiritual fields in the Qur'an.

16. H S N Compared with J M L

Jamal in the Qur'an is more connected with beauty and an appearance that shows itself before the human being:

وَلَكُمْ فِيهَا جَمَالٌ

An-Nahl 16:6

But its field is not confined entirely to bodily appearance. The Qur'an speaks of sabr jamil, safh jamil, and hajr jamil: a beautiful and worthy manner in conduct.

Hasan is much broader and more frequent in the Qur'an. It comes for action, result, judgment, speech, loan, provision, form, and final outcome.

As a tendency:

  • jamal highlights visible beauty and appearance;
  • hasan includes a wider goodness and worthiness in outward form, action, and result.

17. H S N Compared with S L H

Salah is close to being right, fit for function, removal of corruption, and being in a proper state.

Hasan gives attention to the goodness and pleasing quality or form.

A righteous act is an act that is right, constructive, and opposite corruption. A hasan or ahsan act highlights the desirable quality and good manner of that act.

The two fields can apply to the same act, but they are not one. For this reason, the Qur'an says:

مَنْ عَمِلَ صَالِحًا

And elsewhere:

مَنْ أَحْسَنَ عَمَلًا

Salah highlights the rightness and constructive nature of the act; ihsan highlights the goodness of its execution.

19. Conclusion

The root ح س ن in the Qur'an moves around goodness, beauty, worthiness, and pleasing quality.

Final proposed definition:

H S N: being good, beautiful, and worthy; or doing something well and conveying good to another.

This root includes outward form and appearance, but also action, speech, relationship, result, and final outcome. Hasan can be beauty of form, but most Qur'anic uses concern something recognized as good through insight and ethical judgment.

Hasanah is sometimes a good act and sometimes a blessing or pleasant event. Hasanat, too, may be good acts or blessings depending on context.

Ihsan has two important directions:

  • doing the act well;
  • conveying good and kindness to another.

These two directions meet in the character of the muhsin, but in analyzing verses they must be recognized distinctly.

Ahsan is not merely more beautiful; it expresses the best and most fitting manner, judgment, speech, act, or final outcome. The Qur'an calls the human being to choose allati hiya ahsan, not merely something that is not bad.

Al-husna is sometimes the best reward and sometimes a feminine adjective. In the expression al-asma' al-husna, this word is the direct adjective of “the names,” and is not itself an independent name for The God. At the same time, a derivative of this root appears in describing The God's act and superiority: The God has made created things good, and He is ahsan al-khaliqin.

The root ح س ن approaches su' in the contrast between good and bad, khayr in benefit and superiority, tayyib in purity and pleasantness, jamal in beauty, and salah in rightness and constructive soundness; but none of these covers its whole field.

The language of Forqan should remain contextual in translating this root: sometimes “beautiful,” sometimes “good,” sometimes “worthy,” sometimes “good deed,” sometimes “blessing,” sometimes “doing good,” and sometimes “better.” One fixed equivalent restricts the Qur'anic range of this root.
