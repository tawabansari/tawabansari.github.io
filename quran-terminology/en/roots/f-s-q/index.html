Method Note

F S Q is one of the words that has often been turned into a tool of exclusion in religious history. Religious groups and powers have sometimes called every intellectual, political, or social opponent fāsiq in order to remove that person's credibility, dignity, or right to speak. The Qur'an does not use the word in that way.

In the Qur'an, a fāsiq is not simply someone who disagrees with a school, group, jurist, ruler, or official reading. A fāsiq is someone who has gone out from the boundary of truth and covenant, and this departure appears in behavior: breaking covenant, cutting necessary bonds, spreading corruption in the land, transmitting news without trust, attacking people's honor without proof, or disobeying a clear command of The God.

For Forqan translation and reflection, fāsiq must not become an identity label for eliminating others. It must be understood through Qur'anic signs, not through sectarian bias or social hostility.

Central Meaning

F S Q in the Qur'an points to leaving the boundary, going out from the proper limit, breaking away from covenant and moral order, and practical disobedience before a clear command of The God. Before it is a social or sectarian label, fisq in the Qur'an is an ethical and behavioral description.

The semantic core of F S Q is leaving a boundary and coming out from a protective covering. Arabic lexical discussions mention the image of a date or fruit coming out from its skin: فَسَقَتِ الرُّطَبَةُ (fasaqat al-ruṭabah). The image clarifies the Qur'anic meaning: something leaves its proper covering and place and becomes exposed to corruption, harm, or decay.

فِسْق (fisq) is the state or behavior of leaving the boundary of truth and command. فَاسِق (fāsiq) is someone whose behavior shows such departure. الْفَاسِقُون (al-fāsiqūn) in the Qur'an are usually identified through covenant-breaking, corruption, denial, injustice, or clear disobedience.

Material Image

The material image of fisq is a seed or fruit that has come out of its protective skin. The covering preserves, nurtures, and protects. When the fruit comes out of that covering, it is no longer in the same guarded and healthy state. Ethically, the image becomes this: the human being leaves the preserving boundary of covenant, trust, justice, and the command of The God, exposing self and society to corruption.

Some Arabic reports also connect a small form of this root with a mouse because it comes out of its hole and causes damage. If taken carefully, this image helps only with one point: fisq is a departure that can have destructive effects. In the Qur'an, the main criterion is leaving command, covenant, and the boundary of truth.

This image helps explain why fisq is joined with corruption, cutting bonds, and breaking covenant. Leaving the boundary is not only an inward state. It has outward effects and damages the order of relation between the human being, The God, people, and the earth.

Historical and Arabic Usage Note

In classical Arabic, F S Q is tied to going out and protruding beyond a limit. This departure may be seen materially in fruit coming out of its skin, and ethically in leaving obedience, covenant, and the proper boundary.

In the Qur'an, the meaning is used with ethical and social precision. A fāsiq is not merely someone without a religious name. A person may stand inside a religious space and yet, through covenant-breaking, corruption, lying, slander, or unjust judgment, leave the boundary of truth. The Qur'anic criterion is behavior and practical departure from truth, not outer belonging to a group.

The Qur'an Directly Defines the Fāsiqūn

One of the most important passages for F S Q appears at the opening of Surat Al-Baqarah. In Al-Baqarah 2:26, the Qur'an says:

وَمَا يُضِلُّ بِهِ إِلَّا الْفَاسِقِينَ (wa-mā yuḍillu bihi illā al-fāsiqīn)

He does not let anyone go astray by it except the fāsiqīn.

Then Al-Baqarah 2:27 immediately defines them:

الَّذِينَ يَنقُضُونَ عَهْدَ اللَّهِ مِن بَعْدِ مِيثَاقِهِ وَيَقْطَعُونَ مَا أَمَرَ اللَّهُ بِهِ أَن يُوصَلَ وَيُفْسِدُونَ فِي الْأَرْضِ ۚ أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْخَاسِرُونَ (alladhīna yanquḍūna ʿahda Allāhi min baʿdi mīthāqihi wa-yaqṭaʿūna mā amara Allāhu bihi an yūṣala wa-yufsidūna fī al-arḍ ulā'ika humu al-khāsirūn)

This verse is foundational for a Qur'an-centered definition of fisq. The fāsiq is identified by three practical signs: breaking covenant, cutting what must be joined, and spreading corruption in the land. None of these is a sectarian label. All are behaviors.

Fisq and Covenant-Breaking

The first sign of the fāsiqūn in Al-Baqarah 2:27 is breaking the covenant: يَنقُضُونَ عَهْدَ اللَّهِ (yanquḍūna ʿahda Allāh). Covenant may be divine, ethical, innate, faith-based, or social; any covenant that establishes truth and responsibility. The fāsiq breaks it after the mīthāq, after the covenant has become clear and binding.

In this sense, fisq is departure from commitment. One who keeps covenant only as long as it serves personal interest has, in Qur'anic logic, left the boundary of trust.

Fisq and Cutting Required Bonds

The second sign in the same verse is وَيَقْطَعُونَ مَا أَمَرَ اللَّهُ بِهِ أَن يُوصَلَ (wa-yaqṭaʿūna mā amara Allāhu bihi an yūṣal). Fisq is not only abandoning an individual act. It may be cutting a relation that must be preserved: kinship, justice, trust, human dignity, connection of truth with those who have a right to it, or the connection between society and moral responsibility.

For this reason, fisq is tied to social destruction. Someone who cuts necessary bonds has not only left a personal boundary; that person also damages the structure of relationships.

Fisq and Corruption in the Land

The third sign in Al-Baqarah 2:27 is corruption in the land: وَيُفْسِدُونَ فِي الْأَرْضِ (wa-yufsidūna fī al-arḍ). Fisq is close to fasād, but not identical. Fisq is leaving the boundary; fasād is the damage, decay, and disorder produced by that departure.

Fisq is therefore not merely a hidden personal state. When it reaches its Qur'anic form, its effect appears in relationships and society.

News From a Fāsiq and the Need for Verification

In Al-Hujurat 49:6, the Qur'an says:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِن جَاءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌ بِنَبَإٍ فَتَبَيَّنُوا أَن تُصِيبُوا قَوْمًا بِجَهَالَةٍ فَتُصْبِحُوا عَلَىٰ مَا فَعَلْتُمْ نَادِمِينَ (yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū in jā'akum fāsiqun bi-naba'in fa-tabayyanū an tuṣībū qawman bi-jahālatin fa-tuṣbiḥū ʿalā mā faʿaltum nādimīn)

Here fāsiq appears in relation to news and informational trust. The problem is not merely the person's belief. The issue is that the report may be untrustworthy, polluted, or dangerous, and may push society into harming others out of ignorance.

The verse does not command elimination or violence. It commands تَبَيَّنُوا (tabayyanū): verify and clarify. The aim is to protect reality and prevent harm to innocent people.

Fisq and Violating People's Honor

In An-Nur 24:4, those who make sexual accusations and do not bring enough witnesses are called fāsiqūn:

وَالَّذِينَ يَرْمُونَ الْمُحْصَنَاتِ ثُمَّ لَمْ يَأْتُوا بِأَرْبَعَةِ شُهَدَاءَ فَاجْلِدُوهُمْ ثَمَانِينَ جَلْدَةً وَلَا تَقْبَلُوا لَهُمْ شَهَادَةً أَبَدًا ۚ وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْفَاسِقُونَ (wa-alladhīna yarmūna al-muḥṣanāti thumma lam ya'tū bi-arbaʿati shuhadā'a fa-jlidūhum thamānīna jaldatan wa-lā taqbalū lahum shahādatan abadan wa-ulā'ika humu al-fāsiqūn)

Here fisq is tied to injustice against honor and social security. One who makes a sexual accusation without evidence has left the boundary of truth, the trust of speech, and the sanctity of the human being. The Qur'an calls such a person fāsiq because of destructive and harmful behavior, not because of intellectual disagreement.

Fisq and Unjust Judgment

In Al-Ma'idah 5:47, the Qur'an says:

وَلْيَحْكُمْ أَهْلُ الْإِنجِيلِ بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ فِيهِ ۚ وَمَن لَّمْ يَحْكُم بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْفَاسِقُونَ (wa-l-yaḥkum ahlu al-injīli bimā anzala Allāhu fīhi wa-man lam yaḥkum bimā anzala Allāhu fa-ulā'ika humu al-fāsiqūn)

In this verse, fisq concerns judgment and ruling. One who sets aside the divine criterion of truth and justice, and judges by desire, power, interest, or partisanship, has left the boundary of sound judgment.

This verse should not become a tool of takfir and exclusion. Its context concerns responsibility to judge by what The God has sent down. The subject is judicial and ethical behavior before truth, not labeling every intellectual opponent.

Fisq and Clear Disobedience

About Iblis, Al-Kahf 18:50 says:

وَإِذْ قُلْنَا لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ اسْجُدُوا لِآدَمَ فَسَجَدُوا إِلَّا إِبْلِيسَ كَانَ مِنَ الْجِنِّ فَفَسَقَ عَنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّهِ (wa-idh qulnā li-l-malā'ikati usjudū li-ādama fa-sajadū illā iblīsa kāna mina al-jinni fa-fasaqa ʿan amri rabbih)

The expression is direct: فَفَسَقَ عَنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّهِ (fa-fasaqa ʿan amri rabbih). Fisq means leaving the command of his Lord. The issue with Iblis was not ignorance. He departed from a clear command.

The particle عن (ʿan) matters. Fisq from the command of the Lord means breaking away from the orbit of command and leaving the boundary that had to be kept.

Fisq and the Behavior of the People of Lut

In Al-Ankabut 29:34, it says:

إِنَّا مُنزِلُونَ عَلَىٰ أَهْلِ هَٰذِهِ الْقَرْيَةِ رِجْزًا مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ بِمَا كَانُوا يَفْسُقُونَ (innā munzilūna ʿalā ahli hādhihi al-qaryati rijzan mina al-samā'i bimā kānū yafsuqūn)

Here fisq concerns the corrupt and transgressive behavior of the people of Lut. The verse does not attach a signless label; the preceding story shows behavior marked by violation, breaking boundaries, and moral corruption.

Here too, fisq is a behavioral condition, not the name of a social group to be eliminated without evidence.

Fisq and Faith

In As-Sajdah 32:18, the Qur'an says:

أَفَمَن كَانَ مُؤْمِنًا كَمَن كَانَ فَاسِقًا ۚ لَّا يَسْتَوُونَ (a-fa-man kāna mu'minan ka-man kāna fāsiqan lā yastawūn)

This contrast shows that fisq is opposed to lived faith. Faith in the Qur'an is not mere claim. It requires loyalty, action, awe, justice, and responsibility. The fāsiq is someone who has left this practical boundary.

If fisq appears opposite faith, it must not be reduced to disagreement with our group. The Qur'an itself identifies faith and fisq by ethical and behavioral signs.

Fisq in Food and Lawful Boundaries

In Al-An'am 6:121, the Qur'an says:

وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا مِمَّا لَمْ يُذْكَرِ اسْمُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ وَإِنَّهُ لَفِسْقٌ (wa-lā ta'kulū mimmā lam yudhkar ismu Allāhi ʿalayhi wa-innahu la-fisq)

Here fisq concerns leaving the boundary of command in eating. An act that should remain within the frame of remembering The God and the lawful boundary has left that frame. This use shows that fisq does not only describe people; sometimes the act or state itself is called fisq.

Distinguishing Qur'anic Uses

In Al-Baqarah 2:26-27, the fāsiqūn are those who break the covenant of The God, cut necessary bonds, and spread corruption in the land. In Al-Hujurat 49:6, fāsiq appears in the field of news and informational trust. In An-Nur 24:4, fisq is tied to sexual accusation and the violation of people's honor. In Al-Ma'idah 5:47, fisq concerns not judging by what The God has sent down.

In Al-Kahf 18:50, fisq is leaving a clear command of the Lord. In Al-Ankabut 29:34, fisq describes the corrupt and transgressive behavior of the people of Lut. In As-Sajdah 32:18, the fāsiq stands opposite the believer. In Al-An'am 6:121, the departure from a lawful boundary in eating is itself called fisq.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In فَفَسَقَ عَنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّهِ (fa-fasaqa ʿan amri rabbih), the particle عن (ʿan) is important. Fisq here is departure from the command of the Lord. The structure shows that the root is tied to going out from a reference point and boundary.

In إِن جَاءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌ بِنَبَإٍ (in jā'akum fāsiqun bi-naba'), fāsiq is joined to news. The issue is social trust and reliability in transmitting information. The command فَتَبَيَّنُوا (fa-tabayyanū) shows that the Qur'anic response to suspect news is verification, not excitement and harm.

In أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْفَاسِقُونَ (ulā'ika humu al-fāsiqūn), the pronoun of separation and the definite predicate emphasize that these behaviors are clear instances of fisq. But that emphasis must be understood through the behaviors mentioned, not through unrestricted labeling.

In وَإِنَّهُ لَفِسْقٌ (wa-innahu la-fisq), the lām of emphasis shows that the act or state is a clear departure from the boundary of command.

Difference From Nearby Roots and Words

F S Q comes close to K F R, but they are not identical. Kufr is covering and denying clarified truth. Fisq is practical departure from the boundary of truth, covenant, or command. They may gather in one person, but their meanings are not the same.

F S Q differs from Ẓ L M. Ẓulm is putting something in the wrong place and violating a right. Fisq is leaving the proper limit and boundary. Every fisq may lead to ẓulm, but ẓulm emphasizes violation and misplacement more strongly.

F S Q is close to F J R. Fujūr is tearing through the boundary of taqwa and moral rupture. Fisq is broader and may include any departure from covenant, command, trust, or the boundary of truth.

F S Q differs from F S D. Fasād is corruption and the breakdown of order. Fisq is leaving the boundary; fasād is its destructive result in the land and society. In Al-Baqarah 2:27, the two stand together: the fāsiqūn spread corruption in the land.

F S Q is close to ʿ Ṣ Y, but not identical. ʿIṣyān is disobedience. Fisq is disobedience that becomes departure from the proper boundary. Not every act of disobedience is full fisq, but when defiance leaves the orbit of covenant and clear command, it becomes fisq.

F S Q differs from N F Q. Nifāq is the split between outward appearance and inward reality. Fisq is leaving the boundary of truth in behavior. A hypocrite may be fāsiq, but fisq does not always mean nifāq.

Forqan Translation Method

In Forqan translation, fāsiq must not automatically mean irreligious, our opponent, sinner in a general sense, or member of an outside group. In many cases, the behavioral meaning should be kept alive in explanation: one who has left the boundary of truth, covenant-breaker, one who has left a clear command, or one who has gone out from the boundary of trust and justice.

The main translation may keep fāsiq because it is an important Qur'anic term. But the root note must make clear that a fāsiq in the Qur'an is known by practical signs, not by a sectarian label.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

فِسْق (fisq): fisq; departure from the boundary of truth; leaving command and covenant.

فَاسِق (fāsiq): fāsiq; one who has left the boundary of truth; covenant-breaker and one who has left trust, in explanation.

الْفَاسِقُون (al-fāsiqūn): the fāsiqūn; those who have gone out from the boundary of truth and covenant.

فَفَسَقَ عَنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّهِ (fa-fasaqa ʿan amri rabbih): so he left the command of his Lord; so he departed from the boundary of his Lord's command; so he broke away from the orbit of his Lord's command, in explanation.

إِن جَاءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌ بِنَبَإٍ (in jā'akum fāsiqun bi-naba'): if a fāsiq brings you news; if someone not keeping the boundaries of trust brings you news, in explanation.

وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْفَاسِقُونَ (wa-ulā'ika humu al-fāsiqūn): those are the fāsiqūn; those are the ones who have left the boundary of truth.

وَإِنَّهُ لَفِسْقٌ (wa-innahu la-fisq): indeed, it is fisq; it is a clear departure from the boundary of command.

Summary

F S Q in the Qur'an points to leaving the limit, coming out from protective covering, and abandoning the proper boundary of covenant, trust, and command. A fāsiq is one whose behavior shows this departure: breaking covenant, cutting necessary bonds, spreading corruption in the land, bringing news without trust, attacking people's honor, moving judgment away from truth, or leaving a clear command of the Lord.

Therefore, fisq in the Qur'an is not a social label for eliminating opponents. It is an ethical and behavioral description that must be measured by the Qur'an's own signs. A person does not become fāsiq merely because of religious, political, or intellectual disagreement with a group. Fisq becomes meaningful when departure from truth, covenant, trust, justice, and clear command is visible in behavior.

This precision takes the word out of misuse and returns it to its Qur'anic place: ethical assessment of leaving the boundary of truth, not a tool of power for excluding human beings.
