Core meaning: falling into harmful hardship or distress; severe suffering and injury, and in some uses falling into corruption or sin

The Arabic root ع ن ت concerns hardship, distress, and harm that place a person in a difficult and damaging situation. عَنِتَ means to suffer such hardship, while أَعْنَتَهُ means that another person subjected him to it.

The root can express more than the difficulty of performing a task: it can denote hardship involving injury, overwhelming pressure, ruin, or even falling into sin.

Someone working hard to build a house differs from someone trapped under unjust and harmful conditions. Both may experience difficulty, but the second situation involves pressure and injury of a kind that عَنَت can describe.

The root occurs five times in five Quranic verses: Al-Baqarah 2:220, Al Imran 3:118, An-Nisa 4:25, At-Tawbah 9:128, and Al-Hujurat 49:7.

These occurrences concern burdensome requirements, social harm, distress connected with marriage, human suffering, and the harmful consequences of mistaken decisions.

1. The root in early Arabic

عَنَت: hardship involving harm

Early dictionaries explain العَنَت as encountering hardship and falling into distress.

عَنِتَ الرَّجُلُ means that the man suffered hardship; أَعْنَتَهُ غَيْرُهُ means that someone else subjected him to hardship and distress.

The same sources also record corruption, destruction, sin, and injury within this word family.

These senses should not be treated as identical without considering context.

Hardship may cause injury; severe injury may lead to ruin; and sinful conduct can expose a person to harmful consequences. Yet not every hardship is a sin, and sin does not simply mean physical suffering.

The broader semantic range must therefore be distinguished from particular applications.

Quranic usage shows that عَنَت could denote harmful hardship and distress in the Arabic of the revelation period. Classical dictionaries record the same semantic field. Dating each particular sense to pre-Islamic Arabic nevertheless requires independent, datable evidence; meanings recorded in later dictionaries cannot all be assigned to that period without examination.

A physical image: injury and a bone breaking again

One concrete use in early Arabic concerns bones.

عَنِتَ الْعَظْمُ describes a bone that has broken or has been injured and broken again after healing.

أَعْنَتَ الْجَابِرُ الْكَسِيرَ describes a bone-setter who worsens an injury through rough or careless treatment.

This use gives a concrete image of harm accompanied by hardship.

Consider a broken leg that is healing. If a caregiver carelessly causes the same bone to break again, the caregiver has done more than complicate treatment: a new and more serious injury has been inflicted.

A verse attributed to Ruʾbah ibn al-ʿAjjāj reads:

فَأَرْغَمَ اللَّهُ الْأُنُوفَ الرُّغَّمَا
مَجْدُوعَهَا وَالْعَنِتَ الْمُخَشَّمَا

fa-arghama allāhu al-unūfa al-rughghamā / majdūʿahā wa-al-ʿanita al-mukhashshamā

“May God rub those humiliated noses in the dust—the severed ones and the injured, battered ones.”

Here العَنِت occurs alongside a description of a damaged nose and conveys injury or breakage. Repeated injury is attested in the lexical discussion, but need not be added to the translation of this line.

Ruʾbah belonged to the Islamic period, spanning Umayyad and Abbasid rule. This is therefore evidence from classical Arabic of that period, not independent proof of pre-Islamic usage.

Its relevance is that عَنَت was not limited to mental difficulty or ordinary discomfort: it could describe actual bodily injury.

The bone-related use alone does not establish that every other meaning historically developed from bone breakage. That claim would require independent historical evidence.

تَعَنُّت: making difficulties for someone else

تَعَنَّتَ can describe deliberately causing another person trouble or hardship.

A person may ask a question to discover the truth; another may ask the same question in order to trap the respondent, find a fault, or impose needless difficulty.

The second behavior can be called تَعَنُّت.

This accounts for the associated senses of troublesome exactingness and obstinacy.

That ethical coloring belongs to the particular form and use of تَعَنَّتَ. It does not imply that everyone who experiences عَنَت in the Quran is obstinate or sinful.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important forms in this Arabic word family include:

عَنِتَ: suffered hardship, distress, or injury.

عَنَت: hardship, difficulty, harmful distress; in some contexts, corruption or sin.

أَعْنَتَ: subjected another to hardship or distress, or caused injury.

إِعْنَات: causing another person hardship or distress.

عَنَّتَ: treated someone harshly or required something difficult.

تَعَنَّتَ: acted with troublesome exactingness, sought to cause someone difficulty, or behaved obstinately.

مُتَعَنِّت: an exacting or obstinate person, or someone seeking another's faults in order to cause trouble.

عَنِت: broken or injured, in the usage concerning bones.

عَنُوت: difficult to traverse or climb, describing a mound or elevation.

The Quran uses three constructions from this family: the basic verb عَنِتَ, the Form IV verb أَعْنَتَ, and the noun عَنَت.

عَنِتُّمْ occurs in two verses, لَعَنِتُّمْ in one, لَأَعْنَتَكُمْ in one, and الْعَنَتَ in one.

Forms such as تَعَنَّتَ, مُتَعَنِّت, and إِعْنَات are known from the dictionaries but do not occur in the Quran.

3. Quranic uses

Al-Baqarah 2:220: imposing hardship and allowing ease

فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ ۗ وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْيَتَامَىٰ ۖ قُلْ إِصْلَاحٌ لَهُمْ خَيْرٌ ۖ وَإِنْ تُخَالِطُوهُمْ فَإِخْوَانُكُمْ ۚ وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ الْمُفْسِدَ مِنَ الْمُصْلِحِ ۚ وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَأَعْنَتَكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ

fī al-dunyā wa-al-ākhirah wa-yasʾalūnaka ʿani al-yatāmā qul iṣlāḥun lahum khayr wa-in tukhāliṭūhum fa-ikhwānukum wa-allāhu yaʿlamu al-mufsida mina al-muṣliḥ wa-law shāʾa allāhu la-aʿnatakum inna allāha ʿazīzun ḥakīm

Al-Baqarah 2:220

“Concerning this world and the Hereafter. They ask you about orphans. Say: setting their affairs right is better. If you share life with them, they are your brothers. The God knows the corrupter from the reformer. Had The God willed, He would have subjected you to hardship. Indeed, The God is Mighty, Wise.”

Al-Baqarah 2:220

لَأَعْنَتَكُمْ is a Form IV verb expressing the act of causing others hardship.

عَنِتَ describes the person experiencing hardship; أَعْنَتَ describes the agent imposing it on someone else.

The context concerns caring for orphans and their relationship with other households.

If care had required guardians to separate every aspect of the orphans' lives from their own, that burden could have hindered their willingness or ability to provide proper care.

Instead of prohibiting shared living, the verse makes improving the orphans' circumstances the criterion and calls them the addressees' brothers.

Here عَنَت concerns difficulty and pressure that could have been imposed on guardians.

The verse also distinguishes the مُفْسِد from the مُصْلِح. Ease in shared living therefore does not authorize exploiting an orphan's property or rights.

The statement says that God could have placed people under hardship, while in this matter providing a way consistent with reform and shared human life.

أَعْنَتَكُمْ does not here mean punishment for sin. Its direct sense is imposing hardship, and the context concerns making the responsibility of orphan care burdensome.

Al Imran 3:118: those who desire others' suffering

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَتَّخِذُوا بِطَانَةً مِنْ دُونِكُمْ لَا يَأْلُونَكُمْ خَبَالًا وَدُّوا مَا عَنِتُّمْ قَدْ بَدَتِ الْبَغْضَاءُ مِنْ أَفْوَاهِهِمْ وَمَا تُخْفِي صُدُورُهُمْ أَكْبَرُ ۚ قَدْ بَيَّنَّا لَكُمُ الْآيَاتِ ۖ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā tattakhidhū biṭānatan min dūnikum lā yaʾlūnakum khabālan waddū mā ʿanittum qad badati al-baghḍāʾu min afwāhihim wa-mā tukhfī ṣudūruhum akbar qad bayyannā lakumu al-āyāti in kuntum taʿqilūn

Āl ʿImrān 3:118

“You who have believed, do not take confidants from outside your own: they spare no effort to corrupt your affairs and desire your suffering. Hatred has become apparent from their mouths, and what their hearts conceal is greater. We have made the signs clear to you, if you use reason.”

Al Imran 3:118

عَنِتُّمْ is a past-tense form of the basic verb.

وَدُّوا مَا عَنِتُّمْ indicates that the people being described desire the addressees' suffering and distress.

عَنَت itself denotes hardship and harm; the fact that others desire it is expressed by وَدُّوا.

The root ع ن ت does not itself mean hostility or ill will. A person may experience hardship through natural causes, whereas this verse concerns people who want others to suffer.

The accompanying expression لَا يَأْلُونَكُمْ خَبَالًا describes sparing no effort to cause corruption and harm.

Together, خَبَال and عَنَت portray damaging conduct and a desire for others' distress.

In a reading attentive to the descriptions within this verse, the people concerned display identifiable conduct: seeking to corrupt, desiring others' suffering, and expressing hostility. This contextual observation should be distinguished from a claim that all interpreters agree on the scope of مِنْ دُونِكُمْ.

On this reading, assessment of the danger takes those described behaviors into account; the root ع ن ت alone does not determine the boundaries of the group concerned.

Lexically, عَنِتُّمْ means “you suffered hardship or distress”; the expression as a whole concerns wishing suffering and harm upon the addressees.

An-Nisa 4:25: fearing serious distress

وَمَنْ لَمْ يَسْتَطِعْ مِنْكُمْ طَوْلًا أَنْ يَنْكِحَ الْمُحْصَنَاتِ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ فَمِنْ مَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ مِنْ فَتَيَاتِكُمُ الْمُؤْمِنَاتِ ۚ وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِإِيمَانِكُمْ ۚ بَعْضُكُمْ مِنْ بَعْضٍ ۚ فَانْكِحُوهُنَّ بِإِذْنِ أَهْلِهِنَّ وَآتُوهُنَّ أُجُورَهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ مُحْصَنَاتٍ غَيْرَ مُسَافِحَاتٍ وَلَا مُتَّخِذَاتِ أَخْدَانٍ ۚ فَإِذَا أُحْصِنَّ فَإِنْ أَتَيْنَ بِفَاحِشَةٍ فَعَلَيْهِنَّ نِصْفُ مَا عَلَى الْمُحْصَنَاتِ مِنَ الْعَذَابِ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنْ خَشِيَ الْعَنَتَ مِنْكُمْ ۚ وَأَنْ تَصْبِرُوا خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ

wa-man lam yastaṭiʿ minkum ṭawlan an yankiḥa al-muḥṣanāti al-muʾmināti fa-min mā malakat aymānukum min fatayātikumu al-muʾmināt wa-allāhu aʿlamu bi-īmānikum baʿḍukum min baʿḍ fa-inkiḥūhunna bi-idhni ahlihinna wa-ātūhunna ujūrahunna bi-al-maʿrūfi muḥṣanātin ghayra musāfiḥātin wa-lā muttakhidhāti akhdān fa-idhā uḥṣinna fa-in atayna bi-fāḥishatin fa-ʿalayhinna niṣfu mā ʿalā al-muḥṣanāti mina al-ʿadhāb dhālika li-man khashiya al-ʿanata minkum wa-an taṣbirū khayrun lakum wa-allāhu ghafūrun raḥīm

Al-Nisāʾ 4:25

“Whoever among you lacks the means to marry free believing women, then from your believing young women whom your right hands possess. The God knows your faith best; you are of one another. Marry them with their people's permission and give them their due honorably, as chaste women, neither engaging in illicit intercourse nor taking secret lovers. Once married, if they commit an indecency, their punishment is half that of free women. This is for whoever among you fears serious distress; and being patient is better for you. The God is Forgiving, Merciful.”

An-Nisa 4:25

الْعَنَتَ is a noun in لِمَنْ خَشِيَ الْعَنَتَ مِنْكُمْ.

Its broader sense is harmful hardship or distress, while the verse concerns marriage and the circumstances of those unable to afford marriage to free believing women.

Classical lexical and exegetical discussions commonly explain الْعَنَتَ here as illicit intercourse or falling into sin; hardship and ruin are also recorded explanations.

This reading has clear contextual support: the verse concerns marriage, chastity, sexual relationships, and their limits.

The broader lexical range must be distinguished from a word's contextual sense. A broad root meaning does not rule out a more specific sense in a particular verse.

Here الْعَنَتَ can express the distress associated with the risk of falling into sexual wrongdoing. Its other uses involving hardship do not invalidate that contextual interpretation.

Illicit intercourse or sin is thus a well-attested interpretation here, although it should not be imposed on every occurrence of the root.

Comparison with the other verses makes this distinction clearer.

In Al-Baqarah 2:220, أَعْنَتَكُمْ concerns making orphan care burdensome; in At-Tawbah 9:128, عَنِتُّمْ concerns people's suffering; and in Al-Hujurat 49:7, لَعَنِتُّمْ describes the difficult consequences of mistaken decisions.

None of these other occurrences necessarily concerns illicit intercourse.

Forqan may retain “serious distress” in the translation of An-Nisa 4:25 while explaining the established interpretation involving fear of sexual sin. “Fear of falling into sin” is another contextual translation; the broader root meaning alone does not decide between them.

The verse also says وَأَنْ تَصْبِرُوا خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ. This guidance concerns the situation described; it does not make عَنَت lexically equivalent to an inability to be patient.

At-Tawbah 9:128: human suffering weighs upon the Messenger

لَقَدْ جَاءَكُمْ رَسُولٌ مِنْ أَنْفُسِكُمْ عَزِيزٌ عَلَيْهِ مَا عَنِتُّمْ حَرِيصٌ عَلَيْكُمْ بِالْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَءُوفٌ رَحِيمٌ

la-qad jāʾakum rasūlun min anfusikum ʿazīzun ʿalayhi mā ʿanittum ḥarīṣun ʿalaykum bi-al-muʾminīna raʾūfun raḥīm

Al-Tawbah 9:128

“A Messenger from among yourselves has come to you. Your suffering weighs upon him; he is deeply concerned for you, and compassionate and merciful toward the believers.”

At-Tawbah 9:128

عَنِتُّمْ is the same basic verb found in Al Imran 3:118.

The important difference is the response to other people's suffering.

Al Imran 3:118 describes people who desire the addressees' suffering.

At-Tawbah 9:128 presents the Messenger as someone upon whom people's suffering weighs heavily.

In both verses, the lexical meaning concerns hardship and distress, but the responses to it are very different.

The comparison confirms that عَنَت does not inherently denote hostility or ill will.

Nor does عَنِتُّمْ by itself identify the cause of suffering.

People may suffer through poverty, social injury, others' misconduct, or the consequences of their own decisions. The word denotes the difficult condition; identifying its cause requires context and other evidence.

عَزِيزٌ عَلَيْهِ indicates that people's suffering is burdensome and painful for the Messenger.

A natural translation of عَزِيزٌ عَلَيْهِ مَا عَنِتُّمْ is therefore:

“Your suffering weighs upon him.”

Al-Hujurat 49:7: harmful consequences of following mistaken decisions

وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ فِيكُمْ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ۚ لَوْ يُطِيعُكُمْ فِي كَثِيرٍ مِنَ الْأَمْرِ لَعَنِتُّمْ وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ حَبَّبَ إِلَيْكُمُ الْإِيمَانَ وَزَيَّنَهُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ وَكَرَّهَ إِلَيْكُمُ الْكُفْرَ وَالْفُسُوقَ وَالْعِصْيَانَ ۚ أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الرَّاشِدُونَ

wa-iʿlamū anna fīkum rasūla allāh law yuṭīʿukum fī kathīrin mina al-amri la-ʿanittum wa-lākinna allāha ḥabbaba ilaykumu al-īmāna wa-zayyanahu fī qulūbikum wa-karraha ilaykumu al-kufra wa-al-fusūqa wa-al-ʿiṣyān ulāʾika humu al-rāshidūn

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:7

“Know that the Messenger of The God is among you. If he followed you in many matters, you would suffer hardship. But The God has made faith beloved to you and adorned it in your hearts, and has made disbelief, transgression, and disobedience hateful to you. Those are the rightly guided.”

Al-Hujurat 49:7

لَعَنِتُّمْ derives from the basic verb عَنِتَ and carries the lām introducing the consequence of the conditional construction.

لَوْ يُطِيعُكُمْ ... لَعَنِتُّمْ expresses a conditional relationship: if the Messenger followed them in many affairs, they themselves would suffer hardship and harm.

Here عَنَت is the potential consequence of mistaken decisions.

The preceding verse, Al-Hujurat 49:6, stresses verifying a report before acting, so that people do not harm a group out of ignorance and then regret their conduct.

This connection indicates that hasty decisions or reliance on unverified reports can have consequences that also entangle those making the decisions.

For example, if a group demands action against others on the basis of a false report, and the decision-maker complies without checking, innocent people may be harmed and the community may face conflict, regret, and damaging consequences.

The problem is not merely that implementing the decision is difficult: the decision itself can cause distress.

عَنَت here does not simply mean regret. Regret appears in the preceding verse; this verse describes a difficult and harmful situation that may result from such conduct.

4. Comparison with related roots

ع ن ت and ع س ر

عُسْر concerns difficulty in an action or situation and contrasts with يُسْر, ease.

For example, Al-Baqarah 2:185 says that God desires ease for people, not hardship.

عَنَت also concerns difficulty, with distress, pressure, and harmful suffering prominent in its attested uses.

An activity may be difficult without injuring the person doing it, such as learning a complex skill through sustained practice.

Imposing conditions that trap or harm someone, by contrast, can exemplify عَنَت.

The roots overlap, but should not be substituted for each other in every sentence without attention to context.

ع ن ت and ش ق ق

مَشَقَّة also concerns difficulty and suffering and can describe strenuous, demanding work.

An-Nahl 16:7 refers to reaching a land that people could otherwise reach only with great exertion.

That passage concerns the difficulty of travel and the effort needed to reach a destination.

عَنَت can encompass suffering of this kind, though many uses concern becoming trapped in harmful circumstances or imposing pressure on someone else.

مَشَقَّة can therefore help explain عَنَت, but does not cover every branch of its meaning.

ع ن ت and ح ر ج

حَرَج concerns constraint, difficulty, and restriction, and in some contexts relates to sin or responsibility arising from it.

Expressions denying حَرَج can be compared with expressions such as لَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ in discussions of removing restrictions. جُنَاح, however, belongs to ج ن ح and is not a derivative of ح ر ج.

عَنَت often describes the difficult and harmful condition affecting someone, whereas حَرَج may focus on the constraint or obstacle confronting them.

This is not an absolute boundary: both words may apply to the same situation.

Someone subjected to unnecessary and overwhelming requirements may experience both constraint and harmful suffering.

Each word nevertheless needs to be understood through its own form and context.

ع ن ت and ض ر ر

ضَرَر denotes harm or injury.

عَنَت can also involve the difficult, pressuring circumstances in which someone becomes caught.

A person may suffer financial loss in an accident without undergoing prolonged difficulty; that loss can be ضَرَر.

Someone enduring harmful conditions over a long period and struggling to escape them is in a situation that عَنَت can describe.

Long duration is not essential to عَنَت, however. The severity and nature of the distress also matter.

ع ن ت and أ ث م

إِثْم concerns sin and the moral responsibility associated with wrongdoing.

Dictionaries record sin among the senses of عَنَت, but this overlap does not make the two words identical in every verse.

In An-Nisa 4:25, the context of marriage and chastity supports interpreting عَنَت as falling into sinful conduct.

In At-Tawbah 9:128, however, people's suffering does not necessarily mean that they have sinned.

Likewise, the potential hardship of caring for orphans in Al-Baqarah 2:220 does not itself mean committing sin.

Sin is therefore a particular sense or application in some contexts, not a meaning to impose on every Quranic occurrence.

5. Distinguishing عَنِتَ from عَنَتِ الْوُجُوهُ

The Quran contains another form whose spelling resembles عَنِتَ but belongs to a different root.

Ta-Ha 20:111

وَعَنَتِ الْوُجُوهُ لِلْحَيِّ الْقَيُّومِ وَقَدْ خَابَ مَنْ حَمَلَ ظُلْمًا

wa-ʿanati al-wujūhu li-al-ḥayyi al-qayyūmi wa-qad khāba man ḥamala ẓulmā

Ṭā Hā 20:111

“Faces have humbled themselves before the Living, the Sustainer; and whoever bears wrongdoing has failed.”

عَنَتِ here belongs to ع ن و, not ع ن ت.

عَنَا يَعْنُو in this use denotes submission and bowing down.

The final tāʾ in عَنَتِ is the feminine marker of the past-tense verb. الْوُجُوهُ is the broken plural of وَجْه; as a nonhuman plural, it takes feminine singular agreement here. The originally unvoweled tāʾ receives a kasrah in connected speech to avoid two consecutive unvoweled consonants.

The similarity in spelling must not cause this form to be merged with عَنِتَ from ع ن ت.

Ta-Ha 20:111 is therefore not counted among the occurrences of ع ن ت.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

A basic equivalent is “to suffer hardship or distress,” but the form and direction of the action must be preserved in each verse.

لَأَعْنَتَكُمْ — Al-Baqarah 2:220

Brief root meaning: harmful hardship and distress; causing someone difficulty.

Contextual meaning: God could have imposed hardship upon the addressees in carrying out their responsibility.

Proposed translation: “Had The God willed, He would have subjected you to hardship.”

عَنِتُّمْ — Al Imran 3:118

Brief root meaning: suffering harmful hardship and distress.

Contextual meaning: the addressees' suffering and distress, desired by those who wish them harm.

Proposed translation: “They desire your suffering.”

الْعَنَتَ — An-Nisa 4:25

Brief root meaning: hardship, constraint, and harmful distress.

Contextual meaning: distress feared in the circumstances of marriage; a well-attested interpretation identifies it as the risk of illicit intercourse or falling into sin.

Proposed translation: “For whoever among you fears serious distress,” with an explanation of the established interpretation concerning fear of falling into sin.

عَنِتُّمْ — At-Tawbah 9:128

Brief root meaning: suffering hardship and distress.

Contextual meaning: people's suffering, which is painful for the Messenger to bear.

Proposed translation: “Your suffering weighs upon him.”

لَعَنِتُّمْ — Al-Hujurat 49:7

Brief root meaning: suffering harmful hardship and distress.

Contextual meaning: falling into hardship as a consequence of following mistaken decisions.

Proposed translation: “You would suffer hardship.”

عَنَت should not invariably be translated as sin, illicit intercourse, destruction, or punishment.

These meanings may be relevant to particular lexical uses or contexts, but are not the equivalent of every Quranic form of this root.

7. Conclusion

The root ع ن ت concerns suffering hardship, distress, and constraint that may involve injury and harmful consequences.

Lexical uses also include breakage and bodily injury, corruption, destruction, or falling into sin.

Its Quranic uses should not all be limited to any one of these applications.

In Al-Baqarah 2:220, it concerns hardship that could have been imposed on people.

In Al Imran 3:118, it is the distress desired by those who wish others harm.

In An-Nisa 4:25, it concerns feared distress in the circumstances of marriage, commonly interpreted as fear of falling into sexual sin.

In At-Tawbah 9:128, it is suffering that weighs painfully upon the Messenger.

In Al-Hujurat 49:7, it expresses becoming entangled in the difficult and harmful consequences of mistaken decisions.

These uses show that عَنَت is neither necessarily sin nor merely ordinary effort: it describes hardship and distress that place pressure upon a person and may cause harm.

Final definition of the root ع ن ت:

“To suffer hardship, pressure, or harmful distress; in a transitive form, to subject someone else to that condition. The hardship may be physical, social, practical, or moral, and in some contexts involves corruption, serious injury, or sin. The precise sense depends on the form and context.”
