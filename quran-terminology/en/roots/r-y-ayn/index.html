Central meanings: height and elevation, associated with increase and abundance; in another branch, return

1. Introducing the root and its semantic field

The Arabic root R Y ʿ has several seemingly different uses. Rīʿ denotes elevated ground, a pass, or a road among heights. Rayʿ in another usage refers to increase, produce, and yield. The verb rāʿa yarīʿu also means returning in one of its uses.

Early lexical accounts show that the root should not be confined to one simple, all-encompassing meaning. One analysis identifies two principal branches: height and elevation, and return. Increase and abundance are discussed under the first.

The root occurs only once in the Qur’an: rīʿin in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:128. Here it has a spatial meaning, referring to an elevated place or passage through the terrain.

2. Principal forms and derivatives

رِيع and رَيْع — rīʿ and rayʿ

Spatially, these denote raised ground, a hill, a pass, a road, or a passage. Both kasrah and fatḥah on the rāʾ are recorded.

رِيعة and رَيعة — rīʿah and rayʿah

These denote elevated places or raised ground. Lexical accounts differ over the singular and plural relationships of these forms.

أَرْياع، رُيُوع and رِياع — aryāʿ, ruyūʿ, and riyāʿ

These are recorded plurals of rīʿ in spatial uses, referring to heights or passages.

رَيْع — rayʿ

In the branch concerning increase, this means growth, yield, or produce. Rayʿ al-zarʿ, for example, concerns the crop’s increase over the initial quantity. In economic usage it also denotes income or revenue from property or land.

راعَ يَرِيعُ — rāʿa yarīʿu

The verb has recorded uses for increasing or becoming more abundant in growth and production, and, in another branch, returning.

أَراعَ — arāʿa

In uses concerning crops and living things, this refers to increased yield, growth, or production. Arāʿat al-shajaratu, for example, means that the tree bore more fruit.

مَرِيعة — marīʿah

Describing land, fertile and productive.

رَيْعان — rayʿān

The beginning or prominent, flourishing part of a period. Rayʿān al-shabāb refers to early youth or its prime.

Only rīʿin occurs in the Qur’an. The other forms help explain the root’s semantic range and its wider uses.

3. Height, roads, and increase in Arabic

Elevated and raised ground

One clear use of rīʿ refers to land higher than its surroundings.

This may be a hill, pass, raised slope, or part of a mountain route. It need not denote a large mountain or lofty summit.

Arabic also uses rīʿ for a road. Some lexical accounts particularly describe a road opening out from a mountain or passing among heights.

There is an understandable spatial connection: a mountain road may cross a pass, rise, or passage between mountains.

Some early definitions also give “road” without a mountain restriction. Mountain terrain must therefore not be made an indispensable condition of every use; each context establishes the precise sense.

Increase and yield

Another branch concerns increase and abundance.

Early lexical accounts record examples such as:

Rāʿa al-ṭaʿāmu: the quantity or yield of food increased.

Arāʿat al-shajaratu: the tree bore more fruit.

Arḍun marīʿatun: fertile, productive land.

The use of rayʿ for crop yield and income is intelligible within this semantic field, without establishing a definite historical order among the senses.

Here increase is concrete: more crops, fruit, or food. Sharing a root does not justify importing this meaning into a verse concerned with location.

Return

The other principal branch concerns returning.

Early Arabic uses rāʿa yarīʿu for something or someone returning to a previous place, condition, or direction.

This is not identical to height or increase. Although return and yield can sometimes be conceptually connected, the evidence does not justify reducing every meaning to a single image.

Each branch should therefore be examined through its actual usage.

4. Early Arabic witnesses

Rīʿ as a road

A line attributed to the pre-Islamic poet al-Musayyab ibn ʿAlas reads:

فِي الْآلِ يَخْفِضُهَا وَيَرْفَعُهَا
رِيعٌ يَلُوحُ كَأَنَّهُ سَحْلٌ

fī al-āli yakhfiḍuhā wa-yarfaʿuhā / rīʿun yalūḥu ka-annahu saḥlun

Its sense is:

“Amid the mirage, a road gleaming like white cloth carries the caravan now down and now up.”

Saḥl here means white cloth. The poet compares the visible road in the landscape to a white fabric.

Rīʿ in this line means a road or passage. It illustrates the spatial usage in early Arabic beyond explanations of the Qur’anic verse.

Rīʿ as returning

Ṭarafah ibn al-ʿAbd’s Muʿallaqah describes a she-camel:

تَرِيعُ إِلَى صَوْتِ الْمُهِيبِ وَتَتَّقِي
بِذِي خُصَلٍ رَوْعَاتِ أَكْلَفَ مُلْبِدِ

tarīʿu ilā ṣawti al-muhībi wa-tattaqī / bi-dhī khuṣalin rawʿāti aklafa mulbidi

Its sense is:

“She returns to the caller’s voice and with her tufted tail wards off the alarming advances of a dark, matted-haired male.”

Tarīʿu here means returning. Independently of elevation and increase, it illustrates the root’s second branch.

These witnesses, transmitted as poetry by pre-Islamic poets, illustrate different uses of R Y ʿ. Dating a particular wording depends on its transmission and attribution.

5. Qur’anic usage

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:128

أَتَبْنُونَ بِكُلِّ رِيعٍ آيَةً تَعْبَثُونَ

a-tabnūna bi-kulli rīʿin āyatan taʿbathūna

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:128

Translation:

“Do you build a landmark on every height, engaging in idle activity?”

The verse forms part of Hūd’s address to the people of ʿĀd.

Bi-kulli rīʿin specifies the location of construction: on every height or at each prominent passage.

Āyatan names what they were building; here it can mean a distinct, conspicuous sign or structure.

Taʿbathūna characterizes their conduct as idle activity lacking a proper purpose.

The criticism comes from taʿbathūna, not from rīʿ itself. Rīʿ names a location; ostentation, wastefulness, tyranny, and idleness are not its lexical meaning.

Height or road?

Two principal explanations are offered for rīʿ here:

An elevated, conspicuous place, or a road or passage, sometimes specifically in mountain terrain.

Both have lexical support. The scene allows either: people erect signs and structures on heights or along visible routes.

Given the established sense of raised ground and its connection with erecting a landmark, “on every height” is a concise, clear main translation.

The senses of road and pass can be retained in the explanation.

The following verses

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:129 concerns constructing maṣāniʿ and the prospect of lasting indefinitely. Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:130 condemns their tyrannical conduct in exercising force.

Together with Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:128, these verses portray a powerful society capable of construction but departing from mindfulness and justice in its use of power and resources.

The passage does not declare building or using elevated ground intrinsically forbidden. It addresses construction associated with idle activity, imagined permanence, and tyrannical conduct.

Nor do these verses alone establish that every landmark or tall building exemplifies the conduct of ʿĀd. Assessing an action requires its purpose, manner, and consequences.

That conclusion comes from the passage as a whole and should not be added to rīʿ’s lexical meaning.

6. Distinctions from related words

R Y ʿ (rīʿ) and J B L (jabal)

Jabal means a mountain in the Qur’an, naming a particular natural landform.

Spatial rīʿ also denotes a rise, pass, or passage. A rīʿ is not necessarily a large mountain, nor is every jabal called a rīʿ.

R Y ʿ (rīʿ) and R B W (rabwah)

Rabwah also denotes raised ground, as in Al-Baqarah 2:265.

The words overlap in referring to elevated land. Rīʿ also has recorded road and pass senses, but this distinction should not become an absolute rule governing every use of either root.

R Y ʿ (tarīʿu) and R W ʿ (rawʿ)

These roots resemble one another in writing but do not have identical semantic fields.

Tarīʿu in Ṭarafah’s line means returning, whereas rawʿ and rawʿah concern fear and alarm.

Both forms in one line clearly illustrate why similar roots must be distinguished.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

The principal proposed equivalent for rīʿin in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:128 is “height.”

A word explanation may read: “Elevated ground, a pass, or a passage among heights.” This proposal does not replace the existing verse translation or authored word-box explanations.

For other lexical uses, rayʿ expressing increase may be translated as “yield,” “produce,” or “increase.” Rāʿa yarīʿu in the return branch means “returned” or “came back.”

Rīʿ in the verse should not become “profit,” “income,” “ostentation,” or “a pointless building”: those belong either to another branch or to analysis of other words in the verse.

8. Conclusion

R Y ʿ has two important branches: height and elevation, associated with increase and abundance, and return, found in distinct verbal uses.

Only rīʿin occurs in the Qur’an, in Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:128, referring to an elevated place or visible passage.

The condemnation of ʿĀd does not arise from a negative lexical meaning of the root; it emerges from the full sentence and its relation to the people’s idle and tyrannical behavior.

“Height” is therefore an appropriate equivalent here, while an explanation may preserve the possibilities of a pass or road. Yield and return belong to the root’s broader history and usage, but should not be transferred to this Qur’anic occurrence without contextual support.
