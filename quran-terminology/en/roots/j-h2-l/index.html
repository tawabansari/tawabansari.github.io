Introduction to the Root

The root J H L in the Arabic lexical family is one of the roots whose meaning cannot be reduced to one simple English word such as “ignorance.”

In Arabic sources, especially in the concise statement of Ibn Faris, two principal directions are preserved for this root:

خلاف العلم

the opposite of knowledge;

والخفة وخلاف الطمأنينة

and lightness, agitation, or the opposite of settled calm.

This is a very important point. Jahl can mean not knowing, but it can also describe a condition in which a human being does not act from balance, forbearance, settled judgment, and measured response.

Therefore, in the Qur’an, jahl is sometimes set against ilm, and sometimes it appears in the field of conduct, emotional reaction, haste, roughness, tribal heat, and lack of calm.

Qur’anic Use of the Root

This root appears in the Qur’an in several forms, including:

يَجْهَلُونَ

تَجْهَلُونَ

جَهِلَ

جَاهِل

الْجَاهِلُونَ

الْجَاهِلِينَ

بِجَهَالَةٍ

جَهُولًا

جَاهِلِيَّة

These forms occur in different contexts: lack of knowledge, action without verification, sin committed in a state of jahl, rash conduct, social interaction with the jahlun, and the larger pattern called jahiliyyah.

Thus the root must be read in each context. One uniform translation is not enough for all cases.

Short Meaning of the Root

Short meaning of the root:

Ignorance and lack of knowledge; and also lightness, rashness, and unmeasured behavior arising from lack of knowledge, forbearance, or settled calm.

Or more briefly:

Ignorance, jahl, rashness, and unmeasured conduct.

Two Foundational Axes: Jahl Opposite Knowledge, and Jahl Opposite Forbearance and Settled Calm

To understand this root correctly, these two axes must be kept together:

First axis: cognitive jahl

Not knowing, being unaware, not perceiving a reality, or having incomplete knowledge.

Second axis: behavioral jahl

Lightness, instability, haste, acting without measure, and conduct that does not come from forbearance and calm.

If only the first axis is kept, many Qur’anic uses become weak. If only the second axis is kept, clear cases of actual not-knowing are lost.

Material Image: Boiling Over and Leaving Calm

In the behavioral branch, the root may be pictured as a human state that leaves inner weight, restraint, and calm. A person becomes light, reactive, stirred, and unmeasured. He may have information, but his action is not governed by knowledge, forbearance, and balance.

This is why jahl can appear beside sin, anger, tribal heat, social harshness, or mocking a serious matter.

In this picture, jahl is not merely darkness in the mind; it can also be a disturbance in conduct.

Al-Baqarah 2:273; Jahl as Real Not-Knowing

لِلْفُقَرَاءِ الَّذِينَ أُحْصِرُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ لَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ ضَرْبًا فِي الْأَرْضِ يَحْسَبُهُمُ الْجَاهِلُ أَغْنِيَاءَ مِنَ التَّعَفُّفِ تَعْرِفُهُم بِسِيمَاهُمْ لَا يَسْأَلُونَ النَّاسَ إِلْحَافًا

For the poor who have been confined in the way of The God and cannot move freely through the land; the unaware person supposes them rich because of their restraint. You know them by their mark; they do not ask people insistently.

Al-Baqarah, 273

In this verse, الْجَاهِلُ means someone who truly does not know the state of the poor. Their modesty and dignity keep them from asking insistently, so the unaware person supposes they are rich.

This is one of the clearest Qur’anic examples of cognitive jahl: actual lack of knowledge about the reality of another person’s condition.

Al-Hujurat 49:6; Jahl and Action Without Verification

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِن جَاءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌ بِنَبَإٍ فَتَبَيَّنُوا أَن تُصِيبُوا قَوْمًا بِجَهَالَةٍ فَتُصْبِحُوا عَلَىٰ مَا فَعَلْتُمْ نَادِمِينَ

O you who have faith, if a fasiq comes to you with a report, then verify, lest you harm a people in a state of jahl and then become regretful over what you have done.

Al-Hujurat, 6

Here, بِجَهَالَةٍ is connected to action before verification.

The verse does not say only that the doer had no information at all. It warns against acting on a report before tabayyun. Thus jahl here is not mere absence of data; it is a condition in which action moves ahead of verified knowledge.

The result is:

فَتُصْبِحُوا عَلَىٰ مَا فَعَلْتُمْ نَادِمِينَ

that you may become regretful over what you have done.

So in this context, jahl is a state in which a person may receive a report, but does not place it under verification, and action is produced before knowledge becomes firm.

“Bijahalah”; Structural Note

The expression بِجَهَالَةٍ is not exactly the same as saying:

because of complete ignorance

The letter باء can indicate circumstance, accompaniment, or manner.

Thus in several verses, بجهالة can mean:

in a state of jahl

with jahl

out of jahl

This jahl may be lack of knowledge, but it may also be lack of measure, lack of restraint, weak judgment, or following desire while the truth has not been properly held in view.

An-Nisa 4:17; Doing Evil “Bijahalah”

إِنَّمَا التَّوْبَةُ عَلَى اللَّهِ لِلَّذِينَ يَعْمَلُونَ السُّوءَ بِجَهَالَةٍ ثُمَّ يَتُوبُونَ مِن قَرِيبٍ فَأُولَٰئِكَ يَتُوبُ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ وَكَانَ اللَّهُ عَلِيمًا حَكِيمًا

Repentance accepted by The God is for those who do evil in a state of jahl, then repent soon after. For them The God turns back; and The God is knowing, wise.

An-Nisa, 17

This verse connects عمل السوء with بجهالة, and then connects both with repentance soon afterward.

The text does not say that they did not know at all that the act was evil. Rather, the act occurred in a state of jahl: desire, haste, weakness, lack of restraint, or a failure of right judgment overcame the person.

This is why repentance and return are meaningful. The person comes out of that state and returns toward correction.

Al-Anam 6:54; Jahl, Return, and Correction

وَإِذَا جَاءَكَ الَّذِينَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِآيَاتِنَا فَقُلْ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ كَتَبَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ الرَّحْمَةَ أَنَّهُ مَن عَمِلَ مِنكُمْ سُوءًا بِجَهَالَةٍ ثُمَّ تَابَ مِن بَعْدِهِ وَأَصْلَحَ فَأَنَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ

And when those who have faith in Our signs come to you, say: Peace be upon you. Your Lord has written mercy upon Himself: whoever among you does evil in a state of jahl, then repents after it and reforms, then He is forgiving, merciful.

Al-Anam, 54

Again the structure is:

عَمِلَ ... بِجَهَالَةٍ

ثُمَّ تَابَ

وَأَصْلَحَ

Here jahl is not a final state. It can be broken by repentance and reform.

Thus jahl is not necessarily permanent disbelief or fixed identity; sometimes it is a corrupt state of action from which a human being can return.

An-Nahl 16:119; The Same Structure in the Context of Repentance

ثُمَّ إِنَّ رَبَّكَ لِلَّذِينَ عَمِلُوا السُّوءَ بِجَهَالَةٍ ثُمَّ تَابُوا مِن بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ وَأَصْلَحُوا إِنَّ رَبَّكَ مِن بَعْدِهَا لَغَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ

Then indeed your Lord, for those who did evil in a state of jahl, then repented after that and reformed, indeed your Lord after that is forgiving, merciful.

An-Nahl, 119

This verse preserves the same pattern:

عمل سوء بجهالة

repentance after that

reform

forgiveness and mercy

The repetition of this structure in several places shows that بجهالة is an important Qur’anic expression. It is not merely a casual equivalent for “by mistake.”

Does “Bijahalah” Mean Every Sin?

Some exegetical statements say that every sin is, in some sense, jahl.

This can be a useful moral reflection: when a person disobeys The God, he has acted against the weight of knowledge, even if he knew the rule in words.

But in root study, the matter must be expressed carefully.

We should not turn this reflection into a lexical definition and say:

jahl = every sin

The safer formulation is:

Many sins occur in a state of jahl, because the human being does not act with the weight of true knowledge, restraint, and awareness of consequence.

This keeps the distinction between lexical meaning, Qur’anic context, and moral reflection intact.

Yusuf 12:89; Being Jahil Despite Knowing the Act Itself

قَالَ هَلْ عَلِمْتُم مَّا فَعَلْتُم بِيُوسُفَ وَأَخِيهِ إِذْ أَنتُمْ جَاهِلُونَ

He said: Do you know what you did to Yusuf and his brother when you were jahil?

Yusuf, 89

The brothers of Yusuf knew what they had done outwardly. They did not need someone to inform them that they had separated Yusuf from his father and wronged him.

Yet Yusuf says:

إِذْ أَنْتُمْ جَاهِلُونَ

This shows that jahl here cannot mean only lack of information.

They were jahil in judgment, in jealousy, in moral vision, in measuring consequences, and in recognition of the reality of what they were doing.

Thus jahl can be a state in which a person knows the surface of an act, but is blind, light, and unmeasured regarding its truth, weight, and outcome.

Yusuf 12:33; Knowledge Exists, Yet the Danger of Jahil Conduct Remains

قَالَ رَبِّ السِّجْنُ أَحَبُّ إِلَيَّ مِمَّا يَدْعُونَنِي إِلَيْهِ وَإِلَّا تَصْرِفْ عَنِّي كَيْدَهُنَّ أَصْبُ إِلَيْهِنَّ وَأَكُن مِّنَ الْجَاهِلِينَ

He said: My Lord, prison is dearer to me than what they call me toward. And if You do not turn their plot away from me, I may incline toward them and become among the jahilin.

Yusuf, 33

Yusuf knows the danger. He does not lack information. Yet he says that if the plot is not turned away from him, he may incline and become among the jahilin.

So جاهلين here cannot simply mean people who do not know.

It refers to entering a state of conduct where desire overcomes knowledge, restraint, and steadfastness.

This verse is very important because it shows that the opposite of jahl in some contexts is not merely information, but firmness of self, restraint, and refuge in The God.

Al-Baqarah 2:67; Jahl and Turning a Serious Command into Mockery

وَإِذْ قَالَ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تَذْبَحُوا بَقَرَةً قَالُوا أَتَتَّخِذُنَا هُزُوًا قَالَ أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ أَنْ أَكُونَ مِنَ الْجَاهِلِينَ

And when Musa said to his people: Indeed The God commands you to slaughter a cow. They said: Do you take us in mockery? He said: I seek refuge in The God from being among the jahilin.

Al-Baqarah, 67

In this context, Musa connects treating a serious command as jest with being among the jahilin.

Again, jahl is not merely shortage of data; the quality of behavior is also in view.

Hud 11:46; Jahl and Crossing the Boundary of Knowledge

قَالَ يَا نُوحُ إِنَّهُ لَيْسَ مِنْ أَهْلِكَ إِنَّهُ عَمَلٌ غَيْرُ صَالِحٍ فَلَا تَسْأَلْنِ مَا لَيْسَ لَكَ بِهِ عِلْمٌ إِنِّي أَعِظُكَ أَنْ تَكُونَ مِنَ الْجَاهِلِينَ

He said: O Nuh, he is not of your family; indeed it is an unrighteous act. So do not ask Me about that of which you have no knowledge. I admonish you lest you be among the jahilin.

Hud, 46

This verse shows one of the clearest connections between knowledge and jahl.

First it says:

مَا لَيْسَ لَكَ بِهِ عِلْمٌ

and then:

أَنْ تَكُونَ مِنَ الْجَاهِلِينَ

So one form of jahl is that a person does not accept the boundary of his own not-knowing, and asks or judges where he has no knowledge.

Al-Furqan 25:63; “Jahilun” in Social Conduct

وَعِبَادُ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الَّذِينَ يَمْشُونَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ هَوْنًا وَإِذَا خَاطَبَهُمُ الْجَاهِلُونَ قَالُوا سَلَامًا

And the servants of Ar-Rahman are those who walk upon the earth gently, and when the jahilun address them, they say words of peace.

Al-Furqan, 63

Here the jahilun are recognized in a communicative situation.

The issue is not merely that they have less information. Their manner of address causes the servants of Ar-Rahman not to enter the same pattern of reaction.

This usage fits the branch of lightness, harshness, and lack of forbearance.

Al-Araf 7:199; Turning Away from the Jahilin

خُذِ الْعَفْوَ وَأْمُرْ بِالْعُرْفِ وَأَعْرِضْ عَنِ الْجَاهِلِينَ

Take to pardon, command what is recognized, and turn away from the jahilin.

Al-Araf, 199

This verse also concerns social relation.

The command does not mean: turn away from every person who does not know something. In this context, الجاهلين are people whose pattern of behavior should not be entered into.

Thus “jahil” can be a behavioral description, not merely a report about how much information someone has.

Al-Qasas 28:55; Idle Speech and the Jahilin

وَإِذَا سَمِعُوا اللَّغْوَ أَعْرَضُوا عَنْهُ وَقَالُوا لَنَا أَعْمَالُنَا وَلَكُمْ أَعْمَالُكُمْ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ لَا نَبْتَغِي الْجَاهِلِينَ

And when they hear idle speech, they turn away from it and say: For us are our works and for you are your works. Peace be upon you. We do not seek the jahilin.

Al-Qasas, 55

In this verse:

idle speech → turning away → the jahilin

forms a behavioral network.

Here too “jahil” is more about the mode of interaction and speech than a label of intelligence or schooling.

Jahl Opposite Hilm; An Important Contrast, But Not the Only Contrast

In classical Arabic and in some semantic studies, hilm is counted among the most important behavioral opposites of jahl.

Hilm in this field is not limited to passive calm. It is close to self-restraint, dignity, endurance, and the ability to govern reaction.

By contrast, the behavioral branch of jahl may move toward:

  • unrestrained anger
  • partisanship
  • haste
  • reactivity
  • unmeasured conduct

But this should not lead to the conclusion:

The real opposite of jahl is only hilm and not ilm.

The Qur’an itself has clear cases of cognitive jahl. More precise is to say:

In the cognitive field, jahl stands opposite ilm; and in the behavioral field, it can stand opposite hilm and settled calm.

Jahl Does Not Mean Low Intelligence

The Qur’an does not use jahil as an intelligence test.

A person may be clever, capable, and aware of many things, and still act jahil in a specific matter.

For this reason, equivalents such as:

fool

for jahil often exceed the capacity of the word and become more insulting than the text.

The equivalent must be chosen according to context among:

ignorant, unaware, jahil, unmeasured, rash.

Jahul; Intensification of the Quality

إِنَّا عَرَضْنَا الْأَمَانَةَ عَلَى السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَالْجِبَالِ فَأَبَيْنَ أَن يَحْمِلْنَهَا وَأَشْفَقْنَ مِنْهَا وَحَمَلَهَا الْإِنْسَانُ إِنَّهُ كَانَ ظَلُومًا جَهُولًا

Indeed We offered the trust to the heavens and the earth and the mountains, but they refused to bear it and were fearful of it; and the human being bore it. Indeed he was very unjust and very jahil.

Al-Ahzab, 72

جَهُول is a form that indicates intensity or abundance of the quality.

But the morphological pattern alone does not determine exactly which side of the field of jahl is intensified.

The pairing:

ظَلُومًا جَهُولًا

places two independent qualities side by side.

One should not infer from the order that being jahul is the lexical cause of being zalum. The verse does not establish an explicit causal relation between the two.

“Jahiliyyah” in the Qur’an

The form جَاهِلِيَّة appears four times in the Qur’an, each time in a specific construction:

ظَنَّ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ

حُكْمَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ

تَبَرُّجَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ الْأُولَىٰ

حَمِيَّةَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ

These four constructions show that the Qur’an does not use “jahiliyyah” as an empty label; it links it with supposition, judgment, display, and heated partisanship.

But from these four uses we should not immediately construct a fourfold political, psychological, cultural, and cognitive system. Such classifications may be secondary reflection, not the meaning of the word itself.

Al Imran 3:154; “Zann Al-Jahiliyyah”

ثُمَّ أَنزَلَ عَلَيْكُم مِّن بَعْدِ الْغَمِّ أَمَنَةً نُّعَاسًا يَغْشَىٰ طَائِفَةً مِّنكُمْ وَطَائِفَةٌ قَدْ أَهَمَّتْهُمْ أَنفُسُهُمْ يَظُنُّونَ بِاللَّهِ غَيْرَ الْحَقِّ ظَنَّ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ يَقُولُونَ هَل لَّنَا مِنَ الْأَمْرِ مِن شَيْءٍ قُلْ إِنَّ الْأَمْرَ كُلَّهُ لِلَّهِ يُخْفُونَ فِي أَنفُسِهِم مَّا لَا يُبْدُونَ لَكَ يَقُولُونَ لَوْ كَانَ لَنَا مِنَ الْأَمْرِ شَيْءٌ مَّا قُتِلْنَا هَاهُنَا قُل لَّوْ كُنتُمْ فِي بُيُوتِكُمْ لَبَرَزَ الَّذِينَ كُتِبَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْقَتْلُ إِلَىٰ مَضَاجِعِهِمْ وَلِيَبْتَلِيَ اللَّهُ مَا فِي صُدُورِكُمْ وَلِيُمَحِّصَ مَا فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ بِذَاتِ الصُّدُورِ

Then after grief He sent down upon you security, drowsiness covering a group of you, while another group was concerned only with themselves, thinking about The God other than the truth, the supposition of jahiliyyah. They say: Is anything of the matter ours? Say: The matter belongs entirely to The God. They hide within themselves what they do not show you. They say: If anything of the matter had been ours, we would not have been killed here. Say: Even if you had been in your homes, those for whom killing had been written would have gone forth to their places of falling, so that The God might test what is in your breasts and purify what is in your hearts; and The God knows what is within the breasts.

Al Imran, 154

The verse itself explains ظن الجاهلية:

يَظُنُّونَ بِاللَّهِ غَيْرَ الْحَقِّ

So in this case, jahiliyyah is connected with an untrue supposition about The God.

More than that should not be placed inside the lexical definition.

Al-Maidah 5:50; “Hukm Al-Jahiliyyah”

أَفَحُكْمَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ يَبْغُونَ وَمَنْ أَحْسَنُ مِنَ اللَّهِ حُكْمًا لِّقَوْمٍ يُوقِنُونَ

Do they seek the judgment of jahiliyyah? And who is better than The God in judgment for a people who are certain?

Al-Maidah, 50

Here jahiliyyah is attached to:

حُكْم

So a mode of judgment can be described as jahiliyyah.

But the phrase alone does not define all the details of a legal or political order. Those details must be studied from the wider context of the verses on judgment.

Al-Ahzab 33:33; “Tabarruj Al-Jahiliyyah Al-Ula”

وَقَرْنَ فِي بُيُوتِكُنَّ وَلَا تَبَرَّجْنَ تَبَرُّجَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ الْأُولَىٰ وَأَقِمْنَ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتِينَ الزَّكَاةَ وَأَطِعْنَ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ الرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًا

And remain with settled dignity in your houses and do not display yourselves with the display of the first jahiliyyah; and establish salat, give zakat, and obey The God and His Messenger. The God only intends to remove impurity from you, people of the house, and to purify you completely.

Al-Ahzab, 33

The presence of:

الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ الْأُولَىٰ

shows that this usage has at least a prior reference or earlier pattern in view.

Therefore, it is not correct to erase every historical dimension of “jahiliyyah.”

At the same time, the verse speaks of tabarruj of jahiliyyah; that is, a behavior can be attributed to that pattern.

Al-Fath 48:26; “Hamiyyat Al-Jahiliyyah” and Sakinah

إِذْ جَعَلَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الْحَمِيَّةَ حَمِيَّةَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ فَأَنزَلَ اللَّهُ سَكِينَتَهُ عَلَىٰ رَسُولِهِ وَعَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَأَلْزَمَهُمْ كَلِمَةَ التَّقْوَىٰ وَكَانُوا أَحَقَّ بِهَا وَأَهْلَهَا وَكَانَ اللَّهُ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمًا

When those who covered truth placed in their hearts heated partisanship, the heated partisanship of jahiliyyah, then The God sent down His sakinah upon His Messenger and upon the believers and made them adhere to the word of taqwa; and they were more entitled to it and its people. And The God is knowing of every thing.

Al-Fath, 26

This verse is one of the most important witnesses for the behavioral branch of J H L.

On one side of the structure is:

الْحَمِيَّةَ حَمِيَّةَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ

and on the other side:

سَكِينَتَهُ

This contextual contrast harmonizes strikingly with Ibn Faris’s second branch of jahl:

الخفة وخلاف الطمأنينة

But we must remain precise: the verse does not say jahl = hamiyyah or sakinah = hilm. Hamiyyah is one particular form of jahiliyyah in this context, and sakinah is the opposite response in the structure of the verse.

Is “Jahiliyyah” Only a Historical Period?

The four Qur’anic uses make a simple “yes” or “no” difficult.

On one hand, the expression:

الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ الْأُولَىٰ

shows that at least in one case a former reference or period is in view.

On the other hand, the Qur’an attaches jahiliyyah to:

supposition, judgment, display, and heated partisanship.

Thus “jahiliyyah” in the Qur’an is not merely a historical name empty of qualities; patterns and qualities are also attributed to it.

But to conclude from this that “jahiliyyah in the Qur’an is a fully transhistorical term for every intellectual or political system in all times” is an additional interpretive step.

The Qur’anic text allows us to speak of jahili qualities; unlimited historical generalization requires a separate argument.

J H L Compared with AYN L M

AYN L M relates to knowing and recognition.

One of the two principal fields of J H L is exactly the opposite of knowledge.

But jahl is not only lack of data; it can also appear in the quality of action.

Therefore:

A human being may have knowledge and still act jahil.

Yusuf 12:33 and 12:89 are very important for this distinction.

J H L Compared with H L M

Hilm inclines toward forbearance, self-restraint, and governing reaction.

In the behavioral branch of jahl, lightness and lack of settled calm are prominent.

Thus the two can stand opposite each other in social conduct:

jahl → unrestrained reaction, rashness, harshness.

hilm → restraint, dignity, endurance, and measured response.

But this contrast must not replace the contrast jahl / ilm; both are real in the language.

J H L Compared with S F H

Safah inclines toward lightness of judgment, weightlessness of opinion, and immaturity in conduct.

Jahl can overlap with this field, especially in the branch of lightness and immature behavior.

But jahl also has the clear meaning of unawareness and opposite-of-knowledge.

So the two are not complete synonyms.

The finer boundary should be studied independently in the root S F H.

J H L Compared with Z L M

Zulm and jahl occur side by side in Al-Ahzab 72:

ظَلُومًا جَهُولًا

This pairing proves that the two descriptions are distinct.

But it does not prove:

Jahl is the lexical or dominant cause of zulm.

In some situations jahl may become a ground for zulm, but causality must be proven from that context.

In a root article, the boundary between words should be preserved:

jahl → unawareness or unmeasured conduct.

zulm → transgression and injustice in its own field.

Challenges of Understanding and Translation

First, jahl should not always be translated as “not knowing.”

Second, not every case should be translated as “rashness” either; Al-Baqarah 273 is a clear example of actual unawareness.

Third, بجهالة should not be restricted to “he did not know at all that the act was evil.”

Fourth, the exegetical statement “every disobedience is jahl” should not be turned into the dictionary definition of the root.

Fifth, the contrast jahl / hilm is important in behavior, but it does not replace jahl / ilm.

Sixth, jahil does not mean “fool.”

Seventh, jahul indicates intensity of the quality, but the morphological pattern alone does not determine the exact type of jahl.

Eighth, jahiliyyah should not be reduced only to a calendar name, but the four Qur’anic uses should not be turned into an unlimited transhistorical theory either.

Ninth, hamiyyat al-jahiliyyah opposite sakinah is an important witness for the branch of lightness and opposite-of-calm, but hamiyyah is not synonymous with jahl itself.

Tenth, the pairing zaluman jahula does not prove a causal relation between zulm and jahl.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For يَجْهَلُونَ / تَجْهَلُونَ:

In a cognitive context:

do not know / are unaware

In a behavioral context:

act jahil / act without measure

For جَاهِل:

ignorant / unaware

and in a behavioral context:

jahil / unmeasured / rash

For بِجَهَالَةٍ:

in a state of jahl

or:

out of jahl

and the contextual explanation should specify whether the jahl in that case is more unawareness or unmeasured conduct.

For جَاهِلِيَّة:

It is better to preserve the word:

jahiliyyah

and translate its construction:

supposition of jahiliyyah

judgment of jahiliyyah

display of jahiliyyah

heated partisanship of jahiliyyah

For جَهُول:

very jahil

or according to context:

deeply unaware and unmeasured.

Short Meaning of the Root for Forqan

Short meaning of the root:

Ignorance and unawareness; and also rashness and unmeasured behavior arising from lack of knowledge, forbearance, or settled calm.

Shorter version:

Unawareness, jahl, rashness, and unmeasured conduct.

Conclusion

The root J H L in Arabic and the Qur’an is not merely “lack of information.”

Ibn Faris preserves two principles for it:

خلاف العلم

and:

الخفة وخلاف الطمأنينة

The Qur’an also shows both fields.

In Al-Baqarah 273, al-jahil is truly someone who does not know the condition of the poor.

In Al-Hujurat 6, jahl is linked with action before verification.

In Yusuf 89, Yusuf’s brothers knew their act, but in the quality of judgment and conduct they were jahil.

In Yusuf 33, Yusuf has knowledge, yet knows that surrendering to pressure could place him among the jahilin.

In Al-Furqan 63, Al-Araf 199, and Al-Qasas 55, jahl appears in the manner of social interaction.

In the verses of bijahalah, jahl can accompany evil action and then be broken through repentance and reform; but the root by itself does not allow all sins to be defined as “jahl” without distinction.

In the four uses of jahiliyyah, the Qur’an speaks of:

zann al-jahiliyyah, hukm al-jahiliyyah, tabarruj al-jahiliyyah, and hamiyyat al-jahiliyyah.

These structures show that jahiliyyah is not only a historical name empty of content; features and patterns are attributed to it. Yet extending it into an unlimited transhistorical theory goes beyond the word itself.

And in Al-Fath 26, the placement of hamiyyat al-jahiliyyah opposite sakinah is one of the clearest Qur’anic witnesses for the branch of “lightness and opposite-of-settled-calm.”

The final definition of this root may be stated as follows:

J H L in the Arabic family has two principal fields: unawareness and the opposite of knowledge; and lightness, instability, and unmeasured conduct in contrast to forbearance and settled calm. The Qur’an preserves both fields. Thus a jahil is sometimes someone who truly does not know, and sometimes someone who, despite the possibility of knowing or even despite having knowledge, acts against right measure because of haste, desire, partisanship, or lack of self-restraint.
