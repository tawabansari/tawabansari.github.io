The Qur’anic Root Q D W

Following an example and aligning conduct with another’s way

Qur’anic forms

اقْتَدِهْ

iqtadih

Take as your example
مُقْتَدُونَ

muqtadūn

Those following an example

In early Arabic, Q D W concerns taking a person or thing as an example and aligning one’s conduct or course with a preceding model. Qudwah and qidwah denote a person or thing taken as an example.

This semantic field is well documented in classical Arabic sources, and the Qur’anic usages attest it in the period of revelation. Lexical registration alone does not establish a pre-Islamic date; this discussion does not rely on an independently dated pre-Islamic witness for emulation.

Taqaddat bihi dābbatuhu describes a mount keeping to the course of a road. Qidā rumḥin is a separate expression meaning “a spear’s measure,” not “an example.” Its derivational analysis is disputed, so it should not be used alone to define the Qur’anic sense of emulation.

The shared idea within the emulation branch is a model or path with which a person aligns their conduct and movement.

Forms examined: qadw, qudwah, qidwah, qidā, taqaddā, iqtadā, iqtidāʾ, and muqtadin.

Qur’anic forms: the imperative iqtadih and the plural active participle muqtadūn. Each occurs once: two occurrences in total.

1. Core meaning: taking an example and following behind

In iqtidāʾ, a person places an example before themselves and aligns their conduct or course with it.

A student walking behind a teacher has followed the teacher. When the student takes the teacher’s reasoning, conduct, or working method as a model, the student has emulated the teacher.

Iqtidāʾ therefore involves more than moving behind another person: it includes taking a model.

Qudwah is the example followed; iqtidāʾ is the act of taking that example; muqtadin is the person doing so.

Arabic lexicons also record a branch concerning pleasant scent or flavor in food. Its historical relationship to emulation is unclear, and neither Qur’anic occurrence belongs to it.

2. Taking the earlier Prophets’ guidance as an example

أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ هَدَى اللَّهُ ۖ فَبِهُدَاهُمُ اقْتَدِهْ ۗ قُلْ لَا أَسْأَلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًا ۖ إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا ذِكْرَىٰ لِلْعَالَمِينَ

Ulāʾika lladhīna hadā llāhu, fa-bi-hudāhumu qtadih. Qul lā asʾalukum ʿalayhi ajran, in huwa illā dhikrā li-l-ʿālamīn.

Those are the ones whom The God has guided; so take their guidance as your example. Say, “I ask you no reward for it. It is only a reminder for all peoples.”

Al-An'am 6:90

The preceding verses name Abraham, Noah, Isaac, Jacob, Moses, Jesus, and other Prophets, presenting them as guided people.

Here, Muhammad is commanded to take their guidance as his example.

The object of emulation is hudāhum: their guidance. The command concerns the guidance they received from The God, rather than an indiscriminate imitation of every personal habit and action.

Al-Anʿām 6:88 explicitly identifies this as the guidance of The God.

The Prophets lived in different periods, faced different social conditions, and addressed different communities. Al-Māʾidah 5:48 also speaks of differing laws and ways.

Following their shared guidance therefore does not imply that every detail of their laws and ways of life was identical.

The command also shows that Muhammad is himself addressed by divine guidance and directed to take the earlier Prophets’ guidance as his example. Their stories are more than accounts of past events; they contribute to the guidance of Muhammad and the Qur’an’s other audiences.

The form iqtadih

Iqtadih is a form VIII imperative. Its basic imperative form is iqtadi, with the final root consonant omitted.

In Ḥafṣ, the final hāʾ is hāʾ al-sakt and remains unvowelled both at a pause and in connected recitation.

Some recitations omit it in connected speech. Ibn ʿĀmir’s reading gives it kasrah; one grammatical explanation treats it as a pronoun referring to the act of emulation.

The central command remains the same: take the earlier Prophets’ guidance as your example.

3. Emulating ancestors: when inheritance displaces guidance

وَكَذَٰلِكَ مَا أَرْسَلْنَا مِنْ قَبْلِكَ فِي قَرْيَةٍ مِنْ نَذِيرٍ إِلَّا قَالَ مُتْرَفُوهَا إِنَّا وَجَدْنَا آبَاءَنَا عَلَىٰ أُمَّةٍ وَإِنَّا عَلَىٰ آثَارِهِمْ مُقْتَدُونَ

Wa-kadhālika mā arsalnā min qablika fī qaryatin min nadhīrin illā qāla mutrafūhā innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā ummatin wa-innā ʿalā āthārihim muqtadūn.

And likewise, before you, We sent no warner to any town without its affluent people saying, “We found our forefathers following a way, and we are following their traces.”

Az-Zukhruf 43:23

Muqtadūn is the masculine plural of a form VIII active participle. It describes people who take another’s way as their example.

Āthārihim, from A TH R, concerns what the ancestors left behind, including beliefs, traditions, and patterns of conduct.

The preceding verse describes the current opponents making a similar appeal to their ancestors; verse 43:23 then recalls comparable claims by affluent people in earlier communities:

بَلْ قَالُوا إِنَّا وَجَدْنَا آبَاءَنَا عَلَىٰ أُمَّةٍ وَإِنَّا عَلَىٰ آثَارِهِمْ مُهْتَدُونَ

Bal qālū innā wajadnā ābāʾanā ʿalā ummatin wa-innā ʿalā āthārihim muhtadūn.

Rather, they said, “We found our forefathers following a way, and we are guided by following their traces.”

Az-Zukhruf 43:22

The difference between muhtadūn and muqtadūn in the two verses is significant.

In Al-Zukhruf 43:22, the speakers claim to be guided. In Al-Zukhruf 43:23, affluent members of earlier communities describe emulating ancestral traces. The two verses expose a similar argument without describing exactly the same group.

Following a path is not, by itself, evidence of being guided. Someone may faithfully follow an inherited way that is itself mistaken.

The next verse makes the problem explicit:

قَالَ أَوَلَوْ جِئْتُكُمْ بِأَهْدَىٰ مِمَّا وَجَدْتُمْ عَلَيْهِ آبَاءَكُمْ ۖ قَالُوا إِنَّا بِمَا أُرْسِلْتُمْ بِهِ كَافِرُونَ

Qāla a-wa-law jiʾtukum bi-ahdā mimmā wajadtum ʿalayhi ābāʾakum, qālū innā bi-mā ursiltum bihi kāfirūn.

He said, “Even if I have brought you something better guiding than what you found your forefathers following?” They said, “We reject what you have been sent with.”

Az-Zukhruf 43:24

The problem was not simply following ancestors. These people refused to reconsider their inheritance even when confronted with better guidance.

Al-Baqarah 2:170, Al-Māʾidah 5:104, and Luqmān 31:21 criticize the same kind of conduct.

A person may inherit true beliefs and good conduct. The Qur’an does not demand abandoning them merely because they are old; yet a belief’s age is not proof of its truth.

4. Emulation derives its value from its object

The two Qur’anic usages of Q D W distinguish deliberate emulation of guidance from obstinate adherence to inherited practice.

In Al-Anʿām 6:90, Muhammad is commanded to follow the Prophets’ guidance. In Al-Zukhruf 43:23, affluent people invoke ancestral traces while resisting new guidance.

Both involve emulation, but the object and criterion differ.

The Qur’an also identifies good examples for people:

لَقَدْ كَانَ لَكُمْ فِي رَسُولِ اللَّهِ أُسْوَةٌ حَسَنَةٌ لِمَنْ كَانَ يَرْجُو اللَّهَ وَالْيَوْمَ الْآخِرَ وَذَكَرَ اللَّهَ كَثِيرًا

La-qad kāna lakum fī rasūli llāhi uswatun ḥasanatun li-man kāna yarjū llāha wa-l-yawma l-ākhira wa-dhakara llāha kathīrā.

Indeed, in the Messenger of The God you have a good example, for whoever hopes for The God and the Last Day and remembers The God often.

Al-Ahzab 33:21

Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:4 likewise presents Abraham and those with him as a good example to follow.

The Qur’an thus does not reject learning from others. People can learn from another’s experience, knowledge, conduct, and perseverance.

The danger is making a person or tradition the final criterion of truth, such that no evidence or guidance can correct one’s beliefs and conduct.

5. Comparison with related roots

Q D W (iqtidāʾ) and T B ʿ (ittibāʿ)

Ittibāʿ has a broader range: following a person, path, command, thought, or something ahead of oneself.

Iqtidāʾ places greater emphasis on taking an example and aligning conduct with an earlier model.

In Ṭā Hā 20:78, Pharaoh pursues Moses and his companions. T B ʿ describes this movement, but Pharaoh has not taken Moses as his example.

Iqtidāʾ and ittibāʿ overlap in following a path or guidance, but not every act of following is emulation.

Q D W (iqtidāʾ) and A S W (uswah)

Uswah and qudwah are close in meaning. Both can denote an example or model to follow.

Uswah and qudwah name the example; taʾassī and iqtidāʾ name the act of taking it as a model.

The Qur’an uses uswah in uswatun ḥasanah, where ḥasanah identifies the example as good. The value of iqtidāʾ likewise depends on its object and the verse’s context.

Q D W (iqtidāʾ) and H D Y (hidāyah)

Hidāyah concerns guiding, showing the way, and leading; iqtidāʾ concerns taking an example and proceeding according to it.

Al-Anʿām 6:90 places the two together: The God guided the Prophets, and Muhammad is commanded to take their guidance as his example.

Al-Zukhruf 43:22 and Al-Zukhruf 43:23 also distinguish a claim to guidance from the act of emulation.

Emulation can be a means of guidance, but not every instance brings a person to the right path.

Q D W (iqtidāʾ) and Ṭ W ʿ (iṭāʿah)

Obedience concerns accepting and carrying out a command; emulation concerns taking someone’s way, method, or conduct as a model.

Someone can carry out another’s command without treating that person as a model for life. Equally, someone can learn from another’s good conduct without receiving a command from them.

Emulation and obedience can accompany one another without having identical lexical meanings.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

Depending on context, iqtadā can be rendered “took as an example,” “emulated,” or “followed another’s way.”

In Al-Anʿām 6:90, the command to emulate can be rendered “so take their guidance as your example.”

In Al-Zukhruf 43:23, the statement containing muqtadūn can be rendered “and we are following their traces.”

Qudwah itself does not occur in the Qur’an. In lexical discussion, it can be translated as an example, model, or pattern followed.

Brief root meaning: Taking a person, path, or earlier model as an example and aligning one’s conduct and course with it.

Conclusion

Q D W concerns taking an example and following a model’s way.

The Qur’an uses it in two contexts: emulating the earlier Prophets’ guidance and following ancestral traces.

In the first, divine guidance is the criterion. In the second, inherited practice has acquired such authority that its followers reject even better guidance.

Emulation is neither a virtue nor a fault in itself. Its value depends on the soundness of the path taken as a model.

Guidance is the criterion for choosing an example; the example should not replace the criterion of guidance.
