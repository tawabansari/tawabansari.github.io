Spread and hanging coverings, winglike movement, and furnishings for comfort

The Arabic family R F R F concerns spreading, hanging, gentle movement or fluttering, and coverings extending from something or spread out for sitting and reclining.

Rafrafa describes moving the wings; rafrafah describes wing-fluttering and also a piece of covering or furnishing; rafraf names spread cloth, hanging borders, coverings, spreads, and in some uses cushions or supports.

Early Arabic also applies the word to hanging branches and foliage, projecting tent coverings, hanging armor sections, and other objects.

This range matters: the family should not be confined to a bird’s wing movement or one kind of cushion.

Similarity between a spread wing, hanging cloth, and a drooping branch does not establish that every use historically arose from one original image.

R F R F occurs once in the Qur’an, in Al-Raḥmān’s description of comfort and bounty in the gardens of the Hereafter:

مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ رَفْرَفٍ خُضْرٍ وَعَبْقَرِيٍّ حِسَانٍ

muttakiʾīna ʿalā rafrafin khuḍrin wa-ʿabqariyyin ḥisānin

Al-Raḥmān 55:76

“Reclining on green cushions and beautiful, exquisite carpets.”

Rafraf accompanies ʿabqarī and relates to the reclining expressed by the active participle muttakiʾīna. Its application therefore concerns furnishings, spreads, or supports for comfort.

Lexical and interpretive explanations differ over the precise referent, including cushions, bed coverings, carpets, spread cloth, and even verdant gardens.

These explanations must be assessed against the Qur’anic wording and Arabic usage.

Qur’anic form: رَفْرَفٍ, in Al-Raḥmān 55:76.

Qur’anic morphological analysis identifies this as a quadriliteral root.

Arabic also records رَفْرَفَ, يُرَفْرِفُ, رَفْرَفَة, رَفْرَف, رَفَارِف, رَفْرَاف, and مُرَفْرِف.

Related forms raffa, rafīf, and raff are associated with this family in lexical accounts, but the quadriliteral R F R F must be distinguished morphologically from doubled R F F.

1. Root structure and the relationship with R F F

R F R F: a repeated quadriliteral structure

R F R F contains four root consonants in a repeated pattern:

R — F — R — F

The initial rāʾ and fāʾ recur in the second half.

This structure is familiar in Arabic.

Zalzala and waswasa, for example, also have repeated quadriliteral structures.

Repetition in such forms can emphasize recurring movement or events.

Rafrafa al-ṭāʾiru likewise describes repeated movement of a bird’s wings.

Repeated consonants do not make every form of the family necessarily express repeated motion.

Qur’anic rafraf names a kind of spread or support; it is not itself a verb of wing-flapping.

Quadriliteral structure and each derivative’s meaning require separate examination.

Rafrafa and raffa

Early Arabic records raffa for wing movement, shining, trembling, and fresh vegetation.

R F F has two recorded semantic fields: sucking or sipping, and movement or brilliance.

In the field of movement, rafrafah concerns moving a bird’s wings.

This shows the semantic closeness recognized between raffa and rafrafa.

Some lexical arrangements give R F R F a separate entry and examine its quadriliteral uses independently.

Two methodological points must therefore be retained together.

First, doubled R F F and quadriliteral R F R F have linguistic and semantic connections.

Second, those connections do not prove that every rafraf use arose from one particular raff meaning without sufficient historical evidence.

Qur’anic counting and morphological analysis should retain the quadriliteral R F R F.

Raff and rafīf help illuminate the semantic range but should not be counted as Qur’anic occurrences of the quadriliteral root.

2. Rafrafa al-ṭāʾiru: moving a bird’s wings

One clear application concerns birds.

Wing movement is explained as follows:

الرَّفْرَفَةُ: تَحْرِيكُ الطَّائِرِ جَنَاحَيْهِ فِي الْهَوَاءِ وَهُوَ لَا يَبْرَحُ مَكَانَهُ

al-rafrafatu taḥrīku al-ṭāʾiri janāḥayhi fī al-hawāʾi wa-huwa lā yabraḥu makānahu

“Rafrafah: a bird moving its wings in the air without leaving its place.”

This describes a particular kind of wing movement.

The bird moves its wings but may remain in the same area or nearby.

Another explanation states:

رَفَّ الطَّائِرُ وَرَفْرَفَ: حَرَّكَ جَنَاحَيْهِ فِي الْهَوَاءِ

raffa al-ṭāʾiru wa-rafrafa ḥarraka janāḥayhi fī al-hawāʾi

“The bird raffa or rafrafa: it moved its wings in the air.”

Another account describes moving the wings around something in preparation for alighting on it.

Rafrafah can thus accompany flight, approach to a landing place, or remaining within an area of air.

It should not simply mean flight in general.

A bird may travel far with spread wings, perch on a branch, or repeatedly move its wings in the air.

Rafrafa highlights wing movement rather than every state of flight.

Does rafrafah always involve rising?

No.

Some lexical explanations connect wing movement with rising or hovering.

The verb alone does not determine the bird’s exact direction.

It may rise, descend, or remain briefly within one area while fluttering.

Rafrafa should therefore not always be translated “rose upward.”

Nor does this use make Qur’anic rafrafin khuḍrin necessarily green wings or green birds.

The verse in Al-Raḥmān concerns reclining on something, not a bird’s movement.

Translation must preserve this distinction.

3. Rafraf: spread cloth and delicate coverings

The lexical explanation of delicate coverings states:

وَالرَّفْرَفُ: الثَّوْبُ مِنَ الدِّيبَاجِ وَغَيْرِهِ إِذَا كَانَ رَقِيقًا حَسَنَ الصَّنْعَةِ

wa-al-rafrafu al-thawbu mina al-dībāji wa-ghayrihi idhā kāna raqīqan ḥasana al-ṣanʿati

“Rafraf: cloth of brocade or another material, when fine and well made.”

This definition matters for the Qur’anic use.

Rafraf here names neither a bird nor wing movement.

It concerns cloth or a covering distinguished by delicacy and workmanship.

Dībāj concerns silk fabric or fine woven cloth in Arabic.

The phrase mina al-dībāji wa-ghayrihi indicates that rafraf can be brocade or another material.

Not every rafraf should therefore be assumed to be silk.

Material is a separate characteristic requiring evidence in each use.

Thiyābun khuḍrun: green cloths

The textile use is described as follows:

وَالرَّفْرَفُ: ضَرْبٌ مِنَ الثِّيَابِ خُضْرٌ تُبْسَطُ

wa-al-rafrafu ḍarbun mina al-thiyābi khuḍrun tubsaṭu

“Rafraf: a kind of green cloth that is spread out.”

Its individual unit is introduced as follows:

الْوَاحِدَةُ رَفْرَفَةٌ

al-wāḥidatu rafrafatun

“One piece is a rafrafah.”

Three characteristics appear here.

First, the referent is cloth.

Second, the cloth is used for spreading.

Third, the example described is green.

This does not make rafraf etymologically mean green.

The Qur’an itself supplies khuḍrin as a separate adjective.

Greenness describes the verse’s rafraf, rather than constituting the root’s necessary meaning.

Well-known examples of such cloth may have been green at a particular time, but that would not make rafraf of another color impossible.

Is rafraf always a floor spread?

Some early uses describe cloth spread out for sitting or reclining.

Others name a hanging part of a covering or tent.

Rafraf must therefore not always be confined to floor coverings.

One cloth may lie across the ground or a bed; another may hang from a tent edge or an elevated covering.

Spreading, extension, or a free hanging portion may connect these uses.

Their precise historical relationship nevertheless requires independent evidence.

4. Borders of tents, coverings, and armor

Rafraf al-fusṭāṭ

Fusṭāṭ concerns a tent or pavilion.

Rafraf al-fusṭāṭ names a portion of tent cloth extending or hanging downward.

One early explanation describes cloth sewn to the lower part of a pavilion.

This clarifies the word’s sense of a hanging border.

The cloth extends from the tent’s main body and may move in the air.

Its shape can resemble a bird’s spread or hanging wing.

That resemblance does not establish a definite historical derivation.

It shows only that Arabic knew rafraf for hanging or extended sections of a covering.

Rafraf al-dirʿ

Rafraf al-dirʿ is also recorded.

Dirʿ here concerns armor.

Its rafraf can refer to a hanging or extended section.

One early use describes a covering of mail rings attached to a helmet, falling over the back or around the neck.

Another explanation concerns a section extending beyond an armor edge or hanging downward.

This resembles a tent’s rafraf in shape.

Both extend a covering beyond its main body.

Rafraf is not therefore always armor or military equipment.

Nor does this usage make the heavenly supports in Al-Raḥmān metallic or rigid.

That verse concerns comfort and reclining.

Al-rafraf: a curtain or covering

Arabic also uses rafraf for a curtain or covering separating two spaces.

This relates to spread or hanging cloth.

Curtains, floor spreads, and cushions nevertheless differ.

A curtain generally covers or separates spaces.

A floor spread provides a surface for sitting or resting.

Cushions and supports provide comfort when leaning.

All may be textile, but they have different functions.

Muttakiʾīna ʿalā therefore helps identify the intended use in rafrafin khuḍrin.

5. Rafraf al-aykah: hanging branches and foliage

Rafraf al-aykah is also recorded.

Aykah concerns dense trees or a thicket.

This use is explained as follows:

وَرَفْرَفُ الْأَيْكَةِ مَا تَهَدَّلَ مِنْ غُصُونِهَا

wa-rafrafu al-aykati mā tahaddala min ghuṣūnihā

“The thicket’s rafraf is the part of its branches that hangs down.”

Rafraf is thus not confined to cloth and manufactured furnishings.

Spread and hanging branches can also receive this description.

The branches’ shape and position matter here.

This does not make every tree or leaf lexically a rafraf.

Extension and hanging vegetation are prominent in this use.

A Hudhalī poetic witness

A poem attributed to Muʿaṭṭal al-Hudhalī says:

لَهُ أَيْكَةٌ لَا يَأْمَنُ النَّاسُ غَيْبَهَا
حَمَى رَفْرَفًا مِنْهَا سِبَاطًا وَخِرْوَعَا

lahu aykatun lā yaʾmanu al-nāsu ghaybahā / ḥamā rafrafan minhā sibāṭan wa-khirwaʿā

“He has a thicket whose hidden places people fear; he has made its rafraf, sibāṭ, and khirwaʿ plants his protected territory.”

The line describes a lion and its habitat.

Aykah denotes a thicket or stand of trees.

Ghaybahā concerns its hidden, unseen part.

Rafrafan concerns vegetation here.

Sibāṭan and khirwaʿan are also explained through plants and shrubs.

A botanical explanation of this witness states:

الرَّفْرَفُ شَجَرٌ مُسْتَرْسِلٌ يَنْبُتُ بِالْيَمَنِ

al-rafrafu shajarun mustarsilun yanbutu bi-al-Yamani

“Rafraf is a drooping tree or shrub that grows in Yemen.”

Rafraf here is not merely a generic leaf: it can name a plant or hanging vegetation.

Attribution and date of the poem

The line appears in lexical accounts and collections of Hudhalī poetry.

Its attribution differs.

Some accounts assign it to Muʿaṭṭal al-Hudhalī.

Others name Maʿqil ibn Khuwaylid al-Hudhalī.

Given the disputed attribution and lack of a clear date for this line, the poet’s name alone should not establish its period conclusively.

Its composition should therefore not be placed definitively before the Qur’an without independent evidence.

With that qualification, it remains an important witness to early rafraf for vegetation.

Does rafraf in Al-Raḥmān mean gardens or plants?

Some early interpretations explain it as verdant gardens.

Its use for branches and foliage makes that interpretation linguistically connected rather than arbitrary.

A possible meaning must nevertheless be distinguished from the meaning best suited to a particular context.

Al-Raḥmān says:

مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ رَفْرَفٍ خُضْرٍ

muttakiʾīna ʿalā rafrafin khuḍrin

Al-Raḥmān 55:76

“Reclining on green rafraf.”

Reclining, expressed by the active participle muttakiʾīna together with ʿalā, supplies a clear contextual clue for supports and furnishings.

Spread cloth, cushions, or bed coverings therefore fit the sentence more directly.

Verdant gardens remain an established reading, but require further explanation of reclining on gardens.

The botanical use alone should not impose garden as the verse’s meaning.

6. Rafraf: cushions, spreads, or gardens?

Early interpretations offer several explanations of rafrafin khuḍrin.

Recognizing their differences is necessary for independently assessing translation.

First reading: spreads and furnishings

One early reading associates rafraf with busuṭ and furush.

Busuṭ concerns spread coverings and floor furnishings.

Furush concerns bedding and furnishings laid out for rest.

Rafraf is thus a covering or furnishing on which a person reclines.

This fits muttakiʾīna ʿalā directly.

Second reading: cushions and supports

Another explanation uses marāfiq or makhādd.

Marāfiq here concerns reclining supports and cushions.

Makhādd is the plural of mikhaddah, cushion or pillow.

This reading highlights reclining itself.

Someone reclining may rest against a cushion or soft support.

“Green cushions” therefore has established lexical and interpretive support.

It does not make rafraf exclusively a cushion in all Arabic uses.

That is one recorded referent.

Third reading: bed borders and hanging coverings

Some explanations connect rafraf with extra cloth hanging from bedding.

This relates to tent and armor borders.

It may describe cloth spread across a bed with parts hanging down its sides.

The verse does not specify the precise shape of those borders.

Their dimensions, material, number of layers, or geometry cannot therefore be established from this explanation.

Fourth reading: verdant gardens

Another reading explains rafraf as riyāḍ al-jannah: gardens and meadows of the Garden.

This relates to the botanical usage and the adjective khuḍrin.

The sentence provides a more immediate clue for reclining furnishings, however.

It does not simply describe gardens or plants; it describes people reclining on rafraf.

Cushions, supports, and spreads therefore make more natural translations here.

The early interpretive disagreement should nevertheless be recorded.

The article should not imply that all lexicographers and interpreters always recognized only one fixed referent.

7. Muttakiʾīna ʿalā rafrafin khuḍrin: the Qur’anic construction

The root’s only Qur’anic occurrence reads:

مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ رَفْرَفٍ خُضْرٍ وَعَبْقَرِيٍّ حِسَانٍ

muttakiʾīna ʿalā rafrafin khuḍrin wa-ʿabqariyyin ḥisānin

Al-Raḥmān 55:76

“Reclining on green cushions and beautiful, exquisite carpets.”

Each word’s contribution must be distinguished to understand rafraf precisely.

Muttakiʾīna: the state of reclining

Muttakiʾīna belongs to W K ʾ and is the sound masculine plural active participle of Form VIII.

It is accusative here and describes people in a reclining state.

A grammatical analysis treats it as a circumstantial expression, a ḥāl.

Grammatical accounts discuss its exact connection to preceding expressions.

The basic state is clear: comfort and reclining in the gardens described.

This contextual clue matters.

When people recline on something, a support, cushion, bed, or furnishing is a natural contextual referent.

Rafraf should not itself mean the act of reclining.

Reclining is expressed by muttakiʾīna.

Rafraf names what they recline on.

ʿAlā rafrafin

ʿAlā is a preposition.

Rafrafin is an indefinite genitive noun.

It names the object on which reclining takes place.

It is therefore a noun, not rafrafa, the verb of wing-fluttering.

The morphological distinction is clear.

“While flapping their wings” or “on green birds” does not fit the sentence and context.

ʿAlā itself does not determine the furnishing’s material.

It can accompany sitting or reclining on many different things.

The type of rafraf follows from lexical usage and the verse’s other clues.

Khuḍrin: a plural adjective

Khuḍrin is the plural of akhḍar, green.

It belongs to KH Ḍ R, not R F R F.

It modifies rafrafin here.

This construction has a morphological implication.

Rafraf appears singular in form but can have collective meaning as a noun.

Grammatical accounts offer two classifications: a collective noun and a collective generic noun.

Both allow the word to denote a collection of spreads or pieces of rafraf.

This explains the plural adjective khuḍrin.

Rafraf is not an ordinary broken or sound plural in form, but its collective meaning explains the plural modifier.

Natural Persian and English can therefore use “green cushions” or “green spreads.”

Green should not become part of rafraf’s derivational meaning.

Greenness comes from khuḍrin.

Collective noun or collective generic noun?

Rafrafah is recorded for one piece and rafraf for the collection.

Some grammatical accounts call rafraf a collective noun.

Others classify it as a collective generic noun.

The analyses are close but not terminologically identical.

A collective noun can denote multiple people or things without being the ordinary plural of its singular.

A collective generic noun denotes a kind collectively, with a unit distinguished by a marker such as tāʾ.

Rafrafah for one piece fits the latter analysis.

For translation, both yield a similar point: a collection or kind of spreads and furnishings is intended.

Its singular-looking form does not necessarily mean exactly one cushion or cloth.

Rafārif: a recorded plural

Rafārif is also recorded as a plural of rafraf.

It does not appear in the Qur’anic text used here, although some reading reports mention it.

Rafārif is transmitted in some reports of the verse’s reading.

The Ḥafṣ reading used for this study has rafrafin.

A reported reading should therefore not change the article’s verse text to rafārifa.

The variation illuminates morphological possibilities; the study’s base form remains rafrafin.

8. Wa-ʿabqariyyin ḥisān: distinguishing rafraf and ʿabqarī

The same verse continues:

وَعَبْقَرِيٍّ حِسَانٍ

wa-ʿabqariyyin ḥisānin

Al-Raḥmān 55:76

“And on beautiful, exquisite carpets.”

This accompanies rafrafin khuḍrin.

ʿAbqarī does not belong to R F R F.

It requires its own lexical examination.

ʿAbqarī: exquisite woven furnishings

Lexical and interpretive accounts use ʿabqarī for fine, well-made carpets and textiles.

Some explanations concern thick, patterned carpets.

Others apply it to outstanding, beautiful woven goods more generally.

Arabic also extends it to anything exceptionally fine, remarkable, or outstanding.

The modern meaning genius or highly intelligent person must not enter this verse.

Al-Raḥmān 55:76 concerns furnishings and comfort.

ʿAbqarī here therefore concerns exquisite textiles or carpets.

Ḥisān: the adjective of beauty

Ḥisān is the plural of ḥasan.

It belongs to Ḥ S N and means beautiful or fine.

It modifies ʿabqariyyin here.

Two distinct characteristics are expressed:

The rafraf are green.

The ʿabqarī furnishings are beautiful and exquisite.

Khuḍrin should not directly modify ʿabqarī, nor should ḥisān become part of rafraf’s own meaning.

All these objects belong to the verse’s overall bounty and beauty, but its wording must remain distinct.

Are rafraf and ʿabqarī the same thing?

Some lexical uses allow both words to describe spreads and carpets.

Their semantic fields therefore overlap.

The verse nevertheless joins them as separate terms with wa, “and.”

Translation and explanation should preserve the distinction.

Rafraf can, for example, be cushions or spread support coverings, while ʿabqarī is exquisite carpeting or woven furnishing.

This neither denies lexical overlap nor omits either term.

Rendering both simply as “beautiful carpets” loses part of the verse’s construction.

9. Rafraf in the gardens of Al-Raḥmān

The verse’s position within Al-Raḥmān matters.

The surah describes the gardens of the Hereafter and their blessings.

The first description includes:

مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ فُرُشٍ بَطَائِنُهَا مِنْ إِسْتَبْرَقٍ وَجَنَى الْجَنَّتَيْنِ دَانٍ

muttakiʾīna ʿalā furushin baṭāʾinuhā min istabraqin wa-janā al-jannatayni dānin

Al-Raḥmān 55:54

“Reclining on beds whose linings are thick brocade, with the fruit of the two gardens close at hand.”

Later, after two other gardens and their blessings are introduced, it says:

مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ رَفْرَفٍ خُضْرٍ وَعَبْقَرِيٍّ حِسَانٍ

muttakiʾīna ʿalā rafrafin khuḍrin wa-ʿabqariyyin ḥisānin

Al-Raḥmān 55:76

“Reclining on green cushions and beautiful, exquisite carpets.”

The expressions resemble one another in structure and subject.

Both use muttakiʾīna ʿalā.

Both concern furnishings for comfort in the described gardens.

Furush and rafraf

Furush belongs to F R SH and concerns bedding and things spread out.

Qur’anic rafraf concerns a kind of spread or support.

Their referents can overlap.

They nevertheless come from different roots.

Al-Raḥmān 55:54 describes the bedding’s linings.

Al-Raḥmān 55:76 describes green rafraf and beautiful ʿabqarī.

Baṭāʾinuhā min istabraq should not be transferred to the rafraf of verse 76 without evidence.

Verse 76 does not specify their exact material.

Do the descriptions represent different levels of bounty?

Interpretations differ over the relationship between the surah’s two pairs of gardens.

Some distinguish their rank or degree.

Rafraf alone does not establish furnishings inferior or superior to the beds in verse 54.

That assessment requires examining the surah’s whole structure and the related descriptions.

A root study should not construct a definite hierarchy of Paradise solely from different furniture names.

Lexically, both verses clearly concern reclining and enjoying furnishings for comfort.

10. A symbol of peace or an actual object?

Literary readings may treat rafraf as a symbol of comfort, peace, or beauty.

The direct meaning and its broader implication must be distinguished.

The verse describes reclining on green rafraf and beautiful ʿabqarī.

That image conveys comfort and bounty.

Rafraf itself does not lexically mean peace.

It names a kind of covering or spread.

Comfort emerges from reclining and the overall scene.

A direct translation should therefore not replace rafrafin khuḍrin with spiritual tranquility or inward purity.

Any such proposal should be examined separately as literary or interpretive reflection.

Nor does the verse establish the exact physical materials, cushion dimensions, number of beds, or manufacturing method.

The Qur’an does not give those details.

The clear furnishing-related meaning should be retained without adding unsupported particulars.

Do the heavenly rafraf move or fly?

Arabic rafrafa can describe wing movement.

In Al-Raḥmān 55:76, however, rafraf is a noun naming what people recline on.

No separate verb describes those furnishings flying or moving.

Similarity to rafrafah therefore does not establish that the cushions or beds fly through the air.

Nor does it turn them into birds or flying vehicles.

The verse’s structure and context do not establish such a claim.

11. R F R F and nearby roots and words

R F R F (rafraf) and R F F (raff, rafīf)

These families are close in sound and meaning.

Raff sometimes concerns movement, trembling, shining, or softness.

Rafīf also describes vegetation’s sheen and freshness.

Rafrafa in its bird-related use concerns moving wings.

Qur’anic morphological analysis assigns the noun rafraf to quadriliteral R F R F.

Raff and rafīf should not be counted as additional Qur’anic occurrences of this root.

Semantic proximity also does not make rafraf in Al-Raḥmān necessarily brilliance or motion.

Reclining and furnishings are the prominent clues there.

R F R F (rafraf) and F R SH (firāsh)

Study of F R SH

Firāsh names something spread out for sitting or sleeping.

Rafraf likewise has a spread or bed-covering use.

Their applications can overlap.

Early rafraf also covers tent borders, hanging coverings, and tree branches.

Its field is therefore broader than a single kind of bedding.

Al-Raḥmān 55:54 uses furush; Al-Raḥmān 55:76 uses rafraf.

The study must preserve this lexical distinction.

R F R F (rafraf) and W S D (wisādah)

Wisādah concerns a cushion or reclining support.

Interpretations explain rafraf in Al-Raḥmān through makhādd and marāfiq.

Those referents are close to cushions.

Rafraf is nevertheless not always a cushion lexically.

Cushions is a defensible contextual rendering, not the family’s exclusive, permanent meaning.

R F R F (rafraf) and B S Ṭ (basṭ)

Basṭ concerns spreading.

Bisāṭ names something spread out.

Early explanations also interpret rafraf through busuṭ.

Spreading is therefore clear in its textile and floor-covering use.

Basṭ and rafraf are not one root.

Many things can be spread, whereas Qur’anic rafraf names a furnishing for comfort.

R F R F (rafraf) and R F ʿ (rafʿ)

Study of R F ʿ

Rafaʿa means raising or lifting.

Rafrafa can involve wing movement or elevation in some uses.

Their root structures differ.

R F ʿ occurs repeatedly in the Qur’an; R F R F occurs once.

Occurrences of rafaʿa must not be assigned to R F R F.

Similar sound does not make rafrafin khuḍrin things rising or suspended in the sky.

The verse itself describes reclining on rafraf.

R F R F (rafraf) and KH Ḍ R (khuḍrah)

Study of KH Ḍ R

Khuḍrah concerns greenness.

In Al-Raḥmān 55:76, khuḍrin modifies rafrafin.

Other verses also describe plants, clothing, and other things as green.

Greenness is not itself rafraf’s meaning.

The furnishing and its color must be distinguished.

“Green cushion” likewise has two semantic components: cushion and green.

Their combination does not make cushion originally mean greenness.

R F R F (rafraf) and ʿ B Q R (ʿabqarī)

Study of ʿ B Q R

ʿAbqarī in Al-Raḥmān concerns fine carpets and textiles.

Rafraf likewise concerns a kind of spread or support.

They are close within the field of furnishings.

They belong to different word families.

Translation should preserve both names.

The modern use of ʿabqarī for an exceptionally intelligent person should not be transferred to the verse’s textiles.

12. Modern uses and their distinction from the Qur’anic meaning

Modern rafraf usage also concerns wings, flags, or fabric moving in the air.

A flag that rafrafa moves in waves under the action of air.

This relates to a bird’s rafrafah through motion and repetition.

Modern Arabic also uses rafraf for a vehicle’s fender or wing above a wheel.

Some technical uses describe projecting surfaces or structural sections.

These names can relate to covering, extension, and projection.

They must not be imposed on Qur’anic language.

Al-Raḥmān 55:76 supplies no indication of a car, flying device, flag, or mechanical instrument.

Modern and Qur’anic applications therefore require separate treatment.

13. Translation proposals for Forqan

Distinguish wing movement, spread coverings, hanging portions, and furnishings for comfort.

رَفْرَفَ — rafrafa: flapped or moved the wings; in some uses, light cloth or another object fluttered.

يُرَفْرِفُ — yurafrifu: flaps, flutters, or moves in waves in the air, according to its subject.

رَفْرَفَة — rafrafah: wing-fluttering; separately, one piece of rafraf or spread covering.

رَفْرَف — rafraf: spread or hanging cloth, a covering, floor furnishing, or support; Qur’anically, a cushion or spread for comfort.

رَفَارِف — rafārif: plural of rafraf; spreads, hanging coverings, or supports according to context.

رَفْرَاف — rafrāf: in early uses, a wing or a bird moving its wings; sometimes a male ostrich.

مُرَفْرِف — murafrif: flapping or fluttering; something moving like wings or waves.

رَفْرَفُ الْفُسْطَاطِ — rafraf al-fusṭāṭ: a tent’s border or hanging portion.

رَفْرَفُ الدِّرْعِ — rafraf al-dirʿ: a hanging or extended section of armor.

رَفْرَفُ الْأَيْكَةِ — rafraf al-aykah: hanging branches or vegetation of a thicket.

رَفْرَفَ عَلَى الْقَوْمِ — rafrafa ʿalā al-qawm: an early use for showing people compassion and tenderness; related to care and support, but not the direct meaning in Al-Raḥmān.

Al-Raḥmān 55:76

مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ رَفْرَفٍ خُضْرٍ وَعَبْقَرِيٍّ حِسَانٍ

muttakiʾīna ʿalā rafrafin khuḍrin wa-ʿabqariyyin ḥisānin

Al-Raḥmān 55:76

A proposed Forqan rendering:

“Reclining on green cushions and beautiful, exquisite carpets.”

This fits one established lexical and interpretive reading.

“Green cushions” is close to makhādd and marāfiq.

“Beautiful, exquisite carpets” conveys ʿabqariyyin ḥisān in context.

The independent root explanation should clarify that rafraf is not exclusively cushions: it also covers spreads, delicate coverings, hanging borders, and other referents.

For a broader rendering specifying the referent less narrowly, one could say:

“Reclining on green spreads and beautiful, exquisite carpets.”

Or:

“Reclining on green supports and beautiful, exquisite woven furnishings.”

These alternatives do not make green cushions incorrect; they leave the referent broader.

Green cushions is defensible in context and early usage.

Brief root meaning

R F R F: spreading and hanging coverings, and winglike movement; nominally, an extended and sometimes hanging covering or spread.

Brief meaning in the verse

رَفْرَفٍ — rafrafin: cushions, reclining supports, or spreads for comfort, described separately as green in the verse.

The verse’s word box should not merge green into rafraf’s independent meaning: that adjective belongs to KH Ḍ R.

14. Conclusion

Established R F R F uses concern winglike movement, spreading, hanging, and coverings for comfort or concealment.

Verbally, rafrafa al-ṭāʾiru concerns moving a bird’s wings.

Nominal rafraf may name delicate cloth, spreads, cushions, supports, tent borders, hanging armor, or drooping thicket branches.

These uses share aspects of shape and position, but should not all be assigned one definite original image without sufficient historical evidence.

R F R F is a repeated quadriliteral Arabic structure.

Lexical accounts connect it semantically with R F F, but Qur’anic counting and morphological analysis must distinguish them.

Early poetry records rafraf for hanging vegetation.

The line attributed to Muʿaṭṭal al-Hudhalī concerns a lion inhabiting a thicket and controlling its hanging vegetation.

The attribution varies, and the date of composition is unclear.

It is valuable linguistic evidence, but not an unqualified proof of when the word originated before the Qur’an.

The root occurs once in the Qur’an, in Al-Raḥmān 55:76.

People recline on rafrafin khuḍrin and ʿabqariyyin ḥisān.

Early explanations include cushions, supports, spreads, bed coverings, and verdant gardens.

The precise referent has therefore not had only one uncontested interpretation.

The structure and context favor furnishings and supports, because muttakiʾīna ʿalā describes reclining on something.

Rafraf also carries collective meaning and takes the plural adjective khuḍrin.

Green is a separate characteristic, not the root’s independent meaning.

ʿAbqarī is another term in the sentence and concerns fine carpets and textiles here.

The two should not collapse into one undifferentiated general word.

Al-Raḥmān 55:76 also connects conceptually with Al-Raḥmān 55:54: both describe reclining on furnishings in the gardens of the Hereafter.

This similarity does not transfer every material, dimension, or relative value from one verse to the other.

Nor does wing movement in rafrafa establish moving or flying heavenly rafraf.

The verse uses a noun for what people recline on.

Rafraf should likewise not become the abstract idea of peace or happiness.

Comfort emerges from the scene as a whole; the word itself concerns a furnishing or covering used for reclining.

Final definition: R F R F concerns winglike movement, spreading, and hanging coverings or extended portions. Rafrafa expresses wing movement; nominal rafraf can denote cloth, coverings, spreads, cushions, or supports. The Qur’an uses this root only in Al-Raḥmān 55:76, where reclining and comfort point to a spread furnishing or support. Green cushions fits lexical evidence and context, but cushions are one recorded referent, not the family’s entire range. Green color, beauty, material, and exact construction do not follow from the root alone; accompanying words and context must establish them.
