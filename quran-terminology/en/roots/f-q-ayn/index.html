1. Introduction to the Root

The root “F Q ʿ” in early Arabic has several different semantic branches, and not all of its derivatives can be confidently returned to one single material meaning. Early root lexicons also treat this family as a collection of varied usages, not as a root whose every derivative is built from one clear and unified rule.

The most important semantic branches of this root are:

  • a color becoming pure, intense, and visible, especially yellow;
  • a type of soft white desert truffle;
  • the rising of bubbles and foam on the surface of water or drink;
  • splitting or popping something with a sound;
  • making the finger joints crack;
  • a severe event or affliction;
  • the worsening of living conditions and reaching severe poverty;
  • some limited usages concerning the sudden release of air and its sound.

The only Qur'anic derivative of this root is “فَاقِع”, and in the Qur'an it belongs to the branch of intensity, purity, and visible clarity of color.

The condensed Qur'anic meaning of the root is this:

“F Q ʿ” in Qur'anic usage indicates a color that has become pure, full, visible, and visually prominent.

2. The Main Branches of Meaning

2.1. Intensity and Purity of Color

One of the most important usages of this root in Arabic is the description of a color whose intensity and purity are visible:

أَصْفَرُ فَاقِعٌ
pure and full yellow

“Fāqiʿ” was especially used with the color yellow and indicated a strong, clear, and visible yellow. However, in some early usages it also appeared with other colors, such as red and white, and in those cases it emphasized the purity or prominence of the color.

So the color-related meaning of this root is not simply “yellow.” The name of yellow comes from the root “Ṣ F R”; “Fāqiʿ” expresses the degree and quality of that color.

2.2. Desert Truffle

“Faqʿ” or “Fiqʿ” was the name of a kind of desert truffle from the family of “kamʾah.” This truffle was white, soft, and regarded as less valuable than some other types. It came out from beneath the ground, and its whiteness became visible on the surface of the soil.

From this usage, Arabic formed expressions in which a lowly and unprotected person was compared to this truffle, because it lay on the surface of the ground and the feet of animals could easily uproot it or trample it.

This branch does not occur in the Qur'an.

2.3. Bubbles and Foam

“Faqāqīʿ” is the plural of “fuqqāʿah” and means bubbles that appear on the surface of water or drink. A bubble rises from the surface of liquid, becomes round and prominent, and then bursts.

“Fuqqāʿ” was also the name of a foamy drink whose bubbles and foam rose to the surface. In this branch, the main material element is the rising and becoming prominent of something on a surface.

2.4. Splitting and Making a Sound

“Faqqaʿa aṣābiʿahu” means to make the finger joints crack. Pressure on the joint produces a sudden sound.

“Tafqīʿ” was also used for pressing and bursting a leaf or petal that makes a sound when it splits.

In these usages, three semantic elements appear:

  • pressure;
  • splitting or bursting;
  • a sudden sound.

Some later usages of this root concerning bursting, the splitting of a wound, or the explosion of something also stand in the continuation of this branch.

2.5. Severe Event or Affliction

“Fāqiʿah” was used for a severe event, calamity, or affliction, and its plural is “fawāqiʿ”:

فَوَاقِعُ الدَّهْر
the severe events and afflictions of time

In this branch, “fāqiʿah” is an event that descends on a person with intensity and changes his condition. The exact connection of this meaning with the branch of color or truffle is not clear, although the notion of intensity is prominent in it.

2.6. Distress and Severe Poverty

“Afqaʿa” in some early usages means to fall into poverty and a bad condition. “Faqīr mufqiʿ” is someone who has reached the extreme of poverty and misery.

This usage also expresses intensity of condition: not merely poverty, but visible and severe poverty.

3. Were All the Meanings Built from One Image?

In some branches of this root, one can observe a shared image of rising, becoming prominent, or suddenly splitting:

  • the truffle emerges from the soil and appears on the surface of the ground;
  • the bubble rises on the surface of water;
  • the petal splits under pressure;
  • the finger joint produces a sudden sound;
  • the fāqiʿ color appears prominently before the eye.

This visual similarity may explain part of the semantic expansion of the root; but the lexical evidence is not enough to prove that all these meanings necessarily arose from one single origin. For that reason, it is more precise to treat “F Q ʿ” as a family with several historical branches, among some of which semantic resemblance exists.

In the Qur'anic branch, the main element is not splitting or bursting, but purity, intensity, and prominence of color.

5. Historical and Classical Usage

In descriptive Arabic, to show the intensity of each color, a fitting adjective was usually used with it. For example, black could be described with an adjective meaning very dark, white with an adjective meaning very clear and bright, and red with an adjective meaning full and deep red.

“Fāqiʿ” was one of the known adjectives for the intensity of yellow:

أَصْفَرُ فَاقِعٌ

This combination did not merely say that something was yellow; it said that its yellowness was visible, pure, and full.

In some texts, “Fāqiʿ” is also used of other colors. Therefore, its broader meaning can be understood as “a pure and prominent color”; but its established pairing with “aṣfar” shows that its famous and clear usage was the intensity of yellow.

6. Qur'anic Usage

The root “F Q ʿ” appears only once in the Qur'an:

قَالُوا ادْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ يُبَيِّنْ لَنَا مَا لَوْنُهَا قَالَ إِنَّهُ يَقُولُ إِنَّهَا بَقَرَةٌ صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَوْنُهَا تَسُرُّ النَّاظِرِينَ

They said, “Call upon your Lord for us, that He make clear to us what its color is.” He said, “He says it is a yellow cow; its color is pure and striking, pleasing those who look.”

Al-Baqarah 2:69

The Qur'anic form of the root:

فَاقِعٌ

This word is an adjective on the pattern “fāʿil”, and in this verse it expresses the quality of the cow’s color.

7. Specialized Study of “Fāqiʿ”

7.1. “Fāqiʿ” Is Not the Name of a Color

In the expression:

صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَوْنُهَا

The name of the color comes from “ṣafrāʾ.” “Ṣafrāʾ” means yellow. “Fāqiʿ” does not introduce another color; rather, it explains the quality of the yellowness.

Therefore:

  • “Ṣafrāʾ” determines the type of color;
  • “Fāqiʿ” shows its intensity, purity, and visible presence;
  • “Lawnuhā” clarifies that this intensity belongs to its color.

The full meaning of the expression is:

a yellow cow whose yellowness is pure, full, and visible.

7.2. Intensity or Purity?

In explaining “Fāqiʿ”, two close components appear:

  • intensity of color;
  • purity and clarity of color.

In color usage, these two are not separate from one another. A color that has no interfering mixture or dullness appears clearer and stronger. For this reason, “Fāqiʿ” can be translated both as “intense yellow” and as “pure and clear yellow.”

However, “intense” in Persian or English may sometimes be heard as harsh or unpleasant. In this verse, the continuation of the sentence shows that what is meant is a color that is vivid and pleasing:

تَسُرُّ النَّاظِرِينَ
it pleases those who look.

So in this context, “full,” “pure,” “bright,” and “striking” together transfer the semantic range of the word more accurately.

7.3. “Fāqiʿ” and Brightness of Color

“Fāqiʿ” does not necessarily mean pale or close to white. In Qur'anic usage, the word indicates a yellowness that has reached its complete and visible degree.

A weak, faded, or pale color does not stand prominently before the eye; but a “fāqiʿ” color has a strong visual presence. Therefore, “deep and pure yellow” is closer to the meaning of the verse than “pale yellow.”

The word “bright” can also transfer part of the meaning, provided that brightness here is understood as the vividness and clarity of the color, not necessarily physical reflection of light.

7.4. Does “Fāqiʿ” Mean Beautiful?

“Fāqiʿ” in itself does not mean “beautiful” or “joy-giving.” The word expresses the quality of the color. Its pleasing effect is understood from the following clause:

تَسُرُّ النَّاظِرِينَ

So the semantic construction of the verse has two stages:

First, the color is introduced in its type and intensity:

صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَوْنُهَا

Then the effect of that color on the viewer is stated:

تَسُرُّ النَّاظِرِينَ

This separation shows that “fāqiʿ” means pure and striking color; and the joy of the viewers is the result of seeing that color, not the lexical meaning of “fāqiʿ” itself.

8. The Grammatical Structure of “Fāqiʿun Lawnuhā”

In the expression:

بَقَرَةٌ صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَوْنُهَا

“Ṣafrāʾ” directly describes “baqarah”, but “fāqiʿ” is connected with “lawn.”

In simple language, the sentence does not say:

the cow itself is fāqiʿ.

Rather, it says:

its color is fāqiʿ.

“Lawn” in Arabic is masculine; therefore “fāqiʿ” also comes in the masculine form, even though “baqarah” and “ṣafrāʾ” are feminine.

If “fāqiʿ” were directly describing the cow itself, one would expect its feminine form to be used; but the structure of the verse attributes the intensity to “lawnuhā”:

its color is pure and full.

Grammatically, “fāqiʿun lawnuhā” can be understood as a descriptive expression that returns to the cow through “lawnuhā.” In some grammatical analyses, “lawnuhā” is treated as dependent on the adjective “fāqiʿ”; in others, the two are analyzed as a descriptive clause. In both analyses, the semantic result is the same: “fāqiʿ” expresses the quality of the color.

9. The Relation of “Fāqiʿ” with “Lā Shiyata Fīhā”

Later in the same conversation, the cow is described as:

مُسَلَّمَةٌ لَا شِيَةَ فِيهَا
sound and without marking; there is no patch of another color in it.

“Fāqiʿun lawnuhā” and “lā shiyata fīhā” point to two different aspects:

  • “Fāqiʿun lawnuhā” describes the purity, intensity, and visible presence of the yellow color;
  • “Lā shiyata fīhā” states the absence of a patch, mark, or different color.

As a result, the complete image is a cow whose yellow color is both full and visible, and also not interrupted by a patch of another color.

10. Relation with Nearby Roots

F Q ʿ (Faqaʿa) and Ṣ F R (Ṣufrah)

“Ṣ F R” expresses the type of color: yellowness.

“F Q ʿ” in the Qur'anic expression specifies the degree and quality of that color: pure, full, and prominent.

Therefore:

صَفْرَاء
yellow

and:

صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَوْنُهَا
a yellow whose color is pure and striking.

F Q ʿ (Faqaʿa) and L W N (Lawn)

“L W N” expresses the general category of color. “F Q ʿ” describes the quality and degree of its prominence.

In the verse, all three levels stand together:

  • “Lawn”; the basic category of color;
  • “Ṣafrāʾ”; the type of color;
  • “Fāqiʿ”; its intensity and purity.

F Q ʿ (Faqaʿa) and N Ṣ ʿ (Naṣaʿa)

“Nāṣiʿ” is also used for a pure, clear, and bright color. Its semantic field overlaps with “Fāqiʿ.”

The main difference is in established usage: “Fāqiʿ” is especially known in combination with yellow, while “Nāṣiʿ” has a more general range in describing clarity and brightness of color.

F Q ʿ (Faqaʿa) and Q N ʾ (Qanaʾa)

“Qāniʾ” is used to describe intense and full red, just as “Fāqiʿ” is used to describe pure and intense yellow.

These adjectives do not merely repeat the name of the color; they show its degree of saturation and visual force.

F Q ʿ (Faqaʿa) and F Q ʾ (Faqaʾa)

“F Q ʾ” is a separate root that indicates splitting, piercing, or bringing out the content of something, and its well-known usage concerns the eye.

Because of phonetic closeness and the presence of the meaning of splitting in some usages of “F Q ʿ”, overlap is sometimes seen between derivatives of these two roots. But “Fāqiʿ” in the verse of Al-Baqarah is undoubtedly from the root “F Q ʿ” and relates to the intensity and purity of color.

11. Translation Suggestions

For the expression:

صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَوْنُهَا

The following translations are linguistically suitable:

yellow, and its color is pure and striking.

the yellowness of its color is full and bright.

a yellow cow with a pure and striking color.

a cow with a clear and deep yellowness.

The translation “bright yellow” may transfer part of the visual force, but if “bright” is understood as pale, it weakens the intensity present in “Fāqiʿ.”

The translation “deep yellow” transfers the intensity, but by itself does not show the component of clarity and purity of color.

The combination “pure and striking yellow” preserves both components more effectively.

12. Conclusion

The root “F Q ʿ” in early Arabic has several semantic branches: intensity and purity of color, desert truffle, bubbles, foam, bursting with a sound, severe event, and severe distress. Among some of these branches one can observe similarities such as rising, becoming prominent, splitting, and intensity; but they do not all necessarily arise from one single material image.

The only Qur'anic derivative of this root is “Fāqiʿ” in Al-Baqarah 2:69:

صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَوْنُهَا

In this expression:

  • “Ṣafrāʾ” determines the yellow color;
  • “Fāqiʿ” shows its purity, intensity, and prominence;
  • “Lawnuhā” specifies that this description relates to the cow’s color;
  • “Tasurru an-nāẓirīn” states the pleasing effect of that color on those who look.

Therefore, the condensed meaning of “Fāqiʿ” in the verse is:

a color that has reached a visible degree of purity, fullness, and striking presence.
