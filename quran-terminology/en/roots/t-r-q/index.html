Core Meaning

The root ت ر ق in the Qur'an is a limited and mostly anatomical root. Its axis is tarquwah, the collarbone between the lower neck and the shoulder, and its plural taraqi.

This root occurs in the Qur'an in only one form:

كَلَّا إِذَا بَلَغَتِ التَّرَاقِيَ

No; when it reaches the collarbones.

Al-Qiyamah 75:26

In this verse, the taraqi are part of the image of the final moment of death, when life has reached the upper border of the chest near the throat and separation has come close. The word taraqi itself does not mean death, soul, or spirit; that meaning is understood from the surrounding context.

Important Forms

تَرْقُوَة: the lexical singular; the bone between the hollow at the lower neck and the shoulder. This form does not occur in the Qur'an, but it is necessary for understanding التراقي.

تَرَاقِي: the plural of tarquwah; the collarbones, the two bones at the lower neck and upper chest. Its Qur'anic form occurs in Al-Qiyamah 75:26.

تَرَائِق: reported in some older usage instead of taraqi. It may help show the history of the word, but it does not occur in the Qur'an and should not be made to carry a separate Qur'anic meaning.

تَرَقَاهُ: a lexical verb meaning to strike or aim at someone's collarbone. This form also does not occur in the Qur'an and is not central to the verse in Al-Qiyamah.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of T R Q in the Qur'an is a specific body part, not a broad moral or social field. This root should not be confused with the later Arabic and Persian word “taraqqi,” meaning progress, advancement, or rising. That belongs to the root ر ق ي, not ت ر ق.

Taraqi should also not be merged with tara'ib, even though both sound close and both relate to the upper body. Taraqi belongs to ت ر ق and refers to the collarbones. Tara'ib belongs to ت ر ب and must be understood in the field of that root.

So this root should not be pulled into meanings such as rising, progress, advancement, or spiritual elevation. In this location, the Qur'an speaks through a bodily and tangible image.

Material Image

The material image of this root is the two narrow, prominent bones at the border of the lower neck, shoulders, and upper chest. This area lies near the throat, breath, voice, and the visible boundary between chest and neck.

In Al-Qiyamah, the body is in the final stage of ordinary life. Something reaches the taraqi; the text does not name it, but the context shows death and separation. Life appears to have reached the uppermost border of the chest near the throat, and departure from worldly life has drawn near.

This image should not be replaced with heavy literary expressions such as “the cage of the soul,” nor with a precise medical theory about the soul leaving the body. The Arabic text names a body part, and the context clarifies the situation of dying.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, tarquwah means the bone between the hollow at the lower neck and the shoulder, and its plural is taraqi. In older descriptions, this area could be mentioned in relation to the upper chest, throat, and the moment of dying.

This does not mean that Arabic gives a precise medical theory about the soul exiting the body. The language describes the visible scene: breathing has become difficult, life is leaving its final bodily borders, and death has approached the throat and upper chest.

For understanding the Qur'an, this is enough: taraqi means the collarbones, and in Al-Qiyamah 75:26 this body part stands inside the image of the final stage of separation from worldly life.

Reaching the Taraqi at Death

كَلَّا إِذَا بَلَغَتِ التَّرَاقِيَ

No; when it reaches the collarbones.

Al-Qiyamah 75:26

In this verse, the verb balaghat appears, but the subject is not stated explicitly. Many translations clarify the sentence by adding “the soul,” “life,” or “the self”: “when the soul reaches the collarbones.” This explanatory addition is understandable, but the Arabic text itself does not name the subject.

Reaching the taraqi means reaching the last bodily border in the scene of dying. The scene is neither a mere medical report nor a free metaphor. The body, breath, throat, upper chest, and recognition of separation gather into one compressed image.

The Omitted Subject and the Focus on the Situation

The omission of the subject in balaghat keeps the verse from entering a definition of “what” has reached the collarbones. The speech focuses on the situation itself: a moment when death has drawn near, the body has reached the border of separation, and ordinary ways of escape close.

In explanatory English one may say “the soul,” “life,” or “the self” reached the taraqi; but in the original wording this is not stated. For that reason, a closer translation can say, “when it reaches the collarbones,” then explain that the context points to life or the soul reaching its final stage.

This omission keeps the text compressed and grave. Instead of discussing the essence of the spirit, the verse shows the body at the threshold of separation.

Taraqi and Raq: Sound Nearness, Not One Root

كَلَّا إِذَا بَلَغَتِ التَّرَاقِيَ ۝ وَقِيلَ مَنْ رَاقٍ

No; when it reaches the collarbones; and it is said, “Who is a healer?”

Al-Qiyamah 75:26-27

Al-taraqi and raq are close in sound, but they are not from the same root. Taraqi belongs to ت ر ق. Raq belongs to ر ق ي and here points to a healer, charm-reciter, or someone called upon for treatment and rescue.

The pairing strengthens the scene: first the bodily border of death is shown; then people ask who can treat or save. But one should not extract from this sound-nearness a meaning such as “one who lifts the soul upward” for raq. That reading is not certain and does not fit careful root distinction.

Taraqi and Recognized Separation

وَظَنَّ أَنَّهُ الْفِرَاقُ

And he realized that it was separation.

Al-Qiyamah 75:28

After reaching the taraqi and the search for a healer, the verse speaks of recognizing separation. The sequence matters: something reaches the collarbones; it is said, “Who is a healer?”; then the person realizes that this is the separation.

So taraqi is not understood as an isolated word. Its place is inside the scene: the bodily border of death, the effort of those around to find treatment, awareness of the approaching firaq, and then the final movement toward the Lord.

Taraqi and Hulqum

فَلَوْلَا إِذَا بَلَغَتِ الْحُلْقُومَ

Then why, when it reaches the throat-passage...

Al-Waqi'ah 56:83

Hulqum and taraqi are close, but not identical. Hulqum is the throat-passage, the passage of breath and food. Taraqi are the bones on both sides of the lower neck and upper chest.

In Al-Waqi'ah, reaching the hulqum is paired with the inability of those present to bring life back:

وَأَنْتُمْ حِينَئِذٍ تَنْظُرُونَ

While you at that moment are looking on.

Al-Waqi'ah 56:84

In Al-Qiyamah, reaching the taraqi comes in the bodily and psychological sequence of separation: reaching the collarbones, asking for a healer, recognizing separation, leg joining with leg, and the final driving toward the Lord.

Similar Uses

Taraqi is close to hulqum, but hulqum is the throat-passage while taraqi are the bones beside the upper chest.

Taraqi is also not the same as hanajir. In وَبَلَغَتِ الْقُلُوبُ الْحَنَاجِرَ, the image is one of intense fear and pressure: the hearts have reached the throats, not that hearts have physically moved. But in Al-Qiyamah 75:26, the scene is the actual context of dying.

Taraqi differs from sadr. Sadr is the chest in a broader sense; taraqi is the bony upper border of the chest beside the lower neck.

Taraqi differs from tara'ib. Tara'ib in Al-Tariq belongs to another area of the front body and comes from the root ت ر ب, not ت ر ق.

Taraqi differs from unuq. Unuq is the neck; taraqi are the lower neck where it connects to the shoulders and upper chest.

Taraqi is near nahr, but nahr points more to the front lower neck and upper chest, while the taraqi are the two collarbones at the sides of that zone.

Grammar and Structure

In بَلَغَتِ التَّرَاقِيَ, the verb balaghat is a past feminine singular verb. Its subject is not mentioned in the sentence.

Semantically, the understood subject may be the nafs, ruh, life, or soul, but the text does not name it. If the implied subject is nafs or ruh, the feminine verb is natural; but because the subject is not stated, one should not assert with certainty that the verse means only one of those words.

Al-taraqi is the destination or object reached. Something has reached this area. The plural taraqi is natural even though human beings have two collarbones, because Arabic can use the plural for paired organs or a compound bodily region. In English, “the collarbones” is better than a singular “collarbone.”

Kalla at the beginning creates the force of denial, restraint, and cutting off an illusion. Its precise discussion belongs to its own word study.

Nearby Roots

ت ر ق differs from ح ل ق. H L Q relates to the throat and sometimes ring-like form; T R Q points to the bones on both sides of the lower neck and upper chest.

ت ر ق is not ح ن ج ر. Hanjara and hanajir are close to the throat-place of voice and breath; the taraqi are outer and bony.

ت ر ق differs from ص د ر. Sadr is the chest more generally; taraqi is the specific upper bony part of that region.

ت ر ق should not be mixed with ت ر ب. Taraqi belongs to T R Q, while tara'ib belongs to T R B. Sound similarity is not enough to merge roots.

ت ر ق also differs from ر ق ي. R Q Y can relate to rising, healing, or ruqyah; T R Q in the Qur'an relates to the collarbones.

ت ر ق should also remain distinct from ع ن ق and ن ح ر. Unuq names the neck; nahr points to the front lower neck and upper chest; taraqi names the side bones at this border.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For تَرْقُوَة: collarbone; the bone between the neck and shoulder; the upper-chest bone.

For تَرَاقِي: collarbones; the two bones at the lower neck; the collarbone area.

For بَلَغَتِ التَّرَاقِيَ: it reached the collarbones; it reached the collarbone area.

In explanatory translation one may say: “the soul reached the collarbones” or “life reached the upper border of the chest near the throat.” But in a closer translation, the omitted subject is better preserved: “when it reaches the collarbones.”

For كَلَّا إِذَا بَلَغَتِ التَّرَاقِيَ: No; when it reaches the collarbones. Or in a clearer explanatory version: No; when [the soul] reaches the collarbones.

A short card note can say: “Taraqi: the collarbones; the two bones between the lower neck and the shoulders. In this verse, a sign of the final stage of dying.”

Summary

The root ت ر ق is a limited and mostly anatomical root. Its axis is the collarbone, the bone between the lower neck and the shoulder, and its plural taraqi.

In the Qur'an, this root occurs only in Al-Qiyamah, in the image of the final moment of death:

كَلَّا إِذَا بَلَغَتِ التَّرَاقِيَ

No; when it reaches the collarbones.

Al-Qiyamah 75:26

The subject of balaghat is omitted in the text. This omission moves attention away from defining the essence of the soul and toward the situation of death itself: life reaching the upper border of the chest and separation drawing near.

Taraqi is close to hulqum and hanajir, but it is not identical with them. Hulqum is the throat-passage, hanajir are the throats, and taraqi are the bones on both sides of the upper chest. In Al-Waqi'ah, reaching the hulqum highlights the inability of those present to bring life back; in Al-Qiyamah, reaching the taraqi stands in a bodily and psychological sequence of separation.

The pairing of taraqi and raq is striking in sound, but the two are not from the same root and should not be used to extract a meaning of lifting the soul upward.

The specific core of T R Q is the collarbones and the bony area between the neck, shoulders, and upper chest; an area that the Qur'anic context presents as one of the signs of the final stage of dying.
