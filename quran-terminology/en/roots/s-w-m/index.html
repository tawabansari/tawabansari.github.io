1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root س و م moves through several related fields: sending or letting livestock go out to graze, placing someone continually under a condition, imposing hardship or humiliation, and marking or distinguishing something with a sign.

In the Qur'an, this root appears in uses that at first seem different:

  • Pharaoh's people continually subjected the Children of Israel to a harsh torment;
  • human beings send their livestock to graze in vegetation;
  • horses, stones, or forces are described with forms such as مُسَوَّمَة and مُسَوِّمِينَ;
  • and سِيمَا points to a sign by which a person is recognized.

The common center of these uses cannot be reduced only to driving from behind, nor only to branding. The wider meaning is placing a person or thing in a particular path, condition, or sign. Sometimes this appears as sending livestock out; sometimes as continual imposition of torment; and sometimes as marking for distinction.

The shortest expression of the field is therefore:

To send forth or continually place under something; and, in another branch, to mark and distinguish.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

سَامَ / يَسُومُ

To keep someone under a task, hardship, or condition, or to impose that condition upon them.

يَسُومُونَكُمْ

They were continually placing you under, exposing you to, or afflicting you with something. In the verses about Pharaoh, the imposed matter is سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ.

أَسَامَ / يُسِيمُ

To send livestock out to graze or let it go in pasture.

تُسِيمُونَ

You send your livestock to graze or pasture them.

سَوَّمَ

To mark, put a sign on, or make something specified and distinguished by a sign.

مُسَوَّمَة

Passive participle: marked, specified, or distinguished.

In some Arabic uses, this word can also describe livestock or horses let out to pasture. The precise meaning must therefore be known from context.

مُسَوِّمِين

Plural active participle: those who mark, those who are marked, or those who have a distinguishing sign. Reading and context affect the exact meaning.

سِيمَا / سِيمَاء

A sign, mark, or feature by which a person or thing is recognized.

There is lexical discussion about the derivational relation of سِيمَا to س و م and to the root و س م. Since وَسْم also means marking or branding, the historical connection should not be treated as certain without explanation. In the semantic network of the Qur'an, however, سِيمَا is clearly close to the field of marking and distinction.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The core of س و م can be summarized in two close branches:

First, sending forth or continually placing under something;

Second, marking and distinguishing.

In تُسِيمُونَ, livestock are sent to pasture. The doer places them in an environment where they graze.

In يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ, a group is continually placed under an evil and humiliating torment.

In مُسَوَّمَة, something has a sign or mark that separates it from others.

These uses can be brought together through the general idea of assigning a condition, direction, or mark to something. But this closeness should not be turned into a forced theory of calibration, compulsory identity-erasure, or programmed trajectory.

In some contexts the root points to continuation, but continuation does not always come from the root alone. The imperfect verb, historical context, and type of action may show repetition and duration.

5. The Material Image of the Root

One of the clearest material images of this root is livestock that the owner or shepherd sends toward pasture.

وَمِنْهُ شَجَرٌ فِيهِ تُسِيمُونَ

The animals are placed in an environment suitable for grazing. They spread in that land and eat from its vegetation.

Another image is an animal or object marked so it may be known. In tribal and pastoral life, marking mattered for recognizing ownership, type, destination, or status.

But not every sign should automatically be taken as a brand made with heated metal. A mark may be color, form, incision, visible sign, or any distinguishing feature.

Also, سَمُوم, meaning a scorching wind or penetrating heat, belongs to the root س م م and should not be added to س و م. Similar letters are not proof of a shared root.

Words related to poison and death also belong to another family and are not derivational evidence for explaining س و م.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, سَامَ الماشيةَ or أَسَامَهَا was connected with sending livestock to pasture.

سَامَهُ خَسْفًا meant to impose humiliation or make a person bear degradation. In this structure, something is not offered freely for the person to accept; the person is placed under pressure.

سَامَهُ العذابَ means to afflict him with torment or impose torment upon him.

In the field of buying and selling, سَامَ السِّلْعَةَ and المساومة became connected with proposing a price and negotiating over the value of a commodity. In that use, a price is put forward or assigned to the item. But this branch should not make every Qur'anic use of the root intrinsically about exhausting bargaining.

سَوَّمَ can mean to mark and distinguish. With horses and livestock, the mark could show ownership or a special feature.

سِيمَا is used for a sign by which a person is recognized. That sign may be natural, acquired, or produced by repeated conduct.

7. History of Understanding, Misuse, or Semantic Restriction

One common restriction is translating يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ only as "they tortured you." That gives the general meaning, but it weakens the relation of the verb to the continual imposition of a condition.

In the verses about the Children of Israel, Pharaoh's torment was not a single accidental event or isolated punishment. The verses speak about killing sons, keeping women alive, and placing a community under pressure. The imperfect verb and the historical context can therefore show duration and organized oppression.

However, the entire economic structure of Egypt, forced-labor workshops, or a complete program of identity erasure should not be placed inside the lexical meaning of يسومونكم unless it is established by the verses or separate historical evidence.

Another restriction is translating سِيمَا only as a physical mark on the face. Sima can be a visible sign, but it is not necessarily a brand, wound, or bodily alteration. In some verses the Qur'an speaks of recognizing people through their sima; that recognition may come from expression, behavior, or a visible trace.

Also, مُسَوَّمَة should not always be translated as targeted or calibrated. The basic meaning is marked, specified, or distinguished. Destination and assignment may be understood from the context of punishment, not from the word alone.

8. Methodological Note

Several branches must be kept separate when studying this root.

First, continual imposition of a condition, as in:

يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ

Second, sending livestock out to pasture, as in:

فِيهِ تُسِيمُونَ

Third, marking or being marked with a sign, as in:

الْخَيْلِ الْمُسَوَّمَةِ
حِجَارَةً مِنْ طِينٍ مُسَوَّمَةً
بِخَمْسَةِ آلَافٍ مِنَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ مُسَوِّمِينَ

Fourth, sima and the sign of recognition, as in:

سِيمَاهُمْ فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ

The meaning of one branch should not be imposed on every form. Sending livestock to pasture is not necessarily violent or coercive. Likewise, marking stones of punishment does not mean driving them like a herd.

In morphology, continuation should not be added to every form of the root. Continuation in يسومونكم is understood from the imperfect verb and the context of repeated oppression. But مُسَوَّمَة is a passive participle and expresses only the state of being marked or specified.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

A. Imposing torment on the Children of Israel

وَإِذْ نَجَّيْنَاكُمْ مِنْ آلِ فِرْعَوْنَ يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ يُذَبِّحُونَ أَبْنَاءَكُمْ وَيَسْتَحْيُونَ نِسَاءَكُمْ

And when We saved you from the people of Pharaoh; they were continually afflicting you with the worst torment, slaughtering your sons and keeping your women alive.

Al-Baqarah 2:49

يَسُومُونَكُمْ shows that torment was imposed upon them and that they were continually exposed to it.

The following clauses explain some forms of this torment:

  • killing the sons;
  • keeping the women alive;
  • placing the community in a condition of fear, weakness, and domination.

A suitable translation may be:

They were continually afflicting you with a harsh torment.

Or:

They were imposing the worst torment upon you.

The translation "they were driving you toward torment" highlights an image of the root, but may suggest that torment was only a physical destination.

B. Repetition of the structure in the story of Pharaoh

يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ

This phrase, or a close structure, appears in several places as a reminder of Pharaoh's oppression.

Its repetition shows that the issue was not an isolated incident. Pharaoh's rule turned torment into a continuing condition.

However, expressions such as "human herding" or "turning people into livestock" should be presented as social critique and metaphor, not as the direct meaning of the verb.

C. Sending livestock out to pasture

هُوَ الَّذِي أَنْزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً لَكُمْ مِنْهُ شَرَابٌ وَمِنْهُ شَجَرٌ فِيهِ تُسِيمُونَ

He is the One who sent down water from the sky; from it you have drink, and from it grows vegetation in which you pasture your livestock.

An-Nahl 16:10

In this verse, تُسِيمُونَ refers to pasturing or sending livestock out to graze.

The object is not stated in the wording, but livestock is understood from linguistic culture and the context of pasture.

This use shows that the root is not negative in itself. Sending livestock to pasture is part of the human benefit from water and vegetation.

D. Marked horses

زُيِّنَ لِلنَّاسِ حُبُّ الشَّهَوَاتِ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ وَالْبَنِينَ وَالْقَنَاطِيرِ الْمُقَنْطَرَةِ مِنَ الذَّهَبِ وَالْفِضَّةِ وَالْخَيْلِ الْمُسَوَّمَةِ

Love of desired things has been adorned for people: women, children, heaped-up stores of gold and silver, and marked horses.

Ali 'Imran 3:14

الخيل المسومة has several lexical possibilities:

  • marked and excellent horses;
  • adorned and prepared horses;
  • or horses let out to pasture.

The context of valuable possessions strengthens the meaning of distinguished and recognized horses, but branded and priced should not be treated as the only certain meaning.

A good base translation is:

excellent, marked horses.

E. Angels described as مُسَوِّمِين

يُمْدِدْكُمْ رَبُّكُمْ بِخَمْسَةِ آلَافٍ مِنَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ مُسَوِّمِينَ

Your Lord will support you with five thousand angels, marked.

Ali 'Imran 3:125

مُسَوِّمِين may refer to angels marked with a sign, or to angels who themselves mark. Reading and morphological analysis affect the exact meaning.

Translations such as "having a sign," "marked," or "marking" are more careful than technical phrases such as calibrated military forces.

The verse states divine support and the distinction of those forces. The exact outward details of the signs are not stated in the verse.

F. Stones described as مُسَوَّمَة

وَأَمْطَرْنَا عَلَيْهَا حِجَارَةً مِنْ سِجِّيلٍ مَنْضُودٍ
مُسَوَّمَةً عِنْدَ رَبِّكَ

And We rained upon it stones of layered sijjil, marked with your Lord.

Hud 11:82-83

مُسَوَّمَة describes the stones as specified and marked.

The mark may point to their being assigned for punishment or being specified with the Lord. But phrases such as fixed impact orbit, physical calibration, or flawless targeting add technical language that is not inside the word itself.

Base translation:

marked with your Lord.

G. Sima in faces

سِيمَاهُمْ فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ مِنْ أَثَرِ السُّجُودِ

Their sign is in their faces, from the effect of prostration.

Al-Fath 48:29

The verse says their sign is in their faces from the effect of prostration. This sign is not necessarily a physical callus on the forehead.

Wajh in the Qur'an can include the face, the visible condition, and the direction of personality. The effect of prostration may appear in humility, calm, behavioral bearing, or a visible sign.

Therefore translating سِيمَاهُمْ as a physical brand-mark is too restrictive.

A more precise translation:

Their sign is in their faces, from the effect of prostration.

H. Recognizing the needy by sima

تَعْرِفُهُمْ بِسِيمَاهُمْ

You recognize them by their sign.

Al-Baqarah 2:273

This verse is about needy people whom the ignorant suppose to be free of need because of their restraint.

Sima here is a sign by which the careful and aware person recognizes their real need. This sign may be in their state, face, clothing, or behavior.

Sima is not always a sign produced by branding or bodily injury.

I. Recognizing criminals by sima

يُعْرَفُ الْمُجْرِمُونَ بِسِيمَاهُمْ

The criminals will be recognized by their sign.

Ar-Rahman 55:41

In this verse, sima is a sign that reveals the condition of the criminals.

The verse does not explain exactly what bodily or psychological shape that sign has. What is certain is that their identity and condition become manifest.

10. Examination of Important Qur'anic Derivatives

يَسُومُونَ

This verb points to imposing and continually placing someone under something.

In the Pharaoh verses, the thing imposed is سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ. The negative meaning is therefore clear from the object and context.

The imperfect verb can show continuation and repetition, but the entire economic and political structure of Pharaoh should not be inserted into the translation of the verb.

تُسِيمُونَ

This form from Form IV points to sending livestock out to graze.

It relates to يسومونكم through the element of placing something in a path or condition, but one use is natural and beneficial while the other is oppressive and imposed.

مُسَوَّمَة

This passive participle indicates being marked, specified, or distinguished.

Its subjects in the Qur'an differ:

  • horses;
  • stones of punishment.

The translation should therefore fit the described object.

مُسَوِّمِين

This form appears about angels. Its base meaning is connected with having signs or marking.

Without further evidence, the exact type of mark or form of mission should not be specified.

سِيمَا

Sima is a sign through which the truth or condition of a person is recognized.

This sign may:

  • come from prostration and humility;
  • reveal the hidden need of a person;
  • or manifest the condition of criminals.

Sima is not merely facial appearance, and at the same time it is not necessarily a bodily brand.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

سَامَهُ الشَّيْءَ

In the structure:

يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ

The pronoun كُمْ is the first object and سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ is the thing imposed upon them.

The structure may be understood as:

They were imposing the worst torment upon you.

أَسَامَ in a contextually understood object

فِيهِ تُسِيمُونَ

In it you pasture.

The object is not stated, but livestock is understood from the context. This omission is natural in Arabic because the matter of grazing was clear to the audience.

مُسَوَّمَة as an adjective

الْخَيْلِ الْمُسَوَّمَةِ

المسومة is an adjective of الخيل and agrees with it.

In:

حِجَارَةً ... مُسَوَّمَةً

مسومة is an adjective of حجارة.

The same morphological form appears in two different contexts; the meaning must therefore be completed by the described noun and the wider context.

سِيمَاهُمْ فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ

سيماهم is the subject, and في وجوههم is the predicate or connected to the predicate. The structure states the place where the sign appears or is recognized.

مِنْ أَثَرِ السُّجُودِ clarifies the source or relation of this sign to prostration.

12. S W M Compared with Q W D

The root ق و د is connected with leading and drawing from the front. A leader goes ahead and another follows.

In the branch of grazing, س و م more strongly points to sending livestock to pasture and placing it in the path of grazing.

With caution one may say:

قاد: led from the front;

أسام: sent livestock out to pasture.

But one should not build an absolute rule that س و م always means driving from behind and ق و د always means pulling from the front. Actual language use can be broader.

13. S W M Compared with S W Q

The root س و ق points to driving and moving something toward a destination.

س و م in the grazing branch is close to sending livestock out, but in other branches it expands toward imposing a condition and marking.

S W Q stresses the act of moving toward a destination. S W M can also stress placing under a repeated condition or marking for distinction.

Therefore translating يسومونكم only as "they were driving you" may not fully show the branch of imposition.

14. S W M Compared with ʿ Dh B

The root ع ذ ب expresses the torment or punishment itself.

In:

يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ

س و م shows how the Children of Israel were placed under the condition of torment, while ع ذ ب identifies the suffering and punishment itself.

So the two are not synonyms:

سامهم العذاب: he imposed torment upon them;

العذاب: the torment or punishment itself.

15. S W M and Sima Compared with W S M

The root و س م is clearly connected with marking, branding, and putting a sign.

The word سِيمَا has often been explained, historically and lexically, through the field of وَسْم as well. Sound and structural changes in language can explain the relation between these forms.

Therefore, one should not claim with complete certainty that all of sima's meaning is extracted only from sending livestock.

In Qur'anic analysis it is better to say:

  • S W M in some derivatives relates to sending forth and imposing;
  • in derivatives such as musawwama and musawwimin, marking is prominent;
  • and sima belongs to the field of sign and recognition and has a close lexical relation with W S M.

This caution prevents building uncertain derivational chains.

16. Specialized Discussion and Structural Note: Pharaoh's Imposition and Sign in Qur'anic Language

The use of يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ in the story of the Children of Israel shows that Pharaoh's oppression was not merely a set of accidental acts of violence. The imperfect verb and the explanation after it show a community continually placed under pressure:

يُذَبِّحُونَ أَبْنَاءَكُمْ وَيَسْتَحْيُونَ نِسَاءَكُمْ

The killing of sons and keeping women alive targeted the demographic structure and future power of the community. From the context, then, one may conclude that Pharaoh's torment had a systematic and repeated character.

The link between this use and تُسِيمُونَ is linguistically worth noting. On one side, livestock are sent into pasture; on the other, human beings are continually placed into an imposed condition. The shared element is placing another in a path or condition determined by the doer.

But this connection should not become the claim that the Qur'an directly calls the Children of Israel a herd or that Pharaoh precisely used a technology of human herding. Such language may be a powerful social metaphor, but the Qur'anic text does not state it explicitly.

What can be said with confidence is that Pharaoh's domination restricted the agency of a community and continually exposed them to torment. Rescue from Pharaoh was coming out of that imposed condition.

In another branch of the root, مُسَوَّمَة and سِيمَا raise the issue of sign and distinction.

Sign in the Qur'an does not have one single function. Marked horses are presented as valuable and recognized possessions. Marked stones are specified for punishment. Angels described as musawwimin arrive in support with a sign or distinguishing quality. Human beings may also be known by their sima.

But one must distinguish between a mark of ownership, a mark of mission, and a sign of human condition. None of these must necessarily be a physical brand.

In:

سِيمَاهُمْ فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ مِنْ أَثَرِ السُّجُودِ

The effect of prostration produces a sign in their faces and visible state. This effect may be visible, but restricting it to a scar or callus on the forehead narrows the field of the verse. Real prostration also affects humility, calm, orientation, and behavior.

In:

تَعْرِفُهُمْ بِسِيمَاهُمْ

The sign of the needy is such that the Prophet or a perceptive person recognizes it, while others may suppose them free of need. This sima is not an explicit public brand; it is a subtle sign recognized by attention.

And in:

يُعْرَفُ الْمُجْرِمُونَ بِسِيمَاهُمْ

The condition of the criminals becomes so manifest that they are recognized by their signs.

Sima can therefore be understood as a sign that reveals condition and identity, but not necessarily ethnic, bodily, or unchangeable identity. Conduct, state, and outcome may shape a person's sima.

The relation between imposition and sign should also not be overextended. يسومونكم and سيماهم belong to a distant lexical field together, but the Qur'an does not say that every imposed condition must be registered on the human body as a physical brand.

The more precise conclusion is:

S W M in the Qur'an, on one side, points to continually placing a person or animal in a condition or path; and on another side, in derivatives of marking, distinguishes persons and things by a sign for recognition or mission. Pharaoh's imposition placed human beings in a condition of torment; sima and taswim distinguish persons and things for recognition or for a specified task.

17. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ

They were continually imposing the worst torment upon you.

They were continually afflicting you with a harsh torment.

They were placing you under the worst torment.

"They were driving you toward torment" can be used as an explanatory image, but the base translation should make imposition of torment clear.

تُسِيمُونَ

You pasture your livestock.

You send your livestock out to graze.

الْخَيْلِ الْمُسَوَّمَةِ

Excellent, marked horses.

Marked horses.

Horses let out to pasture, only if this possibility is discussed lexically.

مُسَوَّمَةً عِنْدَ رَبِّكَ

Marked with your Lord.

Specified with your Lord.

"Calibrated to strike" is not a direct translation.

الْمَلَائِكَةِ مُسَوِّمِينَ

Marked angels.

Angels bearing a sign.

Angels who mark, according to morphological analysis and reading.

سِيمَاهُمْ

Their sign.

The mark by which they are recognized.

Their distinguishing sima, where the context suits.

"A concrete brand-mark" is too bodily and too restricted.

18. Conclusion

The root س و م in the Qur'an has several related branches: sending livestock out to pasture, continually subjecting a human being to an imposed condition, and marking or distinguishing persons and things.

In تُسِيمُونَ, livestock are sent out to graze. This use is natural and not negatively valued.

In يَسُومُونَكُمْ سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ, the Children of Israel are continually exposed to Pharaoh's torment. The imperfect verb and the explanation of killing sons and keeping women alive reveal the continuation and systematic nature of this oppression.

In مُسَوَّمَة and مُسَوِّمِين, the concept of being marked or marking is prominent. The type and purpose of the sign must be known from context and should not be replaced by technical language such as calibration or impact trajectory.

سِيمَا is a sign through which the condition of a person or group is recognized. This sign may be open or subtle, bodily or behavioral. The verses do not allow it to be limited only to a physical brand or a forehead callus.

Words such as سموم and سمّ belong to the root س م م and should not be used to expand the field of س و م. Likewise, the link of sima with the root و س م should be kept carefully as a philological issue.

The shortest expression of the root's core is:

Placing a person or thing in a particular path or condition, sometimes through sending forth and continual imposition, and sometimes through marking for distinction and recognition.
