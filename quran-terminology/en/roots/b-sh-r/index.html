Linguistic Note

The core of ب ش ر (B SH R) is tied to the visible human surface and to an inner effect becoming outwardly apparent. Bashar names the human being as visible, embodied, with skin and face. He is seen, walks, eats, meets others, and exists in material life.

Bishārah comes from the same field. News reaches a person, and its effect appears in the face, state, skin, gaze, and behavior. In most Qur’anic uses, bishārah is good news: news that opens the face and makes hope visible. But the root itself is not only “making happy.” Its root sense is delivering news that has visible effect.

For this reason, the Qur’an sometimes speaks of bishārah of punishment. In those cases, the word of good news is joined to bitter content and reverses the listener’s expectation. A more precise center is: a presence or news whose effect appears upon the human surface and state.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of B SH R is the visible human surface, skin and face, and the appearing of the effect of news or inner state upon a person. From this, bishārah is news whose effect becomes visible in the self, face, and behavior, usually good news, though in some contexts a painful and warning news also uses this root.

So B SH R should not be reduced only to pleasant good news. The root includes the human being as visibly embodied, impactful news, visible rejoicing from news, and direct bodily contact.

Material Image

The material image is a human face changed by receiving news. News reaches the person; complexion, openness of the face, worry, smile, calm, or anxiety shows it. Something has happened inwardly, but its effect appears outwardly.

From here, the link between bashar and bishārah becomes clear. Bashar is the being with visible surface and skin. Bishārah is news that passes inward and appears outwardly. Another branch is mubāsharah: direct contact, surface to surface, without distance or mediation.

Classical Language Note

In classical Arabic, B SH R is connected with skin, the human surface, the opening of the face, and delivering news. Basharah is close to the visible surface of human skin, where color, state, softness, hardness, shame, fear, or joy may appear.

Jild is broader and means skin as bodily covering, including animal skin. Basharah is closer to the visible human surface and its seen expression. From this, bashar points to the human being from the angle of visible embodiment, and from the change of that surface through news comes the meaning of bishārah.

Bashar: Human as Visibly Embodied

One important use of this root is bashar as human being. In 18:110, the Qur’an says: قُلْ إِنَّمَا أَنَا بَشَرٌ مِّثْلُكُمْ يُوحَىٰ إِلَيَّ (qul innamā anā basharun mithlukum yūḥā ilayya). The Messenger is not an angelic being separated from human life. He is a bashar like the people, but he receives revelation.

In 23:24, the chiefs of the people of Nūḥ say: مَا هَٰذَا إِلَّا بَشَرٌ مِّثْلُكُمْ (mā hādhā illā basharun mithlukum). Here, being bashar is used as an excuse for denial. They imagined that a divine message should not come through a human being with body, needs, eating, walking, and ordinary life. The Qur’an rejects that assumption.

Basharan Sawiyyā: Visible Appearance Before Maryam

19:17 is important for understanding bashar: فَتَمَثَّلَ لَهَا بَشَرًا سَوِيًّا (fa-tamaththala lahā basharan sawiyyā). The Qur’an does not say insānan sawiyyā; it says basharan sawiyyā.

The choice is precise. Maryam faced a visible, seen appearance in human form, appearing before her as a complete and proportionate bashar. This verse shows that bashar often points to the visible, bodily, and observable side of the human being.

Good News of Birth and Mercy

One of the clearest uses of B SH R is delivering good news about birth and mercy. In 3:39, the Qur’an says: أَنَّ اللَّهَ يُبَشِّرُكَ بِيَحْيَىٰ (anna Allāha yubashshiruka bi-Yaḥyā). Bishārah here is news of a birth that is unexpected and joyful for Zakariyyā; it is not mere information, but opening and mercy.

About Maryam, 3:45 says: إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُبَشِّرُكِ بِكَلِمَةٍ مِّنْهُ (inna Allāha yubashshiruki bi-kalimatin minh). In these two verses, bishārah is tied to future, birth, divine choosing, and mercy.

Bushrā: Good News and Opening

The word بُشْرَى (bushrā) appears as good news and glad tidings. In 11:69, the Qur’an says: وَلَقَدْ جَاءَتْ رُسُلُنَا إِبْرَاهِيمَ بِالْبُشْرَىٰ (wa-laqad jāʾat rusulunā Ibrāhīma bi-al-bushrā). The messengers came with news that opened a new future for Ibrāhīm.

In 51:28, after Ibrāhīm felt fear, the Qur’an says: لَا تَخَفْ وَبَشَّرُوهُ بِغُلَامٍ عَلِيمٍ (lā takhaf wa-bashsharūhu bi-ghulāmin ʿalīm). Bishārah comes against fear and changes the inner state.

Good News for Believers

The Qur’an commands the Messenger to give good news to the believers. In 2:25, it says: وَبَشِّرِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ (wa-bashshiri alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt). Bishārah here concerns future reward; faith and righteous action are joined to news that lights the future.

In 9:112, the Qur’an says briefly: وَبَشِّرِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ (wa-bashshiri al-muʾminīn). The Qur’an does not only warn; it also places hope, future, and mercy before the human being.

Bashīr and Nadhīr

In the Qur’an, بَشِير (bashīr) often appears beside نَذِير (nadhīr). In 2:119, it says: إِنَّا أَرْسَلْنَاكَ بِالْحَقِّ بَشِيرًا وَنَذِيرًا (innā arsalnāka bi-al-ḥaqqi bashīran wa-nadhīrā).

Bashīr is one who gives glad tidings; nadhīr is one who warns. Together, they show the balance of the divine message: not mere frightening, and not irresponsible pleasant news, but announcing both sides of the path. In 6:48, messengers are described as مُبَشِّرِينَ وَمُنذِرِينَ (mubashshirīna wa-mundhirīn).

News of Punishment

Bishārah in the Qur’an is not always pleasant good news. In 4:138, it says: بَشِّرِ الْمُنَافِقِينَ بِأَنَّ لَهُمْ عَذَابًا أَلِيمًا (bashshiri al-munāfiqīna bi-anna lahum ʿadhāban alīmā). In 9:34, it says: فَبَشِّرْهُم بِعَذَابٍ أَلِيمٍ (fa-bashshirhum bi-ʿadhābin alīm).

In this use, bishārah does not have its usual pleasant sense. Bitter news comes in the form of “good news,” reversing expectation. The one who imagines security, profit, or victory is faced with news that reveals his true future.

Istibshār: Rejoicing From News and Future

The form istibshār points to receiving the effect of good news and rejoicing in it. In 3:170, the Qur’an says: فَرِحِينَ بِمَا آتَاهُمُ اللَّهُ مِن فَضْلِهِ وَيَسْتَبْشِرُونَ (fariḥīna bimā ātāhumu Allāhu min faḍlihi wa-yastabshirūn). Joy arises from the favor of The God and the security of the future.

In 9:111, it says: فَاسْتَبْشِرُوا بِبَيْعِكُمُ الَّذِي بَايَعْتُم بِهِ (fa-istabshirū bi-bayʿikumu alladhī bāyaʿtum bih). Istibshār here concerns accepting a covenantal exchange with The God and rejoicing in the promised future.

Winds as Glad Tidings

The Qur’an also speaks of bishārah in nature. In 7:57, it says: وَهُوَ الَّذِي يُرْسِلُ الرِّيَاحَ بُشْرًا بَيْنَ يَدَيْ رَحْمَتِهِ (wa-huwa alladhī yursilu al-riyāḥa bushran bayna yaday raḥmatih). The winds come before rain and carry the announcement of mercy.

This use shows that bishārah is not only human speech. A natural sign may also bear good news.

Mubāsharah: Direct Contact

From this same root, بَاشِرُوهُنَّ (bāshirūhunna) appears in the verse of fasting, 2:187. Here, mubāsharah concerns direct and intimate marital contact.

This use is not the same as news-giving bishārah, but it is connected to the root’s core of visible surface, skin, and unmediated contact. Mubāsharah means direct nearness, not remote or merely theoretical relation.

Distinguishing Qur’anic Uses

In 18:110, bashar points to the Messenger’s humanity. In 19:17, bashar points to a visible appearance in human form. In 3:39 and 3:45, bishārah is news of birth and mercy.

In 2:25, bishārah is the promise of the Garden for believers. In 2:119 and 6:48, bashīr appears beside nadhīr and shows the prophetic role. In 4:138 and 9:34, bishārah of punishment creates threat and reversal. In 2:187, mubāsharah concerns direct marital contact.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In يُبَشِّرُكَ بِيَحْيَىٰ (yubashshiruka bi-Yaḥyā), the bāʾ marks what the bishārah concerns. In وَبَشِّرِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا (wa-bashshiri alladhīna āmanū), the imperative commands the Messenger to deliver good news to the people of faith.

In بَشِّرِ الْمُنَافِقِينَ بِأَنَّ لَهُمْ عَذَابًا أَلِيمًا (bashshiri al-munāfiqīna bi-anna lahum ʿadhāban alīmā), the structure is that of bishārah, but the content is punishment. That tension gives the verse its severe, warning force. In بَشِيرًا وَنَذِيرًا (bashīran wa-nadhīrā), the two descriptions form the message’s double role.

In يَسْتَبْشِرُونَ (yastabshirūn), Form X shows receiving the effect of good news and rejoicing in it. In فَتَمَثَّلَ لَهَا بَشَرًا سَوِيًّا (fa-tamaththala lahā basharan sawiyyā), bashar marks the form of appearance. In بَاشِرُوهُنَّ (bāshirūhunna), Form III points to direct contact and mutual nearness.

Difference From Nearby Words

B SH R is close to khabar, but they are not identical. Khabar is any report or information; it may be neutral, historical, simple, or without felt effect. Bishārah is news tied to future, destiny, reward, mercy, birth, or punishment, whose emotional effect appears in the person.

B SH R differs from n-dh-r. Indhār is warning about danger; bishārah is closer to opening hope and good news. The messengers are both bashīr and nadhīr because the divine message contains both hope and warning.

B SH R is also not the same as faraḥ or surūr. Faraḥ is joy itself, and surūr is inward gladness. Bishārah is the news that may produce joy and gladness. B SH R also differs from insān, jasad, and jild: insān is broader, jasad points to body, jild is skin as bodily covering, and bashar shows the human being from the angle of visible human embodiment.

Forqan Translation Method

Forqan should not translate B SH R the same way everywhere. Bashar often means human being, but from the angle of visible body, material presence, and human embodiment. Bishārah is usually good news, but in punishment verses its threatening reversal must be preserved.

Bashīr is best rendered as bearer of glad tidings or bringer of hopeful news, but when it appears with nadhīr, the balance of good news and warning must remain visible. Mubāsharah concerns direct and intimate contact and should not be mixed with news-giving bishārah.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

  • basharun mithlukum: a bashar like you; or a human being like you, with the same human presence and needs.
  • fa-tamaththala lahā basharan sawiyyā: he appeared to her as a complete, proportionate bashar; or he appeared before her in a complete and visible human form.
  • yubashshiruka bi-Yaḥyā: He gives you bishārah of Yaḥyā; or He gives you good news of the coming of Yaḥyā.
  • bi-al-bushrā: with glad tidings; or with good news.
  • wa-bashshiri alladhīna āmanū: give glad tidings to those who have faith; or give the people of faith good news.
  • bashīran wa-nadhīrā: bearer of glad tidings and warner.
  • bashshiri al-munāfiqīna bi-anna lahum ʿadhāban alīmā: inform the hypocrites that they have a painful punishment; or give them “glad tidings” of a painful punishment, preserving the threatening reversal.
  • yastabshirūn: they rejoice from good news; or they become glad from the glad tidings.
  • al-riyāḥa bushran: the winds as glad tidings; or winds bearing the news of mercy.
  • bāshirūhunna: have direct marital contact with them; or be intimate with them directly.

Summary

The Qur’anic root B SH R begins from the visible human surface, skin, face, and the outward appearing of inner effect. From this, bashar names the human being from the angle of visible embodiment, and bishārah names news whose effect becomes visible in the self, face, and state.

In the Qur’an, this root is used for the humanity of messengers, visible appearance in human form, good news of birth, promises of mercy and the Garden, the prophetic role as bashīr, the joy of believers, winds that announce rain, and even news of punishment in a severe threatening reversal. The branch of mubāsharah concerns direct and intimate contact.

In short: B SH R in the Qur’an points to the human visible surface and to the appearing of the effect of news upon a person; it begins from skin, face, and visible presence, then extends to human embodiment, bishārah, impactful news, visible rejoicing, and direct contact.
