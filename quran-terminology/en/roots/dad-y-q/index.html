Narrowness, constraint, and limited capacity: from restricted space to inner pressure and imposing hardship

In early Arabic, ض ي ق concerns narrowness and insufficient capacity, opposite to breadth and spaciousness. ضَاقَ describes a place or situation that is, or becomes, too restricted for what it contains or what must be done.

In tangible uses, it describes confined places, narrow paths, or restricted room for movement. A path difficult for several people to pass through can be called ضَيِّق.

In extended uses, it can describe straitened living conditions, restricted resources, pressure, difficulty enduring a situation, or feeling unable to cope.

ضَاقَ صَدْرِي does not necessarily mean the chest physically becomes smaller. It can describe pressure that makes endurance, composure, or coping difficult.

ضَيَّقَ means to constrict or impose restrictions. A person or other cause can make circumstances harder for someone and reduce their scope for action.

The root occurs thirteen times in five morphological groups in the Quran. Its uses range from narrow places and inner distress to deliberately constraining another person.

The shared core is insufficient room or scope in a place, situation, or capacity to face something. The kind, cause, and ethical significance of the constraint depend on context.

1. Meaning in early Arabic and ancient witnesses

Arabic sources set ضِيق and ضَيْق against سَعَة: narrowness against spaciousness.

ضَاقَ الشَّيْءُ describes something with insufficient or restricted capacity.

ضَاقَ الْمَكَانُ means “the place became narrow.”

ضَاقَ صَدْرُهُ describes someone's chest becoming constrained in enduring a situation.

ضَاقَ بِهِ ذَرْعًا describes finding one's capacity insufficient to deal with something.

Constraint can therefore characterize a place or describe someone's relationship to a place, event, or problem.

A witness from ʿAmr ibn al-Ahtam

ʿAmr ibn al-Ahtam, a poet and orator who lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods, says:

لَعَمْرُكَ مَا ضَاقَتْ بِلَادٌ بِأَهْلِهَا
وَلَكِنَّ أَخْلَاقَ الرِّجَالِ تَضِيقُ

la-ʿamruka mā ḍāqat bilādun bi-ahlihā / wa-lākinna akhlāqa al-rijāli taḍīqu

Translation: “By your life, lands do not grow narrow for their people; rather, people's dispositions grow narrow.”

The verse places tangible and extended uses of the root together.

ضَاقَتْ بِلَادٌ in the first half concerns the land becoming cramped.

أَخْلَاقَ الرِّجَالِ تَضِيقُ in the second attributes narrowness to human dispositions.

The poet contrasts land's capacity with people's conduct. There may be enough land, while narrow-mindedness, stinginess, or misconduct makes shared life difficult.

The narrowness of character is not bodily shrinkage. It concerns limitations in generosity, conduct, and acceptance of others.

Early literary collections, including Al-Mufaḍḍaliyyāt, attribute this line to ʿAmr ibn al-Ahtam. His lifetime spans the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods, but the precise date of this line has not been independently established.

It is useful evidence for early Arabic usage, without securely dating this particular verse before the Quran.

Narrow capacity in Zuhayr's poetry

A verse attributed to Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā reads:

وَحَبْسُهُ نَفْسَهُ فِي كُلِّ مَنْزِلَةٍ
يَكْرَهُهَا الْجُبَنَاءُ الضَّاقَةُ الْعَطَنِ

wa-ḥabsuhu nafsahu fī kulli manzilatin / yakrahuhā al-jubanāʾu al-ḍāqatu al-ʿaṭani

Translation: “And his holding himself firm in every position that narrow-capacity cowards detest.”

الضَّاقَةُ الْعَطَنِ describes those lacking the breadth and endurance to face a difficult situation.

Literally, عَطَن concerns the place where camels gather near water. Its breadth or narrowness can become an image of human generosity or limited capacity.

Here, narrowness extends from place to behaviour.

This does not make ضِيق mean fear. Cowardice comes from الْجُبَنَاءُ; الضَّاقَةُ الْعَطَنِ emphasizes limited capacity.

Early lexical sources attribute the line to Zuhayr, but no contemporary document independently fixes its date and earliest wording here. Its historical limits should be retained.

What the witnesses show

Both examples show that early Arabic narrowness was not confined to places.

The root could express limitations of conduct, capacity, and coping.

This does not prove that the nonphysical meaning arose later. The imagery is connected, but historical sequence needs independent evidence.

A defensible early Arabic definition is:

Insufficient space or scope in a place or situation, restricting movement, reception, endurance, or action.

For people, this may appear as hardship, pressure, narrow-mindedness, or reduced endurance.

2. Forms and derivatives

ض ي ق has a weak middle radical. Depending on the verbal form, its yāʾ may appear as alif or remain yāʾ.

The basic verbal paradigm is:

ضَاقَ يَضِيقُ

It means to become narrow or experience constraint.

Important forms include:

ضَاقَ: became narrow; lacked sufficient room or capacity.

يَضِيقُ: becomes constrained or experiences pressure in relation to something.

ضَيْق and ضِيق: narrowness or insufficient scope; in some uses, pressure and difficult circumstances.

ضَيِّق: narrow, restricted, or of limited capacity.

ضَائِق: the active participle of ضَاقَ, describing something or someone in a state of constraint.

ضَيَّقَ: constricted or imposed restrictions.

تَضْيِيق: constricting or reducing room and scope.

تَضَايَقَ: became cramped or constrained; in some constructions, mutual constriction or incompatibility developed.

تَضَيَّقَ: became narrow or restricted.

مَضِيق: a confined place or passage; figuratively, a difficult situation.

مَضَايِق: plural of مَضِيق; confined passages or difficult situations.

ضِيقَة and ضَيْقَة: constraint; also poverty and hardship in recorded uses.

مُضِيق: someone in financial or other straits; according to its verbal construction, also someone who constricts.

ضَائِقَة: a difficult predicament; also used in modern Arabic for financial crisis or hardship.

Not all these forms occur in the Quran.

Forms actually found in the Quran

The thirteen occurrences fall into five morphological groups:

First, seven form-I verbs: At-Tawbah 9:25; At-Tawbah 9:118, twice; Hūd 11:77; Al-Ḥijr 15:97; Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:13; and Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:33.

Second, one form-II verb, تُضَيِّقُوا, in At-Ṭalāq 65:6.

Third, two occurrences of ضَيِّقًا, in Al-Anʿām 6:125 and Al-Furqān 25:13.

Fourth, two nominal occurrences of ضَيْقٍ, in An-Naḥl 16:127 and An-Naml 27:70.

Fifth, one active participle, ضَائِقٌ, in Hūd 11:12.

They share a semantic core, but perform different roles.

ضَاقَ usually describes becoming or being constrained.

ضَيِّق describes narrowness as a condition.

ضَائِق attributes the condition to a person or thing.

The form-II ضَيَّقَ can describe creating constraint for someone else.

The distinction between experiencing hardship and imposing hardship is especially important here.

ضَيْق and ضِيق

Early sources record both vocalizations for narrowness.

Some lexicographers distinguish them, associating ضِيق more with tangible restriction, such as houses and clothing, and ضَيْق with constraint of the chest and inner distress.

ضَيْق is also explained as a reduced form of ضَيِّق.

These distinctions should not be made absolute. Recorded uses overlap, and interpretation depends on construction and context.

The nominal ضَيْقٍ in An-Naḥl 16:127 and An-Naml 27:70 concerns pressure arising from opponents' behaviour.

Those two occurrences do not establish that ضَيْق is exclusively psychological throughout Arabic.

3. Narrow places and limited room

The clearest tangible Quranic use describes a narrow place.

Al-Furqān 25:13

وَإِذَا أُلْقُوا مِنْهَا مَكَانًا ضَيِّقًا مُقَرَّنِينَ دَعَوْا هُنَالِكَ ثُبُورًا

wa-idhā ulqū minhā makānan ḍayyiqan muqarranīna daʿaw hunālika thubūrā

Al-Furqān 25:13

Translation: “When they are cast into a narrow place within it, bound together, they call there for destruction.”

ضَيِّقًا modifies مَكَانًا.

مَكَان identifies a place; ضَيِّق describes its restricted capacity.

مُقَرَّنِينَ also describes the people as bound together.

The narrow place and the binding are separate features presented together.

The root ض ي ق does not itself mean that people are chained; the binding comes from مُقَرَّنِينَ.

Nor does ضَيِّق specify dimensions, geometry, wall material, or physical architecture.

The clear meaning is a place of restricted room in which people are confined.

The root thus retains a tangible spatial meaning in the Quran.

When spacious earth becomes narrow

At-Tawbah 9:25 recalls Ḥunayn, when people were impressed by their numbers, but those numbers did not avail them in battle.

The earth, despite its breadth, became narrow for them, and they turned away in retreat.

The earth did not physically shrink: بِمَا رَحُبَتْ explicitly acknowledges its spaciousness.

The constraint concerns people who, facing danger and confusion, cannot find sufficient safety or scope to cope despite the surrounding space.

It is not merely an inward feeling detached from external reality: battle, danger, and retreat are real events.

Yet the earth's narrowing does not describe a change in its physical area.

Juxtaposing ضَاقَتْ and رَحُبَتْ distinguishes environmental breadth from restricted human possibilities.

Earth and selves constrained in At-Tawbah 9:118

At-Tawbah 9:118

وَعَلَى الثَّلَاثَةِ الَّذِينَ خُلِّفُوا حَتَّىٰ إِذَا ضَاقَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ الْأَرْضُ بِمَا رَحُبَتْ وَضَاقَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ أَنْفُسُهُمْ وَظَنُّوا أَنْ لَا مَلْجَأَ مِنَ اللَّهِ إِلَّا إِلَيْهِ ثُمَّ تَابَ عَلَيْهِمْ لِيَتُوبُوا ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ

wa-ʿalā al-thalāthati alladhīna khullifū ḥattā idhā ḍāqat ʿalayhimu al-arḍu bi-mā raḥubat wa-ḍāqat ʿalayhim anfusuhum wa-ẓannū an lā maljaʾa mina allāhi illā ilayhi thumma tāba ʿalayhim li-yatūbū inna allāha huwa al-tawwābu al-raḥīm

Al-Tawbah 9:118

Translation: “And toward the three who were left behind, until the earth, despite its breadth, became narrow for them, and their own selves became narrow for them, and they realized there was no refuge from The God except toward Him. Then He turned toward them so that they might turn back. Indeed, The God is the Oft-Returning, the Especially Merciful.”

ضَاقَتْ occurs twice here.

First, the earth becomes narrow for them.

Then their own selves become constrained.

These are not identical expressions.

The first concerns their position in the surrounding world: spacious earth offers them no felt room for relief.

The second expresses pressure experienced within themselves.

The experiences can be connected, but their subjects differ: earth and the human self.

The verse then speaks of no refuge from God except toward Him, followed by His merciful turning and their return.

The context connects their constraint with their moral situation and difficult experience.

But ضَاقَ does not itself mean sin, shame, or repentance. Those themes come from the narrative and other clauses.

Nor does this imply that everyone experiencing inner distress has sinned or is being punished by God. The word describes constraint; each cause needs separate consideration.

4. The constrained chest: inner pressure amid difficulty

A major Quranic use is the expression of a chest becoming constrained.

The bodily term صَدْر is used in connection with inner state, thought, and capacity to cope.

Moses' constrained chest

Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:13

وَيَضِيقُ صَدْرِي وَلَا يَنْطَلِقُ لِسَانِي فَأَرْسِلْ إِلَىٰ هَارُونَ

wa-yaḍīqu ṣadrī wa-lā yanṭaliqu lisānī fa-arsil ilā hārūn

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:13

Translation: “My chest becomes constrained and my tongue does not flow freely, so send to Aaron.”

In Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:12, Moses expresses fear that his message will be denied.

He then mentions his constrained chest and difficulty speaking freely.

يَضِيقُ is an imperfect verb, with صَدْرِي as its subject.

Moses describes his own condition in facing a difficult mission.

Constriction of the chest should not be equated with lack of fluency.

وَلَا يَنْطَلِقُ لِسَانِي is a separate clause describing difficulty speaking freely.

The conditions may be connected, but ضِيق does not lexically mean a speech impairment.

Facing a major mission, Moses asks God to involve Aaron.

His request does not imply loss of faith or of his commission.

The account depicts Moses' human condition: he recognizes the difficulty of a weighty responsibility and seeks divine help to carry it out.

Muhammad's constrained chest

Hūd 11:12

فَلَعَلَّكَ تَارِكٌ بَعْضَ مَا يُوحَىٰ إِلَيْكَ وَضَائِقٌ بِهِ صَدْرُكَ أَنْ يَقُولُوا لَوْلَا أُنْزِلَ عَلَيْهِ كَنْزٌ أَوْ جَاءَ مَعَهُ مَلَكٌ ۚ إِنَّمَا أَنْتَ نَذِيرٌ ۚ وَاللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ وَكِيلٌ

fa-laʿallaka tārikun baʿḍa mā yūḥā ilayka wa-ḍāʾiqun bihi ṣadruka an yaqūlū lawlā unzila ʿalayhi kanzun aw jāʾa maʿahu malakun innamā anta nadhīrun wa-allāhu ʿalā kulli shayʾin wakīl

Hūd 11:12

Translation: “Perhaps you would leave aside some of what is revealed to you, and your chest be constrained by it, because they say: Why has no treasure been sent down to him, or an angel come with him? You are only a warner, and The God is guardian over everything.”

The form here is ضَائِقٌ.

It is the active participle of ضَاقَ, describing the chest's constraint.

The passage concerns pressure from those demanding treasure or an accompanying angel from Muhammad.

Muhammad is directly addressed. The prospect of leaving aside revelation and experiencing constraint is part of that address.

The prospective wording does not establish that he actually withheld revelation.

The following statement clarifies his role: إِنَّمَا أَنْتَ نَذِيرٌ.

His task is warning and conveying the message, not fulfilling every condition opponents demand for accepting it.

Inner constraint can thus arise from social pressure, opposition, and others' demands.

Al-Ḥijr 15:97 states God's knowledge of Muhammad's constrained chest because of what they say.

Al-Ḥijr 15:98 commands him to glorify his Lord with praise and be among those who prostrate.

These verses neither deny Muhammad's difficulty nor identify it as unbelief.

Constraint and openness to guidance

Al-Anʿām 6:125

فَمَنْ يُرِدِ اللَّهُ أَنْ يَهْدِيَهُ يَشْرَحْ صَدْرَهُ لِلْإِسْلَامِ ۖ وَمَنْ يُرِدْ أَنْ يُضِلَّهُ يَجْعَلْ صَدْرَهُ ضَيِّقًا حَرَجًا كَأَنَّمَا يَصَّعَّدُ فِي السَّمَاءِ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يَجْعَلُ اللَّهُ الرِّجْسَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ

fa-man yuridi allāhu an yahdiyahu yashraḥ ṣadrahu li-l-islāmi wa-man yurid an yuḍillahu yajʿal ṣadrahu ḍayyiqan ḥarajan ka-annamā yaṣṣaʿʿadu fī al-samāʾi kadhālika yajʿalu allāhu al-rijsa ʿalā alladhīna lā yuʾminūn

Al-Anʿām 6:125

Translation: “Whomever The God wills to guide, He opens his chest to submission; whomever He wills to lead astray, He makes his chest narrow and constricted, as though he were struggling to ascend into the sky. Thus The God places defilement upon those who do not believe.”

ضَيِّقًا describes the chest and is accompanied by حَرَجًا.

Its counterpart, يَشْرَحْ صَدْرَهُ, describes opening the chest to submission.

The contrast matters.

Openness is connected here with receiving guidance, and constraint with the opposing condition.

ضِيق and حَرَج should not be collapsed into one word. Both concern restriction and difficulty, and together intensify the description of constriction.

كَأَنَّمَا يَصَّعَّدُ فِي السَّمَاءِ provides an image of severe difficulty.

Struggling to ascend into the sky illustrates the chest's condition.

The simile should not be turned into a medical definition: ضِيق itself denotes neither oxygen shortage nor a specified disease.

The verse addresses guidance and inner response, not a diagnostic rule identifying belief through breathing or illness.

The constrained chests of Moses and Muhammad elsewhere show that the expression does not always mean rejection of guidance.

The same expression can describe different situations without making their causes or ethical significance identical.

Al-Anʿām 6:125 concerns guidance. Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:13 concerns Moses' mission. Hūd 11:12 and Al-Ḥijr 15:97 concern opponents' statements affecting Muhammad.

The root consistently involves restriction, while cause, subject, and result vary.

“Do not be in distress”: responding to schemes

An-Naḥl 16:127 and An-Naml 27:70 address Muhammad concerning distress over opponents' schemes.

An-Naḥl 16:127

وَاصْبِرْ وَمَا صَبْرُكَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ ۚ وَلَا تَحْزَنْ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا تَكُ فِي ضَيْقٍ مِمَّا يَمْكُرُونَ

wa-ṣbir wa-mā ṣabruka illā bi-allāhi wa-lā taḥzan ʿalayhim wa-lā taku fī ḍayqin mimmā yamkurūn

Al-Naḥl 16:127

Translation: “Be patient; your patience is only through The God. Do not grieve over them, and do not be in distress over what they scheme.”

ضَيْقٍ is nominal, preceded by فِي.

فِي ضَيْقٍ pictures someone as being within a constraining situation.

Here the restriction is pressure arising from opponents' schemes, rather than physical confinement.

حُزْن and ضِيق occur separately in the verse.

The former concerns grief; the latter, constriction and pressure.

They can accompany one another without being exact synonyms.

An-Naml 27:70 expresses a closely related instruction.

The repeated instruction recognizes the pressure of opposition while calling Muhammad to perseverance rather than remaining constrained by it.

Patience here concerns steadfastness in the right course, not silence toward every injustice or an absolute refusal to act.

5. ضَاقَ بِهِمْ ذَرْعًا: difficulty coping

Hūd 11:77

وَلَمَّا جَاءَتْ رُسُلُنَا لُوطًا سِيءَ بِهِمْ وَضَاقَ بِهِمْ ذَرْعًا وَقَالَ هَٰذَا يَوْمٌ عَصِيبٌ

wa-lammā jāʾat rusulunā lūṭan sīʾa bihim wa-ḍāqa bihim dharʿan wa-qāla hādhā yawmun ʿaṣīb

Hūd 11:77

Translation: “When Our Messengers came to Lot, he was distressed about them and felt unable to cope concerning them. He said: This is a difficult day.”

ضَاقَ بِهِمْ ذَرْعًا is an established Arabic idiom.

ذَرْع concerns measuring and the arm's span. The idiom evokes insufficient reach or capacity for facing something.

Idiomatic usage describes difficulty bearing or managing a situation.

The Messengers' arrival concerns Lot because of his people's behaviour.

The following verses reveal their intention to assault his guests.

The predicament therefore connects with Lot's responsibility toward his guests and the danger threatening them.

ضَاقَ is not a lexical synonym for fear. Anxiety, difficulty, and limited capacity are understood through the idiom and context.

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:33 repeats the expression concerning Lot.

The Messengers tell him not to fear or grieve: they will save him and his family, except his wife, who remains behind.

The connection supports understanding his distress in relation to a real danger; the passages do not identify it as weak faith or distrust of God.

A person can have a proper responsibility yet feel their capacity restricted by others' harmful power or conduct.

Constraint is therefore not always the sufferer's own fault; it can arise from others' injustice.

6. Imposing constraint: لِتُضَيِّقُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ

At-Ṭalāq 65:6

أَسْكِنُوهُنَّ مِنْ حَيْثُ سَكَنْتُمْ مِنْ وُجْدِكُمْ وَلَا تُضَارُّوهُنَّ لِتُضَيِّقُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ ۚ وَإِنْ كُنَّ أُولَاتِ حَمْلٍ فَأَنْفِقُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ حَتَّىٰ يَضَعْنَ حَمْلَهُنَّ ۚ فَإِنْ أَرْضَعْنَ لَكُمْ فَآتُوهُنَّ أُجُورَهُنَّ ۖ وَأْتَمِرُوا بَيْنَكُمْ بِمَعْرُوفٍ ۖ وَإِنْ تَعَاسَرْتُمْ فَسَتُرْضِعُ لَهُ أُخْرَىٰ

askinūhunna min ḥaythu sakantum min wujdikum wa-lā tuḍārrūhunna li-tuḍayyiqū ʿalayhinna wa-in kunna ulāti ḥamlin fa-anfiqū ʿalayhinna ḥattā yaḍaʿna ḥamlahunna fa-in arḍaʿna lakum fa-ātūhunna ujūrahunna wa-ʾtamirū baynakum bi-maʿrūfin wa-in taʿāsartum fa-sa-turḍiʿu lahu ukhrā

Al-Ṭalāq 65:6

Translation: “House them where you live, according to your means, and do not harm them to constrain them. If they are pregnant, spend on them until they deliver their burden. If they nurse for you, give them their payment and consult together honourably. If you find matters difficult between you, another woman will nurse for him.”

This is the root's only form-II occurrence in the Quran.

ضَاقَ and ضَيَّقَ

Earlier uses mainly describe a narrow place or a person's constrained circumstances.

تُضَيِّقُوا describes creating constraint for someone else.

Form-II ضَيَّقَ can be transitive: the subject restricts another person's or thing's circumstances.

لِتُضَيِّقُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ means “in order to constrain them.”

لِـ expresses purpose here.

The verse does more than describe women's hardship: it prohibits harmful conduct intended to make their circumstances difficult.

Harm and constriction

Two independent roots appear together:

تُضَارُّوهُنَّ belongs to ض ر ر and concerns causing harm.

تُضَيِّقُوا belongs to ض ي ق and concerns restricting circumstances and possibilities.

A single action can involve both.

For example, financial pressure or deliberately difficult living conditions may be used to force another person to comply.

Such conduct involves both injury and imposed constraint.

The roots remain distinct. Harm can occur without sustained restriction, while restriction can take various forms.

At-Ṭalāq 65:6 brings both into its treatment of wrongful conduct toward women.

Constriction as a means of pressure

The passage concerns responsibilities associated with marital separation.

Providing housing according to means appears beside the prohibition of harmful constriction.

The verse then addresses pregnancy expenses, nursing payment, and honourable consultation.

This arrangement indicates that disagreement and separation do not license imposed hardship and deprivation.

A contemporary illustration would be deliberately worsening housing or essential support despite having the means, to pressure a woman into surrendering her rights.

This illustrates the concept; it does not derive every detail of contemporary law from تُضَيِّقُوا.

The condemned conduct is causing harm in order to constrain another person and pressure them toward an unwelcome situation.

Not every limitation is wrongful constriction.

Someone may genuinely lack resources. مِنْ وُجْدِكُمْ explicitly attends to their means.

Thus, genuine scarcity must be distinguished from deliberately imposing hardship when fair treatment is possible.

This distinction fits both the root and the passage.

7. Comparison with related roots

ض ي ق (ḍayq) and ض ن ك (ḍank)

ضَنْك concerns straitened, difficult circumstances, particularly in living conditions.

Ṭā Hā 20:124 contains مَعِيشَةً ضَنْكًا.

ضِيق can also concern constrained living, but its Quranic uses extend across place, chest, coping capacity, and imposed restriction.

The families are close without one necessarily being merely a stronger version of the other.

Nor does ضَنْك in livelihood imply that ضِيق cannot concern economic hardship: early Arabic uses the latter family for poverty and straitened means too.

ض ي ق (ḍayq) and ح ر ج (ḥaraj)

حَرَج concerns difficulty, constraint, and restriction in Quranic usage.

Al-Anʿām 6:125 places ضَيِّقًا and حَرَجًا together.

Both can contribute to describing one condition.

Their co-occurrence alone does not establish that ḥaraj is always a more severe stage of ḍīq.

Ḍīq emphasizes insufficient room or scope; ḥaraj often concerns difficulty and a constraining predicament.

Al-Ḥajj 22:78 speaks of no ḥaraj being placed in religion. That does not erase every responsibility, effort, or difficulty; the kind of restriction and the context matter.

ض ي ق (ḍayq) and ع س ر (ʿusr)

عُسْر concerns difficulty in action or in passing through a situation.

Ash-Sharḥ 94:5 and Ash-Sharḥ 94:6 describe ease accompanying hardship.

Ḍīq more directly evokes restricted room or possibility.

A task may be difficult despite adequate tools, space, and time.

Conversely, a simple task may be constrained by insufficient time, space, or resources.

Difficulty of action and restriction of circumstances can accompany one another without being identical.

ض ي ق (ḍayq) and و س ع (saʿah)

و س ع provides a close opposite to ضِيق.

Arabic breadth can describe space, ability, resources, and human capacity.

وُسْعَهَا in Al-Baqarah 2:286 concerns a person's capacity.

ذُو سَعَةٍ in At-Ṭalāq 65:7 describes someone with ample means.

Breadth, like constraint, therefore has tangible and extended uses.

It does not always mean abundant wealth: sometimes capacity to fulfil a responsibility is at issue.

Conversely, ضِيق does not always mean poverty or moral deficiency.

The word's subject and context determine the kind of breadth or constraint.

ض ي ق (ḍayq) and ش ر ح (sharḥ)

ش ر ح, when applied to the chest, concerns opening it.

Al-Anʿām 6:125 contrasts openness to submission with narrowness and constriction.

Ash-Sharḥ 94:1 also speaks of opening Muhammad's chest.

Opening can thus portray inner readiness and capacity.

The two roots are not exact opposites in every use.

شَرْح can concern opening and making clear, while ضِيق concerns restriction.

Their contrast is clear in the chest passages, without becoming a universal rule for all derivatives.

ض ي ق (ḍayq) and ض ر ر (ḍarar)

ضَرَر concerns harm and injury.

ضِيق concerns narrowness and restriction.

Their distinction matters in At-Ṭalāq 65:6, where they occur together.

Someone can harm another by restricting resources and creating hardship.

But not all harm occurs through restriction, and not every constraint results from deliberate injury.

تَضْيِيق and إِضْرَار should therefore not be merged in translation or analysis.

8. Translation guidance for Forqan

One fixed equivalent cannot serve every occurrence of ض ي ق.

“Narrowness” and “becoming constrained” are broad equivalents, but syntax and the verb's subject matter.

ضَيِّقًا in Al-Furqān 25:13 can be “narrow,” since it describes a place.

In At-Tawbah 9:25 and At-Tawbah 9:118, “the earth became narrow for them” retains the image without replacing it with “fear” or “psychological deadlock.”

ضَاقَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ أَنْفُسُهُمْ in At-Tawbah 9:118 can retain “their own selves became constrained for them,” with explanation of the inward experience.

ضَاقَ بِهِمْ ذَرْعًا in Hūd 11:77 and Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:33 is idiomatic. “He felt unable to cope concerning them” conveys its established sense.

A literal rendering about the measure of his arm becoming narrow would not communicate the idiom.

يَضِيقُ صَدْرُكَ and يَضِيقُ صَدْرِي in Al-Ḥijr 15:97 and Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:13 can retain “your chest becomes constrained” and “my chest becomes constrained.”

In Hūd 11:12, ضَائِقٌ can be expressed naturally within the sentence as a constrained chest, while recognizing that the Arabic uses a participle rather than an imperfect verb.

ضَيِّقًا حَرَجًا in Al-Anʿām 6:125 can be “narrow and constricted.” The two words should not disappear into a single undifferentiated equivalent.

فِي ضَيْقٍ in An-Naḥl 16:127 and An-Naml 27:70 can be “in distress” or “in straits,” preserving a constraining situation.

لِتُضَيِّقُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ in At-Ṭalāq 65:6 means “in order to constrain them.”

The form-II verb assigns agency. “So that they are in difficulty” alone obscures those actively creating the constraint.

Brief root meaning for Forqan word boxes

A proposed shared brief meaning is:

“Narrowness and insufficient room or scope; restriction of place, opportunity, or capacity to cope.”

Each contextual meaning should be written separately.

For ضَيِّقًا in Al-Furqān 25:13: “a place with restricted room.”

For يَضِيقُ in Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:13: “Moses' chest becoming constrained in facing a difficult mission.”

For ضَاقَ in Hūd 11:77: “feeling limited in ability to face the danger threatening his guests.”

For تُضَيِّقُوا in At-Ṭalāq 65:6: “deliberately restricting and worsening the women's circumstances.”

A shared semantic core thus takes different roles in different constructions.

9. Summary

In early Arabic, ض ي ق denotes narrowness and insufficient capacity, opposite to breadth.

This is readily illustrated by confined places and paths, and also applies to limited capacity, difficult circumstances, and inward pressure.

Early poetry applies narrowness to land and conduct alike, without establishing a definite historical order between physical and extended meanings.

The thirteen Quranic occurrences demonstrate this range.

Al-Furqān 25:13 concerns a narrow place.

At-Tawbah 9:25 and At-Tawbah 9:118 describe spacious earth becoming narrow for people. At-Tawbah 9:118 separately mentions their constrained selves.

Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:13, Hūd 11:12, and Al-Ḥijr 15:97 describe Moses' or Muhammad's chest under difficult circumstances.

Hūd 11:77 and Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:33 describe Lot's predicament concerning threatened guests.

An-Naḥl 16:127 and An-Naml 27:70 instruct Muhammad not to remain distressed over opponents' schemes.

Al-Anʿām 6:125 contrasts constriction with openness to guidance.

At-Ṭalāq 65:6 prohibits deliberately constraining women in the context of separation and responsibilities for support and housing.

Constraint is therefore not always a moral fault in the sufferer. Heavy responsibility, others' injustice, or real hardship can produce it.

By contrast, someone may misuse power or resources to impose hardship. These situations differ ethically.

The final definition is:

ض ي ق: narrowness or insufficient scope in space, circumstances, or capacity to face something, restricting movement, acceptance, endurance, or action; in causative constructions, imposing such restriction on another.

This covers confined places, inner pressure, and actively restricting another person's circumstances.

A key distinction emerges: experiencing constraint is not the same as constraining others. The first may arise from hardship, responsibility, or others' conduct; the second can be a deliberate violation of justice and rights.
