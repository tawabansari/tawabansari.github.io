1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ش ك ر in the Qur'an is close to recognizing a gift, not disregarding it, the appearance of its effect, and a fitting response to it. Shukr is not merely saying a sentence that praises the giver; it creates a relationship between gift, recognition, and response.

A human being receives something, recognizes its value, attributes it to its proper source, and then takes a fitting stance toward it. For this reason, shukr in the Qur'an can be connected with eating pure provision, righteous action, worship, the right use of abilities, faithfulness during testing, and acceptance of guidance.

A ni'mah is not only possession or material comfort. Life, hearing, sight, the heart, guidance, rescue, security, provision, the power to act, pardon, and the opportunity to choose can all stand in the place of a gift.

Even so, shukr should not be restricted to "productivity," "turning input into output," or any other technical expression. The right use of a gift is one manifestation of shukr, not the whole meaning of it.

The shortest expression for this root is:

Recognizing a gift or received good, not disregarding it, and showing a right and fitting response to it.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

شَكَرَ / يَشْكُرُ

He gave shukr, or he gives shukr; that is, he showed a thankful response before a gift, good, or guidance.

اشْكُرْ / اشْكُرُوا

Give shukr, or give shukr collectively.

شُكْر

Shukr itself and the thankful response.

شُكُور

Thanks, gratitude, or a thankful response. This form, with dammah on the shin, appears in constructions such as:

لَا نُرِيدُ مِنْكُمْ جَزَاءً وَلَا شُكُورًا

We want from you neither recompense nor thanks.

Al-Insan 76:9

Here it means thanks and shukr.

شَاكِر

One who gives shukr.

This form is used both for the human being and as a description of The God, but the quality of the meaning is not identical in the two uses.

شَكُور

Very thankful, or one in whom shukr is abundant, stable, and prominent.

This form is also used both for the human being and as a description of The God.

مَشْكُور

An effort or action that has not been overlooked, has been accepted, and has been answered.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of SH K R cannot be expressed only by "saying thank you." In contemporary English, thanking often narrows to a verbal or social reaction; but the Qur'anic use of shukr is broader.

Several elements are joined together in shukr.

First, there is a gift, good, or guidance.

Second, the receiver recognizes it and does not disregard it.

Third, the receiver attributes it to its proper source.

Fourth, the effect of this recognition appears in stance, speech, choice, or action.

Fifth, the response remains aligned with the truth and direction of that gift.

Therefore, shukr may be understood as a visible and fitting response to received good or to a truth that has become clear.

This response does not always have one form. Shukr for provision may be pure and responsible use of it. Shukr for knowledge may be acting on the basis of awareness. Shukr for power may be refraining from oppression. Shukr for guidance may be accepting and following the path that has become clear to the human being.

But the result of every gift should not be inserted directly into the translation of the word shukr. Righteous action, worship, justice, giving, and the right use of blessing are not the lexical meaning of shukr itself; rather, in different contexts they can be manifestations of shukr.

5. The Material Image of the Root

In Arabic lexical sources, this root was not used only in the field of human gratitude. They speak of an animal in which the effect of a little feed appears as growth and fatness, or of an animal that, with limited grazing, produces more milk.

The shared point in these images is the appearance of the effect of what has been received. Food does not remain without effect in the body; its result appears in growth or milk.

This image can help us understand shukr. Human shukr also does not stop at an inner state or at language; the effect of the gift should appear in the way the human being uses, chooses, or acts.

Even so, this material image must not be turned into a theory of "the maximum output from the minimum input." Shukr does not always mean increase in quantity, economic productivity, or greater production.

A thankful response to a blessing may be consuming less, refraining from using power, preserving another person's right, or leaving a harmful act. So the measure of shukr is not only the amount of output; it is the correctness and fittingness of the response.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In early Arabic, shukr was connected with recognizing good, making it known, and responding to ihsan.

In lexical sources, shukr is close to recognizing ihsan and expressing or making it known.

Constructions such as these are also reported: شَكَرَهُ, شَكَرَ لَهُ, and شَكَرَ النِّعْمَةَ.

These constructions show that shukr can be directed both toward the giver and toward the gift or blessing that has been recognized.

The material uses connected with fatness, increase of milk, or the appearance of the effect of feed also show that the element of the appearance of effect was present in the historical field of this root.

But this element must be placed beside Qur'anic usage and should not be treated as the only final definition of the root.

7. The Restriction of the Meaning of Shukr

In common religious and social usage, shukr is sometimes almost restricted to saying phrases such as "praise be to The God" or "thanks to The God."

Such speech can be part of shukr, but by itself it does not contain the whole Qur'anic meaning.

A person may speak about blessing with the tongue, yet waste that same blessing, turn it into a tool of oppression, or ignore the responsibility that arises from it.

The Qur'an does not tell the family of Dawud merely to utter a thankful phrase. It says:

اعْمَلُوا آلَ دَاوُودَ شُكْرًا

Act, family of Dawud, as shukr.

Saba 34:13

In this construction, shukr is explicitly connected with action. Action is not the lexical translation of shukr itself; but the verse shows that shukr can be realized in action and can remain incomplete without practical effect.

On the other side, shukr should not be restricted to a fixed set of religious acts. The form of the response depends on the type of gift and the context. Every gift creates a responsibility fitting to itself.

8. Methodological Note

In studying the root SH K R, three levels must remain distinct from one another.

Direct Meaning

Recognizing a gift, good, or guidance and giving a fitting response to it.

Contextual Manifestation

Action, worship, right use of provision, acceptance of guidance, justice in power, or faithfulness in trial.

Wider Reflection

Ethical, social, and theological consequences that are gained from the collection of verses.

For example, from the verse:

اعْمَلُوا آلَ دَاوُودَ شُكْرًا

Act, family of Dawud, as shukr.

Saba 34:13

one can understand that shukr is not only verbal; but the word shukr should not be translated directly as "righteous action" in every verse.

Likewise, the image of an animal in whose body the effect of food appears is a witness to the presence of the meaning of appearing effect; but one should not say that shukr only means yield, growth, or increase.

Shukr in hardship must also be understood carefully. The Qur'an does not force the human being to call oppression itself, illness itself, or calamity itself a blessing. A person can be thankful in the midst of hardship for what still remains available, for guidance, for the possibility of return, for the power to endure, or for help that has arrived; but suffering should not be called a "hidden blessing" without reason.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

a. Shukr in Response to Pardon and a New Opportunity

ثُمَّ عَفَوْنَا عَنْكُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

Then We pardoned you after that, so that you might give shukr.

Al-Baqarah 2:52

Shukr here is the response to pardon and a new opportunity.

It is not only a feeling of gratitude. A community that has been pardoned must show a behavior different from its past.

The same structure appears after they are raised again after death:

ثُمَّ بَعَثْنَاكُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ مَوْتِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

Then We raised you after your death, so that you might give shukr.

Al-Baqarah 2:56

Shukr here is the response to the return of life and to a fresh opportunity.

b. Shukr in Contrast with Kufr

فَاذْكُرُونِي أَذْكُرْكُمْ وَاشْكُرُوا لِي وَلَا تَكْفُرُونِ

So remember Me and I will remember you; and give shukr to Me, and do not cover truth toward Me.

Al-Baqarah 2:152

The placement of "give shukr" opposite "do not commit kufr" shows that the contrast is deeper than thanking and bad manners.

Shukr in this verse is a positive response to The God, to His remembrance, and to His guidance. Kufr, in the opposite direction, is covering, rejecting, or making this relationship ineffective.

c. Shukr in Response to Provision

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ وَاشْكُرُوا لِلَّهِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ

O you who have faith, eat from the pure things We have provided for you, and give shukr to The God, if it is Him alone you worship.

Al-Baqarah 2:172

Shukr here is placed beside eating from pure provision and worshiping The God.

The right response to provision is recognizing its source and using it in a pure and responsible way.

In another verse it says:

فَكُلُوا مِمَّا رَزَقَكُمُ اللَّهُ حَلَالًا طَيِّبًا وَاشْكُرُوا نِعْمَتَ اللَّهِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ

So eat from what The God has provided you, lawful and pure, and give shukr for the blessing of The God, if it is Him alone you worship.

An-Nahl 16:114

Here the blessing itself is the object of shukr. The blessing should not be denied, polluted, or turned away from its right direction.

d. Shukr in Response to Guidance

وَلِتُكَبِّرُوا اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَاكُمْ وَلَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

And so that you may magnify The God for what He guided you to, and so that you might give shukr.

Al-Baqarah 2:185

Guidance here is itself a gift.

Shukr for guidance is not only saying thanks; it is accepting and living by the path that has become clear.

e. Shukr in Response to Cognitive Abilities

وَاللَّهُ أَخْرَجَكُمْ مِنْ بُطُونِ أُمَّهَاتِكُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ شَيْئًا وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَالْأَفْئِدَةَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

And The God brought you out from the bellies of your mothers while you knew nothing, and He made for you hearing, sights, and hearts, so that you might give shukr.

An-Nahl 16:78

In this context, shukr is connected with the right use of the tools of knowing.

Hearing, sight, and heart are not only biological possibilities; they are means of receiving, discerning, and responding.

But shukr should not be translated directly as "productive use of the senses." Their right use is a manifestation of shukr.

f. Shukr During Power and Testing

هَٰذَا مِنْ فَضْلِ رَبِّي لِيَبْلُوَنِي أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ

This is from the favor of my Lord, to test me: will I give shukr or will I cover truth?

An-Naml 27:40

In this verse, blessing is not only reward; it is also a test.

More power and more access do not automatically make the human being thankful. What determines the matter is the response that the human being gives to that power.

The verse continues:

وَمَنْ شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشْكُرُ لِنَفْسِهِ وَمَنْ كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّي غَنِيٌّ كَرِيمٌ

And whoever gives shukr gives shukr only for himself; and whoever covers truth, then my Lord is Free of need and Generous.

An-Naml 27:40

Shukr adds nothing to The God. Its benefit returns to the human being, because shukr sets the human being's relationship with the truth of the gift and its responsibility right.

g. Shukr in the Wisdom of Luqman

وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا لُقْمَانَ الْحِكْمَةَ أَنِ اشْكُرْ لِلَّهِ

And We certainly gave Luqman wisdom: give shukr to The God.

Luqman 31:12

Shukr in this verse is connected with wisdom.

Seeing the gift, recognizing its source, and giving the right response to it are part of wisdom.

Then it says:

وَمَنْ يَشْكُرْ فَإِنَّمَا يَشْكُرُ لِنَفْسِهِ وَمَنْ كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَنِيٌّ حَمِيدٌ

And whoever gives shukr gives shukr only for himself; and whoever covers truth, then The God is Free of need, Praised.

Luqman 31:12

The contrast of shukr and kufr appears again in the context of gift and wisdom.

h. Shukr Toward The God and Toward Parents

أَنِ اشْكُرْ لِي وَلِوَالِدَيْكَ إِلَيَّ الْمَصِيرُ

Give shukr to Me and to your parents; to Me is the final return.

Luqman 31:14

This verse shows that shukr is not used only in the relationship between the human being and The God. A human being can also have a thankful response toward those who played a role in bringing good and upbringing to him.

But shukr toward parents is not on the same level as shukr toward The God. The verse itself, with:

إِلَيَّ الْمَصِيرُ

To Me is the final return.

Luqman 31:14

clarifies the final point of return.

The following verses also show that obedience to parents is not absolute and unrestricted.

i. Shukr as Action

اعْمَلُوا آلَ دَاوُودَ شُكْرًا وَقَلِيلٌ مِنْ عِبَادِيَ الشَّكُورُ

Act, family of Dawud, as shukr; and few of My servants are shakur.

Saba 34:13

This verse is one of the clearest witnesses for the practical dimension of shukr.

The blessings given to the family of Dawud had to be answered in their action.

Acting as shukr does not mean producing the greatest material result; it means using the blessing rightly in a direction compatible with justice, worship, and responsibility.

j. Shukr and Increase

لَئِنْ شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ

If you give shukr, I will surely increase you.

Ibrahim 14:7

The verse does not state exactly what is increased.

The context may bring increase of blessing or benefit close to mind, but the translation should not add to the text what the expression has not explicitly stated.

What is clear is that shukr is connected with divine increase, but this increase should not be restricted only to material possession.

k. Shukr and the Guidance of the Path

إِنَّا هَدَيْنَاهُ السَّبِيلَ إِمَّا شَاكِرًا وَإِمَّا كَفُورًا

We guided him to the path, whether he becomes shakir or very covering and ungrateful.

Al-Insan 76:3

In this verse, shukr is the positive response to the path that has been shown, and being kafur is the negative response to that same guidance.

So shukr is not only a reaction to material blessing; it is also the human being's response to the clarification of the path.

l. Shukr Without Seeking Human Reward

إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ اللَّهِ لَا نُرِيدُ مِنْكُمْ جَزَاءً وَلَا شُكُورًا

We feed you only for the face of The God; we want from you neither recompense nor thanks.

Al-Insan 76:9

شُكُورًا in this verse, with dammah on the shin, means thanks or a thankful response, not the adjective "very thankful."

The doers of good do not make their action dependent on people's appreciation.

10. Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

شَكَرَ / يَشْكُرُ

The verb shukr is usually used for the human being's response to blessing, favor, rescue, or guidance.

This verb sometimes comes with the letter لِـ:

وَاشْكُرُوا لِلَّهِ

Give shukr to The God.

And sometimes the blessing itself is its direct object:

وَاشْكُرُوا نِعْمَتَ اللَّهِ

Give shukr for the blessing of The God.

This difference shows that attention can be directed both to the giver and to the gift received.

شَاكِر

Shakir is one who stands in the position of shukr.

About the human being, this form is placed opposite kafur. Therefore, it is not merely the description of someone who says thanks; it is the description of someone who gives a positive response to guidance and gift.

شَكُور

The form shakur indicates abundance, stability, or prominence of shukr.

Nuh is called:

عَبْدًا شَكُورًا

A servant who was shakur.

Al-Isra 17:3

meaning a servant whose prominent and stable quality was shukr.

In the phrase:

وَقَلِيلٌ مِنْ عِبَادِيَ الشَّكُورُ

the speech is about a stable state that appears in the servant's life and action.

شُكْر

It is the act or state of shukr itself.

شُكُور

With dammah on the shin, it is thanks or a thankful response, as in:

وَلَا شُكُورًا

مَشْكُور

About the one who wants the Hereafter and strives for it with fitting striving, the Qur'an says:

فَأُولَٰئِكَ كَانَ سَعْيُهُمْ مَشْكُورًا

Those are the ones whose striving was accepted and answered.

Al-Isra 17:19

It is also said to the people of the Garden:

إِنَّ هَٰذَا كَانَ لَكُمْ جَزَاءً وَكَانَ سَعْيُكُمْ مَشْكُورًا

This was a recompense for you, and your striving was accepted and answered.

Al-Insan 76:22

Mashkur means that their striving before The God was not overlooked and was not wasted.

11. Derivatives Related to Divine Attributes

The root SH K R is attributed to The God in the forms Shakir and Shakur in the Qur'an.

This use does not carry exactly the same meaning as it does for the human being, because The God does not receive a blessing from outside Himself and has no need.

The Qur'an says:

وَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ شَاكِرٌ عَلِيمٌ

And whoever willingly adds good, then The God is Shakir and Knowing.

Al-Baqarah 2:158

The God being Shakir comes in relation to the good of the human being. He does not overlook good, He knows it, accepts it, and responds to it.

It also says:

مَا يَفْعَلُ اللَّهُ بِعَذَابِكُمْ إِنْ شَكَرْتُمْ وَآمَنْتُمْ وَكَانَ اللَّهُ شَاكِرًا عَلِيمًا

What would The God do with your punishment, if you give shukr and have faith? And The God is Shakir and Knowing.

An-Nisa 4:147

The joining of Shakir with Knowing is important. His response to human action rests on complete knowledge, not on limited appearance or deficient judgment.

About His being Shakur, it says:

إِنَّهُ غَفُورٌ شَكُورٌ

Indeed, He is Forgiving, Shakur.

Fatir 35:30

And:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ شَكُورٌ

Indeed, The God is Forgiving, Shakur.

Ash-Shuraa 42:23

In the verse of Ash-Shuraa, before the description Shakur, it says:

وَمَنْ يَقْتَرِفْ حَسَنَةً نَزِدْ لَهُ فِيهَا حُسْنًا

And whoever acquires a good deed, We increase for him good within it.

Ash-Shuraa 42:23

So The God being Shakur can indicate that He does not overlook the limited good of the human being, accepts it, and increases it.

But increasing should not become a fixed part of the translation of every use of Shakir and Shakur. The safer core is this:

He recognizes the good of servants, does not overlook it, does not waste it, and gives it a fitting response.

The translation "grateful" about The God may create the misconception that He, like the human being, is the receiver of a blessing.

For Forqan, it is better to preserve the base word Shakir or Shakur in translation and explain:

The One who does not overlook the good and striving of servants and responds to it.

12. Grammatical and Structural Notes

Shukr with the Letter لِـ

In the Qur'an, constructions like these appear:

اشْكُرُوا لِي

اشْكُرُوا لِلَّهِ

اشْكُرُوا لَهُ

The letter لِـ shows the direction and destination of shukr. The thankful response returns toward the giver.

Shukr with a Direct Object

In some verses, the blessing itself is the object of the verb:

أَنْ أَشْكُرَ نِعْمَتَكَ

وَاشْكُرُوا نِعْمَتَ اللَّهِ

Grammatically, the blessing is the object of shukr. In natural English, one can say:

that I may give shukr for Your blessing.

Or:

that I may be thankful before Your blessing.

Shukr in a Purpose Construction

The phrase:

لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

comes repeatedly after the mention of blessing, guidance, rescue, or human capacities.

This construction presents shukr as the invited response and the aim of the address.

La'alla should not be interpreted as doubt or ignorance on the part of The God. From the angle of address, the construction expresses the openness of possibility and the invitation of the human being to actualize that response.

شُكْرًا in Saba 34:13

In:

اعْمَلُوا آلَ دَاوُودَ شُكْرًا

shukran can clarify the state or purpose of the action:

Act as shukr.

Or:

Perform action of the kind of shukr.

In both cases, the connection of shukr with action is clear.

Contrasting Structure

The Qur'an places shukr directly opposite kufr several times:

أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ

And:

إِمَّا شَاكِرًا وَإِمَّا كَفُورًا

This contrasting structure is one of the most important Qur'anic ways of clarifying the semantic field of both roots.

13. SH K R Compared with K F R

The root SH K R is placed directly opposite K F R in several verses.

Kufr, in its linguistic core, is connected with covering and hiding. In Qur'anic contexts, this covering can occur toward truth, signs, guidance, or blessing.

Shukr, in the opposite direction, does not disregard the gift or truth that has been received. It recognizes it and gives a fitting response to it.

This tendency can be expressed as follows:

Shukr: recognizing a gift or truth and making a fitting response to it appear.

Kufr: covering, disregarding, or rejecting what has reached the human being or become clear to him.

But this contrast should not be turned into "conductivity" and "insulation." Such images may be useful for a limited explanation, but they do not form the meaning of the root.

Also, not every use of kufr is merely ingratitude, and not every use of shukr is merely acknowledging blessing. The precise meaning of each is specified by context.

14. SH K R Compared with H M D

Shukr and hamd may both come close to thanks or praise in English, but their semantic fields are not identical.

Hamd is closer to praise on the basis of goodness, perfection, or a praiseworthy act. A person can make hamd for someone because of a good quality or worthy act, even if that good did not reach him directly.

Shukr is usually before a gift, ihsan, or good that has been received. In it there is a relationship between receiver, gift, and response.

So one can cautiously say:

Hamd: praising goodness and perfection;

Shukr: responding to a gift and received good.

This difference is not absolute. Hamd is also not only verbal speech, and shukr is also not always restricted to receiving material benefit.

In Forqan translation, hamd and shukr should not be reduced to one single word without necessity.

16. Conclusion

The root SH K R in the Qur'an is not restricted to saying thanks or expressing satisfaction.

Shukr begins with recognizing a gift, good, or guidance, but it does not stop at inner recognition. What has been received or made clear to the human being must affect his response.

The semantic core of this root may be expressed as:

Recognizing a gift or good, not disregarding it, and showing a right and fitting response to it.

The material uses of this root in Arabic, such as the appearance of the effect of food in growth or milk, help explain the element of appearing effect; but these images are not the complete and final definition of shukr.

Shukr does not mean obtaining the greatest output from the smallest input. The measure of shukr is not the abundance of the result; it is the correctness and fittingness of the response.

In the Qur'an, the subject of shukr may be provision, guidance, rescue, pardon, power, hearing, sight, heart, life, parents, or the opportunity to act. The thankful response also takes different forms depending on the type of gift.

Shukr stands opposite K F R. Shukr recognizes the gift and truth and gives a fitting response to it; kufr covers it, disregards it, or refuses the right response to it.

Shukr is also not identical with hamd. Hamd is closer to praising goodness and perfection, while shukr has a more direct relationship with a received gift and the response to it.

The verse:

اعْمَلُوا آلَ دَاوُودَ شُكْرًا

Act, family of Dawud, as shukr.

Saba 34:13

shows that shukr can be realized in action.

The verses:

أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ

Will I give shukr, or will I cover truth?

An-Naml 27:40

and:

إِمَّا شَاكِرًا وَإِمَّا كَفُورًا

Whether he becomes shakir or very covering and ungrateful.

Al-Insan 76:3

also show that shukr is one of the two fundamental stances of the human being before gift and guidance.

When Shakir and Shakur are used about The God, the meaning is not receiving blessing or neediness. He does not overlook the good and striving of servants, accepts it, does not waste it, and responds to it.

A distinction must be kept between شَكُور as an adjective and شُكُور meaning thanks. The difference in the vowel of the shin creates an important morphological and semantic difference.

Final suggested definition for Forqan:

Shukr: recognizing a gift, good, or guidance, attributing it to its proper source, not disregarding its value, and showing a right and fitting response in awareness, speech, or action.
