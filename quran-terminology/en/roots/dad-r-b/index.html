Linguistic Note

The semantic core of Ḍ R B may be described as creating a decisive and perceptible effect on something or within a situation. Sometimes this effect is physical contact, as in striking with a staff. Sometimes it is movement, as in traveling through the land. Sometimes it is speech, as in setting forth an example. Sometimes it is placing something upon something, as in casting a covering. Sometimes it is creating distance or a barrier.

For this reason, Ḍ R B must not be flattened into one English word. If every occurrence is translated “strike,” verses like ضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا (ḍaraba Allāhu mathalan) and ضَرَبْتُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ (ḍarabtum fī al-arḍ) are damaged. If the physical meaning is denied entirely, verses like اضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ الْحَجَرَ (iḍrib bi-ʿaṣāka al-ḥajar) are also misread.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of Ḍ R B is decisive effect. Context determines the form of that effect: striking, placing, setting out, setting forth an example, creating a barrier, covering, turning aside, or suspending a state.

In this root, outcome matters. Ḍarb is an act that changes the situation, whether in a body, a path, a listener’s understanding, or a social relationship.

Material Image

The material image is a hand or tool descending upon something and leaving an effect. That effect may produce sound, breakage, splitting, movement, separation, covering, or a change of state.

From this image, broader meanings emerge. When an example is “struck,” a meaning is impressed upon the listener’s mind. When one “strikes in the land,” one leaves stillness and enters movement. When a wall is “struck” between two groups, relation and passage are interrupted.

Historical and Semitic Note

In classical Arabic, Ḍ R B has a clear meaning of striking and bringing one thing into contact with another. This should not be denied. But Arabic also uses the root for setting forth examples, traveling in the land, pitching a tent, minting coins, placing barriers, and taking a concrete action.

Comparisons with other Semitic languages must be handled carefully. Sound similarity alone is not decisive proof. Such comparisons may only show that fields like impact, separation, driving away, tearing away, and changing a state are close in imagery. For Qur’anic interpretation, the primary evidence remains Qur’anic Arabic and the structure of the verses themselves.

Ḍarb as Physical Striking

In 2:60, the Qur’an says: فَقُلْنَا اضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ الْحَجَرَ (fa-qulnā iḍrib bi-ʿaṣāka al-ḥajar). Here اضْرِب (iḍrib) means strike with the staff, because the instrument and physical context are explicit.

In a battle setting, 47:4 says: فَإِذَا لَقِيتُمُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا فَضَرْبَ الرِّقَابِ (fa-idhā laqītumu alladhīna kafarū fa-ḍarba al-riqāb). The physical meaning exists and must not be erased.

Setting an Example

In 2:26, the Qur’an says: إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَسْتَحْيِي أَن يَضْرِبَ مَثَلًا مَّا بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا (inna Allāha lā yastaḥyī an yaḍriba mathalan mā baʿūḍatan fa-mā fawqahā).

No physical striking is involved. يَضْرِبَ مَثَلًا (yaḍriba mathalan) means to set forth an example and place it before the listener’s understanding. The effect remains, but it is upon perception and thought.

In 16:75, the Qur’an also says: ضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا عَبْدًا مَّمْلُوكًا لَّا يَقْدِرُ عَلَىٰ شَيْءٍ (ḍaraba Allāhu mathalan ʿabdan mamlūkan lā yaqdiru ʿalā shay').

Traveling in the Land

In 4:101, the Qur’an says: وَإِذَا ضَرَبْتُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَلَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ أَن تَقْصُرُوا مِنَ الصَّلَاةِ (wa-idhā ḍarabtum fī al-arḍi fa-laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an taqṣurū mina al-ṣalāh).

ضَرَبْتُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ (ḍarabtum fī al-arḍ) means traveling and setting out in the land. One leaves the fixed place, traverses a path, and exits a state of stillness.

In 73:20, the Qur’an says: وَآخَرُونَ يَضْرِبُونَ فِي الْأَرْضِ يَبْتَغُونَ مِن فَضْلِ اللَّهِ (wa-ākharūna yaḍribūna fī al-arḍi yabtaghūna min faḍli Allāh).

Setting Upon, Casting, or Covering

In 2:61, the Qur’an says: وَضُرِبَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ الذِّلَّةُ وَالْمَسْكَنَةُ (wa-ḍuribat ʿalayhimu al-dhillatu wa-l-maskanah). Humiliation and destitution were cast over them and imposed upon them.

In 18:11, the Qur’an says: فَضَرَبْنَا عَلَىٰ آذَانِهِمْ فِي الْكَهْفِ سِنِينَ عَدَدًا (fa-ḍarabnā ʿalā ādhānihim fī al-kahfi sinīna ʿadadā). A blocking state was placed over their ears.

In 24:31, the Qur’an says: وَلْيَضْرِبْنَ بِخُمُرِهِنَّ عَلَىٰ جُيُوبِهِنَّ (wa-l-yaḍribna bi-khumurihinna ʿalā juyūbihinna). This does not mean physical beating; it means casting their coverings over the chest opening.

Striking the Feet

In the same verse of An-Nur, the Qur’an says: وَلَا يَضْرِبْنَ بِأَرْجُلِهِنَّ لِيُعْلَمَ مَا يُخْفِينَ مِن زِينَتِهِنَّ (wa-lā yaḍribna bi-arjulihinna li-yuʿlama mā yukhfīna min zīnatihinna).

Here ḍarb refers to striking the feet, but the point is the effect: a sound or motion that makes hidden adornment known. Again, the core is perceptible effect.

Placing a Barrier or Distance

In 57:13, the Qur’an says: فَضُرِبَ بَيْنَهُم بِسُورٍ لَّهُ بَابٌ (fa-ḍuriba baynahum bi-sūrin lahu bāb). A wall or barrier was placed between them.

In 43:5, the Qur’an says: أَفَنَضْرِبُ عَنكُمُ الذِّكْرَ صَفْحًا (afa-naḍribu ʿankumu al-dhikra ṣafḥā). With the preposition عَن (ʿan), the meaning moves toward turning aside, setting away, and withdrawing.

Ḍarb in An-Nisa 4:34

4:34 is one of the most sensitive uses of this root: وَاللَّاتِي تَخَافُونَ نُشُوزَهُنَّ فَعِظُوهُنَّ وَاهْجُرُوهُنَّ فِي الْمَضَاجِعِ وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ (wa-allātī takhāfūna nushūzahunna fa-ʿiẓūhunna wa-uhjurūhunna fī al-maḍājiʿi wa-iḍribūhunna).

The common traditional reading takes وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ (wa-iḍribūhunna) as “beat them.” In Arabic, the physical meaning exists and cannot be declared impossible. The question is which meaning fits this verse, this structure, and the Qur’an as a whole.

The verse gives three stages: admonition, bed-separation, and then ḍarb. If the third stage is physical beating, the verse suddenly moves from speech and bed-distance to an undefined bodily permission without specifying instrument, limit, intensity, witness, authority of diagnosis, or the woman’s right of defense.

If وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ (wa-iḍribūhunna) is understood within the field of decisive action, suspending the normal state, stronger distancing, and moving the crisis toward arbitration, its link with the next verse becomes more natural: وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ شِقَاقَ بَيْنِهِمَا فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا مِّنْ أَهْلِهِ وَحَكَمًا مِّنْ أَهْلِهَا (wa-in khiftum shiqāqa baynihimā fa-bʿathū ḥakaman min ahlihi wa-ḥakaman min ahlihā).

Why the Beating Reading Is Problematic

If وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ (wa-iḍribūhunna) means physical beating, the verse does not specify the instrument, intensity, limit, witness, safeguard, or the woman’s right of defense. Yet the Qur’an does not usually speak without limits in matters of body and honor.

In 5:45, it says: وَالْجُرُوحَ قِصَاصٌ (wa-l-jurūḥa qiṣāṣ). Bodily injury is not treated lightly. In bodily punishment, 24:2 gives number and subject explicitly: فَاجْلِدُوا كُلَّ وَاحِدٍ مِّنْهُمَا مِائَةَ جَلْدَةٍ (fa-ijlidū kulla wāḥidin minhumā mi'ata jaldah).

The ending of An-Nisa 4:34 restrains male power: فَإِنْ أَطَعْنَكُمْ فَلَا تَبْغُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ سَبِيلًا ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيًّا كَبِيرًا (fa-in aṭaʿnakum fa-lā tabghū ʿalayhinna sabīlan inna Allāha kāna ʿaliyyan kabīrā). If they return from the crisis, no path of pressure, oppression, or revenge may be sought against them.

Qiwamah, Nushuz, and Darb in This Context

In 4:34, قَوَّامُونَ (qawwāmūn) points to upholding responsibility, not male monarchy over women. If qiwamah is protective responsibility, it cannot become a right to harm a woman’s body.

قَانِتَاتٌ (qānitāt) should not be reduced to “obedient to husbands.” Qunut in the Qur’an is directed toward The God. The righteous woman in this verse is described first in relation to The God.

نُشُوز (nushūz) is not every disagreement, objection, or independent judgment by a woman. It is a departure from balance, a breach of the righteous covenant, and a serious relational crisis. The verse responds first with reminder, then bed-separation, then decisive action to suspend the normal state of the relationship and move toward arbitration.

Distinguishing Qur’anic Uses

In 2:60, ḍarb is striking the stone with a staff. In 47:4, it appears in a battle context. In 2:26 and 16:75, it means setting an example.

In 4:101 and 73:20, ḍarb in the land means travel. In 24:31, casting the khumur over the juyub means covering. In 57:13, it means placing a barrier between two groups. In 4:34, the verse structure and the following arbitration verse make the decisive nonphysical reading stronger than the beating reading.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In ضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا (ḍaraba Allāhu mathalan), the object مَثَلًا (mathalan) shows that the meaning is setting forth an example. In ضَرَبْتُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ (ḍarabtum fī al-arḍ), فِي (fī) forms the field of movement. In ضُرِبَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ الذِّلَّةُ (ḍuribat ʿalayhimu al-dhillah), على (ʿalā) strengthens the sense of something being cast over and imposed.

In فَضُرِبَ بَيْنَهُم بِسُورٍ (fa-ḍuriba baynahum bi-sūr), بَيْنَهُم (baynahum) shows barrier-making. In أَفَنَضْرِبُ عَنكُمُ الذِّكْرَ (afa-naḍribu ʿankumu al-dhikr), عن (ʿan) moves the meaning toward turning away and setting aside.

In وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ (wa-iḍribūhunna), no preposition appears and the object is direct. Linguistically, the physical reading is possible, but it is not the only possibility. Because the root has a wide Qur’anic field, context must decide what kind of decisive effect or action is meant.

Difference From Nearby Roots

Ḍ R B is close to Ṭ R Q, but not the same. Ṭarq is closer to knocking, coming by night, and path. Ḍarb is wider and includes striking, traveling, setting an example, and creating a barrier.

Ḍ R B differs from L Ṭ M, J L D, W K Z, Q T L, and B Ṭ SH. Laṭm is closer to slapping; jald is striking the skin or flogging; wakz is a sudden bodily blow; qatl is taking life; baṭsh is a severe assault with power. Ḍarb may be bodily, but it is not always bodily.

Ḍ R B also differs from H J R and F R Q. Hajr is leaving or distancing; farq is separating and distinguishing. Ḍarb may create separation or a barrier, but its root-core is decisive effect.

Forqan Translation Method

In Forqan translation, Ḍ R B should not be locked into one equivalent. ضَرَبَ مَثَلًا (ḍaraba mathalan) should be “set forth an example” or “gave an example.” ضَرَبَ فِي الْأَرْضِ (ḍaraba fī al-arḍ) should be “traveled in the land” or “set out in the land.” وَلْيَضْرِبْنَ بِخُمُرِهِنَّ (wa-l-yaḍribna bi-khumurihinna) should be “let them cast their coverings over their chest openings.”

In An-Nisa 4:34, “beat them” is socially dangerous and Qur’anically dependent on restrictions the verse itself does not give. A more cautious and Qur’an-coherent rendering is: “suspend the normal state of the relationship decisively,” “take decisive separation,” or in explanatory translation, “distance decisively and move the crisis toward arbitration.”

Translation Suggestions

  • For ضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا (ḍaraba Allāhu mathalan): The God set forth an example; The God gave an example.
  • For ضَرَبْتُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ (ḍarabtum fī al-arḍ): you traveled in the land; you set out in the land.
  • For اضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ الْحَجَرَ (iḍrib bi-ʿaṣāka al-ḥajar): strike the stone with your staff.
  • For ضُرِبَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ الذِّلَّةُ (ḍuribat ʿalayhimu al-dhillah): humiliation was cast over them; humiliation was imposed upon them.
  • For فَضَرَبْنَا عَلَىٰ آذَانِهِمْ (fa-ḍarabnā ʿalā ādhānihim): We placed a barrier over their ears; We cast a blocking state over their ears.
  • For وَلْيَضْرِبْنَ بِخُمُرِهِنَّ عَلَىٰ جُيُوبِهِنَّ (wa-l-yaḍribna bi-khumurihinna ʿalā juyūbihinna): let them cast their coverings over their chest openings.
  • For وَلَا يَضْرِبْنَ بِأَرْجُلِهِنَّ (wa-lā yaḍribna bi-arjulihinna): let them not strike their feet.
  • For فَضُرِبَ بَيْنَهُم بِسُورٍ (fa-ḍuriba baynahum bi-sūr): a wall was placed between them; a barrier was set between them.
  • For وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ (wa-iḍribūhunna): suspend the normal state of the relationship decisively; take decisive separation; distance decisively and move the crisis toward arbitration.

Summary

The root Ḍ R B in the Qur’an points to decisive and perceptible effect. Sometimes this is physical striking; sometimes setting forth an example, traveling, casting something over something, covering, placing a barrier, or turning aside and interrupting a state. Therefore, this root must not be reduced to one fixed equivalent, especially “beating.”

In An-Nisa 4:34, the physical meaning is not linguistically impossible. Yet the verse context, the staged handling of the crisis, qiwamah as responsibility, nushuz as serious relational rupture, the restraining end of the verse, and the immediate move to arbitration in the next verse make the nonphysical reading stronger.
