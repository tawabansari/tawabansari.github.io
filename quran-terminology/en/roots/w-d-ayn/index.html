Root و د ع — Leaving, resting, and entrusting

Qur’anic and lexical forms

(daʿ)

The Arabic root و د ع concerns leaving, ceasing to pursue something, allowing rest, and depositing something elsewhere. Its uses include leaving a person or activity, bidding farewell, ease and rest, and entrusting something for safekeeping.

In the Quran, the root occurs four times in three principal forms: دَعْ, وَدَّعَكَ, and مُسْتَوْدَع, the last occurring twice.

These uses differ in meaning. One verse commands leaving aside harm; another declares that The God has not forsaken His Messenger; two others speak of creatures' places of settlement and deposit.

Understanding this family requires distinguishing leaving, allowing rest, and entrusting. All may involve relinquishing direct involvement, but the object and sentence structure determine the precise meaning.

1. The root in early Arabic

Early Arabic witnesses illustrate three important uses: ceasing an activity, allowing something to rest, and entrusting something for safekeeping.

Leaving and desisting

A poem attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Dhū al-Iṣbaʿ al-ʿAdwānī reads: Transmissions vary, including لم versus لن and أضع versus أضق; these differences do not alter the meaning of تدعا in this witness.

إِنَّكُمَا صَاحِبَيَّ لَنْ تَدَعَا

innakumā ṣāḥibayya lan tadaʿā

لَوْمِي وَمَهْمَا أَضِقْ فَلَنْ تَسَعَا

lawmī wa-mahmā aḍiq fa-lan tasaʿā

“My two companions, you will not cease blaming me; however straitened I become, you will not grant me relief.”

Here تَدَعَا derives from وَدَعَ يَدَعُ and means leaving or desisting from an activity.

The poet complains that his companions will not stop reproaching him. The point is ending a behavior, rather than leaving a place or physically separating.

This usage shows that language attributed to the pre-Islamic period employed the root for ceasing an activity that could otherwise continue.

The meaning here is abandoning reproach or ceasing to blame, rather than forgetting, forgiving, or approving someone's conduct.

Allowing rest and ease

In the same collection attributed to Dhū al-Iṣbaʿ al-ʿAdwānī, the poet describes his young horse:

أُقْصِرُ مِنْ قَيْدِهِ وَأُودِعُهُ

uqṣiru min qaydihi wa-ūdiʿuhu

حَتَّى إِذَا السَّرْبُ رِيعَ أَوْ فَزِعَا

ḥattā idhā al-sirbu rīʿa aw faziʿā

“I shorten its tether and let it rest, until the company is alarmed or frightened.” Shortening the tether concerns the restraint itself; it should not be translated as reducing restrictions.

He describes a horse that he does not needlessly exhaust and keeps ready for a time of need. Later in the ode, it moves ahead of the other horses when danger comes.

Here أُودِعُهُ means allowing the horse to rest and sparing it unnecessary exertion.

This sense relates to دَعَة, used in Arabic for ease, rest, and life without hardship.

Leaving something alone does not always imply neglect or abandonment. It may allow a creature to rest without unnecessary disturbance.

This matters because a word such as “leaving” can suggest separation or severed ties, whereas some members of this Arabic family concern ease and safekeeping.

Entrusting and safeguarding a trust

An ode attributed to the pre-Islamic poet ʿAlqamah ibn ʿAbadah opens:

هَلْ مَا عَلِمْتَ وَمَا اسْتُودِعْتَ مَكْتُومُ

hal mā ʿalimta wa-mā istūdiʿta maktūmu

أَمْ حَبْلُهَا إِذْ نَأَتْكَ الْيَوْمَ مَصْرُومُ

am ḥabluhā idh naʾatka al-yawma maṣrūmu

“Is what you know and what was entrusted to you kept concealed, or is her bond now severed since she has gone far from you?”

The poet speaks of a relationship that distance and separation may have ended.

اسْتُودِعْتَ refers to something entrusted to a person; in this context, secrets and matters concerning the relationship.

Entrusting a secret differs from abandoning it. The person who confides in another expects that person to preserve the secret and refrain from disclosure.

This illustrates another branch of و د ع: something passes beyond the depositor's immediate control but is entrusted to another for safekeeping.

Early collections attribute this ode to ʿAlqamah. As with other poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period, however, the poet's dating alone does not establish a certain date for each line.

What the historical witnesses show

These three witnesses show that the family had more than one use in language attributed to the pre-Islamic period.

تَدَعَا expresses desisting from conduct, أُودِعُهُ allowing a horse to rest, and اسْتُودِعْتَ having something entrusted to oneself.

A semantic connection can be proposed: withdrawing something from pursuit, pressure, or direct control, whether by ceasing an act, allowing a creature to rest, or entrusting something to another.

This connection does not prove that every sense historically developed from a single original image. Each branch must be studied through independent evidence and actual usage.

2. Forms and semantic core

Important Arabic forms include:

وَدَعَ: left, relinquished, or ceased pursuing something. Other uses of the family concern settling and resting.

يَدَعُ: leaves or relinquishes; the imperfect of وَدَعَ.

دَعْ: leave, relinquish, or desist; the imperative of the same verb.

وَدَّعَ: bade farewell, saw someone off, or left someone. Some contexts emphasize forsaking or ending companionship.

تَوْدِيع: bidding farewell or seeing someone off.

وَدَاع: parting and farewell.

وَدِيعَة: something deposited with another for safekeeping; a trust.

أَوْدَعَ: entrusted or deposited something with another. Other uses express allowing rest.

اسْتَوْدَعَ: entrusted something for safekeeping or assigned its care to another.

مُسْتَوْدَع: something deposited, or a place where something is deposited for safekeeping.

دَعَة: ease, rest, and freedom from hardship.

وَدِيع: calm, gentle, and at ease.

مُوَادَعَة: mutual cessation of hostilities, a truce, or agreement to refrain from hostility.

These forms are recorded in Arabic, but not all occur in the Quran.

Dictionaries also record وَدَع and وَدَعَة for cowries or similar marine shells used as beads and ornaments. This use is absent from the Quran, and its derivational relationship to leaving, resting, or entrusting is not clearly established. The shell sense should therefore not enter the core definition of the Quranic usages.

Semantic core

For the principal branches relevant to the Quran, the following core may be proposed:

Leaving a person, activity, or thing by discontinuing pursuit, companionship, or direct control, or depositing something elsewhere for safekeeping.

The kind of relinquishment matters.

A person may cease an activity.

Someone may leave another person and part from them.

Someone may allow something to rest.

Someone may entrust property or a secret to another to keep it safe.

These uses are related, but they cannot all be translated simply as “abandoning.”

Depositing something with another does not necessarily mean treating it as ownerless or worthless. Entrusting can involve confidence and an expectation of care.

By contrast, ceasing a behavior may simply mean ending it, without any deposit or safekeeping being involved.

The shared core helps explain family relationships, but each derivative requires its own analysis.

3. Quranic usages

A. Al-Aḥzāb 33:48 — Leaving aside opponents' harm

وَلَا تُطِعِ الْكَافِرِينَ وَالْمُنَافِقِينَ وَدَعْ أَذَاهُمْ وَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى اللَّهِ ۚ وَكَفَىٰ بِاللَّهِ وَكِيلًا

wa-lā tuṭiʿi al-kāfirīna wa-al-munāfiqīna wa-daʿ adhāhum wa-tawakkal ʿalā allāhi wa-kafā bi-allāhi wakīlā

Al-Aḥzāb 33:48

“Do not obey the disbelievers and hypocrites; leave aside their harm and rely on The God. The God suffices as trustee.”

دَعْ is the imperative of وَدَعَ يَدَعُ.

Its short appearance results from the omission of the root's first letter, wāw, in the imperative form.

It belongs to و د ع, not د ع و, the root of calling or invoking.

The object is أَذَاهُمْ, “their harm.” Grammatically, the pronoun can indicate those causing harm or those receiving it: leaving aside the harm they cause, or refraining from harming them in return. The discussion below follows the former reading without excluding the latter.

A direct rendering of دَعْ أَذَاهُمْ is:

“Leave aside their harm.”

What does this leaving aside involve?

The sentence must be read in context.

In Al-Aḥzāb 33:45, Muhammad is addressed as Prophet and assigned the functions of witnessing, bringing good news, and warning.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:46 speaks of calling toward The God and providing illumination.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:47 commands him to give good news to the believers.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:48 then places three commands together: do not obey disbelievers and hypocrites, leave aside their harm, and rely on The God.

This sequence indicates that the Prophet should not abandon his mission and principles or obey opponents because of their pressure and harm.

On the reading that they cause the harm, دَعْ أَذَاهُمْ directs him away from becoming absorbed in it or making it the basis of his response. On the other reading, it directs him to refrain from retaliatory harm.

دَعْ does not mean approving the harmful conduct.

Leaving aside their harm also differs from obeying them, which the verse explicitly forbids.

Without considering its addressee and context, this command should not become a universal ruling that every oppressed person must relinquish their rights and take no action against injustice.

The verse addresses the Prophet within his mission. Reliance and steadfastness under pressure invite reflection, but extending every detail to every situation requires independent Quranic evidence.

Here دَعْ means setting aside or leaving, rather than necessarily forgiving the offenders, forgetting injustice, or approving their conduct.

B. Al-Ḍuḥā 93:3 — Your Lord has not forsaken you

مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ وَمَا قَلَىٰ

mā waddaʿaka rabbuka wa-mā qalā

Al-Ḍuḥā 93:3

“Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor has He hated you.”

وَدَّعَكَ is a form II perfect verb with a second-person singular object pronoun.

رَبُّكَ is the subject, and the pronoun كَ in وَدَّعَكَ refers to Muhammad.

مَا وَدَّعَكَ negates the action: your Lord has not forsaken you.

Arabic وَدَّعَ can mean bidding farewell or leaving. An ordinary farewell may accompany travel or separation without implying hostility or a permanent rupture.

Here, however, the subject is the Lord's relationship with His Messenger, rather than an ordinary separation between two people.

“Has not forsaken” is therefore more suitable than “has not said goodbye.”

Distinguishing وَدَّعَكَ from قَلَىٰ

The verse contains two different verbs:

وَدَّعَكَ, from و د ع, means forsaking or leaving.

قَلَىٰ, from ق ل ي, means hating or feeling aversion.

They should not be treated as synonyms.

Someone can part from another without hatred. Conversely, hatred and aversion do not necessarily entail physical separation.

The Quran negates both meanings separately: neither forsaking nor hating.

The object of وَمَا قَلَىٰ is not repeated explicitly, but the preceding clause shows that the same addressee remains in view.

This structural difference should not make the translation appear to concern two unrelated subjects.

Connections within the surah

Al-Ḍuḥā 93:1 and Al-Ḍuḥā 93:2 swear by daylight and the night as it settles.

Al-Ḍuḥā 93:3 then declares that Muhammad has not been forsaken by his Lord.

Al-Ḍuḥā 93:4 speaks of the Hereafter being better for him, and Al-Ḍuḥā 93:5 promises his Lord's giving.

Al-Ḍuḥā 93:6, Al-Ḍuḥā 93:7, and Al-Ḍuḥā 93:8 then recall examples of divine care and guidance in his life.

This continuity shows that مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ reassures him of his Lord's continuing attention and care.

Historical reports sometimes associate the verse with an interruption in revelation. The verse itself gives neither the duration nor the historical details of such an interruption.

Those details need not be inserted into the translation to explain وَدَّعَكَ.

The precise meaning is not forsaking, rather than necessarily denying a particular interruption in revelation or asserting that no interval ever separated messages.

Form II alone does not establish intensity, repetition, or permanence of leaving. The known usage of the verb and its context determine the sentence's meaning.

C. Al-Anʿām 6:98 — Human settlement and deposit

وَهُوَ الَّذِي أَنْشَأَكُمْ مِنْ نَفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ فَمُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمُسْتَوْدَعٌ ۗ قَدْ فَصَّلْنَا الْآيَاتِ لِقَوْمٍ يَفْقَهُونَ

wa-huwa alladhī anshaʾakum min nafsin wāḥidatin fa-mustaqarrun wa-mustawdaʿun qad faṣṣalnā al-āyāti li-qawmin yafqahūn

Al-Anʿām 6:98

“He is the One who brought you into being from a single self; then a place of settlement and a place of deposit. We have explained the signs in detail for people who understand.”

مُسْتَوْدَعٌ belongs to form X.

Derived from اسْتَوْدَعَ, it can denote something deposited or the place where it is deposited.

Its companion مُسْتَقَرٌّ comes from ق ر ر and concerns settling and establishment.

Their pairing matters for understanding the meaning.

مُسْتَقَرّ denotes a place of settlement.

مُسْتَوْدَع denotes a place of deposit or something placed in that condition.

These concepts are related but not identical.

Something settled somewhere occupies that location. Something entrusted stands in a relationship of deposit to its location or custodian.

A deposit can be reclaimed or transferred, but مُسْتَوْدَع itself does not specify the time of transfer or length of stay.

Does مُسْتَوْدَع here mean the mother's womb?

Several interpretations have been proposed.

Some relate مُسْتَقَرّ to the mother's womb and مُسْتَوْدَع to a preceding location.

Others connect settlement and deposit with earthly life, death, and subsequent stages.

Grammatically, مُسْتَوْدَع can function as a place noun or passive participle.

Recognized readings also transmit مُسْتَقَرّ with a fatḥah on qāf and مُسْتَقِرّ with a kasrah. These can distinguish a place of settlement from a description of a person or being that has settled.

These morphological and grammatical differences do not by themselves determine which particular stage of human life each term denotes.

The verse names neither the womb, the father's loins, nor the grave.

None of these referents should therefore replace the lexical meaning in the base translation without sufficient evidence.

The context concerns human emergence from a single self and the signs of creation. The terms' relationship to human locations and stages of existence can accordingly be explored.

Deriving detailed reproductive biology or the structure of stages before and after death from these two words alone exceeds their direct meaning.

Why “place of deposit” matters

مُسْتَوْدَع is not simply another name for a fixed location alongside مُسْتَقَرّ. The former concerns depositing for safekeeping, whereas the latter emphasizes settling.

This distinction can help illuminate different human conditions within creation.

The language of entrusting alone does not establish a complete theory of bodily ownership, the nature of the self, or life after death in this verse.

The lexical meaning must remain clear, and ontological reflections must be assessed against independent evidence.

D. Hūd 11:6 — Divine knowledge of every creature's settlement and deposit

وَمَا مِنْ دَابَّةٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا عَلَى اللَّهِ رِزْقُهَا وَيَعْلَمُ مُسْتَقَرَّهَا وَمُسْتَوْدَعَهَا ۚ كُلٌّ فِي كِتَابٍ مُبِينٍ

wa-mā min dābbatin fī al-arḍi illā ʿalā allāhi rizquhā wa-yaʿlamu mustaqarrahā wa-mustawdaʿahā kullun fī kitābin mubīn

Hūd 11:6

“There is no creature moving on the earth but its provision rests with The God. He knows its place of settlement and its place of deposit; all is in a clear record.”

مُسْتَوْدَعَهَا has the same derivation and structure as مُسْتَوْدَعٌ in Al-Anʿām 6:98, with the pronoun هَا attached.

That pronoun refers to دَابَّةٍ.

The subject therefore extends beyond humans to every creature moving on earth.

This difference in scope matters.

In Al-Anʿām 6:98, أَنْشَأَكُمْ addresses human beings.

Hūd 11:6 concerns مَا مِنْ دَابَّةٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ, a broader category.

The God speaks of creatures' provision and His knowledge of their places of settlement and deposit.

مُسْتَوْدَع in relation to creatures

مُسْتَقَرَّهَا denotes the creature's place of settlement.

مُسْتَوْدَعَهَا denotes its place of deposit or keeping.

These expressions may relate to different conditions and stages of animal life, but the verse does not specify one exclusive referent.

Lexical and exegetical explanations propose locations of life, prenatal locations, places of death, and conditions of keeping.

The sentence structure does not establish that مُسْتَوْدَع means only a womb or only a grave.

The central point is the breadth of divine knowledge, encompassing creatures' provision, settlement, and deposit.

وَيَعْلَمُ makes that point explicit.

كُلٌّ فِي كِتَابٍ مُبِينٍ also emphasizes the recording and clarity of what is mentioned.

Here مُسْتَوْدَع describes creatures' condition within divine knowledge, rather than naming a legal institution, a particular warehouse, or a biological stage defined by the verse itself.

Connecting Al-Anʿām and Hūd

Al-Anʿām 6:98 and Hūd 11:6 both pair مُسْتَقَرّ with مُسْتَوْدَع.

Al-Anʿām concerns human emergence.

Hūd concerns creatures' provision and locations.

The repeated pairing is not confined to one historical event or particular group.

Conversely, no unargued assumption can establish that both verses necessarily refer to an identical bodily location.

Their shared core distinguishes settling from being deposited; the precise referent must be considered within each context.

4. Morphology, meaning, and interpretive difficulties

Why does دَعْ not begin with wāw?

The root is و د ع, but its Quranic imperative is دَعْ.

Some Arabic verbs beginning with wāw omit it in the imperfect or imperative.

In this family, وَدَعَ and يَدَعُ yield the imperative دَعْ.

Omitting wāw does not create a new root.

دَعْ should also not be confused with ادْعُ from د ع و.

دَعْ means leave or relinquish.

ادْعُ means call or summon.

Despite a similar appearance, they differ in root, structure, and meaning.

Why is the perfect وَدَعَ less common?

Arabic تَرَكَ has taken the place of the simple perfect وَدَعَ in many uses.

Nevertheless, يَدَعُ and دَعْ remain familiar forms.

Lexicographers discuss this situation and the rarity of certain members of the family.

Rarity does not mean impossibility or nonexistence.

Arabic poetic witnesses also preserve forms less frequent in ordinary speech.

The absence of the simple perfect from the Quran does not establish that the Quran intentionally prohibited it or denied some particular meaning.

Farewell and leaving

Ordinarily, farewell accompanies parting from someone.

It does not always mean permanent abandonment, hostility, or a complete end to the relationship.

Two people may say goodbye before a journey while expecting to meet again.

In Al-Ḍuḥā 93:3, وَدَّعَكَ occurs in a context that denies abandonment.

The translation must fit that context.

In an ordinary account of travel and departure, however, “saying goodbye” or “bidding farewell” could be more appropriate.

Being entrusted and being abandoned

A major interpretive problem is equating مُسْتَوْدَع with something simply abandoned.

An entrusted object is placed with another for safekeeping.

This relationship may involve protection, trust, and the possibility of reclaiming it.

Abandoning may merely involve withdrawing further control or pursuit.

مُسْتَوْدَع cannot therefore be translated simply as “abandoned.”

Nor does the word alone guarantee complete safety or a particular period of keeping. The quality of care depends on context and other evidence.

دَعَة and rest

دَعَة concerns ease and comfort in Arabic.

A connection with leaving difficult work or being released from pressure is intelligible.

Yet every use of دَعْ should not be translated as resting.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:48 commands leaving aside harm. Calm or avoidance of conflict may follow, but the verb directly means leave or set aside.

In the horse poem, allowing rest is appropriate because the subject is sparing unnecessary pressure and exertion.

The object of the verb helps determine which branch of meaning is prominent.

5. Comparison with neighboring roots

و د ع compared with ت ر ك

ت ر ك expresses leaving, relinquishing, leaving behind, and desisting.

This overlaps with certain uses of و د ع.

For example, دَعْ أَذَاهُمْ in Al-Aḥzāb 33:48 can be explained as leaving aside their harm.

The two families do not coincide in every usage.

مُسْتَوْدَع concerns depositing for safekeeping, which being merely “left” does not fully convey.

In human usage, وَدَاع has an established sense of farewell and parting, whereas تَرْك need not imply any farewell ceremony or utterance.

Leaving may be a suitable equivalent in some verses, but it cannot replace the entire family of و د ع.

و د ع compared with و ذ ر

و ذ ر also concerns relinquishing and leaving.

The Quran uses forms such as ذَرْ and ذَرُوا.

دَعْ and ذَرْ are close in some constructions and may both be rendered “leave.”

Their practical differences nevertheless require contextual examination.

In Al-Baqarah 2:278, وَذَرُوا مَا بَقِيَ مِنَ الرِّبَا commands relinquishing what remains of riba.

In Al-Aḥzāb 33:48, وَدَعْ أَذَاهُمْ concerns leaving aside opponents' harm.

The objects and subjects differ, but these two examples do not establish that و ذ ر is always financial or و د ع always concerns harm or human relationships.

Both share the field of leaving and relinquishing; their precise differences require examination of their independent usages.

و د ع compared with ه ج ر

ه ج ر concerns distancing oneself, leaving, and breaking away from something or someone.

In وَاهْجُرْهُمْ هَجْرًا جَمِيلًا in Al-Muzzammil 73:10, distancing oneself from opponents is qualified as beautiful.

In one respect, this approaches دَعْ أَذَاهُمْ in Al-Aḥzāb 33:48.

The expressions are nevertheless distinct.

هَجْر here emphasizes distancing and leaving a relationship or companionship.

دَعْ in Al-Aḥzāb 33:48 directly concerns leaving aside harm.

The behaviors may be connected in practice, but one expression's entire meaning cannot be transferred to the other.

و د ع compared with أ م ن

The entrusting branch of و د ع concerns depositing something with another.

أ م ن also occurs in the vocabulary of trusts, confidence, and security.

Entrusting something normally involves an expectation of safekeeping and return.

Entrusting and being trustworthy are nevertheless different concepts.

اسْتِيدَاع concerns the act of entrusting or the condition of being entrusted.

Trust and security concern confidence, safety, and responsibilities of keeping.

مُسْتَوْدَع alone cannot be taken to express every legal or ethical obligation associated with a trust.

Such obligations must be established from the agreement, context, or independent verses about trustworthiness.

و د ع compared with ق ر ر

This comparison is especially important because مُسْتَقَرّ and مُسْتَوْدَع occur together in Al-Anʿām 6:98 and Hūd 11:6.

ق ر ر concerns settling, establishment, and stability.

مُسْتَقَرّ can denote a place of settlement.

مُسْتَوْدَع, by contrast, concerns being deposited for safekeeping.

The basic distinction is that settling is prominent in the former, and being entrusted in the latter.

This can relate to the temporary character of some situations, since a deposit may later be reclaimed or transferred.

It does not follow that مُسْتَقَرّ is always everlasting or مُسْتَوْدَع always brief.

Duration and character must be established from context and the verse's other words.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

Translation must distinguish the four Quranic occurrences. These are editorial proposals and do not replace existing verse word boxes.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:48 — دَعْ

“And leave aside their harm.”

This rendering is brief and faithful to the sentence structure.

An explanation can clarify that leaving aside harm is neither obeying opponents nor approving their conduct.

Al-Ḍuḥā 93:3 — وَدَّعَكَ

“Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor has He hated you.”

“Has not abandoned you” is also acceptable. The proposed Persian equivalent retains the family's semantic connection particularly well.

“Has not said goodbye to you” is unnatural here because the subject is not an ordinary departure for travel.

Al-Anʿām 6:98 — مُسْتَوْدَعٌ

“Then a place of settlement and a place of deposit.”

“Place of deposit” preserves the locative meaning without restricting its referent to a womb, grave, or another stage.

If discussing the passive-participle analysis, “deposited” can also be explained.

The two possibilities should not be combined in one base translation without reason.

Hūd 11:6 — مُسْتَوْدَعَهَا

“He knows its place of settlement and its place of deposit.”

“Place of deposit” is again a clear equivalent.

Since the verse concerns every creature moving on earth, the term should not be restricted directly to one stage of human life.

Non-Quranic forms likewise require equivalents suited to context:

وَدَاع: farewell and parting.

وَدِيعَة: a trust or deposited object.

اسْتَوْدَعَ: entrusted for safekeeping.

دَعَة: ease and rest.

وَدِيع: calm and gentle.

مُوَادَعَة, in relations between parties: cessation of hostilities, truce, or an agreement to refrain from hostility.

A shared root does not require an identical translation for every derivative.

7. Conclusion

و د ع forms a broad Arabic family whose principal branches concern leaving, desisting, allowing rest, and depositing for safekeeping.

Poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period illustrates ceasing conduct, allowing a horse to rest, and having something entrusted to someone.

The root occurs four times in the Quran.

دَعْ in Al-Aḥzāb 33:48 commands leaving aside opponents' harm.

وَدَّعَكَ in Al-Ḍuḥā 93:3 expresses that Muhammad has not been forsaken by his Lord.

مُسْتَوْدَعٌ in Al-Anʿām 6:98 accompanies مُسْتَقَرّ in the context of human emergence.

مُسْتَوْدَعَهَا in Hūd 11:6 concerns creatures' place of deposit within divine knowledge of their provision and locations.

These uses do not allow the entire family to be restricted to either abandoning or entrusting.

Nor should rest, farewell, and entrusting be read into every Quranic occurrence merely to create an apparent unity.

The root may be defined as leaving or setting aside a person, activity, or thing, with branches concerning withdrawal of companionship or pursuit, allowing rest, and depositing something for safekeeping.

دَعْ highlights leaving and relinquishing; form II وَدَّعَ can emphasize parting and forsaking; مُسْتَوْدَع concerns being deposited or a place of deposit.

Forqan should therefore render دَعْ as “leave,” وَدَّعَكَ in its negated clause as “has not forsaken you,” and مُسْتَوْدَع as “place of deposit” or, where grammatically appropriate, “deposited.”

These distinctions preserve the linguistic meanings and prevent interpretive referents or later legal and religious terminology from replacing the Quranic words themselves.
