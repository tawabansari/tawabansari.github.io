Qur'anic Root Ayn D L

Making things correspond, setting straight, equalizing, and placing each thing in a relation that prevents crookedness and one-sidedness

1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ع د ل moves in the field of making two sides correspond, setting something straight, equalizing, compensating with an equivalent, and preventing a relation from bending toward one side. It can mean justice in judgment and conduct, fairness between parties, proportioning the human form, ransom or substitute, and setting a counterpart beside the Lord. It must not be reduced in every verse to numerical equality.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives

عَدَلَ / يَعْدِلُ: to act justly, set as equivalent, turn aside, or place a counterpart. اِعْدِلُوا: act justly. تَعْدِلُوا: establish justice or make equivalent. عَدَّلَ: proportion and arrange. عَدْل: justice, equivalence, substitute, or ransom.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The central meaning is placing two things in a relation where one does not wrongly weigh against the other, or placing something opposite another as its equivalent. Ethical justice, structural proportion, ransom, and false equivalence in shirk all grow from this field, while context decides whether the relation is true or false.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The material image is two sides of a load or two pans of a scale. If one side becomes too heavy, the carrier tilts. Qur'anic justice, however, is not merely equal weight; sometimes it means giving each side its fitting right.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

Arabic usage connects the root with counterparts, paired loads, justice, substitute, ransom, and turning aside from a path. The field includes material equivalence, legal and ethical justice, compensation, and motion away from a direction.

7. History of Understanding, Misuse, or Semantic Restriction

A common restriction is reducing 'adl to complete equality. Another is making every use a mathematical theory of the universe. In ransom verses, the clearer meaning is that no substitute or ransom will be accepted; not necessarily that the substitute is merely formal.

8. Methodological Note

Separate justice in behavior, proportioning structure, setting a counterpart, substitute/ransom, and turning aside. Do not cover all with one fixed translation. Positive or negative value is decided by context.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

Proportion in human creation

الَّذِي خَلَقَكَ فَسَوَّاكَ فَعَدَّلَكَ

The One who created you, then fashioned you, then proportioned you.

Al-Infitar 82:7

Fa'addalaka points to proportion in human structure.

Justice in behavior and testimony

وَلَا يَجْرِمَنَّكُمْ شَنَآنُ قَوْمٍ عَلَىٰ أَلَّا تَعْدِلُوا اعْدِلُوا هُوَ أَقْرَبُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ

Let not hatred of a people drive you not to act justly. Act justly; that is nearer to taqwa.

Al-Ma'idah 5:8

Justice in polygyny

فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ أَلَّا تَعْدِلُوا فَوَاحِدَةً

If you fear that you will not act justly, then one.

An-Nisa' 4:3

وَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعُوا أَنْ تَعْدِلُوا بَيْنَ النِّسَاءِ وَلَوْ حَرَصْتُمْ فَلَا تَمِيلُوا كُلَّ الْمَيْلِ

You will never be able to be fully just between women, even if you are eager; so do not incline completely.

An-Nisa' 4:129

The two verses distinguish inward affection from outward responsibility.

Setting equals beside the Lord

ثُمَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا بِرَبِّهِمْ يَعْدِلُونَ

Yet those who covered truth set equals beside their Lord.

Al-An'am 6:1

Substitute and ransom

وَلَا يُؤْخَذُ مِنْهَا عَدْلٌ

No substitute will be taken from it.

Al-Baqarah 2:48

وَإِنْ تَعْدِلْ كُلَّ عَدْلٍ لَا يُؤْخَذْ مِنْهَا

And if it offers every substitute, it will not be taken from it.

Al-An'am 6:70

Truth and justice in the word of the Lord

وَتَمَّتْ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ صِدْقًا وَعَدْلًا

The word of your Lord was completed in truth and justice.

Al-An'am 6:115

Justice in speech

وَإِذَا قُلْتُمْ فَاعْدِلُوا وَلَوْ كَانَ ذَا قُرْبَىٰ

And when you speak, act justly, even if it concerns a relative.

Al-An'am 6:152

10. Examination of Important Qur'anic Derivatives

'Adl in justice

Guarding right and avoiding unjust inclination.

'Adl in ransom

A substitute or ransom offered against the self or responsibility.

'Adala in shirk

Setting another reference beside the Lord.

'Addala in creation

Proportioning and arranging the parts of a structure.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

With bi-, as in birabbihim ya'dilun, the phrase identifies the One beside whom others are falsely set. With bayna, the sides of the relation are identified. La yu'khadhu minha 'adl denies every substitute.

12. Ayn D L Compared with Q S T

Q S T concerns giving the right share and measure. 'Adl stresses avoiding one-sided inclination and holding a relation upright.

13. Ayn D L Compared with S W Y

S W Y concerns fashioning and setting upright; ta'dil emphasizes proportion and measured relation.

14. Ayn D L Compared with M Y Z / W Z N

Measure and scale concern weighing; 'adl is the fair relation that should result.

15. Ayn D L Compared with H K M

H K M concerns judgment and decision; 'adl is the quality that judgment must have.

16. Specialized Discussion

The root works in proportion in creation, preventing oppressive inclination in human relations, and placing something opposite another as equivalent. In shirk this becomes a false equivalence; in ransom it becomes an ineffective attempt to transfer personal responsibility.

17. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

Justice contexts: justice, fairness, just conduct. Ransom contexts: substitute, ransom, equivalent. Fa'addalaka: He proportioned you. Birabbihim ya'dilun: they set equals beside their Lord. Sidqan wa 'adlan: in truth and justice.

18. Conclusion

The root Ayn D L points to making things correspond, setting them straight, placing an equivalent, and preventing a relation from wrongly leaning toward one side. It appears in justice, creation, ransom, and false equivalence beside the Lord.
