The Qur’anic Root K B D (ك ب د)

Hardship, toil, and enduring life’s difficulties

1. Meaning in early Arabic

ك ب د has several branches: the liver, hardship, enduring difficulty, and the middle of something. Some physical uses also express thickness and density.

كَبِد, with kasrah on the ba’, names the liver; كَبَد, with fatḥah, denotes hardship, suffering, and toil.

A line attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Al-Aʿsha reads:

فَمَا أُجْشِمْتِ مِنْ إِتْيَانِ قَوْمٍ هُمُ الْأَعْدَاءُ فَالْأَكْبَادُ سُودُ

What hardship you undertook in visiting a people who are enemies, whose livers are black!

أَكْبَاد is the plural of كَبِد. Blackened livers here evoke hostility and resentment. In early Arabic expressions of emotion, the liver, like the heart, could represent human feelings.

A line attributed to Labid ibn Rabiʿah, lamenting his brother Arbad, reads:

عَيْنِ هَلَّا بَكَيْتِ أَرْبَدَ إِذْ قُمْنَا وَقَامَ الْخُصُومُ فِي كَبَدٍ

O eye, why did you not weep for Arbad, when we and our opponents stood in hardship?

This line is transmitted in variant forms. كَبَد in this setting concerns the difficulty of confrontation.

Historical accounts place Arbad’s death in the early Islamic period. The line therefore witnesses language near the time of revelation, rather than securely pre-Islamic usage.

Poetic and lexical evidence attests the liver and endurance of hardship within this family. The historical order of these meanings and their precise derivational relationship remain uncertain.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important Arabic forms include:

  • كَبِد: liver.
  • كِبْد and كَبْد: alternative forms for liver.
  • أَكْبَاد and كُبُود: livers.
  • كَبَد: hardship, toil, and suffering.
  • كَبَدَهُ: struck or injured his liver.
  • كَابَدَ: endured the difficulty of something.
  • مُكَابَدَة: enduring hardship; struggling with difficulties.
  • كُبَاد: pain or disease of the liver.
  • تَكَبَّدَتِ الشَّمْسُ: the sun reached the middle of the sky.
  • تَكَبَّدَ اللَّبَنُ: the milk thickened.

Form examined in the Qur’an:

كَبَدٍ

The only Qur’anic form is كَبَد in Al-Balad 90:4. كَبِد, “liver,” and derivatives such as مُكَابَدَة and تَكَبَّدَ do not occur.

3. Semantic core and lexical branches

Ibn Faris identifies intensity and strength as the family’s core. In human life, this approaches hardship, toil, and endurance of difficult circumstances.

Nevertheless, the family’s different uses should not all be forced out of a single physical image.

In anatomy, كَبِد names the liver. In spatial and structural expressions, it can also denote an inner or middle part:

  • كَبِدُ السَّمَاءِ: the middle of the sky.
  • كَبِدُ الْأَرْضِ: the earth’s interior and what lies within it.
  • كَبِدُ الْقَوْسِ: the central part of a bow, where it is held.

كَابَدَ الْأَمْرَ means enduring a task’s difficulty. مُكَابَدَة describes confronting hardship and striving to bear or overcome it.

Enduring severe cold, traveling a difficult route, passing a trying night, or confronting life’s problems can fall within this range.

Arabic associates the liver with suffering and emotions, but it cannot be established that hardship developed from the organ’s name, or the reverse.

For Qur’anic interpretation, what matters is the established meaning of كَبَد as hardship and toil.

4. Qur’anic use

كَبَد: humanity created amid hardship

Here كَبَد concerns difficult conditions encountered in human life.

Coming into being, growing, meeting needs, pursuing goals, enduring illness, assuming responsibility, and confronting problems are examples of difficulties people may experience.

Many needs require effort. Even strength, knowledge, wealth, and security do not remove every difficulty.

The verse does not say that every moment is painful or that people were created solely to suffer. فِي كَبَدٍ presents hardship as a fundamental condition of human life, not its exclusive purpose.

Kabad in the context of Al-Balad

After mentioning creation amid hardship, the Qur’an addresses human assumptions about power and possessions:

It then mentions capacities granted to humanity and presents the image of a difficult ascent:

The following verses connect traversing that ascent with freeing someone from bondage, feeding in a time of hunger, caring for orphans and the poor, faith, patience, and mercy.

This context broadens understanding of kabad. Life’s difficulty is not confined to meeting personal needs; responsibility toward others and good action may also require endurance, generosity, and effort.

People can expend their strength on accumulating wealth, acquiring power, and asserting superiority, or use it to traverse the difficult ascent of goodness and responsibility.

Both paths may involve hardship, but the purpose and manner of action determine the value of the effort.

Does kabad mean an upright human posture?

Some early exegetical reports interpret فِي كَبَدٍ as creation in an upright, balanced form. Another interpretation connects it with the sky and its middle.

These readings have been associated with lexical branches including the middle, prominence, and thickness.

Hardship and toil, however, have clearer support in familiar Arabic usage and fit Al-Balad’s context, particularly its difficult ascent and human responsibility.

“Amid hardship” is therefore the preferable rendering here.

5. Syntax and structure

لَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا الْإِنْسَانَ فِي كَبَدٍ answers the oaths opening Al-Balad.

The lam in لَقَدْ and قَدْ with the perfect verb emphasize the statement’s realization and certainty.

كَبَدٍ is an indefinite noun in the pattern فَعَل, governed in the genitive by فِي.

فِي is significant: humanity is placed amid hardship. This differs from stating a purpose of creation. “We created humanity in order to suffer” adds a purpose absent from the direct construction.

الْإِنْسَانَ here refers to humanity generally, not exclusively to a particular person or group.

6. Comparison with related roots

ك ب د (كَبَد) and ك د ح (كَدْح)

كَدْح concerns effort, striving, and work involving toil.

In Al-Balad 90:4, kabad describes difficult conditions in which humanity is created. In Al-Inshiqaq 84:6, kadḥ emphasizes human striving through life.

One focuses more on circumstances, the other on human effort, although their meanings overlap.

ك ب د (كَبَد) and ع س ر (عُسْر)

عُسْر denotes difficulty and lack of ease, in contrast with يُسْر.

Both kabad and ʿusr concern difficulty. Kabad in Al-Balad 90:4 describes the general conditions of human life, whereas ʿusr can also describe a particular difficult situation.

Creation amid kabad is compatible with experiencing ease. The Qur’an speaks of ease alongside difficulty.

ك ب د (كَبَد) and ن ص ب (نَصَب)

One branch of نَصَب denotes fatigue and suffering arising from exertion or endurance.

In Al-Kahf 18:62, Moses speaks of fatigue from traveling.

Kabad can describe a difficult condition encountered; naṣab can describe fatigue and suffering arising from that encounter.

Describing Paradise, the Qur’an states:

This shows that fatigue and suffering are not humanity’s unavoidable fate throughout every stage of existence.

ك ب د (كَبَد) and ش ق ق (شَقّ)

شَقّ primarily concerns splitting and separating. Some uses of its family express difficulty and hardship.

In Al-Nahl 16:7, بِشِقِّ الْأَنْفُسِ describes the great hardship involved in carrying loads to a destination without the help of livestock.

It foregrounds the strain of traveling and expending strength.

Kabad in Al-Balad offers a more general description of difficult human circumstances.

ك ب د (كَبَد) and ش ق و (شَقَاء)

شَقَاء concerns suffering, difficulty, and an unhappy outcome, and can contrast with felicity.

Hardship in human life does not mean divine guidance was sent to increase suffering.

People are created amid difficult conditions, but guidance can help them discern the right course, overcome difficulties, and avoid needless suffering.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

Arabic word or expressionSuggested rendering
كَبِدliver
كَبَدhardship; toil
كَابَدَendured hardship
مُكَابَدَةenduring difficulty; struggling with hardship
فِي كَبَدٍamid hardship

For Al-Balad 90:4, “We have indeed created humanity amid hardship” preserves the direct construction without adding an assertion about creation’s purpose.

Conclusion

ك ب د has branches concerning the liver, the inner or middle part of something, thickness, hardship, and endurance.

Al-Balad uses kabad to describe a fundamental condition of human life, a course involving effort, responsibility, and difficulties.

Hardship does not exclude joy and ease, nor does it establish suffering as the purpose of creation.

Al-Balad directs attention to how people confront difficulty and use their capacities: for superiority over others, or to traverse the demanding ascent of goodness, freedom, patience, and mercy.
