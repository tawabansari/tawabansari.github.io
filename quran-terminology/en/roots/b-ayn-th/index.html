1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ب ع ث moves in the field of raising, stirring into motion, bringing out of a stopped state, and sending toward a purpose. Someone or something that is in stillness, sleep, death, or inaction enters a new state through ba'th; or a person is raised from within a community and sent to perform a task.

In the Qur'an this root appears in several main contexts: raising the human being after death, awakening after sleep, raising a messenger or prophet within a people, sending an agent or arbiter, appointing a ruler or representative, raising a force against another group, and a person rising into action.

Across these uses one shared axis remains:

A being is brought out of its prior state and moved toward a new condition, position, or action.

Ba'th is not only “making alive,” because messengers, arbiters, a raven, and forces can also be ba'th. It is not only “sending,” because the dead and the sleeping are also ba'th. Its precise meaning must be read from what is raised, the state it had before ba'th, and the destination or action that follows.

Short meaning:

Raising and setting into motion from a state of stillness, sleep, death, or inaction; and sending into a new state or mission.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

The root ب ع ث occurs in the Qur'an sixty-seven times in five morphological forms: the simple verb بَعَثَ, the Form VII verb اِنْبَعَثَ, the noun بَعْث, the passive participle مَبْعُوثُون, and the verbal noun اِنْبِعَاث. Forms such as بَعِيث do not occur in the Qur'an and should not be listed as Qur'anic derivatives.

بَعَثَ، يَبْعَثُ and its inflected forms

This is the most frequent form of the root. Depending on context, it can approach these meanings:

  • raising after death
  • awakening after sleep
  • sending a messenger or agent
  • appointing a person into a position
  • raising a force or punishment
  • sending someone to perform a task

For example:

ثُمَّ بَعَثْنَاكُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ مَوْتِكُمْ

“Then We raised you after your death.”

And:

فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا مِنْ أَهْلِهِ

“Then send an arbiter from his family.”

The verbal form is one, but the type of raising is determined by the context.

بَعْث

This noun or verbal noun points to the act of raising itself, especially in the context of being raised after death:

مَا خَلْقُكُمْ وَلَا بَعْثُكُمْ إِلَّا كَنَفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ

Your creation and your raising are only like a single soul.

Luqman 31:28

مَبْعُوثُون

This is the plural passive participle for those who will be raised:

أَئِذَا مِتْنَا وَكُنَّا تُرَابًا وَعِظَامًا أَئِنَّا لَمَبْعُوثُونَ

“When we have died and become dust and bones, shall we really be raised?” In the Qur'an this form is mostly connected with being raised after death.

اِنْبَعَثَ

This Form VII verb points to rising up or being set into motion from the side of the person himself:

إِذِ انْبَعَثَ أَشْقَاهَا

When the most wretched of them rose up.

Ash-Shams 91:12

Here the person moves into action. The subject is directly mentioned, and the form highlights the beginning of his movement.

اِنْبِعَاث

This is the verbal noun of Form VII:

وَلَٰكِنْ كَرِهَ اللَّهُ انْبِعَاثَهُمْ فَثَبَّطَهُمْ

But The God disliked their rising and setting out, so He held them back.

At-Tawbah 9:46

In this verse, inbi'ath is connected with preparing, going out, and joining movement in the field.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of ب ع ث may be stated as:

Raising something from a state of stoppage and bringing it into new motion, presence, or action.

This “stoppage” appears in different Qur'anic forms.

Sometimes the stoppage is death:

وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ يَبْعَثُ مَنْ فِي الْقُبُورِ

And that The God raises whoever is in the graves.

Al-Hajj 22:7

Sometimes it is sleep:

ثُمَّ يَبْعَثُكُمْ فِيهِ

Then He raises you in it.

Al-Anam 6:60

Sometimes it is the absence of a messenger within a society:

فَبَعَثَ اللَّهُ النَّبِيِّينَ

Then The God raised the prophets.

Al-Baqarah 2:213

Sometimes it is the lack of an agent or representative for a task:

فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا مِنْ أَهْلِهِ

Then send an arbiter from his family.

An-Nisa 4:35

Sometimes a person is sitting back or withholding himself and then rises into action:

إِذِ انْبَعَثَ أَشْقَاهَا

In all these uses, ba'th is not merely spatial relocation. A change of state and role takes place: the sleeper awakens, the dead rise, the messenger gains a commissioned presence among people, the arbiter enters the dispute, or a person moves into an act.

Still, “mission” should not be forced into every use of the root. Those raised after death are not necessarily sent on a mission like a messenger; they rise for presence, account, and encounter with the result of their deeds.

Nor should ba'th be defined through modern expressions such as injecting energy, launching a system, activating a circuit, or leaving entropy. Such phrases may be modern images, but they are not part of the lexical or Qur'anic meaning of the root.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The material image suitable for ba'th is a being that is seated, asleep, or motionless, and is then stirred to rise and move.

In linguistic usage, ba'th may be used for raising a person or animal from its place and sending it toward a task. The central element in this image is transition from stillness to movement; yet this movement is usually not without direction. The one who is ba'th enters a state or task in which he was not previously present.

The Qur'an extends this image across several fields:

  • the dead are raised from graves
  • the sleeping are awakened from sleep
  • a messenger is raised within a community
  • an arbiter is sent to settle conflict
  • a raven is brought into the scene to show an act
  • a person rises to perform an action
  • a group is appointed and sent against another society

The image of a seed sprouting may be worth contemplating because it resembles emergence from stillness, but the Qur'an does not use derivatives of ب ع ث for plant growth. Therefore it should not be presented as the historical and certain image of the root.

The image of “starting a machine” may help a modern reader, but if it enters the base definition it reduces ba'th to a mechanical theory and hides the difference between awakening, sending, appointing, and raising after death.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, بَعَثَهُ can mean that he raised him, made him get up from his place, or sent him toward a task. Ba'th is therefore connected both with movement and with directing movement.

Someone who merely walks is not necessarily “mab'uth.” Ba'th usually shows an agent who brings a person or thing out of its prior state and directs it toward a new condition or action.

From this field several uses arise:

  • ba'th al-rasul: raising and sending the messenger
  • ba'th al-jund: sending a force
  • ba'th al-hakam: sending an arbiter
  • ba'th al-mayyit: raising the dead
  • ba'th al-na'im: awakening the sleeper

These uses show that “sending” and “raising” are not unrelated branches. In sending too, a person leaves a prior state and moves toward a destination or new action.

But wherever ba'th occurs, one should not automatically imagine military dispatch, a sudden shock, or authoritative sending. In the Qur'an, a raven, an arbiter, and even one of the Companions of the Cave sent to buy food are connected with this same root.

7. History of Understanding and Restricting the Meaning

One common restriction is to equate ba'th only with resurrection. Raising after death is one of the most important Qur'anic uses of this root, but it is not the whole field.

The Qur'an uses the same verb for messengers:

وَلَقَدْ بَعَثْنَا فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ رَسُولًا

for arbiters:

فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا

for a raven:

فَبَعَثَ اللَّهُ غُرَابًا

and for the awakening of the Companions of the Cave:

ثُمَّ بَعَثْنَاهُمْ

Therefore a fixed translation such as “made alive” or “resurrected” is wrong in every context.

On the other side, some modern explanations try to make ba'th merely a material process explainable by terms such as energy, neural circuit, stored data, or hardware reconstruction. The Qur'an states the reality of raising after death, but it does not explain its physical mechanism.

From the fact that ba'th after death is “bodily” or involves the reappearance of the human being, one cannot conclude that the Qur'an is presenting a theory about body information stored in soil or a physical impulse being transmitted. Such explanations are outside the text.

Another restriction is to connect every use of ba'th with a sacred mission. The Qur'an says that The God raised powerful servants against Bani Isra'il, and it also says that human beings may send an arbiter or agent. Thus ba'th does not have a fixed positive or negative value by itself; its value depends on the subject, object, purpose, and context.

8. Methodological Note

In studying ب ع ث, three levels must be distinguished:

Direct meaning: raising, setting into motion, or sending.

Type of change in context: awakening from sleep, rising after death, being sent for a mission, being appointed into a role, or beginning action.

Interpretive question: the bodily manner of raising after death, the nature of the sleep of the Companions of the Cave, the mechanism of human reconstruction, or the relation of ba'th to ruh.

The Qur'an states the principle of ba'th clearly, but it does not explain every detail of how it occurs. Concepts such as a stored data base, preserved genetic code, frequency impulse, or divine processing system should not be presented as the meaning of the root or the statement of the verse.

A tendency-level difference should also be kept between ba'th and irsal. A messenger is sent, but in some places the Qur'an says that The God raised him among the people. Ba'th can emphasize the creation of a commissioned presence and the person's rising into a new role, while irsal more directly indicates sending a messenger from a sender to recipients. This distinction is not absolute.

Likewise with hash r: ba'th usually emphasizes being raised, while hashr emphasizes gathering and driving a group together. But verses must be read individually; one should not build a fixed mechanical sequence for every event of the afterlife from this distinction alone.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

Ba'th After Death

One of the main uses of this root is the raising of the human being after death:

ثُمَّ بَعَثْنَاكُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ مَوْتِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

Then We raised you after your death, so that you might give thanks.

Al-Baqarah 2:56

بَعَثْنَاكُمْ points to bringing them out of the state of death and returning them to presence and life. The word itself does not explain the mechanism of that return.

فَأَمَاتَهُ اللَّهُ مِائَةَ عَامٍ ثُمَّ بَعَثَهُ

So The God caused him to die for one hundred years, then raised him.

Al-Baqarah 2:259

The clear contrast between أماته and بعثه shows that ba'th here is returning the person from death into living awareness.

وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ يَبْعَثُ مَنْ فِي الْقُبُورِ

And that The God raises whoever is in the graves.

Al-Hajj 22:7

In this context ba'th is not only leaving the grave; human beings are raised for encounter with truth and with their deeds. Account and recompense are contextual consequences of ba'th, not the lexical meaning of the word itself.

Denial of Ba'th

أَئِذَا مِتْنَا وَكُنَّا تُرَابًا وَعِظَامًا أَئِنَّا لَمَبْعُوثُونَ

When we have died and become dust and bones, shall we really be raised?

As-Saffat 37:16

Their question concerns the possibility of return after bodily disintegration. مبعوثون denotes those who, after death, are brought again into a state of presence and awareness.

زَعَمَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَنْ لَنْ يُبْعَثُوا

Those who covered truth claimed that they will never be raised.

At-Taghabun 64:7

In the Qur'an, denial of ba'th is not merely denial of a natural event; it is also denial of the human return to responsibility and answerability.

Ba'th After Sleep

وَهُوَ الَّذِي يَتَوَفَّاكُمْ بِاللَّيْلِ وَيَعْلَمُ مَا جَرَحْتُمْ بِالنَّهَارِ ثُمَّ يَبْعَثُكُمْ فِيهِ لِيُقْضَىٰ أَجَلٌ مُسَمًّى

He is the One who takes you by night and knows what you have earned by day; then He raises you in it, so that a named term may be completed.

Al-Anam 6:60

Ba'th here is awakening the human being from sleep and returning him to daily activity. The verse connects sleep with tawaffi, but it does not say that sleep is identical to death in every respect.

يَبْعَثُكُمْ فِيهِ shows that after the nightly stoppage, the human being returns to the field of work and daily life.

The Ba'th of the Companions of the Cave

ثُمَّ بَعَثْنَاهُمْ لِنَعْلَمَ أَيُّ الْحِزْبَيْنِ أَحْصَىٰ لِمَا لَبِثُوا أَمَدًا

Then We raised them, so that it would be known which of the two groups had better counted the term they remained.

Al-Kahf 18:12

وَكَذَٰلِكَ بَعَثْنَاهُمْ لِيَتَسَاءَلُوا بَيْنَهُمْ

And thus We raised them so that they would question one another.

Al-Kahf 18:19

Their ba'th points to the end of a long sleep or stoppage and their return to awareness and interaction. After ba'th they speak, measure the time they remained, and send one of themselves to the city.

فَابْعَثُوا أَحَدَكُمْ بِوَرِقِكُمْ هَٰذِهِ إِلَى الْمَدِينَةِ

So send one of you with this silver of yours to the city.

Al-Kahf 18:19

In one context, The God raises them from sleep; then they themselves send one of their companions for a task. This pairing shows the natural breadth of the root.

Raising a Messenger Within a Community

وَلَقَدْ بَعَثْنَا فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ رَسُولًا أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَاجْتَنِبُوا الطَّاغُوتَ

We certainly raised in every community a messenger: worship The God and avoid taghut.

An-Nahl 16:36

Here ba'th points to raising a messenger within a community for the delivery of call and warning. The phrase في كل أمة identifies the field of the messenger's presence and action; it does not speak of a hidden cellular mechanism within society.

لَقَدْ مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِذْ بَعَثَ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًا مِنْ أَنْفُسِهِمْ

The God favored the believers when He raised among them a messenger from themselves.

Ali Imran 3:164

The messenger's ba'th among people is not the appearance of a foreign being. The verse stresses that the messenger is from themselves. Ba'th makes his role and presence manifest in the community.

Raising Prophets After Human Difference

كَانَ النَّاسُ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً فَبَعَثَ اللَّهُ النَّبِيِّينَ مُبَشِّرِينَ وَمُنْذِرِينَ

Human beings were one community; then The God raised the prophets as bearers of good news and warners.

Al-Baqarah 2:213

Here ba'th is accompanied by two roles: giving good news and warning. These roles are not the meaning of ba'th itself; they are the work of the prophets who were raised.

Sending Arbiters to Resolve Conflict

وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ شِقَاقَ بَيْنِهِمَا فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا مِنْ أَهْلِهِ وَحَكَمًا مِنْ أَهْلِهَا

If you fear a split between the two, then send an arbiter from his family and an arbiter from her family.

An-Nisa 4:35

In this verse, ba'th means sending people into the process of resolving a dispute. The arbiters move from absence in the matter into presence and action within it. This use shows that ba'th is not necessarily an action unique to The God or to extraordinary matters; human society can also ba'th someone for a task.

The Ba'th of the Raven

فَبَعَثَ اللَّهُ غُرَابًا يَبْحَثُ فِي الْأَرْضِ لِيُرِيَهُ كَيْفَ يُوَارِي سَوْأَةَ أَخِيهِ

Then The God sent a raven scratching in the earth, to show him how to cover the exposed body of his brother.

Al-Ma'idah 5:31

The raven's ba'th concerns bringing or sending it into a scene where its act becomes instructive for the human being. The Qur'an does not call the raven an operational unit equipped with information; it says that The God sent a raven that scratched in the earth and whose act showed something to the human being.

Appointing a Ruler or Leader

ابْعَثْ لَنَا مَلِكًا نُقَاتِلْ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ

Raise for us a king, so we may fight in the way of The God.

Al-Baqarah 2:246

إِنَّ اللَّهَ قَدْ بَعَثَ لَكُمْ طَالُوتَ مَلِكًا

The God has raised Talut for you as king.

Al-Baqarah 2:247

Here ba'th is connected with placing Talut into a social role and position. “Sent” conveys part of the meaning, but “appointed and raised” or “appointed for you as king” is clearer in this context.

Raising Naqibs

وَبَعَثْنَا مِنْهُمُ اثْنَيْ عَشَرَ نَقِيبًا

And We raised from among them twelve naqibs.

Al-Ma'idah 5:12

This verse points to appointing twelve persons in the role of naqib. Ba'th here is not merely spatial movement; it concerns assigning a role and entering them into a new responsibility.

Raising a Force Against a Society

فَإِذَا جَاءَ وَعْدُ أُولَاهُمَا بَعَثْنَا عَلَيْكُمْ عِبَادًا لَنَا أُولِي بَأْسٍ شَدِيدٍ

When the promise of the first of the two came, We raised against you servants of Ours possessing severe might.

Al-Isra 17:5

Ba'th here is sending a powerful group against Bani Isra'il. The continuation says they moved through the dwellings; ba'th is the prelude to their presence and action. This use shows that ba'th can involve dispatching force, but not every occurrence of the root should be read militarily.

Raising Punishment or an Agent of Harm

قُلْ هُوَ الْقَادِرُ عَلَىٰ أَنْ يَبْعَثَ عَلَيْكُمْ عَذَابًا مِنْ فَوْقِكُمْ أَوْ مِنْ تَحْتِ أَرْجُلِكُمْ

Say: He is able to raise against you a punishment from above you or from beneath your feet.

Al-Anam 6:65

Here ba'th concerns sending or bringing forth punishment against a group. The word itself does not identify the type or mechanism of the punishment.

Raising to a Station

عَسَىٰ أَنْ يَبْعَثَكَ رَبُّكَ مَقَامًا مَحْمُودًا

It may be that your Lord will raise you to a praised station.

Al-Isra 17:79

In this verse, ba'th is paired with maqaman mahmuda. The matter is not only awakening or sending; it is being raised to a praised station. Ba'th highlights transfer into a new state and station.

Inbi'ath for Action

إِذِ انْبَعَثَ أَشْقَاهَا

When the most wretched of them rose up.

Ash-Shams 91:12

انبعث expresses the beginning of a person's movement toward action. The negative charge comes from the person and act, not from Form VII itself.

وَلَوْ أَرَادُوا الْخُرُوجَ لَأَعَدُّوا لَهُ عُدَّةً وَلَٰكِنْ كَرِهَ اللَّهُ انْبِعَاثَهُمْ فَثَبَّطَهُمْ

Had they intended to go out, they would have prepared equipment for it; but The God disliked their rising and setting out, so He held them back.

At-Tawbah 9:46

In this verse, انبعاث is connected with going out, preparation, and being held back. Its meaning is rising and setting out to join the field.

10. Examination of Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

بَعَثَ in the meaning of raising after death

In this use, the subject is usually The God and the object is the dead human being or the dead generally:

يَبْعَثُ مَنْ فِي الْقُبُورِ

\n

Al-Hajj 22:7

يَوْمَ يَبْعَثُهُمُ اللَّهُ جَمِيعًا

\n

Al-Mujadila 58:6

مَنْ بَعَثَنَا مِنْ مَرْقَدِنَا

\n

Ya-Sin 36:52

The prior state is death or being in the grave; the state after ba'th is living and conscious presence.

بَعَثَ in the meaning of awakening

In Al-Anam 6:60 and the story of the Companions of the Cave, the prior state is sleep. Ba'th shows the end of that state and return to awareness and action. Translating it “made alive” in the story of the Cave may add more than the text requires; “raised” or “awakened” is more fitting.

بَعَثَ in the meaning of sending

In cases such as sending an arbiter, a raven, an agent, or gatherers, “send” is the natural translation:

فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا

فَبَعَثَ اللَّهُ غُرَابًا

وَابْعَثْ فِي الْمَدَائِنِ حَاشِرِينَ

\n

Ash-Shuara 26:36

Still, the link with raising and entering someone into new action remains.

بَعَثَ in the meaning of appointing

In the cases of Talut and the naqibs, “appointing” or “placing in a role” is more natural than “sending”:

قَدْ بَعَثَ لَكُمْ طَالُوتَ مَلِكًا

\n

Al-Baqarah 2:247

بَعَثْنَا مِنْهُمُ اثْنَيْ عَشَرَ نَقِيبًا

بَعْث

The noun ba'th in the Qur'an mostly appears in discussion of human beings being raised. It names the event of raising itself, not necessarily every stage of gathering, account, and recompense.

مَبْعُوثُون

This form shows the state of those upon whom ba'th occurs. In the Qur'an it is usually found in the speech of deniers or in emphasis on being raised after death.

اِنْبَعَثَ and اِنْبِعَاث

Form VII highlights movement from the angle of the one who rises. Yet one should not infer from the form that the act is wholly spontaneous and has no external factor. Decision, provocation, character, and circumstances may all have a role in the movement.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

بَعَثَ with a direct object

The verb ba'atha usually takes an object that is raised or sent:

بَعَثَهُ

He raised him.

بَعَثْنَاهُمْ

We raised them.

يَبْعَثُهُمُ اللَّهُ

The God raises them.

The type of ba'th is known from the state of the object and the continuation of the sentence.

بَعَثَ فِي

بَعَثَ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًا

\n

Ali Imran 3:164

بَعَثْنَا فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ رَسُولًا

\n

An-Nahl 16:36

The preposition في marks the field of presence and activity of the messenger: he is raised within that community. By itself, the structure does not indicate inner infiltration, cellular placement, or a hidden mechanism.

بَعَثَ إِلَى

بَعَثْنَا مِنْ بَعْدِهِمْ مُوسَىٰ وَهَارُونَ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ وَمَلَئِهِ

After them We raised Musa and Harun toward Firawn and his chiefs.

Yunus 10:75

Here إلى identifies the destination of the call or movement.

بَعَثَ عَلَى

بَعَثْنَا عَلَيْكُمْ عِبَادًا لَنَا

\n

Al-Isra 17:5

يَبْعَثَ عَلَيْكُمْ عَذَابًا

\n

Al-Anam 6:65

The preposition على shows the direction of a force or punishment coming upon a group.

بَعَثَ لِـ

قَدْ بَعَثَ لَكُمْ طَالُوتَ مَلِكًا

\n

Al-Baqarah 2:247

The لـ relates Talut to the community: he is appointed for them and in relation to their social need.

بَعَثَ with a second object or role-like state

In بَعَثَ لَكُمْ طَالُوتَ مَلِكًا, the word ملكا expresses the position or role in which Talut has been raised. In يَبْعَثَكَ رَبُّكَ مَقَامًا مَحْمُودًا, the structure points to transfer into a praised station, but the translation should not become a theological theory beyond the phrase.

Passive forms in resurrection contexts

يُبْعَثُونَ

تُبْعَثُونَ

أُبْعَثُ حَيًّا

\n

Maryam 19:33

These forms focus on those upon whom ba'th occurs. In many such verses the agent is clear from context: The God is the raiser.

12. B AYN TH (BA'ATHA) Compared with R S L (ARSALA)

The root ر س ل is connected with sending, dispatching, and delivering a message. A rasul is one who has been sent.

Ba'th also refers in some places to sending a messenger or agent, but its field is wider: the dead, the sleeping, a ruler, and even a raven can be ba'th.

As a tendency, irsal emphasizes sending from an origin toward a destination, while ba'th emphasizes raising a person or thing into new presence, motion, or role.

The two can overlap for one messenger: he is sent by The God and raised among a community. The difference should not be made absolute, and the Qur'an does not set a fixed technical order in which every ba'th must precede every irsal.

13. B AYN TH (BA'ATHA) Compared with N SH R (NASHARA)

The root ن ش ر is connected with opening, spreading, scattering, and making something spread out. In the context of coming alive, nushur points to life spreading open after death.

Ba'th and nushur are close in discussion of the afterlife, but their angle is not identical: ba'th emphasizes being raised from the state of death; nushur can emphasize coming forth, spreading out, and being dispersed after that.

Still, ba'th should not be treated as only the individual beginning and nushur as a fixed later stage in every case. The fields of the two roots must be read in their verses.

14. B AYN TH (BA'ATHA) Compared with H SH R (HASHARA)

The root ح ش ر is connected with gathering and driving a group toward a place.

As a tendency, ba'th emphasizes being raised and rising; hashr emphasizes gathering and collective movement toward a shared field or destination.

Human beings may be raised and then gathered, but a root article should not turn this difference into a fixed mechanical chart for all afterlife verses. Ba'th can be individual or collective; hashr more strongly highlights the collective dimension.

15. B AYN TH (BA'ATHA) Compared with Q W M (QAMA)

The root ق و م is connected with standing, being established, maintaining, and taking responsibility.

One who is ba'th may rise, but not every qiyam is ba'th. Qiyam may be the act of the person himself, while ba'th usually indicates an agent who raises the person or brings him into a new state.

In the afterlife both images may be present: people are raised, and they stand for the Lord of the worlds. Ba'th highlights the cause or beginning of entry into that state; qiyam highlights the standing itself.

16. B AYN TH (BA'ATHA) Compared with KH R J (KHARAJA)

The root خ ر ج points to coming out from within a place or condition. In afterlife verses, coming out of graves may be a result of ba'th.

But ba'th and khuruj are not identical. Ba'th emphasizes being raised; khuruj emphasizes coming out from a previous place or boundary. Someone may be ba'th for a mission without leaving an enclosed space; someone may leave a house without being ba'th. Translating ba'th as “bringing out” is suitable only in contexts where khuruj is part of the event.

17. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

بَعَثَ in the context after death

  • raised
  • raised again
  • raised from death
  • made alive and set upright

The base translation “raised” best preserves the field of the root. “Made alive” may be clearer in some verses, but it weakens the link with other uses of ba'th.

يُبْعَثُونَ

  • are raised
  • are set upright again
  • are raised from death

بَعَثَ in the context of sleep

  • awakened
  • raised
  • raised from sleep

In the story of the Companions of the Cave, “We raised them” preserves the root, while “We awakened them” clarifies the contextual meaning.

بَعَثَ رسولاً

  • raised a messenger
  • sent a messenger
  • raised a messenger among them

For a root article, “raised” is more precise; in smooth verse translation, “sent” may be natural but does not show the full field of the root.

فَابْعَثُوا حَكَمًا

Then send an arbiter. In this context, “raise” is awkward in English and “send” better conveys the function of the verse.

بَعَثَ لَكُمْ طَالُوتَ مَلِكًا

  • appointed Talut for you as king
  • raised Talut for you as king

“Appointed” makes the social role and new position clearer.

فَبَعَثَ اللَّهُ غُرَابًا

  • Then The God sent a raven
  • Then The God raised a raven

“Sent” is more natural in English, while root explanation can preserve the connection with ba'th.

بَعَثْنَا عَلَيْكُمْ عِبَادًا

  • We raised servants against you
  • We sent servants against you

اِنْبَعَثَ

  • rose up
  • moved into action
  • set out to act

اِنْبِعَاثَهُمْ

  • their rising
  • their setting out
  • their moving out

بَعْث

  • raising
  • ba'th
  • being raised after death

“Resurrection” may convey the larger event, but it is not always the exact equivalent of ba'th itself because in English it can carry the whole afterlife event rather than only the act of raising.

مَبْعُوثُون

  • those who are raised
  • the raised ones
  • those raised again

Phrases such as “mission-activated operational units” should be avoided; the Qur'anic use of this form is mostly about human beings raised after death.

18. Conclusion

The root ب ع ث in the Qur'an is not limited to being made alive after death. It appears across a wide field of raising, awakening, sending, appointing, and setting into motion.

Final proposed definition:

B AYN TH: raising and bringing out of a state of stillness, sleep, death, or inaction, and bringing into presence, motion, position, or new action.

In ba'th after death, the human being returns from death to living and conscious presence. In ba'th after sleep, the sleeper awakens. In the ba'th of a messenger, a person from within a community is raised to deliver signs and fulfill the role of messengership. In the ba'th of an arbiter or agent, someone is sent for a defined task. In the ba'th of Talut and the naqibs, people are appointed to a new position.

This diversity shows that the core of the root is neither “injecting energy” nor only “sending.” The central axis is change of state and entry into new movement or role.

Ba'th approaches irsal in sending, qiyam in rising, khuruj in coming out, nushur in spreading after death, and hashr in gathering human beings; but none of these is its complete equivalent.

Compared with irsal, ba'th emphasizes being raised and entering a new role or motion. Compared with hashr, ba'th highlights the rising itself, while hashr is more connected with collective gathering. These differences are tendencies and should not be turned into mechanical and fixed stages.

The story of the Companions of the Cave is a clear example of ba'th after sleep, but it does not provide a technical explanation of their biological preservation. The general ba'th of human beings after death is also an explicit Qur'anic truth, but the Qur'an does not explain it with expressions such as stored information, neural circuits, energy impulses, or hardware reconstruction.

The task of root study is to preserve the direct meaning:

Someone or something that had been in a prior stopped state is raised and enters presence, motion, or new action.
