Principal meanings: displeasure and grievance over another's conduct; turning from objectionable conduct and restoring goodwill; physically, a threshold, step, or raised feature crossed by ascending.

1. The root in early Arabic

The triliteral root ع ت ب has several semantic branches. In human relations, a principal branch concerns displeasure and grievance. عَتَبَ عَلَيْهِ means being displeased with someone or finding fault with that person's conduct.

This displeasure may be expressed as reproach, without necessarily implying hostility, severed relations, or punishment. Often someone reproaches a person within an existing relationship, expecting objectionable conduct to change.

For example, a broken promise may upset a friendship. One friend recalls the conduct and expresses displeasure; correction or an acceptable explanation from the other may remove the grievance and restore goodwill.

إِعْتَاب, عُتْبَى, and اسْتِعْتَاب are understandable within this relationship. This is a semantic explanation, not a definitive historical sequence of derivation.

Three states should remain distinct: displeasure arising, efforts to remove it, and goodwill being restored afterward.

This distinction is central to the Quranic uses.

A pre-Islamic poetic witness: grievance and restored goodwill

A poem attributed to Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī, a pre-Islamic poet, includes:

فَإِنْ أَكُ مَظْلُومًا فَعَبْدٌ ظَلَمْتَهُ

fa-in aku maẓlūman fa-ʿabdun ẓalamtahu

وَإِنْ تَكُ ذَا عُتْبَى فَمِثْلُكَ يُعْتَبُ

wa-in taku dhā ʿutbā fa-mithluka yuʿtabu

Translation according to the passive reading: If I have been wronged, I am a servant you have wronged; and if you have a grievance, someone such as you deserves to have his goodwill restored.

The poet addresses Al-Nuʿmān ibn al-Mundhir, seeking to clear an accusation against himself.

Aware of the ruler's displeasure, he seeks to repair their damaged relationship.

In the passive reading يُعْتَبُ adopted here, someone such as the addressee is to be satisfied through removing the cause of grievance. The line is thus concerned with restoring goodwill, not merely expressing reproach.

It attests removing grievance and restoring goodwill, alongside reproach, in poetry transmitted from the pre-Islamic period.

The poem has variant readings and explanations. Khizānat al-adab explicitly explains يُعْتَبُ as passive: conduct is brought back to what pleases him. Another explanation emphasizes the addressee's acceptance of reconciliation. The grammatical reading and its translation must agree.

Another witness: appeasement used ironically

A poem attributed to Bishr ibn Abī Khāzim, a pre-Islamic poet, says:

غَضِبَتْ تَمِيمٌ أَنْ تُقَتَّلَ عَامِرٌ

ghaḍibat tamīmun an tuqattala ʿāmirun

يَوْمَ النِّسَارِ فَأُعْتِبُوا بِالصَّيْلَمِ

yawma al-nisāri fa-uʿtibū bi-l-ṣaylami

Translation: Tamīm became angry that ʿĀmir had been slaughtered on the day of al-Nisār; so they were “appeased” with the sword and a devastating calamity.

الصَّيْلَم denotes a sword or a crushing calamity here.

أُعْتِبُوا is ironic. Whereas ordinary إِعْتَاب removes displeasure, here the answer to anger is violent confrontation rather than reconciliation.

The passive فأُعْتِبُوا adopted here follows the cited lexicographical transmission. Unvocalized spellings alone do not establish distinct active and passive readings; the point is the ironic response to anger.

This too is transmitted as a pre-Islamic poet's verse.

Together, the witnesses attest a field involving displeasure, repairing relations, and restoring goodwill, useful for understanding the Quranic uses.

The material branch: thresholds and steps

عَتَبَة denotes a doorway threshold or a step.

A threshold may rise above the ground, requiring a person to lift a foot when crossing it.

عَتَبَاتُ الدَّرَجِ refers to steps or rungs: successive raised surfaces on which a person places a foot to ascend.

Raised stages on a mountain route are also called عَتَبَات.

Some lexicographers connect this physical branch with displeasure through an image of difficulty or obstruction.

Resemblance alone does not establish their historical derivation. Thresholds and steps, and grievance and goodwill, remain distinct attested uses requiring their own analysis.

2. Lexical forms and the word family

Principal forms include عَتَبَ, يَعْتِبُ, عَتْب, عِتَاب, مَعْتَبَة, عَاتَبَ, مُعَاتَبَة, أَعْتَبَ, إِعْتَاب, عُتْبَى, اسْتَعْتَبَ, اسْتِعْتَاب, مُعْتَب, عَتَبَة, and عَتَبَات.

عَتَبَ: being displeased or aggrieved

عَتَبَ عَلَيْهِ means having a grievance about someone's conduct.

A person disappointed by a friend's broken promise illustrates this field, especially when expecting the relationship to improve.

عَتْب and مَعْتَبَة are nouns or verbal nouns associated with this state.

Displeasure does not itself prove the complainant is right: a grievance can be justified or unjustified.

Someone may also be displeased without ever expressing it.

Feeling displeasure and voicing it should therefore be distinguished.

عَاتَبَ and مُعَاتَبَة: reproach and recalling objectionable conduct

عَاتَبَ is Form III and concerns reproach, recalling unwelcome conduct, and seeking explanation or correction.

مُعَاتَبَة names this process.

Among friends or close associates, reproach may provide an opportunity to clarify misunderstanding and repair relations.

Two people may interpret conduct differently. Explaining their respective grievances can lead to understanding and resolution.

Nevertheless, the action need not be mutual: one may reproach another without receiving a grievance in return.

Form III here does not require equal reciprocal participation in every use.

عُتْبَى: goodwill restored after displeasure

عُتْبَى is a significant member of this family.

In human relationships it concerns removing displeasure and restoring goodwill.

This differs from عَتْب itself.

عَتْب names the grievance, whereas عُتْبَى concerns its removal.

If someone apologizes for wrongdoing and a friend becomes satisfied, the resulting restoration belongs to this field.

لَكَ الْعُتْبَى offers to remove the addressee's grievance and return to what pleases that person. It need not declare that satisfaction has already been achieved.

Nor is عُتْبَى merely a spoken apology: explanation, changed conduct, or reparation may be required.

أَعْتَبَ: removing displeasure

أَعْتَبَ is Form IV and commonly concerns removing grievance and restoring satisfaction.

أَعْتَبَهُ can concern satisfying someone through changed conduct; usage also includes granting a request for reconciliation and becoming satisfied with its seeker.

The roles of the participants can therefore differ across contexts.

إِعْتَاب names the act or its result.

Each sentence requires identifying who was displeased, who acted to repair the relationship, and whose satisfaction was restored.

The Quranic الْمُعْتَبِينَ derives from this form, as discussed under Fuṣṣilat 41:24.

اسْتَعْتَبَ: seeking the removal of displeasure

اسْتَعْتَبَ is Form X.

In a principal use, someone seeks removal of displeasure and restored goodwill.

An offender may ask an injured person to become satisfied or allow a way of making amends.

In another use, the displeased person asks the offender to change conduct and restore goodwill.

Thus two related directions are possible: seeking satisfaction from the displeased person, or seeking corrective action from the person responsible for the grievance.

They belong to the same relationship but differ in agent and addressee.

This matters in Quranic passages using both active and passive forms.

عَتَبَة: threshold and step

The physical branch names a threshold, step, or raised feature crossed or stepped upon.

Its plural is عَتَبَات.

عَتَبَ also describes an uneven gait or limping, as when an injured animal walks on three legs while holding up the fourth.

Lexicographical explanation compares this movement with climbing steps.

عَتَب also denotes defects or impairment in certain implements or bodily parts.

These branches do not occur in the Quran, but illustrate the family's wider range.

3. Quranic uses

The root occurs five times in four verses, in two principal morphological constructions.

Four occurrences are Form X verbs; one is a Form IV passive participle.

يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ occurs three times; يَسْتَعْتِبُوا and الْمُعْتَبِينَ once each.

All occur within passages about the Hereafter, accountability, and the outcome of conduct or denial.

عِتَاب, عُتْبَى, and عَتَبَة do not independently occur in the Quran.

A. يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ: being called to make amends on the day of testimony

وَيَوْمَ نَبْعَثُ مِنْ كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ شَهِيدًا ثُمَّ لَا يُؤْذَنُ لِلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا وَلَا هُمْ يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ

wa-yawma nabʿathu min kulli ummatin shahīdan thumma lā yuʾdhanu li-lladhīna kafarū wa-lā hum yustaʿtabūn

Al-Naḥl 16:84

And on the day We raise a witness from every community, permission will then not be given to those who disbelieved, nor will they be called upon to seek restored approval.

Al-Naḥl 16:84

يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ is a passive imperfect of Form X.

The corresponding active is يَسْتَعْتِبُونَ; the passive is يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ.

The vowels matter: the passive leaves the requester unnamed and presents those discussed as the people from whom the action would be sought.

Close to its grammatical structure, the clause says they will no longer be asked to act to remove displeasure and seek approval.

Contextually, this concerns the end of the opportunity for correction and reparation.

The role of لَا يُؤْذَنُ

Before this clause comes ثُمَّ لَا يُؤْذَنُ لِلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا.

يُؤْذَنُ belongs to أ ذ ن and concerns permission being given.

يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ then follows from ع ت ب.

The verbs do not mean the same thing.

The first concerns permission; the second being called to remove displeasure and seek approval.

Together they distinguish this stage from worldly life.

In worldly life, a person can reflect, correct wrongdoing, seek another's satisfaction, and repair harm where possible.

The verse concerns witnesses being raised and the absence of the ordinary stage of invitation to amend conduct.

The isolated affirmative يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ does not mean “permission is not given.” Negation comes from لَا, while the explanatory loss of opportunity comes from context and the preceding clause.

Connection with subsequent verses

Al-Naḥl 16:85 says that when wrongdoers see the punishment, it is neither lightened nor deferred.

Al-Naḥl 16:87 describes their submission that day and the disappearance of what they had fabricated.

This sequence places Al-Naḥl 16:84 at a stage when deeds and claims become manifest.

Negated istiʿtāb here concerns the end of that opportunity for correction, not a claim that people are denied the possibility of return in worldly life.

B. يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ: excuses no longer benefiting

فَيَوْمَئِذٍ لَا يَنْفَعُ الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا مَعْذِرَتُهُمْ وَلَا هُمْ يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ

fa-yawmaʾidhin lā yanfaʿu alladhīna ẓalamū maʿdhiratuhum wa-lā hum yustaʿtabūn

Al-Rūm 30:57

On that day, the excuses of those who did wrong will not benefit them, nor will they be called upon to seek restored approval.

Al-Rūm 30:57

This also uses passive يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ.

Here it accompanies مَعْذِرَتُهُمْ, “their excuse.”

مَعْذِرَة belongs to ع ذ ر and concerns an excuse or explanation offered to remove blame or responsibility.

Istiʿtāb, by contrast, concerns removing displeasure and restoring approval.

The concepts are close but distinct.

Excuses, apologies, and changed conduct

Someone may explain conduct while attempting to show that no wrong was committed.

Another may admit wrongdoing and seek to repair it to satisfy the injured person.

The former chiefly concerns explanation and defence.

The latter concerns removing grievance and repairing a relationship.

An excuse or apology does not itself constitute reparation; actual reparation may extend beyond words.

Al-Rūm 30:57 mentions both: their excuse does not benefit them, and they are not called upon to seek approval.

Connection with resurrection

Al-Rūm 30:55 describes the Hour's arrival and the offenders' estimate of how long they remained.

In Al-Rūm 30:56, those given knowledge and faith identify the day of resurrection.

Al-Rūm 30:57 then denies benefit from excuses and negates istiʿtāb.

The context is a stage after truth has become manifest and accountability has arrived.

An excuse alone cannot alter past conduct.

This does not make every worldly excuse or apology worthless. The statement concerns a particular day, and its scope must be preserved.

C. يَسْتَعْتِبُوا and الْمُعْتَبِينَ: requesting approval without receiving it

فَإِنْ يَصْبِرُوا فَالنَّارُ مَثْوًى لَهُمْ ۖ وَإِنْ يَسْتَعْتِبُوا فَمَا هُمْ مِنَ الْمُعْتَبِينَ

fa-in yaṣbirū fa-l-nāru mathwan lahum wa-in yastaʿtibū fa-mā hum mina al-muʿtabīn

Fuṣṣilat 41:24

If they endure, the Fire is their dwelling; and if they seek restored approval, they will not be among those whose request is granted.

Fuṣṣilat 41:24

This verse places two different forms of ع ت ب in one sentence.

يَسْتَعْتِبُوا is an active imperfect of Form X, jussive after the conditional particle إِنْ, with its final nūn omitted.

الْمُعْتَبِينَ is the sound masculine plural of the Form IV passive participle.

The first concerns requesting the removal of displeasure.

The second concerns that request being accepted and approval being restored.

Request and acceptance

Someone can seek another's satisfaction without the injured person accepting the request.

An apology may also precede any actual change of conduct or repair of harm.

Requesting and obtaining approval are therefore distinct states.

يَسْتَعْتِبُوا names the request; الْمُعْتَبِينَ names those for whom it is granted.

Here, even if they request it, they will not be among those whose request is accepted.

Translating both merely as “repent” or “apologize” would erase this distinction.

Connection with endurance

The verse begins فَإِنْ يَصْبِرُوا فَالنَّارُ مَثْوًى لَهُمْ.

It then mentions seeking approval.

Two situations are contrasted: enduring the existing condition and seeking to change it through restored approval.

Neither brings the desired release at the stage described.

Here patience concerns enduring punishment. It should not automatically be equated with praiseworthy steadfastness in responsibility or truth.

Nor does requesting approval itself establish that the petitioners have genuinely changed.

The verse reports a request and its nonacceptance. Claims about their inward state require additional Quranic evidence.

The preceding context

Fuṣṣilat 41:20 describes hearing, sight, and skin testifying about deeds.

Fuṣṣilat 41:22 recalls their assumption that The God did not know much of what they did.

Fuṣṣilat 41:23 identifies this assumption as causing their ruin.

Fuṣṣilat 41:24 follows with their dwelling in the Fire and the ineffectiveness of seeking restored approval.

They are thus confronted with the reality of their deeds.

The request belongs to accountability: it seeks a changed condition after deeds and consequences become manifest.

D. يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ: the end of opportunity after mocking the signs

ذَٰلِكُمْ بِأَنَّكُمُ اتَّخَذْتُمْ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ هُزُوًا وَغَرَّتْكُمُ الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا ۚ فَالْيَوْمَ لَا يُخْرَجُونَ مِنْهَا وَلَا هُمْ يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ

dhālikum bi-annakumu ittakhadhtum āyāti allāhi huzuwan wa-gharratkumu al-ḥayātu al-dunyā fa-l-yawma lā yukhrajūna minhā wa-lā hum yustaʿtabūn

Al-Jāthiyah 45:35

That is because you took The God's signs in mockery and worldly life deceived you. Today they will not be brought out of it, nor will they be called upon to seek restored approval.

Al-Jāthiyah 45:35

This is the third passive يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ occurrence.

Before negating istiʿtāb, the verse identifies the cause of their situation.

They mocked divine signs and were deceived by worldly life.

The criticism concerns specific conduct toward those signs, beyond merely an unknowing mistake or interpersonal difference of interpretation.

Mockery and deception by worldly life

هُزُوًا belongs to ه ز أ and concerns mockery.

غَرَّتْكُمُ belongs to غ ر ر and describes worldly life deceiving the addressees.

These expressions establish the background to the outcome that follows.

Al-Jāthiyah 45:33 describes their evil deeds becoming manifest and what they mocked encompassing them.

Al-Jāthiyah 45:34 concerns forgetting the encounter of that day and its consequence.

Al-Jāthiyah 45:35 then states that they will not be brought out and will not be called upon to make amends.

What the negation conveys

Two negations appear together:

لَا يُخْرَجُونَ مِنْهَا concerns not being brought out of the place discussed.

وَلَا هُمْ يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ concerns not being called upon to remove displeasure and seek approval.

These meanings should not be merged.

The former concerns their location; the latter indicates that the stage of being called to correct conduct is no longer open to them.

An explanatory rendering may say that an opportunity to make amends is no longer available.

“Opportunity” is not a separate Arabic word in the verse; it is a contextual explanation of the passive construction.

4. Active يَسْتَعْتِبُوا and passive يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ

This is the root's central Quranic morphological distinction.

يَسْتَعْتِبُوا in Fuṣṣilat 41:24 is active: the people themselves seek the removal of displeasure.

يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ in Al-Naḥl 16:84, Al-Rūm 30:57, and Al-Jāthiyah 45:35 is passive: they are the people from whom action is sought, without the requester being named.

Arabic istiʿtāb can concern seeking satisfaction from the displeased person or seeking correction from the offender.

Two related explanations of the Quranic passive are therefore encountered.

One preserves the request's direction: they are no longer called upon to correct conduct and seek approval.

The other explains its outcome: an opportunity for seeking approval and making amends is no longer afforded.

The first explains the passive request; the second states its contextual consequence. They are not identical literal translations, even though their contextual outcomes are close.

The passive voice must remain visible.

“They do not seek approval” is not an accurate direct rendering of يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ.

Nor is “they do not repent”: the word concerns a request to remove displeasure, not directly whether repentance occurs.

الْمُعْتَبِينَ in Fuṣṣilat 41:24 is a passive participle of أَعْتَبَ, describing those whose request for restored approval is granted.

The Quran distinguishes requesting, being called upon to act, and having a request accepted.

5. Comparison with related roots

عَتَبَ and لَامَ: grievance and blame

ل و م concerns blaming someone and attributing fault or shortcoming.

عَتَبَ عَلَيْهِ can also involve complaint and reproach.

Within close relationships, reproach often involves displeasure and an expectation of repair.

Someone may reproach a friend for breaking a promise while wanting the conduct to change and the friendship to continue.

Blame may simply identify fault without seeking reconciliation.

This is not an absolute boundary; the terms can be closely related in Arabic.

The عُتْبَى and إِعْتَاب branches nevertheless give restored goodwill particular prominence in ع ت ب.

عُتْبَى and رِضَا: removed displeasure and satisfaction

The lexical family of رِضَا concerns satisfaction and approval.

عُتْبَى also concerns satisfaction, usually following grievance or displeasure.

Someone may be satisfied with a friend without any prior disagreement.

If a wrong occurs and is corrected, restoring satisfaction, the situation is especially close to عُتْبَى and إِعْتَاب.

Their established use emphasizes approval restored after displeasure.

اسْتِعْتَاب and عُذْر: seeking approval and offering an excuse

ع ذ ر concerns excuses, explanation, and removing blame.

Istiʿtāb concerns seeking removed displeasure and renewed approval.

An excuse may be offered but rejected.

Someone may also admit fault without offering an excuse and gain approval through changed conduct.

The concepts are related but not identical.

Al-Rūm 30:57 includes both, so they should not collapse into one meaning in translation.

اسْتِعْتَاب and تَوْبَة: seeking approval and returning

ت و ب concerns return.

Religiously, human repentance involves turning from wrongdoing toward the right way.

Istiʿtāb may accompany that return, but its focus is seeking removal of displeasure and restored approval.

They may meet in one ethical process without being lexical equivalents.

Someone who wronged another may repent, while removing the injured person's grievance may also require restoring a right or repairing harm.

Al-Zumar 39:53 discourages despair of divine mercy.

Al-Zumar 39:54 commands returning to the Lord before punishment arrives.

These passages illuminate the difference between an opportunity to return in worldly life and the manifestation of consequences in the Hereafter.

That relationship does not justify directly translating يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ as “they repent” or “their repentance is accepted.”

عَتَبَ and سَخِطَ: grievance and displeasure

س خ ط concerns displeasure and dissatisfaction.

One branch of عَتَبَ also concerns displeasure.

In ع ت ب, مُعَاتَبَة and عُتْبَى give prominence to grievance and its removal in relationships.

سَخَط may simply name displeasure or anger without including a process of discussion and repair.

The distinction is not absolute, but helps explain the Quranically relevant branch.

أَعْتَبَ and عَفَا: restored approval and pardon

ع ف و concerns pardon, forgiveness, and relinquishing pursuit of a wrong or its effects.

Iʿtāb can likewise involve satisfaction after wrongdoing.

They are not necessarily identical.

Someone may pardon a wrong and forgo punishment while the previous relationship remains incompletely restored.

Changed conduct and reparation may also restore goodwill.

Pardon and renewed satisfaction can coincide while describing different aspects of the relationship.

ع ت ب and ع ت د: distinct roots

ع ت د concerns readiness and preparation.

Quranic عَتِيد denotes being present and ready, as in Qāf 50:18.

ع ت ب concerns grievance, displeasure, seeking approval, and the physical branches of thresholds and steps.

Sharing two initial letters does not make the roots identical.

أَعْتَدَ, “prepared,” must not be confused with أَعْتَبَ, “removed displeasure.”

6. Semantic relationships across Quranic passages

Seeking approval is not identical to actual reform

A key distinction separates a request for approval from action that can establish it.

Someone may repeatedly apologize while continuing the offending conduct.

The request exists without actual correction.

Another may admit fault, restore someone's right, and change conduct, joining the request to real action.

In the afterlife passages, seeking release from consequences does not itself prove that the person would act differently if returned to the previous circumstances.

Al-Anʿām 6:27 describes people wishing to be sent back.

Al-Anʿām 6:28 says that if returned, they would resume what they had been forbidden.

Wanting release from consequences is therefore not necessarily genuine reform.

This should not be generalized without evidence to everyone seeking forgiveness or another chance in worldly life. These verses concern a particular group and situation.

An ended opportunity does not negate mercy in worldly life

All four ع ت ب passages are situated in the Hereafter.

They should not induce despair of correction and return during worldly life.

Al-Zumar 39:53 describes divine forgiveness and prohibits despair.

Al-Zumar 39:54 commands return before punishment arrives.

A stage allowing understanding and reform differs from one in which consequences become manifest.

The ع ت ب passages emphasize the end of being called to make amends or the nonacceptance of the request in the afterlife situation described.

This relates to responsibility now: present opportunity should not be neglected on the assumption that it will remain indefinitely after consequences appear.

Istiʿtāb and human responsibility

In social life, removing a grievance need not stop at expressing regret.

Where financial harm has occurred, compensation can form part of correction.

Where someone's reputation has been harmed, correcting the statement and its effects matters.

Where duties have been neglected, changed conduct and fulfilled commitments can help restore trust.

These examples show that seeking approval can involve practical action.

They are possible ways of removing grievance, not a single exhaustive lexical definition.

The root relates grievance, requests for correction, and restored satisfaction; the dispute and circumstances determine the form of correction.

7. Translation suggestions for Forqan

Translations should distinguish displeasure, seeking its removal, and acceptance of the request.

عَتَبَ عَلَيْهِ: was aggrieved with him; disliked his conduct.

عَتْب and عِتَاب: grievance and reproach expressing displeasure.

عَاتَبَ: reproached; recalled another's objectionable conduct.

مُعَاتَبَة: reproach; discussion of a grievance.

عُتْبَى: restored goodwill after displeasure.

أَعْتَبَ: removed grievance; granted or brought about restored satisfaction.

اسْتَعْتَبَ: sought removal of displeasure and approval; or called upon the offender to correct conduct.

8. Synthesis

The root has several branches. Its principal interpersonal branch concerns grievance over conduct and efforts to restore goodwill.

عَتَبَ concerns displeasure; عَاتَبَ can express reproach; عُتْبَى concerns approval after grievance; اسْتَعْتَبَ concerns seeking its restoration.

Al-Nābighah's transmitted poetry illustrates restoring goodwill; Bishr's verse uses the same field ironically against anger and violent confrontation.

The material branch includes thresholds, steps, raised stages on a route, irregular movement, and certain defects. Imagery alone cannot establish a definitive historical relationship among all these uses.

The Quran uses the root five times, all in passages concerning the Hereafter and accountability.

Passive يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ occurs in Al-Naḥl 16:84, Al-Rūm 30:57, and Al-Jāthiyah 45:35: in those situations, the people are no longer called upon to seek restored approval.

Fuṣṣilat 41:24 joins active يَسْتَعْتِبُوا to passive participle الْمُعْتَبِينَ, distinguishing request from acceptance.

Istiʿtāb should not be reduced to a spoken apology or repentance. It may accompany apology, changed conduct, or reparation, while focusing on requested removal of displeasure.

The opportunity for reform in worldly life differs from afterlife accountability. The Quran invites return while describing later situations where the same opportunity is no longer available to the groups concerned.

Final definition: In interpersonal use, ع ت ب concerns displeasure and grievance, with related meanings of reproach, seeking correction, and restoring goodwill. اسْتِعْتَاب seeks removal of displeasure or renewed approval; إِعْتَاب concerns restoring satisfaction. Its five Quranic occurrences describe the end of being called to make amends or the nonacceptance of such requests in the Hereafter. The physical branch includes thresholds, steps, and raised terrain, but does not occur in these Quranic passages.
