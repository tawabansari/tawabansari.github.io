Core meaning: finding fault, belittling, and diminishing someone’s or something’s standing; in Qur’anic usage, despising people on the basis of outward judgment.

1. The root in early Arabic and pre-Islamic poetry

The Arabic root ز ر ي concerns faultfinding, blame, belittling, and disregarding worth. It appears in personal conduct, social relations, and judgments about others’ status, ability, or actions.

زَرَى عَلَيْهِ commonly means finding fault with or blaming someone. أَزْرَى بِهِ emphasizes belittling, diminishing standing, or treating someone as insignificant. اِزْدَرَاهُ likewise means despising or holding in contempt.

An early lexical analysis explains the core through احتقار and تهاون: treating something as insignificant and regarding it with dismissiveness.

This involves more than speaking about someone’s faults. Without naming any fault, a person may regard another as inferior in status, wealth, appearance, or standing.

The distinction matters: criticizing a particular action and despising the person are not identical.

Pre-Islamic evidence: contempt for poverty in ʿUrwah ibn al-Ward

The pre-Islamic poet ʿUrwah ibn al-Ward describes the standing of rich and poor. The following two selected verses are not consecutive in the transmitted poem:

دَعِينِي لِلْغِنَى أَسْعَى فَإِنِّي
رَأَيْتُ النَّاسَ شَرُّهُمُ الْفَقِيرُ

daʿīnī li-al-ghinā asʿā fa-innī / raʾaytu al-nāsa sharruhumu al-faqīru

وَيُقْصِيهِ النَّدِيُّ وَتَزْدَرِيهِ
حَلِيلَتُهُ وَيَنْهَرُهُ الصَّغِيرُ

wa-yuqṣīhi al-nadiyyu wa-tazdarīhi / ḥalīlatuhu wa-yanharuhu al-ṣaghīru

Meaning of these verses:

“Let me strive for wealth, for I have seen people regard the poor man as the lowest. The gathering excludes him, his wife despises him, and even a child rebukes him.”

تَزْدَرِيهِ describes the wife’s treatment of the poor man.

It is not merely criticism of a particular act; it belittles the person and his standing because of poverty.

The poet portrays wealth influencing respect and social position. A poor person may be dismissed despite honorable qualities.

The evidence illustrates contempt in social relationships in poetry transmitted from before Islam.

ʿUrwah is a pre-Islamic poet, and these verses circulate in poetry attributed to him and literary accounts. They support the early usage within that transmitted tradition, rather than independently dating every derivative of the root.

One poet’s portrayal should not become a judgment about all Arabs’ attitudes toward poverty. The evidence establishes usage, not a uniform social outlook.

Defining the attested early meaning

The poem attests social contempt, while lexical accounts document faultfinding and blame. Together they describe the family as follows:

زري concerns finding fault, blame, and diminishing standing; forms such as ازدرى describe despising or devaluing a person, even without establishing any actual fault.

This preserves two related aspects: faultfinding and contempt.

It also shows that contempt can arise from the observer’s judgment rather than a real defect in the person despised.

2. Important forms and derivatives

زَرَى يَزْرِي

The basic triliteral verb, as in زَرَى عَلَيْهِ, means finding fault, blaming, or expressing dissatisfaction with someone’s conduct.

زَرَى عَلَيْهِ فِعْلَهُ

zarā ʿalayhi fiʿlahu

“He found fault with his conduct and blamed him.”

This can criticize a particular act without necessarily denying all the person’s human worth.

زَرْي and زِرَايَة

These recorded verbal nouns denote faultfinding, blame, and reproach.

Depending on context, زراية may express objection or dissatisfaction with another’s conduct.

Not every instance should therefore be translated as severe contempt without examination.

زَارٍ

The active participle of زرى.

زَارٍ عَلَى فُلَانٍ

zārin ʿalā fulānin

“Someone who finds fault with or blames so-and-so.”

In some usages, the person regards another as unworthy of attention or respect.

Elsewhere, the meaning may be dissatisfaction with conduct alone.

أَزْرَى يُزْرِي

This Form IV verb, in أَزْرَى بِهِ, means belittling, diminishing standing, or treating someone or something as insignificant.

أَزْرَى بِالْأَمْرِ

azrā bi-al-amri

“He treated the matter as unimportant or failed to appreciate its worth.”

أَزْرَى بِفُلَانٍ

azrā bi-fulānin

“He diminished his standing or held him in contempt.”

Some usages concern attributing a fault or making an accusation that damages someone’s standing.

إِزْرَاء

The verbal noun of أزرى denotes contempt, disregard for worth, or diminished standing.

It may concern a person, knowledge, conduct, possessions, or another subject.

Its meaning is therefore not limited to verbal insult.

تَزَرَّى

This is Form V, tafaʿʿul. تَزَرَّى عَلَيْهِ means finding fault, reproaching, or expressing dissatisfaction.

It does not occur in the Qur’an.

زَارَى

Lexicons record زَارَى فُلَانٌ فُلَانًا for admonishing or reproaching another.

The behavior may be reciprocal in some usages.

Form III alone, however, does not prove that both parties always reproach each other equally or with the same intensity.

اِزْدَرَى يَزْدَرِي

This Form VIII verb means despising, holding in contempt, or treating someone or something as insignificant.

اِزْدَرَاهُ

izdarāhu

“He despised him.”

يَزْدَرِيهِ

yazdarīhi

“He despises him.”

A contemptuous judgment about the person or thing is prominent in this usage.

This is the branch used in the Qur’an.

اِزْدِرَاء

The verbal noun of ازدرى is also used as a loanword in Persian.

It means belittling, contempt, or looking down on another from a position of superiority.

مُزْدَرٍ

The active participle denotes someone who despises or devalues another.

مُزْدَرًى is the passive participle: someone or something held in contempt.

مِزْرَاء

This describes a person who frequently finds fault with or reproaches others.

Its form alone does not determine every aspect of the person’s character or intention.

زَرِيّ

سِقَاءٌ زَرِيٌّ is recorded for a medium-sized waterskin, between small and large.

This differs from contempt and faultfinding.

Shared letters do not prove that the vessel description derives from despising. This usage should be recorded separately without an unsupported connection to the evaluative senses.

Only تَزْدَرِي occurs in the Qur’an.

3. Morphology and the differences among زرى, أزرى, and ازدرى

The three verbs belong to one lexical family but show differences in usage.

زرى عليه commonly concerns faultfinding and blame.

أزرى به emphasizes devaluing, treating as insignificant, or damaging standing.

The familiar usage of ازدرى also concerns contempt and despising.

These are tendencies, not absolute boundaries across Arabic; lexicons sometimes explain the verbs through overlapping meanings.

Why does the morphological base ازترى appear as ازدرى?

اِزْدَرَى belongs to Form VIII.

Its morphological analysis places the Form VIII tāʾ after zāy, where it becomes dāl.

The analytical base is اِزْتَرَى, while the attested form is اِزْدَرَى. This explains a morphological rule, not an independently documented historical sequence.

The change is phonological and morphological. The dāl does not make the root ز د ر.

The root remains ز ر ي.

Nor does the sound change alone imply greater intensity, constant repetition, or deliberately conscious contempt.

The degree and character of the behavior depend on usage and context.

4. Qur’anic usage

The root occurs once in the Qur’an, in Hūd 11:31.

Hūd 11:31

وَلَا أَقُولُ لَكُمْ عِنْدِي خَزَائِنُ اللَّهِ وَلَا أَعْلَمُ الْغَيْبَ وَلَا أَقُولُ إِنِّي مَلَكٌ وَلَا أَقُولُ لِلَّذِينَ تَزْدَرِي أَعْيُنُكُمْ لَنْ يُؤْتِيَهُمُ اللَّهُ خَيْرًا ۖ اللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا فِي أَنْفُسِهِمْ ۖ إِنِّي إِذًا لَمِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

wa-lā aqūlu lakum ʿindī khazāʾinu Allāhi wa-lā aʿlamu al-ghayba wa-lā aqūlu innī malakun wa-lā aqūlu li-alladhīna tazdarī aʿyunukum lan yuʾtiyahumu Allāhu khayran Allāhu aʿlamu bi-mā fī anfusihim innī idhan la-mina al-ẓālimīn

Hūd 11:31

Translation:

“I do not tell you that I possess the treasuries of The God, nor do I know the unseen, nor do I say that I am an angel. Nor do I say of those whom your eyes despise that The God will never grant them good. The God knows best what is within them. Then I would indeed be among the wrongdoers.”

تَزْدَرِي is a Form VIII imperfect verb.

أَعْيُنُكُمْ, “your eyes,” is its subject.

Those despised are followers of Noah whom the leaders accord no worth or standing.

Why does the verse say “your eyes despise”?

Ordinary language can attribute a person’s perception and judgment to the eyes, as in “looking upon someone with contempt.”

The eye is not portrayed as an independent agent deciding another’s worth apart from the person’s judgment.

تَزْدَرِي أَعْيُنُكُمْ describes the addressees’ contemptuous regard.

Attributing the action to the eyes relates to their outward standards. They devalue those they consider socially inferior as followers of the messenger.

ازدراء itself does not mean seeing or looking; it means despising.

أعينكم supplies the image of vision within the sentence.

The connection with Hūd 11:27

In Hūd 11:27, the disbelieving leaders of Noah’s people say:

فَقَالَ الْمَلَأُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْ قَوْمِهِ مَا نَرَاكَ إِلَّا بَشَرًا مِثْلَنَا وَمَا نَرَاكَ اتَّبَعَكَ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ هُمْ أَرَاذِلُنَا بَادِيَ الرَّأْيِ وَمَا نَرَى لَكُمْ عَلَيْنَا مِنْ فَضْلٍ بَلْ نَظُنُّكُمْ كَاذِبِينَ

fa-qāla al-malaʾu alladhīna kafarū min qawmihi mā narāka illā basharan mithlanā wa-mā narāka ittabaʿaka illā alladhīna hum arādhilunā bādiya al-raʾyi wa-mā narā lakum ʿalaynā min faḍlin bal naẓunnukum kādhibīn

Hūd 11:27

Translation:

“The leaders of his people who disbelieved said: We see you only as a human like ourselves, and we see that only those we regard as our lowliest have followed you, at first appearance. We see no advantage you have over us; rather, we think you are liars.”

أَرَاذِلُنَا describes people whom the leaders consider lowly.

It conveys their judgment of Noah’s followers, not the Qur’an itself declaring those people morally inferior.

In Hūd 11:31, Noah answers this outlook.

He refuses to regard people as excluded from divine good merely because of social standing or appearance.

This connection identifies تزدري as contempt based on outward and social standards.

Outward appearance and what is within a person

Hūd 11:31 continues:

اللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا فِي أَنْفُسِهِمْ

Allāhu aʿlamu bi-mā fī anfusihim

Hūd 11:31

“The God knows best what is within them.”

The sentence contrasts the leaders’ outward judgment with God’s knowledge.

They judge the followers through social position and personal impressions, while Noah refuses to declare them excluded from divine good.

This does not mean that Noah claims complete certainty about all his followers’ inner intentions.

On the contrary, he explicitly says in the same verse that he does not know the unseen.

It therefore does not follow that everyone socially disadvantaged is necessarily morally superior simply because of that disadvantage.

Just as wealth and status do not establish greater human worth, poverty alone does not prove pure intentions or right conduct.

Judgment must go beyond appearance and social standing; complete knowledge of the inner person belongs to God.

Contempt and wrongdoing

Noah concludes:

إِنِّي إِذًا لَمِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

innī idhan la-mina al-ẓālimīn

Hūd 11:31

“Then I would indeed be among the wrongdoers.”

This refers back to his preceding statements.

Declaring those despised by the leaders excluded from divine good would be wrongdoing. The surrounding verses also reject expelling the believers.

This is therefore more than a simple difference in taste.

Contempt can lead to injustice when it becomes a basis for denying rights, respect, or good.

That conclusion follows from the verse as a whole. زري does not itself mean injustice, but its context connects it with justice and mistaken judgment.

5. From contempt to exclusion

In Hūd 11:29, Noah states:

وَمَا أَنَا بِطَارِدِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا

wa-mā anā bi-ṭāridi alladhīna āmanū

Hūd 11:29

“I will not drive away those who have believed.”

Then Hūd 11:30 asks:

وَيَا قَوْمِ مَنْ يَنْصُرُنِي مِنَ اللَّهِ إِنْ طَرَدْتُهُمْ أَفَلَا تَذَكَّرُونَ

wa-yā qawmi man yanṣurunī mina Allāhi in ṭaradtuhum a-fa-lā tadhakkarūn

Hūd 11:30

Translation:

“My people, who would help me against The God if I drove them away? Will you not take heed?”

These verses show that the contempt concerns more than a private opinion: it affects the followers’ place in the community and the prospect of their expulsion.

The context suggests the leaders’ objection to accepting those they considered lowly alongside themselves. This is a contextual inference, not a separate explicit command to expel them quoted here.

Noah rejects their exclusion.

Social contempt can thus lead to practical deprivation. When power and prestige determine acceptance or rejection, rights and justice become subordinate to social position.

ʿUrwah’s pre-Islamic poem also links contempt with exclusion.

It portrays a poor man excluded from the gathering and despised even within his household.

Noah’s words connect disdain for his followers with the prospect of driving them away.

The comparison shows the Qur’an using a familiar word from Arabic social relationships to expose mistaken judgment and exclusion based on outward standing in Noah’s story.

6. Connections with other Qur’anic verses

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:111 and Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:114

In Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:111, Noah’s people say:

قَالُوا أَنُؤْمِنُ لَكَ وَاتَّبَعَكَ الْأَرْذَلُونَ

qālū a-nuʾminu laka wa-ittabaʿaka al-ardhalūn

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:111

“They said: Shall we believe you when the lowliest have followed you?”

This presents the same contemptuous outlook from another angle.

In Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:114, Noah replies:

وَمَا أَنَا بِطَارِدِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ

wa-mā anā bi-ṭāridi al-muʾminīn

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:114

“I will not drive away the believers.”

Both Qur’anic accounts confront contempt and the question of excluding followers with the messenger’s clear response.

The opponents’ class judgments do not determine human worth.

Al-Anʿām 6:52

Al-Anʿām 6:52 commands that those calling upon their Lord morning and evening, seeking His face, should not be driven away.

This relates thematically to Noah’s conduct.

Both concern people whom others may regard as lacking social prestige.

The Qur’an does not make wealth, office, or the approval of the powerful the sole criterion for relating to them.

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:11

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:11 forbids believers to ridicule one another, reminding them that those ridiculed may be better than the mockers.

It connects conceptually with ز ر ي through mistaken judgments of human worth.

In Hūd 11:31, the leaders despise Noah’s followers.

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:11 prohibits ridiculing and belittling others.

In both, superficial personal judgment should not determine another’s worth.

سخر and زري are nevertheless not lexically identical. Ridicule may involve speech, behavior, or laughter, whereas contempt can remain an attitude.

7. Comparison with related roots

ز ر ي (اِزْدِرَاء) and ر ذ ل (رَذَالَة)

رذل concerns lowliness or inferiority and may describe social position or a negative evaluation of someone.

ازدراء is the act of despising another.

In Hūd 11:27, أراذلنا is the designation the leaders apply to Noah’s followers.

In Hūd 11:31, تزدري describes those leaders’ contemptuous conduct and outlook.

One concerns a description attributed to people, the other the act of despising them.

ز ر ي (اِزْدِرَاء) and س خ ر (سُخْرِيَة)

سخر concerns ridicule and mockery.

ازدراء means treating someone as insignificant or beneath regard.

Someone may despise another without openly mocking them.

Conversely, mockery may express contempt.

The behaviors often accompany one another but are not lexically identical.

ز ر ي (زَرَى) and ع ي ب (عَيْب)

عيب denotes a defect or shortcoming.

زرى عليه can mean finding fault with someone or their conduct.

ازدرى may occur without any real defect: it concerns a person’s contemptuous judgment of another.

Poverty is not inherently a moral defect, yet ʿUrwah’s poem portrays the poor man despised for that condition.

Contempt can therefore rest on a mistaken, unfounded judgment.

ز ر ي (اِزْدِرَاء) and ه و ن (هَوَان)

هوان concerns humiliation and low standing.

ازدراء describes belittling or holding another in contempt.

Social contempt may cause someone to be regarded as dishonored or insignificant.

Not every difficult or low-status social condition, however, results from deliberate contempt by others.

Semantic proximity does not make هوان and ازدراء the same root.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

The following equivalents suit the main forms. These proposals do not replace installed verse translations or existing authored explanations.

زَرَى عَلَيْهِ: found fault with him; blamed him.

زَرَى عَلَيْهِ عَمَلَهُ: criticized his conduct or reproached him for it.

زَرْي and زِرَايَة: faultfinding, blame, or reproach.

زَارٍ: one who finds fault or reproaches.

أَزْرَى بِهِ: despised him, diminished his standing, or devalued him.

إِزْرَاء: contempt, belittling, or disregard for worth and standing.

تَزَرَّى عَلَيْهِ: found fault with or reproached him.

اِزْدَرَى: despised, held in contempt, or treated as insignificant.

اِزْدِرَاء: despising or contempt.

مُزْدَرٍ: one who despises or belittles.

مُزْدَرًى: someone or something despised.

مِزْرَاء: someone who frequently finds fault with others.

Proposed rendering of Hūd 11:31

For the phrase:

وَلَا أَقُولُ لِلَّذِينَ تَزْدَرِي أَعْيُنُكُمْ لَنْ يُؤْتِيَهُمُ اللَّهُ خَيْرًا

wa-lā aqūlu li-alladhīna tazdarī aʿyunukum lan yuʾtiyahumu Allāhu khayran

Hūd 11:31

A suitable rendering is:

“Nor do I say of those whom your eyes despise that The God will never grant them good.”

“Despise” preserves the direct meaning of تزدري.

Attributing the action to أَعْيُنُكُمْ also preserves the verse’s imagery.

A brief root definition could read:

“Finding fault, despising, and treating as insignificant.”

For the verse-specific meaning:

“Despising Noah’s followers on the basis of outward and social judgments.”

“Looking” is insufficient for تزدري: one may look at another without despising them.

“Hating” is also imprecise, since hatred and contempt differ. Someone may consider another inferior without feeling hatred toward them.

9. Conclusion

Poetry transmitted from before Islam attests contempt, while lexical accounts also document faultfinding and blame in ز ر ي.

ʿUrwah’s poem uses تزدري for despising someone because of poverty and treating his standing as low.

زرى عليه commonly concerns blame and faultfinding, while familiar uses of أزرى به and ازدرى emphasize belittling and diminished standing.

Only تَزْدَرِي occurs in the Qur’an, in Hūd 11:31.

It describes the leaders’ treatment of Noah’s followers, whom they regarded as lowly and insignificant.

Noah rejects that judgment and refuses to declare his followers excluded from divine good or to drive them away on that basis.

The Qur’anic usage thus relates to human worth, outward judgment, social discrimination, and justice.

The root itself does not mean poverty, discrimination, or injustice. Those concepts arise from the story’s context and the consequences of contempt.

In Qur’anic usage, the root means despising and devaluing another: conduct that may arise from outward judgment and a sense of superiority without reflecting the despised person’s actual worth.
