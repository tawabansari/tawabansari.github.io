Core meaning: folding, rolling, and gathering something from an extended state into a compact one; broader uses include concealing and traversing.

1. The root in early Arabic

The triliteral root ط و ي is known through طَوَى and its verbal noun طَيّ. Its principal branch concerns folding or rolling so that spread-out parts approach and overlap. الطَّيُّ contrasts with النَّشْرُ: the latter unfolds or spreads, while the former gathers what was extended.

Cloth, hide, a written sheet, a scroll, or another flexible object may be folded or rolled. It need not be destroyed or reduced in substance: its shape and the arrangement of its parts change. Once gathered, it occupies less space, and some parts may cover others.

This image makes broader uses intelligible: concealing something within, bringing a matter to a close, or passing through distances and stages of travel.

A witness from pre-Islamic poetry

The Muʿallaqah attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā contains:

وَكَانَ طَوَى كَشْحًا عَلَى مُسْتَكِنَّةٍ

wa-kāna ṭawā kashḥan ʿalā mustakinnatin

فَلَا هُوَ أَبْدَاهَا وَلَمْ يَتَقَدَّمِ

fa-lā huwa abdāhā wa-lam yataqaddami

Translation: He had harboured a hidden design within himself; he had neither disclosed it nor yet advanced to carry it out.

كَشْح means the flank or side of the body. طَوَى كَشْحًا عَلَى مُسْتَكِنَّةٍ depicts retaining and concealing something within, as though enclosing a secret behind one's flank.

The verse concerns Ḥuṣayn ibn Ḍamḍam, who concealed a vengeful intention until an opportunity arose.

The quoted wording is attested in early explanations of the verse. The relevant witness is طَوَى كَشْحًا for harbouring something within; it does not depend on choosing among variants of the second half-line.

The verse is preserved in familiar transmissions and early commentaries of Zuhayr's Muʿallaqah. Its attribution matters for the antiquity of the figurative use, although the particular verse's date is not independently established.

It shows that keeping something concealed within was an established early use of this family.

Physical folding is also clearly recorded in classical Arabic, and the Quran directly attests it in the language of revelation. Zuhayr's verse alone does not demonstrate that every later extension already existed before Islam.

2. The word family and its extensions

Important forms include طَوَى, يَطْوِي, طَيّ, طِيَّة, طَوِيَّة, طَاوٍ, مَطْوِيّ, انْطَوَى, تَطَوَّى, and طَوِيّ.

The family has several branches and is not confined to folding cloth.

Folding and rolling up

طَوَيْتُ الثَّوْبَ means “I folded the garment”; طَوَيْتُ الصَّحِيفَةَ means folding or rolling a sheet or written page.

طَيّ names the action, while مَطْوِيّ describes something folded or rolled.

انْطَوَى can describe something gathering or curling into itself, such as cloth or a bent, contracted body.

The principal meaning is gathering extended parts into a compact arrangement, rather than the object's destruction.

Coils and folds

Arabic uses forms of this family for garment folds, layers of fat, intestinal coils, and the coils of a snake.

أَطْوَاء denotes overlapping folds or layers. مَطَاوِي is likewise recorded for folds, coils, or layers.

When a snake coils, its bodily parts approach and wind around one another. A comparable image appears in fabric folds and internal bodily coils.

The root is therefore not restricted to making a straight crease. Rolling, bending, and gathering layers also belong to its range.

Concealing and harbouring

طَوَى كَشْحَهُ عَلَى أَمْرٍ describes concealing a decision, secret, or intention within oneself.

طَوَى عَنْهُ الْحَدِيثَ is also recorded for withholding an account from someone.

As in Zuhayr's verse, the concealed object is now a decision, statement, or thought, rather than cloth or writing.

The shared image is intelligible: folding cloth can hide part of it, just as someone can retain a statement or intention without revealing it.

طَوِيَّة denotes an intention, inward disposition, or what one conceals in the heart. طِيَّة can denote an intention, destination, or need being pursued.

Intention should not be generalized to every derivative. Quranic نَطْوِي concerns the heaven and foregrounds the image of folding, not concealing an intention.

Traversing and leaving behind

طَوَى الْبِلَادَ is also recorded for traversing lands and covering distances.

A traveller covers the distance between successive stopping places. The expression presents the distance spread ahead as being gathered up and left behind through movement.

طَيُّ الْأَرْضِ can accordingly concern travelling over the earth and bringing distance within reach.

This is an ordinary linguistic use for travel. Later technical expressions or accounts of extraordinary movement cannot be imposed on the root's earliest meaning without evidence.

Hunger and an empty abdomen

الطَّوَى also denotes hunger. طَاوٍ and طَيَّان can describe a hungry person whose abdomen is empty and drawn in.

This branch evokes the physical image of an empty, contracted abdomen.

An image-based relationship with folding or contraction is conceivable, but hunger is not part of every derivative's meaning. The transitive طَوَى, “fold,” must also be distinguished from forms expressing hunger.

طَوِيّ: a masonry-lined well

طَوِيّ is recorded for a well whose walls have been lined with stone or brick.

طَيُّ الْبِئْرِ concerns building and lining the well's walls by placing materials alongside and above one another.

This specialized well-building sense is recorded lexically but has no Quranic derivative.

The family thus includes folding and rolling alongside concealment, traversing, hunger, and well-building. Similar imagery does not establish a single history or certain common origin for every branch.

3. Quranic occurrences

This study considers five occurrences in four verses and four forms: three direct derivatives concerning folding, and two occurrences of the name طُوًى, discussed separately because its derivation is uncertain.

نَطْوِي occurs once, طَيّ once, مَطْوِيَّات once, and طُوًى twice.

The first three directly concern folding or rolling. The two instances of طُوًى belong to the Moses narrative and require separate semantic treatment.

A. نَطْوِي and طَيّ: rolling up the heaven

يَوْمَ نَطْوِي السَّمَاءَ كَطَيِّ السِّجِلِّ لِلْكُتُبِ ۚ كَمَا بَدَأْنَا أَوَّلَ خَلْقٍ نُعِيدُهُ ۚ وَعْدًا عَلَيْنَا ۚ إِنَّا كُنَّا فَاعِلِينَ

yawma naṭwī al-samāʾa ka-ṭayyi al-sijilli li-l-kutubi ka-mā badaʾnā awwala khalqin nuʿīduhu waʿdan ʿalaynā innā kunnā fāʿilīn

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:104

The day We roll up the heaven like a scroll rolled over writings. As We began the first creation, We shall repeat it—a promise binding upon Us. We shall indeed accomplish it.

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:104

Two forms of ط و ي occur in the same sentence:

نَطْوِي is an imperfect verb from طَوَى, meaning “We roll up” or “We fold.”

طَيّ is its verbal noun, naming folding or rolling.

The sentence begins with يَوْمَ and describes a future event. The God speaks of rolling up the heaven through the familiar image of a written scroll being rolled up.

The scroll image

Before modern book formats became widespread, writing could be placed on long sheets or scrolls rolled for storage.

An open scroll exposes an extended written surface. Rolling it brings its parts over one another and out of their spread-out arrangement.

كَطَيِّ السِّجِلِّ لِلْكُتُبِ is understood here through that image. السجل has other transmitted explanations, but “scroll” is the reading adopted in this translation.

The comparative كَـ likens the heaven's rolling to the rolling of a scroll, making the event intelligible through a familiar human action.

The clear point of comparison is a change from an extended to a gathered, rolled arrangement.

نطوي alone specifies neither the heaven's resulting material, its final dimensions, nor the physical mechanism. These details do not follow from the lexical meaning.

Nor should the image be translated merely as destruction or disappearance. طَيّ primarily denotes gathering and rolling; absolute destruction is not necessary to that action.

Rolling up and renewed creation

The verse continues: كَمَا بَدَأْنَا أَوَّلَ خَلْقٍ نُعِيدُهُ.

This places the rolling of the heaven alongside the end of the present order and its renewal.

The image of a gathered surface thus accompanies a divine promise of renewed creation.

نُعِيدُهُ belongs to ع و د and expresses returning or repeating. Renewed creation should not replace the lexical meaning of نطوي.

نطوي conveys rolling up; نعيده conveys repeating. Their relationship comes from the verse's construction and context.

B. مَطْوِيَّات: the rolled-up heavens

وَمَا قَدَرُوا اللَّهَ حَقَّ قَدْرِهِ وَالْأَرْضُ جَمِيعًا قَبْضَتُهُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ وَالسَّمَاوَاتُ مَطْوِيَّاتٌ بِيَمِينِهِ ۚ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَىٰ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

wa-mā qadarū allāha ḥaqqa qadrihi wa-l-arḍu jamīʿan qabḍatuhu yawma al-qiyāmati wa-l-samāwātu maṭwiyyātun bi-yamīnihi subḥānahu wa-taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn

Al-Zumar 39:67

They did not esteem The God as He deserves. The whole earth is in His grasp on the Day of Resurrection, and the heavens are rolled up in His right hand. Glory be to Him, exalted above what they associate with Him.

Al-Zumar 39:67

مَطْوِيَّات is the sound feminine plural of مَطْوِيَّة, a passive participle from ط و ي.

Its grammatical contrast with نَطْوِي is clear: the verb attributes rolling to an agent, while مطويات describes things in the resulting rolled-up condition.

السَّمَاوَاتُ is plural here, accompanied by feminine-plural مَطْوِيَّاتٌ. Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:104 uses singular السَّمَاءَ. Translation should preserve that distinction, which alone does not prove two separate events.

مَطْوِيَّات and قَبْضَتُهُ

The verse describes the earth as قَبْضَتُهُ and the heavens as مَطْوِيَّاتٌ بِيَمِينِهِ.

قَبْضَة belongs to ق ب ض and concerns grasping, possession, or gathering within a grasp.

مَطْوِيَّات supplies the image of rolled-up heavens.

Together, these images emphasize divine power and sovereignty over earth and heavens. The verse begins by criticizing failure to esteem The God properly and ends by declaring Him above association with partners.

The image therefore serves an argument about divine power and oneness, rather than merely describing celestial dimensions or shape.

بِيَمِينِهِ should not be converted into a claim of a humanlike physical hand. The verse declares divine transcendence above association with partners, while Al-Shūrā 42:11 states that nothing is like Him.

The imagery of right hand, grasp, and rolled-up heavens conveys sovereign greatness in intelligible language. It does not establish divine bodily anatomy or the physical mechanism of the Hereafter.

مَطْوِيَّات nevertheless retains its lexical meaning of rolled up or gathered. Reducing it simply to “under His power” would replace the verse's image with a contextual implication.

4. طُوًى: the sacred valley's name or a description?

Two occurrences of طُوًى in the Moses narrative are considered alongside this family, without treating the name's derivation as certain.

First occurrence

إِنِّي أَنَا رَبُّكَ فَاخْلَعْ نَعْلَيْكَ ۖ إِنَّكَ بِالْوَادِ الْمُقَدَّسِ طُوًى

innī anā rabbuka fa-khlaʿ naʿlayka innaka bi-l-wādi al-muqaddasi ṭuwā

Ṭā Hā 20:12

Indeed, I am your Lord. Remove your sandals; you are in the sacred valley of Ṭuwā.

Ṭā Hā 20:12

Second occurrence

إِذْ نَادَاهُ رَبُّهُ بِالْوَادِ الْمُقَدَّسِ طُوًى

idh nādāhu rabbuhu bi-l-wādi al-muqaddasi ṭuwā

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:16

When his Lord called him in the sacred valley of Ṭuwā.

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:16

In both verses, طُوًى follows الْوَادِ الْمُقَدَّسِ.

طُوًى is recorded as a place name, and many lexicographers identify it as the valley where Moses was called.

On that reading, الْوَادِ الْمُقَدَّسِ طُوًى means “the sacred valley of Ṭuwā”: a valley bearing that name.

Other lexical explanations of the form and meaning also exist.

The proposed sense of repetition or doubling

Some lexical sources associate طُوًى or طِوًى with something folded twice or repeated.

Some interpreters accordingly understand the phrase as a valley sanctified twice, or with repeated sanctification.

This is a proposed interpretation based on that lexical sense. The verses themselves contain no separate expression “twice”: they give الْمُقَدَّسِ followed by طُوًى. The interpretation should therefore not be presented as an explicit, undisputed statement of the text.

Reading طُوًى as a place name is grammatically natural after الوادي المقدس, where it can identify the valley.

Known recitations read طُوًى with and without tanwīn. Explanations include its status as the name of a place or locality. Case behaviour alone does not prove the twice-sanctified interpretation.

The place-name reading is the clearer choice for the base translation. A possible historical relationship with folding or repetition can remain in the lexical discussion; resemblance to a root alone cannot determine a name's established meaning.

Distinguishing Ṭuwā from طَوَى

This distinction matters when presenting a root study.

In نَطْوِي السَّمَاءَ, the verb directly expresses rolling up.

In مَطْوِيَّاتٌ, the passive participle describes a rolled-up condition.

In الْوَادِ الْمُقَدَّسِ طُوًى, however, the word is understood as a place name.

Even if the name originally arose from this family, its derivation need not be the intended meaning every time it is used. Established place names can be used without reference to their original meanings.

“The valley folded twice” or “the valley sanctified twice” should therefore not be presented as the certain, direct translation.

5. Related roots

Ṭ W Y and N SH R: folding and spreading

These roots contrast in one of their principal uses.

طَيّ gathers or folds something previously extended; نَشْر opens or spreads something gathered or closed.

Al-Takwīr 81:10, for example, speaks of records being spread open.

A scroll illustrates the distinction: initially rolled shut, it is opened for reading and then rolled again.

Not every Quranic use of ن ش ر reverses folding. The material contrast should not be generalized to every derivative of both families.

Ṭ W Y and Q B Ḍ: rolling and grasping

ق ب ض concerns taking, gathering within a grasp, and holding in one's possession.

Al-Zumar 39:67 combines the families, describing the earth in divine grasp and the heavens rolled up.

قبض emphasizes grasp and possession; طي emphasizes a change from an extended to a gathered or rolled arrangement.

One can fold cloth without clenching it in a fist, or grasp a stone without folding it.

The verbs overlap in gathering but are not identical.

Ṭ W Y and K W R: folding and winding around

ك و ر concerns winding, gathering, and wrapping around.

Al-Zumar 39:5 uses يُكَوِّرُ for winding night over day and day over night.

طوى, by contrast, can express folding or rolling something upon itself.

In the familiar image of تكوير, wrapping one thing over another is prominent. طي in folding a sheet or rolling a scroll highlights gathering the parts of one thing.

This is a tendency, not an absolute division: folding and winding can occur together.

Night and day are described as wound over one another in Al-Zumar 39:5; in Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:104, the heaven is compared to a rolled scroll. The different images should be preserved.

Ṭ W Y and L F F: folding and wrapping

ل ف ف also concerns wrapping, bringing together, and gathering.

لفّ and طيّ can be very close, both applying to cloth and other flexible objects.

طَيّ can foreground folding one part onto another, whereas لفّ more often concerns wrapping something around itself or another object.

“Folding” and “wrapping” can therefore clarify a distinction in some contexts, but this is not an absolute rule for all Arabic usage.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

First determine whether the word expresses a folding action, a resulting condition, or a place name.

For material طَوَى and يَطْوِي, “fold,” “roll,” “roll up,” or “gather” may fit. A sheet or garment naturally takes “fold”; a scroll or extended object gathered by rolling may be better rendered “roll up.”

For طَيّ, “folding” or “rolling” is suitable.

مَطْوِيّ and مَطْوِيَّات can be rendered “folded,” “rolled,” or “rolled up,” according to context.

For طُوًى in the Moses narrative, retain the name Ṭuwā and discuss lexical alternatives in the study.

Proposed verse translations

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:104:

“The day We roll up the heaven like a scroll rolled over writings. As We began the first creation, We shall repeat it—a promise binding upon Us. We shall indeed accomplish it.”

Al-Zumar 39:67:

“They did not esteem The God as He deserves. The whole earth is in His grasp on the Day of Resurrection, and the heavens are rolled up in His right hand. Glory be to Him, exalted above what they associate with Him.”

Ṭā Hā 20:12:

“Indeed, I am your Lord. Remove your sandals; you are in the sacred valley of Ṭuwā.”

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:16:

“When his Lord called him in the sacred valley of Ṭuwā.”

Brief meanings proposed for word boxes

Brief root meaning: folding, rolling, and gathering something extended; other uses include concealing and traversing.

نَطْوِي — Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:104: We roll up; gathering the heaven from its extended state.

طَيّ — Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:104: folding or rolling; here, rolling a written scroll.

مَطْوِيَّات — Al-Zumar 39:67: rolled up and gathered; describing the heavens on the Day of Resurrection.

طُوًى — Ṭā Hā 20:12: Ṭuwā, the name of the sacred valley where Moses was called.

طُوًى — Al-Nāziʿāt 79:16: Ṭuwā, the same sacred valley in the Moses narrative.

7. Conclusion

The principal branch of ط و ي concerns folding, rolling, and gathering something extended. Cloth, sheets, and scrolls illustrate it clearly; other uses concern concealment, bodily contraction, and traversing distance.

The verse attributed to Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā attests figurative طوى for harbouring a secret or intention in early Arabic. Dictionaries also record folds, hunger, travel, and well lining. Each branch requires its own evidence rather than being forced into a narrow definition.

The five occurrences studied here comprise three direct folding derivatives and two instances of the separately discussed place name. In Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:104, نطوي and طيّ describe rolling up the heaven like a scroll. In Al-Zumar 39:67, مطويات describes the rolled-up heavens. Both passages concern divine power and Resurrection, with one expressing the action and the other its resulting condition.

In Ṭā Hā 20:12 and Al-Nāziʿāt 79:16, طُوًى accompanies الوادي المقدس. The clearer reading identifies the valley where Moses was called. Proposed derivations involving doubling or repeated sanctification do not replace the proper name in direct translation.

Final definition: the principal branch of ط و ي describes folding or rolling so that extended parts approach and overlap. Related uses include concealment and traversing. Quranic نطوي, طيّ, and مطويات concern the action or result of rolling up; طُوًى in the Moses narrative functions as a place name whose exact derivation is not established by the verses themselves.
