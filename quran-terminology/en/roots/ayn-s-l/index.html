Central meaning: honey, the sweet food produced by bees. Another lexical branch records swift and oscillating movement.

1. The root in early Arabic

The triliteral root ع س ل has two important branches. One concerns honey, its production and collection, and extensions based on its sweetness and pleasantness. The other concerns swift, oscillating animal movement and the flexing and shaking of objects.

In early Arabic, عَسَل names the sweet substance produced by bees. It names a recognized food rather than everything sweet.

Related forms include عَسَّلَ, عَاسِل, عَسَّال, مُعَسَّل, and مَعْسُول, concerning production, collection, addition, and mixing of honey.

عَاسِل can name someone who collects and extracts honey from its location. طَعَامٌ مُعَسَّلٌ is food prepared with honey.

The name also extends to similar substances. Sweet date syrup and certain plant exudates have been compared with honey or called honey because of their sweetness or appearance. Bee honey remains the familiar primary referent.

The other branch includes عَسَلَ and عَسَلان for swift, oscillating movement, recorded for wolves and foxes, horses, and vibrating spears.

These branches should not be reduced to an invented common meaning. Without historical evidence, neither the naming of honey from its slow flow nor the naming of a wolf's gait from honey's movement can be asserted.

Root study should identify attested meanings rather than impose a shared image at any cost.

For the Quranic occurrence, عَسَل has its familiar meaning of honey. Its application to paradise does not establish how that honey is produced. The movement branch should not be imported into this noun's meaning.

2. Early poetic evidence and its dating

Honey was a familiar food in Arabia and neighboring regions. Collecting it from natural bee colonies and using it in food formed part of the material background of the word.

One early witness is attributed to Labid ibn Rabi'ah:

بِأَشْهَبَ مِنْ أَبْكَارِ مُزْنِ سَحَابَةٍ
وَأَرْيِ دُبُورٍ شَارَهُ النَّحْلَ عَاسِلُ

bi-ashhaba min abkāri muzni saḥābatin / wa-aryi dubūrin shārahu al-naḥla ʿāsilu

“With clear water from a cloud's first rain, and bees' honey extracted by a honey gatherer.”

The verse is understood as describing wine mixed with rainwater and honey. أَرْي is another name for honey, and عَاسِل denotes its gatherer or extractor.

The sweet substance is called أَرْي while its collector is عَاسِل, showing the root's connection with harvesting honey.

Labid lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods. This verse is transmitted in lexical sources, but its individual date has not been independently established in this review. It is useful evidence of early usage, without automatically proving a date before the Quran's revelation.

A second witness, attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Aws ibn Hajar, concerns movement and oscillation:

تَقَاكَ بِكَعْبٍ وَاحِدٍ وَتَلُذُّهُ
يَدَاكَ إِذَا مَا هُزَّ بِالْكَفِّ يَعْسِلُ

taqāka bi-kaʿbin wāḥidin wa-taladhdhuhu / yadāka idhā mā huzza bi-l-kaffi yaʿsilu

“It shields you with a single shaft joint, and your hands delight in it; when shaken in the hand, it quivers.”

يَعْسِلُ here describes a spear's movement and vibration, rather than sweetness or coating with honey. كَعْب concerns a joint of the spear shaft; the lexical point is its flexing when shaken.

This witness shows that عَسَلَ could be a movement verb in early Arabic without directly referring to honey.

The food and movement branches thus have early attestations, but these witnesses establish neither their historical sequence nor a single shared semantic origin.

3. Lexical and Quranic forms

Important forms include عَسَل, عَسَّلَ, تَعْسِيل, عَاسِل, عَسَّال, مُعَسَّل, مَعْسُول, عَسَلَ, عَسَلان, عَسُول, and عَسَّالَة.

These forms have different meanings in different uses.

عَسَل: honey, the sweet substance produced by bees.

عَسَّلَ: in the food branch, producing honey or coating and sweetening something with it.

عَاسِل and عَسَّال: a honey gatherer; additional senses are recorded according to the construction and object described. These senses should not be generalized to every occurrence.

مُعَسَّل and مَعْسُول: mixed with or sweetened by honey.

عَسَلَ and عَسَلان: in the movement branch, swift or oscillating movement, such as a wolf's gait or a spear's flexing.

عَسُول: in some early uses, an animal characterized by a particular enduring or swift gait; also recorded for a supple, quivering spear.

Shared root letters do not make these forms identical in meaning. Food-related مُعَسَّل and movement-related عَسُول are distinct uses.

The Quran contains only عَسَلٍ from this family, in Muhammad 47:15.

It is a noun, not a verb or adjective. In مِنْ عَسَلٍ it is genitive after مِنْ.

It is indefinite, followed by the descriptive word مُصَفًّى.

The noun's meaning should therefore be distinguished from the added description.

4. عَسَلٍ مُصَفًّى: purified honey in the description of paradise

The root's sole direct Quranic occurrence is in Muhammad:

مَثَلُ الْجَنَّةِ الَّتِي وُعِدَ الْمُتَّقُونَ ۖ فِيهَا أَنْهَارٌ مِنْ مَاءٍ غَيْرِ آسِنٍ وَأَنْهَارٌ مِنْ لَبَنٍ لَمْ يَتَغَيَّرْ طَعْمُهُ وَأَنْهَارٌ مِنْ خَمْرٍ لَذَّةٍ لِلشَّارِبِينَ وَأَنْهَارٌ مِنْ عَسَلٍ مُصَفًّى ۖ وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا مِنْ كُلِّ الثَّمَرَاتِ وَمَغْفِرَةٌ مِنْ رَبِّهِمْ ۖ كَمَنْ هُوَ خَالِدٌ فِي النَّارِ وَسُقُوا مَاءً حَمِيمًا فَقَطَّعَ أَمْعَاءَهُمْ

mathalu al-jannati allatī wuʿida al-muttaqūna fīhā anhārun min māʾin ghayri āsinin wa-anhārun min labanin lam yataghayyar ṭaʿmuhu wa-anhārun min khamrin ladhdhatin li-l-shāribīna wa-anhārun min ʿasalin muṣaffan wa-lahum fīhā min kulli al-thamarāti wa-maghfiratun min rabbihim ka-man huwa khālidun fī al-nāri wa-suqū māʾan ḥamīman fa-qaṭṭaʿa amʿāʾahum

Muḥammad 47:15

Muhammad 47:15

“The description of the garden promised to the God-conscious: in it are rivers of water unspoiled, rivers of milk whose taste has not changed, rivers of wine delightful to drinkers, and rivers of purified honey. There they have every kind of fruit and forgiveness from their Lord. Is such an outcome like that of one abiding in the Fire, where they are given scalding water that tears their intestines apart?”

Four drinks or flowing substances describe paradise here: water, milk, wine, and honey.

Each receives a particular description.

The water is غَيْرِ آسِنٍ, not spoiled or stale.

The milk is described by لَمْ يَتَغَيَّرْ طَعْمُهُ: its taste has not changed.

The wine is لَذَّةٍ لِلشَّارِبِينَ: delightful to drinkers.

The honey is مُصَفًّى: purified or clarified.

These qualities come from distinct words in the verse, not from ع س ل alone.

The meaning of مُصَفًّى

مُصَفًّى belongs to ص ف و and concerns clarification, purification, and removing impurities.

It is a Form II passive participle describing عَسَلٍ here.

In ordinary usage, clarifying honey may mean removing wax particles, hive debris, or other unwanted matter.

The verse does not describe how paradise's honey is produced or purified. مُصَفًّى states its quality, without establishing a particular apparatus, filter, or physical procedure there.

Purification need not mean colorlessness. Color, clarity, texture, and impurity content are distinguishable. “Colorless honey” or “completely transparent honey” would narrow the wording without evidence.

The preferred rendering of عَسَلٍ مُصَفًّى is “purified honey,” or “clarified honey.”

The meaning of أَنْهَارٌ مِنْ عَسَلٍ

أَنْهَارٌ is the plural of نَهْر, a river or stream. مِنْ عَسَلٍ identifies the substance of these rivers.

The expression describes rivers of honey, rather than merely water containing a little honey.

Earthly honey's viscosity, stickiness, flow rate, and storage conditions should not automatically be attributed to honey in paradise.

The verse names a familiar blessing without specifying all its natural or physical properties.

The opening مَثَلُ matters as well: the Quran offers an intelligible description of paradise, naming its blessings in familiar language.

مَثَلُ alone does not establish that water, milk, wine, and honey lack any reality there and are merely moral symbols. Nor does it justify transferring every material property of earthly examples to the hereafter.

The verse clearly promises rivers of purified honey; their complete nature is not explained here.

5. The connection with al-Nahl 16:69

Although عَسَل occurs only in Muhammad 47:15, al-Nahl also describes the bees' product.

Al-Nahl 16:68 speaks of God's inspiration to the bee to make homes in mountains, trees, and structures people raise.

The following verse reads:

ثُمَّ كُلِي مِنْ كُلِّ الثَّمَرَاتِ فَاسْلُكِي سُبُلَ رَبِّكِ ذُلُلًا ۚ يَخْرُجُ مِنْ بُطُونِهَا شَرَابٌ مُخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَانُهُ فِيهِ شِفَاءٌ لِلنَّاسِ ۗ إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لِقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ

thumma kulī min kulli al-thamarāti fa-slukī subula rabbiki dhululan yakhruju min buṭūnihā sharābun mukhtalifun alwānuhu fīhi shifāʾun li-l-nāsi inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-qawmin yatafakkarūn

Al-Naḥl 16:69

Al-Nahl 16:69

“Then eat of all the fruits and follow your Lord's ways made easy for you. From their bellies comes a drink of varying colors, in which there is healing for people. In that is a sign for people who reflect.”

The verse does not use the name عَسَل, but شَرَابٌ مُخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَانُهُ, a drink of varying colors. The translation above relates ذُلُلًا to the ways; another established analysis relates it to the bees as compliant or guided. This difference does not change the lexical distinction between honey and drink.

Given the explicit reference to bees and their product, the familiar referent of this drink is honey.

The two expressions nevertheless need lexical distinction.

Muhammad 47:15 names the substance: عَسَل.

Al-Nahl 16:69 describes the product as شَرَاب and specifies its qualities.

Those descriptions should not all be incorporated into the lexical definition of honey.

مُخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَانُهُ describes varying colors, but color variation is not part of the meaning of عَسَل.

Likewise, فِيهِ شِفَاءٌ لِلنَّاسِ states that there is healing in it; عَسَل does not lexically mean medicine or healing.

Healing and the scope of the wording

شِفَاءٌ is indefinite. Indefiniteness alone does not settle the complete scope of the statement; the verse affirms healing without naming every disease or specifying a method of use.

A linguistic account of the phrase is therefore distinct from determining particular therapeutic applications. The latter cannot be derived from the definition of the root alone.

In particular, the wording does not state that honey replaces every other treatment in every circumstance.

Conversely, treating it as merely an ethical metaphor lacks a basis in this context of bees, their homes, food, and an actual product.

The verse presents the bees' production of something beneficial as a sign inviting reflection.

Muhammad 47:15 and al-Nahl 16:69 both illuminate honey as a familiar blessing, while addressing different subjects: a blessing in paradise and a sign in nature.

6. The movement branch: عَسَلان

Arabic lexical sources record عَسَلَ and عَسَلان for swift and oscillating movement.

For wolves and foxes, the word can describe running or swift motion accompanied by head movement and an undulating gait.

For horses, عَسَلان also describes speed accompanied by bodily movement.

عَسَلَ الرُّمْحُ can describe a spear flexing and quivering when shaken.

The movement branch is therefore not limited to speed; manner of motion, oscillation, and flexibility can also matter.

A spear shaken in the hand may bend and straighten along its shaft, a motion described by عَسَلَ. A wolf's or horse's movement involves bodily locomotion.

These uses do not all share exactly one feature. Not every عَسَلان necessarily involves head movement, and not every oscillation can be called عسلان.

None of these movement meanings is conveyed by Quranic عَسَلٍ مُصَفًّى.

This distinction matters because deriving every sense from one physical image, without supporting evidence, imposes a new meaning rather than clarifying the language.

7. Related roots and distinctions

ع س ل and ش ه د

شَهْد is used for honey, especially honey within its wax comb.

عَسَل is the more general name of the sweet substance produced by bees.

Both may refer to the same substance in some uses, while their naming conventions and contexts remain distinct.

Nor should شَهْد, honey, be merged with شَهِدَ, bearing witness, into one simple definition merely because of written resemblance. Their historical and morphological relationships require separate examination.

ع س ل and أ ر ي

أَرْي is another Arabic name for honey, also found in early poetry.

In the verse attributed to Labid, أَرْيِ دُبُورٍ refers to bees' honey.

Different names may share a referent without sharing a root.

عَسَل and أَرْي belong to separate lexical families even when they name the same substance.

ع س ل and ح ل و

ح ل و concerns sweetness and pleasant taste.

عسل names a particular substance.

Honey is ordinarily sweet, but not everything sweet is honey: dates, other fruits, and plant syrups may also be sweet.

Sweetness is a familiar property of honey, not a sufficient definition of it.

Speech described as مَعْسُول may be sweet or pleasant. This metaphor draws on a property of honey without turning speech into the substance itself.

ع س ل and ص ف و

This distinction is especially relevant to Muhammad 47:15.

عَسَل names the substance; مُصَفًّى states its quality.

Honey may contain accompanying particles or differ in cloudiness and appearance. مُصَفًّى emphasizes purification.

The roots should therefore not be merged by defining honey itself as “a completely purified substance.”

That quality comes from a separate descriptor in the verse.

ع س ل and ش ر ب

شَرَاب is a general word for drink, applicable to various drinkable substances.

Al-Nahl 16:69 uses it for the bees' product.

عَسَل names a particular substance that can be eaten or drunk and used in preparing other foods and drinks.

The words share a referent in the interpretation of al-Nahl discussed here, but their lexical meanings differ.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

The sole Quranic form is عَسَلٍ in Muhammad 47:15.

Its natural Persian equivalent is the familiar word for honey, also the appropriate English rendering.

In the phrase:

وَأَنْهَارٌ مِنْ عَسَلٍ مُصَفًّى

wa-anhārun min ʿasalin muṣaffā

Muḥammad 47:15

The proposed translation is:

“And rivers of purified honey.”

This distinguishes the substance from its description.

عَسَلٍ refers to honey; مُصَفًّى expresses its purification.

“Pure honey” is also familiar, but may suggest authenticity or freedom from adulteration as well as purification. The proposed Persian wording emphasizes purification; “purified honey” preserves that emphasis in English.

Proposal for the Forqan word box:

Word: عَسَلٍ

Root: ع س ل

Word type: Indefinite singular mass noun, genitive after مِنْ.

Brief root meaning: Bee honey; in another branch, swift and oscillating movement.

Meaning in this verse: Honey, the sweet substance of the described rivers, qualified as purified by مُصَفًّى.

Healing, sweetness in general, flowing movement, and spiritual purity should not replace the substance's name in the brief explanation. Each belongs to a property, usage, or separate branch.

Conclusion

ع س ل has two important branches: honey and words concerning its production, collection, and use; and swift, oscillating movement of animals and objects.

Early witnesses show honey as a known food and related words for its collection. The movement branch is also recorded for wolves, horses, and flexible spears.

The existence of both branches does not establish a shared original image. Neither honey's movement nor sweetness should be made the origin of every sense without evidence.

Only عَسَلٍ occurs in the Quran, in Muhammad 47:15, describing paradise's rivers and qualified by مُصَفًّى.

Without naming honey, al-Nahl 16:69 describes the bees' product, its varying colors, and healing. It helps identify the referent without reducing honey's lexical meaning to healing or treatment.

The Quranic meaning of ع س ل is honey, a familiar substance described as purified in Muhammad 47:15. Its properties, including sweetness and benefit, and the separate movement branch must not replace the noun's direct meaning.
