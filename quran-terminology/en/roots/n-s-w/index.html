1. Short Introduction to the Root

The word family ن س و appears in the Qur'an mainly through two forms, نِسَاء and نِسْوَة, and points to women, whether as a general class or as a particular group.

نِسَاء is the Qur'an's general word for women. It can refer to all women, the women of a society, the women of a household, wives, or a group of women in relation to men. Its exact meaning is determined by annexation, definiteness or indefiniteness, accompanying description, and context.

نِسْوَة also means a group of women, but in Qur'anic usage it more often introduces a specific and identifiable group, such as the women in the city who spoke about the story of the wife of the Aziz and Yusuf.

Women have a foundational role in the continuation of generations, family, upbringing, economy, social relations, and the structure of society. But these human and social realities should not be placed inside the lexical definition of نساء. At its base, the word points to women, not to a reproductive hardware system, a connective tissue of civilization, or one single biological function.

The Qur'an also does not introduce women only from the angle of birth-giving and motherhood. In Qur'anic verses, women may be believers, migrants, pledge-givers, inheritors, owners, wives, mothers, daughters, witnesses, social actors, subjects of oppression, or persons with independent moral responsibility.

The shortest statement for this family is therefore:

Nisa and niswah refer to women, generally or as a specified group.

2. Important Forms and Words of This Family

نِسَاء

A collective noun for women. Its singular is not formed from the same word, and in common Arabic usage امرأة is its semantic singular.

The Qur'an uses this form for women in general, women of a people, wives, women of a family, and various groups of women.

The Qur'anic morphological database places نساء under the entry ن س و and records 59 Qur'anic uses for it. This classification is useful for arranging morphological data, but it does not by itself settle every historical debate about the derivation of the word.

نِسْوَة

A collective noun meaning a group of women.

This form appears in the Qur'an about a specific group of women in the city in the story of Yusuf:

وَقَالَ نِسْوَةٌ فِي الْمَدِينَةِ

A group of women in the city said.

Yusuf 12:30

Niswah does not inherently mean a female information network. In that story, those women transmit information among themselves, but that feature comes from the narrative context, not from the dictionary meaning of the word.

نِسَائِكُمْ

Your women or your wives, depending on context.

The attached pronoun specifies the group to which the women are related. In some verses, the meaning is the women of a people, while in others it is the wives of the addressees.

نِسَائِهِنَّ

Women connected with them, or women of their social circle.

The kind of relation must be understood from the verse context and should not always be restricted to all women of society or only female relatives.

4. Semantic Core

The core of this word family is women in the plural.

Meanings such as birth-giving, weaving society, receptivity, nurturing civilization, or continuation of lineage are not independently encoded in the word itself. These matters may be part of the experience and role of some women, but all women cannot be defined only through reproduction, motherhood, or maintenance of social structure.

Women in the Qur'an are not a single uniform group with one fixed function. The verses speak of women in many different situations:

  • believing and Muslim women;
  • hypocritical or associating women;
  • wives of prophets;
  • mothers and daughters;
  • migrant women;
  • pledge-giving women;
  • women with property and inheritance;
  • women needing protection;
  • women with independent decision and choice;
  • and women placed within structures of oppression.

So the lexical meaning of نساء is women, while the identity, action, status, and responsibility of each group is known from the context of the verses.

5. The Human and Social Image of the Word

Unlike many verbal roots, نساء cannot be confidently traced to one material image such as weaving, delay, forgetfulness, or birth-giving as the definite source of meaning.

The direct image of the word is the collective presence of women in society. Women may appear in family, city, migration, covenant, inheritance, marriage, separation, good action, faith, opposition, or social conversation.

Some Qur'anic uses show women's role in birth and the continuation of generations, but many uses have no direct relation to pregnancy or reproduction. For example:

وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتُ

or:

الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَالْمُسْلِمَاتِ

In such structures, women and men are both introduced as possessors of faith, will, and responsibility, not as two different instruments in a population-production system.

A lexical article should therefore avoid reducing women to reproductive bodies or instruments of lineage continuity.

6. Use in Early Arabic

In Arabic, نساء and نسوة both refer to a plural of women, and their common semantic singular is امرأة.

This difference between singular and plural is not unique to women in Arabic. Languages sometimes use different word bases for the singular and plural of one concept. Therefore, the absence of a singular formed from the same word does not prove that the word necessarily represents an independent civilizational body.

Lexical sources offer several explanations of the relation between nisa and niswah. Some treated niswah as a plural and nisa as a plural for a larger group; others considered both to be plurals of imra'ah from a different word base. Lexical reports also state that nisa and niswah are used for a group of women and that the nisbah to nisa is formed as niswi.

The existence of نسوة can support placing this family under ن س و, but by itself it does not prove theories such as connection with weaving, gradual birth-giving, or delay.

7. History of Understanding, Misuse, or Semantic Restriction

One important distortion in understanding نساء is defining women only through body, reproduction, and service to the continuation of generations.

This view may seem to speak about the importance of women, but in practice it reduces their full personhood to a biological function. The Qur'an does not present woman only as birth-giver, mother, wife, or keeper of the family. A woman before The God is an independent person with faith, action, covenant, responsibility, and her own accounting.

Another restriction is turning نساء into a fully abstract concept such as delayed elements, newly emerging matters, or vulnerable class in every verse. Clear contexts of marriage, divorce, inheritance, mothers, daughters, wives, and family relations show that the base meaning of nisa is women.

On the other side, rulings or reports about one group of women should not be turned into the fixed nature of all women. If a group of women in the city speak about Yusuf, one cannot conclude that نسوة in the language means a network of rumor or information surveillance.

The correct method is to preserve the base meaning of the word and understand the features of each group from the sentence and story of the verse itself.

8. Methodological Note

In studying نساء, four levels must be distinguished:

First, lexical meaning: women.

Second, morphological structure: a collective noun whose common singular comes from another word, امرأة.

Third, contextual role: wives, women of a people, women of a household, migrant women, or another group specified by the verse.

Fourth, social analysis: the role of women in family, population, economy, upbringing, power, or resistance.

Social analysis can matter, but it should not replace lexical meaning.

Also, placing نساء under ن س و in morphological classification does not prove a foundational meaning such as connective tissue. Similarity between ن س و and ن س ج is not enough to prove a shared root; the letter jim in nasaj is part of another root.

The same rule applies to ن س أ and ن س ي. Sound resemblance alone does not allow us to translate women as those delayed or those forgotten.

9. Qur'anic Uses

A. Women as part of human beings with independent responsibility

إِنَّ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَالْمُسْلِمَاتِ وَالْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ وَالْقَانِتِينَ وَالْقَانِتَاتِ وَالصَّادِقِينَ وَالصَّادِقَاتِ وَالصَّابِرِينَ وَالصَّابِرَاتِ

Indeed, Muslim men and Muslim women, believing men and believing women, obedient men and obedient women, truthful men and truthful women, patient men and patient women...

Al-Ahzab 33:35

Although the word nisa does not appear in this verse, the structure is important for understanding the Qur'anic place of women. Women are introduced as independent possessors of faith, truthfulness, patience, remembrance, and action.

Women should therefore not be defined only through their role in producing generations.

B. Nisa in marriage relations

فَانْكِحُوا مَا طَابَ لَكُمْ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ

Then marry those women who are pleasing to you.

An-Nisa 4:3

مِنَ النِّسَاءِ here points to women in the position of persons with whom marriage is being discussed.

It does not mean the reproductive part of civilization. The subject of the verse is marriage, responsibility of justice, and its limits.

C. Women, ownership, and inheritance

لِلرِّجَالِ نَصِيبٌ مِمَّا تَرَكَ الْوَالِدَانِ وَالْأَقْرَبُونَ وَلِلنِّسَاءِ نَصِيبٌ

For men there is a share of what parents and close relatives leave, and for women there is a share.

An-Nisa 4:7

In this verse, women are holders of financial right. They are not merely dependents of men or instruments for preserving lineage.

The parallel structure للرجال نصيب and للنساء نصيب establishes that both groups have a right of inheritance.

D. Women and independent earning

لِلرِّجَالِ نَصِيبٌ مِمَّا اكْتَسَبُوا وَلِلنِّسَاءِ نَصِيبٌ مِمَّا اكْتَسَبْنَ

For men there is a share of what they have earned, and for women there is a share of what they have earned.

An-Nisa 4:32

The verb اكتسبن is attributed to the women themselves. They are presented as actors of action and owners of their own share.

This use does not agree with a definition that treats women only as receptive and reproductive bodies.

E. Women and social covenant

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ إِذَا جَاءَكَ الْمُؤْمِنَاتُ يُبَايِعْنَكَ

O Prophet, when believing women come to you pledging allegiance to you...

Al-Mumtahanah 60:12

Although the word here is المؤمنات, it matters for understanding the Qur'anic concept of women. Women independently enter a social and moral covenant.

No man pledges in their place, and their responsibility is not derived from the identity of the men of their family.

F. Niswah in the city

وَقَالَ نِسْوَةٌ فِي الْمَدِينَةِ امْرَأَتُ الْعَزِيزِ تُرَاوِدُ فَتَاهَا عَنْ نَفْسِهِ

A group of women in the city said: the wife of the Aziz is seeking to seduce her young man.

Yusuf 12:30

نسوة introduces a group of women among whom the news had circulated.

The verse shows that these women were present in the social space of the city and spoke about an event. But from this instance one cannot build a dictionary meaning of capillary information-transfer network for niswah.

The precise translation is:

A group of women in the city.

G. The women of the Children of Israel in the Pharaonic system

يُذَبِّحُونَ أَبْنَاءَكُمْ وَيَسْتَحْيُونَ نِسَاءَكُمْ

They were slaughtering your sons and keeping your women alive.

Al-Baqarah 2:49

نساءكم here means the women of the Children of Israel. Its contrast with أبناءكم shows that Pharaoh's policy distinguished between the sons and the women.

يستحيون in this structure means keeping alive. The verse does not explain exactly for what purpose the women were kept alive.

Exploitation, forced service, humiliation, or population control may have been consequences of such a policy, but none of these possibilities should be added to the translation without evidence.

The translation "they kept your connective tissue alive for identity assimilation" removes the explicit meaning of women and replaces it with an unproven theory.

H. Women as one of the desired things of worldly life

زُيِّنَ لِلنَّاسِ حُبُّ الشَّهَوَاتِ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ وَالْبَنِينَ وَالْقَنَاطِيرِ الْمُقَنْطَرَةِ

The love of desired things has been adorned for people: women, children, and heaped-up stores of wealth.

Ali 'Imran 3:14

The verse names a set of attractions of worldly life. من النساء in this context points to sexual and emotional attraction toward women.

One should not conclude from this verse that women are only instruments of reproductive multiplication or part of the hardware of the earth. The verse speaks about human attachment to worldly desires, then compares the provision of the world with what is with The God.

Also, للناس is all people, but the list is expressed from the angle of the dominant addressee and social structure of that period. The verse is not giving an essential definition of woman.

I. Women and honorable conduct

وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

Live with them in an honorable and recognized manner.

An-Nisa 4:19

Women in this context are the other party in an ethical relationship, and men are commanded to live with them according to ma'ruf.

The verse does not allow women to be treated as inherited property. This example shows that studying nisa must also consider women's rights, dignity, and will.

J. Migrant women

إِذَا جَاءَكُمُ الْمُؤْمِنَاتُ مُهَاجِرَاتٍ فَامْتَحِنُوهُنَّ

When believing women come to you as migrants, examine them.

Al-Mumtahanah 60:10

The women in this verse have chosen faith and migration and have left their previous environment.

This verse again shows that woman in the Qur'an is not simply a body for birth-giving or a passive element; she can make a costly theological, political, and social decision.

10. Examination of Important Words in the Qur'an

نِسَاء

Nisa is a collective noun for women.

This word can acquire a more specific meaning in different structures:

  • nisaakum: your women;
  • nisa an-nabi: the women of the Prophet;
  • nisa al-mu'minin: the women of the believers;
  • nisa al-'alamin: women of the worlds or women of the people of the time, according to context;
  • min an-nisa: from among women.

The meaning wives is sometimes gained from context, but نساء in itself does not always mean wives.

نِسْوَة

Niswah is also a plural of women, but in Qur'anic usage it introduces a limited and specified group.

Its difference from nisa should not be turned into an absolute rule such as nisa means the civilizational body and niswah means a small information network. The clearer difference is:

nisa: a more general plural of women;

niswah: a group of women, often more limited and specified.

امرأة

Imra'ah is the common singular word for woman. The Qur'an uses it for a specified woman or a woman connected to a man:

  • the wife of the Aziz;
  • the wife of Pharaoh;
  • the wife of Nuh;
  • the wife of Lut.

The basic difference is:

imra'ah: one woman;

nisa or niswah: women.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

Nisa as a collective noun

Nisa is plural in meaning, and its common singular is امرأة.

This may be understood as a plural from a different singular word. The difference between the singular and plural base does not by itself produce a special philosophical or civilizational meaning.

النِّساء

The definite article may introduce the women under discussion, a known group of women, or women as a class. The type of definiteness is determined by context.

مِنَ النِّسَاءِ

The particle من can indicate partitive meaning:

فَانْكِحُوا مَا طَابَ لَكُمْ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ

that is, from among women.

نِسَاءَكُمْ

Annexation to a pronoun does not always mean ownership in the sense of thinghood. "Your women" may express familial, communal, or social relation.

Just as قومكم is your people, نساءكم are the women connected to the society or household of the addressees.

نِسْوَةٌ فِي الْمَدِينَةِ

The indefiniteness of نسوة introduces an unnamed group of women. The phrase في المدينة specifies the environment in which they are present or speaking.

12. N S W Compared with M R A / Imra'ah

امرأة introduces one woman.

نساء and نسوة introduce a group of women.

This is not the difference between an individual quality and a civilizational body. The basic difference is singular and plural.

In the Qur'an:

وَقَالَ نِسْوَةٌ فِي الْمَدِينَةِ امْرَأَتُ الْعَزِيزِ...

niswah is the group of women, and imra'at al-Aziz is one specified woman.

This co-occurrence clearly shows the difference between the two words.

13. N S W Compared with A N Th / Untha

أُنثى opposite ذَكَر points to female sex and can be used for human beings and other living beings.

نساء specifically points to human women in plural form.

So:

untha: a female from the angle of sex;

imra'ah: a human woman in singular form;

nisa/niswah: human women in plural form.

Every human woman is untha, but not every untha in language is necessarily a human woman.

14. N S W Compared with Dh K R and R J L

The natural counterpart of أنثى in sexual classification is ذكر.

Opposite امرأة or نساء, the words رجل and رجال are often used for a human man or men.

One should not infer from this contrast that men are the hard hardware arms and women are the soft reproductive tissue of civilization. Such a fixed division restricts the diversity of human gifts, duties, and roles to a biological and predetermined template.

The Qur'an treats men and women alike as holders of faith, action, property, inheritance, responsibility, and accountability. Real physical differences exist, but one cannot extract from them a single unchangeable social role for every human being.

15. Specialized Discussion and Philological Note: The Place of Nisa and Niswah

Placing نساء and نسوة under the entry ن س و is useful for arranging Qur'anic data. The Qur'anic morphological database also classifies nisa under ن س و.

But three matters must be distinguished:

  • morphological classification of the word;
  • historical derivation;
  • and Qur'anic meaning.

From the existence of نسوة, one can see that waw has an important place in the structure of this family. But this evidence alone does not prove that the foundational meaning of the root is birth-giving, connective tissue, or civilizational stability.

Linking ن س و with ن س ج is also not valid as root method. The root nasaj has the letters n, s, and j, and the meaning of weaving comes from that structure. Sharing the first two letters is not enough to prove unity of root or to transfer the meaning of nasaj to nisa.

Connecting nisa with ن س أ, meaning delay, must also be examined cautiously. Even if an old lexicographer offered such an explanation, one cannot conclude that women in Arabic are named as beings delayed in creation or rank. Such an explanation may reflect the cultural view of the explainer rather than preserve the historical meaning of the word.

The same issue exists with linking the word to ن س ي and forgetfulness. No secure linguistic basis allows us to translate nisa as forgetful ones or forgotten ones. The form nisa in Qur'anic verses clearly points to women, and none of the contexts require forgetfulness as its meaning.

The precise position of this article is therefore:

Nisa and niswah in Qur'anic usage point to women. Classifying them under ن س و is suitable for morphological work, but the foundational and pre-Qur'anic meaning of this family is not historically clear enough to build a theory of birth-giving, weaving society, delay, or forgetfulness upon it.

This caution does not diminish the value and role of women. On the contrary, it prevents their status from being defined through speculative etymology. The dignity of women in the Qur'an comes from clear verses about their faith, action, right, inheritance, covenant, choice, justice, and responsibility, not from an unproven story about the origin of their name.

In the Pharaonic verses the same boundary must be observed. The Qur'an says:

وَيَسْتَحْيُونَ نِسَاءَكُمْ

that is, they were keeping your women alive.

From the contrast with slaughtering the sons, one may understand that the Pharaonic system had a different policy for the two groups. But the exact reason for keeping the women alive is not stated. Assimilation of lineage, forced labor, exploitation, or population control are historical possibilities and should not be placed inside the translation or the meaning of nisa.

Also, the survival of women did not mean the healthy preservation of the civilizational tissue of the Children of Israel. They themselves were victims of the same oppressive system. Being kept alive in a structure of enslavement is not necessarily rescue, honor, or protection.

A study of nisa must therefore see woman as a complete human being: having a body, but more than the body; having the possibility of motherhood, but not limited to motherhood; a member of society, but not merely an instrument for society's survival; and a holder of independent faith, will, right, and responsibility.

16. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

نِسَاء

women.

Where context means wives:

wives or women, according to the structure of the verse.

"The female body of society" and "the reproductive part of the civilizational lineage" are not translations of the word.

النِّسَاء

the women.

the group of women, in explanatory context.

نِسْوَة

a group of women.

a number of women.

"the capillary surveillance network of society" is added and unsuitable.

نِسَاءَكُمْ

your women.

your wives, only in verses where the marriage context requires that meaning.

يَسْتَحْيُونَ نِسَاءَكُمْ

they were keeping your women alive.

they were leaving your women alive.

The possible purpose of this policy should be examined separately and not inserted into the translation.

مِنَ النِّسَاءِ وَالْبَنِينَ

from women and children.

This phrase does not need translations such as a chain of hardware attractions for reproductive multiplication.

17. Conclusion

نساء and نسوة in the Qur'an point to women. Nisa is the more general plural form, while niswah in Qur'anic usage introduces a more limited and specified group of women. The common semantic singular of both is امرأة.

Placing this family under ن س و is acceptable for morphological classification, but speculative meanings such as weaving society, gradual birth-giving, delay, or forgetfulness should not be extracted from it.

Women in the Qur'an are not introduced only from the angle of reproduction. They are holders of faith, action, property, inheritance, pledge, migration, choice, and independent responsibility. Motherhood and continuation of generations are one dimension in the lives of some women, not the complete definition of the word nisa and not the whole identity of woman.

In the story of Pharaoh, نساءكم points to the women of the Children of Israel. The Qur'an says they were kept alive, but does not state the exact reason for this policy. Theories about forced labor, assimilation of lineage, or population control should not be inserted into the translation without evidence.

In the story of Yusuf, نسوة are a group of women in the city. Conversation and news transmission are the behavior of that group in that story, and do not turn the dictionary meaning of niswah into a female information network.

The shortest statement for this family is:

Nisa and niswah are names for women in plural form; nisa has a more general usage, while niswah more often introduces a limited and specified group of women.
