Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root AYN M Y (ʿ M Y)

Blindness, not seeing, the covered path of sight, ambiguity, and the loss of observation or discernment

Important Forms from This Root

عَمِيَ / يَعْمَى

ʿamiya / yaʿmā; became blind, did not see, became unable to see, or became unclear.

عَمًى

ʿaman; blindness, not seeing, loss of discernment.

أَعْمَى / عُمْي

aʿmā / ʿumy; blind, sight-closed; the blind.

عَمِيّ / عَمِيَتْ

ʿamiyy / ʿamiyat; blind, unseen, sight-closed; became hidden or unclear.

تَعْمَى / أَعْمَىٰهُ

taʿmā / aʿmāhu; becomes blind; made him blind or removed his discernment.

عَمَاء

ʿamāʾ; ambiguity, enveloping concealment, thick cloud or mist that erases boundaries.

Central Meaning

The root AYN M Y (ʿ M Y) means blindness, not seeing, the covered path of sight, ambiguity, and the loss of the ability to observe or discern. This root does not only refer to physical blindness. At the material level, ʿamā means that the eye does not see or the path of seeing is blocked. At the epistemic level, it means that the human being does not see signs, truth, the path, or the difference between right and wrong.

So ʿamā means that something is there to be seen, but the instrument of seeing or discernment has stopped working; or the human being’s relation to that truth has become so dim and unclear that he no longer sees it. In this sense, ʿamā is not simply the absence of an image; it is the failure of discerning sight.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of AYN M Y (ʿ M Y) is the closure of the path of seeing and discernment. The issue is not always the absence of light. Sometimes light exists, the sign exists, the path exists, but the eye or inner discernment does not see. In this way, ʿamā differs from ẓulumāt: darknesses refer to a dark environment; ʿamā refers to inability to see from the side of the viewer.

Simply put, ẓulumāt means the environment is dark; ʿamā means the eye or discernment does not see. But ʿamā is not only a defect of the seeing instrument. Sometimes the human being’s relation to truth becomes dim and unclear. Truth remains in its place, but it becomes unseen for the addressee, not because truth changed, but because his sight, direction, or receptivity became closed.

In the Qur’an, this distinction is important. The signs may be clear, yet because of inner ʿamā a person does not see them.

Material Image

The first material image is a blind eye or closed sight. Something stands before a person, but he does not see it. The path is before him, but he does not recognize it. A sign is in the road, but his eye does not receive it.

Another material image is a covered, boundary-lost space, like dense cloud or mist that covers the horizon and makes mountain, path, sign, and direction indistinguishable. In this condition, things have not disappeared, but their boundaries are erased from human sight.

In Arabic usage, ʿamā may also approach murkiness and the loss of clarity, like water or waves that become muddy and disturbed and lose transparency. This image shows that ʿamā is not only not-seeing; sometimes it is the loss of contrast and distinction.

From this image, the epistemic meaning of ʿamā develops. A human being may see with the physical eye but not with the eye of discernment. He may see the signs of The God, the consequences of action, the truth of history, or the danger of the road, yet fail to understand. Thus ʿamā is the loss of visual and meaningful discernment.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, ʿamā is used for blindness and not seeing. أَعْمَى is one whose eye does not see. But the root also appears in broader meanings from early usage, such as a matter becoming unclear, the path becoming hidden, or inability to recognize direction.

When something is not clear to a person, it may enter the field of ʿamā: the matter has become covered and unseen for him. The use of عَمَاء for cloud or enveloping cover shows that the root is also tied to environmental ambiguity and the loss of boundaries. In this image, the problem is not only the eye; the field of sight is covered in a way that removes discernment.

In Arabic usage, ʿamā may apply to the eye, the road, the heart, and understanding. This extension is natural because seeing, in Arabic as in many languages, is close to understanding and discernment.

Qur’anic Usage

ʿUmy with Ṣumm and Bukm

One famous Qur’anic use appears in Surat al-Baqarah: صُمٌّ بُكْمٌ عُمْيٌ فَهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ; deaf, mute, blind, so they do not return 2:18.

Here, ʿumy appears beside ṣumm and bukm. Together, the three form the image of the channels of relation to truth being closed: ṣumm means the path of hearing is closed; bukm means the path of speech and response is closed; ʿumy means the path of seeing and discernment is closed. Thus ʿamā here is not merely physical blindness; it is the closure of discerning sight toward truth.

Eyes Do Not Go Blind; Hearts Go Blind

One of the most important verses for this root says: فَإِنَّهَا لَا تَعْمَى الْأَبْصَارُ وَلَٰكِن تَعْمَى الْقُلُوبُ الَّتِي فِي الصُّدُورِ; the eyes do not go blind, but the hearts within the breasts go blind 22:46.

This verse draws a crucial boundary. In many cases, the main problem is not the physical eye; it is the heart, meaning the inner center of understanding, orientation, and discernment. A person may have eyes, see the world, see history, and see signs, yet his heart may have lost discernment. Guidance-level ʿamā is therefore blindness of the center of understanding, not a physical defect.

ʿAmā Toward the Signs

The Qur’an speaks of people becoming blind toward The God’s signs or failing to see them. The sign is clear, but for one who has ʿamā, it does not perform its revealing effect. A sign comes to be seen, understood, and followed as a path. But if the human being lacks discerning sight, the sign does not become a path for him. This is tied to light and darknesses: light exists, but the inner eye does not see.

Fa-ʿamiyat ʿalaykum: Truth Becoming Unseen for the Addressee

In the speech of Noah to his people, the Qur’an says: أَرَأَيْتُمْ إِن كُنتُ عَلَىٰ بَيِّنَةٍ مِّن رَّبِّي وَآتَانِي رَحْمَةً مِّنْ عِندِهِ فَعَمِيَتْ عَلَيْكُمْ أَنُلْزِمُكُمُوهَا وَأَنتُمْ لَهَا كَارِهُونَ; have you considered: if I am upon a clear proof from my Lord, and He has given me mercy from Himself, then it has become unseen to you, shall we compel you to it while you dislike it? 11:28.

In this verse, عَمِيَتْ is related to truth or mercy, but the precise meaning is not that truth itself became blind. Rather, that truth became unseen, covered, and unclear for the addressees. Truth remains clear in its own place, but for one who dislikes it or pushes it away, the relation to truth falls into ʿamā.

ʿAmā and Losing the Road

One who does not see also loses the road. For this reason, ʿamā in the Qur’an is tied to misguidance and the inability to move correctly. Seeing is not only observing objects; it is also recognizing the path. When ʿamā occurs, the human being does not distinguish path, danger, destination, and sign. Guidance-level ʿamā is the breakdown of wayfinding in truth.

ʿAmā in This World and the Hereafter

The Qur’an speaks of ʿamā in this world and the Hereafter: وَمَن كَانَ فِي هَٰذِهِ أَعْمَىٰ فَهُوَ فِي الْآخِرَةِ أَعْمَىٰ وَأَضَلُّ سَبِيلًا; whoever is blind in this world will be blind in the Hereafter and more astray in path 17:72.

This verse is not about physical blindness. It concerns blindness toward guidance and truth. One who does not see the path of truth in this world appears in the Hereafter in a more intense condition of not seeing and misguidance. This use shows that ʿamā can be an inner state with final consequences.

ʿAmā Opposite Knowledge

Surat al-Raʿd says: أَفَمَن يَعْلَمُ أَنَّمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ مِن رَّبِّكَ الْحَقُّ كَمَنْ هُوَ أَعْمَىٰ; is one who knows that what has been sent down to you from your Lord is the truth like one who is blind? 13:19.

Here, aʿmā is not placed opposite a sighted person, but opposite one who knows. This is important. ʿAmā in this context is not merely visual inability; it is epistemic inability. One who does not see the truth of revelation lacks more than an image; he lacks knowledge and alignment of understanding with reality.

Human Note

As with ṣamam and bukm, physical ʿamā and moral ʿamā must be distinguished. People who are physically blind are not being degraded. The Qur’an elsewhere separates physical conditions and responsibilities. But when the Qur’an uses ʿamā in contexts of denial, misguidance, or not returning, it refers to the closure of discerning sight and inner insight.

Translation and commentary should make clear that guidance-level ʿamā is not contempt for physical blindness.

Difference from Nearby Roots

B Ṣ R is seeing, sight, insight, and discernment. ʿAmā is the opposite of seeing and insight. R ʾ Y is seeing, opinion, observation, or view; ʿamā is inability to see or discern, not simply lacking an opinion. N Ẓ R is looking, considering, and gazing; a person may look yet still be in ʿamā, meaning there is looking but no discernment.

Ẓ L M is darkness and concealment. Darknesses darken the environment; ʿamā disables the seeing instrument or the relation of seeing. GH SH W is covering and placing a veil. A covering may cause ʿamā, but ʿamā itself is not seeing. KH T M is sealing and closure; sealing may be upon the heart, hearing, or sight, while ʿamā is the state of not seeing.

Ṣ M M is the closed path of hearing. B K M is the closed path of expression. ʿAmā is the closed path of seeing and discernment. AYN SH W (ʿ SH W) is weak sight, distorted seeing, or moving in darkness; ʿamā is more complete and more severe. L B S is mixing and making things confused; labs may cause confusion, while ʿamā is the loss of sight and discernment.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, ʿamā should not always be taken only in the medical sense of “the blind,” especially when it appears in contexts of denying truth, signs, or guidance. In physical contexts, “blind” may fit, while avoiding wording that sounds degrading in contemporary English.

In guidance contexts, “blind toward truth,” “sight-closed,” “unable to discern,” or “without insight toward the signs” may be more precise. In فَعَمِيَتْ عَلَيْكُمْ, the translation should show that truth became unseen and unclear for the addressee, not that truth changed in itself. In Surat al-Raʿd 13:19, ʿamā should be understood as epistemic inability and lack of discerning sight toward truth.

In the phrase ṣumm bukm ʿumy, the translation should show that three main paths of relation to truth have been closed: hearing, speaking, and seeing. In Surat al-Ḥajj 22:46, it should be clear that the main blindness is in the heart, not the physical eye.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

عَمًى (ʿaman): blindness, not seeing, inability of discernment, unclear sight.

أَعْمَى (aʿmā): blind, sight-closed, unable to discern truth.

عُمْي (ʿumy): the blind; in guidance context, those blind toward truth.

تَعْمَى (taʿmā): becomes blind, loses sight, loses discernment.

عَمِيَتْ عَلَيْكُمْ (ʿamiyat ʿalaykum): became unseen to you, became unclear to you, became covered from your discerning sight.

أَعْمَىٰهُ (aʿmāhu): made him blind, removed his discerning sight.

عَمَاء (ʿamāʾ): enveloping concealment, ambiguity, blurring of boundaries.

صُمٌّ بُكْمٌ عُمْيٌ: deaf, mute, blind; or closed in hearing, closed in expression, closed in sight.

لَا تَعْمَى الْأَبْصَارُ: the eyes do not go blind.

وَلَٰكِن تَعْمَى الْقُلُوبُ: but the hearts go blind.

Linguistic Summary

The root AYN M Y (ʿ M Y) denotes blindness, not seeing, the closure of the path of sight, ambiguity, and the loss of discernment. At the material level, ʿamā means that the eye does not see, or the field of sight becomes so covered and unclear that boundaries are no longer distinguished. At the Qur’anic level, ʿamā may point to the heart becoming blind and insight being lost. The sign, path, light, and indication may be present, yet the human being does not see them.

Its difference from ẓulumāt is that darknesses make the environment dark, while ʿamā disables the instrument, relation, or power of seeing. Its difference from ṣamam and bukm is that ṣamam closes the path of hearing, bukm closes the path of expression, and ʿamā closes the path of seeing and discernment. The main image is this: truth stands before the human being, but seeing does not happen; the path exists, but the eye of discernment is closed or the field of discernment has become unclear.
