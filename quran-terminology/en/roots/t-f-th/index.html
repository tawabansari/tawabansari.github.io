Core Meaning

The root ت ف ث comes through the word tafath. This material occurs in the Qur'an only in one form:

ثُمَّ لْيَقْضُوا تَفَثَهُمْ وَلْيُوفُوا نُذُورَهُمْ وَلْيَطَّوَّفُوا بِالْبَيْتِ الْعَتِيقِ

Then they should bring their bodily dishevelment to an end, fulfill their vows, and make tawaf around the Ancient House.

Al-Hajj 22:29

This is a rare and limited Arabic root. Its meaning is known mostly through the Hajj context and through lexical usages connected with bodily dishevelment, dirt, and unkemptness.

T F TH originally points to bodily dishevelment, dirt, and an unkempt bodily state, especially a condition that remains on the body during a period of abstention, restriction, or ihram and is then brought to an end.

In the Hajj context, tafath may include uncut hair, untrimmed nails, dust and traces of travel, an unkempt appearance, and the bodily traces of ihram. Therefore, qada al-tafath means ending this state and bringing the body out of dishevelment and restriction.

The central meaning may be summarized as the total bodily traces and dishevelment caused by a period of abstention, which must be removed and concluded when that period ends.

Important Forms

تَفَث is a noun or verbal noun: dishevelment, dirt, bodily unkemptness, and the bodily traces left from a period of abstention. In the Qur'an it appears in construct with a plural pronoun.

تَفَثَهُمْ is the Qur'anic form of the root. It may be explained as their dishevelment, their bodily dishevelment, their bodily traces of ihram, or their body's unkempt condition.

تَفِثَ is used lexically for becoming disheveled, dusty, or dirty. This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

تَفِث is a lexical adjective for a person who is disheveled, dirty, or unkempt. This form also does not occur in the Qur'an.

Tafath is not one body part or one isolated act such as shaving. It refers to a broader state that is ended through several bodily acts.

Translations such as “their rites” or “their Hajj duties” are too broad and remove the bodily image of the word. On the other side, translating it only as “dirt” is also incomplete, because hair, nails, dishevelment, and the traces of ihram become faint.

Linguistic Note

The center of T F TH is a temporary bodily state. A pilgrim in ihram avoids some ordinary acts of bodily grooming: hair grows, nails are not trimmed, dust and traces of travel remain on the body, and one's appearance leaves ordinary grooming behind.

Tafath is not merely “dirt,” and it is not all the rites of Hajj. If it is taken only as dirt, hair, nails, and dishevelment are removed from the meaning. If it is taken as all the rites, the bodily aspect of the word and its distinction from vow and tawaf disappear.

More precisely, tafath is the unkempt bodily state created or preserved during ihram and then brought to an end at a stage of the rites.

Material Image

The material image of this root is a pilgrim after days of travel, heat, dust, and abstention. The body carries the traces of that period: hair has not been cut, nails have not been trimmed, ordinary grooming has been delayed, and fatigue, travel, and restriction have settled upon the outward appearance.

At the end of this stage, the body is attended to again: hair is shaved or shortened, nails are trimmed, the body is washed and ordered, dishevelment is removed, and the pilgrim comes out of part of the restrictions of ihram.

Tafath is not the hair, nails, or dust by themselves. It is the whole bodily condition that has come from delaying ordinary bodily grooming.

This word shows that the body participates in Hajj. The body carries the traces of travel, restriction, abstention, and worship; then, within the order of the rites, it exits that state.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, tafath is connected with dishevelment, dirt, unkempt hair and nails, and removing the traces of that condition. Lexical sources mention trimming nails, cutting or shaving hair, removing dirt, and ending the bodily state of ihram as examples of qada al-tafath.

One should be cautious about independent pre-Qur'anic evidence for this word. Its certain and important use in the Qur'an appears in the Hajj context; therefore, one should not build a long historical or pre-Islamic picture for the word on the basis of guesswork.

The stronger points are these: tafath is a bodily state; it is connected with dishevelment and unkemptness; in Hajj it is close to ending ihram and returning bodily order; and it is not a general name for all rites.

Tafath in the Hajj Sequence

The previous verse says:

لِيَشْهَدُوا مَنَافِعَ لَهُمْ وَيَذْكُرُوا اسْمَ اللَّهِ فِي أَيَّامٍ مَعْلُومَاتٍ عَلَىٰ مَا رَزَقَهُمْ مِنْ بَهِيمَةِ الْأَنْعَامِ

So that they may witness benefits for themselves and remember the name of The God, during known days, over what He has provided for them from the grazing animals.

Al-Hajj 22:28

Then it says:

ثُمَّ لْيَقْضُوا تَفَثَهُمْ

Then they should bring their bodily dishevelment to an end.

Al-Hajj 22:29

Thumma shows a later stage after part of the Hajj rites. The pilgrim has kept a restricted and unkempt bodily state for a time; now the time arrives to end it.

Why Qada Comes With Tafath

The Qur'an does not say to wash, clean, or shave tafath. It says:

لْيَقْضُوا تَفَثَهُمْ

They should qada their tafath; that is, bring it to completion and end it.

The verb qada here does not mean judging. It is closer to completing, fulfilling, and bringing something to an end.

This choice shows that tafath is not a simple piece of dirt or a small isolated thing. It is a state that had its time and now must be ended. The pilgrim carried this state on the body during ihram and now exits it.

In matters such as debt, qada means discharging and ending a remaining matter. With tafath as well, the concern is ending a remaining bodily state. But tafath does not mean debt; the connection is in the verb qada and the sense of ending what remains.

The Body as Part of the Rites

The rites of Hajj are not only abstract intentions or spoken words. The body is present in them: it travels, leaves daily comfort, bears restrictions, carries heat and dust, leaves hair and nails unattended, and then exits that state at the appointed time.

If tafath is translated as “Hajj duties” or “rites,” part of the verse disappears. At this point the verse speaks specifically about the body: a body that has borne a special state and must now bring that state to an end.

Therefore, later reflective phrases such as “the dust of multiplicities” or “the path of the body” should not be treated as the direct meaning of the root. Even if used as secondary reflection, they do not replace the lexical meaning.

Tafath and the End of Ihram

Tafath is connected with the bodily traces of the period of ihram. When the pilgrim performs qada of tafath, delayed bodily actions take place: shaving or shortening hair, trimming nails, washing the body, removing dust and dishevelment, and returning to ordinary bodily grooming.

Qada of tafath is a bodily act and a sign of passing from one ritual stage to another.

At the same time, tafath should not be equated with the legal term tahallul. Tahallul means exiting the prohibitions of ihram; qada of tafath means attending to the bodily traces of that period. They may be close in practice, but they are not the same lexically.

Tafath, Vow, and Tawaf

The verse lists three expressions one after another:

لْيَقْضُوا تَفَثَهُمْ
وَلْيُوفُوا نُذُورَهُمْ
وَلْيَطَّوَّفُوا بِالْبَيْتِ الْعَتِيقِ

They should bring their bodily dishevelment to an end; fulfill their vows; and make tawaf around the Ancient House.

This separation shows that tafath, vow, and tawaf are not the same thing. Tafath is the bodily state; the vow is a commitment to be fulfilled; and tawaf is movement around the House.

The three verbs are also precise: qada for ending a state, wafa for fully fulfilling a commitment, and tawaf for moving around a center.

Body, Commitment, and Center

The order of the verse is notable: ending the bodily condition; fulfilling commitments; and moving around the Ancient House.

As a reflection, one may say that after ending bodily traces and fulfilling commitments, the pilgrim places his motion around a center. But this reflection should not replace the direct meaning of the word. Tafath still means bodily dishevelment and the bodily traces of the period of abstention.

Tafath and Al-Bayt Al-Atiq

At the end of the verse comes al-bayt al-atiq. The word atiq can be connected with antiquity, oldness, and in some uses freedom or release.

The adjacency of ending a bodily state and making tawaf around the Ancient House has reflective capacity. But this meaning should not be transferred into the root T F TH. The lexical meaning of tafath remains the bodily condition and dishevelment of the body. The discussion of atiq belongs in the separate root A T Q.

Is Tafath Only Hair and Nails?

Hair and nails are among the clearest examples of tafath, but the word is probably broader than them. Tafath may include disheveled hair, untrimmed nails, dust of travel, an unkempt appearance, and the bodily traces of ihram.

Therefore, “trimming hair and nails” shows only part of qada of tafath, not the full meaning of the word.

Is Tafath the Same as the Rites?

Lexically, no. The verse mentions vow and tawaf separately. If tafath meant all the rites of Hajj, the sentence structure would become unclear.

Tafath is a bodily state to be ended, not the name of all Hajj actions. Some Hajj actions may occur at the time of qada of tafath, but occurring at the same time does not make two meanings identical.

Similar Uses

Tafath and wasakh: Wasakh means dirt or filth. Tafath can include wasakh, but it is broader, because it also includes hair, nails, dishevelment, and the traces of ihram.

Tafath and daran: Daran is closer to lasting or stuck dirt. Tafath may include dirt, but it is not limited to stuck dirt.

Tafath and sha'ath: Sha'ath concerns disheveled hair and appearance. Sha'ath is one manifestation of tafath. Not every sha'ath is ritual tafath, but tafath can include sha'ath.

Tafath and ghubar: Ghubar, dust, may settle on the body, clothing, or environment and may be part of the traces of travel. But tafath is not only dust; it is the whole bodily state during abstention.

Tafath and taharah: Taharah concerns purity and removing impurity or a barrier. Tafath is a state that is brought to an end. Qada of tafath may include washing and cleaning, but the word itself does not mean taharah.

Tafath and halq: Halq means shaving hair. Halq may be one act through which tafath is ended, but it is not tafath itself.

Tafath and taqsir: Taqsir means shortening hair, especially in Hajj. It can be an example of ending tafath, not a full equivalent.

Tafath and nusuk: Nusuk concerns an act of worship, sacrifice, or rite. Tafath is a bodily state within the framework of the rites, not all the rites themselves.

Tafath and ihram: Ihram is entering the ritual state of restriction. Tafath is the bodily traces that remain during that period.

Tafath and tahallul: Tahallul is exiting the prohibitions of ihram. Qada of tafath is the bodily transition and attending to the traces of that period. The two are close, but lexically distinct.

Grammar and Structure

In ثُمَّ لْيَقْضُوا تَفَثَهُمْ, thumma marks a transition to the next stage, after mention of remembrance, sacrifice, or part of the Hajj rites.

لْيَقْضُوا comes with the lām of command: they should end, complete, or discharge.

Qada here does not mean judging. It is closer to completing and bringing to an end.

تَفَثَهُمْ is tafath in construct with a plural pronoun: their tafath. Each pilgrim, or the group, brings the bodily traces of that state to an end.

The difference between the three verbs of the verse matters: li-yaqdu comes with tafath because a state is ended; wa-li-yufu comes with vows because a commitment is fully fulfilled; and wa-li-yattawwafu comes with the House because movement around a center is performed.

For this reason, all of these should not be flattened into a general phrase such as “perform their rites.”

Nearby Roots

T F TH and W S KH (wasakh): Wasakh is dirt or filth. Tafath is the broader bodily state in the period of ihram and abstention.

T F TH and D R N (daran): Daran is lasting or stuck dirt. Tafath is not limited to this kind of dirt.

T F TH and SH AYN TH (sha'ath): Sha'ath is dishevelment of hair and appearance. Tafath can include sha'ath, but is not limited to it.

T F TH and GH B R (ghubar): Dust is one trace of travel. Tafath is the whole bodily state and traces of abstention.

T F TH and T H R (taharah): Taharah is purity and removing impurity. Tafath is a state that ends, not purity itself.

T F TH and H L Q (halq): Halq is shaving hair. Halq may be part of qada of tafath.

T F TH and Q S R (taqsir): Taqsir is shortening hair. It is also one practical example of ending tafath, not tafath itself.

T F TH and N S K (nusuk): Nusuk is a rite or ritual act. Tafath is a bodily state that is ended within the rites.

T F TH and H R M (ihram): Ihram is entering ritual restriction. Tafath is the bodily traces of that restriction.

T F TH and Q D Y (qada): Qada is the act of ending; tafath is what is ended.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For تَفَث: dishevelment; bodily dishevelment; bodily traces of ihram; bodily dirt and unkemptness; the bodily state of a period of abstention.

For تَفَثَهُمْ: their dishevelment; their bodily dishevelment; their bodily traces of ihram; the unkempt state of their bodies.

For قَضَى تَفَثَهُ: he brought his dishevelment to an end; he removed the bodily traces of his ihram; he ended his body's unkempt state; he removed from his body the traces of the period of abstention.

ثُمَّ لْيَقْضُوا تَفَثَهُمْ

Then they should bring their bodily dishevelment to an end.

Then they should remove their dishevelment.

Then they should remove the bodily traces of their ihram.

Then they should bring the unkempt state of their bodies to an end.

Then they should attend to their bodies and remove the traces of the period of abstention.

A translation close to the structure of the verse:

ثُمَّ لْيَقْضُوا تَفَثَهُمْ وَلْيُوفُوا نُذُورَهُمْ وَلْيَطَّوَّفُوا بِالْبَيْتِ الْعَتِيقِ

Then they should bring their bodily dishevelment to an end, fulfill their vows, and make tawaf around the Ancient House.

A more explanatory translation:

Then they should remove the bodily traces of their ihram, fully fulfill their vows, and make tawaf around the Ancient House.

Summary

T F TH is a limited and bodily root that appears in the Qur'an only in the Hajj context. Tafath indicates the body's dishevelment, dirt, and unkemptness during a period of abstention.

During ihram, the pilgrim delays some ordinary bodily grooming and carries on the body the traces of travel, heat, and restriction. After reaching a certain stage, he must perform qada of his tafath; that is, bring this state to an end and bring the body out of its traces.

The choice of the verb qada matters. Tafath is not merely dirt to be washed off; it is a remaining state that must be concluded. For this reason, the verse distinguishes between qada of tafath, fulfillment of vows, and tawaf.

This word shows that the body is not secondary or hidden in Hajj. The body bears restriction, takes on dishevelment, and then exits that condition within the frame of the rites. At the same time, symbolic layers about bodily release or its connection with al-bayt al-atiq should remain secondary reflections, not the lexical meaning of T F TH.

T F TH is close to wasakh, daran, sha'ath, ghubar, taharah, halq, taqsir, nusuk, ihram, and tahallul, but it is not identical with any of them. Its special core is the total bodily traces and dishevelment caused by a period of abstention, and ending that state by returning the body toward ordinary order.
