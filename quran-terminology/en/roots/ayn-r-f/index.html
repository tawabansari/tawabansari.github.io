Core Meaning

The root ع ر ف in its basic sense points to knowing, recognizing, discerning, the appearance of a sign, and a thing coming out of ambiguity and unfamiliarity. Knowledge in this root is not only mental information; it is a kind of recognition. Something has such a sign, trace, face, scent, place, or prominence that it separates from what is other than it and becomes identifiable.

From this field comes the word ma'ruf: something known, fitting, clear, recognizable, and familiar to sound fitrah and sound reason. Ma'ruf in the Qur'an is not merely “the common custom of people.” If ma'ruf is reduced to tribal habit, the desire of power, religious custom, or social taste, its Qur'anic meaning is lost. Ma'ruf is what can be recognized in the horizon of good, justice, dignity, mercy, and truth.

Important Forms

عرف is a past-tense verb and points to knowing or recognizing:

وَإِذَا سَمِعُوا مَا أُنزِلَ إِلَى الرَّسُولِ تَرَىٰ أَعْيُنَهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ الدَّمْعِ مِمَّا عَرَفُوا مِنَ الْحَقِّ

And when they hear what was sent down to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflowing with tears because of what they recognized of the truth.

Al-Ma'idah 5:83

يعرفون is a plural present-tense verb and points to continuing recognition or knowing:

يَعْرِفُونَهُ كَمَا يَعْرِفُونَ أَبْنَاءَهُمْ

They recognize him as they recognize their own children.

Al-Baqarah 2:146

تعرف is a present-tense verb and can point to discerning from an evident sign:

تَعْرِفُ فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ نَضْرَةَ النَّعِيمِ

You recognize in their faces the freshness and brightness of bliss.

Al-Mutaffifin 83:24

اعترف is form VIII and points to confession: the human being recognizes something inwardly, does not push it away, faces it, and admits it:

وَآخَرُونَ اعْتَرَفُوا بِذُنُوبِهِمْ

And others confessed their sins.

At-Tawbah 9:102

معروف is a description from this same root: something known, fitting, recognizable, and acceptable in the field of good:

وَقُولُوا لَهُمْ قَوْلًا مَّعْرُوفًا

And speak to them a ma'ruf and fitting word.

An-Nisa' 4:5

And also:

قَوْلٌ مَّعْرُوفٌ وَمَغْفِرَةٌ خَيْرٌ مِّن صَدَقَةٍ يَتْبَعُهَا أَذًى

A ma'ruf word and forgiveness are better than charity followed by harm.

Al-Baqarah 2:263

العرف also appears in the Qur'an:

خُذِ الْعَفْوَ وَأْمُرْ بِالْعُرْفِ وَأَعْرِضْ عَنِ الْجَاهِلِينَ

Take pardon, command 'urf, and turn away from the ignorant.

Al-A'raf 7:199

Here, 'urf points to recognized fittingness, clear good conduct, and a matter discernible in the field of good.

الأعراف is also from this field:

وَعَلَى الْأَعْرَافِ رِجَالٌ

And on the a'raf are men.

Al-A'raf 7:46

A'raf points to heights, borders, or overlooking places from which recognition and discernment become possible.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of ع ر ف turns around a knowledge that arises through sign, prominence, trace, familiarity, and distinction. A thing is known when it comes out of anonymity, alienness, or ambiguity and becomes recognizable.

This recognition can be sensory, such as recognizing a face, appearance, scent, or sign. It can be ethical, such as recognizing truth, good, or fitting conduct. It can be social, such as qawl ma'ruf, living together in ma'ruf, or commanding ma'ruf. In all these uses, the central axis is that something becomes clear in the horizon of sound recognition.

In Arabic, 'urf can also refer to the mane or prominent part of an animal: something high, visible, and distinct. It is also close to scent or fragrance, something recognized by its spread. These two images help us understand the root: ع ر ف is connected with prominence, appearance, discernment, and sensory familiarity. Ma'ruf in the ethical field is conduct whose signs of fittingness and good are evident; not conduct that is hidden, ambiguous, alien, or imposed.

But Qur'anic ma'ruf must be distinguished from raw social custom. Social custom may be right or wrong. A society may become used to oppression, treat discrimination as normal, or call wrongdoing the tradition of the fathers. The Qur'an does not make such habits the criterion of truth. Qur'anic ma'ruf is what is recognizable in the field of sound recognition, living fitrah, justice, mercy, and guidance; not everything people have become used to.

Material Image

The material image of this root can be seen in several scenes.

First, something prominent that can be seen from afar, like the mane of an animal or a height separated from the surrounding surface. Such a thing is not hidden or lost; it is seen and helps discernment. From this field, a'raf comes close to heights or overlooking places, places from which borders, faces, and groups can be recognized.

Second, there is a scent that spreads and is recognized by smell. Fragrance does not need long argument; its presence is known through sense. This image is useful for understanding ma'ruf. In the ethical field, ma'ruf is a conduct from which sound fitrah does not feel alienated. Like a good scent, it carries a sign of health and fittingness.

Third, there is a face recognized among a crowd. A person recognizes someone he knows by signs, state, face, or trace. The Qur'an too speaks of recognizing faces and marks.

From these images, the meaning of ma'ruf becomes clearer. Ma'ruf is conduct whose signs of fittingness and good are visible; not something called “known” merely because of social repetition or the pressure of power.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, ع ر ف points to knowing, recognizing, being familiar, having a sign, and discerning. عرفت الشيء means “I knew it” or “I recognized it.” معرفة is from this same field and points to knowledge and awareness.

'Urf is also used for a prominent and visible part, such as the mane of an animal or something distinguished from other parts. 'Urf is also close to fragrance: a scent known by its spread. These sensory uses show that the root is not merely mental knowing, but is connected with appearance, sign, discernment, and familiarity.

In Arabic usage, ma'ruf refers to something known, approved, and acceptable. But in Qur'anic reading, this knownness must not be reduced to raw tribal habit or a corrupt social norm. The Qur'an repeatedly criticizes the habits of the fathers, blind imitation, and corrupt traditions. Therefore, Qur'anic ma'ruf must be understood in relation to truth, good, justice, and guidance.

For more precise pre-Qur'anic usage, reliable evidence from pre-Qur'anic poetry or ancient Arabic usage should be examined. If reliable evidence is found, it can be added.

Clear Recognition: Like Recognizing Children

The Qur'an says about some of the People of the Book:

الَّذِينَ آتَيْنَاهُمُ الْكِتَابَ يَعْرِفُونَهُ كَمَا يَعْرِفُونَ أَبْنَاءَهُمْ

Those to whom We gave the Book recognize him as they recognize their own children.

Al-Baqarah 2:146

In this verse, recognition is compared with the closest kind of familiarity: recognizing one's own child. So this is not vague or distant knowing; it is clear, close, and recognizable knowledge.

The same meaning appears in another verse:

الَّذِينَ آتَيْنَاهُمُ الْكِتَابَ يَعْرِفُونَهُ كَمَا يَعْرِفُونَ أَبْنَاءَهُمُ

Those to whom We gave the Book recognize him as they recognize their own children.

Al-An'am 6:20

This use shows that ع ر ف is connected with clear discernment through signs, earlier familiarity, and close recognition.

Recognizing Truth

In Surah Al-Ma'idah, it says:

وَإِذَا سَمِعُوا مَا أُنزِلَ إِلَى الرَّسُولِ تَرَىٰ أَعْيُنَهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ الدَّمْعِ مِمَّا عَرَفُوا مِنَ الْحَقِّ

And when they hear what was sent down to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflowing with tears because of what they recognized of the truth.

Al-Ma'idah 5:83

In this verse, recognition of truth is not only theoretical awareness. Hearing revelation, recognizing truth, and the emotional trace in the eyes come together. They recognize something harmonious with truth, and it affects them inwardly.

This use is important for understanding ma'ruf. Qur'anic ma'ruf must be recognizable with truth. Whatever a tribe or power names ma'ruf is not Qur'anic ma'ruf if it is separated from truth.

Recognition Through Face, Mark, and Effect

The Qur'an speaks of recognizing faces and signs:

تَعْرِفُ فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ نَضْرَةَ النَّعِيمِ

You recognize in their faces the freshness and brightness of bliss.

Al-Mutaffifin 83:24

And about criminals:

يُعْرَفُ الْمُجْرِمُونَ بِسِيمَاهُمْ

The criminals are recognized by their marks.

Ar-Rahman 55:41

In these verses, recognition happens through effect, appearance, and sign. ع ر ف is not only inward knowing; it is discernment from mark and appearance. The same field of meaning is present in ma'ruf: ma'ruf is something whose signs of fittingness, good, and health are evident.

Confession: Recognition That Passes Beyond Self-Deception

اعترف is from this same root and points to knowing together with admission:

وَآخَرُونَ اعْتَرَفُوا بِذُنُوبِهِمْ

And others confessed their sins.

At-Tawbah 9:102

Confession is not merely knowing. It is facing the recognized reality and admitting it. In this use, the human being does not hide his sin, justify it, or push it outside the field of recognition. He recognizes it as sin and accepts responsibility for it.

This is ethically important. If recognition is truthful, it can bring the human being to confession. A person who recognizes error but covers it with justification has not yet brought recognition into sidq.

Ma'ruf in Speech

The Qur'an speaks of qawl ma'ruf:

وَقُولُوا لَهُمْ قَوْلًا مَّعْرُوفًا

And speak to them a ma'ruf and fitting word.

An-Nisa' 4:5

In this verse, ma'ruf relates to speech. Qawl ma'ruf means speech recognized in fittingness: respectful, clear, healthy, and suitable to the addressee. The verse concerns people whose wealth is managed by others; so ma'ruf speech means that even when the other person is in a weaker position, speech must preserve dignity and fittingness.

Another verse says:

قَوْلٌ مَّعْرُوفٌ وَمَغْفِرَةٌ خَيْرٌ مِّن صَدَقَةٍ يَتْبَعُهَا أَذًى

A ma'ruf word and forgiveness are better than charity followed by harm.

Al-Baqarah 2:263

Here qawl ma'ruf is set opposite charity followed by harm. This shows that ma'ruf is not merely a common act or an apparently good action. Ma'ruf speech is speech that preserves human dignity. If charity comes with humiliation and harm, it falls below ma'ruf speech and forgiveness.

Ma'ruf in Family and Marital Relations

The Qur'an says about marital life:

وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

And live with them according to ma'ruf and fittingness.

An-Nisa' 4:19

Ma'ruf here relates to the way of life and companionship. The relationship with a spouse must rest on sound ethical recognition, fittingness, dignity, gentleness, and regard for right. Ma'ruf here is not merely the custom of people, because many customs concerning women can be oppressive. The Qur'an places ma'ruf in the field of humane and right-bearing companionship.

In the verse of divorce, it also says:

فَأَمْسِكُوهُنَّ بِمَعْرُوفٍ أَوْ فَارِقُوهُنَّ بِمَعْرُوفٍ

Then keep them according to ma'ruf, or separate from them according to ma'ruf.

At-Talaq 65:2

This verse shows that ma'ruf is required both in continuing a relationship and in ending it. Even separation must not become revenge, humiliation, harm, or injustice. Ma'ruf means recognized fittingness even at the time of conflict and separation.

Ma'ruf in Economy and Social Responsibility

The Qur'an says about providing food and clothing for mothers:

وَعَلَى الْمَوْلُودِ لَهُ رِزْقُهُنَّ وَكِسْوَتُهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

And upon the father is their provision and clothing according to ma'ruf.

Al-Baqarah 2:233

Ma'ruf here points to measure, quality, fittingness, and regard for right in providing the needs of life. This ma'ruf must be consistent with ability, need, dignity, and justice.

In the same verse, it says:

لَا تُكَلَّفُ نَفْسٌ إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا

No self is burdened except according to its capacity.

Al-Baqarah 2:233

So ma'ruf in economy is neither extravagance nor oppressive constriction. It is fitting provision within ability and right.

Commanding Ma'ruf

The direct connection of this root with the root أ م ر appears in the phrase commanding ma'ruf:

وَلْتَكُن مِّنكُمْ أُمَّةٌ يَدْعُونَ إِلَى الْخَيْرِ وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ

And let there be from among you an ummah that calls to good, commands ma'ruf, and forbids munkar.

Ali 'Imran 3:104

In this verse, commanding ma'ruf comes beside calling to good. This order is important. Ma'ruf must be understood in the horizon of good. If something named ma'ruf is separated from good, justice, dignity, and truth, it only bears the name ma'ruf.

Another verse says:

كُنتُمْ خَيْرَ أُمَّةٍ أُخْرِجَتْ لِلنَّاسِ تَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَتَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ وَتُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ

You were the best ummah brought out for the people: you command ma'ruf, forbid munkar, and have faith in The God.

Ali 'Imran 3:110

Here ma'ruf must not be reduced to a corrupt social custom or the command of religious power. Commanding ma'ruf is calling and directing toward recognized fittingness in the field of good, not a license for religious coercion or inspecting people.

'Urf: Commanding Recognized Fittingness

In Surah Al-A'raf, it says:

خُذِ الْعَفْوَ وَأْمُرْ بِالْعُرْفِ وَأَعْرِضْ عَنِ الْجَاهِلِينَ

Take pardon, command 'urf, and turn away from the ignorant.

Al-A'raf 7:199

العرف in this verse points to recognized fittingness, good and discernible conduct, and a path close to ma'ruf. This verse is important in three directions: taking pardon, commanding 'urf, and turning away from the ignorant. Calling to fittingness must therefore come with the spirit of pardon and avoidance of ignorant entanglement.

العرف in this verse must not be reduced to raw habit of people, because the Qur'an itself criticizes blind following of the habits of the fathers. 'Urf here is recognized fittingness in the horizon of good and sound reason.

A'raf: A Place of Recognition and Discernment

Surah Al-A'raf speaks of the a'raf:

وَعَلَى الْأَعْرَافِ رِجَالٌ يَعْرِفُونَ كُلًّا بِسِيمَاهُمْ

And on the a'raf are men who recognize each by their marks.

Al-A'raf 7:46

In this verse, a'raf is joined with recognition through marks. The root ع ر ف returns again to discernment through sign. A'raf can point to heights or borders from which recognition and discernment become possible.

This use shows that the root ع ر ف is connected with sign, mark, border, height, and discernment. Recognition is not only information; it is the ability to see differences and signs.

The Garden Made Known and Prepared

In Surah Muhammad, it says:

وَيُدْخِلُهُمُ الْجَنَّةَ عَرَّفَهَا لَهُمْ

And He will enter them into the Garden which He made known and prepared for them.

Muhammad 47:6

This verse uses the form عرّف. Its meaning can include making known, showing, familiarizing, preparing, or determining. In connection with the field of scent and sign, one can also understand that the Garden is not alien or unfamiliar to them; The God has made it known, prepared, and receivable for them.

This use shows that ع ر ف is not only the human being's recognition of things. Sometimes something is made known, specified, and familiar for the human being.

Similar Uses

ع ر ف sometimes points to clear recognition of a person or truth, such as recognizing the Messenger or recognizing truth.

Sometimes it points to recognition through effect and appearance, such as recognizing bliss in faces or recognizing criminals by their signs.

Sometimes it reaches confession: recognition that passes beyond self-deception and becomes verbal and ethical acknowledgement.

Sometimes it relates to ma'ruf: what is recognizable in the field of good, fittingness, and sound recognition.

Sometimes it points to 'urf: recognized and acceptable fittingness, not every social habit.

Sometimes it relates to a'raf: a place or border from which recognition and discernment become possible.

Sometimes it relates to 'arrafa: making known, familiarizing, determining, or preparing something to be received.

Grammar and Structure

In يعرفونه كما يعرفون أبناءهم, the comparison with كما shows that recognition is clear and close, not a distant guess or vague familiarity.

In مما عرفوا من الحق, the particle من shows that their recognition came from the manifestation or portion of truth. Hearing revelation makes them recognize truth, and its effect appears in the eyes.

In تعرف في وجوههم, recognition happens in the faces. The particle في shows that the sign is seen within the faces themselves.

In يعرفون كلا بسيماهم, recognition is through the marks. The ba' in بسيماهم shows the instrument of recognition.

In اعترفوا بذنوبهم, the ba' shows acknowledgement of something known and accepted. Confession brings recognition close to verbal responsibility.

In قول معروف, ma'ruf describes speech. The speech itself must be recognized in fittingness, health, and suitability.

In بالمعروف, the ba' shows that behavior is done by the criterion and method of ma'ruf, as in عاشروهن بالمعروف or فأمسكوهن بمعروف.

In وأمر بالعرف, 'urf is the object of command. But the context with خذ العفو and أعرض عن الجاهلين shows that this command must not be understood as harsh, ignorant, or conflict-seeking.

In عرّفها لهم, form II shows that the Garden was made known, prepared, or determined for them; by divine act it came out of unfamiliarity and became receivable for them.

Nearby Roots

ع ل م points to knowing, awareness, and encompassing what is known. Ma'rifah from ع ر ف has more of the color of recognition, discernment, and knowing through sign or familiarity. Every ma'rifah is a kind of ilm, but not every ilm is necessarily ma'rifah in the sense of sign-based recognition.

د ر ي points to realizing or knowing something, sometimes after searching or asking. ع ر ف is more concerned with discernment and familiarity.

ب ص ر points to seeing, sight, and insight. ع ر ف can accompany seeing, but its axis is recognition and discernment, not mere seeing.

ش ع ر points to subtle awareness, sensing, and delicate perception. Ma'rifah is clearer and more diagnostic.

ذ ك ر points to remembrance, the return of meaning into presence, and coming out of forgetfulness. ع ر ف is recognition and discernment; dhikr is the return of meaning into presence.

ن ك ر is the important opposite of this root. Nakir points to unfamiliarity, alienness, reprehensibility, and being rejectable. Ma'ruf and munkar in the Qur'an stand on this contrast.

ح س ن points to goodness and beauty of quality. Ma'ruf may have husn, but ma'ruf is discussed from the angle of recognized fittingness.

خ ي ر points to good and real value. Ma'ruf must be in the field of khayr, but khayr and ma'ruf are not identical. Khayr is value and direction; ma'ruf is recognized fittingness in behavior and society.

ق س ط points to measured justice and giving the correct share. Ma'ruf often accompanies qist in social relations, but qist is more precisely about the justice of shares.

س ي م points to sign, mark, and appearance. ع ر ف can happen through sima, but sima is the sign, and 'urf is recognition through the sign.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

عرف: knew, recognized, discerned.

يعرفون: they know, they recognize, they discern.

يعرفونه كما يعرفون أبناءهم: they recognize him clearly, as they recognize their own children.

تعرف في وجوههم: you recognize in their faces; you discern from their faces.

عرفوا من الحق: they recognized of the truth; they recognized the truth.

اعترفوا بذنوبهم: they confessed their sins; they faced their sins and admitted them.

معروف: recognized fittingness, a matter known in good, conduct acceptable to sound fitrah, discernible goodness, depending on context.

قول معروف: fitting speech, dignified speech, respectful speech free from harm.

عاشروهن بالمعروف: live with them according to fittingness recognized in good.

أمسكوهن بمعروف: keep them in a fitting manner.

فارقوهن بمعروف: separate from them in a fitting and just manner.

يأمرون بالمعروف: they command ma'ruf; they direct toward recognized fittingness; they keep the signs of good alive.

العرف: recognized fittingness, good and acceptable conduct, a clear matter in good.

الأعراف: a'raf, heights or borders of discernment, a place of recognition through signs.

عرّفها لهم: He made it known to them, prepared and familiarized it for them, determined it and made it receivable for them.

Related Entry

This root is directly connected with the entry أ م ر, because the Qur'anic phrase يأمرون بالمعروف cannot be fully understood without understanding ع ر ف and ma'ruf. Also, for the opposite side, the entry ن ك ر should be written separately.

Summary

The root ع ر ف points to knowing, recognizing, discerning, becoming familiar, the prominence of a sign, and a thing coming out of an unknown state. Its material image can be seen in a visible mane or prominence, a recognizable scent, a recognizable face, and overlooking heights.

In the Qur'an, this root is used for recognizing the Messenger, recognizing truth, recognizing through faces and marks, confessing sin, qawl ma'ruf, ma'ruf conduct, commanding ma'ruf, 'urf, a'raf, and making the Garden known or prepared.

Ma'ruf in the Qur'an is not merely the common custom of people. Ma'ruf is what can be recognized in the field of sound recognition, good, fittingness, justice, and dignity. A corrupt custom, a tradition of the fathers, or a power-made habit does not become Qur'anic ma'ruf merely by being common.

The difference between ع ر ف and ilm is that ilm is broader awareness, while 'urf emphasizes recognition, discernment, and sign-based familiarity. The difference between ma'ruf and husn is that husn shows good and beautiful quality, while ma'ruf is recognized and discernible fittingness in conduct. The difference between ma'ruf and khayr is that khayr is general value and direction, while ma'ruf is the social and behavioral appearance of what is recognized in the horizon of good.

In Forqan translation, ma'ruf should not be translated only as “custom” or “what people approve.” Depending on context, “recognized fittingness,” “ma'ruf conduct,” “a matter known in good,” “fitting speech,” “dignified speech,” or “discernible goodness” may be more precise.
