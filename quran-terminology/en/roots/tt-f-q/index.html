Beginning and undertaking an action: from covering the body with garden leaves to Solomon's action toward the horses

The Arabic root ṭ f q concerns beginning and engaging in an action. طَفِقَ يَفْعَلُ means that someone began doing something or set about it. It describes entry into the action itself, rather than merely deciding or intending to act.

This verb belongs to a group expressing the beginning of an action in conjunction with another verb. طَفِقَ يَعْمَلُ means “he began working,” and طَفِقَ يَتَكَلَّمُ means “he began speaking.”

Some usages also approach continued engagement in an activity. Nevertheless, the verb itself determines neither duration, intensity, nor completion. An action may end shortly after beginning or continue for some time; context establishes the extent and character of continuation.

The root occurs three times in the Quran, exclusively as a perfect verb: وَطَفِقَا twice for Adam and his spouse, and فَطَفِقَ once for Solomon.

In the first two passages, Adam and his spouse begin covering themselves with garden leaves after their private parts become apparent. In the third, Solomon calls the horses back and undertakes an action described as مَسْحًا; the translation adopted here understands it as passing his hands over their legs and necks.

These occurrences show that طفق does not name covering or stroking. It expresses beginning and engaging in an action identified by the following verb or expression.

1. Historical meaning and early Arabic usage

طَفِقَ: beginning and engaging in an action

Classical Arabic records طَفِقَ يَفْعَلُ كَذَا for beginning or setting about something.

It is explained through expressions such as أَخَذَ يَفْعَلُ and جَعَلَ يَفْعَلُ: someone set about doing something.

For example, طَفِقَ يَمْشِي means “he began walking,” and طَفِقَ يَضْحَكُ means “he began laughing.”

The second verb identifies the activity in these examples.

طفق does not itself mean walking or laughing; it indicates entry into that activity.

Lexical explanations sometimes associate it with ظَلَّ يَفْعَلُ: engaging in an activity and continuing it.

This shows that it expresses more than a decision or mental beginning. In its usual construction, the action has actually begun and the agent is engaged in it.

It does not follow that every instance describes a long, uninterrupted activity. Such features depend on the sentence and situation.

طَفِقَ as remaining attached

Arabic dictionaries also explain طَفِقَ through لَزِمَ.

لزم concerns remaining with, adhering to, or not leaving something or somewhere.

طَفِقَ الْمَوْضِعَ is recorded for staying in or remaining attached to a place.

Here remaining is prominent, rather than beginning a second action.

This may relate semantically to طفق يفعل: someone engaged in an activity remains involved with it for a time.

This is a semantic explanation, however. The evidence does not conclusively establish whether beginning developed from remaining or conversely.

The two usages should therefore be distinguished: beginning and undertaking an action, and remaining attached to something.

Attaining an objective

Some lexical reports record طَفِقَ فُلَانٌ بِمَا أَرَادَ for someone attaining what they wanted or succeeding in reaching their objective.

أَطْفَقَهُ اللَّهُ بِهِ is likewise recorded for The God enabling someone to attain it.

This branch differs from the usual طفق يفعل construction.

One concerns engaging in an action; the other emphasizes attaining a desired thing.

Their shared root does not make Quranic طفق mean success. In its three Quranic occurrences, the construction indicates beginning and undertaking an action.

These additional usages illuminate the family's recorded range but should not enter verse translations without contextual evidence.

Historical witnesses and their limits

Older dictionaries record beginning, engaging in an action, and remaining attached. Reports of Bedouin usage also concern attaining an objective.

Among the evidence examined here, no independent verse or text both securely dated before Islam and using طفق in the relevant sense has been sufficiently established.

Dictionary examples should not therefore be attributed to pre-Islamic poets, nor should a recorded dictionary meaning alone establish pre-Islamic use of that form.

The Quran itself directly attests the verb in the language of revelation. All three occurrences support beginning and undertaking an action.

Thus the attested Quranic meaning is beginning and engaging in an action. Other usages are recorded in classical Arabic, but their dates of origin and precise relationship to this sense remain uncertain.

2. Morphology and syntax

طَفِقَ is an unaugmented triliteral verb formed from ط, ف, and ق.

The perfect طَفِقَ with kasrah on the second radical is established; طَفَقَ with fatḥah is also recorded in some usages and lexical accounts.

The imperfect forms يَطْفَقُ and يَطْفِقُ are recorded. Their variation does not necessarily establish separate meanings.

Only two written forms occur in the Quran:

وَطَفِقَا in Al-Aʿrāf 7:22 and Ṭā Hā 20:121.

فَطَفِقَ in Ṣād 38:33.

All three occurrences are perfect verbs.

A verb of inception

Arabic grammar classifies طفق among verbs expressing the start of an action.

These generally accompany an imperfect verb and express the beginning of its activity.

For example:

طَفِقَ الرَّجُلُ يَعْمَلُ

This means “the man began working.”

طفق marks the beginning and engagement; يعمل identifies the activity.

In the usual inceptive construction, طفق الرجل alone is incomplete unless the activity is understood from context.

It therefore requires an expression identifying the activity, whether stated or recoverable from context.

Traditional syntax treats it as a deficient verb of inception with a subject-like noun and predicate. Its predicate is an imperfect verbal clause without أَنْ, which may be omitted when indicated by context.

For understanding the verse, the essential point is that طفق marks undertaking the main activity rather than naming it.

Does it mean being close to acting?

Some grammatical classifications discuss طفق alongside كاد and كرب.

Their meanings nevertheless differ significantly.

كَادَ يَفْعَلُ concerns coming close to doing something.

طَفِقَ يَفْعَلُ describes actually beginning it.

كَادَ يَسْقُطُ can mean that someone nearly fell without actually falling.

طَفِقَ يَمْشِي means that walking began.

Thus nearing an action and beginning it are distinct.

In all three Quranic occurrences, the participants undertake the action rather than merely approach its threshold.

Does it establish prolonged continuation?

Lexical accounts sometimes connect طفق with continuing and remaining engaged in an action.

طفق يكتب can be understood as beginning to write and becoming occupied with writing.

The expression alone does not determine how long the writing lasts.

It might last moments or continue for hours.

Likewise, طفقا يخصفان describes beginning to cover the body with leaves, without specifying duration.

فطفق مسحا describes Solomon undertaking the action, rendered here as passing his hands over the horses, without specifying repetitions or duration.

Beginning and engagement are clear; the precise duration and continuation require further evidence.

3. وَطَفِقَا in the account of Adam and his spouse

Al-Aʿrāf 7:22 states:

فَدَلَّاهُمَا بِغُرُورٍ فَلَمَّا ذَاقَا الشَّجَرَةَ بَدَتْ لَهُمَا سَوْآتُهُمَا وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِنْ وَرَقِ الْجَنَّةِ وَنَادَاهُمَا رَبُّهُمَا أَلَمْ أَنْهَكُمَا عَنْ تِلْكُمَا الشَّجَرَةِ وَأَقُلْ لَكُمَا إِنَّ الشَّيْطَانَ لَكُمَا عَدُوٌّ مُبِينٌ

fa-dallāhumā bi-ghurūrin fa-lammā dhāqā al-shajarata badat lahumā sawʾātuhumā wa-ṭafiqā yakhṣifāni ʿalayhimā min waraqi al-jannah wa-nādāhumā rabbuhumā a-lam anhakumā ʿan tilkumā al-shajarati wa-aqul lakumā inna al-shayṭāna lakumā ʿaduwwun mubīn

Al-Aʿrāf 7:22

“So he drew them down through deception. When they tasted the tree, their private parts became apparent to them, and they began joining garden leaves over themselves. Their Lord called to them: Did I not forbid you that tree and tell you that Satan is a clear enemy to you?”

Al-Aʿrāf 7:22

وَطَفِقَا combines the conjunction وَ with the dual perfect طَفِقَا.

The final alif refers to both Adam and his spouse. In the deficient-verb analysis, it occupies the subject-like noun position of طفق, while the alif in يخصفان is the subject of that verb.

يَخْصِفَانِ is also dual and identifies the action they began.

خَصَفَ concerns joining and connecting components such as leaves.

Here Adam and his spouse join garden leaves to cover themselves.

The expression has two components:

طَفِقَا indicates that the two undertook an action.

يَخْصِفَانِ identifies that action.

Covering or joining leaves follows from خ ص ف and the rest of the sentence, not from ط ف ق itself.

Beginning the action after their private parts became apparent

Before وَطَفِقَا comes:

بَدَتْ لَهُمَا سَوْآتُهُمَا

badat lahumā sawʾātuhumā

Al-Aʿrāf 7:22

“Their private parts became apparent to them.”

The Quran then describes beginning to cover themselves with garden leaves.

The sequence presents the action as a response to the exposure of what had previously been covered.

Covering the body relates here to a new situation following eating from the tree.

طفق itself does not mean feeling shame, fearing, or hiding.

Nor does it specify how quickly they worked or how long they took to provide the covering.

Those features should not be extracted from the inceptive verb alone.

Does طفقا establish completion?

The verse says they undertook joining leaves over themselves.

This establishes an actual beginning but does not independently describe the number of leaves, the final covering, or the duration.

It should not be translated as “they completed making the covering.”

Conversely, “they began” should not imply that they merely intended to cover themselves without doing anything.

يخصفان names the activity that began.

The distinction matters: beginning establishes the onset of an action, but does not alone establish its completion.

4. The occurrence in Ṭā Hā: the same action in another context

Ṭā Hā 20:121 states:

فَأَكَلَا مِنْهَا فَبَدَتْ لَهُمَا سَوْآتُهُمَا وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِنْ وَرَقِ الْجَنَّةِ وَعَصَىٰ آدَمُ رَبَّهُ فَغَوَىٰ

fa-akalā minhā fa-badat lahumā sawʾātuhumā wa-ṭafiqā yakhṣifāni ʿalayhimā min waraqi al-jannah wa-ʿaṣā ādamu rabbahu fa-ghawā

Ṭā Hā 20:121

“Then they both ate from it, their private parts became apparent to them, and they began joining garden leaves over themselves. Adam disobeyed his Lord and went astray.”

Ṭā Hā 20:121

The same expression occurs:

وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِنْ وَرَقِ الْجَنَّةِ

wa-ṭafiqā yakhṣifāni ʿalayhimā min waraqi al-jannah

Ṭā Hā 20:121

And they began joining garden leaves over themselves.

Its morphology and meaning match Al-Aʿrāf 7:22.

Both passages refer to Adam and his spouse in the dual. Both identify يخصفان as the activity and garden leaves as the covering material.

The differences lie elsewhere in the narratives.

Al-Aʿrāf emphasizes Satan's deception, exposure, and the Lord's subsequent address.

Ṭā Hā relates the event to the previous covenant, Satan's temptation, Adam's disobedience, and the subsequent divine turning and guidance.

These contextual differences do not change the root's meaning.

Both occurrences express beginning and undertaking the activity.

Does the repeated expression give the root a repetitive meaning?

The appearance of وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ in two chapters does not make طفق itself mean repeatedly performing an action.

The two verses narrate one event in two contexts.

The repeated expression supports confidence in its Quranic sense, but does not establish repetition as an inherent meaning of the root.

Repetition of a narrative and repetition encoded in a verb are different matters.

Beginning an action and Adam's responsibility

Ṭā Hā 20:121 ends with Adam's disobedience. Ṭā Hā 20:122 describes his Lord choosing him, turning toward him, and guiding him.

The narrative therefore extends beyond exposure and covering to disobedience and subsequent return.

Repentance, complete remorse, or return to guidance should not be extracted from طفقا alone.

Its role is the beginning of a particular action after exposure. Turning and guidance are expressed through other words and verbs later in the account.

5. فَطَفِقَ in Solomon's account: beginning an action in a different construction

The third and final Quranic occurrence concerns Solomon.

Ṣād 38:33 states:

رُدُّوهَا عَلَيَّ فَطَفِقَ مَسْحًا بِالسُّوقِ وَالْأَعْنَاقِ

ruddūhā ʿalayya fa-ṭafiqa masḥan bi-l-sūqi wa-l-aʿnāq

Ṣād 38:33

“Bring them back to me; then he began passing his hands over their legs and necks.”

Ṣād 38:33

The preceding verses describe fine, swift horses presented to Solomon in the evening.

Here he commands that they be brought back.

The narrative then says:

فَطَفِقَ مَسْحًا بِالسُّوقِ وَالْأَعْنَاقِ

fa-ṭafiqa masḥan bi-l-sūqi wa-l-aʿnāq

Ṣād 38:33

Then he began passing his hands over their legs and necks.

The syntax differs from the first two occurrences.

In Al-Aʿrāf and Ṭā Hā, طفقا is followed by the imperfect يخصفان.

Here فطفق is followed by the verbal noun مَسْحًا.

The role of فَطَفِقَ

فَطَفِقَ consists of فَـ and the perfect طَفِقَ.

فَـ connects the action with the preceding event.

Solomon is the one acting; in the deficient-verb analysis, the subject-like noun of طفق is an implicit pronoun referring to him.

مَسْحًا is the verbal noun of م س ح, here rendered as passing the hands over or wiping.

In a recognized syntactic analysis, the imperfect naming the activity is omitted while its verbal noun remains. The construction can be explained as:

فَطَفِقَ يَمْسَحُ مَسْحًا

That is, “then he began wiping.”

In this analysis, مَسْحًا is a cognate accusative of the omitted verb يَمْسَحُ; that verbal clause supplies the predicate of طفق.

The different surface constructions should not produce two unrelated meanings for طفق.

All three retain beginning and undertaking an action.

Does طفق determine Solomon's precise action?

The verb itself does not say that Solomon stroked, washed, injured, or killed the horses.

The action must be understood from مَسْحًا بِالسُّوقِ وَالْأَعْنَاقِ and the narrative context.

Interpretations of this مسح differ. Some understand passing the hands over the horses' legs and necks; others understand a different action.

Disagreement about مسح is not disagreement about طفق.

In either reading, طفق expresses beginning the activity.

Determining the exact action requires separate examination of م س ح, the construction بِالسُّوقِ وَالْأَعْنَاقِ, the narrative, and other Quranic clues.

A study of ط ف ق should not be constructed to prove a predetermined conclusion about Solomon's conduct.

Beginning is not completion

فطفق in Ṣād 38:33 shows Solomon undertaking an action after the horses are returned.

The verb does not specify how many horses were involved, the duration, or how the action ended.

Such details do not follow from its meaning.

The same principle applies: inception is clear, while full extent and character require other evidence.

6. Comparison with related roots

ط ف ق and ب د أ: beginning and setting about an action

ب د أ concerns beginning and the first stage of an activity.

طفق likewise expresses inception but normally accompanies another verb naming the activity undertaken.

بَدَأَ الْعَمَلَ means “he began the work.”

طَفِقَ يَعْمَلُ means “he began working” or “he set to work.”

The expressions are close in meaning but differ grammatically.

The Quran also uses بدأ for initiating creation. In expressions contrasting initiation with restoration, restoration is expressed by يُعِيدُ, from ع و د; that meaning should not be attributed to بدأ or transferred to طفق without evidence.

In its three Quranic occurrences, طفق initiates another action rather than serving as a general term for every beginning or act of creation.

ط ف ق and أ خ ذ: taking and starting

أ خ ذ primarily concerns taking and obtaining.

أَخَذَ يَفْعَلُ can nevertheless mean beginning to do something.

In that construction it overlaps with طَفِقَ يَفْعَلُ.

Quranic أخذ also has many usages involving taking, receiving, calling to account, and seizing.

These cannot all be reduced to beginning an action.

طفق, by contrast, occurs in the Quran only in the inceptive construction.

Similarity within one construction does not make the entire semantic fields identical.

ط ف ق and ج ع ل: starting and placing

جعل has multiple Arabic meanings and constructions.

Quranic usages include placing, bringing into being, transforming, or establishing a relationship between things.

جَعَلَ يَفْعَلُ can also mean beginning to do something.

In that construction it approaches طفق.

This does not make every Quranic جعل a verb of inception.

Each occurrence must be understood through its construction.

In طفقا يخصفان, طفقا supplies inception and يخصفان the activity. In other constructions, جعل may itself name placing or producing something.

ط ف ق and ش ر ع: inception and opening a way

ش ر ع concerns opening or making a way accessible, entering an activity, and, in other usages, religious law or a path.

شَرَعَ فِي الْعَمَلِ can mean “he began working.”

In this usage it resembles طفق.

ش ر ع also has particular Quranic applications concerning a way and religious law, as in Al-Shūrā 42:13.

Beginning an activity does not exhaust that root's meaning.

Conversely, the three Quranic occurrences of طفق do not directly express legislation or establishing a religious method.

ط ف ق and ك و د: actual inception and nearing an action

كَادَ is discussed alongside syntactically related verbs, but its semantic difference from طفق matters.

كاد can express nearing an event without its actual occurrence.

طفق describes the beginning of an action actually undertaken.

طفقا يخصفان does not mean that Adam and his spouse merely almost placed leaves over themselves; they set about doing it.

Likewise, فطفق مسحا describes Solomon beginning an action rather than merely approaching it.

This is one of the clearest Quranic semantic features of طفق.

ط ف ق and ل ز م: engagement and remaining attached

ل ز م concerns remaining with or attached to something without leaving it.

Dictionaries explain some usages of طفق through لزم.

Someone undertaking an action may remain occupied with it for a time.

Beginning an action and remaining engaged throughout are nevertheless distinct.

Quranic طفق conveys inception and engagement; prolonged or uninterrupted continuation requires another contextual indication.

7. Distinguishing similar-looking roots

ط ف ق resembles several other roots in its letters, but similar spelling or sound should not merge their meanings.

ط ف أ concerns fire going out or being extinguished. Quranic أَطْفَأَهَا concerns the fire of war, and يُطْفِئُوا attempts to extinguish divine light.

This has no established lexical equivalence with beginning an action.

ط ف ف concerns giving short measure or weight in its Quranic usage. الْمُطَفِّفِينَ in Al-Muṭaffifīn 83:1 names those who shortchange.

Shared first radicals do not transfer shortchanging to طفق.

ط ف ل, including طِفْل, concerns a child and childhood and differs from طفق.

The four families should therefore be distinguished in Quranic root studies.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

The principal equivalents are beginning and setting about an activity.

طَفِقَ: he began, set about an action, or became engaged in it.

طَفِقَا: the two began or undertook an action.

طَفِقَ يَفْعَلُ: he began doing that action.

وَطَفِقَا: and the two began.

فَطَفِقَ: then he began or set about it.

طَفَقَ and the imperfect forms يَطْفَقُ and يَطْفِقُ are also recorded in Arabic but do not occur in the Quran.

The following renderings suit the three Quranic occurrences.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:22

وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِنْ وَرَقِ الْجَنَّةِ

wa-ṭafiqā yakhṣifāni ʿalayhimā min waraqi al-jannah

Al-Aʿrāf 7:22

“And they began joining garden leaves over themselves.”

Ṭā Hā 20:121

وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِنْ وَرَقِ الْجَنَّةِ

wa-ṭafiqā yakhṣifāni ʿalayhimā min waraqi al-jannah

Ṭā Hā 20:121

“And they began joining garden leaves over themselves.”

In both, “began” renders طفقا, while joining leaves renders يخصفان and its accompanying words.

Ṣād 38:33

فَطَفِقَ مَسْحًا بِالسُّوقِ وَالْأَعْنَاقِ

fa-ṭafiqa masḥan bi-l-sūqi wa-l-aʿnāq

Ṣād 38:33

“Then he began passing his hands over their legs and necks.”

The Persian equivalent of “set about” naturally renders فطفق here.

It expresses Solomon engaging in an activity without requiring a literal reconstruction of the omitted Arabic verb in Persian.

The basic translation should not replace طفق with “wanted,” “decided,” “was about to,” or “completed”: none alone matches actual inception.

Nor should it invariably become “continued uninterruptedly.” Context may suggest continuation, but the verb itself specifies no precise duration.

“Began” and “set about doing” are natural, defensible Quranic equivalents.

9. Summary

ط ف ق concerns beginning, becoming engaged, and setting about an action.

Dictionaries also record remaining attached and, in particular expressions, attaining an objective. These usages should not enter the Quranic passages without evidence.

The historical evidence examined here does not sufficiently establish an independently dated pre-Islamic poetic witness. The Quran directly attests the verb in the language of revelation, with three semantically consistent occurrences.

In Al-Aʿrāf 7:22 and Ṭā Hā 20:121, Adam and his spouse begin joining garden leaves to cover themselves after their private parts become apparent.

In Ṣād 38:33, Solomon undertakes an action described by مَسْحًا after the horses are returned.

The first two occurrences have طفقا followed by يخصفان. The third has فطفق followed by مسحا; a recognized analysis supplies an omitted يمسح.

All three have the same function: indicating inception and engagement in an activity identified by the accompanying expression.

It resembles بدأ, أخذ, and جعل in certain constructions but differs significantly from كاد. كاد can express only nearing an action, whereas Quranic طفق expresses its actual beginning.

The Quranic definition is beginning and undertaking a particular action, with possible continuation, without the verb alone determining its duration, intensity, repetition, or completion.

This explains all three Quranic occurrences.

طفق contains neither covering nor stroking in its own meaning. It indicates undertaking an activity whose nature is specified by the following verb or phrase.

Understanding it requires distinguishing inception, the nature of the action, and its eventual outcome.

The three passages describe actions beginning, but the root itself does not judge their rightness or wrongness, duration, or completion. Those features depend on the full construction and context.
