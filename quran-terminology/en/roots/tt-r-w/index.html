Qur'anic Root

Qur'anic Root Ṭ R W

Freshness, liveliness, softness, and the preservation of a thing's natural quality before staleness, dryness, or corruption.

Important Forms

Qur'anic formṭariyyan
طَرِيًّا

Fresh; an adjective of laḥm in the phrase laḥman ṭariyyan.
Verbal nounṭarāwah
طَرَاوَة

Freshness, liveliness, softness, and preserved natural quality.
Form IIṭarrā
طَرَّى

To freshen, soften, give freshness, or make something pleasant in texture.

To praise and add in praise; should not be confused with ṭariyy in the description of meat.

Core Meaning

The root ط ر و appears through the word طَرِيًّا.

This material is also treated under ط ر ي in some lexicons. Its final letter is a weak letter, and in derived forms it shifts between waw and ya'. In this study, the form Ṭ R W is preserved according to the accepted morphological classification for the Qur'anic word ṭariyy, without ignoring the difference in root placement.

Ṭ R W points to freshness, liveliness, softness, and the preservation of a thing's natural quality: a state in which the material has not yet been altered by staleness, dryness, withering, or corruption.

In the Qur'an, this root occurs only in the description of meat obtained from water:

لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا

That is, fresh meat whose texture and natural quality are still preserved.

Important Forms

طَرِيّ is an adjective meaning fresh, lively, newly emerged, and possessing natural softness and moisture. This is the only Qur'anic form of the root.

طَرِيَّة is the feminine form of ṭariyy.

طَرَاوَة refers to freshness, liveliness, softness, and preserved natural quality.

طَرَاءَة is another verbal-noun form in the same field.

طَرُوَ and طَرِيَ are found in lexical sources for something becoming fresh, soft, or lively.

طَرَّى in form II means to make something fresh or soft and to give it freshness. In food usage, it may indicate making food soft and pleasant in condition.

أَطْرَى means to praise and to add in praise of someone. This branch does not occur in the Qur'an, and the precise historical relation between it and ṭariyy in the sense of freshness requires caution.

مُطَرًّى describes something softened, prepared, or made pleasant in condition through additives or treatment.

From this root, the Qur'an uses only the masculine singular accusative form طَرِيًّا.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of Ṭ R W is not merely “newness.” Ṭariyy also expresses a sensory and material quality.

Something that is ṭariyy:

has not passed through much time since its emergence, harvesting, or separation;

has preserved its natural quality;

has not yet changed through staleness;

has a lively and flexible texture;

does not show signs of withering, corruption, or wearing out.

Therefore ṭariyy should not be translated only as “soft.” Not everything soft is fresh. It should also not be understood only as “moist,” because something may be moist but stale or unsound.

In Qur'anic usage, “fresh” is the central translation. Softness, liveliness, and preserved natural moisture are accompanying features.

Material Image

The clearest material image is the flesh of an aquatic creature newly obtained from the water. Its texture still preserves its natural state and has not changed through staleness, long storage, or corruption.

This freshness is not a stable and permanent state. Freshness is a quality that remains for a time after living material is separated from its environment, and then it may diminish.

For this reason, ṭariyy may show not only present freshness, but also nearness to an unspoiled beginning: the material has not yet moved far from its first quality.

Another image is a fresh leaf or branch. A ṭariyy leaf still has its natural water and flexibility; after time, it may wither, dry, or darken.

Fresh fruit is similar. Its freshness appears in its scent, juice, texture, and natural state.

Still, the way freshness is lost is not the same in every material:

a plant may dry and wither;

meat may become stale, altered, or corrupt;

fruit may soften, darken, sour, or rot.

Therefore the opposite of ṭariyy must be determined according to the type of thing and its context, not through one fixed word.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, al-ṭariyy is explained through freshness, liveliness, and natural softness. Lexicons bring it close to al-ghaḍḍ, meaning something still fresh, newly emerged, and unchanged.

About meat, one could say:

طَرُوَ اللَّحْمُ

Or:

طَرِيَ اللَّحْمُ

meaning that the meat was fresh and in good condition.

The verbal nouns ṭarāwah and ṭarā'ah express the same quality.

Ṭariyy was not used only for food. A plant, fruit, branch, fabric, or material that still preserved its natural softness and liveliness could also be described in this field.

The form طَرَّى الطعام was used for softening, preparing, or making food pleasant in condition.

أطرى فلاناً also means to add in praising someone. There may be an imaginable closeness between beautifying and softening speech and making a material soft and pleasant, but one should not turn this resemblance into a certain historical derivation.

To say that praise “softens” or “freshens” reality is a possible rhetorical explanation, but unless there is clear historical evidence, it should not be reported as the definite origin of iṭrā'.

Qur'anic Usage

Fresh Meat from the Sea

وَهُوَ الَّذِي سَخَّرَ الْبَحْرَ لِتَأْكُلُوا مِنْهُ لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا وَتَسْتَخْرِجُوا مِنْهُ حِلْيَةً تَلْبَسُونَهَا

And He is the One who made the sea serviceable, so that you may eat from it fresh meat and extract from it adornment that you wear.

Al-Nahl 16:14

In this verse, طَرِيًّا is the adjective of لَحْمًا.

The word expresses the edible quality of the meat at the time of benefit: meat that is fresh and has not yet changed through staleness.

“Soft meat” is not enough as a translation, because softness is only part of the word's field. “Fresh meat” is closer to the verse's usage.

In the same verse, the sea provides two different types of benefit:

لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا

fresh meat

and:

حِلْيَةً تَلْبَسُونَهَا

adornment that you wear.

This pairing creates a clear difference between two types of material:

meat is for eating, and its freshness matters for the quality of consumption;

adornment is for wearing and keeping, and its function is not tied to immediate consumption.

Still, this contrast should not be expanded beyond the text. The verse does not say that adornment is the “hardest” or “most lasting” material, and ṭariyy does not lexically mean “certainly quick to spoil.”

The reliable point is that freshness is a meaningful quality for food that changes quickly, while adornment is presented with a different and more lasting function. From one environment, the verse brings out both fresh food and ornamental material.

Fresh Meat from Two Different Bodies of Water

وَمَا يَسْتَوِي الْبَحْرَانِ هَٰذَا عَذْبٌ فُرَاتٌ سَائِغٌ شَرَابُهُ وَهَٰذَا مِلْحٌ أُجَاجٌ وَمِنْ كُلٍّ تَأْكُلُونَ لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا وَتَسْتَخْرِجُونَ حِلْيَةً تَلْبَسُونَهَا

The two bodies of water are not equal: this one is sweet, fresh, and pleasant to drink, and that one is salty and bitter; and from each you eat fresh meat and extract adornment that you wear.

Fatir 35:12

In this verse too, the phrase لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا appears.

The verse distinguishes between fresh water and salty water, but it also shows that both can be sources of fresh food:

وَمِنْ كُلٍّ تَأْكُلُونَ لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا

And from each you eat fresh meat.

Ṭariyy describes the meat, not the water. The sweetness or salinity of the water does not change the meaning of the meat's freshness.

In this verse too, fresh meat appears beside adornment. This repetition shows that the Qur'an distinguishes between two kinds of benefit:

an edible benefit in which freshness is prominent;

an ornamental benefit in which extraction and wearing are mentioned.

This contrast brings the meaning of ṭariyy out of abstraction. Freshness here is a real and perceptible quality of an edible material.

Freshness and Time-Limited Quality

Ṭariyy in these two verses does not mean “in the process of spoiling.” Nor does the word directly say that the meat must be consumed immediately.

But freshness is a concept that gains meaning only in relation to the passage of time. Something is called fresh because it has not yet lost its initial quality.

Therefore, two elements are present in ṭariyy:

the natural quality that is still present;

the possibility that this quality may be lost with time.

The second element is understood from the concept of freshness, not because the word by itself indicates quick corruption or a specific duration.

In meat, loss of freshness may lead to staleness, change of smell, change of texture, or corruption. In plants, it may lead to withering or dryness.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

Temporal freshness: Ṭariyy may describe something for which not much time has passed since harvesting, hunting, or preparation. In laḥman ṭariyyan, this dimension is essential: the meat is not stale.

Preservation of natural quality: Freshness does not point only to time. The material still preserves its first quality. This quality may include smell, texture, moisture, flexibility, and soundness.

Softness: Softness is one common feature of something ṭariyy, but it is not the whole meaning. Fresh meat may be soft, but meat softened through corruption cannot be called ṭariyy in that same sense.

Moisture: Natural moisture is related to freshness, but not everything moist is fresh. Something may be moist, stale, or corrupt.

Plant liveliness: In plants, freshness is more often opposed to withering and dryness.

Meat freshness: In meat, freshness is more often opposed to staleness, change of smell, change of texture, and corruption. Therefore the opposite of ṭariyy should not always be limited to yābis, dry.

Praise and iṭrā': أطرى in lexical usage means to praise and add in praise. This branch is not the same as laḥman ṭariyyan.

There may be a kind of making pleasant or agreeable in both, but this is an imaginal analysis, not the Qur'anic meaning or a firmly established historical derivation.

Grammar and Structure

لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا

In both verses, لَحْمًا is the object of the verb of eating and is accusative.

طَرِيًّا is an adjective of laḥm and agrees with it in gender, number, definiteness, and grammatical state:

لَحْمًا: singular, masculine, indefinite, accusative

طَرِيًّا: singular, masculine, indefinite, accusative

The natural English structure is:

fresh meat.

Ṭariyy is not an independent circumstantial phrase; it describes the quality of the meat itself.

لِتَأْكُلُوا مِنْهُ

In Al-Nahl 16:14, the lam in li-ta'kulū expresses the purpose or result of making the sea serviceable:

so that you may eat from it.

Minhu shows the source of the meat, and the pronoun returns to al-baḥr.

وَمِنْ كُلٍّ تَأْكُلُونَ

In Fatir 35:12, min kull returns to each of the two bodies of water:

and from each you eat.

Placing min kull before the verb highlights the shared capacity of both environments to provide this food.

The Pairing of Meat and Adornment

In both verses, a similar structure is repeated:

لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا

حِلْيَةً تَلْبَسُونَهَا

Laḥm is described with the adjective ṭariyy because the quality of food is in view.

Ḥilyah is described with the clause talbasūnahā because its ornamental and wearable function is in view.

Therefore the main contrast is not simply “soft versus hard.” It is two kinds of material with two kinds of benefit and two kinds of description:

food in terms of its quality for consumption;

adornment in terms of its function of being worn.

Nearby Roots

Ṭ R W and J D D (jadd): J D D points to being new, becoming renewed, and arising in contrast to oldness.

Ṭariyy, beyond temporal newness, also expresses the sensory and natural quality of the material.

Everything ṭariyy is fresh, but not everything new is ṭariyy.

Ṭ R W and Gh Ḍ Ḍ (ghaḍḍ): Gh Ḍ Ḍ is one of the closest fields to ṭariyy and points to freshness, liveliness, and being untouched or newly emerged.

Ghaḍḍ may emphasize youth, new emergence, and delicacy more strongly. Ṭariyy in food usage more clearly shows freshness and the natural quality of texture.

This distinction is not absolute, and lexicons sometimes explain ṭariyy through ghaḍḍ.

Ṭ R W and R Ṭ B (raṭb): R Ṭ B points to moisture and juiciness.

Ṭariyy may be moist, but moisture by itself does not guarantee freshness. Something may be moist but stale or corrupt.

Ṭ R W and L Y N (layn): L Y N points to softness and flexibility in contrast to harshness and hardness.

Ṭariyy is often soft, but softness is not its whole meaning. Something may be soft without being fresh.

Ṭ R W and N Ḍ R (naḍr): N Ḍ R points to freshness, visible liveliness, and brightness of appearance.

Ṭariyy more often expresses the fresh and sound quality of texture; naḍārah is more often seen through appearance and color.

Ṭ R W and Y B S (yabs): Y B S points to dryness and loss of moisture.

In plants, ṭariyy may stand opposite yābis. But in the context of meat, yābis is not the complete opposite, because meat may lose freshness through staleness or corruption without becoming dry.

Ṭ R W and F S D (fasād): Fasād points to the loss of soundness, order, or natural quality.

Ṭariyy in the use of meat is connected with remaining sound and fresh, but fasād is not its exact opposite either. Something may no longer be ṭariyy while not yet having reached full corruption.

Ṭ R W and N T N (natan): N T N points to becoming foul-smelling or having an unpleasant odor.

In meat, loss of freshness may lead to natan, but ṭariyy does not directly mean “odorless,” and natan is only one sign of corruption.

Ṭ R W and Q D D (qadad): Q D D in food usage may relate to cutting meat and preparing it in strips; qadīd is usually cut and dried meat.

Qadīd is not necessarily corrupt or impure; it may be a method of preserving meat. Therefore, in contrast to ṭariyy, the main difference is between fresh meat and processed or dried meat, not between sound and corrupt.

Ṭ R W and Kh B Th (khabath): Kh B Th points to impurity, badness, or undesirability.

Meat that is not ṭariyy is not necessarily khabīth. Dried or preserved meat may not be fresh, but it may still be sound and edible.

So “khabīth” should not be treated as the direct lexical opposite of ṭariyy.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For طَرِيّ: fresh; lively; fresh and pleasant in condition; having preserved natural quality; soft and fresh, in suitable explanation.

For طَرَاوَة: freshness; liveliness; freshness of condition; preservation of natural quality.

For طَرَاءَة: freshness; liveliness; fresh state.

For طَرَّى: made fresh; softened; gave freshness; made pleasant in condition.

For أَطْرَى: praised; added in praise; described with abundant praise.

This form does not occur in the Qur'an and should not be confused with ṭariyy in the description of meat.

لِتَأْكُلُوا مِنْهُ لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا

So that you may eat from it fresh meat.

وَمِنْ كُلٍّ تَأْكُلُونَ لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا

And from both you eat fresh meat.

“Soft meat” is not enough, because it removes the central element of freshness.

“Fresh and soft meat” reveals more of the sensory meaning, but it is more explanatory for the main translation.

For Forqan, “fresh meat” is the best direct equivalent, while liveliness, softness, and preservation of natural quality can be explained in the linguistic note.

Summary

Ṭ R W points to freshness, liveliness, and preservation of a thing's natural quality. In living and edible materials, this quality is often joined to softness, natural moisture, and not having changed through staleness.

In the Qur'an, this root occurs only in the phrase لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا. Ṭariyy in this context is fresh meat that has not yet lost its natural quality.

The pairing of fresh meat with adornment shows that one environment can provide two different kinds of benefit: food whose freshness matters for consumption, and adornment extracted for wearing. This contrast is between two functions and two types of material, not simply between softness and hardness.

In plants, ṭariyy may stand against dryness and withering; but in meat, it is better understood against staleness and alteration of quality. The distinctive feature of this root is the joining of temporal freshness, material liveliness, and preservation of natural condition.
