Short Introduction to the Root

The root ق و ي moves in the semantic field of force, ability, power, and firmness. Its core is possessing the capacity needed to act, endure, resist, or have an effect.

This strength does not have one fixed form in the Qur’an. Sometimes the discussion is about the bodily or practical ability of a human being, sometimes about the collective power and resources of a people, sometimes about the firmness of a structure, and sometimes about the power of The God.

From the beginning, a distinction must be made among the main forms of this root:

قُوَّة names force and ability itself.

قَوِيّ describes someone or something that possesses strength.

الْقَوِيّ is an explicit description of The God in the Qur’an.

مُقْوِين is a special derivative whose meaning cannot simply be returned from “strength” to “the strong ones”; this form has its own path of usage in Arabic.

Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

This root is seen in the Qur’an in three main forms:

قُوَّة

Force, ability, power, or firmness, according to the context.

قَوِيّ

Strong, able, possessing power.

مُقْوِين

Plural active participle from Form IV; it appears in al-Waqiah 56:73 about those for whom fire is a benefit, and in lexical usage it has been connected with desert-dwellers and travelers through open desert land.

The Qur’anic family of this root shows that “strength” can be both a capacity and the description of the one who possesses that capacity.

The Semantic Core of the Root

The defensible core of ق و ي can be stated as follows:

Possessing sufficient force and ability to act, endure, resist, or have an effect.

This definition clarifies several points.

Strength does not necessarily mean violence. Someone may be strong because he can perform a task, because he resists pressure, or because he has enough resources for action.

Strength is not necessarily an ethical virtue either. In the Qur’an, a trustworthy person is called قوي, and an ifrit from the jinn also describes himself as قوي. Ethical value comes from the context and accompanying descriptions.

Likewise, “firmness” is one manifestation of strength, not its meaning in every case.

The Material Image of the Root

The simplest image of this root is a body or structure that has the ability to carry, hold, stand firm, or perform work.

An arm that lifts a burden, a strand that becomes firm after being twisted, a people that has the ability to confront, or a person who can carry out an assignment all stand within the field of this root.

From this tangible experience, language can also use strength for social, practical, and recognized capacities. But this material image is only for explanation; strength in the Qur’an is not limited to muscle or war.

Use in Arabic

In Arabic, قَوِيَ points to becoming or being possessed of strength, and قُوَّة names that ability.

Strength can be used about the body, opinion, a group, a tool, a structure, or the capacity to perform work. Thus the field of the word has had room from the beginning for various uses.

In Qur’anic study, the base meaning should be preserved and the exact type of strength should be taken from the context of each verse.

“Take ... With Strength”: Taking With Ability and Firmness

خُذُوا مَا آتَيْنَاكُم بِقُوَّةٍ

“Take what We have given you with strength.”

Al-Baqarah 2:63

This expression appears in the context of the covenant. قوة here is not merely muscular force; the subject is taking and holding what was given to them.

In natural translation one may say:

“Take what We have given you with firmness and full ability.”

But translation and explanation must be distinguished. The base meaning of قوة is force and ability; “firmness” and “resolve” are natural results of this word in this context.

The Strength of Human Beings and Societies

كَانُوا أَشَدَّ مِنكُمْ قُوَّةً وَأَكْثَرَ أَمْوَالًا وَأَوْلَادًا

“They were stronger than you in force and greater in wealth and children.”

At-Tawbah 9:69

In this verse, قوة comes beside wealth and children. This pairing shows that the power of a people may be understood in a field broader than bodily strength.

But wealth and children are not themselves the meaning of قوة; they are three separate elements in the sentence.

Increase in Strength

وَيَزِدْكُمْ قُوَّةً إِلَىٰ قُوَّتِكُمْ

“And He will add strength to your strength.”

Hud 11:52

This structure shows that strength can increase and is gradable.

The exact type of this increase should not be restricted without evidence to body, wealth, population, or political power. The verse speaks of an increase in ability, and the wider context clarifies its bounds.

Lut’s Wish for Strength

قَالَ لَوْ أَنَّ لِي بِكُمْ قُوَّةً أَوْ آوِي إِلَىٰ رُكْنٍ شَدِيدٍ

“He said: If only I had strength against you, or could take refuge in a firm support.”

Hud 11:80

قوة here is the ability to confront and act.

The following expression, رُكْنٍ شَدِيدٍ, states a firm support. The pairing of the two shows that قوة and شدة are not complete synonyms.

Strength focuses on ability; severity or firmness may highlight the solidity or hardness of a state.

The Firmness of a Thread

وَلَا تَكُونُوا كَالَّتِي نَقَضَتْ غَزْلَهَا مِن بَعْدِ قُوَّةٍ أَنكَاثًا

“Do not be like the woman who undid her spun thread after firmness, into loose strands.”

An-Nahl 16:92

In this verse, the subject of strength is a twisted thread. Therefore, “firmness” is a natural translation.

This is an important witness showing that قوة can also be used about the firm and resistant state of a structure.

But this use should not make “firmness” the fixed equivalent of every occurrence of strength.

“There Is No Strength Except By The God”

مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

“What The God wills; there is no strength except by The God.”

Al-Kahf 18:39

The structure لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ negates the independence of strength.

This verse does not say that human beings have no real ability at all; the Qur’an elsewhere speaks explicitly of the strength of human beings and peoples. The issue in this context is human independence and self-sufficiency before the source of ability.

So a distinction must be made between two things:

Having strength

and

Being independent in strength

The verse breaks the second, not the existence of every kind of ability in created beings.

Preparedness With Whatever Strength Is Possible

وَأَعِدُّوا لَهُم مَّا اسْتَطَعْتُم مِّن قُوَّةٍ

“Prepare against them whatever you are able of strength.”

Al-Anfal 8:60

قوة here is something that can be prepared for defense and confrontation.

The continuation of the verse mentions one example of preparedness, but the word strength should not be limited only to that example.

The core of the expression is “whatever ability is available to you.”

Strong and Trustworthy in a Human Being

إِنَّ خَيْرَ مَنِ اسْتَأْجَرْتَ الْقَوِيُّ الْأَمِينُ

“The best one you can hire is the strong, the trustworthy.”

Al-Qasas 28:26

In this verse, القوي is used about a human being considered suitable for work.

The pairing of القوي and الأمين shows:

قوي: ability to perform the work.

أمين: being trustworthy.

These two features are independent. Strength alone does not guarantee trustworthiness, justice, or moral righteousness.

Strong and Trustworthy in the Speech of the Ifrit

وَإِنِّي عَلَيْهِ لَقَوِيٌّ أَمِينٌ

“And I am surely strong and trustworthy for it.”

An-Naml 27:39

In this verse too, قوي expresses the ability to carry out the assignment, and أمين expresses trustworthiness.

This witness confirms that the root ق و ي itself has no fixed ethical value.

“Possessor of Strength” in Description of the Messenger

إِنَّهُ لَقَوْلُ رَسُولٍ كَرِيمٍ ۝ ذِي قُوَّةٍ عِندَ ذِي الْعَرْشِ مَكِينٍ

“Indeed, it is the word of a noble messenger; possessor of strength, established in place with the Owner of the Throne.”

At-Takwir 81:19–20

Here ذِي قُوَّةٍ describes a messenger whom the verse also calls كريم and مكين.

The verse itself does not name that messenger at this point. Therefore, the root article should not insert a name from outside the text into the meaning of the verse.

ذي قوة means “possessor of strength / possessing ability.” What exact kind of ability this is is examined from the continuation of the context and independent study of the surah; the construction alone does not determine all details.

Stages of Human Weakness and Strength

اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَكُم مِّن ضَعْفٍ ثُمَّ جَعَلَ مِن بَعْدِ ضَعْفٍ قُوَّةً ثُمَّ جَعَلَ مِن بَعْدِ قُوَّةٍ ضَعْفًا وَشَيْبَةً

“The God is the One who created you from weakness, then after weakness made strength, then after strength made weakness and old age.”

Ar-Rum 30:54

In this verse, weakness and strength stand directly opposite one another.

Human strength is a stage and is not permanent; the human being reaches strength from weakness and then returns again to weakness.

This contrast is one of the clearest witnesses for the core of the root: قوة is the state of possessing ability opposite ضعف.

Derivatives Connected to Divine Names or Descriptions

الْقَوِيّ

الْقَوِيّ is an explicit description of The God in the Qur’an.

إِنَّ رَبَّكَ هُوَ الْقَوِيُّ الْعَزِيزُ

“Indeed, your Lord, He is The Strong, The Mighty.”

Hud 11:66

And:

وَهُوَ الْقَوِيُّ الْعَزِيزُ

“And He is The Strong, The Mighty.”

In several verses the indefinite descriptive form also appears:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَقَوِيٌّ عَزِيزٌ

“Indeed, The God is strong and mighty.”

In divine use, the core of “ability and power” remains, but it should not be reduced to bodily force, tools, matter, or physical energy. These limitations come from the concept of created strength, not from the root itself.

Nor should conclusions such as “infinity” or “absolute intensity” be extracted merely from the pattern فعيل. What is direct is the Qur’anic description قوي and the contexts connected with His power.

The Pairing of قَوِيّ With عَزِيز

In many divine uses, قوي appears together with عزيز.

قوي denotes ability and power.

عزيز belongs to another semantic field connected with might, predominance, and being inaccessible.

The two are companions in expression, not complete synonyms. The meaning of عزيز should not be inserted into the definition of قوي.

قَوِيّ and شَدِيدُ الْعِقَاب

إِنَّ اللَّهَ قَوِيٌّ شَدِيدُ الْعِقَابِ

“Indeed, The God is strong, severe in punishment.”

Al-Anfal 8:52

Here قوي expresses power, and شديد العقاب expresses the quality of punishment.

So “strong” itself does not mean “severe in punishment.”

مُقْوِين: A Special Branch of This Root

نَحْنُ جَعَلْنَاهَا تَذْكِرَةً وَمَتَاعًا لِّلْمُقْوِينَ

“We made it a reminder and a benefit for the muqwin.”

Al-Waqiah 56:73

مُقْوِين is, morphologically, the plural active participle from Form IV and from this root, but its meaning is not “the strong ones.”

In lexical usage, أَقْوَى is connected with entering قواء, meaning empty and desert land. From this angle, مقوين has been applied to desert-dwellers, travelers of the desert, or those who benefit from fire in such an environment.

This derivative is an important example showing that a root connection does not always mean preserving one simple equivalent in all derivatives.

To explain this relationship, one should not assume that مقوي necessarily means someone whose strength has been depleted or someone named through the removal of strength; such an explanation is not made certain by the word structure itself.

For natural translation, one may use “desert travelers,” “those traveling through open desert,” or a close expression, while giving the lexical explanation separately.

Comparison With Nearby Roots

ق و ي Compared With ض ع ف

This contrast is direct in ar-Rum 30:54.

قوة: possessing ability.

ضعف: decrease or absence of sufficient ability.

In that verse, these two express different stages of the human condition.

ق و ي Compared With و ه ن

فَمَا وَهَنُوا لِمَا أَصَابَهُمْ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَمَا ضَعُفُوا وَمَا اسْتَكَانُوا

Al Imran 3:146

This verse places wahn, weakness, and istikanah together and shows that they are not complete synonyms.

وهن tends toward slackening and the reduction of firmness.

ضعف toward inability or reduced ability.

استکانت toward collapsing, surrendering, or falling into a state of submissive helplessness.

But one should not build from a single verse an absolute rule such as: wahn is always only “psychological” and weakness always only “bodily.” The field of these roots must be examined separately from all their uses.

ق و ي Compared With ش د د

قوة focuses more on having ability.

شدة focuses more on intensity, hardness, or firmness of state.

In قوة and ركن شديد, both appear in one verse and show this difference.

ق و ي Compared With ع ز ز

In divine description, القوي العزيز appear beside one another many times.

قوي: possessing power and ability.

عزيز: possessing might and predominance.

Their pairing should not lead to merging the two roots.

ق و ي Compared With ق د ر

ق د ر has a broad field that includes measure, determination, and ability.

ق و ي highlights force and ability itself more directly.

This is a difference in tendency and should not be turned into an absolute boundary without an independent study of the root ق د ر.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

قُوَّة

قوة is a noun and can appear in different grammatical structures:

أَشَدَّ مِنكُمْ قُوَّةً

Here قوة specifies the field of comparison: “stronger than you in force.”

قَوِيّ

قوي is an adjective:

الْقَوِيُّ الْأَمِينُ

Two independent descriptions for one person.

And:

هُوَ الْقَوِيُّ الْعَزِيزُ

Two independent descriptions for The God.

From the pairing of two descriptions, one should not insert the meaning of one into the other.

بِقُوَّةٍ

In:

خُذُوا مَا آتَيْنَاكُم بِقُوَّةٍ

The letter باء specifies the manner of the action “take.” “With strength” in this context comes close to taking seriously, firmly, and with full ability.

ذِي قُوَّةٍ

In:

ذِي قُوَّةٍ

ذي expresses possession, and قوة is something that the messenger possesses.

Therefore, “strong” is the natural resulting translation, but the more precise literal structure is “possessor of strength.”

Challenges of Understanding and Translation

First, قوة should not always be translated as “force” in the sense of coercive force. In some languages, this can carry a tone of compulsion and violence, while قوة has a broader range of ability and power.

Second, قوي should not automatically become “righteous,” “suitable,” or “trustworthy.” Ethics comes from other descriptions and the context.

Third, الْقَوِيّ in description of The God should not be physicalized. The root preserves ability and power, but it does not turn divine being and divine power into a material mechanism.

Fourth, خُذُوا ... بِقُوَّةٍ should not be translated in a way that makes “resolve” or “commitment” the dictionary meaning of strength itself. These are contextual explanations.

Fifth, مُقْوِين should not be translated “the strong ones.” This derivative has a special branch of usage and must be understood separately.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For قُوَّة:

“strength,” “ability,” “power.”

“Firmness” in contexts such as an-Nahl 16:92.

For قَوِيّ about a human being:

“strong,” “able.”

For الْقَوِيّ in description of The God:

“The Strong” or “The Able.”

For:

خُذُوا مَا آتَيْنَاكُم بِقُوَّةٍ

“Take what We have given you with firmness and full ability.”

For:

لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

“There is no strength except by The God.”

For:

ذِي قُوَّةٍ

“possessor of strength,” or in more fluent English “strong.”

For:

الْقَوِيُّ الْأَمِينُ

“strong and trustworthy.”

For مُقْوِين:

“desert travelers” or “travelers through the desert,” with the lexical explanation kept separate.

Short Root Meaning for Forqan

Short root meaning:

force, ability, power, firmness.

“Firmness” is better understood as one manifestation of strength, not as the fixed meaning of every use.

Conclusion

The root ق و ي moves around the axis of possessing force and ability for action, endurance, resistance, or effect.

قُوَّة names strength itself, and قَوِيّ describes the possessor of that strength.

In human beings and societies, strength can increase, decrease, stand opposite weakness, appear in work or defense, or come close to firmness in a structure such as a twisted thread.

The Qur’an also uses الْقَوِيّ as an explicit description of The God. In this use, the field of power and ability remains, but the limitations of created strength are not transferred to the divine description.

The form مُقْوِين shows that derivatives of a root are not always understood through a mechanical translation of its core; this word in lexical usage relates to desert-dwellers and travelers through the desert.

The final definition of this root can be stated as follows:

ق و ي expresses force and ability that make action, endurance, resistance, or effect possible. The kind of this strength — bodily, practical, social, structural, or divine — becomes clear from the subject, structure, and context of the verse.
