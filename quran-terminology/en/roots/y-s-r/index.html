Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root Y S R

Ease, opening, availability, smoothness of action, capacity and access; and in the branch of maysir, using ease or chance to gain benefit

Important Forms from This Root

ي س ر (y s r)

The root field; ease, opening, availability, and smooth movement of action.

يَسُرَ / يَيْسُرُ (yasura / yaysuru)

Became easy; became accessible; became free of difficulty.

يَسَّرَ / يُيَسِّرُ (yassara / yuyassiru)

Made easy; made available; smoothed the way.

يُسْر / يَسِير (yusr / yasīr)

Ease and opening; easy, light, small, or low in difficulty.

مَيْسَرَة / يَسَار (maysarah / yasār)

Opening and capacity; financial breadth and ability.

مَيْسِر (maysir)

Gambling; chance-based play; profit-seeking through luck and win-loss structures.

Central Meaning

The root Y S R is a broad and foundational Arabic root. Its central meaning returns to ease, opening, availability, smoothness of action, and the absence of hardship and constriction.

This root is not negative in itself. On the contrary, in many uses it carries a merciful, positive, and humane meaning. يُسْر (yusr) stands opposite عُسْر (ʿusr): yusr means ease and opening; ʿusr means hardship, tightness, and difficulty.

So the general meaning of yusr must be distinguished from the specific use of مَيْسِر (maysir). Maysir is one branch of this root, not the whole meaning of the root.

The semantic core may be summarized like this: something is not difficult, closed, or far from access; rather, it is smooth, doable, accessible, or joined to opening.

Yusr and ʿUsr

One of the clearest contrasts of this root is the contrast between yusr and ʿusr. ʿUsr means hardship, constriction, pressure, and difficulty. Yusr means the path opens, the burden becomes lighter, and the matter becomes bearable or doable.

When the Qur’an says فَإِنَّ مَعَ الْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا (fainna maʿa al-ʿusri yusranā), it is not speaking about gambling or easy profit. It is speaking about opening alongside hardship. In the structure of life, hardship is not sealed and absolute; a way of ease, possibility, and exit is also placed with it. This wording appears in 94:5.

In this use, yusr is a hopeful and merciful concept.

Yusr and Obligation

The Qur’an says: يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ بِكُمُ الْيُسْرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ الْعُسْرَ (yurīdu allāh bikumu al-yusra walā yurīdu bikumu al-ʿusra). This use in 2:185 shows that, in Qur’anic reasoning, religion and obligation do not come to break the human being. The aim is to open the path of growth, not to impose meaningless pressure.

Here yusr means responsible ease, not unrestricted looseness. It is a path in which the human being can move, be repaired, and not be forced beyond capacity.

So yusr in the Qur’an does not always mean comfort-seeking. It means a path aligned with capacity, ability, and divine mercy.

Taysīr toward Yusrā and ʿUsrā

One of the most subtle uses of this root appears in Surat al-Layl: فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُ لِلْيُسْرَىٰ (fasanuyassiruhu lilyusraā) and also فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُ لِلْعُسْرَىٰ (fasanuyassiruhu lilʿusraā). These appear in 92:7 and 92:10.

These two expressions show that taysīr does not always mean positive reward. Taysīr means making a path smooth and removing friction. If the human being moves in the path of giving, God-consciousness, and affirming truth, his way toward opening is smoothed. But if he is stingy, sees himself as self-sufficient, and denies truth, his way toward difficulty is also smoothed.

So taysīr means preparation and smooth movement of a path, but its value depends on the destination. The path may lead to يُسْرَىٰ (yusraā), or it may lead to عُسْرَىٰ (ʿusraā).

Yusr and Maysarah

The word مَيْسَرَة (maysarah) points to opening and ability. In the context of debt, when the Qur’an says to wait until maysarah if the debtor is in hardship, it means until opening and ability to pay become available.

In 2:280, it says: فَنَظِرَةٌ إِلَىٰ مَيْسَرَةٍ (fanaẓirah ilā maysarah). This use is very important for economic ethics. In Qur’anic reasoning, when a person is in ʿusr, pressure must not be increased upon him. He must be given time until maysarah, until opening and capacity return.

Here yusr is connected to mercy, breathing room, and rebuilding human capacity.

Yusr and Yasīr

The word يَسِير (yasīr) points to something easy, light, small, or low in difficulty. Depending on context, it may mean easy or little. This shows that the root Y S R is also connected with lightening a burden and reducing difficulty.

In this branch, yusr is not only a large opening. Sometimes it points to lightness, smallness, or ease of a matter.

Yusr and Yasār

In some Arabic usage, the idea of wealth and financial breadth also arises from this root. A person in يَسَار (yasār) is in financial ease; his hand is more open, and he has capacity to act or pay.

Yasār also means the left side. Regarding its exact relation to ease, one may say cautiously that in some analyses the left side or left hand is seen as a helper and companion of the right hand in performing work. But this point must not alter the primary meaning of the root. The root remains ease, opening, and smoothness of action.

Maysir as a Specific Branch

Now we can come to مَيْسِر (maysir). Maysir comes from this same root, but it has developed a specific meaning. In Qur’anic usage, maysir points to gambling and chance-based games. The traditional translation “gambling” is correct and should be respected for its historical referent.

But if we look from the root Y S R, maysir is not only one specific game. It is connected with seeking benefit through ease, chance, drawing lots, or unequal distribution. A person wants to obtain wealth without proportionate work, without producing value, or through win-loss structures.

This broader reading does not replace the traditional reading; it is its analytical layer. The traditional reading says maysir means gambling. The structural reading asks: what pattern does gambling have? The answer: easy, chance-based, emotionally charged profit that depends on another person’s loss.

The Qur’an mentions wine and maysir in 2:219: يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْخَمْرِ وَالْمَيْسِرِ (yasalūnaka ʿani al-khamri wal-maysiri).

Historical Image of Maysir

In pre-Islamic Arabia, maysir referred to gambling-like games and drawing lots over shares. One example involved dividing camel meat by using marked sticks or arrows. This made division easy and quick from one angle, but the result depended on chance and lots.

The winner could receive a share, while the loser could carry a heavy cost. In such a structure, much wealth may move, but no new value is produced. One side’s gain comes from another side’s loss or deprivation.

This is what separates maysir from sound trade.

Maysir and Trade

Sound trade also contains risk, but in trade the risk is tied to work, goods, service, transfer of value, and meeting need. A trader may profit or lose, but his activity can create real benefit for society.

In maysir, the risk is more gambling-like. The aim is not production or service; the aim is winning through chance, volatility, or another person’s loss.

The core difference is this: trade accepts risk for production or transfer of value; maysir uses risk to win through chance, volatility, or the loss of others.

Maysir and Ribā

Maysir is not the same as ribā, but both can separate from sound economics. In ribā, increase is often tied to pressure, debt, or guaranteed gain. In maysir, profit comes from chance, volatility, game, or win-loss structure.

So ribā is closer to guaranteed increase and pressure upon need. Maysir is closer to chance-based, emotionally charged, and imbalanced profit.

Both can distance the human being from real work, justice, and economic responsibility.

Maysir in Today’s World

If we take only the narrow reading, maysir is the casino and betting. This reading is correct and should not be abandoned. But if we also see the structural layer, some modern phenomena can be analyzed more clearly.

Any activity in which the primary gain comes from chance, volatility, collective excitement, unequal information, or another side’s loss, without producing real value, approaches the logic of maysir.

But caution is necessary. Not every risky investment is maysir. Not every purchase and sale of an asset is maysir. The criterion is whether the activity is connected to production, service, real ownership, or healthy circulation of value, or whether it is merely a game of volatility and chance.

In 5:90, maysir appears with wine and other corrupting practices, and avoidance is commanded. The issue is not only the game itself, but its effect on reason, justice, human relation, and social health.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

يُسْر (yusr): ease; opening; spaciousness in contrast to hardship.

عُسْر (ʿusr): hardship; tightness; difficulty.

يَسَّرَ (yassara): made easy; made available; smoothed the way.

يَسِير (yasīr): easy; light; little; low in difficulty.

مَيْسَرَة (maysarah): opening; capacity; financial ease and ability to pay.

يَسَار (yasār): wealth; financial breadth; open-handed capacity; left side.

مَيْسِر (maysir): gambling; chance-based play; profit-seeking through luck and win-loss; in a broader analysis, seeking gain disproportionate to work and production.

يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ بِكُمُ الْيُسْرَ (yurīdu allāh bikumu al-yusra): God wants ease and opening for you.

فَإِنَّ مَعَ الْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا (fainna maʿa al-ʿusri yusranā): so with hardship there is ease and opening.

فَنَظِرَةٌ إِلَىٰ مَيْسَرَةٍ (fanaẓirah ilā maysarah): then a delay until opening and ability to pay.

فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُ لِلْيُسْرَىٰ (fasanuyassiruhu lilyusraā): so We will smooth his way toward opening; We will prepare him for the easier path.

فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُ لِلْعُسْرَىٰ (fasanuyassiruhu lilʿusraā): so We will smooth his way toward hardship; his path will be made easy toward tightness and impasse.

يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْخَمْرِ وَالْمَيْسِرِ (yasalūnaka ʿani al-khamri wal-maysiri): they ask you about wine/intoxicant and maysir; they ask you about intoxicant and gambling/chance-based profit-seeking.

Summary

The root Y S R refers to ease, opening, availability, and smoothness of action. In its foundation, it is positive and merciful, and it stands opposite ʿusr: hardship and tightness.

But taysīr does not always mean positive reward. It means the smoothing of a path. If the human being’s path is toward growth, taysīr leads him toward yusrā. If his path is toward stinginess, denial, and false self-sufficiency, that same smoothing can lead him toward ʿusrā.

From this same root comes maysir, a word that in Qur’anic usage points to gambling. Gambling is the correct historical translation, but the structural layer can also be seen: seeking profit through ease, chance, volatility, and win-loss without proportionate work and production.

So the whole root Y S R must not be reduced to maysir, and maysir must not be treated only as an old game. The root is broad and merciful; maysir is a specific warning branch of the same field of ease, where ease separates from growth and mercy and becomes chance-based profit-seeking.
