Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root S M AYN (S M ʿ)

Hearing, listening attentively, receiving the message, and responsibility after hearing

Qur’anic forms of this root

The root س م ع (S M AYN / S M ʿ) may look like one of the simplest roots of the Qur’an because its first meaning is “to hear.” But in the Qur’an, samʿ is not merely sound reaching the ear. It is the beginning of receiving a message, attention, proof being established, and responsibility after hearing.

The central meaning of the root

The root س م ع (S M AYN / S M ʿ) appears simple because the first meaning that comes to mind is “hearing.” But this simplicity can mislead. If samʿ is reduced to sound reaching the ear, a major part of the Qur’an’s view of the human being, guidance, responsibility, and ethical response is lost.

In the Qur’an, hearing is not merely waves of sound reaching the ear. Samʿ is the beginning of guidance, the first stage of proof being established, and sometimes a witness against the human being who heard but did not want to understand.

Whoever hears the message of truth no longer remains in the same position as before hearing. Hearing brings the human being into the field of choice: either he accepts the message and responds to it, or he turns away from it and carries a heavier responsibility.

The central meaning of this root can be understood this way: in the Qur’an, S M AYN means hearing the message and entering responsibility after hearing.

Hearing and listening attentively

سَمِعَ / سَمِعُوا (samiʿa / samiʿū) means he heard and they heard. This may be ordinary hearing, but in many verses hearing is joined with responsibility. Whoever hears God’s speech, signs, warning, or call to truth is no longer like someone to whom no message has arrived.

يَسْمَعُ / يَسْمَعُونَ (yasmaʿu / yasmaʿūn) means he hears and they hear. The Qur’an speaks of people who hear outwardly, but their hearing does not reach understanding, acceptance, or reform. This failure to hear is not physical deafness; it is inner closure. Sound reaches the ear, but the message does not enter the heart.

اِسْمَعْ / اِسْمَعُوا (ismaʿ / ismaʿū) means hear and listen. This command is not only a call to receive sound; it is a call to attention, wakefulness, and readiness to receive truth.

اِسْتَمَعَ / اِسْتَمَعُوا / يَسْتَمِعُ / يَسْتَمِعُونَ (istamaʿa / istamaʿū / yastamiʿu / yastamiʿūn) is stronger than ordinary hearing. Hearing can happen unintentionally, but istimāʿ means that a person turns intentionally toward speech. In this sense, listening is an inner orientation.

From here the importance of listening to the Qur’an becomes clear. Listening to the Qur’an is not merely hearing sound; it requires presence, attention, humility, and readiness to understand. On the other side, listening to lies, rumor, distortion, and polluted speech is also not harmless. Whatever a human being willingly listens to finds a path into him.

In some uses, especially the form تَسَمَّعَ / يَسَّمَّعُونَ (tasammaʿa / yassammaʿūn), this branch can point to eavesdropping or seeking to listen through a wrong path. Here hearing is not a virtue. The Qur’an shows that not every hearing is pure and not every search for information is rightful. The ear, like the tongue and eye, has an ethical boundary.

Conveying the message and making someone truly hear

أَسْمَعَ / يُسْمِعُ (asmaʿa / yusmiʿu) means to make hear, to bring something to hearing, or to make the message reach real hearing. This branch of the root clarifies one of the important boundaries of guidance. The Qur’an distinguishes between conveying the message and making someone truly hear.

The messenger can convey the message, recite the signs, warn, and invite. But he does not own people’s hearts. Someone who has closed himself and turned away from truth cannot be forced into true hearing. True hearing is connected to human readiness and the will of God.

This principle breaks every form of religious monopoly. No person or group owns people’s guidance. The human mission is clear communication, justice in speech, beautiful invitation, and avoidance of coercion. Guidance belongs to God; the human being is responsible only within the field of communication and sincere counsel.

Samʿ as the faculty of receiving

سَمْع (samʿ) as a noun points to hearing, the faculty of hearing, and the capacity to receive sound or message. The Qur’an often places samʿ beside sight and fuʾād or afʾidah. This pairing is not accidental.

The human being is created with instruments of receiving and understanding: he hears, sees, and weighs inwardly. These instruments are blessings, but their being blessings creates responsibility.

God as Samīʿ

سَمِيع (samīʿ) means hearing or All-Hearing. When the Qur’an calls God Samīʿ, it must not be understood like limited human hearing. God is Hearing: no sound, supplication, complaint, intention, hidden speech, or polluted whisper is hidden from Him.

God being Samīʿ is both mercy and warning. It is mercy for the oppressed person whose voice is not heard on earth. It is warning for the one who imagines that hidden speech, slander, distortion, or polluted whisper remains outside divine account.

In Al-Mujādilah 58:1, it says:

قَدْ سَمِعَ اللَّهُ قَوْلَ الَّتِي تُجَادِلُكَ فِي زَوْجِهَا

God has heard the statement of the woman who argues with you concerning her husband.

And in Az-Zukhruf 43:80, it says:

أَمْ يَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّا لَا نَسْمَعُ سِرَّهُمْ وَنَجْوَاهُم

Do they think that We do not hear their secret and their private conversation?

In this view, God being Samīʿ is not a dry theological concept. It is God’s presence before supplication, injustice, whisper, complaint, and hidden truth. No sound is lost in His order.

Sammāʿūn: listeners to falsehood

سَمَّاعُونَ (sammāʿūn) means those who listen much or listen with desire. In the Qur’an, this word sometimes appears in a negative context. In Al-Māʾidah 5:41, it says:

سَمَّاعُونَ لِلْكَذِبِ

Listeners to falsehood.

This expression is very important. The Qur’an does not only criticize the liar; it also criticizes the listener to lies. A lie does not remain alive only by the tongue of the liar. It also lives through ears that carelessly, eagerly, or fanatically receive it and spread it.

A society is not polluted only by producing falsehood; it is also polluted by irresponsible consumption of falsehood. If ears thirst for rumor, distortion, slander, and polluted speech, the market of lies will always remain alive. The human being is not only responsible for what he says; he is also responsible for what he willingly and trustingly listens to.

Hearing and obedience

One of the major axes of this root is the connection between hearing and response. The Qur’an speaks of people who say سَمِعْنَا وَأَطَعْنَا (samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā): we heard and obeyed. This expression appears in Al-Baqarah 2:285.

Opposite them are those who say سَمِعْنَا وَعَصَيْنَا (samiʿnā wa-ʿaṣaynā): we heard and disobeyed. This expression appears in Al-Baqarah 2:93.

This contrast shows that hearing truth brings the human being into responsibility. Complete hearing is not merely receiving sound. It is hearing that leaves an effect, creates response, and brings the human being closer to right action.

Of course, this does not mean blind obedience. The Qur’an calls the human being to hearing with reason, discernment, and following the best speech. Qur’anic hearing is neither thoughtless imitation nor selfish indifference. Qur’anic hearing means listening with wakefulness, measuring by truth, and responding with righteous action.

Hearing, sight, and inner hearts

The Qur’an often places hearing, sight, and afʾidah together. These three are the instruments of human responsibility. Hearing is the path by which the message enters. Sight is the path by which signs are seen. Fuʾād or qalb is the center of weighing, receiving, and inner orientation.

If the ear is closed, the message does not enter. If the eye is blind, signs are not seen. If the fuʾād or qalb is polluted, what is received does not become guidance. So samʿ in the Qur’an is the front line of the message entering, but it is not enough by itself. Hearing must reach understanding, discernment, faith, and action.

Methodological note

In translating this root, one should not always be satisfied with one word. “Hearing” is enough in some verses, but in many places the difference between hearing, listening attentively, making hear, improper eavesdropping, and responsible hearing must be preserved.

If all forms of this root are translated only as “hearing,” part of the Qur’an’s ethical geometry is lost. The Qur’an wants to show that the human being is not passive before the message. He chooses whether to listen or not, understand or not, accept or distort, obey or disobey.

Suggested rendering in Forqan

This root should be rendered according to context.

  • samiʿa: heard, received, heard the message, heard with responsibility.
  • yasmaʿu / yasmaʿūn: he hears, they hear; or in a negative context: they hear but do not accept.
  • ismaʿ / ismaʿū: hear, listen, listen attentively, pay attention to the message.
  • istamaʿa / yastamiʿūn: listened attentively, they listen attentively, listened with awareness.
  • asmaʿa / yusmiʿu: made hear, brought to hearing, brought the message inward.
  • tasammaʿa / yassammaʿūn: eavesdropping, listening secretly, seeking to hear through a wrong path.
  • samʿ: hearing, faculty of hearing, reception of sound or message.
  • samīʿ: Hearing, All-Hearing, the One from whom no sound or speech is hidden.
  • sammāʿūn: listeners, those who listen much, those who listen eagerly; in a negative context: listeners to falsehood.

Summary

س م ع (S M AYN / S M ʿ) in the Qur’an is not only the root of hearing sound. It shows the receiving of a message and responsibility after hearing. The human being enters the field of guidance through the ear, but guidance occurs only when hearing reaches the heart, understanding, acceptance of truth, and righteous action.

The Qur’an calls the human being to conscious hearing: not blind hearing, not listening to lies, and not indifference to truth after the message has arrived.

In the Forqan method, samʿ should be understood this way: hearing is the beginning of responsibility. The human ear is not only an instrument for receiving sound; it is a gate through which either truth enters the heart, or pollution does.
