Qur’anic root qāf rā ʿayn (ق ر ع)

Knocking, striking, and overwhelming events

In early Arabic, the root ق ر ع denotes knocking or striking something. A blow may produce a sound, alert someone, cause harm, or confront an opponent.

Historical and literary witness

A verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet al-Mutalammis al-Ḍubaʿī reads:

لِذِي الْحِلْمِ قَبْلَ الْيَوْمِ مَا تُقْرَعُ الْعَصَا / وَمَا عُلِّمَ الْإِنْسَانُ إِلَّا لِيَعْلَمَا

Long before today, the staff was struck to alert the discerning; a human being is taught only in order to learn.

The verse is preserved in the Aṣmaʿiyyāt and is associated with the Arabic proverb إِنَّ الْعَصَا قُرِعَتْ لِذِي الْحِلْمِ. The proverb describes someone for whom a brief signal is enough to prompt awareness and attentiveness.

Here, qarʿ means striking to produce a sound and attract attention. The poem is transmitted under al-Mutalammis’s name; that attribution supports its use as an early literary witness, without independently establishing the exact date of composition.

Forms examined: قَرَعَ، يَقْرَعُ، قَرْع، قَارِع، قَارِعَة، قَوَارِع، قِرَاع، مُقَارَعَة، تَقْرِيع، مِقْرَعَة، قُرْعَة، اِقْتِرَاع and أَقْرَع.

Qur’anic form: قَارِعَة. It occurs five times: 13:31, 69:4, 101:1, 101:2, and 101:3.

1. Central meaning and semantic branches

In Arabic, قَرَعَ الْبَابَ means to knock on a door, and قَرَعَهُ بِالْعَصَا means to strike someone with a staff.

مِقْرَعَة names an implement used for striking; مُقَارَعَة denotes confrontation in which the two sides strike one another.

This meaning also extends beyond physical action. تَقْرِيع denotes severe rebuke, while قَرَعَهُ بِالْحَقِّ describes confronting someone with truth or a compelling argument.

The expression قَرَعَتْهُ قَوَارِعُ الدَّهْرِ describes a person struck by the hardships of time.

Just as a physical blow can shake a body or alter its condition, a major event can transform human life, disturb peace, and disrupt a community’s order.

In this usage, قَارِعَة denotes a severe, striking event that befalls a person or community and leaves a powerful impact.

The word family also has other branches. قُرْعَة denotes a lot, and اِقْتِرَاع selection by drawing lots. أَقْرَع describes someone who has lost the hair of the head; قَرْع, in another use, names a type of gourd.

The historical relationship of all these branches to striking is not firmly established. The Qur’an uses the branch concerning striking events.

2. Qāriʿah: a striking event in worldly life

وَلَوْ أَنَّ قُرْآنًا سُيِّرَتْ بِهِ الْجِبَالُ أَوْ قُطِّعَتْ بِهِ الْأَرْضُ أَوْ كُلِّمَ بِهِ الْمَوْتَىٰ ۗ بَلْ لِلَّهِ الْأَمْرُ جَمِيعًا ۗ أَفَلَمْ يَيْأَسِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَنْ لَوْ يَشَاءُ اللَّهُ لَهَدَى النَّاسَ جَمِيعًا ۗ وَلَا يَزَالُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا تُصِيبُهُمْ بِمَا صَنَعُوا قَارِعَةٌ أَوْ تَحُلُّ قَرِيبًا مِنْ دَارِهِمْ حَتَّىٰ يَأْتِيَ وَعْدُ اللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُخْلِفُ الْمِيعَادَ

Wa-law anna qurʾānan suyyirat bihi l-jibālu aw quṭṭiʿat bihi l-arḍu aw kullima bihi l-mawtā. Bal li-llāhi l-amru jamīʿā. A-fa-lam yayʾasi lladhīna āmanū an law yashāʾu llāhu la-hadā l-nāsa jamīʿā. Wa-lā yazālu lladhīna kafarū tuṣībuhum bi-mā ṣanaʿū qāriʿatun aw taḥullu qarīban min dārihim ḥattā yaʾtiya waʿdu llāh. Inna llāha lā yukhlifu l-mīʿād.

And if there were a Qur’an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split apart, or the dead were spoken to… Rather, all command belongs to The God. Have those who believe not yet realized that, if The God willed, He would guide all people? Those who disbelieve continue to be struck by a calamity because of what they have done, or it settles near their dwelling, until the promise of The God comes. Indeed, The God does not fail His appointment.

Ar-Ra'd 13:31

Here قَارِعَةٌ is indefinite and denotes a severe, striking event. The translation of the opening preserves the unexpressed answer to the conditional with an ellipsis. كُلِّمَ is passive: the dead are spoken to, rather than being made to speak. Rendering يَيْأَس as “realized” follows an attested interpretation; another interpretation retains the sense of giving up hope, here concerning the opponents’ acceptance of faith. Neither interpretation changes the meaning of qāriʿah.

بِمَا صَنَعُوا connects the calamity described here to the deeds of the people identified in this passage as those who disbelieve. It does not establish a general rule that every calamity reveals a fault or sin in each person affected.

There are two grammatical analyses of أَوْ تَحُلُّ قَرِيبًا مِنْ دَارِهِمْ. In one, the subject of تَحُلُّ is the calamity: it settles near their dwelling. In the other, the verb addresses Muhammad: he settles near their dwelling. These are analyses of the subject, not necessarily different recitations.

In both analyses, qāriʿah denotes a severe, threatening event, without restricting it to a single kind of occurrence.

As a reflection beyond the direct lexical meaning, a society founded on oppression, corruption, aggression, and violations of rights can create conditions for its own troubles. Loss of public trust, insecurity, economic crises, and social conflicts may be consequences of human conduct; they are not exclusive definitions of qāriʿah.

Al-Rūm 30:41 likewise speaks of corruption appearing on land and sea because of what human hands have earned.

Nevertheless, 13:31 does not warrant concluding that every person afflicted by a severe event suffers because of their own disbelief or sin. Al-Anfāl 8:25 warns of a trial whose harm does not fall exclusively upon wrongdoers.

3. Qāriʿah in Sūrat al-Ḥāqqah

كَذَّبَتْ ثَمُودُ وَعَادٌ بِالْقَارِعَةِ

Kadhdhabat Thamūdu wa-ʿĀdun bi-l-qāriʿah.

Thamūd and ʿĀd denied the Striking Event.

Al-Haqqah 69:4

Unlike 13:31, this verse uses qāriʿah in the definite form.

Sūrat al-Ḥāqqah begins by introducing an immense event:

الْحَاقَّةُ

Al-ḥāqqah.

The Certain Event.

Al-Haqqah 69:1

مَا الْحَاقَّةُ

Mā l-ḥāqqah.

What is the Certain Event?

Al-Haqqah 69:2

وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الْحَاقَّةُ

Wa-mā adrāka mā l-ḥāqqah.

And what will make you know what the Certain Event is?

Al-Haqqah 69:3

The Qur’an then speaks of Thamūd and ʿĀd denying the Striking Event.

In this context, al-ḥāqqah and al-qāriʿah express two aspects of the Resurrection. Al-ḥāqqah emphasizes its truth and certainty; al-qāriʿah emphasizes its overwhelming impact.

The following verses describe the fate of Thamūd and ʿĀd. Al-Ḥāqqah 69:5 recounts the destruction of Thamūd, and 69:6 the destruction of ʿĀd by a fierce, violent wind.

The surah then turns to the events of the Resurrection.

The account of these communities illustrates the fulfillment of divine warnings in human history. The power that can transform the order of a community’s life can also transform the order of the world.

4. Al-Qāriʿah: the Striking Event of the Resurrection

Sūrat al-Qāriʿah opens with three short verses:

الْقَارِعَةُ

Al-qāriʿah.

The Striking Event!

Al-Qari'ah 101:1

مَا الْقَارِعَةُ

Mā l-qāriʿah.

What is the Striking Event?

Al-Qari'ah 101:2

وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الْقَارِعَةُ

Wa-mā adrāka mā l-qāriʿah.

And what will make you know what the Striking Event is?

Al-Qari'ah 101:3

Morphologically, قَارِعَة has the feminine active-participle pattern فَاعِلَة. In Arabic, it also functions as the name of a severe, striking event.

Its threefold repetition at the opening of the surah directs attention to the magnitude of an event beyond ordinary human experience.

The Qur’an then depicts that day:

يَوْمَ يَكُونُ النَّاسُ كَالْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ

Yawma yakūnu l-nāsu ka-l-farāshi l-mabthūth.

The day when people will be like scattered moths,

Al-Qari'ah 101:4

وَتَكُونُ الْجِبَالُ كَالْعِهْنِ الْمَنْفُوشِ

Wa-takūnu l-jibālu ka-l-ʿihni l-manfūsh.

and the mountains will be like carded, colored wool.

Al-Qari'ah 101:5

These two verses place two images together: the scattering of humanity and the loss of the mountains’ solidity.

Mountains, symbols of grandeur and stability in everyday experience, will resemble carded wool; people who possess order, power, and social structures in worldly life will be scattered like moths.

Qāriʿah is not merely a name for a loud sound, an earthquake, or a physical collision. It describes an event before which the familiar order of the world is transformed.

Yet Sūrat al-Qāriʿah does not end with this transformation:

فَأَمَّا مَنْ ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ

Fa-ammā man thaqulat mawāzīnuh.

As for the one whose scales are heavy,

Al-Qari'ah 101:6

فَهُوَ فِي عِيشَةٍ رَاضِيَةٍ

Fa-huwa fī ʿīshatin rāḍiyah.

they will be in a satisfying life.

Al-Qari'ah 101:7

وَأَمَّا مَنْ خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُ

Wa-ammā man khaffat mawāzīnuh.

But as for the one whose scales are light,

Al-Qari'ah 101:8

فَأُمُّهُ هَاوِيَةٌ

Fa-ummuhu hāwiyah.

their abode will be Hāwiyah.

Al-Qari'ah 101:9

The magnitude of the Striking Event lies not only in the transformation of the physical world, but also in the disclosure of the outcome of human life and the beginning of another stage of existence.

Before an event that removes the solidity of mountains, what determines a person’s fate is the weight of their deeds, rather than worldly power, wealth, or standing.

5. Comparison with related roots

ق ر ع (qarʿ) and ض ر ب (ḍarb)

Ḍarb has a broader semantic range. Besides striking, it occurs in expressions for setting forth a parable, traveling, and placing a barrier.

In its branch concerning blows, qarʿ emphasizes knocking or a strike that produces a sound or perceptible effect.

In the Qur’anic uses of qāriʿah, this image of striking conveys the severity of an event’s impact on human beings and the world.

ق ر ع (qarʿ) and ط ر ق (ṭarq)

Both roots can denote knocking on a door or striking a surface.

The range of ṭarq also includes a path, a way, and coming by night, whereas the branch of qarʿ under discussion concerns the strike itself and its effect.

In Sūrat al-Ṭāriq, the Qur’an identifies al-ṭāriq with the piercing star; in Sūrat al-Qāriʿah, it presents al-qāriʿah as the immense event of the Resurrection.

Similarity between their physical images does not make their Qur’anic referents identical.

ق ر ع (qāriʿah) and ص ع ق (ṣāʿiqah)

Ṣāʿiqah concerns an overwhelming event that may cause unconsciousness, loss of human strength, or death.

Qāriʿah is a broader designation for a severe, striking event and does not necessarily specify its type or mechanism.

A ṣāʿiqah can thus be described as a striking calamity. This semantic comparison does not establish that the two words are interchangeable in every Qur’anic context.

ق ر ع (qāriʿah) and ز ل ز ل (zalzalah)

Zalzalah denotes shaking and violent movement, whereas qāriʿah emphasizes the impact and severity of an event that befalls humanity or the world.

In Sūrat al-Zalzalah, the shaking of the earth is an event of the Resurrection. Sūrat al-Qāriʿah describes the scattering of humanity, the transformation of mountains, and human destiny after that event.

An earthquake may therefore form part of the picture of the Striking Event, but the two words do not mean the same thing.

ق ر ع (qāriʿah) and ح ق ق (ḥāqqah)

Ḥāqqah belongs to ح ق ق, a family concerning truth, established reality, and realization.

Qāriʿah belongs to ق ر ع, a family concerning knocking and striking.

Both titles in Sūrat al-Ḥāqqah concern the Resurrection: one foregrounds its certainty and reality, the other its overwhelming impact.

6. Translation choices for Forqan

In al-Raʿd 13:31, قَارِعَةٌ may be rendered “a striking calamity” or “a devastating event.”

In al-Ḥāqqah 69:4 and al-Qāriʿah 101:1, 101:2, and 101:3, الْقَارِعَةُ may be rendered “the Striking Event.”

Translating qāriʿah simply as “the Resurrection” identifies its referent in that setting but loses the image of striking; it is also unsuitable for its use in 13:31.

Brief root meaning: Knocking or delivering a blow that produces a sound or perceptible effect; figuratively, a severe, striking event befalling a person or community.

Conclusion

The root ق ر ع concerns knocking, striking, and the resulting impact. Qāriʿah denotes a severe, striking event that befalls a person or community.

The Qur’an uses this word both for severe events in worldly life and for the immense event of the Resurrection.

Qāriʿah is not the exclusive name of one natural phenomenon. Its impact may appear in the transformation of a community’s life or of the world’s order.

In Sūrat al-Qāriʿah, this immense image leads to the weighing of deeds. When even mountains lose their solidity, what carries decisive value for a human being is the weight of their deeds.
