Qur'anic Root J Z Y

1. Short Introduction

The root ج ز ي moves in the field of answering an act, placing something opposite another thing, sufficing on behalf of another, and paying or settling what belongs to an account, right, or obligation.

The core of this root is not only "reward." In the Qur'an, jazā' can be a good response to a good act, a harmful response to a harmful act, compensation for a service, payment of a right, or sufficing in place of another person. What remains in all these uses is a prior act, right, or obligation on one side, and a fitting response placed opposite it on the other side.

For this reason, jazā' is value-neutral by itself. Whether it is positive or negative is decided by the act and the context. The jazā' of the doer of good is not the same as the jazā' of the criminal, but both follow one linguistic structure: something is placed in response to what they did and settles the account of that act.

In some verses this root comes with عَنْ, as in لَا تَجْزِي نَفْسٌ عَنْ نَفْسٍ شَيْئًا. In that structure, the verb moves toward the meaning of sufficing on behalf of someone else or carrying something from another person's account. The Qur'an negates that possibility on the Day of Account: no self can stand in place of another self or discharge anything from its responsibility.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives

جَزَىٰ / يَجْزِي: to answer an act, return its fitting result, or pay what stands opposite it.

جُزِيَ / تُجْزَىٰ / يُجْزَوْنَ: passive forms, where a person or group receives the response of what they have done.

جَزَاء: the response placed opposite an act, right, or obligation. It may be reward, punishment, compensation, replacement, or fitting result.

نُجَازِي: a Form III expression used in Saba' 34:17, emphasizing a marked response to a settled pattern, while the context must remain the guide.

جَازٍ: an active participle; one who could suffice on behalf of another or carry something from another's account.

جِزْيَة: a fixed payment in a legal and political relation. Its root connection is with discharging something in response to an obligation, but its full historical and legal reality cannot be derived from the root alone.

4. Central Meaning

The central meaning of J Z Y is: something is placed in response to an act, right, or obligation in a way that answers it and moves its account toward sufficiency or settlement.

This meaning contains several elements. First, an act has been done, a right has arisen, a service has been rendered, harm has been caused, or an obligation has been formed. Second, a response is placed opposite it. Third, that response is connected to the earlier act or obligation. Fourth, the response can complete the account, return the result, or discharge what was due.

هَلْ جَزَاءُ الْإِحْسَانِ إِلَّا الْإِحْسَانُ

Is the response of ihsan anything but ihsan?

Ar-Rahman 55:60

وَجَزَاءُ سَيِّئَةٍ سَيِّئَةٌ مِثْلُهَا

And the response of an evil is an evil like it.

Ash-Shura 42:40

So the goodness or harm of jazā' is not decided by the root alone. The act and the context clarify the nature of the response.

5. Material Image

The material image is an open account. Someone has done something, taken on a right, rendered service, caused harm, or created an obligation. A response is then set opposite that record: wage, compensation, result, punishment, reward, or a payment that discharges the obligation.

When the suitable response is placed against the act or obligation, the account moves toward sufficiency and settlement. In the structure جَزَىٰ عَنْهُ, another person or thing stands in the place where it could suffice on behalf of the first person. The Qur'an denies this on the Day of Account: no self can discharge anything from another self's account.

This image should not be mixed with the root J Z Z, which relates to cutting or shearing. Similar letters are not enough to prove a shared derivation. The image of an open account and a settling response fits the Qur'anic structures of J Z Y far better.

6. Classical Arabic Usage

In Arabic usage, jazāhu bimā fa'ala points to answering a person according to what he did. That answer may be good or bad, and its nature is determined by the act and the words around it.

The structure جَزَىٰ عَنْهُ is connected with sufficing on behalf of another and discharging something in that person's place. This helps explain why the Qur'an says that no self will suffice for another self on the Day of Account.

The link with payment is also clear. A jazā' response is not scattered or unrelated; it stands opposite a prior act, service, or obligation. From here the root can move from financial and legal relations to moral and eschatological response.

7. Misreadings and Reductions

A common reduction is to translate jazā' always as "reward." In English, reward usually sounds positive, but Qur'anic jazā' may be the response of good or the response of wrong. The Qur'an speaks of the jazā' of the doers of good and the jazā' of the wrongdoers.

Another reduction is to turn jazā' into uncontrolled emotional revenge. Qur'anic language often stresses the relation between act and response. The response of evil is limited by likeness, while good may receive an increase by the favor of The God.

مَنْ جَاءَ بِالْحَسَنَةِ فَلَهُ عَشْرُ أَمْثَالِهَا وَمَنْ جَاءَ بِالسَّيِّئَةِ فَلَا يُجْزَىٰ إِلَّا مِثْلَهَا

Whoever comes with a good deed, for him is ten like it; and whoever comes with an evil deed is not answered except with its like.

Al-An'am 6:160

So jazā' stands inside justice, while the response to good may be expanded by divine favor.

8. Methodological Note

Several levels must be kept separate: the lexical meaning, the kind of response, the measure of proportionality, the agent of the act, the relation of jazā' with mercy and repentance, and the translation required by each structure.

"Rewarded" is suitable only in positive contexts. Elsewhere, "answered," "recompensed," "paid," "given the result of," "compensated," or "sufficed on behalf of" may be more accurate.

9. Qur'anic Uses

No self suffices for another self

وَاتَّقُوا يَوْمًا لَا تَجْزِي نَفْسٌ عَنْ نَفْسٍ شَيْئًا

And be mindful of a day when no self will suffice anything for another self.

Al-Baqarah 2:48

The structure تَجْزِي ... عَنْ does not mean reward here. It means that no self can stand in place of another, discharge anything from its account, or reduce its responsibility. The same structure returns in Al-Baqarah 2:123.

Even the closest family relation does not replace account

وَاخْشَوْا يَوْمًا لَا يَجْزِي وَالِدٌ عَنْ وَلَدِهِ وَلَا مَوْلُودٌ هُوَ جَازٍ عَنْ وَالِدِهِ شَيْئًا

And fear a day when no father will suffice anything for his child, nor will any child be one who suffices anything for his father.

Luqman 31:33

The verse chooses the father-child relation, one of the strongest human bonds, to show that moral account is not transferred automatically to another person.

Every self receives according to striving and earning

لِتُجْزَىٰ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ بِمَا تَسْعَىٰ

So that every self may be answered according to what it strives for.

Ta Ha 20:15

الْيَوْمَ تُجْزَىٰ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ

Today every self is answered according to what it earned.

Ghafir 40:17

The preposition bi- ties the response to striving and earning. Jazā' is not unrelated to the human path.

The response of ihsan

هَلْ جَزَاءُ الْإِحْسَانِ إِلَّا الْإِحْسَانُ

Is the response of ihsan anything but ihsan?

Ar-Rahman 55:60

Here the relation between act and response is qualitative. Ihsan is answered with ihsan, though divine generosity can expand the response far beyond the limited human act.

The response of evil is limited by likeness

وَجَزَاءُ سَيِّئَةٍ سَيِّئَةٌ مِثْلُهَا

And the response of an evil is an evil like it.

Ash-Shura 42:40

The word mithluhā sets a boundary. The response must not exceed the first wrong. The verse then opens the door to pardon and reform.

No added injustice in answering evil

وَلَا يُجْزَى الَّذِينَ عَمِلُوا السَّيِّئَاتِ إِلَّا مَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

Those who did evil deeds are not answered except with what they used to do.

Al-Qasas 28:84

This shows two Qur'anic principles: justice in the response of evil, and favor in the response of good.

Jazā' of kufr

ذَٰلِكَ جَزَيْنَاهُمْ بِمَا كَفَرُوا وَهَلْ نُجَازِي إِلَّا الْكَفُورَ

That is what We answered them with because they covered truth; and do We answer in that way except the persistent coverer of truth?

Saba' 34:17

The phrase bimā kafarū makes the basis of the response clear. What reached them is not presented as unrelated to their own direction.

Jazā' of sabr

أُولَٰئِكَ يُجْزَوْنَ الْغُرْفَةَ بِمَا صَبَرُوا

They are answered with the high chamber because they were steadfast.

Al-Furqan 25:75

The response is tied to sabr, but this does not require a mechanical equality between a limited human act and the vastness of the Hereafter.

Jazā' in human relations and payment

إِنَّ أَبِي يَدْعُوكَ لِيَجْزِيَكَ أَجْرَ مَا سَقَيْتَ لَنَا

My father calls you so that he may pay you the wage of what you watered for us.

Al-Qasas 28:25

This is one of the clearest witnesses for the meaning of payment and settlement. Jazā is the act of paying; ajr is the wage that is paid.

10. Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

جَزَىٰ: answering and discharging

The verb changes by structure. Jazāhu bi'amalihi means he answered him according to his act. Jazāhu ajran means he paid him a wage. Jazā 'anhu means he sufficed on his behalf.

جَزَاء: response placed opposite an act

Jazā' may be positive or negative: the response of the doers of good, the response of the wrongdoers, or a response that is wifāqā, fully fitting.

جَازٍ: one who suffices on behalf of another

In Luqman 31:33, jāzin 'an wālidihi is not simply a rewarder. It is one who could stand in place of his father and carry something from his account. The verse denies this.

جِزْيَة: fixed payment

Jizyah is a fixed payment in a concrete legal and political context. The root helps us see the idea of discharging a due, but it does not by itself define all later legal history.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

Jazā with عَنْ

When the verb comes with 'an, the meaning shifts toward sufficing on behalf of another: lā tajzī nafsun 'an nafsin shay'an.

When it comes with bi-, the preposition often marks the basis of the response: bimā kasabat, bimā ṣabarū, bimā kafarū.

When it comes with a direct object such as ajra, the verb can mean to pay that wage or compensation.

12. J Z Y Compared with A J R

Ajr is wage, recompense, or reward as the thing given. Jazā is the answering or settling act, and jazā' is the response placed opposite the act. In Al-Qasas 28:25 both appear together: liyajziyaka ajra mā saqayta lanā.

13. J Z Y Compared with TH W B

Thawāb is tied to return: what comes back to the human being from an act. Jazā' is broader: response, payment, compensation, or punishment. Thawāb usually carries a more positive religious tone, while jazā' depends on context.

14. J Z Y Compared with W F Y

Wafā' is fulfilling and giving something in full. Jazā' is the response placed opposite an act or obligation. They can meet when the response is fully paid, but the roots look from different angles.

15. J Z Y Compared with Q D Y

Qaḍā points to deciding, completing, or carrying out a matter. Jazā' points to the response that answers an act or settles an account. Judgment may lead to jazā', but the two are not the same root idea.

16. J Z Y Compared with A Q B

'Āqibah and 'iqāb are tied to what follows after an act. Jazā' is the response placed opposite the act. The aftermath and the recompense may meet, but their angles differ.

17. Suggested Renderings for Forqan

For جَزَىٰ: answered, recompensed, paid, gave the fitting result, or sufficed on behalf of, depending on structure.

For جَزَاء: response, recompense, fitting response, penalty, reward, or compensation according to context.

For لَا تَجْزِي نَفْسٌ عَنْ نَفْسٍ: no self will suffice for another self.

For تُجْزَىٰ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ: every self is answered according to what it earned.

For جِزْيَة: fixed due/payment, with explanation from context.

18. Summary

The root J Z Y points to answering an act, right, or obligation with what stands opposite it and settles its account. It can mean reward, punishment, compensation, payment, or sufficing on behalf of another, but none of those English words alone covers the whole root.

The decisive question is always structure and context: Is the verse speaking about a good act, an evil act, payment, or the impossibility of one self carrying another self's account? Once that is seen, the root becomes precise rather than vague.
