Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root R J AYN (R J ʿ)

Returning, going back, turning from a path, the return of an effect, and returning to a point of reference

Important Forms from This Root

رَجَعَ / يَرْجِعُ

rajaʿa / yarjiʿu; returned, went back, refers back.

رُجُوع / رَجْع

rujūʿ / rajʿ; return, coming back, return of an effect, restoration or recurrence.

الرُّجْعَىٰ

al-rujʿā; the final return, decisive return, final place of return.

رَاجِع / مَرْجِع

rājiʿ / marjiʿ; one returning; place of return, reference point, final place of judgment.

أَرْجَعَ / رَجَّعَ

arjaʿa / rajjaʿa; returned something, brought back, repeated, restored.

تَرَاجَعَ / رَجْعَة

tarājaʿa / rajʿah; returned mutually, withdrew from a position; a single return or return after separation.

Central Meaning

The root R J AYN (R J ʿ) means returning, referring back, going back from a path, returning to a previous point or state, the return of an effect, and being returned toward a reference. This root is not only physical backward movement. Sometimes rujūʿ is a person returning from a path he took. Sometimes a word in dialogue returns to the other side. Sometimes a matter or judgment is referred back to its authority.

At a deeper level, rujūʿ may be the return of the human being after departure, misguidance, or distance, back toward The God, truth, or his own origin. The root is tied to return-motion, return to reference, return of effect, and the change of direction from going away to coming back.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of R J AYN (R J ʿ) is turning back from one direction toward a previous direction or reference point. Movement matters in this root, but not only physical movement. Rujūʿ may occur in place, speech, decision, judgment, faith, repentance, relationship, nature, cycle, or destiny.

رَجَعَ means that someone or something returns from a path of departure, distance, or initial movement toward an origin, reference point, defined place, or previous state. For this reason, R J ʿ is connected to marjiʿ, the reference or place of return. What returns does not return directionlessly; it returns to a place, person, ruling, cycle, or origin.

رَجْع may also indicate the return of an effect or the restoration of a result: something travels a path and then reappears as effect, answer, rain, yield, or echo.

Material Image

The first material image is a person who has gone out from somewhere and then returns to that place or previous direction. A traveler leaves home and comes back. A sound returns from a mountain as echo. Rain comes from the sky and revives the earth. A word is spoken and its answer returns to the speaker or to the other side.

Another material image is a cycle of restoration. Something leaves a place, travels a course, and then returns as effect or result. Water rises from earth and sea, moves in the sky, and returns as rain. This return is not merely going back; it is a return that restores life and effect.

In all these images there is a returning movement. R J ʿ is not mere stopping. Rujūʿ means a change of direction or the return of an effect. Something that was moving forward, going away, being issued, or distancing from its origin turns its path back, or its effect returns.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, رَجَعَ is used for returning from travel, returning from a path, returning from a decision, and speech or answer returning. رَجْع may refer to the return of sound, the return of an effect, the return of rain and water, or yield and after-result.

Uses connected with sound and echo show that this root is not only about human beings. Anything that issues from somewhere and then comes back may enter the field of R J ʿ. In old Arab life, return from travel, return of caravans, return from battle, return of rain, return of reply in dialogue, and the yield of animals and land all belonged naturally to this root’s field.

Qur’anic Usage

Spatial Return

رَجَعَ can mean returning from a place or path. In the story of Moses: وَلَمَّا رَجَعَ مُوسَىٰ إِلَىٰ قَوْمِهِ غَضْبَانَ أَسِفًا; when Moses returned to his people angry and grieved 7:150.

Here, rujūʿ is spatial return. Moses returns from the place of receiving revelation to his people. This use shows that the root may be fully concrete and movement-based, without carrying moral positivity or negativity by itself.

The Final Return to The God

One of the most important Qur’anic uses is the return of human beings and all affairs to The God: إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ; we belong to The God, and to Him we are returning 2:156.

In this verse, رَاجِعُونَ does not only mean death, although it appears in the context of loss and death. Its deeper meaning is that The God is the origin and reference of the human being. The human being is not independent from The God, and his path is not without reference. Rujūʿ here is existential and destined return to The God.

Another form appears in Surat al-ʿAlaq: إِنَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ الرُّجْعَىٰ; surely the final return is to your Lord 96:8. الرُّجْعَىٰ highlights the decisive final return. However far a person seems to move through self-sufficiency, heedlessness, or transgression, the end of his return is to the Lord.

The Final Reference of Affairs

The Qur’an repeatedly says that the return or reference of affairs is to The God: إِلَى اللَّهِ مَرْجِعُكُمْ جَمِيعًا; to The God is the return-place of all of you 5:48. A similar form says: إِلَيْهِ مَرْجِعُكُمْ جَمِيعًا; to Him is the return-place of all of you 10:4.

Here, marjiʿ is not only a physical location. It is the place of judgment, reckoning, truth, and the disclosure of results. Every difference, action, hidden thing, and human path finally returns to the divine reference. No human movement is left without return or account.

Returning from Misguidance or a Wrong Path

Rujūʿ may also have a corrective meaning: someone returns from a wrong path. In this sense, rujūʿ is close to repentance, but not identical with it. Tawbah is more specifically an inner and moral return to The God, with remorse and correction. Rujūʿ is broader and may include any return from a path, decision, misguidance, or wrong condition.

Not Returning in Guidance

Surat al-Baqarah says: صُمٌّ بُكْمٌ عُمْيٌ فَهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ; deaf, mute, blind, so they do not return 2:18.

Here, لَا يَرْجِعُونَ means they do not return. In light of the previous verses, they have moved away from light, guidance, and discernment, and they no longer activate the path of return within themselves. This is not spatial return; it is return from closure and misguidance. When hearing, expression, and sight are closed, the ability of guidance-level return is also lost.

Not Returning to Worldly Life after Destruction

Another kind of لَا يَرْجِعُونَ appears in the Qur’an: وَحَرَامٌ عَلَىٰ قَرْيَةٍ أَهْلَكْنَاهَا أَنَّهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ; forbidden upon a settlement We destroyed is that they return 21:95.

This is not the same as al-Baqarah 2:18. In al-Baqarah, the issue is not returning in guidance while living under closure of discernment. In al-Anbiyāʾ 21:95, the issue is not returning after destruction: the return to worldly life or previous condition has been closed.

Return in Dialogue and Returned Speech

R J ʿ may also appear in dialogue: a word, answer, blame, or argument returns between two sides. The Qur’an says: يَرْجِعُ بَعْضُهُمْ إِلَىٰ بَعْضٍ الْقَوْلَ; some of them return speech to others 34:31.

Another example appears in the story of the Queen of Saba: فَنَاظِرَةٌ بِمَ يَرْجِعُ الْمُرْسَلُونَ; so I will see with what the messengers return 27:35. Here, return is both physical and informational. The messengers return, but they also return with response, result, or news.

Rajʿ as Returned Effect and Natural Cycle

The form رَجْع appears in the Qur’an: وَالسَّمَاءِ ذَاتِ الرَّجْعِ; by the sky possessing rajʿ 86:11. The next verse says: وَالْأَرْضِ ذَاتِ الصَّدْعِ; and the earth possessing splitting 86:12.

The placement of rajʿ beside ṣadʿ is important. The sky possesses return: restoration and sending back, often understood as the return of rain and the water cycle. The earth possesses splitting: it opens and plant life comes out. In this context, rajʿ is not merely “rain”; it is the sky’s returning and restoring system.

Returning the Sight for Reinspection

Surat al-Mulk says: فَارْجِعِ الْبَصَرَ هَلْ تَرَىٰ مِن فُطُورٍ; return the sight: do you see any rupture? 67:3. Then: ثُمَّ ارْجِعِ الْبَصَرَ كَرَّتَيْنِ; then return the sight twice again 67:4.

Here, rujūʿ is attached to sight. It means return your gaze, re-examine, inspect again. This use shows that rujūʿ can be a method of investigation and precision. The first look may be surface-level; returning sight means sending the seeing faculty back again to test, inspect, and search for rupture or defect.

Mutual Return in Relationship

The Qur’an also uses the tafaʿul form of this root: فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا أَن يَتَرَاجَعَا; then there is no blame upon the two of them if they return to one another 2:230. Here, يَتَرَاجَعَا points to mutual return in relationship. The form matters because the return is not one-sided; relational and reciprocal return is being described.

Difference from Nearby Roots

T W B is inner and moral return to The God with remorse and correction. Rujūʿ is broader and includes any kind of return. AYN W D (ʿ W D) emphasizes coming again or repeated return; rujūʿ emphasizes return to a reference or previous direction. R D D often means returning something, rejecting, or pushing back; rujūʿ is the act of returning or referring to a reference.

A W B is spiritually close to rujūʿ, but more strongly colored by repeated and humble return to The God. N K Ṣ often means retreat or blameworthy backward movement, while rujūʿ may be positive or negative. Q L B concerns overturning and changing state; rujūʿ concerns return toward a reference.

Ṣ R F emphasizes turning and redirecting; rujūʿ emphasizes the return itself. K R R emphasizes repetition or coming back again; rujūʿ is broader and includes reference. Ṣ Y R / M Ṣ Y R concerns becoming, ending, or arriving at an outcome. Maṣīr does not necessarily mean returning to a previous origin, while rujūʿ is intrinsically tied to return toward a reference, origin, or defined point.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, رَجَعَ should not always be translated only as “returned,” although this is correct in many places. If the context is spatial, “returned” fits. If the context is divine and final, “return to The God,” “place of return,” or “final return” may be more precise.

If the context is judgment or disagreement, “referring the matter back to its reference” may be better. If the context is repentance and correction, “turning back from the wrong path” may be more precise. If the context is لَا يَرْجِعُونَ, one must distinguish: is it non-return in guidance, or non-return to worldly life after destruction?

If the context is رَجْع in nature, “returning effect,” “restoration,” “cycle of return,” or “returning system” may be more precise than simple return. If rujūʿ is attached to sight, as in فَارْجِعِ الْبَصَرَ, “return the gaze for reinspection” carries the sense better than a flat “look again.”

Forqan Translation Suggestions

رَجَعَ / يَرْجِعُ (rajaʿa / yarjiʿu): returned, went back, refers back.

رُجُوع (rujūʿ): return, referring back.

رَجْع (rajʿ): return of effect, restoration, return of rain, returning cycle.

الرُّجْعَىٰ (al-rujʿā): the final return, decisive return, final place of return.

مَرْجِع (marjiʿ): place of return, reference, final place of judgment.

تَرَاجَعَ (tarājaʿa): returned mutually, withdrew from a position, returned to one another.

إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ: we belong to The God, and to Him we are returning; or we return to our existential origin and reference.

فَهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ: so they do not return; or they do not enter the path of return.

وَالسَّمَاءِ ذَاتِ الرَّجْعِ: by the sky possessing return; or the sky possessing a returning/restoring system.

فَارْجِعِ الْبَصَرَ: return the sight; or send your gaze back for reinspection.

Linguistic Summary

The root R J AYN (R J ʿ) denotes returning, referring back, turning from a path, the return of an effect, and return to a reference. At the material level, rujūʿ means that someone or something returns from a path it took. At the natural level, rajʿ may refer to the return of an effect and a restoring cycle, such as rain. In dialogue, speech may return between parties. In law and relationship, tarājuʿ may indicate mutual return. At the Qur’anic level, rujūʿ can point to the return of the human being and all affairs to The God.

Its difference from tawbah is that tawbah is moral and inner return to The God, while rujūʿ is broader. Its difference from radd is that radd more often points to returning something or pushing it back, while rujūʿ is the return itself and the orientation toward reference. Its difference from maṣīr is that maṣīr shows outcome and becoming, while rujūʿ shows return to reference. The main image is this: something has moved away from its origin, path, reference, or cycle, and now returns toward it; or its effect comes back again.
