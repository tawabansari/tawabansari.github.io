Core meaning: in the shelter branch, a protected enclosure for keeping animals or hiding; in Qur’anic usage, carpets and furnishings spread for sitting, comfort, and adornment.

1. The root and its meaning in early Arabic

The Arabic family ز ر ب has several semantic branches. زَرْب denotes a sheepfold. زَرِيبَة also describes an animal enclosure, shelter, or hunter’s hiding place. In another branch, زَرَابِيّ names carpets, rugs, and furnishings spread for sitting and comfort.

These senses appear together in lexicons, but shared root letters alone do not establish a single historical origin for all of them.

An early lexical analysis describes the shelter branch as follows:

الزَّاءُ وَالرَّاءُ وَالْبَاءُ أَصْلٌ يَدُلُّ عَلَى بَعْضِ الْمَأْوَى

al-zāʾu wa-al-rāʾu wa-al-bāʾu aṣlun yadullu ʿalā baʿḍi al-maʾwā

“The letters zāy, rāʾ, and bāʾ indicate a kind of shelter or dwelling place.”

Sheepfolds and hunters’ hiding places illustrate this definition.

This is clear for زَرْب and زَرِيبَة: a place accommodates an animal or person, separating and protecting them to some extent from the outside.

Qur’anic زَرَابِيّ, however, denotes furnishings spread out. The sense “sheepfold” alone cannot prove that a carpet was historically named زربية because it covered or protected something.

A non-Arabic origin has also been proposed for زربية. Some lexical accounts relate it to patterned fabric or textiles associated with a particular locality. The evidence reviewed here does not settle its historical origin.

Two questions must therefore remain distinct: زرب as shelter and زرابي as carpets or spread furnishings.

Identifying pre-Qur’anic usage

A word’s appearance in later dictionaries does not alone establish its pre-Qur’anic meaning. Independent evidence from pre-Islamic poetry or other datable records must be considered.

Early lexicons record زرب for a fold or protected place. This fits pastoral life and the use of enclosures for keeping animals.

For زرابي meaning carpets, no independently dated pre-Islamic poetic example has been established in this study.

Consequently, this study cannot determine precisely when, where, or through what derivation زرابي entered Arabic before the Qur’an.

Its presence in the Qur’an is certain, and early lexical accounts recognize the meaning of carpets and spread furnishings.

Poetic evidence attributed to Arṭāh ibn Suhayyah

Arṭāh ibn Suhayyah, whose life extended into the Umayyad period, describes disagreement among his relatives:

وَنَحْنُ بَنُو عَمٍّ عَلَى ذَاكَ بَيْنَنَا
زَرَابِيُّ فِيهَا بِغْضَةٌ وَتَنَافُسُ

wa-naḥnu banū ʿammin ʿalā dhāka baynanā / zarābiyyu fīhā bighḍatun wa-tanāfusu

Translation:

“Though we are cousins and kinsmen, between us lie carpets in which hatred and rivalry are woven.”

Here زرابي is metaphorical: hostility and rivalry among relatives are likened to carpets spread between them.

The image depends on understanding زرابي as spread furnishings.

The verse thus provides literary evidence for this figurative usage, but its precise date requires independent evidence.

Accounts of the poet’s lifetime differ, and the date of this particular poem is not established here. It should not be presented as a securely dated pre-Islamic example merely from its attribution.

The lexical meaning of زرابي is clear; its first emergence and derivational relationship to زرب require separate evidence.

2. Important forms and derivatives

زَرْب and زِرْب

In the spatial branch, these denote a fold, livestock enclosure, or place where animals are kept safely.

زَرْبُ الْغَنَمِ means a sheepfold.

Its plural is زُرُوب.

زرب also occurs for an entrance or passage into an enclosure.

This relates to animal housing: an enclosure has both a protected boundary and a route for entering and leaving.

زَرِيبَة

This denotes a fold or livestock enclosure.

It also describes a hunter’s hiding place or an animal’s concealed shelter.

Its plural is زَرَائِب.

Here زريبة names a place, not a carpet or rug.

زَرَبَ يَزْرُبُ

This means making a fold for animals or bringing them into an enclosure.

زَرَبَ الْغَنَمَ

zaraba al-ghanama

“He brought the sheep into the fold.”

زَرَبَ لِلْغَنَمِ

zaraba li-al-ghanami

“He made a fold for the sheep.”

This usage directly concerns the protected-place branch.

اِنْزَرَبَ

This means entering a fold, shelter, or hiding place.

اِنْزَرَبَ الصَّائِدُ

inzaraba al-ṣāʾidu

“The hunter entered his hiding place.”

The action involves entering and concealing oneself within a particular space.

زَرِبَ الْمَاءُ

A verb of this form is also recorded for flowing water.

زَرِبَ الْمَاءُ

zariba al-māʾu

“The water flowed.”

زِرْب also denotes a watercourse.

This is distinct from the shelter branch. A possible association with water passing through a defined course can be considered, but without historical evidence it cannot establish the word’s origin.

زِرْبِيَّة and زَرْبِيَّة

These denote a carpet, rug, or furnishing spread for sitting and reclining.

Lexicons record different vowel patterns, including زُرْبِيَّة.

Some definitions specify a carpet with fine pile. Others are broader, encompassing carpets, rugs, and some cushions or supports.

The familiar plural is زَرَابِيّ.

This branch concerns an article of domestic comfort, not a fold or hiding place.

زَرَابِيّ

This is the plural of زربية and denotes carpets and spread furnishings in the Qur’an.

Lexical descriptions include soft carpets, piled furnishings, and rugs for sitting.

زرابي is the only Qur’anic form discussed under this lexical family.

أَزْرَبَ النَّبْتُ and اِزْرَبَّ

Lexicons record a form for vegetation turning yellow or red while some green remains.

This condition is also described as زَرَابِيُّ النَّبْتِ.

The mixture of plant colors is prominent here.

Some lexical explanations compare this colorful vegetation to patterned carpets.

The resemblance does not prove that carpets were named after plants or, conversely, that the plant expression arose from carpets.

This usage helps explain the semantic range and imagery but should not be presented as a settled history of derivation.

3. Qur’anic usage

زرابي, discussed under ز ر ب in Arabic lexicons, occurs once in the Qur’an, in Al-Ghāshiyah 88:16. This lexical grouping does not establish its historical derivation.

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:16

وَزَرَابِيُّ مَبْثُوثَةٌ

wa-zarābiyyu mabthūthah

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:16

Translation:

“And carpets spread out in various places.”

زَرَابِيُّ is a broken plural of زربية in the nominative case, not a collective noun in the technical grammatical sense.

مَبْثُوثَةٌ describes it.

زرابي names carpets and furnishings; مبثوثة describes their being spread out and distributed.

The two contributions should therefore be distinguished:

زَرَابِيّ names the furnishings.

مَبْثُوثَة comes from ب ث ث and concerns scattering or spreading in different places.

Being distributed is therefore not part of the lexical meaning of ز ر ب itself.

Context

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:8 describes faces enjoying delight that day.

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:10 places them in an elevated Garden.

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:12 mentions a flowing spring.

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:13, Al-Ghāshiyah 88:14, Al-Ghāshiyah 88:15, and Al-Ghāshiyah 88:16 then describe furnishings for comfort and hospitality: raised couches, drinking vessels, cushions, and carpets.

Together they depict comfort and a prepared setting.

In this context, زرابي are furnishings provided for sitting, resting, and adorning the place.

A carpet or rug may lie on the ground or a seating surface, but the verse does not specify its exact material or construction.

“Carpets spread out in various places” conveys the central meaning without adding silk, a particular color, thickness, or size.

The relationship between زرابي and مبثوثة

مبثوثة is a passive participle from ب ث ث.

بث concerns scattering and spreading.

Here the description indicates carpets spread and provided at different points within the setting.

Contextually, this agrees with furnishings being ready for comfort.

It does not imply that the carpets have been piled up haphazardly.

Distribution need not mean disorder: objects may be placed throughout a setting, each in an appropriate place.

Nor does the phrase specify the number of carpets. Plurality and distribution indicate multiplicity and extent, not an exact count.

4. Comparison with other furnishings

ز ر ب (زَرَابِيّ) and ن م ر ق (نَمَارِق)

Al-Ghāshiyah 88:15 mentions نمارق before زرابي.

نمارق is the plural of نمرقة, used for cushions and small supports.

زرابي, by contrast, denotes carpets and spread furnishings.

The two may be used together in one gathering, but their usual functions differ.

A cushion primarily supports a reclining person or rests beneath the body; a carpet provides a surface for sitting and comfort.

Some broader definitions of زرابي include supports and seating furnishings, so the terms can overlap.

Nevertheless, their separate mention in Al-Ghāshiyah favors “cushions” for نمارق and “carpets” for زرابي.

مَصْفُوفَة and مَبْثُوثَة

In Al-Ghāshiyah 88:15, the cushions are مَصْفُوفَة: arranged side by side.

In Al-Ghāshiyah 88:16, the carpets are مَبْثُوثَة: spread out and distributed.

This distinction contributes to the imagery.

The cushions are described as arranged, and the carpets as spread throughout the setting.

This does not mean that cushions can never be distributed or carpets can never be arranged neatly.

Each feature is emphasized in this particular image and should not become an absolute lexical rule.

ز ر ب (زَرَابِيّ) and ف ر ش (فَرْش)

ف ر ش concerns spreading and preparing a surface for sitting or sleeping.

فراش can denote bedding or something spread for use.

Qur’anic زرابي names a type of carpet or furnishing.

“Carpets” therefore suits زرابي, but it does not contain the entire semantic range of ف ر ش.

For example, the verb فرش can express spreading, whereas زرابي here names the object spread.

The action of spreading is conveyed by مبثوثة, not by the plural زرابي alone.

ز ر ب (زَرَابِيّ) and ع ب ق ر (عَبْقَرِيّ)

عبقري also describes fine furnishings in the Qur’an.

Al-Raḥmān 55:76 speaks of reclining upon green rafraf and beautiful ʿabqarī.

Lexical descriptions associate عبقري with fine, patterned textiles and carpets.

It therefore overlaps with زرابي in furnishings for comfort and adornment.

The words belong to different lexical families, however, and one word’s features cannot be transferred to the other without evidence.

Both may describe the Garden’s blessings, but a shared subject does not make their lexical meanings identical.

5. Zarābī in the imagery of the Garden

In Al-Ghāshiyah 88:16, زرابي forms part of a setting furnished for comfort and enjoyment.

The Qur’an uses objects recognizable from earthly life: seating, drinking vessels, cushions, and carpets.

These words make the image intelligible.

Understanding an earthly word does not mean knowing every quality of a blessing in the Hereafter.

Al-Sajdah 32:17 states that no soul knows what delights have been kept hidden for them.

This indicates the limits of human knowledge concerning such blessings beyond the descriptions provided.

زرابي in Al-Ghāshiyah therefore means carpets and furnishings; their ultimate material, construction, color, and qualities cannot be determined conclusively from earthly carpets.

Conversely, uncertainty about the Hereafter’s qualities does not justify discarding the clear lexical meaning and making زرابي entirely abstract.

The Qur’an speaks of carpets, and that linguistic meaning should remain in translation.

Reflection on the Garden’s comfort and blessings is distinct from defining the word.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

The following equivalents suit the main forms. They do not replace installed verse translations or existing authored explanations.

زَرْب: fold, protected enclosure, or livestock shelter.

زِرْب: fold or an entrance and place for keeping animals, in the relevant usage.

زَرِيبَة: fold, animal shelter, or hunter’s hiding place.

زَرَبَ الْغَنَمَ: he brought the sheep into the fold.

اِنْزَرَبَ: entered a shelter or hiding place.

زَرِبَ الْمَاءُ: the water flowed.

زِرْبِيَّة / زَرْبِيَّة: carpet, rug, or furnishing spread for sitting.

زَرَابِيّ: carpets or spread furnishings.

A proposed rendering of Al-Ghāshiyah 88:16 is:

“And carpets spread out in various places.”

A brief root definition could read:

“A protected place; in the carpet branch, rugs and spread furnishings.”

For the verse-specific meaning:

“Carpets and furnishings provided for comfort.”

This distinction matters: defining the entire family by an assumed meaning such as “enclosing” or “covering” would subordinate the established meaning of زرابي to an unproven derivation.

Nor should “distributed” be treated as part of زرابي itself; that feature comes from مبثوثة.

7. Conclusion

The Arabic family ز ر ب has several semantic branches. زرب and زريبة denote a fold, shelter, or protected place. In another usage, زربية and زرابي name carpets and furnishings for sitting and comfort.

Lexicons also record flowing water and descriptions of mixed plant colors.

These senses are grouped lexically, but a common historical origin for all of them has not been established.

Early Arabic evidence and the Qur’anic usage establish the carpet meaning, but without datable evidence this study cannot identify precisely when it circulated before Islam or how it was first named.

The Qur’an contains a single occurrence of زَرَابِيّ, in Al-Ghāshiyah 88:16.

It denotes carpets and furnishings provided in the Garden, while مبثوثة describes their being spread out and distributed.

The appropriate meaning of زرابي here is carpets and spread furnishings.

This fits the preceding furnishings and requires no imposed theory connecting carpets historically with folds, shelters, or plant colors.

A precise account should preserve the lexical range while explaining each branch through its own evidence and distinguishing established usage from proposed derivation.
