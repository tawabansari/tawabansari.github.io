1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ع ف و appears in the Qur'an in two prominent semantic branches.

First, pardon for fault and sin: passing over the pursuit of a fault, censure, punishment, or the demand of a right.

Second, increase and surplus: something increases until it is more than the needed amount or the original amount. The use of الْعَفْوَ in Al-Baqarah 2:219 belongs to this branch: what a person can spend in good after meeting his real needs and the needs of those for whom he is responsible.

In some Arabic uses, afw is also connected with the effacing of a trace, such as a mark removed from the surface of the earth. This image can help us understand pardon for sin: the fault really happened, but its pursuit and censure are left aside, and it is not allowed to imprison the relationship forever.

Even so, the root ع ف و should not be turned into a "waste-filtering system," "neutralization of negative charge," or "release of energy." These are modern expressions outside the language of the Qur'an.

The shortest statement of this root is:

to pass over pursuing and censuring a fault; and in another branch, surplus and what remains beyond need.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives

عَفَا / يَعْفُو

He pardoned, passed over, did not censure, or removed the trace of something.

In some contexts, it also points to increase and abundance.

عَفَوْنَا

We pardoned and did not censure.

عَفَا اللَّهُ عَنْكَ

The God pardoned you.

يَعْفُوا

That he pass over, refrain, or not demand his right.

وَلْيَعْفُوا

Let them pardon.

عَفْو

Pardon, passing over censure, or what is surplus and available to be given.

الْعَفْو

In Al-Baqarah 2:219, what is surplus beyond need and whose giving does not place a person in improper hardship.

عَفُوّ

One who pardons greatly; one who repeatedly passes over censure.

This form appears in the Qur'an as an attribute of The God.

عَافِينَ

Those who pardon.

حَتَّىٰ عَفَوْا

Until they increased, became abundant, or entered ease and plenty.

Morphologically, the main Qur'anic forms of this root appear as the verb afa, the noun afw, the attribute afuww, and the active participle afin.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The root ع ف و has two established branches that should not be forced into one another.

The first branch moves through these meanings: leaving a demand; passing over punishment; giving up a personal right; and removing or not pursuing the trace of a fault.

The second branch moves through these meanings: surplus, becoming abundant, and something remaining beyond the needed amount.

One can see a possible semantic nearness between the two branches: in both, something goes beyond the limit of obligation and need. In moral pardon, the holder of the right goes beyond applying his full right and does not demand it. In financial afw, wealth has gone beyond the measure of need and a portion remains to be given.

But this connection should be presented as a possible explanation, not as a definitive historical definition of the root.

The better base definition preserves both branches independently:

Afw is either passing over censure, or the amount of surplus that remains after need.

5. The Material Image of the Root

One known image of this root is a trace that is effaced from the earth. Wind blows and the footprint or mark left on the soil becomes faint or disappears.

This image is useful for understanding moral pardon, but it must be used carefully. Afw does not mean denying that the fault occurred. The footprint truly existed; afw means that this trace is not made forever into the basis of pursuit, revenge, and continuing hostility.

Another image is a plant that becomes abundant and dense. This image belongs to the branch of increase, and in:

حَتَّىٰ عَفَوْا

Until they increased and became abundant.

Al-Araf 7:95

it comes close to increase and abundance.

In the financial context, the clear image is this:

A person measures his real needs and responsibilities. What remains after those needs, and whose giving does not collapse life or waste the rights of dependents, is afw.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, عَفَا عَنْهُ means he pardoned him and did not censure him for his fault.

عَفَا الأَثَرُ can point to the effacing and disappearance of a trace.

عَفَا الشَّيْءُ in some uses comes close to increasing, becoming surplus, or becoming abundant.

العَفْو can indicate pardon, ease, and what is surplus beyond need.

Therefore, the use of abundance should not be treated as an artificial metaphor from moral pardon, nor should moral pardon be reduced to abundance. Both are present in the historical field of this root, and the context determines which meaning is intended.

7. History of Understanding, Misuse, or Semantic Restriction

One restriction is translating every derivative of ع ف و as "forgiving."

This translation is suitable in many verses, but in:

حَتَّىٰ عَفَوْا

Until they increased and became abundant.

Al-Araf 7:95

"they forgave" is not correct; the context speaks of increase and abundance.

In Al-Baqarah 2:219, al-afw also does not mean pardon from sin. It is the answer to the question about the amount to spend.

Another restriction is equating afw with forgetting a fault or ignoring wrongdoing. Afw can be a virtue, but the Qur'an connects it with repair:

فَمَنْ عَفَا وَأَصْلَحَ

So whoever pardons and repairs.

Ash-Shuraa 42:40

So afw must not become a tool for continuing oppression, silencing the harmed person, or allowing the wrongdoer to escape responsibility.

The opposite error is turning afw into a technical and non-human process. The Qur'an speaks of will, right, mercy, repair, and relationship; not of filters, electric charge, or automatic network cleansing.

8. Methodological Note: Do All Uses of Afw Have One Meaning?

In studying this root, three uses must be distinguished.

First, pardon from fault:

عَفَا اللَّهُ عَنْهَا

Second, a human being passing over his own right:

وَلْيَعْفُوا وَلْيَصْفَحُوا

Third, abundance and surplus beyond need:

حَتَّىٰ عَفَوْا

قُلِ الْعَفْوَ

The definition of one should not be imposed on all cases.

Also, afw does not always mean the complete removal of all natural consequences of an act. A person may pass over personal punishment, while compensation, repair of behavior, or return of a right may still remain.

Passing over censure is not the same as making every consequence of the action vanish.

9. A Short Look at Related Semitic Languages

Determining precise relations between ع ف و and other Semitic languages requires specialized evidence.

In this article, Arabic and Qur'anic usage is sufficient for identifying the semantic field: pardon and leaving censure; effacing a trace; and abundance or surplus.

Without clear evidence, this root should not be connected to a theory of "a system returning to saturation" or "the release of energy flow."

10. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

A. Divine pardon after the fault of Bani Isra'il

ثُمَّ عَفَوْنَا عَنْكُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

Then We pardoned you after that, so that you might be grateful.

Al-Baqarah 2:52

The verse comes after mention of Bani Isra'il's calf-worship.

Afw here does not mean denying the fault. The fault is named and its responsibility is clear, but the door of return and continued guidance is not closed.

The purpose of afw is also stated:

لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

So that you might be grateful.

Al-Baqarah 2:52

So afw is an opportunity for repair and gratitude, not permission to repeat the fault.

B. Pardon after the slip at Uhud

وَلَقَدْ عَفَا عَنْكُمْ

And He certainly pardoned you.

Ali Imran 3:152

This verse comes after mention of dispute, disobedience, and weakness among a group of the believers at Uhud.

Divine pardon does not turn their error into correct action. The verse still explains the fault, but it does not impose final censure upon them and it opens the way for rebuilding the community.

C. Pardon of the Prophet

عَفَا اللَّهُ عَنْكَ لِمَ أَذِنْتَ لَهُمْ

The God pardoned you; why did you permit them before the truthful became clear to you?

At-Tawbah 9:43

Pardon comes before the critical question.

This structure shows that afw does not contradict instruction and repair. Censure may be passed over, while the behavior is still examined.

D. Afw in social relations

وَلْيَعْفُوا وَلْيَصْفَحُوا أَلَا تُحِبُّونَ أَنْ يَغْفِرَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ

Let them pardon and pass over; do you not love that The God should forgive you?

An-Nur 24:22

The verse comes in the context of social and personal harm.

Walyafu commands passing over the right of revenge and the continuation of hostility.

Walyasfahu is a close but distinct stage: to pass beyond the page of the fault and not make the relationship revolve only around it.

The verse connects human pardon of others with the human hope for divine forgiveness.

E. Afw together with repair

وَجَزَاءُ سَيِّئَةٍ سَيِّئَةٌ مِثْلُهَا فَمَنْ عَفَا وَأَصْلَحَ فَأَجْرُهُ عَلَى اللَّهِ

The recompense of a bad act is a bad act like it; so whoever pardons and repairs, his reward is upon The God.

Ash-Shuraa 42:40

The verse first recognizes the right of proportional response, then highlights afw when it is joined with repair.

This condition is very important:

عَفَا وَأَصْلَحَ

He pardoned and repaired.

Ash-Shuraa 42:40

Therefore, not every abandonment of punishment or silence is desirable Qur'anic afw. If pardon strengthens the oppressor, continues harm, or destroys a right, the element of repair becomes a serious question.

F. Afw in qisas

فَمَنْ عُفِيَ لَهُ مِنْ أَخِيهِ شَيْءٌ فَاتِّبَاعٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَأَدَاءٌ إِلَيْهِ بِإِحْسَانٍ

So whoever has something pardoned for him by his brother, then there is following up in what is recognized and payment to him with excellence.

Al-Baqarah 2:178

Afw in this verse does not necessarily mean the disappearance of all responsibilities.

The guardian of the slain person may pass over qisas, while payment and excellent conduct still remain.

This verse is one of the clearest witnesses that afw is not the same as lack of responsibility.

G. Afw in financial rights between husband and wife

إِلَّا أَنْ يَعْفُونَ أَوْ يَعْفُوَ الَّذِي بِيَدِهِ عُقْدَةُ النِّكَاحِ

Unless they pardon, or the one in whose hand is the bond of marriage pardons.

Al-Baqarah 2:237

Afw here is passing over a financial right.

The one who has the right may choose not to demand all or part of it.

This use shows that the core of afw is not only an inner feeling; it can be the leaving of a real and enforceable demand.

H. Those who pardon people

وَالْكَاظِمِينَ الْغَيْظَ وَالْعَافِينَ عَنِ النَّاسِ

And those who restrain anger and pardon people.

Ali Imran 3:134

Restraining anger and afw are two distinct stages.

A person may control his anger but still not have passed over the right of retaliation.

Afw is the stage in which a person passes over pursuing another's fault, especially where pardon is closer to repair.

I. “Al-afw” in spending

وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ مَاذَا يُنْفِقُونَ قُلِ الْعَفْوَ

And they ask you what they should spend; say: al-afw.

Al-Baqarah 2:219

Al-afw in this verse is the object of the answer to the question, "What should they spend?" Its linguistic analysis brings it close to surplus, excess, and what is beyond need.

The meaning of the verse can be expressed as:

Spend from what remains after real needs and necessary responsibilities.

This reading has several important results.

First, the Qur'an does not state the amount of spending in this verse as a fixed percentage.

Second, a person should not, through spending, place himself and his dependents into improper deprivation and need.

Third, "need" should not become an excuse for endless accumulation. There is a difference between real need, reasonable security, future responsibility, and greed.

Fourth, financial afw is the portion that a person has the ability to release from personal ownership and can free for society, the needy, and public good.

This verse is about spending. Its connection with zakat matters because it shows that Qur'anic financial responsibility is connected with ability, need, surplus, and the prevention of hardship. But a root article should not extract the whole structure of zakat from this one verse alone.

J. Becoming abundant after hardship

ثُمَّ بَدَّلْنَا مَكَانَ السَّيِّئَةِ الْحَسَنَةَ حَتَّىٰ عَفَوْا

Then We replaced the hardship with good until they increased and became abundant.

Al-Araf 7:95

Here afaw does not mean "they forgave."

The context speaks of hardship being replaced by ease, then increase in population, wealth, or prosperity.

This verse is a clear witness for the branch of increase in the root.

The problem of the people was not that they became abundant. The problem was that they did not take lesson from the change in conditions and said:

Hardship and ease also touched our fathers.

They did not see abundance as a sign calling for responsibility and reflection.

K. Afw in unintended and past rulings

عَفَا اللَّهُ عَمَّا سَلَفَ

The God pardoned what had already passed.

Al-Maidah 5:95

Afw in this verse relates to a past before the ruling has now become clear.

This structure shows that censure may be lifted from the stage before the clear statement of the ruling, while after that a new responsibility begins.

11. Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

عَفَا عَنْ

He pardoned someone or something and did not make it the object of censure and pursuit.

The particle an identifies what censure has been moved away from.

عَفْو

The noun of this act: pardon, leaving a demand, or in a financial context, surplus beyond need.

عَافِين

A plural active participle meaning those who pardon.

عَفُوّ

An intensive form meaning much-pardoning.

عَفَوْا

Depending on context, it may mean "they pardoned" or "they became abundant."

This difference shows that translation must come from the sentence and subject of the verse, not from one fixed equivalent.

12. The Divine Attribute: AL-AFUWW

AL-AFUWW is one of the attributes of The God.

This attribute indicates the abundance and continuity of pardon: He can pass over censure, not impose the burden of sin upon the human being, and open the path of return.

But divine afw must not be turned into an "automatic error-deletion system."

His afw is conscious, joined with justice and wisdom, may be connected with repentance, repair, and moral conditions, and does not arise from inability to punish.

In:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَفُوًّا غَفُورًا

Indeed, The God is much-pardoning, much-forgiving.

An-Nisa 4:99

Afuww and Ghafur come together. This pairing shows that the two words are close, but not identical.

Afw emphasizes passing over censure and setting aside the pursuit of a fault.

Maghfirah is more connected with covering, protecting, and guarding from the consequences of sin.

Even so, one should not construct between them a completely mechanical and absolute boundary.

13. Grammatical and Structural Notes

عَفَا عَنْهُ

The particle an after the verb afa identifies the person or fault from which censure is set aside:

عَفَا اللَّهُ عَنْكَ

The meaning of an is not "draining contamination from an input channel"; its natural meaning is that censure withdraws from the person or matter in question.

وَلْيَعْفُوا

The lam indicates command and encouragement: let them pardon.

الْعَافِينَ عَنِ النَّاسِ

Al-afin is a plural active participle, and an al-nas identifies the people whose faults are pardoned.

قُلِ الْعَفْوَ

Al-afw is accusative and is the direct answer to the question:

مَاذَا يُنْفِقُونَ

That is: What should they spend? What is surplus beyond need.

حَتَّىٰ عَفَوْا

The context before and after the verb shows that its meaning is increase and abundance, not moral pardon.

14. AYN F W Compared with S F H

Afw and safh come together in An-Nur 24:22:

وَلْيَعْفُوا وَلْيَصْفَحُوا

Let them pardon and pass over.

An-Nur 24:22

Afw emphasizes leaving censure, revenge, or the demand of a right.

Safh is connected with turning from the page of the fault, not constantly facing it again, and not continuing blame.

One may cautiously say:

Afw: passing over the right of censure.

Safh: not making the fault the permanent center of the relationship.

A person may refrain from punishment yet constantly recall the fault and poison the relationship with it. In such a case, a visible afw has occurred, but safh has not been completed.

This difference is directional and must not be turned into an absolute formula.

15. AYN F W Compared with GH F R

Afw and maghfirah are very close and appear together in several verses.

Afw emphasizes passing over censure, leaving a demand, and refraining from applying the full legal or moral consequence.

Maghfirah has a stronger connection with covering, protecting, and shielding from the consequences of fault.

One may say:

Afw: not pursuing the fault for censure.

Maghfirah: covering the fault and protecting the human being from its destructive consequence.

But one should not say that afw is "zeroing a charge" and ghafr is "hardware covering." Such expressions move away from the language of the Qur'an.

16. AYN F W Compared with T W B

Tawbah is the return of the wrongdoer from the wrong path.

Afw is the passing over of censure by the holder of the right or by The God.

So:

Tawbah: the wrongdoer's movement toward return.

Afw: passing over the pursuit and punishment of the fault.

Tawbah and afw can meet one another, but they are not the same.

A human being may repent and hope for afw. The God may also, by His grace, pass over some faults.

17. Specialized Note: “QULI AL-AFWA” and Financial Responsibility

Al-Baqarah 2:219 is one of the most important uses of this root in the subject of Qur'anic moral economy:

وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ مَاذَا يُنْفِقُونَ قُلِ الْعَفْوَ

And they ask you what they should spend; say: al-afw.

Al-Baqarah 2:219

The question concerns what, or how much of wealth, should be spent.

The answer is not a fixed number or universal percentage:

الْعَفْوَ

This answer invites the human being to measure: his real needs, the needs of family and dependents, debts and definite responsibilities, reasonable security of life, and what remains after these matters.

What remains after need is the field of spending.

This meaning is consistent with justice, because the human being is not commanded to give wealth in a way that throws himself or his dependents into poverty and dependence.

At the same time, "need" is a flexible word and can become an excuse for greed. A person may call every level of luxury, hoarding, and endless security a need and never reach afw.

Therefore, recognizing afw requires honesty. Is this wealth necessary for a decent life and real responsibility? Or is it being held only from fear, competition, and the desire to accumulate? Is another human need more urgent and fundamental? Is leaving all surplus with one person compatible with purification and social responsibility?

From this angle, al-afw is not merely "unimportant extra money." It is that portion of wealth which, after justly meeting need, a person can release from personal monopoly.

This verse also shows that Qur'anic financial responsibility must consider a person's real ability and condition. A poor person, a debtor, or someone responsible for the essential needs of a family is not in the same condition as a wealthy person whose wealth is abundant and not needed for necessary use.

The connection of this verse with zakat and tazkiyah matters from this angle:

Giving afw reduces deprivation in society and also purifies the human being from hoarding, miserliness, and attachment to wealth.

But one must distinguish between the direct text and a conceptual result. The verse does not use the word "zakat" or "sadaqah" in this sentence; it speaks of spending. Therefore, this verse is one of the Qur'anic foundations for financial responsibility and giving surplus, but the entire structure of zakat must be studied through the full set of related verses.

18. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

عَفَا عَنْهُ

He pardoned him. He passed over censuring him. He did not pursue his fault.

عَفْو

Afw. Pardon. Passing over censure or the demand of a right.

الْعَفْو in Al-Baqarah 2:219

What is surplus beyond need. What is more than real needs. What remains after need.

"Neutralization of waste" is not a translation of the word.

عَفُوّ

Much-pardoning. Much-passing-over.

"Discharge filter" is not a translation and turns the divine attribute into a will-less mechanism.

عَفَوْا in Al-Araf 7:95

They became abundant. They increased. They grew in wealth and numbers, according to context.

عَافِينَ عَنِ النَّاسِ

Those who pardon people. Those who pass over people's faults.

وَلْيَعْفُوا وَلْيَصْفَحُوا

Let them pardon and pass over.

19. Conclusion

The root ع ف و has two important branches in the Qur'an.

The first branch is passing over censure, punishment, or the demand of a right.

The second branch is increase and surplus: something that has become more than need or the previous amount.

These two branches should not be turned, through technical and machine-like language, into a single "cleansing system."

Moral afw does not mean denying the fault. The fault occurred, but the holder of the right passes over full pursuit, revenge, or punishment.

Afw is also not the same as lack of responsibility. In qisas, qisas may be pardoned while payment and excellent conduct remain. In Ash-Shuraa 42:40, afw is connected with repair.

Afw and safh are close, but not identical. Afw is more about leaving censure, while safh is more about not continuing blame and passing beyond the page of the fault.

Afw and maghfirah also overlap. Afw emphasizes passing over pursuing the fault, while maghfirah emphasizes covering and protecting from its consequences.

AL-AFUWW is an attribute of The God, meaning He is much-pardoning. His afw is conscious, wise, and joined with justice, not an automatic and will-less process.

In:

حَتَّىٰ عَفَوْا

Until they increased and became abundant.

Al-Araf 7:95

the root means becoming abundant and increasing.

In:

قُلِ الْعَفْوَ

Say: al-afw.

Al-Baqarah 2:219

afw points to what remains beyond need. The verse does not set a fixed percentage for the amount of spending, but invites the human being to measure real need, responsibility, and surplus.

From this angle, afw in Qur'anic economy is what remains after justly meeting need and can be given to the needy and public good.

The shortest statement of the core of this root is:

passing over censure and demand regarding a fault; and in another branch, surplus and what remains after need.
