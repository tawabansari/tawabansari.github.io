The Arabic root و ذ ر concerns leaving, relinquishing, and desisting from something. This need not involve physical departure: someone may cease an activity, leave something in place, allow another to continue, or leave a person in their existing condition.

Its Quranic contexts vary widely: relinquishing riba, setting aside trade when salat is called, leaving harvested grain in its ears, not neglecting a wife's rights, leaving opponents alone, and Zechariah's prayer not to be left alone. The root occurs forty-five times in forty-three verses.

This breadth shows that the root is not intrinsically positive or negative. Abandoning injustice can be good, while abandoning a right or leaving someone in injustice can be blameworthy. The object, circumstances, and context determine the act's value.

1. The root in early Arabic

In early Arabic, the principal branch concerns leaving and letting be. Asking someone to leave one alone means asking them to stop interfering, restraining, or pursuing.

A clear witness appears in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic poet ʿUrwah ibn al-Ward:

ذَرِينِي وَنَفْسِي أُمَّ حَسَّانَ إِنَّنِي

dharīnī wa-nafsī umma ḥassāna innanī

بِهَا قَبْلَ أَنْ لَا أَمْلِكَ الْبَيْعَ مُشْتَرِي

bihā qabla an lā amlika al-bayʿa mushtarī

“Leave me to myself, Umm Ḥassān; before I lose the power to bargain, I seek to gain something worthwhile with my life.”

The following lines speak of gaining a name and memory that outlast death. He wishes to strive and take risks while alive, without his wife preventing him.

Another line in the same ode reads:

ذَرِينِي أُطَوِّفْ فِي الْبِلَادِ لَعَلَّنِي

dharīnī uṭawwif fī al-bilādi laʿallanī

أُخَلِّيكِ أَوْ أُغْنِيكِ عَنْ سُوءِ مَحْضَرِي

ukhallīki aw ughnīki ʿan sūʾi maḥḍarī

“Let me roam the lands; perhaps I shall leave you alone or free you from the hardship of my presence.”

ذَرِينِي again asks to be left alone. The poet does not want his wife to prevent him from continuing his course.

It does not mean permanent separation from or hostility toward her. He still addresses her and considers his own and his family's future. He asks that an obstacle to his decision and movement be removed.

Arabic poetry sources attribute the ode to ʿUrwah ibn al-Ward, a poet recognized as pre-Islamic. It witnesses the use of ذَرِينِي in language attributed to that period, although the poet's dates alone cannot establish the precise composition and transmission dates of each line.

The witness shows that ذَرْ could mean ceasing interference and leaving a person alone, without necessarily departing from them physically.

Another dictionary branch

Arabic dictionaries also record وَذْرَة under this entry, meaning a piece of meat.

وَذَّرَ اللَّحْمَ means cutting meat into pieces, and وَذَّرَ الْجُرْحَ is reported for incising a wound.

These uses differ from the Quranic meaning of leaving.

A conceptual link between cutting off a piece and separating something can be imagined, but that analogy does not prove that leaving historically developed from cutting meat.

This study has not established an independently datable pre-Quranic witness for this branch. The meaning recorded in classical dictionaries should therefore be distinguished from the leaving sense attested in the cited early poetry.

2. Forms and morphological characteristics

A feature of this root is its limited range of commonly used verbal forms.

Arabic linguistic sources describe the leaving verb as occurring chiefly in the imperfect and imperative, unlike many other verbs.

يَذَرُ: he leaves, relinquishes, or leaves behind.

تَذَرُ: you leave, or she leaves, depending on the subject and construction.

نَذَرُ: we leave or leave behind.

ذَرْ: leave, relinquish, or desist.

ذَرُوا: leave, relinquish, or desist, addressed to a group.

ذَرْنِي: leave me or leave me alone.

ذَرْهُمْ: leave them or let them be.

ذَرُوهُ: leave it or keep it in place, addressed to a group.

ذَرُوهَا: leave her or it alone, addressed to a group.

These forms occur in the Quran. Their differences chiefly concern person, number, attached pronouns, and sentence structure.

Why does ذَرْ not begin with wāw?

The triliteral root is و ذ ر, but its imperative is written ذَرْ.

The initial wāw is omitted in the imperfect and imperative, as happens with some other Arabic verbs beginning with wāw.

ذَرْ is consequently an imperative from و ذ ر, rather than a word with an independent two-letter root.

The imperative may have a connecting particle, as in فَذَرْهُمْ, or a plural ending, as in وَذَرُوا.

The attached فَـ and وَ are connecting particles, not root letters.

Why does its perfect not occur in the Quran?

Classical Arabic mainly uses يَذَرُ and ذَرْ for leaving; the perfect and verbal noun are described as rare or unused.

Arabic employs تَرَكَ for the past and تَرْك as the verbal noun in such circumstances.

For example, familiar usage includes:

ذَرْهُ: leave him.

يَذَرُهُ: he leaves him.

For the past, تَرَكَهُ, “he left him,” is the more familiar form.

Some dictionaries also report rare past forms. A form's rarity should not be equated with absolute impossibility throughout Arabic's history.

The Quran uses this root only as a form I verb in imperfect and imperative constructions. It contains no perfect, verbal noun, or active participle of this branch.

Their absence does not justify inventing a certain, unproven rhetorical purpose for the Quranic selection. The restriction also accords with the verb's recognized linguistic usage.

Semantic core

A defensible core for the Quranic branch is:

Leaving a person, thing, or activity by ceasing action, pursuit, interference, or active keeping, or leaving something in its place and condition.

This covers several uses:

Desisting from an activity, such as riba.

Temporarily setting an activity aside, such as trade when salat is called.

Leaving something in its existing condition, such as grain in its ears.

Leaving someone alone, as requested by ذَرْنِي.

Leaving someone in a condition, such as bewilderment or punishment.

These uses are not identical. The verb's object, prepositions, and accompanying clauses determine its precise meaning.

3. Leaving activities, property, and responsibilities

Al-Baqarah 2:278 — Relinquishing outstanding riba

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَذَرُوا مَا بَقِيَ مِنَ الرِّبَا إِنْ كُنْتُمْ مُؤْمِنِينَ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū ittaqū allāha wa-dharū mā baqiya mina al-ribā in kuntum muʾminīn

Al-Baqarah 2:278

“You who have faith, be mindful of The God and relinquish what remains of riba, if you are believers.”

ذَرُوا is a plural imperative.

Its object is مَا بَقِيَ مِنَ الرِّبَا: what remains of riba.

The command does not merely prohibit starting something new. It asks the addressees to relinquish something already present and outstanding in their financial relationships.

Here ذَرُوا concerns abandoning a claim or continuation of a practice the Quran calls riba.

The root itself does not define riba. Its nature, its distinction from trade, and the limits of financial claims must be studied through the relevant verses.

Al-Baqarah 2:279 separately addresses the principal and avoiding injustice.

The command therefore concerns outstanding riba; the word alone does not require surrendering the entire principal or every financial right.

This accords with the historical meaning of leaving: something previously present and pursued must be set aside.

Al-Anʿām 6:120 — Leaving outward and inward sin

وَذَرُوا ظَاهِرَ الْإِثْمِ وَبَاطِنَهُ ۚ إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَكْسِبُونَ الْإِثْمَ سَيُجْزَوْنَ بِمَا كَانُوا يَقْتَرِفُونَ

wa-dharū ẓāhira al-ithmi wa-bāṭinahu inna alladhīna yaksibūna al-ithma sa-yujzawna bi-mā kānū yaqtarifūn

Al-Anʿām 6:120

“Leave the outward and inward aspects of sin. Those who acquire sin will be recompensed for what they used to commit.”

ذَرُوا is again a plural imperative, but its object differs from the riba verse.

Here the object is ظَاهِرَ الْإِثْمِ وَبَاطِنَهُ.

The command encompasses outward conduct and sin's hidden aspect.

ذَرُوا itself does not mean outward and inward; that scope comes from the descriptions following it.

Here leaving means desisting from sin. Unlike passages in which ذَرْ allows conduct to continue, this command requires ending it.

Al-Jumuʿah 62:9 — Setting aside trade

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا نُودِيَ لِلصَّلَاةِ مِنْ يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ فَاسْعَوْا إِلَىٰ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَذَرُوا الْبَيْعَ ۚ ذَٰلِكُمْ خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū idhā nūdiya li-al-ṣalāti min yawmi al-jumuʿati fa-isʿaw ilā dhikri allāhi wa-dharū al-bayʿa dhālikum khayrun lakum in kuntum taʿlamūn

Al-Jumuʿah 62:9

“You who have faith, when the call to salat is made on Friday, hasten toward remembrance of The God and leave trade. That is better for you, if you know.”

ذَرُوا again means leaving, but in a specified circumstance.

The command follows إِذَا نُودِيَ لِلصَّلَاةِ مِنْ يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ.

The verb alone does not imply abandoning trade forever.

Al-Jumuʿah 62:10 directs people to disperse through the land and seek The God's bounty after salat ends.

Together, the verses illustrate temporary cessation of an activity under particular conditions.

Permanence or temporariness is not fixed in the root; it comes from the command's circumstances and accompanying verses.

Yūsuf 12:47 — Leaving grain in its ears

قَالَ تَزْرَعُونَ سَبْعَ سِنِينَ دَأَبًا فَمَا حَصَدْتُمْ فَذَرُوهُ فِي سُنْبُلِهِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِمَّا تَأْكُلُونَ

qāla tazraʿūna sabʿa sinīna daʾaban fa-mā ḥaṣadtum fa-dharūhu fī sunbulihi illā qalīlan mimmā taʾkulūn

Yūsuf 12:47

“He said: You will cultivate for seven years continuously. Leave what you harvest in its ears, except a little of which you eat.”

This is a particularly clear example of the root's range.

فَذَرُوهُ does not mean abandoning or neglecting the harvest.

Joseph advises leaving it in its ears, removing only a small amount for food.

The advice prepares for the difficult years ahead.

The meaning is leaving something in its condition, rather than abandoning responsibility for it.

This prevents defining the root exclusively as severing a relationship or relinquishing something completely.

Leaving can mean not disturbing something so that it remains as it is.

Al-Baqarah 2:234 and Al-Baqarah 2:240 — Leaving spouses behind at death

Both verses contain يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنْكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا.

That is, those among you who die and leave spouses behind.

يَذَرُونَ does not mean voluntarily abandoning spouses.

Death ends the shared life, while the surviving spouse remains alive.

The construction expresses leaving someone behind after one's departure.

It does not establish that the deceased consciously chose to leave their spouse or acted irresponsibly toward them.

Some uses of leaving describe an event's consequence rather than a voluntary decision.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:129 — Leaving a wife suspended

وَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعُوا أَنْ تَعْدِلُوا بَيْنَ النِّسَاءِ وَلَوْ حَرَصْتُمْ ۖ فَلَا تَمِيلُوا كُلَّ الْمَيْلِ فَتَذَرُوهَا كَالْمُعَلَّقَةِ ۚ وَإِنْ تُصْلِحُوا وَتَتَّقُوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ غَفُورًا رَحِيمًا

wa-lan tastaṭīʿū an taʿdilū bayna al-nisāʾi wa-law ḥaraṣtum fa-lā tamīlū kulla al-mayli fa-tadharūhā ka-al-muʿallaqati wa-in tuṣliḥū wa-tattaqū fa-inna allāha kāna ghafūran raḥīmā

Al-Nisāʾ 4:129

“You will not be able to maintain justice between wives, however keen you are. Do not incline entirely to one side, leaving her as though suspended. If you make amends and remain mindful, The God is forgiving and merciful.”

فَتَذَرُوهَا belongs to the same root.

Unlike preserving grain in Joseph's story, leaving a wife in an unresolved situation is condemned here.

كَالْمُعَلَّقَةِ specifies her condition.

The root itself does not mean injustice or uncertainty. Those arise from the behavior and description in the verse.

Leaving here does not necessarily mean divorce or complete separation. The formal relationship may remain while the woman is deprived of her rights and necessary care.

Leaving may end a relationship, or it may leave someone in a situation where responsibilities toward them are not properly fulfilled.

4. Letting be and allowing an activity to continue

Al-Aʿrāf 7:73 and Hūd 11:64 — Leaving Ṣāliḥ's she-camel free to graze

Ṣāliḥ's people are commanded to leave The God's she-camel alone to eat on His earth.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:73 says:

فَذَرُوهَا تَأْكُلْ فِي أَرْضِ اللَّهِ

fa-dharūhā taʾkul fī arḍi allāh

Al-Aʿrāf 7:73; Hūd 11:64

Hūd 11:64 repeats the command.

ذَرُوهَا does not mean relinquishing ownership or physically departing from the animal; it directs people not to interfere with or obstruct its activity.

The she-camel must be allowed to eat, and people must not harm it.

This resembles ʿUrwah's poetic request that his wife let him pursue his course.

The verb does not itself mean unrestricted freedom. Other commands in the same verses define the people's limits of conduct.

Requests with ذَرُونِي and ذَرْنَا

In Ghāfir 40:26, Pharaoh says:

ذَرُونِي أَقْتُلْ مُوسَىٰ

dharūnī aqtul mūsā

Ghāfir 40:26

“Let me kill Moses.”

ذَرُونِي asks others to step aside and not obstruct Pharaoh's decision.

The Quran reports Pharaoh's words without endorsing his wish.

In Al-Tawbah 9:86, a group says:

ذَرْنَا نَكُنْ مَعَ الْقَاعِدِينَ

dharnā nakun maʿa al-qāʿidīn

Al-Tawbah 9:86

“Let us be with those who stay behind.”

In Al-Fatḥ 48:15, a group says:

ذَرُونَا نَتَّبِعْكُمْ

dharūnā nattabiʿkum

Al-Fatḥ 48:15

“Let us follow you.”

All three requests ask for permission and the removal of an obstacle.

Their purposes nevertheless differ.

Pharaoh wants to kill Moses; one group wishes to remain behind; another wants to join those departing to obtain spoils.

The requests' moral character comes from their content, not from ذَرُونِي or ذَرْنَا itself.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:127 — Leaving in Pharaoh's chiefs' speech

Pharaoh's chiefs ask in Al-Aʿrāf 7:127:

أَتَذَرُ مُوسَىٰ وَقَوْمَهُ لِيُفْسِدُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ

a-tadharu mūsā wa-qawmahu li-yufsidū fī al-arḍ

Al-Aʿrāf 7:127

“Will you leave Moses and his people to cause corruption in the land?”

They continue:

وَيَذَرَكَ وَآلِهَتَكَ

wa-yadharaka wa-ālihatak

Al-Aʿrāf 7:127

“And leave you and your gods.”

Both occurrences belong to the speech of Moses' opponents.

They first ask Pharaoh not to leave Moses and his people alone, then speak of Pharaoh and his gods being abandoned.

The accusation of corruption is theirs. Its inclusion in the Quran does not endorse it.

Leaving the gods concerns abandoning worship and allegiance here, rather than necessarily moving physically away.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:70 similarly reports ʿĀd speaking of leaving the gods worshipped by their fathers.

In Al-Ṣāffāt 37:125, Elijah questions leaving the best of creators and calling upon Baal.

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:166 uses the root for leaving what The God created for the addressees of spouses.

What is left can therefore be a person, an object of worship, a relationship, or an activity.

5. ذَرْهُمْ and leaving people to their conduct's consequences

The Quran repeatedly directs Muhammad to leave groups of opponents alone.

For example, Al-Anʿām 6:91 says:

ثُمَّ ذَرْهُمْ فِي خَوْضِهِمْ يَلْعَبُونَ

thumma dharhum fī khawḍihim yalʿabūn

Al-Anʿām 6:91

“Then leave them playing in their vain discourse.”

Al-Anʿām 6:70 directs leaving those who have made their religion play and diversion.

Al-Anʿām 6:112 and Al-Anʿām 6:137 follow descriptions of misleading conduct with فَذَرْهُمْ.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:180 speaks of leaving those who deviate concerning The God's names.

ذَرْهُمْ can therefore mean ceasing engagement and leaving people to continue their chosen course.

That should not be confused with approving their conduct.

Al-Ḥijr 15:3 — Leaving until the outcome becomes known

ذَرْهُمْ يَأْكُلُوا وَيَتَمَتَّعُوا وَيُلْهِهِمُ الْأَمَلُ ۖ فَسَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ

dharhum yaʾkulū wa-yatamattaʿū wa-yulhihimu al-amalu fa-sawfa yaʿlamūn

Al-Ḥijr 15:3

“Leave them to eat and enjoy themselves, and let hope distract them; they will soon know.”

The imperative accompanies clauses describing their continuing conduct.

The verse ends with فَسَوْفَ يَعْلَمُونَ, indicating future knowledge of the outcome.

Leaving them does not mean approving their actions or treating them as inconsequential.

They remain on their course, but a consequence will later become clear to them.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:54 has the same structure: فَذَرْهُمْ فِي غَمْرَتِهِمْ حَتَّىٰ حِينٍ, leaving them in their engulfing condition for a time.

Al-Zukhruf 43:83 and Al-Maʿārij 70:42 similarly say فَذَرْهُمْ يَخُوضُوا وَيَلْعَبُوا.

Al-Ṭūr 52:45 repeats the command to leave them until they encounter the day described for them.

These related constructions show that leaving may accompany a respite and approaching consequences.

Respite is not intrinsic to ذَرْ; duration and outcome come from its accompanying expressions.

ذَرْنِي — Leaving the matter to The God

Al-Qalam 68:44 says:

فَذَرْنِي وَمَنْ يُكَذِّبُ بِهَٰذَا الْحَدِيثِ ۖ سَنَسْتَدْرِجُهُمْ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

fa-dharnī wa-man yukadhdhibu bi-hādhā al-ḥadīthi sa-nastadrijuhum min ḥaythu lā yaʿlamūn

Al-Qalam 68:44

“Leave Me with whoever denies this discourse. We shall draw them on gradually from where they do not know.”

فَذَرْنِي is The God's address to Muhammad.

The construction does not merely say “abandon Me”; it conveys leaving the deniers' case to The God.

The continuation makes this clear by describing how He will deal with them.

The same construction occurs in Al-Muzzammil 73:11 and Al-Muddaththir 74:11.

Al-Muzzammil 73:11 mentions prosperous deniers and their respite.

In Al-Muddaththir 74:11, The God says:

ذَرْنِي وَمَنْ خَلَقْتُ وَحِيدًا

dharnī wa-man khalaqtu waḥīdā

Al-Muddaththir 74:11

“Leave Me with the one whom I created alone.”

These uses should not be understood physically, as though a human could prevent The God from acting.

The construction expresses leaving the matter and judgment to Him.

That follows from the speaker's role and subsequent verses, rather than from ذَرْ alone.

6. Leaving in a condition: prayer and punishment

Āl ʿImrān 3:179 — Not leaving believers as they are

Āl ʿImrān 3:179 says:

مَا كَانَ اللَّهُ لِيَذَرَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ عَلَىٰ مَا أَنْتُمْ عَلَيْهِ

mā kāna allāhu li-yadhara al-muʾminīna ʿalā mā antum ʿalayh

Āl ʿImrān 3:179

“The God would not leave the believers in the condition you are in.”

The continuation speaks of separating the bad from the good.

يَذَرَ with عَلَىٰ concerns leaving people in a condition.

It does not mean physically departing from believers.

Their existing situation will not simply remain as it is.

This differs from فَذَرْهُمْ فِي غَمْرَتِهِمْ: there leaving in a condition is stated, whereas here it is denied.

The verb itself does not mean testing, purifying, or separating; those ideas come from the continuation.

Al-Anʿām 6:110, Al-Aʿrāf 7:186, and Yūnus 10:11 — Leaving in bewilderment

These verses use forms such as وَنَذَرُهُمْ and وَيَذَرُهُمْ.

All three describe leaving people wandering in their transgression.

The verbs themselves mean leaving them alone.

Transgression and bewilderment are expressed by separate words specifying their condition.

ذَرْ does not intrinsically mean misleading, blinding, or punishing.

In these contexts, leaving may relate to the consequences of human conduct and choice, but that relation must be understood from the complete verse.

Maryam 19:72 — Leaving the wrongdoers

After speaking of saving the mindful, Maryam 19:72 says:

وَنَذَرُ الظَّالِمِينَ فِيهَا جِثِيًّا

wa-nadharu al-ẓālimīna fīhā jithiyyā

Maryam 19:72

“And We leave the wrongdoers there on their knees.”

نَذَرُ means leaving a group in a specified condition.

فِيهَا specifies the location and جِثِيًّا their posture.

Those features are not part of the root's lexical meaning.

The verb alone does not determine the duration or full character of that otherworldly condition.

Ṭā Hā 20:106 — Leaving level ground

Ṭā Hā 20:106 says:

فَيَذَرُهَا قَاعًا صَفْصَفًا

fa-yadharuhā qāʿan ṣafṣafā

Ṭā Hā 20:106

“Then He leaves it a level, smooth plain.”

This follows the account of mountains and the earth's transformation.

يَذَرُ concerns leaving something in the condition resulting from the preceding event.

Becoming smooth and level is not the root's own meaning.

قَاعًا صَفْصَفًا describes the remaining condition.

The example again shows that the root cannot always mean leaving a person or abandoning responsibility.

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:89 — Zechariah's prayer not to be left alone

وَزَكَرِيَّا إِذْ نَادَىٰ رَبَّهُ رَبِّ لَا تَذَرْنِي فَرْدًا وَأَنْتَ خَيْرُ الْوَارِثِينَ

wa-zakariyyā idh nādā rabbahu rabbi lā tadharnī fardan wa-anta khayru al-wārithīn

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:89

“And Zechariah, when he called to his Lord: My Lord, do not leave me alone; You are the best of inheritors.”

لَا تَذَرْنِي is an imperfect verb made jussive by a prohibitive particle; here the construction functions as a supplication or negative request.

Zechariah asks The God not to leave him alone.

فَرْدًا specifies the condition: being alone.

تَذَرْنِي does not itself mean aloneness, but leaving him in that condition.

The prayer occurs in the context of requesting a child.

That does not make every Arabic use of لَا تَذَرْنِي a request for a child. The specific referent comes from Zechariah's story.

Nūḥ 71:26 — Not leaving the disbelievers

وَقَالَ نُوحٌ رَبِّ لَا تَذَرْ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ مِنَ الْكَافِرِينَ دَيَّارًا

wa-qāla nūḥun rabbi lā tadhar ʿalā al-arḍi mina al-kāfirīna dayyārā

Nūḥ 71:26

“Noah said: My Lord, do not leave on the earth a single inhabitant from among the disbelievers.”

لَا تَذَرْ requests that none be left.

مِنَ الْكَافِرِينَ identifies the group, and دَيَّارًا emphasizes that not one individual should remain.

Nūḥ 71:27 explains the request: if they remain, they will lead The God's servants astray.

This is Noah's prayer within his narrative.

It should not be converted into a general command for people to eliminate groups with different beliefs.

The root does not mean killing here; it means leaving, negated by لَا.

Nūḥ 71:23 also uses it in the people's speech, urging one another not to abandon their gods and naming several, including Wadd.

Wadd, وَدّ, is a name associated with و د د, distinct from تَذَرُنَّ, which belongs to و ذ ر.

7. When nothing is left

Two further uses show the meaning of leaving through its negation.

Al-Dhāriyāt 51:42 — The devastating wind

مَا تَذَرُ مِنْ شَيْءٍ أَتَتْ عَلَيْهِ إِلَّا جَعَلَتْهُ كَالرَّمِيمِ

mā tadharu min shayʾin atat ʿalayhi illā jaʿalat-hu ka-al-ramīm

Al-Dhāriyāt 51:42

“It left nothing it came upon without making it like decayed remains.”

مَا تَذَرُ negates leaving.

The sentence also states the result: whatever the wind encountered became like رَمِيم.

It describes the devastating wind mentioned in Al-Dhāriyāt 51:41.

The verb itself does not mean destroying; destruction emerges from the complete sentence and جَعَلَتْ.

مَا تَذَرُ says that it left nothing as it was.

Al-Muddaththir 74:28 — Negating what remains

لَا تُبْقِي وَلَا تَذَرُ

lā tubqī wa-lā tadhar

Al-Muddaththir 74:28

“It neither spares nor leaves anything untouched.”

The verse describes Saqar.

تُبْقِي belongs to ب ق ي and تَذَرُ to و ذ ر.

Their pairing intensifies the image's comprehensiveness.

This does not establish that the two roots always have entirely separate, nonoverlapping meanings throughout Arabic.

Here their combined negation portrays something that leaves no one unharmed or untouched.

The expression alone does not provide a theory of the physical nature of otherworldly fire or the exact stages of punishment.

The root still means leaving or letting be, but its negation accompanies an image of punishment.

8. Comparison with neighboring roots

و ذ ر compared with ت ر ك

These roots are semantically close.

Both can express leaving, relinquishing, desisting, and leaving something behind.

The Quran uses ت ر ك in a wider range of forms, including the perfect, imperfect, passive, and other derivatives.

و ذ ر occurs only in imperfect and imperative constructions.

This morphological difference should not become an absolute semantic divide.

فَتَذَرُوهَا كَالْمُعَلَّقَةِ in Al-Nisāʾ 4:129 describes leaving a woman suspended.

وَتَرَكَهُمْ فِي ظُلُمَاتٍ in Al-Baqarah 2:17 describes leaving people in darkness.

Both constructions can express remaining in a condition, although their contexts and subjects differ.

The Quran also uses both roots for leaving people behind after death.

Their actual overlap should be acknowledged without inventing an artificial boundary to distinguish them.

Forqan's study of ت ر ك examines that family more extensively.

و ذ ر compared with و د ع

و د ع also concerns leaving and desisting.

دَعْ and ذَرْ can have similar meanings in many constructions.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:48, for example, says:

وَدَعْ أَذَاهُمْ

wa-daʿ adhāhum

Al-Aḥzāb 33:48

“Leave aside their harm.”

Al-Anʿām 6:91 says:

ثُمَّ ذَرْهُمْ فِي خَوْضِهِمْ يَلْعَبُونَ

thumma dharhum fī khawḍihim yalʿabūn

Al-Anʿām 6:91

“Then leave them playing in their vain discourse.”

Both can involve ceasing pursuit or engagement with a situation.

و د ع also has branches concerning farewell, ease, and depositing something for safekeeping.

Its whole family should therefore not be equated with و ذ ر.

و ذ ر compared with ن ب ذ

ن ب ذ concerns throwing or casting aside.

Casting something aside can accompany abandoning it in certain Quranic usages.

Its physical uses emphasize casting something away, while و ذ ر may merely mean leaving it undisturbed in its condition.

Yūsuf 12:47 uses فَذَرُوهُ for leaving grain in its ears.

Translating it as “throw it away” would change the verse's meaning.

Despite some overlapping uses, the roots are not fully interchangeable.

و ذ ر compared with ه ج ر

ه ج ر concerns leaving and distancing oneself.

وَاهْجُرْهُمْ هَجْرًا جَمِيلًا in Al-Muzzammil 73:10 describes withdrawing from a group.

ذَرْهُمْ need not mean physical withdrawal.

The addressee may remain in the same setting while ceasing pursuit, interference, or engagement with their conduct.

This matters in some contexts but should not become an absolute rule for every use of either root.

Distinguishing similar-looking roots

Several roots resemble و ذ ر in writing or sound.

ذ ر ر: concerns scattering, particles, and other lexical branches.

ذ ر و: includes scattering and dispersing.

ن ذ ر: occurs in the Quran for vows, warning, and giving warning.

Similar letters do not make these roots identical.

In particular, the familiar Persian and Arabic noun نَذْر differs from نَذَرُ of و ذ ر.

نَذَرُ can mean “we leave,” while نَذْر of ن ذ ر concerns a commitment or vow.

Vowels, morphology, and context distinguish them.

9. Translation proposals for Forqan

Choose the equivalent according to the verb's object and context. These proposals do not replace existing verse word boxes.

Commands to cease an activity can use “desist” or “relinquish.”

In Al-Baqarah 2:278:

“Relinquish what remains of riba.”

In Al-Anʿām 6:120:

“Leave outward and inward sin.”

In Al-Jumuʿah 62:9:

“Leave trade.”

Where something must remain in its condition, “leave in place” is clearer.

In Yūsuf 12:47:

“Leave what you harvest in its ears, except a little of which you eat.”

In Ṭā Hā 20:106:

“Then He leaves it a level, smooth plain.”

Where permission or noninterference is meant, “let” or “leave alone” is more suitable.

In Ghāfir 40:26:

“Let me kill Moses.”

In Al-Aʿrāf 7:73:

“Let her eat on The God's earth.”

For leaving people, “leave alone” or “let be” is appropriate.

In Al-Ḥijr 15:3:

“Leave them to eat and enjoy themselves…”

In Al-Muʾminūn 23:54:

“Leave them in their engulfing condition for a time.”

Prayers concerning remaining should preserve the sense of leaving in a condition.

In Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:89:

“My Lord, do not leave me alone.”

In Nūḥ 71:26:

“My Lord, do not leave on the earth a single inhabitant from among the disbelievers.”

For death, “leave behind” is suitable.

In Al-Baqarah 2:234 and Al-Baqarah 2:240:

“Those who die and leave spouses behind.”

No single Persian equivalent suffices for every use. Leaving, relinquishing, letting be, leaving in place, leaving behind, and permitting each convey greater precision in an appropriate context.

10. Conclusion

The principal Quranic branch of و ذ ر concerns leaving, relinquishing, desisting, and leaving behind.

In poetry attributed to ʿUrwah ibn al-Ward, ذَرِينِي requests noninterference with personal decisions. It shows that the early meaning was not limited to physical departure or permanent separation.

The Quran uses this semantic field in diverse circumstances.

ذَرُوا can command relinquishing riba or sin.

فَذَرُوهُ can command leaving grain in its ears.

ذَرُوهَا can mean not obstructing the she-camel's movement and feeding.

يَذَرُونَ can concern leaving spouses behind after death.

فَتَذَرُوهَا can describe leaving a woman suspended.

ذَرْهُمْ can command leaving opponents to their course and its consequences.

لَا تَذَرْنِي can ask not to be left alone.

لَا تُبْقِي وَلَا تَذَرُ uses negation of leaving to depict punishment.

The semantic core concerns leaving, but the word itself does not determine the relationship, moral value, duration, or outcome.

The Quranic branch means leaving a person, thing, or activity: by ceasing action or pursuit, not obstructing continuation, or leaving someone or something in place and in their existing condition.

This should remain distinct from وَذْرَة, a piece of meat, and the cutting branch, since a certain historical connection has not been established.

Forqan must preserve the distinction between relinquishing and leaving in place. Rendering فَذَرُوهُ فِي سُنْبُلِهِ simply as “abandon it,” as one might relinquish outstanding riba, could obscure the meaning. One concerns ceasing a claim or practice; the other preserves the harvest in its existing condition.

The family's shared meaning should be understood, while each derivative's final translation follows its sentence structure and context.
