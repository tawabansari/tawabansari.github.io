Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root F R SH

Spreading out, laying down, preparing a surface, and making a field suitable for settling, resting, living, or moving

Important Forms from This Root

فَرَشَ / فَرَشْنَاهَا (farasha / farashnāhā)

he spread out; We spread it out and prepared it as a livable ground.

فِرَاش (firāsh)

a bed, bedding, or prepared spread-out surface for contact and settlement.

فُرُش (furush)

beds or couches, especially in images of ease, honor, and prepared comfort.

فَرْش (farsh)

in some contexts, smaller livestock or animals associated with bedding materials.

فَرَاش (farāsh)

moths, butterflies, or light winged insects scattered in movement.

Method Note

The root F R SH should not be reduced to the modern Persian sense of “carpet.” In Qur’anic Arabic, the core is to spread something out and prepare a surface. Something in the field of F R SH moves from being raw, uneven, or unsuitable for direct contact into a usable ground or bed.

This root appears in the Qur’an for the earth, for couches or bedding in Paradise, for livestock, and for the image of scattered winged creatures on the Day of Striking. So the reader should think beyond a household object. The focus is preparing a surface, laying a field open, and in some contexts scattering across a field.

Forms such as فِرَاشًا (firāshan), فَرَشْنَاهَا (farashnāhā), فُرُش (furush), فُرُشٍ مَّرْفُوعَةٍ (furushin marfūʿah), and الْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ (al-farāshi al-mabthūth) show two related directions: a prepared resting surface and a field of scattered, light movement.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of F R SH is spreading something over a surface and preparing it for contact and settlement. When a bed or surface is made firāsh, a living being can sit on it, walk on it, sleep on it, rest on it, or live upon it. So the root is not only “spreading.” It is the preparation of a surface for use.

When the Qur’an calls the earth فِرَاشًا (firāshan), it does not mean that the earth is a household carpet or that it has no mountains, valleys, or unevenness. The more precise meaning is that the earth has been made livable, traversable, and usable for human settlement. It is not raw chaos unsuitable for life. The God has placed it in the role of a prepared ground for the human being.

Material Image

The material image is a person spreading bedding over the ground so that the surface becomes ready for sitting or sleeping. Before the spreading, the ground may be cold, hard, dusty, or uneven. After it is made into a bed or spread surface, it becomes usable, calmer, and more fitting for contact.

This image is then applied to the earth. In the Qur’an, the earth has been spread and prepared for the human being. This does not deny mountains, valleys, or roughness. It means that the earth as a whole has been made suitable for life, movement, cultivation, settlement, and benefit.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, one may say فَرَشْتُ البِسَاط (farashtu al-bisāṭ), meaning “I spread out the mat.” فِرَاش (firāsh) also means bedding, the place where a person rests and lays the body down on a prepared surface. This use is connected with home, stillness, comfort, and direct bodily contact with a surface made ready.

The word فَرَاش (farāsh) is also used for moths, butterflies, or light winged insects. This comes from the image of spread wings, lightness, dispersal, and scattered movement across space or over a surface. So within F R SH there are two close images: spreading out a bed, and light scattering across a field.

Qur’anic Usage

The Earth as a Prepared Bed for the Human Being

In الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ الْأَرْضَ فِرَاشًا (alladhī jaʿala lakumu al-arḍa firāshan), The God made the earth a firāsh for the human being 2:22. The expression shows that the earth has been prepared for settlement, movement, cultivation, and life. The earth is not merely an object underfoot; it is the bed or field of life.

The verb جَعَلَ (jaʿala) is important here. The God placed the earth in the role of firāsh. That means the earth has a function within creation: the human being can settle on it and benefit from it. So فِرَاشًا (firāshan) carries the meaning of preparing and making the ground suitable for life.

Spreading the Earth and Preparing It

In وَالْأَرْضَ فَرَشْنَاهَا فَنِعْمَ الْمَاهِدُونَ (wal-arḍa farashnāhā faniʿma al-māhidūn), The God says that He spread the earth, and how excellent are the preparers 51:48. This verse brings F R SH close to the root M H D. To spread the earth here is to lay it out and prepare it as a living ground.

Again, the meaning is not that the earth is perfectly flat and free of unevenness. It means that the earth, as a whole, has been made ready for living and settlement. The pairing of فَرَشْنَاهَا (farashnāhā) with الْمَاهِدُونَ (al-māhidūn) shows that spreading the earth is a kind of preparing the bed of life, not merely flattening a surface.

Bedding and Couches in Paradise

In مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ فُرُشٍ بَطَائِنُهَا مِنْ إِسْتَبْرَقٍ (muttakiʾīna ʿalā furushin baṭāʾinuhā min istabraq), the people of Paradise recline on couches whose linings are of thick silk brocade 55:54. Another verse says وَفُرُشٍ مَّرْفُوعَةٍ (wafurushin marfūʿah), raised or elevated couches 56:34.

Here F R SH carries the sense of ease, comfort, and honor. The bed is not merely a surface to sit on. It marks an environment prepared for rest, dignity, and safety. In this world, the earth is the bed of life and trial. In Paradise, the furush are beds of peace and blessing.

Farāsh Mabthūth: Scattered People on the Day of Striking

In يَوْمَ يَكُونُ النَّاسُ كَالْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ (yawma yakūnu al-nāsu kal-farāshi al-mabthūth), people on that Day are described as being like scattered farāsh 101:4. Here farāsh is close to moths or light winged insects: small beings in restless, scattered, directionless motion.

This use brings out the meaning of scattering and dispersal. In the world, the earth is a firāsh for the human being: a prepared and relatively stable field of movement and action. But in the scene of the Resurrection, people themselves become like الْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ (al-farāshi al-mabthūth): light, scattered, and unstable. The contrast quietly intensifies the upheaval of that Day.

Farsh Beside Ḥamūlah in Livestock

In وَمِنَ الْأَنْعَامِ حَمُولَةً وَفَرْشًا (wamina al-anʿāmi ḥamūlatan wafarshā), livestock are described as ḥamūlah and farsh 6:142. Ḥamūlah points to larger load-bearing animals. Farsh in this context is often understood as smaller animals closer to the ground, or animals from whose wool, hair, hide, or fiber bedding and spread materials are made.

In either reading, the root’s core remains visible: either animals low and near the ground, or animals that provide material for bedding and spread surfaces. So farsh in this verse is connected with earthly benefit and the living field of pastoral life.

The Earth as a Bed of Trial, Not Only Comfort

When the Qur’an calls the earth a firāsh, it should not be understood only as an image of comfort. The earth is a bed, but it is also a bed of trial. The human being moves on this prepared ground, plants, builds, corrupts or reforms, and leaves the traces of action upon it.

So the earth being firāsh does not permit careless consumption. The God has prepared the earth for the human being, but this preparation comes with responsibility. A prepared bed is a field of action, not a license for heedlessness and ruin.

Difference from Nearby Words

Basṭ means spreading and expanding, with emphasis on width, extension, and openness. Farsh also includes spreading, but it adds the sense of becoming a bed or prepared surface for contact and settlement.

Mahd is preparing a bed, with a shade of nurture, ease, and making something ready for growth. Farsh is closer to the actual spread surface prepared for direct contact. The two roots appear together in 51:48 and clarify one another.

M D D means extending and drawing out. Daḥw is also connected with spreading and preparing the earth, though its details require caution. Saṭḥ points to a surface. Nashr means dispersing or publishing outward, but it does not necessarily mean a prepared bed. Farsh is closest to the image of bedding, a spread surface, and prepared ground.

Translation Method

F R SH should not always be translated as “carpet.” In English too, “carpet” can make the reader think of a household object and miss the Qur’anic sense. Often “bed,” “bedding,” “spread-out ground,” “prepared surface,” “prepared field,” or “ground made ready for settlement” is better.

For الأَرْضَ فِرَاشًا (al-arḍa firāshan), “He made the earth a carpet for you” can mislead if left unexplained. A better rendering is: “He made the earth for you a spread-out and prepared ground.” In Surat al-Qāriʿah, الْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ (al-farāshi al-mabthūth) should not be translated as household carpets. It refers to scattered light winged creatures.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

جَعَلَ لَكُمُ الْأَرْضَ فِرَاشًا (jaʿala lakumu al-arḍa firāshan): He made the earth for you a spread-out bed; He made the earth for you a prepared ground for settlement.

وَالْأَرْضَ فَرَشْنَاهَا (wal-arḍa farashnāhā): and We spread out the earth and prepared it; We laid the earth out like a bed for life.

فُرُشٍ بَطَائِنُهَا مِنْ إِسْتَبْرَقٍ (furushin baṭāʾinuhā min istabraq): couches whose linings are of thick silk brocade; beds whose inner lining is of rich brocade.

وَفُرُشٍ مَّرْفُوعَةٍ (wafurushin marfūʿah): and raised couches; elevated and honored beds.

كَالْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ (kal-farāshi al-mabthūth): like scattered moths; like light winged creatures spread about.

حَمُولَةً وَفَرْشًا (ḥamūlatan wafarshā): load-bearing animals and farsh animals; larger beasts of burden and smaller ground-near livestock; animals from which bedding and spread materials are obtained.

Summary

The root F R SH begins from the image of spreading something out and preparing a bed. What is made farsh becomes ready for contact, sitting, sleeping, living, or moving. In this sense, the earth is the human being’s firāsh: not a carpet, but a wide prepared ground that makes life, movement, provision, and action possible.

In Forqan’s reading, F R SH means preparing a surface for settlement. Sometimes that surface is the earth. Sometimes it is the bedding of blessing. Sometimes it is the spread materials of earthly life. And sometimes it becomes an image of scattered, light, unstable creatures. The root reminds us that human life always takes place upon a prepared ground; that ground is both blessing and responsibility.
