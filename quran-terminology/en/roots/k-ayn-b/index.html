Prominence, projection, the Kaaba, and bodily development

1. Meaning in early Arabic

ك ع ب concerns prominence, projection, elevation, and in some uses a square shape.

Ibn Faris identifies projection and elevation as basic meanings. Established uses include ankle bones, joints of reeds and spear shafts, a square building, and bodily development.

A line attributed to the pre-Islamic poet ʿAntara ibn Shaddad states:

فَطَعَنْتُ بِالرُّمْحِ الْأَصَمِّ كُعُوبُهُ لَيْسَ الْكَرِيمُ عَلَى الْقَنَا بِمُحَرَّمِ

I thrust with a spear whose joints were solid; nobility does not make a man invulnerable to spears.

كُعُوب, plural of كَعْب, denotes the joints or sections of the spear shaft. The early meaning was not restricted to human anatomy.

A poem attributed to the pre-Islamic poet al-Aswad ibn Yaʿfur al-Nahshali also says:

أَهْلُ الْخَوَرْنَقِ وَالسَّدِيرِ وَبَارِقٍ وَالْبَيْتِ ذِي الْكَعَبَاتِ مِنْ سِنْدَادِ

The people of al-Khawarnaq, al-Sadir, Bariq, and the House of the Kaabas at Sindad.

Historical and lexical accounts describe ذُو الْكَعَبَات as a building belonging to certain Arab tribes around which they circumambulated.

A variant replaces الْبَيْتِ ذِي الْكَعَبَاتِ with الْقَصْرِ ذِي الشُّرُفَاتِ, “the palace with battlements.” The first reading should not therefore be treated as conclusive pre-Islamic architectural evidence without accounting for the variation.

Lexicons also record:

  • كَعْبُ الرِّجْلِ: the bony prominence where leg and foot meet.
  • كَعْبُ الْقَصَبِ: a reed’s joint or section between nodes.
  • كَعَّبَ الشَّيْءَ: made it square.
  • كَعَبَ الثَّدْيُ: the breast developed and became prominent.
  • رَجُلٌ عَالِي الْكَعْبِ: a man of high standing and honor.

These usages show the family’s range. Although the precise emergence of every sense cannot be independently dated, the Qur’an attests important parts of this range.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important Arabic forms include:

  • كَعْب: bony prominence; a section or joint of reed or spear shaft.
  • كَعْبَانِ and كَعْبَيْنِ: dual forms.
  • أَكْعُب, كِعَاب, and كُعُوب: plural forms.
  • كَعْبَة: a square building; the sacred House in Mecca.
  • كَعَبَ: became prominent or projected.
  • كُعُوب and كَعَابَة: prominence and bodily development.
  • كَاعِب: a female whose breasts have developed and become prominent.
  • كَوَاعِب: plural of كَاعِب.
  • كَعَّبَ: made square or prominent.
  • تَكْعِيب: making square.
  • مُكَعَّب: made square; a cube in modern geometry.
  • تَكَعَّبَ: became prominent.

Qur’anic forms examined:

الْكَعْبَيْنِ ـ الْكَعْبَةِ ـ الْكَعْبَةَ ـ كَوَاعِبَ

These belong to three nominal derivatives: كَعْب, كَعْبَة, and كَاعِب.

The family’s verbs and verbal nouns such as تَكْعِيب do not occur in the Qur’an.

3. Core meaning and material imagery

Many uses share the idea of something projecting above its surroundings.

Visible and palpable bony prominences occur where the leg meets the foot; Arabic calls them كَعْب.

Reed stems likewise have distinctive joints and nodes. Uses concerning reeds and spear shafts relate to this structure, although some definitions also call the section between nodes كَعْب.

Architectural usage emphasizes elevation and a square form. Early lexicographers connect كَعْبَة with these features.

In bodily development, كَاعِب describes visible breast development and prominence.

A common image is prominence and a distinctive shape, but this does not justify translating every derivative with one fixed equivalent.

كَعْب in the purification verse names a body part, كَعْبَة a particular building, and كَوَاعِب describes females. Each derivative’s established use, not the root alone, determines its precise meaning.

4. Qur’anic usages

الْكَعْبَيْنِ: bony prominences of the feet

الْكَعْبَيْنِ is the dual of كَعْب, genitive after إِلَىٰ.

The verse specifies bodily preparation for salat by naming particular parts.

As الْمَرَافِقِ identifies the elbows and the boundary for washing the arms, الْكَعْبَيْنِ specifies the boundary concerning the feet.

Early lexical and purification discussions record two identifications:

The bony prominences on the sides of the ankle, or the bony prominence on the top of the foot.

The first has broad lexical support; the second nevertheless attests disagreement over the precise anatomical referent.

“Ankle” is a familiar general equivalent but covers the whole region. “Ankle prominences” conveys the lexical image more precisely.

Whether the feet are washed or wiped concerns readings and syntax of أَرْجُلَكُمْ and its relation to preceding verbs. ك ع ب alone does not settle it.

The root identifies a bodily boundary marked by bony prominence.

الْكَعْبَةِ: the destination of the offering

The Kaaba is a particular destination to which the offering is to reach.

بَالِغَ, an active participle of ب ل غ, means reaching; the Kaaba specifies the destination.

The context concerns restrictions on hunting during consecration and responsibility for violating them.

Alongside an offering, the verse mentions feeding the needy and fasting as forms of compensation.

The Kaaba here belongs to the system of rites and responsibilities associated with pilgrimage’s sanctity.

An offering reaching the Kaaba does not necessarily mean taking an animal inside the building. The detailed place and manner of performance require consideration of the wider pilgrimage passages.

الْكَعْبَةَ الْبَيْتَ الْحَرَامَ: support for people

This is the Qur’an’s clearest identification of the Kaaba.

الْكَعْبَةَ names the building; الْبَيْتَ الْحَرَامَ identifies its sacred character; قِيَامًا لِلنَّاسِ expresses its function for people.

The lexical meaning relates to form and elevation; its divine standing and human responsibilities are learned from the verses.

The verse groups the Kaaba with the sacred month, offerings, and garlands, connecting sacred place and time with security and pilgrimage.

لِلنَّاسِ matters: the Kaaba is support for people, not an instrument of ethnic superiority, sectional exploitation, or monopoly by the powerful.

The following verse also helps explain its place:

The House is connected with people’s return, security, purity, and service to The God.

Al-Hajj 22:25 also speaks of equal standing for residents and visitors regarding the Sacred Mosque.

Its sanctity therefore concerns not only preserving a building but also the rights, security, and interests of those for whom it was established.

The Kaaba and its Lord

Although sacred, the building is not itself an object of worship.

The verse clearly distinguishes the House from its Lord.

Its value and sanctity derive from the position assigned by The God. Worship is directed to the Lord, not the stones, walls, or geometry.

Divine oneness, security, pilgrimage, and public benefit are therefore not components of the root’s lexical meaning; they describe the Kaaba’s Qur’anic significance through related verses.

كَوَاعِبَ أَتْرَابًا: developed female companions of equal age

كَوَاعِب is the broken plural of كَاعِب.

In early Arabic, كَاعِب describes a female whose breasts have developed and become prominent, connecting with the root’s idea of projection.

أَتْرَاب, plural of تِرْب from ت ر ب, means peers of equal age.

The words thus describe two distinct features:

كَوَاعِب concerns a specific feature of bodily development; أَتْرَاب concerns equal age.

The surrounding descriptions establish the context:

The passage presents a range of paradisal blessings.

Gardens, fruit, a cup of drink, and physically developed female companions of equal age each depict an aspect of that provision.

Direct lexical meaning should be distinguished from fluent rendering. “Developed women” conveys part of the sense but does not explicitly identify the physical feature.

“Women with developed breasts” conveys the direct lexical sense more explicitly.

“Beautiful companions” or “companions of equal age” alone omit the feminine reference and bodily-development meaning.

كَاعِب does not itself mean virgin. Virginity is expressed through ب ك ر and should not be added without contextual evidence.

كَوَاعِب and age

كَاعِب relates to a stage of bodily development but specifies no numerical age.

أَتْرَاب likewise states equal age without giving a number.

Assigning a particular age without Qur’anic evidence therefore exceeds either word’s meaning.

Concerning paradisal blessings, the Qur’an also states:

The language of these descriptions should be preserved without attributing unstated details of the hereafter to the verses.

The opening promise addresses الْمُتَّقِينَ. Elsewhere the Qur’an expressly promises reward and good life to believing women and men, including al-Nisa 4:124 and al-Nahl 16:97.

One description among the blessings does not establish that all enjoyment of the hereafter belongs to one sex alone.

5. Morphology and syntax

The dual الْكَعْبَيْنِ

كَعْب is singular; its dual is كَعْبَانِ in the nominative and كَعْبَيْنِ in the accusative and genitive.

In al-Maʾida 5:6, إِلَىٰ governs the genitive.

Syntactically, the word specifies the boundary of purification concerning the feet.

الْكَعْبَة

In al-Maʾida 5:95, الْكَعْبَةِ is genitive in a construction with بَالِغَ.

In al-Maʾida 5:97, الْكَعْبَةَ is the first accusative object of جَعَلَ; الْبَيْتَ الْحَرَامَ identifies it, and قِيَامًا is the second object.

The first verse names the offering’s destination; the second names what is established as support for people.

كَوَاعِبَ أَتْرَابًا

كَوَاعِب is a broken plural of كَاعِب on فَوَاعِل.

كَاعِب is specifically feminine in this use and requires no feminine ة ending.

In al-Naba 78:33, كَوَاعِبَ is accusative and coordinated with the preceding blessings; أَتْرَابًا is its accusative description.

كَوَاعِب belongs to a diptote plural pattern and takes no tanwin.

It differs from كَعْبَة, a square building: despite their common root, كَوَاعِب is the plural of كَاعِب, not كَعْبَة.

6. Comparison with related roots

ك ع ب compared with ع ق ب

The anatomical عَقِب denotes the heel and rear of the foot.

كَعْب concerns bony prominence near the leg–foot junction.

Heel and ankle prominence are distinct; “the two heels” does not preserve the precision of الْكَعْبَيْنِ in al-Maʾida 5:6.

ع ق ب also has extensive meanings of following, outcome, and succession absent from ك ع ب.

ك ع ب compared with ر ف ق

مِرْفَق names the elbow, where upper arm and forearm meet.

كَعْب names the bony prominence near the ankle.

Al-Maʾida 5:6 places the plural of مِرْفَق beside the dual of كَعْب to specify boundaries for arms and feet.

Both concern joints but name different body parts.

ك ع ب compared with ب ي ت

بَيْت is a general word for house.

Classical كَعْبَة can denote a square building; Qur’anic الْكَعْبَة names the familiar House in Mecca.

Al-Maʾida 5:97 identifies it as الْبَيْتَ الْحَرَامَ.

The expressions refer to the same place here without being complete lexical synonyms.

Not every Qur’anic بَيْت means the Kaaba: it also describes people’s homes and some animals’ dwellings.

ك ع ب compared with ح ر م

كَعْبَة names the building; حُرْمَة concerns protection from violation, respect, and restrictions.

Architectural or geometric meaning alone does not establish religious sanctity.

The Qur’an identifies this particular building as sacred. Its sanctity comes from that designation, not merely its square shape.

ك ع ب compared with ت ر ب

كَوَاعِب concerns bodily and breast development; أَتْرَاب states equal age.

They occur together in al-Naba 78:33 but retain independent meanings.

أَتْرَاب is not restricted to this phrase: it also occurs in Sad 38:52 and al-Waqiʿa 56:37.

Rendering both as “of equal age” or “at the same stage” erases the distinction.

ك ع ب compared with ب ك ر

بِكْر concerning females denotes virginity or absence of prior sexual relations.

كَاعِب describes breast development and prominence.

One concerns sexual history; the other bodily development.

The Qur’an uses أَبْكَارًا in al-Waqiʿa 56:36 and كَوَاعِبَ in al-Naba 78:33.

“Virgins” without qualification may therefore add a meaning not supplied by كَوَاعِب itself.

ك ع ب compared with ن ه د

Classical نَهَدَ الثَّدْيُ also describes breast development and prominence.

Lexicographers and commentators accordingly explain كَوَاعِب with نَوَاهِد.

The families are close in this use, although ن ه د does not occur in the Qur’an.

7. Suggested renderings for Forqan

Arabic word or expressionSuggested rendering
كَعْبankle prominence; stem joint
الْكَعْبَيْنِthe two ankle prominences
إِلَى الْكَعْبَيْنِup to the ankle prominences
الْكَعْبَةthe Kaaba
هَدْيًا بَالِغَ الْكَعْبَةِan offering reaching the Kaaba
الْكَعْبَةَ الْبَيْتَ الْحَرَامَthe Kaaba, the Sacred House
كَاعِبa female with developed breasts
كَوَاعِبphysically developed women
كَوَاعِبَ أَتْرَابًاphysically developed female companions of equal age

A fluent rendering such as “developed women of equal age” requires the word explanation to preserve the precise bodily meaning.

A more explicit rendering is:

“And women of equal age with developed breasts.”

For الْكَعْبَة, retaining “the Kaaba” is most appropriate. “Cube” or “cube-shaped building” reduces a proper name to a geometric description.

Conclusion

ك ع ب concerns prominence, projection, elevation, and sometimes a square shape.

The Qur’an employs three derivatives:

الْكَعْبَيْنِ marks the boundary concerning feet in purification before salat; الْكَعْبَة names the Sacred House; كَوَاعِب describes bodily development among paradisal blessings.

Prominence connects these uses, but each derivative has its own established meaning.

كَعْبَة lexically names a building, while the Qur’an establishes it as the Sacred House and support for people. Sanctity, security, and responsibility come from the verses, not the root alone.

كَوَاعِب is feminine and concerns bodily development; its meaning should not be reduced to general beauty, equal age, or virginity.
