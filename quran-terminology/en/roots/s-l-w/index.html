1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root س ل و in Arabic is connected with the heart becoming distant from grief, attachment to something lost becoming weaker, forgetting, or finding calm after pain. From the same family, words such as suluw, salwah, sulwan, and tasalli were formed in Arabic.

However, in the Qur'anic text, only the word السَّلْوَىٰ from this family occurs. It does not appear as a psychological state or as the act of consoling someone, but as the name of a provision or food that was provided with al-mann for the Children of Israel.

This word occurs three times in the Qur'an:

المنّ والسلوى

Al-Baqarah 2:57

Al-A'raf 7:160

Ta-Ha 20:80

Early lexical and exegetical sources usually identify as-salwa as a bird similar to quail, or as quail itself. Some also connect its name with the family of s-l-w, meaning something by which a person becomes calm from hardship and worry. But the Qur'an itself does not explain the animal type, shape, color, nutritional composition, or reason for the naming.

Therefore, two levels must remain separate in the Qur'anic study of this root.

The general meaning of the lexical family:

Becoming calm from grief, becoming distant from attachment, or forgetting something that had occupied the heart.

The Qur'anic usage:

As-salwa, the name of a provision or food provided for the Children of Israel.

Short meaning:

In Arabic: becoming calm and distant from grief or attachment. In the Qur'an: as-salwa, the name of a food or provision that was provided with mann for the Children of Israel.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

السَّلْوَىٰ

This is the only Qur'anic form from this family. In all three uses, it appears with the definite article and in the expression:

الْمَنَّ وَالسَّلْوَىٰ

The Qur'an speaks of salwa as something edible:

كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ

This command comes after the mention of mann and salwa, and shows that both were part of the provision and tayyibat provided for the Children of Israel.

سَلَا / يَسْلُو

This is the known verb of the root in Arabic, but it does not occur in the Qur'an.

Sala 'anhu can mean that a person became distant from the memory of someone or something, forgot it, or that their grief concerning it subsided.

This verb is useful for understanding the root family, but it should not be listed among the Qur'anic derivatives.

سَلْوَة

In Arabic, this may refer to something by which a person becomes calm from grief, or to the state of consolation and calm after sorrow.

This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

سُلْوَان

This points to forgetfulness, calm from grief, or something that causes sorrow to lessen. Some early expressions also speak of a drink or legendary thing that supposedly caused forgetfulness, but such reports must not be imposed on the Qur'anic meaning of as-salwa.

تَسَلِّي and تَسْلِيَة

These are forms from the patterns of tafa'ul and taf'il. They relate to becoming calm or calming someone from grief.

These words are common in Arabic and Persian, but they do not occur in the Qur'an.

3. Reference Cards for Qur'anic Derivatives

FormQur'an: yes
السَّلْوَىٰ

The name of a provision or food provided with mann for the Children of Israel.

As-salwa is the name of a provision or food that was provided with mann for the Children of Israel. In early sources it is usually identified as a bird similar to quail, but the Qur'an does not specify the exact species.

This root has no other Qur'anic derivative card. Forms such as sala, salwah, sulwan, tasalla, and tasliyah belong to the lexical family, but they are not Qur'anic forms.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The general core of the family س ل و may be expressed as:

The heart and attention becoming distant from something that had caused grief, attachment, or mental preoccupation.

This distancing may occur as forgetting, lessening of grief, calm, or becoming occupied with something else.

In Arabic one says:

سلا عنه

meaning that he became distant from its memory or grief.

And:

سلّاه عن همّه

meaning that he calmed him from his grief or caused his sorrow to subside.

But the connection between this core and the Qur'anic name as-salwa must be handled carefully. Lexical sources report two paths.

First, as-salwa is the name of a kind of bird.

Second, this name is connected with something that gives consolation, relief, or forgetfulness of grief.

It is possible that the food-name is connected with this family because it brought comfort and removed worry for the Children of Israel. But this is only a lexical possibility. The Qur'an does not say that the name was chosen because of its psychological, nutritional, or calming effect.

Therefore, the Qur'anic definition of as-salwa should not be:

“an anxiety-regulating agent,” “a neural sedative,” “a source of psychological stability,” or “a food that neutralizes the frequency of grief.”

What can be understood with confidence from the verses is that as-salwa was part of the pure provision provided for the Children of Israel.

5. The Material Image of the Root

For the general root meaning, the suitable human image is a heart that, after loss or distance, gradually moves away from constant preoccupation with it. The event has not disappeared from history, but it no longer holds all of a person's attention and strength.

In Persian, this image is close to “coming out of grief over something” or “letting go from the heart of what has been lost.”

But the material image of the Qur'anic use is a table of provision prepared for a group in hardship:

منّ و سلوى

The verses mention it beside the shading cloud, rescue, covenant, and pure provisions. Therefore, salwa is not an idea or a psychological state. It was something from which the Children of Israel could eat.

If the common lexical report that it was a bird is accepted, the material image is a small edible bird provided for them. But the Qur'an says nothing about the method of hunting, cooking, migration, protein amount, or biological features.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In lexical sources, sala 'anhu is connected with forgetting, leaving memory, or becoming calm from grief. Likewise aslahu and sallah mean removing grief from someone or causing consolation.

As-salwah is something by which a person becomes calm from grief. It may be speech, companionship, hope, a replacement, or an event that moves attention away from pain.

Early sources offer two close explanations for as-salwa:

  • something that consoles a person;
  • a bird like quail.

Ar-Raghib al-Isfahani says its root meaning is something that consoles a person, and then reports that salwa has been considered a bird like quail. This shows a lexical connection, but it does not prove that the bird-name was formed only because of its psychological effect.

Lisan al-Arab also registers as-salwa within this family and connects sala, sulwan, and tasalli with forgetfulness or the distancing of grief.

Therefore, a Qur'anic article must distinguish between three things:

The meaning of the verb sala:

He became distant from memory and grief.

The meaning of salwah and sulwan:

Something that brings calm or forgetfulness of grief.

As-salwa in the Qur'an:

The name of a provision or food of the Children of Israel.

7. History of Understanding and Restricting the Meaning

One error is to translate as-salwa simply as “quail” without explanation, as if the Qur'an itself had scientifically specified its animal type with certainty.

The report that salwa was a bird is very early and common, and it has strong probability. But the Qur'an itself uses only the name as-salwa. Therefore, the translation may preserve the word and explain:

“A food that early sources usually identify as a bird similar to quail.”

The opposite error is to reduce the food-name entirely to the meaning of “consolation” and say that The God sent a psychological agent to reduce the anxiety of the Children of Israel.

The verses speak of eating from the tayyibat. Therefore, salwa in this context is edible provision, not a mere metaphor for consolation.

The provision of food may naturally have reduced the anxiety of hunger, but that is the natural result of providing sustenance; it is not the direct lexical meaning of as-salwa in the verses.

Nor should other verses such as:

فَلَا يَحْزُنْكَ قَوْلُهُمْ

or:

وَاصْبِرْ وَمَا صَبْرُكَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

be presented as Qur'anic uses of the root س ل و. Those verses are from the roots ح ز ن and ص ب ر, not س ل و. They may be calming thematically, but they are not lexical witnesses for this root.

Another mistake is to create the divine description musalli. This word does not occur in the Qur'an as a name or description of The God. The fact that The God gives calm, patience, or opening to His servants does not justify making a section on “the divine name al-Musalli” in the root article.

8. Methodological Note

This root is an important example of distinguishing between a lexical family and a Qur'anic form.

A root may have several derivatives in Arabic, while the Qur'an uses only one of them. In that case, the article must not list non-Qur'anic uses as if they were Qur'anic derivatives.

Here:

Qur'anic form:

as-salwa.

Non-Qur'anic forms:

sala, salwah, sulwan, tasalla, tasliyah, musallin.

Likewise, one must not extract medical or psychological properties from the possible etymology of a food-name. Even if as-salwa is connected with the family of consolation, that does not prove that eating it had a special therapeutic effect on the nervous system.

Methodologically, three levels must remain separate:

The general lexical meaning of the root:

Becoming calm and distant from grief or attachment.

The meaning of the name in the verses:

Provision or food of the Children of Israel.

The contextual result:

Removal of hunger, benefiting from blessing, and making the continuation of life possible.

These three levels are related, but they are not identical.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

Salwa in Surat Al-Baqarah

The Qur'an says:

وَظَلَّلْنَا عَلَيْكُمُ الْغَمَامَ وَأَنْزَلْنَا عَلَيْكُمُ الْمَنَّ وَالسَّلْوَىٰ كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ وَمَا ظَلَمُونَا وَلَٰكِنْ كَانُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ يَظْلِمُونَ

And We shaded you with the cloud and sent down upon you mann and salwa: eat from the tayyibat of what We have provided you. They did not wrong Us, but they were wronging themselves.

Al-Baqarah 2:57

Several points are clear from the verse.

First, salwa is something from the category of provision.

Second, together with mann it is included among the tayyibat.

Third, the command kulu, “eat,” shows that the intended thing is edible.

Fourth, its provision is part of the blessings recalled for the Children of Israel.

The verse does not say that salwa was sent for “regulating neural networks” or “reducing psychological shock.” Such explanations must be set aside.

Salwa in Surat Al-A'raf

The Qur'an says:

وَظَلَّلْنَا عَلَيْهِمُ الْغَمَامَ وَأَنْزَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمُ الْمَنَّ وَالسَّلْوَىٰ كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ وَمَا ظَلَمُونَا وَلَٰكِنْ كَانُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ يَظْلِمُونَ

And We shaded them with the cloud and sent down upon them mann and salwa: eat from the tayyibat of what We have provided you. They did not wrong Us, but they were wronging themselves.

Al-A'raf 7:160

The content of this verse is close to Al-Baqarah 2:57. Salwa again appears beside the cloud, mann, and the command to eat from the tayyibat.

The repetition of this structure shows that the Qur'an emphasizes its being provision.

The verb anzalna means sending down or providing from The God. It should not be used to conclude that birds necessarily fell miraculously from the upper sky. The quality of the event is not explained in the verse.

Salwa in Surat Ta-Ha

The Qur'an says:

يَا بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ قَدْ أَنْجَيْنَاكُمْ مِنْ عَدُوِّكُمْ وَوَاعَدْنَاكُمْ جَانِبَ الطُّورِ الْأَيْمَنَ وَنَزَّلْنَا عَلَيْكُمُ الْمَنَّ وَالسَّلْوَىٰ ۝ كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ وَلَا تَطْغَوْا فِيهِ فَيَحِلَّ عَلَيْكُمْ غَضَبِي

O Children of Israel, We rescued you from your enemy, appointed for you the right side of the Mount, and sent down upon you mann and salwa. Eat from the tayyibat of what We have provided you, and do not transgress in it, lest My wrath descend upon you.

Ta-Ha 20:80–81

This context mentions salwa among several blessings:

  • rescue from the enemy;
  • the appointment at the side of the Mount;
  • the provision of mann and salwa;
  • permission to eat from the tayyibat.

But blessing comes with responsibility:

وَلَا تَطْغَوْا فِيهِ

Do not transgress in it.

The pronoun in fihi may refer to provision or to the whole blessing placed before them. The verse shows that proper relation to provision is not only receiving it; excess, ingratitude, or transgression can ruin the human relation with blessing.

This point comes from the verse's context, not from the meaning of the root س ل و.

10. Examination of the Qur'anic Derivative

السَّلْوَىٰ

In all three uses, as-salwa:

  • is definite;
  • appears with al-mann;
  • comes with the verb anzala or nazzala;
  • and is followed by the command to eat from the tayyibat.

This structural stability shows that as-salwa is the name of a known provision in the story of the Children of Israel.

The Qur'an never uses it to mean “consolation,” “calm,” “forgetfulness,” or “reduction of grief.”

Nor does another singular or plural form of this name appear in the Qur'an. Lexical sources sometimes treat salwah as singular and salwa as a collective noun, but this analysis stands outside the direct structure of the verses.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

Association with “al-mann”

In all three verses the structure is fixed:

الْمَنَّ وَالسَّلْوَىٰ

The conjunction places two types of provision beside one another. The verse does not present them as one thing or two names for one substance.

This association caused the two words to become known in translation and Islamic culture as a pair: “mann and salwa.”

The grammatical role of “as-salwa”

In the verses of Al-Baqarah and Al-A'raf, al-mann wa as-salwa are the objects of the verb anzalna.

In Ta-Ha they come with the verb nazzalna.

The grammatical structure therefore points to something that The God provided or sent down for them.

The link with the command “kulu”

In all three contexts, after the mention of mann and salwa, the command to eat from the tayyibat appears:

كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ

This link is one of the strongest internal Qur'anic evidences that salwa was food and edible provision.

The final alif maqsurah

Salwa is written with alif maqsurah. This spelling should not be treated as identical with words from the roots س ل و or س ل ي without morphological examination.

Lexicons report the verb as sala yaslu and sometimes with phonetic variation. A Qur'anic article does not need to resolve every morphological disagreement with certainty. It is enough to keep the lexical family and Qur'anic use separate.

12. S L W Compared with N S Y

The root ن س ي concerns forgetting, losing something from memory, or leaving something from attention.

Sala 'anhu is also sometimes explained in lexical sources through nasiyahu, “he forgot it,” but the two are not completely identical.

As a tendency:

Nasiya:

Something left memory or attention.

Sala:

The heart and attention became distant from attachment or grief concerning something.

A person may not forget an event, while its grief no longer dominates them. In this sense, suluw is not always the destruction of memory.

At the same time, in early usage the borders between the two words can overlap, and an absolutely sharp distinction should not be invented.

13. S L W Compared with H Z N

Huzn is the state of grief itself in relation to loss, harm, or something unpleasant.

Suluw is the movement of becoming distant from grief or the lessening of its control over the heart.

Simply:

Huzn is the state of pain.

Suluw is coming out of that state or becoming calm from it.

But the Qur'an does not use the root س ل و to express the lifting of grief. Verses that comfort the prophets cannot be counted as lexical uses of this root unless a derivative of the root itself appears in the verse.

14. S L W Compared with S K N

The root س ك ن concerns stillness, becoming calm, settling, and place of residence.

Suluw is more connected with the lessening of attachment and grief concerning a particular matter.

A person may find suluw from a loss, while not yet reaching full stillness and assurance in life. A person may also live in a settled place while their grief has not diminished.

Thus:

Sakan: calm, settlement, and stopping of movement.

Suluw: becoming distant from grief or attachment.

This distinction is directional, not absolute.

15. S L W Compared with TT M N

The root ط م ن is close to assurance, settling, and inner calm with stability.

Suluw concerns coming out of intense preoccupation with a loss or pain.

Suluw may be a preliminary step toward assurance, but the two are not one thing:

  • Suluw: the previous grief or attachment loses its dominance.
  • Itmi'nan: the heart reaches stability and settled calm.

The Qur'an uses roots such as s-k-n and t-m-n for the calm of the heart, not s-l-w.

16. S L W Compared with S B R

Sabr concerns holding oneself, remaining firm, and avoiding haste or collapse under pressure.

Suluw points to the lessening of attachment or grief.

A person may be patient while their grief is still very intense. Suluw occurs when that grief or attachment gradually loses its earlier power.

Therefore:

  • Sabr is the way of standing firm in the face of pain.
  • Suluw is the changed inner relation to pain or what has been lost.

Suluw should not be translated as “active patience,” nor should sabr be translated as a “hardware shield.”

17. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

السَّلْوَىٰ

Main suggestion:

Salwa

with a note:

A kind of provision or food provided for the Children of Israel, which early sources usually identify as a bird similar to quail.

In a smoother translation one may write:

mann and the bird salwa

But a definitive translation as “quail” should come with a note of caution, because the Qur'an does not specify the exact species.

سَلَا عَنْهُ

This form is not Qur'anic, but in lexical explanation it may be translated:

  • he became calm from grief over it;
  • he became distant from attachment to it;
  • he set it aside from memory and heart;
  • his grief concerning it subsided.

سَلْوَة

Non-Qur'anic form:

  • consolation;
  • a cause of calm;
  • something that lessens grief.

تَسَلِّي

Non-Qur'anic form:

  • becoming calm from grief;
  • finding consolation;
  • becoming distant from sorrow.

تَسْلِيَة

Non-Qur'anic form:

  • consoling;
  • calming;
  • helping grief lessen.

Expressions such as “damping anxiety oscillation,” “regulating the nervous system,” and “psychological calibration” are neither natural translations nor proven from the root.

18. Conclusion

The root س ل و in Arabic is connected with becoming calm from grief, becoming distant from attachment, and forgetting or weakening the painful memory.

But only one form from this family occurs in the Qur'an:

السَّلْوَىٰ

This word occurs three times, always with al-mann and in the context of provision for the Children of Israel.

Suggested final definition:

S L W: in Arabic, the heart becoming distant from grief or attachment; and in the Qur'an, as-salwa is the name of a provision or food provided with mann for the Children of Israel.

The verses clearly show that salwa was edible and part of the tayyibat of provision. But they do not explain its exact species, manner of provision, nutritional composition, or reason for its naming.

The famous lexical report identifies it as a bird similar to quail. This report may be mentioned, but it should not be presented without caution as the Qur'an's explicit definition.

The possible connection between salwa and consolation is also linguistically noteworthy. This provision may have brought comfort and removed worry for the Children of Israel, but the Qur'an does not present it as a “psychological consolation system.”

Verses that prevent the Prophet from grief or command patience may be calming thematically, but they are not uses of the root س ل و. Likewise tasliyah, salwah, and the description musalli do not occur in the Qur'an and should not be placed in the Qur'anic derivative cards or divine descriptions of this article.

For Forqan translation, the best path is to preserve the word salwa with a clear and careful explanation:

Provision or food of the Children of Israel; in early sources, usually a bird similar to quail.
