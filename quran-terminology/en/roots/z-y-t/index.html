Core meaning: olives, their tree and fruit, and the oil extracted from the fruit. Arabic verbal uses also include applying oil, feeding it to someone, and supplying it to others.

1. The lexical family in early Arabic

The Arabic family ز ي ت concerns a recognizable material and plant: olive oil and the tree producing it. A general abstract meaning need not be assumed to have preceded these concrete names.

زَيْت in its familiar usage is oil extracted from olives.

زَيْتُون names the olive tree and its fruit.

زَيْتُونَة denotes one olive tree or one olive.

Early uses of olive oil extend beyond food to lamp fuel and oiling skin, hair, or other things.

Arabic also has verbs describing actions involving this substance: applying, feeding, or supplying oil. These can be analyzed as denominative verbs without determining the historical order of every form.

A lexical definition and the historical question

An early lexical definition says:

الزَّيْتُ مَعْرُوفٌ، عُصَارَةُ الزَّيْتُونِ

al-zaytu maʿrūfun, ʿuṣāratu al-zaytūn

Meaning:

“Zayt is the familiar substance pressed from olives.”

Three words can be distinguished:

الزَّيْتُون names the tree and its fruit.

الزَّيْتُونَة denotes a single tree or fruit.

الزَّيْت names the extracted oil.

The definition rests on a concrete feature of human life: an olive tree and oil obtained from its fruit. It does not require reducing the family to an abstract principle.

Olives and olive oil have a long history in Mediterranean societies, including uses in food and exchange. Establishing a particular trade route or the date of its connection with Arabia, however, requires specific historical evidence.

A product’s economic history differs from attesting the exact Arabic word in a pre-Islamic poem.

This article supplies no independently dated pre-Islamic Arabic poetic example for زيت or زيتون. That limit of the present evidence does not establish that no such example exists.

Comparable names for olives occur in older regional languages, including ancient Hebrew and Aramaic. Such parallels indicate a wider linguistic history without alone determining how or when the Arabic forms arose.

A Hebrew or Aramaic parallel is therefore not direct evidence for the same Arabic form in pre-Islamic poetry.

A defensible account is:

ز ي ت concerns olive oil and the plant from whose fruit it is extracted. Arabic also forms related verbs for applying, feeding, and supplying oil.

This accords with lexical evidence and Qur’anic usage.

Whether the oil’s name developed from the plant’s name or vice versa requires independent historical evidence.

The formation of زيتون

The precise morphological analysis of زَيْتُون has been disputed.

One analysis connects it with زَيْت and treats its nūn as additional.

Another treats the nūn as radical or proposes a different morphological formation.

Such disagreements do not alter its familiar meaning.

In Qur’anic Arabic, زيتون names the plant or fruit and is grouped with زيت and زيتونة in the lexical classification used here.

Formal similarity alone does not justify reducing زيتون to a simple “زيت + ون” formula and extracting a new meaning from it.

2. Important forms and derivatives

زَيْت

A substance noun, principally referring to olive oil.

The oil is extracted from olives and used in food, lamps, and oiling bodies or objects.

زَيْتُ الزَّيْتُونِ

zaytu al-zaytūn

Meaning: “Olive oil.”

Broader Arabic usage can also apply زيت to vegetable oils generally.

Its direct Qur’anic occurrence in Al-Nūr 24:35 refers to oil from the olive tree named in that verse.

زَيْتُون

A collective noun for olives as a plant kind or collection of fruit.

شَجَرُ الزَّيْتُونِ

shajaru al-zaytūn

Meaning: “Olive trees.”

ثَمَرُ الزَّيْتُونِ

thamaru al-zaytūn

Meaning: “Olive fruit.”

Context can make the tree or the fruit prominent.

The word therefore does not invariably mean a number of individual olives.

When it occurs with palms and other plants in a growth context, the plant kind is in view; when eating produce is discussed, its fruit is also relevant.

زَيْتُونَة

The unit noun corresponding to the collective زيتون.

It can denote one olive tree or one olive fruit.

هَذِهِ زَيْتُونَةٌ

hādhihi zaytūnah

Depending on context: “This is an olive tree” or “This is an olive.”

In Al-Nūr 24:35 it follows شجرة مباركة, so an olive tree is meant.

زَاتَ يَزِيتُ

Recorded for giving or feeding oil to another.

زَاتَ الْقَوْمَ

zāta al-qawm

Meaning: “He fed the group oil.”

This denominative use expresses an action involving the named substance.

زِتُّ الطَّعَامَ

Recorded in early Arabic for mixing or preparing food with oil.

زِتُّ الثَّرِيدَ

zittu al-tharīd

Meaning: “I mixed oil into the tharīd.”

Tharīd is a dish made with bread and other ingredients, to which oil may be added.

Here oil is an ingredient added to food.

زَيَّتَ يُزَيِّتُ

A Form II verb.

One use is applying oil or mixing something with it.

زَيَّتَ الطَّعَامَ

zayyata al-ṭaʿām

Meaning: “He added oil to the food.”

Another use is:

زَيَّتَ الْقَوْمَ

zayyata al-qawm

Meaning: “He supplied the group with oil.”

The material is the same, but the actions differ.

تَزْيِيت

The verbal noun of زيّت: oiling or adding oil.

Modern Arabic also uses it for lubricating tools and machinery.

That industrial application should not be presented as a particular pre-Islamic use.

زَيَّات

Someone who sells or extracts oil.

An occupational noun.

Recorded uses include an oil merchant and an oil presser.

مَزِيت and مَزْيُوت

Both are recorded for food prepared or mixed with oil.

They describe the food’s condition after oil has been added.

اِزْدَاتَ

Recorded for anointing oneself with oil.

اِزْدَاتَ الرَّجُلُ

izdāta al-rajul

Meaning: “The man applied oil to his body.”

This concerns using oil on the body or skin.

أَزَاتُوا

Recorded with the meaning that oil became plentiful among them.

It describes an abundance of the substance.

اِسْتَزَاتَ

Recorded for requesting oil or seeking to receive it.

The formation combines the substance’s name with a request construction.

زَيْتُونِيّ

A relational adjective from زيتون.

It describes something related to olives.

Modern color usage also calls certain shades resembling olives or their leaves “olive.”

That color sense should not be added to the direct meaning of زيتون in the Qur’an.

Only the three nouns زيت, زيتون, and زيتونة occur in the Qur’an; none of the verbs listed above does.

3. Olives in passages about creation and provision

Several verses place olives alongside other plants and fruits.

The contexts concern growth, diversity of produce, human benefit, and recognition of signs of creation.

Al-Anʿām 6:99: growth and diversity

وَهُوَ الَّذِي أَنْزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَأَخْرَجْنَا بِهِ نَبَاتَ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ فَأَخْرَجْنَا مِنْهُ خَضِرًا نُخْرِجُ مِنْهُ حَبًّا مُتَرَاكِبًا وَمِنَ النَّخْلِ مِنْ طَلْعِهَا قِنْوَانٌ دَانِيَةٌ وَجَنَّاتٍ مِنْ أَعْنَابٍ وَالزَّيْتُونَ وَالرُّمَّانَ مُشْتَبِهًا وَغَيْرَ مُتَشَابِهٍ انْظُرُوا إِلَىٰ ثَمَرِهِ إِذَا أَثْمَرَ وَيَنْعِهِ إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكُمْ لَآيَاتٍ لِقَوْمٍ يُؤْمِنُونَ

wa-huwa alladhī anzala mina al-samāʾi māʾan fa-akhrajnā bihi nabāta kulli shayʾin fa-akhrajnā minhu khaḍiran nukhriju minhu ḥabban mutarākiban wa-mina al-nakhli min ṭalʿihā qinwānun dāniyatun wa-jannātin min aʿnābin wa-l-zaytūna wa-l-rummāna mushtabihan wa-ghayra mutashābihin unẓurū ilā thamarihi idhā athmara wa-yanʿihi inna fī dhālikum la-āyātin li-qawmin yuʾminūn

Al-Anʿām 6:99

Translation:

“He is the One who sent water down from the sky. With it We brought forth the growth of every kind, then green growth from which We bring forth grain in clusters; and from the palms, from their spathes, hanging clusters; and gardens of grapes, olives, and pomegranates, similar and dissimilar. Look at their fruit when they bear fruit and at its ripening. In these are signs for people who believe.”

الزَّيْتُونَ occurs alongside grapes and pomegranates.

The context concerns plants growing and producing their crops.

It therefore denotes the familiar plant and fruit, rather than an ethical concept or symbolic title detached from its material meaning.

The continuation directs attention to fruit and ripening.

An olive tree, like other fruit-bearing plants, develops toward bearing and ripening its crop.

Fruit color, size, and quality can vary among trees and varieties.

مُشْتَبِهًا وَغَيْرَ مُتَشَابِهٍ alone specifies no particular olive characteristic, such as color or oil content. It forms part of the wider description of plants and produce.

The main invitation is to observe fruit and its ripening.

Observation of nature is thus connected with recognizing signs of creation.

Al-Anʿām 6:141: produce and rights

وَهُوَ الَّذِي أَنْشَأَ جَنَّاتٍ مَعْرُوشَاتٍ وَغَيْرَ مَعْرُوشَاتٍ وَالنَّخْلَ وَالزَّرْعَ مُخْتَلِفًا أُكُلُهُ وَالزَّيْتُونَ وَالرُّمَّانَ مُتَشَابِهًا وَغَيْرَ مُتَشَابِهٍ كُلُوا مِنْ ثَمَرِهِ إِذَا أَثْمَرَ وَآتُوا حَقَّهُ يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ وَلَا تُسْرِفُوا إِنَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ الْمُسْرِفِينَ

wa-huwa alladhī anshaʾa jannātin maʿrūshātin wa-ghayra maʿrūshātin wa-l-nakhla wa-l-zarʿa mukhtalifan ukuluhu wa-l-zaytūna wa-l-rummāna mutashābihan wa-ghayra mutashābihin kulū min thamarihi idhā athmara wa-ātū ḥaqqahu yawma ḥaṣādihi wa-lā tusrifū innahu lā yuḥibbu al-musrifīn

Al-Anʿām 6:141

Translation:

“He is the One who brought forth gardens, trellised and untrellised, palms and crops with differing produce, and olives and pomegranates, similar and dissimilar. Eat of their fruit when they bear fruit, and give its due on the day of harvest. Do not be excessive; He does not love those who are excessive.”

Olives again occur among agricultural products.

Human benefit and responsibility are added to the themes of creation and diversity.

كُلُوا مِنْ ثَمَرِهِ permits eating the produce.

وَآتُوا حَقَّهُ يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ instructs giving its due at harvest.

وَلَا تُسْرِفُوا prohibits excess.

The sequence connects produce with rights and responsibilities beyond personal ownership and consumption.

Olives are one of the products within that context.

The noun زيتون does not itself mean alms, charity, or a harvest obligation.

Those meanings come from the verse’s separate commands.

This wording specifies neither a percentage of olive produce nor a calculation method. Such a ruling requires separate evidence.

Al-Naḥl 16:11: olives among other plants

يُنْبِتُ لَكُمْ بِهِ الزَّرْعَ وَالزَّيْتُونَ وَالنَّخِيلَ وَالْأَعْنَابَ وَمِنْ كُلِّ الثَّمَرَاتِ إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لِقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ

yunbitu lakum bihi al-zarʿa wa-l-zaytūna wa-l-nakhīla wa-l-aʿnāba wa-min kulli al-thamarāti inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-qawmin yatafakkarūn

Al-Naḥl 16:11

Translation:

“With it He grows for you crops, olives, palms, grapes, and every kind of fruit. In that is a sign for people who reflect.”

الزَّيْتُونَ is coordinated with الزَّرْعَ as an object of يُنْبِتُ.

The verb belongs to ن ب ت and means causing to grow.

Here زيتون therefore concerns the plant, rather than extracted oil alone.

Al-Naḥl 16:10 mentions water sent down and vegetation used by livestock.

Al-Naḥl 16:11 names produce used by people.

The verse then presents this growth as a sign for reflection.

Olives are a concrete example of provision and creation’s diversity.

Mentioning them does not establish that they are the only superior or useful plant.

The same verse also names crops, palms, grapes, and other fruits.

4. Olives among foods and Qur’anic oaths

ʿAbasa 80:29: olives and palms

وَزَيْتُونًا وَنَخْلًا

wa-zaytūnan wa-nakhlan

ʿAbasa 80:29

Translation:

“And olives and palms.”

The passage begins in ʿAbasa 80:24 by inviting people to consider their food.

Subsequent verses describe rainfall, the earth opening, grain, grapes, vegetation, olives, palms, gardens, and further produce.

ʿAbasa 80:32 presents these as provision for people and livestock.

Olives belong here to food and sustenance.

زَيْتُونًا alone does not specify exclusively eating the fruit or using its oil.

Both are familiar benefits of the plant.

“Olives” is sufficient in direct translation; replacing it with “olive oil” is unnecessary.

Al-Tīn 95:1: the oath by fig and olive

وَالتِّينِ وَالزَّيْتُونِ

wa-l-tīni wa-l-zaytūn

Al-Tīn 95:1

Translation:

“By the fig and the olive.”

الزَّيْتُونِ is genitive, coordinated with التِّينِ. In the familiar grammatical analysis, the first wāw introduces the oath and the second coordinates the two nouns.

Lexically, it denotes the familiar olive.

The following verses mention طُورِ سِينِينَ and الْبَلَدِ الْأَمِينِ.

This sequence has led some readings to associate the fig and olive with regions where they are known, in addition to their botanical senses.

The noun زيتون itself does not lexically mean a mountain, city, or mosque.

A geographical reading needs support from the oath’s context and relevant evidence about places.

“The fig and the olive” remains a clear direct translation.

The oath does not itself command worship of the tree or rites around it.

Its role should be considered alongside the surah’s message, particularly Al-Tīn 95:4 and its description of human creation in the finest form.

5. The olive tree and its oil in the light verse

Al-Nūr 24:35

اللَّهُ نُورُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ مَثَلُ نُورِهِ كَمِشْكَاةٍ فِيهَا مِصْبَاحٌ الْمِصْبَاحُ فِي زُجَاجَةٍ الزُّجَاجَةُ كَأَنَّهَا كَوْكَبٌ دُرِّيٌّ يُوقَدُ مِنْ شَجَرَةٍ مُبَارَكَةٍ زَيْتُونَةٍ لَا شَرْقِيَّةٍ وَلَا غَرْبِيَّةٍ يَكَادُ زَيْتُهَا يُضِيءُ وَلَوْ لَمْ تَمْسَسْهُ نَارٌ نُورٌ عَلَىٰ نُورٍ يَهْدِي اللَّهُ لِنُورِهِ مَنْ يَشَاءُ وَيَضْرِبُ اللَّهُ الْأَمْثَالَ لِلنَّاسِ وَاللَّهُ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

Allāhu nūru al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi mathalu nūrihi ka-mishkātin fīhā miṣbāḥun al-miṣbāḥu fī zujājatin al-zujājatu ka-annahā kawkabun durriyyun yūqadu min shajaratin mubārakatin zaytūnatin lā sharqiyyatin wa-lā gharbiyyatin yakādu zaytuhā yuḍīʾu wa-law lam tamsashu nārun nūrun ʿalā nūrin yahdī Allāhu li-nūrihi man yashāʾu wa-yaḍribu Allāhu al-amthāla li-l-nāsi wa-Allāhu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm

Al-Nūr 24:35

Translation:

“The God is the light of the heavens and earth. The likeness of His light is a niche containing a lamp. The lamp is in glass; the glass is like a radiant star, lit from a blessed tree, an olive, neither eastern nor western. Its oil almost shines even though no fire has touched it. Light upon light! The God guides whom He wills to His light. The God sets forth examples for people, and The God knows all things.”

Two distinct forms occur:

زَيْتُونَةٍ

zaytūnatin

Al-Nūr 24:35

And:

زَيْتُهَا

zaytuhā

Al-Nūr 24:35

Their distinction is essential to the picture.

زَيْتُونَة: an olive tree

In مِنْ شَجَرَةٍ مُبَارَكَةٍ زَيْتُونَةٍ, زيتونة identifies the شجرة.

An olive tree is therefore meant.

مباركة describes the tree.

Its fruit and oil have familiar benefits that can inform understanding of its blessedness within the image.

زيتونة itself does not mean blessing; مباركة supplies that description.

The description alone does not establish supernatural powers for every olive tree or make it an object of worship.

The tree forms part of the likeness describing divine light.

زَيْتُهَا: that tree’s oil

The verse continues:

يَكَادُ زَيْتُهَا يُضِيءُ

yakādu zaytuhā yuḍīʾu

Al-Nūr 24:35

Meaning:

“Its oil almost shines.”

زَيْت names the oil.

The pronoun ها refers to the olive tree.

زيتها therefore means that tree’s oil.

The distinction between the plant’s name and the extracted substance is clear.

An olive tree is not oil.

Its fruit also differs in form and character from the extracted oil.

These nevertheless belong to the same lexical family, and the verse connects tree and oil.

Neither eastern nor western

The tree is described as:

لَا شَرْقِيَّةٍ وَلَا غَرْبِيَّةٍ

lā sharqiyyatin wa-lā gharbiyyatin

Al-Nūr 24:35

“Neither eastern nor western.”

The wording negates two directional or geographical descriptions.

One familiar interpretation places the tree in an open location, receiving sunlight from more than one side.

On that reading, sunlight is not confined to the beginning or end of the day: the tree stands where it receives light from both directions.

This is associated in the interpretation with good fruit and fine oil.

The words themselves state no precise duration of exposure, light quantity, or growing conditions.

Another reading emphasizes the larger likeness of divine light rather than a particular earthly tree; it does not restrict that light to a geographical direction.

The verse explicitly presents a likeness, so its figurative dimension matters.

Even so, “eastern” and “western” should remain in direct translation, with interpretations distinguished from the wording.

يَكَادُ زَيْتُهَا يُضِيءُ: almost shining

يَكَادُ expresses proximity to an action’s occurrence.

The verse does not state unconditionally that oil actually generates light like the sun.

Within the likeness, the oil is so closely associated with luminosity that it almost shines before fire touches it.

وَلَوْ لَمْ تَمْسَسْهُ نَارٌ reinforces the image.

In familiar experience, oil serves as lamp fuel and provides light when ignited.

Olive oil was used in oil lamps.

The light verse is not an independent chemical account of olive oil or spontaneous luminescence.

It uses lamp, glass, tree, oil, and illumination to construct a likeness of divine light.

Its figurative character does not empty those objects of their familiar meanings.

The image works through recognizable elements.

Light upon light and the olive imagery

The verse opens by describing The God as the light of the heavens and earth.

It then presents a niche, lamp, glass, radiant star, and oil from an olive tree.

It continues:

نُورٌ عَلَىٰ نُورٍ

nūrun ʿalā nūr

Al-Nūr 24:35

“Light upon light.”

The ending mentions guidance to divine light and examples set forth for people.

The verse centers on divine light, rather than the olive plant itself.

The tree and oil are tangible components helping the audience understand the likeness.

زيت should therefore not be translated directly as revelation, faith, reason, or guidance.

Those concepts may be explored in reflections on the complete likeness; زيت still means oil.

Nor does the olive’s presence assign it an independent role in guiding or saving people apart from The God.

The ending attributes guidance to The God.

6. The oil-bearing tree in Al-Muʾminūn 23:20

Al-Muʾminūn 23:20 says:

وَشَجَرَةً تَخْرُجُ مِنْ طُورِ سَيْنَاءَ تَنْبُتُ بِالدُّهْنِ وَصِبْغٍ لِلْآكِلِينَ

wa-shajaratan takhruju min ṭūri saynāʾa tanbutu bi-l-duhni wa-ṣibghin li-l-ākilīn

Al-Muʾminūn 23:20

Translation:

“And a tree growing from Mount Sinai, producing oil and a relish for those who eat.”

None of زيت, زيتون, or زيتونة occurs here.

The verse describes a tree associated with oil and food.

The familiar identification as an olive tree fits those features: its fruit yields oil, and both fruit and oil are eaten.

The wording of the two passages must nevertheless remain distinct.

Al-Nūr 24:35 explicitly calls the tree زيتونة.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:20 describes it through oil and food without naming the olive.

Its connection with olives is interpretive, supported by the described features, rather than a direct occurrence of ز ي ت.

زيت and دهن

دُهْن, from د ه ن, denotes an oily or fatty substance.

زَيْت principally names olive oil.

دهن has a broader scope, while زيت in the light verse identifies oil from the olive tree.

In this use, زيت is a kind of دهن, but not every دهن is olive oil.

This distinction matters for the two passages.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:20 emphasizes the tree’s production of oil.

Al-Nūr 24:35 names both the tree and its oil.

زيت and صبغ

صِبْغ belongs to ص ب غ.

The root concerns coloring and dipping something into a substance that affects it.

In Al-Muʾminūn 23:20, صِبْغٍ لِلْآكِلِينَ occurs in a food context.

It is therefore understood as a relish or accompaniment for food.

When bread is dipped in oil, the oil serves both as a fatty substance and as a food accompaniment.

زيت itself does not mean صبغ.

The two expressions each contribute their own meaning.

7. Real benefits and figurative use

The Qur’anic occurrences present two principal fields of use.

First, a real plant that grows, bears fruit, and benefits people.

Second, a tree whose oil contributes to the likeness of light.

These are connected.

Familiarity with olives and their oil helps the audience picture the oil lamp in Al-Nūr 24:35.

The likeness draws on recognizable material life.

That figurative use does not mean every Qur’anic olive reference carries the same symbolism.

Al-Anʿām 6:99 concerns growth and ripening.

Al-Anʿām 6:141 also concerns produce and its due at harvest.

Al-Naḥl 16:11 presents olives among plants grown through water.

ʿAbasa 80:29 places them among foods.

Al-Tīn 95:1 includes them in an oath.

Al-Nūr 24:35 makes the tree and oil components of the likeness of divine light.

The lexical meaning remains recognizable while its role in each verse’s argument or imagery changes.

Does blessedness establish absolute superiority?

Al-Nūr 24:35 describes the olive tree as مباركة.

That description is significant.

The tree has real uses as a source of food, oil, and lamp fuel.

The adjective does not establish that other plants lack blessing or that olives surpass every other plant in every dietary, agricultural, or human circumstance.

The Qur’an also presents palms, grapes, grains, and other produce as benefits of creation.

Human needs vary with geography, climate, availability, and living conditions.

A plant’s blessedness does not by itself establish compulsory consumption or replacement of all other foods.

These olive passages state no such requirement.

8. Related words and roots

ز ي ت (زَيْت) and د ه ن (دُهْن)

Qur’anic زيت refers to olive oil.

دهن denotes oily or fatty substances more broadly.

Al-Nūr 24:35 names the olive oil.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:20 uses دهن in describing an oil-bearing tree.

They overlap in oil-related meaning without having identical scope.

ز ي ت (زَيْتُون) and ن ب ت (نَبَات)

نبات concerns growth and vegetation.

زيتون names a particular plant.

Al-Naḥl 16:11 uses يُنْبِتُ for producing growth, with olives among its objects.

Growing is a process; olive is a plant name.

Growth therefore should not be inserted into the brief root definition merely because olive trees grow.

ز ي ت (زَيْتُون) and ت ي ن (تِين)

تين denotes the fig.

زيتون denotes the olive tree or fruit.

Al-Tīn 95:1 joins them in an oath.

The coordination connects their roles in the verse without making them one root or one word a lexical explanation of the other.

ز ي ت (زَيْت) and ن و ر (نُور)

نور concerns light.

زيت names oil.

Al-Nūr 24:35 places olive oil in the image of a lamp and illumination.

Oil and light are nevertheless distinct.

Oil can fuel a lamp and contribute to illumination when ignited.

The same verse connects divine light with guidance.

That figurative relationship does not erase زيت’s material meaning.

ز ي ت (زَيْت) and س ل ط (سَلِيط)

سليط denotes oil in some Arabic usage, particularly sesame oil in certain dialectal accounts.

Lexical reports vary regionally, recording sesame oil in some places and olive oil or oil more broadly elsewhere.

It overlaps with زيت without being an exact equivalent across all dialects and periods.

سليط does not occur in the Qur’an.

ز ي ت (زَيْت) and ز ي ن (زِينَة)

The roots resemble one another in letters but differ in meaning.

زيت concerns oil and the olive plant.

زينة concerns adornment and beauty.

Oil’s use for grooming or skin and hair care does not make the roots identical.

9. Translation proposals for Forqan

The following equivalents suit the article and its Qur’anic discussion. They do not replace installed verse translations or existing authored word-box explanations.

زَيْت: olive oil; in wider Arabic usage, sometimes vegetable oil.

زَيْتُون: olive, its tree or fruit as determined by context.

زَيْتُونَة: one olive tree or one olive.

زَاتَ: gave or fed oil, in the relevant use.

زِتُّ الطَّعَامَ: I added oil to the food or mixed it with oil.

زَيَّتَ: applied or supplied oil.

تَزْيِيت: oiling or adding oil.

زَيَّات: oil presser or oil merchant.

مَزِيت / مَزْيُوت: food mixed with oil.

اِزْدَاتَ: anointed himself with oil.

أَزَاتُوا: oil became plentiful among them.

اِسْتَزَاتَ: requested oil.

زَيْتُونِيّ: related to olives; in modern color usage, olive-colored.

Brief root meaning

“Olives and the oil extracted from their fruit.”

Verse-specific meanings

In Al-Anʿām 6:99, Al-Anʿām 6:141, Al-Naḥl 16:11, and ʿAbasa 80:29:

“The olive plant and its produce, among vegetation and human foods.”

In Al-Tīn 95:1:

“The olive, the familiar plant or fruit included in the oath.”

For زَيْتُونَةٍ in Al-Nūr 24:35:

“An olive tree, whose fruit supplies oil for the lamp in the likeness.”

For زَيْتُهَا:

“That olive tree’s oil.”

Direct translation should not turn زيتونة into “the tree of guidance” or زيتها into “the light of faith.”

Nor should every agricultural زيتون be translated simply as olive oil regardless of context.

Plant, fruit, and oil must remain distinct, while figurative interpretations belong in reflection on the verse.

10. Conclusion

ز ي ت concerns olives and their oil.

زيتون names the tree or fruit; زيتونة one tree or fruit; زيت the extracted oil.

Olive oil has a long regional history as food and lamp fuel. Direct Arabic linguistic evidence must nevertheless be distinguished from historical information and parallels in related languages when describing pre-Islamic usage.

This lexical family occurs seven times in six Qur’anic verses, in three nominal forms.

Five occurrences of زيتون concern creation, produce, food, or an oath.

زيتونة and زيت occur together in Al-Nūr 24:35, connecting the tree with its oil.

In agricultural passages, the olive is a real plant that grows, bears fruit, and benefits people.

In the light verse, this familiar reality contributes to a likeness of divine light.

The likeness does not erase the material meanings of tree and oil, just as those meanings do not prevent reflection on the imagery.

The Qur’anic family names the olive plant, its fruit, and the oil extracted from it.

It does not itself name a moral virtue or mean guidance, blessing, or divine illumination.

Blessedness comes from مباركة, shining from يضيء, and guidance from the larger likeness of light.

Keeping these meanings distinct preserves both the plant’s material reality and its precise role in Qur’anic imagery.
