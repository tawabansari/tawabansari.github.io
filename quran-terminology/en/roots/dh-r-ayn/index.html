Forearm and forelimb; extension and measurement; ability, capacity and limits of power

The Arabic root DH R ʿ relates to extending a hand or forelimb, covering distance and measuring, and the extent of ability and capacity. Dhirāʿ names part of a human or animal limb and also serves as a unit of length. Dharʿ can denote measuring something; in expressions such as ḍāqa bihi dharʿan, it concerns capacity that is constrained by a difficult situation.

The Qur’an uses this root in three semantic fields: the Companions of the Cave’s dog stretches its forelegs across the threshold; Lot finds his ability and resources constrained when the divine messengers arrive; and a chain in a scene of the hereafter is described as seventy cubits long.

An animal’s limbs, human helplessness and a chain’s length initially seem different subjects. Arabic usage shows, however, that dhirāʿ denotes both a limb and a familiar means of measuring length, while dharʿ also describes human capacity and ability.

These meanings should not be forced into a single physical image. When the Qur’an says that Lot ḍāqa bihim dharʿan, it does not mean his arms literally became shorter. It uses a recognized expression for finding one’s capacity and resources constrained.

The root occurs five times in four verses, using the two nominal bases dharʿ and dhirāʿ:

Dharʿan in Hūd 11:77 and Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:33; dhirāʿayhi in Al-Kahf 18:18; and dharʿuhā and dhirāʿan in Al-Ḥāqqah 69:32.

No verb from this root occurs in the Qur’an; all five occurrences are nominal.

1. Central meaning and usage in early Arabic

Limb, extension and movement

Extension and forward movement can be identified as a central meaning connected with several recognized branches of DH R ʿ.

This explanation relates to the familiar use of dhirāʿ.

For the human limb, dhirāʿ is described as the span from the elbow to the end of the middle finger, including the hand. For quadrupeds, it denotes the forelimb or its anterior portion.

Arabic expressions include:

Dharaʿa al-baʿīru yadahu: the camel extended its foreleg forward while walking.

Tadharraʿa al-baʿīru: the camel stretched its forelimb in motion.

Here extension of the limb relates to movement. By moving and extending its forelegs, the animal takes steps and covers distance.

Dharīʿ is also used for an animal or person with a long stride and quick movement.

Speed and long strides are not necessary meanings of every derivative, however. Qur’anic dhirāʿayhi describes the dog’s limbs in a reclining position, rather than its running speed.

Dhirāʿ as a means and unit of measurement

The readily available length of the human hand and forearm also made dhirāʿ a unit for measuring length.

Arabic expressions include:

Dharaʿa al-thawba: he measured the cloth in cubits.

Dharaʿa al-ḥāʾiṭa: he measured the wall’s length or dimensions.

Dharʿu al-shayʾi: a thing’s measurement or length.

Al-dhirāʿ: both the limb and the unit of length.

These were familiar practical uses, from measuring cloth to determining the dimensions of walls, tools and structures.

As a measurement unit, the cubit could have different conventional lengths in different periods and places. Historical occurrences should therefore not all be converted without examination into one universal number of centimeters.

The distinction between limb and measurement unit matters. In Al-Kahf 18:18, dhirāʿ denotes the animal’s limb; in Al-Ḥāqqah 69:32, the same word is a unit of length.

Dharʿ: measure, extent and ability

Besides measurement, dharʿ also expresses ability and capacity.

Recognized expressions include:

Wāsiʿu al-dharʿi: someone with ample capacity, forbearance or ability.

Ḍāqa bi-al-amri dharʿan: his capacity and resources proved constrained in facing a matter.

Mā lī bihi dharʿun: I do not have the ability for that task.

In these expressions, physical measure extends to the range of one’s ability.

Early explanations compare this use with extending the hand and reaching something. Someone whose hand cannot reach a thing has limited access to it; similarly, someone unable to carry out a task is pictured as having a reach that falls short of it.

Another explanation uses the camel’s stride. A camel covers distance by extending its forelegs, but when pushed beyond its capacity it can no longer sustain the same movement.

These images illuminate the relationship between extension and capacity. They should not be treated as a proven historical account of the expression ḍāqa dharʿan. What usage establishes is that the expression denotes ability and capacity.

Other recorded uses

Other recorded derivatives include tadharraʿa, dharraʿa, adhraʿa, dharīʿ, midhrāʿ and dharīʿah.

Midhrāʿ can denote an animal’s forelimb. Some uses of tadharraʿa concern extending the hand or advancing with broad strides.

Dharīʿah is recognized as a means or way of attaining something, and tadharraʿa ilayhi for seeking a means of access or resorting to something.

Dharaʿahu al-qayʾu describes someone overcome by involuntary vomiting. This expression concerns something overtaking the person and overcoming their control.

The family therefore extends beyond limbs and measurement. Its branches should not nevertheless be reduced to an excessively general meaning such as “any movement” or “any ability.”

Actual Qur’anic usage must guide the interpretation of each derivative.

2. Witnesses from early Arabic poetry

Qays ibn al-Khaṭīm: working palm strips on the forearm

Qays ibn al-Khaṭīm, a pre-Islamic poet of the Aws tribe, describes spears and a battle scene:

تَرَى قِصَدَ الْمُرَّانِ تُلْقَى كَأَنَّهَا
تَذَرُّعُ خُرْصَانٍ بِأَيْدِي الشَّوَاطِبِ

tarā qiṣada al-murrāni tulqā ka-annahā / tadharruʿu khurṣānin bi-aydī al-shawāṭib

Translation: You see the broken spear pieces falling, as though they were palm rods that women preparing palm leaves lay on their forearms and strip.

Qiṣad denotes pieces or broken portions of spears; murrān concerns spear wood.

Khurṣān also denotes palm rods and branches in one Arabic use.

Shawāṭib are women who strip palm leaves or other palm material and prepare it for uses such as weaving.

One explanation takes tadharraʿa al-jarīda to mean placing a palm rod on one’s forearm to strip or prepare it.

Another takes tadharruʿ as a cubit-length piece that breaks off and falls, and khirṣān as parts of spears near their points. On that reading, the image concerns spear fragments. The first explanation more clearly accounts for comparing those fragments with palm rods in the women’s hands.

Some transmissions have tahwī instead of tulqā: the spear fragments fall.

The variation between tulqā and tahwī does not substantially alter the falling image. The two explanations of tadharruʿ must nevertheless remain distinct: work on the forearm, or a piece a cubit long.

On the palm-rod reading, this witness describes the material use of the forearm in preparing long objects. The other reading foregrounds measurement and spear fragments. Attribution to a pre-Islamic poet alone does not establish the poem’s exact date relative to the beginning of Qur’anic revelation.

Ḥumayd ibn Thawr: lam yaḍiq dhirāʿan

Ḥumayd ibn Thawr al-Hilālī, a poet who lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods, describes a wolf spending a night hungry:

وَإِنْ بَاتَ وَحْشًا لَيْلَةً لَمْ يَضِقْ بِهَا
ذِرَاعًا وَلَمْ يُصْبِحْ لَهَا وَهُوَ خَاشِعُ

wa-in bāta waḥshan laylatan lam yaḍiq bihā / dhirāʿan wa-lam yuṣbiḥ lahā wa-huwa khāshiʿ

Translation: Even if it spends a night hungry, it is not distressed by it, nor does it greet the morning humbled and helpless before it.

Waḥshan here concerns hunger and an empty stomach. The poet describes a wolf that is not overcome by one hungry night. Rendering the word as solitude obscures the intended sense of this witness.

Lam yaḍiq bihā dhirāʿan means that its ability and capacity do not give way before the difficult situation.

The point is not that the wolf’s limb length remains unchanged. It concerns its ability to endure and get through the conditions.

Some versions have khāḍiʿ instead of khāshiʿ. The final-word variation does not change the meaning of lam yaḍiq bihā dhirāʿan.

Ḥumayd ibn Thawr lived before and after Islam, but this verse’s date is unknown. It cannot confidently be presented as a poem predating the Qur’an.

It nevertheless shows that ḍāqa dhirāʿan was recognized in Arabic poetry for constrained capacity and helplessness before circumstances.

It is directly related in meaning to Qur’anic ḍāqa bihim dharʿan in Lot’s story.

3. Dhirāʿayhi: the forelegs of the Companions of the Cave’s dog

Al-Kahf 18:18 states:

وَتَحْسَبُهُمْ أَيْقَاظًا وَهُمْ رُقُودٌ وَنُقَلِّبُهُمْ ذَاتَ الْيَمِينِ وَذَاتَ الشِّمَالِ وَكَلْبُهُمْ بَاسِطٌ ذِرَاعَيْهِ بِالْوَصِيدِ لَوِ اطَّلَعْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ لَوَلَّيْتَ مِنْهُمْ فِرَارًا وَلَمُلِئْتَ مِنْهُمْ رُعْبًا

wa-taḥsabuhum ayqāẓan wa-hum ruqūdun wa-nuqallibuhum dhāta al-yamīni wa-dhāta al-shimāli wa-kalbuhum bāsiṭun dhirāʿayhi bi-al-waṣīdi lawi iṭṭalaʿta ʿalayhim la-wallayta minhum firāran wa-la-muliʾta minhum ruʿbā

Translation: You would think them awake, though they are asleep. We turn them to the right and left, while their dog stretches its two forelegs across the threshold. If you had looked upon them, you would have fled from them and been filled with fear of them.

Dhirāʿayhi is the dual of dhirāʿ with a possessive pronoun: its two forelimbs.

The pronoun refers to the Companions of the Cave’s dog.

Dhirāʿ names the animal’s forelimb here. “Its two forelegs” or “its two forelimbs” is therefore appropriate.

Bāsiṭun is an active participle from B S Ṭ. In this sentence it describes the dog’s forelegs as stretched out.

The scene accordingly places the dog beside the Companions of the Cave, with its forelegs extended.

Bi-al-waṣīdi specifies its location: at the threshold or entrance of the cave.

The description allows the dog’s bodily position to be imagined, but details not stated by the Qur’an should not be added.

For example, the verse does not say that the dog was attacking, baring its teeth or continuously guarding the cave. Such ideas might arise in reflection on its position, but are not established by dhirāʿayhi and bāsiṭun themselves.

Dhirāʿ is not a measurement unit here. The verse does not say that the dog was two cubits long; it describes its two forelimbs.

This occurrence establishes the familiar bodily sense of dhirāʿ in the Qur’an and is also relevant to understanding the family’s measurement uses.

4. Ḍāqa bihim dharʿan: constrained ability and resources

Hūd 11:77: Lot’s concern for his guests

Hūd 11:77 states:

وَلَمَّا جَاءَتْ رُسُلُنَا لُوطًا سِيءَ بِهِمْ وَضَاقَ بِهِمْ ذَرْعًا وَقَالَ هَٰذَا يَوْمٌ عَصِيبٌ

wa-lammā jāʾat rusulunā lūṭan sīʾa bihim wa-ḍāqa bihim dharʿan wa-qāla hādhā yawmun ʿaṣīb

Translation: When Our messengers came to Lot, he was distressed by their arrival and felt his capacity and resources constrained concerning them. He said, “This is a difficult day.”

Dharʿan appears in the recognized expression ḍāqa bihim dharʿan.

The Arabic expression concerns limited ability, capacity and resources for dealing with a difficult situation.

Dharʿan is accusative and functions as a specification clarifying the respect in which the constraint is experienced.

Early grammatical explanations relate the construction to ḍāqa dharʿī bihi: my capacity became constrained in facing it.

Dharʿan here therefore denotes neither the hand nor a unit of length. The complete phrase is a recognized expression for helplessness and constrained capacity.

Distress and helplessness distinguished

The verse places two expressions in succession:

Sīʾa bihim: he was upset and distressed concerning them.

Wa-ḍāqa bihim dharʿan: his ability and resources became constrained concerning their situation.

The expressions concern one situation but are not entirely synonymous.

The first chiefly expresses Lot’s distress and concern; the second foregrounds his limited ability to face the situation.

The story continues in Hūd 11:78 with the arrival and conduct of Lot’s people. He asks them not to disgrace him concerning his guests and to be mindful of The God.

In Hūd 11:80, he also says:

قَالَ لَوْ أَنَّ لِي بِكُمْ قُوَّةً أَوْ آوِي إِلَىٰ رُكْنٍ شَدِيدٍ

qāla law anna lī bikum quwwatan aw āwī ilā ruknin shadīd

Translation: He said, “If only I had strength against you, or could take refuge in a strong support.”

This helps explain ḍāqa bihim dharʿan. Faced with his people’s conduct, Lot feels a lack of power and support.

His constrained capacity should therefore not be turned into displeasure with the divine messengers themselves. The issue is the difficult situation arising from their arrival and the threatened conduct of his people.

Nor does ḍāqa bihim dharʿan imply weakness in Lot’s faith or ethics. Limited practical ability in the face of danger is not the same as defective faith or intention.

The expression depicts a human situation: someone recognizes danger and tries to prevent it but finds their own power and resources insufficient.

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:33: the same expression with a different continuation

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:33 states:

وَلَمَّا أَنْ جَاءَتْ رُسُلُنَا لُوطًا سِيءَ بِهِمْ وَضَاقَ بِهِمْ ذَرْعًا وَقَالُوا لَا تَخَفْ وَلَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّا مُنَجُّوكَ وَأَهْلَكَ إِلَّا امْرَأَتَكَ كَانَتْ مِنَ الْغَابِرِينَ

wa-lammā an jāʾat rusulunā lūṭan sīʾa bihim wa-ḍāqa bihim dharʿan wa-qālū lā takhaf wa-lā taḥzan innā munajjūka wa-ahlaka illā imraʾataka kānat mina al-ghābirīn

Translation: When Our messengers came to Lot, he was distressed by their arrival and felt his capacity and resources constrained concerning them. They said, “Do not fear or grieve. We will rescue you and your family, except your wife, who will be among those left behind.”

Wa-ḍāqa bihim dharʿan is repeated here.

The continuation differs from Hūd 11:77. In Al-ʿAnkabūt, the messengers tell Lot not to fear or grieve and announce his rescue and that of his family.

Their response shows that his condition involved fear, grief and limited ability to deal with the impending event.

Together the verses show that ḍāqa bihim dharʿan concerns more than inward sorrow. It also involves helplessness before circumstances and limited resources for responding.

Helplessness here does not mean Lot was incapable of everything. He faces constraints in a particular situation.

As in Hūd, the expression should not be turned into literal shortening of Lot’s hands or physical weakness in his limbs.

This clearly demonstrates the extended use of dharʿ for human ability and capacity.

5. Dharʿuhā sabʿūna dhirāʿan: measurement and its unit

Al-Ḥāqqah 69:32 states:

ثُمَّ فِي سِلْسِلَةٍ ذَرْعُهَا سَبْعُونَ ذِرَاعًا فَاسْلُكُوهُ

thumma fī silsilatin dharʿuhā sabʿūna dhirāʿan fa-uslukūh

Study of S L S L

Translation: Then put him into a chain whose length is seventy cubits.

The verse contains two derivatives of one root:

Dharʿuhā: its measurement or length.

Dhirāʿan: a cubit, the unit of length.

The pronoun hā in dharʿuhā refers to the chain. Dharʿ denotes its measurement or length, while sabʿūna dhirāʿan gives the amount of that length.

Dharʿ here is not a figurative expression for ability or resources; it concerns the actual measurement of length.

Dharʿ and dhirāʿ distinguished

Dharʿ here denotes the measurement or its result, whereas dhirāʿ is the unit in which the length is expressed.

The relationship can be compared with “length” and “meter” in present-day usage, although the cubit and meter differ in both magnitude and history.

For example: “This cloth’s length is five cubits.”

“Length” names the quantity measured, while “cubit” names the unit.

The same distinction appears in Al-Ḥāqqah 69:32.

The verse is especially significant for the family because it places the measurement and the unit’s name side by side.

Is seventy cubits symbolic?

The verse explicitly says sabʿūna dhirāʿan: seventy cubits.

A direct translation should retain that number.

Neither DH R ʿ itself nor the presence of seventy establishes that only indefinite abundance is intended.

A figurative reading of the number or unit requires independent evidence and should not replace the direct wording without justification.

The verse also describes a scene of the hereafter. The exact type of cubit, its equivalent in modern units and the chain’s physical properties cannot therefore be specified without evidence.

The text explicitly presents a chain whose stated length is seventy cubits.

Al-Ḥāqqah 69:30 and Al-Ḥāqqah 69:31 describe restraining and punishing the person. Al-Ḥāqqah 69:33 and Al-Ḥāqqah 69:34 explain his condition by mentioning lack of faith and failure to encourage feeding the poor.

Dharʿuhā itself does not mean punishment, penalty or sin. It specifies only the chain’s length.

6. Comparison with related roots

DH R ʿ (dharaʿa) and Z R ʿ (zaraʿa): measurement and cultivation

Study of Z R ʿ

The similar spelling and sound can lead these roots to be confused.

DH R ʿ, with dhāl, concerns the forelimb, extension, measurement and capacity.

Z R ʿ, with zāy, concerns cultivation, seed and growth.

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:64 states:

أَأَنْتُمْ تَزْرَعُونَهُ أَمْ نَحْنُ الزَّارِعُونَ

a-antum tazraʿūnahu am naḥnu al-zāriʿūn

Translation: Is it you who make it grow, or are We the One who makes it grow?

Tazraʿūnahu and al-zāriʿūn belong to Z R ʿ.

Dharʿuhā in Al-Ḥāqqah 69:32 therefore does not mean cultivation or causing growth; dhirāʿayhi in Al-Kahf 18:18 likewise does not concern agriculture.

Similar spelling is not evidence of a shared derivation.

DH R ʿ (dharaʿa) and D R ʿ (dariʿa): forearm and armor

D R ʿ, with dāl, is a different family, including dirʿ, “armor.”

Armor is a covering that protects the body; dhirāʿ names a limb and a measurement unit.

The words resemble one another but do not belong to the same root.

Their resemblance does not warrant translating Qur’anic dhirāʿ as protective covering or armor.

DH R ʿ (dharʿ) and W S ʿ (wusʿ): ability and capacity

Study of W S ʿ

Wusʿ concerns human ability and capacity and is used in the Qur’an for the limits of ability and responsibility.

Al-Baqarah 2:286 states:

لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا

lā yukallifu allāhu nafsan illā wusʿahā

Translation: The God does not charge any soul beyond its capacity.

Dharʿ in ḍāqa dharʿan likewise concerns ability and capacity.

Wusʿ has a broad field of capacity and ability, whereas dharʿ in the construction discussed particularly describes a person’s constrained ability in a specific situation.

This is a tendency rather than an absolute rule for all Arabic uses.

DH R ʿ (dharʿ) and Q D R (qadara): measure and ability

Study of Q D R

Qadara and its derivatives also concern measuring, determining an amount and possessing ability.

In its material use, dharaʿa denotes measuring in cubits, and dharʿ can denote the resulting measurement.

Qadar and taqdīr have a broader field and are not restricted to measuring length with the human limb.

In Al-Ḥāqqah 69:32, dharʿuhā specifically denotes the chain’s length. Broader ideas of determination, destiny or divine apportionment should not be imported into it.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

Translations of this family should distinguish the limb, measurement and capacity.

In Al-Kahf 18:18, dhirāʿayhi denotes the dog’s two forelegs or forelimbs. Forqan’s current Persian wording, meaning “stretches its two forelegs across the threshold,” conveys the intended sense naturally and clearly.

In Hūd 11:77 and Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:33, ḍāqa bihim dharʿan denotes constrained ability and resources before a difficult situation.

Persian expressions corresponding to “he felt helpless concerning their situation” or “his capacity and resources became constrained” can convey the contextual meaning.

In both verses, the distress and grief expressed by sīʾa bihim should preferably remain distinct from the constrained capacity and resources in ḍāqa bihim dharʿan. Both expressions occur in one sentence and should not be reduced to one meaning.

A literal Persian rendering resembling “his hand became narrow concerning them” could mislead readers, since such phrasing may suggest financial hardship in Persian. These verses instead concern constrained ability before danger and difficult circumstances.

In Al-Ḥāqqah 69:32, dharʿuhā means “its length” and dhirāʿan means “a cubit,” the unit of length.

“A chain whose length is seventy cubits” conveys both meanings well.

The cubit should not be converted into a guessed modern measurement, nor should seventy be removed from the translation without justification.

8. Conclusion

DH R ʿ relates to extending a forelimb, extension, measurement and the extent of ability.

Dhirāʿ denotes the human forearm or an animal’s forelimb and also became a unit of length. Dharʿ can denote a thing’s measurement or length; recognized expressions such as ḍāqa dharʿan express constrained ability and capacity.

Early witnesses show this family’s use in practical activities such as measuring, working palm rods and describing animal movement. Literary usage likewise expresses the extent of human or animal capacity through this family.

The Qur’an contains two material fields and one extended semantic field.

In Al-Kahf 18:18, dhirāʿayhi denotes the dog’s two forelimbs.

In Al-Ḥāqqah 69:32, dharʿuhā sabʿūna dhirāʿan specifies the chain’s length in cubits.

In Hūd 11:77 and Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:33, ḍāqa bihim dharʿan expresses Lot’s constrained ability and resources before a difficult situation.

Final definition: In its material branch, DH R ʿ concerns extending the forelimb and measuring length. Dhirāʿ names the limb and the length unit derived from it. Dharʿ concerns measurement and, by extension, a person’s ability and capacity in facing a task or situation.

The limb, physical measure and practical ability are related in this family, but should not receive identical translations. Each derivative’s precise meaning depends on its form and sentence context.
