Core meaning: speed, swiftness, and movement or work accomplished in a short time without slowness or prolonged delay. The verb sāraʿa describes hastening and taking prompt action. The adjective sarīʿ describes someone or something whose movement or work is swift. In the Quran, this root describes people hastening both toward good and into disbelief, sin, and aggression. The God is also described as swift in reckoning and punishment, while sirāʿan describes people emerging swiftly from their graves at the resurrection.

1. The root in early Arabic

The Arabic root S R ʿ concerns speed and swiftness.

Saruʿa al-shayʾu means that something was swift or happened quickly.

Saruʿa al-farasu means that a horse was swift.

Asraʿa al-rajulu means that a man hastened.

Sāraʿa ilā al-amri means taking prompt action to do something.

Surʿa stands opposite buṭʾ.

Buṭʾ concerns slowness and delayed accomplishment; surʿa concerns movement or action in a short time.

This contrast clarifies the root’s basic meaning.

Speed is not confined to physical displacement.

A horse can move swiftly.

A person can perform a task quickly.

News can arrive promptly.

A decision can also be made without delay.

In each case, speed concerns the timing and manner of an action, although its subject and nature differ.

First pre-Islamic witness: sarīʿ al-tamām in al-Nābigha al-Dhubyānī

In a poem associated with a Ghassanid prince, the pre-Islamic poet al-Nābigha al-Dhubyānī says:

هَذَا غُلَامٌ حَسَنٌ وَجْهُهُ
مُقْتَبِلُ الْخَيْرِ سَرِيعُ التَّمَامِ

hādhā ghulāmun ḥasanun wajhuhu / muqtabilu al-khayri sarīʿu al-tamāmi

Translation:

“Here is a handsome youth, with good ahead of him, quick to reach maturity.”

Sarīʿ al-tamām here describes reaching completeness or maturity quickly.

Sarīʿ is an adjective.

Tamām concerns completion and reaching a desired state.

The poet describes the youth’s rapid growth and maturation, rather than a horse or person moving over the ground.

This witness matters because it shows that pre-Islamic S R ʿ was not limited to running or bodily movement.

A process such as growth or maturation can be described as rapid.

Speed then concerns the time in which that process is realized.

The poem is transmitted with differing readings, including muqtabilu al-khayri and mustaqbilu al-khayri.

The expression sarīʿ al-tamām remains present in the familiar readings.

Its association with pre-Islamic Ghassanid figures makes the witness relevant to the historical use of sarīʿ.

As with other pre-Islamic poetry, however, its text has reached us through later written transmission.

Second pre-Islamic witness: la-sarʿāna in Bishr ibn Abī Khāzim

The following verse is attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Bishr ibn Abī Khāzim al-Asadī:

أَتَخْطُبُ فِيهِمْ بَعْدَ قَتْلِ رِجَالِهِمْ
لَسَرْعَانَ هَذَا وَالدِّمَاءُ تَصَبَّبُ

a-takhṭubu fīhim baʿda qatli rijālihim / la-sarʿāna hādhā wa-al-dimāʾu taṣabbabu

Translation:

“Do you seek marriage among them after their men have been killed? How soon you have undertaken this, while the blood is still flowing!”

La-sarʿāna expresses astonishment at how soon an action occurs.

The speaker asks why someone has sought marriage before the consequences of killing and bloodshed have subsided.

The criticism follows from the situation and poetic context.

Sarʿāna itself does not mean an improper marriage proposal or disrespect for the dead.

It expresses how soon the action was undertaken.

The verse has variant readings: one transmission has la-sarʿāna, while another has la-washkāna.

It therefore cannot be presented as an undisputed witness to sarʿāna in pre-Islamic poetry.

Nevertheless, its transmission and the established Arabic expression illustrate astonishment at something happening quickly.

What the early witnesses show

Al-Nābigha’s verse clearly uses sarīʿ for reaching maturity quickly.

The verse attributed to Bishr, subject to its variant reading, illustrates sarʿāna for an action occurring soon.

The same family also describes a horse’s speed, human haste, quick work, and prompt initiative.

A defensible historical definition is therefore:

In early Arabic, S R ʿ denotes movement or action realized in a short time, as opposed to slowness and delay. It can concern bodily movement, growth, speech, decisions, and practical action.

The root itself does not determine whether that movement or action is right or wrong.

Someone may hasten to do good.

Someone may also take the initiative in wrongdoing or sin.

The action’s value is understood from its object and context.

2. Important forms and derivatives

سَرُعَ يَسْرُعُ

This is the family’s basic verb.

It means being swift or occurring quickly.

سَرُعَ الْفَرَسُ

saruʿa al-farasu

“The horse was swift.”

سَرُعَ الْعَمَلُ

saruʿa al-ʿamalu

“The work proceeded quickly.”

An early grammatical distinction sometimes treats saruʿa as a natural or stable quality of speed, whereas asraʿa more often concerns acting or making an effort to do something quickly.

This distinction is useful in some contexts, but should not become an absolute rule for every Arabic usage.

سُرْعَة

This is a familiar verbal noun of the family.

It denotes speed and the absence of slowness in movement or work.

It stands opposite buṭʾ.

سُرْعَةُ السَّيْرِ

surʿatu al-sayri

“Speed of movement.”

سُرْعَةُ الْعَمَلِ

surʿatu al-ʿamali

“Speed of work.”

Speed alone does not determine the quality, accuracy, or correctness of a result.

سَرِيع

An adjective meaning swift, fast, or quickly accomplished.

فَرَسٌ سَرِيعٌ

farasun sarīʿun

“A swift horse.”

عَمَلٌ سَرِيعٌ

ʿamalun sarīʿun

“Work accomplished quickly.”

Sarīʿ occurs ten times in the Quran, always describing The God in connection with reckoning or punishment.

أَسْرَعَ يُسْرِعُ

This is a Form IV verb.

It means hastening, moving faster, or doing something quickly.

أَسْرَعَ فِي السَّيْرِ

asraʿa fī al-sayri

“He hastened in his movement.”

أَسْرَعَ الْعَمَلَ

asraʿa al-ʿamala

In transitive use: “He performed the work quickly.”

This verbal form does not occur in the Quran.

إِسْرَاع

The verbal noun of Form IV.

It denotes hastening or performing something quickly.

This particular noun also does not occur in the Quran.

سَارَعَ يُسَارِعُ

A Form III verb.

It commonly denotes hastening and taking prompt action toward something.

سَارَعَ إِلَى الْخَيْرِ

sāraʿa ilā al-khayri

“He hastened to do good.”

سَارَعَ فِي الْأَمْرِ

sāraʿa fī al-amri

“He acted promptly and swiftly in the matter.”

Form III here does not necessarily imply two people racing each other.

Someone can take prompt action without a specified competitor.

This verb occurs nine times in different Qur’anic forms.

مُسَارَعَة

The verbal noun of Form III.

It denotes hastening, taking initiative, and acting without delay.

In Qur’anic usage, the object can be good, disbelief, sin, or association with another group.

Musāraʿa therefore has no independent moral value.

تَسَرَّعَ

A Form V verb.

It denotes hastening and taking prompt action.

In some uses, especially rash action, it acquires a critical sense.

The negative sense of tasarruʿ in modern Persian should not be transferred to every Qur’anic use of S R ʿ.

Hastening to do good is also praised in the Quran.

أَسْرَع

The family’s elative adjective.

It means faster or fastest in a comparison.

It occurs twice in the Quran:

أَسْرَعُ الْحَاسِبِينَ

asraʿu al-ḥāsibīna

Al-Anʿām 6:62

The swiftest of reckoners.

أَسْرَعُ مَكْرًا

asraʿu makran

Yūnus 10:21

Swifter in planning.

Both concern divine swiftness in comparison with others, but the aspect compared differs.

سِرَاع

The plural of the adjective sarīʿ.

It describes people moving swiftly.

Its accusative form sirāʿan occurs twice in resurrection scenes.

In both verses, it functions as a circumstantial accusative.

Sirāʿan thus describes the people’s swift condition or movement; it is the plural adjective sarīʿ, not the independent verbal noun surʿa.

سَرْعَانَ and سَرَعَانُ الْقَوْمِ

Sarʿāna, also pronounced surʿāna or sirʿāna, expresses how soon something occurs; its construction differs from saraʿānu al-qawmi.

سُرْعَانَ مَا فَعَلَ ذَلِكَ

surʿāna mā faʿala dhālika

“How quickly he did that!”

In another usage, saraʿānu al-qawmi denotes the first members of a group to move or arrive ahead of others.

These forms do not occur in the Quran.

سَرَعْرَع and سِرْع

Sirʿ is used for a fresh, tender vine shoot.

Saraʿraʿ also describes a slender, fresh, tender shoot and, adjectivally, a supple young person.

The shoot’s name has been associated with its rapid growth.

That semantic connection is understandable, but does not establish the name’s precise historical origin.

Names for certain worms or insects are also recorded here. The formation and origin of forms such as yasrūʿ and usrūʿ are disputed, so they should not automatically be treated as certain derivatives of this root.

Forms actually present in the Quran

S R ʿ occurs 23 times in the Quran:

  • Nine Form III verbs, including sāriʿū, yusāriʿūna, and nusāriʿu.
  • Ten occurrences of sarīʿ.
  • Two occurrences of the elative asraʿ.
  • Two occurrences of sirāʿan, the plural adjective.

The uses fall into five thematic groups: hastening in good; hastening in blameworthy conduct; imagining divine haste in providing good things; divine swiftness in reckoning and punishment; and swift human movement at the resurrection.

3. Hastening in good: sāriʿū ilā maghfiratin

Āl ʿImrān 3:133 states:

وَسَارِعُوا إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ وَجَنَّةٍ عَرْضُهَا السَّمَاوَاتُ وَالْأَرْضُ أُعِدَّتْ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ

wa-sāriʿū ilā maghfiratin min rabbikum wa-jannatin ʿarḍuhā al-samāwātu wa-al-arḍu uʿiddat lil-muttaqīna

Āl ʿImrān 3:133

Translation:

“And hasten toward forgiveness from your Lord and a Garden as wide as the heavens and earth, prepared for the mindful.”

Sāriʿū is a Form III imperative.

The command is to hasten.

Its destination is also specified: the Lord’s forgiveness and the Garden.

The Quran does not simply praise speed; it calls people to hasten in a particular direction.

How does one hasten toward forgiveness?

Forgiveness is not a physical object toward which one can run.

Here, sāriʿū ilā maghfiratin concerns prompt action along a path associated with divine forgiveness.

Āl ʿImrān 3:134 describes the mindful as giving in ease and hardship, restraining anger, and pardoning people.

Āl ʿImrān 3:135 describes those who, after committing an indecency or wronging themselves, remember The God, seek forgiveness, and do not knowingly persist in wrongdoing.

These verses illuminate the path of hastening toward forgiveness.

Upon recognizing an error, one should act to correct it and return.

Pride, prejudice, fear of others’ judgment, or personal interests should not become excuses for delaying correction.

Musāraʿa here concerns promptly doing what is right and turning away from error.

Does speed conflict with reflection?

No.

Āl ʿImrān 3:135 itself concerns recognizing wrongdoing and avoiding deliberate persistence in it.

Hastening to correct an error therefore does not mean thoughtless or irresponsible action.

Making amends may require investigating the facts, speaking with the injured person, or finding an appropriate way to restore a right.

Such investigation does not conflict with the command to hasten toward forgiveness.

The distinction is that reflection serves correction, rather than evading responsibility or deliberately postponing it.

In Āl ʿImrān 3:133, speed receives its value from its destination and accompanying conduct: prompt mindfulness, correction, and seeking forgiveness.

Āl ʿImrān 3:114: hastening in good deeds

وَيُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَأُولَٰئِكَ مِنَ الصَّالِحِينَ

wa-yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāti wa-ulāʾika mina al-ṣāliḥīna

Āl ʿImrān 3:114

Translation:

“They hasten in good deeds, and they are among the righteous.”

This describes a group among the People of the Book, introduced in the surrounding verses through faith, reciting divine signs, prostration, enjoining right, and forbidding wrong.

The Quran therefore does not confine hastening in good to a religious label.

Āl ʿImrān 3:113 explicitly states that the People of the Book are not all alike.

In this context, hastening in good is a mark of righteous people.

Khayrāt specifies the direction and object of their haste.

Yusāriʿūna means hastening; the goodness of the destination comes from al-khayrāt.

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:90: hastening in good with prayer and humility

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:90 states:

إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَيَدْعُونَنَا رَغَبًا وَرَهَبًا وَكَانُوا لَنَا خَاشِعِينَ

innahum kānū yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāti wa-yadʿūnanā raghaban wa-rahaban wa-kānū lanā khāshiʿīna

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:90

Translation:

“They used to hasten in good deeds, call upon Us in hope and fear, and remain humble before Us.”

The verse occurs amid the account of Zechariah and other righteous servants.

Hastening in good is accompanied by prayer and humility.

Yusāriʿūna does not itself mean praying or being humble.

These are separate behaviors placed alongside one another.

Their combination shows that prompt good action can coexist with humility and a relationship with the Lord.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:61: hastening and going ahead

أُولَٰئِكَ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَهُمْ لَهَا سَابِقُونَ

ulāʾika yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāti wa-hum lahā sābiqūna

Al-Muʾminūn 23:61

Translation:

“They hasten in good deeds and are foremost in them.”

Yusāriʿūna belongs to S R ʿ, while sābiqūna belongs to S B Q.

They are related but not identical in meaning.

Musāraʿa expresses swift action.

Sabq emphasizes going ahead and being foremost.

The two words express related aspects of the people’s conduct.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:57, Al-Muʾminūn 23:58, Al-Muʾminūn 23:59, and Al-Muʾminūn 23:60 describe reverence for the Lord, faith in His signs, not associating partners with Him, and acting with hearts apprehensive about returning to Him.

Their hastening in good is then stated.

This speed accompanies responsibility and awareness of returning to The God, rather than self-praise or unfounded confidence in one’s superiority.

4. Hastening in disbelief, sin, and aggression

Speed is not always praised in the Quran.

The same verb used for good deeds also describes hastening in disbelief, sin, and aggression.

Speed describes how an action is performed; its object and direction determine its moral value.

Āl ʿImrān 3:176: hastening in disbelief

Āl ʿImrān 3:176 states:

وَلَا يَحْزُنْكَ الَّذِينَ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْكُفْرِ إِنَّهُمْ لَنْ يَضُرُّوا اللَّهَ شَيْئًا

wa-lā yaḥzunka alladhīna yusāriʿūna fī al-kufri innahum lan yaḍurrū Allāha shayʾan

Āl ʿImrān 3:176

Translation:

“Do not let those who hasten in disbelief grieve you; they will never harm The God at all.”

Yusāriʿūna fī al-kufri describes hastening in the direction of disbelief.

Kufr has a separate root and must be understood in its verse context.

Yusāriʿūna does not itself mean disbelief.

It expresses speed and promptness in that course.

It should not follow that every uninformed person, questioner, or person with a different interpretation exemplifies this conduct.

The verse concerns people with particular behavior; identifying disbelief requires understanding their knowledge, conduct, and intentions.

Al-Māʾidah 5:41: hastening in disbelief and a divided profession

Al-Māʾidah 5:41 repeats yusāriʿūna fī al-kufri.

The continuation explains characteristics of the people concerned.

It mentions those who profess belief with their tongues while their hearts have not believed.

Listening to falsehood and displacing words from their proper positions are also mentioned.

Hastening in disbelief is associated here with a divided profession of faith and distortion of truth.

This should not become unrestricted condemnation of every member of a historical or religious community.

The criteria are the behaviors identified by the verse itself.

Al-Māʾidah 5:52: hastening into association with another group

Al-Māʾidah 5:52 states:

فَتَرَى الَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ مَرَضٌ يُسَارِعُونَ فِيهِمْ يَقُولُونَ نَخْشَىٰ أَنْ تُصِيبَنَا دَائِرَةٌ

fa-tarā alladhīna fī qulūbihim maraḍun yusāriʿūna fīhim yaqūlūna nakhshā an tuṣībanā dāʾiratun

Al-Māʾidah 5:52

Translation:

“You see those in whose hearts is sickness hastening among them, saying: We fear that a reversal may strike us.”

Fīhim specifies the object of this hastening.

The preceding context concerns bonds of protection and allegiance with specified groups.

Musāraʿa here therefore concerns entering a relationship or dependency quickly.

A motive is also given: fear of an adverse turn of events.

The haste is not merely bodily motion; it concerns social decisions and alignment.

Speed itself does not mean betrayal or sickness of heart.

Those meanings come from the context and described conduct.

Without considering other verses, this should not be taken as a ban on every form of cooperation, association, or just relationship with people of different beliefs.

The issue is the kind of bond, its motive, and its consequences.

Al-Māʾidah 5:62: hastening in sin, aggression, and illicit gain

وَتَرَىٰ كَثِيرًا مِنْهُمْ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْإِثْمِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ وَأَكْلِهِمُ السُّحْتَ لَبِئْسَ مَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

wa-tarā kathīran minhum yusāriʿūna fī al-ithmi wa-al-ʿudwāni wa-aklihim al-suḥta la-biʾsa mā kānū yaʿmalūna

Al-Māʾidah 5:62

Translation:

“You see many of them hastening in sin, aggression, and consuming illicit gain. Evil indeed is what they used to do.”

Yusāriʿūna describes haste in several blameworthy behaviors.

Ithm concerns sin and wrongdoing.

ʿUdwān concerns transgressing limits and aggression.

Suḥt concerns illicit or tainted gain.

Speed describes morally and socially blameworthy conduct here.

The qualification kathīran minhum matters.

The Quran says many of them, rather than every member without exception.

This qualification must remain in translation and interpretation.

Al-Māʾidah 5:63 also addresses the responsibility of religious scholars and leaders to restrain sinful speech and illicit consumption.

This connection shows that rapid corruption need not be purely individual; it may accompany failures by those responsible for restraining it.

Does hastening in corruption mean abundant corruption?

Speed and frequency can be related, but are not identical.

Someone may do something once and quickly.

Someone may also repeat it many times at long intervals.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:62, yusāriʿūna emphasizes haste and prompt engagement.

The number of people comes from kathīran minhum.

Translating yusāriʿūna merely as “they sin a great deal” would lose the element of speed.

5. Nusāriʿu lahum fī al-khayrāti: a question rejecting an assumption

Al-Muʾminūn 23:55 and Al-Muʾminūn 23:56 state:

أَيَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّمَا نُمِدُّهُمْ بِهِ مِنْ مَالٍ وَبَنِينَ

a-yaḥsabūna annamā numidduhum bihi min mālin wa-banīna

Al-Muʾminūn 23:55

نُسَارِعُ لَهُمْ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ بَلْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ

nusāriʿu lahum fī al-khayrāti bal lā yashʿurūna

Al-Muʾminūn 23:56

Translation:

“Do they imagine that by supplying them with wealth and children We are hastening good things to them? Rather, they do not perceive.”

Nusāriʿu is a first-person plural imperfect Form III verb.

The construction spanning both verses must be retained.

The sentence begins with a-yaḥsabūna, asking about the people’s assumption.

Bal lā yashʿurūna then rejects that understanding.

Is abundant wealth and offspring a sign of divine approval?

The verse rejects that automatic inference.

Someone may possess abundant wealth and children and regard them as certain proof of divine approval and superior standing.

The Quran distinguishes material possession from moral worth.

Wealth can serve good deeds.

It can also serve oppression, pride, or corruption.

Possessions alone do not settle the judgment of a person.

Linguistically, nusāriʿu lahum fī al-khayrāti belongs within a question challenging the audience’s assumption.

It should not be detached and presented as a definitive declaration that The God hastens good things to the group mentioned.

Comparison with Al-Muʾminūn 23:61

Al-Muʾminūn 23:61 describes people who themselves hasten in good deeds.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:56 addresses people imagining that their wealth and children show divine haste in providing good things for them.

The former concerns righteous human behavior.

The latter concerns a mistaken reading of material prosperity.

The meaning of speed remains in both.

The agent, object, and sentence construction differ.

The same expression concerning haste in good things can praise conduct or form part of an assumption that the Quran rejects, depending on agent and context.

6. Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb: swiftness in divine reckoning

Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb occurs eight times:

Al-Baqarah 2:202, Āl ʿImrān 3:19, Āl ʿImrān 3:199, Al-Māʾidah 5:4, Al-Raʿd 13:41, Ibrāhīm 14:51, Al-Nūr 24:39, and Ghāfir 40:17.

In each, sarīʿ describes The God.

Ḥisāb belongs to Ḥ S B and concerns counting, assessing, and taking account.

The combination describes divine reckoning as swift and free from slowness.

What does swift reckoning mean?

Human reckoning may take considerable time.

People depend on tools and time to inspect records, gather information, hear parties, count assets, or examine conduct.

Information may remain hidden from them.

They may forget or make mistakes.

The Quran does not portray divine knowledge as limited by these human capacities.

Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb accordingly expresses that divine reckoning is not constrained by human slowness and incapacity.

Sarīʿ alone, however, does not specify the exact duration of everyone’s final reckoning.

Its timing and manner require further evidence.

Does swift reckoning mean final judgment immediately follows every act?

That does not follow from the adjective alone.

The speed of reckoning differs from when it begins.

An examination may occur at an appointed time without slowness or difficulty once it begins.

The Quran also speaks of a day of reckoning and people returning for judgment.

Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb should not eliminate every interval between conduct and final reckoning.

Nor should one assume that every consequence of conduct appears only after death.

Some actions have individual and social consequences in this world.

Those worldly effects are not necessarily identical to final judgment in the hereafter.

Divine knowledge of conduct, its worldly consequences, and final reckoning should therefore be distinguished.

Al-Baqarah 2:202: reckoning and what people earn

Al-Baqarah 2:202 states:

أُولَٰئِكَ لَهُمْ نَصِيبٌ مِمَّا كَسَبُوا وَاللَّهُ سَرِيعُ الْحِسَابِ

ulāʾika lahum naṣībun mimmā kasabū wa-Allāhu sarīʿu al-ḥisābi

Al-Baqarah 2:202

Translation:

“They have a share of what they have earned, and The God is swift in reckoning.”

The context concerns prayer for good in this world and the hereafter.

A person’s share is then linked to what they have earned.

This connection shows that prayer, choice, conduct, and outcome are not isolated from one another.

Divine swiftness in reckoning accompanies human responsibility for what is earned.

It neither replaces effort nor guarantees immediate fulfillment of every wish.

Āl ʿImrān 3:19 and Āl ʿImrān 3:199: two contexts of reckoning

In Āl ʿImrān 3:19, swift reckoning follows disagreement after knowledge and rejection of divine signs.

In Āl ʿImrān 3:199, the same description follows people among the People of the Book who believe in The God and revelation and do not sell His signs for a small price.

One divine description can occur both in criticism of wrongdoing and in good news for righteous people.

Swift reckoning does not intrinsically mean punishment.

Reckoning can relate to reward or punishment.

The outcome follows from conduct and divine judgment, not speed itself.

Al-Māʾidah 5:4: reckoning, food, and mindfulness

Al-Māʾidah 5:4 discusses food and hunting with trained animals, then commands remembrance of The God’s name and mindfulness.

It ends with inna Allāha sarīʿu al-ḥisābi.

Divine reckoning is connected here with responsibility for observing the relevant limits and commands.

Swift reckoning alone cannot establish an additional rule about permitted or prohibited food.

Those rulings appear elsewhere in the verse.

Al-Raʿd 13:41: reckoning and divine judgment

Al-Raʿd 13:41 states:

وَاللَّهُ يَحْكُمُ لَا مُعَقِّبَ لِحُكْمِهِ وَهُوَ سَرِيعُ الْحِسَابِ

wa-Allāhu yaḥkumu lā muʿaqqiba li-ḥukmihi wa-huwa sarīʿu al-ḥisābi

Al-Raʿd 13:41

Translation:

“The God judges; none can overturn His judgment, and He is swift in reckoning.”

Swift reckoning stands alongside divine judgment and its irreversibility.

The context concerns more than counting deeds; divine judgment is also present.

Sarīʿ does not itself mean judging.

Judgment comes from yaḥkumu, while swift reckoning comes from sarīʿ al-ḥisāb.

Ibrāhīm 14:51: reckoning and recompense for conduct

Ibrāhīm 14:51 states:

لِيَجْزِيَ اللَّهُ كُلَّ نَفْسٍ مَا كَسَبَتْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ سَرِيعُ الْحِسَابِ

li-yajziya Allāhu kulla nafsin mā kasabat inna Allāha sarīʿu al-ḥisābi

Ibrāhīm 14:51

Translation:

“So that The God may recompense every soul for what it has earned. Indeed, The God is swift in reckoning.”

The verse clearly links reckoning and recompense.

Sarīʿ does not itself mean giving recompense.

Li-yajziya expresses recompense.

Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb expresses the absence of slowness in reckoning.

Al-Nūr 24:39: reckoning and human assumptions

Al-Nūr 24:39 compares a group’s deeds to a mirage.

A thirsty person expects water, but encounters a different reality on arrival.

The verse then describes a full divine accounting and ends with wa-Allāhu sarīʿu al-ḥisābi.

Swift reckoning accompanies the disclosure of the true character of human deeds.

Sarīʿ itself does not mean mirage, deception, or the loss of deeds.

Those meanings arise from other parts of the verse.

Ghāfir 40:17: swift reckoning without injustice

الْيَوْمَ تُجْزَىٰ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ لَا ظُلْمَ الْيَوْمَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ سَرِيعُ الْحِسَابِ

al-yawma tujzā kullu nafsin bi-mā kasabat lā ẓulma al-yawma inna Allāha sarīʿu al-ḥisābi

Ghāfir 40:17

Translation:

“Today every soul is recompensed for what it has earned. There is no injustice today. Indeed, The God is swift in reckoning.”

Swift reckoning is accompanied by the denial of injustice.

This combination matters.

In human affairs, rash judgment can cause error and injustice.

This verse, however, joins divine swiftness in reckoning with the absence of injustice.

Divine speed should not be likened to human carelessness or incomplete judgment.

Across the eight uses, speed remains the basic meaning, while reckoning may concern reward, punishment, responsibility, judgment, and disclosure of the reality of deeds.

7. Sarīʿ al-ʿiqāb: swift punishment and the place of mercy

Sarīʿ al-ʿiqāb occurs twice:

Al-Anʿām 6:165 and Al-Aʿrāf 7:167.

Both associate this description with divine forgiveness and mercy.

Al-Anʿām 6:165

إِنَّ رَبَّكَ سَرِيعُ الْعِقَابِ وَإِنَّهُ لَغَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ

inna rabbaka sarīʿu al-ʿiqābi wa-innahu la-ghafūrun raḥīmun

Al-Anʿām 6:165

Translation:

“Indeed, your Lord is swift in punishment, and He is surely forgiving and merciful.”

Al-Aʿrāf 7:167

إِنَّ رَبَّكَ لَسَرِيعُ الْعِقَابِ وَإِنَّهُ لَغَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ

inna rabbaka la-sarīʿu al-ʿiqābi wa-innahu la-ghafūrun raḥīmun

Al-Aʿrāf 7:167

Translation:

“Indeed, your Lord is certainly swift in punishment, and He is surely forgiving and merciful.”

The second verse uses the emphatic lām in la-sarīʿu.

This difference concerns emphasis in the sentence.

Sarīʿ retains the same meaning of swiftness and absence of slowness.

Does swift punishment mean every sin is punished immediately?

No.

The Quran also speaks of respite, repentance, and forgiveness.

These very verses place swift punishment alongside ghafūr raḥīm.

Swift punishment should not be restricted to the claim that The God never allows respite.

Swiftness in executing punishment does not necessarily conflict with respite or forgiveness.

This resembles the distinction between the ability to do something and the decision about when to do it.

The God is not powerless or impeded by slowness in punishment.

Sarīʿ alone does not determine who is punished, under what conditions, or when.

Why do forgiveness and mercy accompany swift punishment?

Their combination prevents a one-sided view of divine judgment.

Speaking only of punishment while overlooking repentance and forgiveness omits part of the Qur’anic picture.

Speaking only of forgiveness while denying responsibility and the possibility of punishment omits another part.

Al-Anʿām 6:165 first speaks of people being made successors on earth, differences in degrees, and their testing.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:167 concerns the conduct and fate of groups among the Children of Israel.

The same divine description occurs in different contexts, accompanied in both by forgiveness and mercy.

Swift punishment does not mean rash judgment or denial of forgiveness.

8. Asraʿ al-ḥāsibīn and asraʿ makran

Asraʿ occurs twice in the Quran.

It is an elative adjective.

The aspect compared differs between the two verses.

Al-Anʿām 6:62: the swiftest reckoner

ثُمَّ رُدُّوا إِلَى اللَّهِ مَوْلَاهُمُ الْحَقِّ أَلَا لَهُ الْحُكْمُ وَهُوَ أَسْرَعُ الْحَاسِبِينَ

thumma ruddū ilā Allāhi mawlāhumu al-ḥaqqi alā lahu al-ḥukmu wa-huwa asraʿu al-ḥāsibīna

Al-Anʿām 6:62

Translation:

“Then they are returned to The God, their true Protector. Surely judgment belongs to Him, and He is the swiftest of reckoners.”

Asraʿ forms a construct phrase with al-ḥāsibīn.

Being a reckoner belongs to the root Ḥ S B.

Asraʿ expresses superiority in speed.

It is directly related to sarīʿ al-ḥisāb.

The two constructions are nevertheless different.

Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb describes the swiftness of divine reckoning.

Asraʿ al-ḥāsibīn emphasizes superior speed compared with reckoners.

This does not imply that The God needs human tools or calculation time.

The context is return to The God and His judgment.

Yūnus 10:21: Allāhu asraʿu makran

Yūnus 10:21 states:

قُلِ اللَّهُ أَسْرَعُ مَكْرًا إِنَّ رُسُلَنَا يَكْتُبُونَ مَا تَمْكُرُونَ

quli Allāhu asraʿu makran inna rusulanā yaktubūna mā tamkurūna

Yūnus 10:21

Translation:

“Say: The God is swifter in counter-planning. Our messengers record what you plot.”

Asraʿ is an elative adjective.

Makran is an accusative specification identifying the respect in which the comparison applies.

The verse begins with people plotting against divine signs after receiving divine mercy.

The response declares The God swifter in countering their plots.

It then speaks of divine messengers recording their conduct.

The subject is the response to hidden, wrongful human plans and behavior.

Is divine makr the same as wrongful human deception?

Arabic makr concerns a plan or strategy that may be concealed.

Its moral value follows from its purpose, manner, and object.

Yūnus 10:21 places human plotting against divine signs alongside the divine response to that conduct.

Wrongful features of human plotting, such as injustice and betrayal, should not therefore be attributed to The God without grounds.

Nor does asraʿ makran imply that people lack choice or responsibility.

The verse speaks of their conduct and its being recorded.

Human responsibility remains, together with divine superiority in countering their plans.

How does speed differ between these expressions?

In Al-Anʿām 6:62, speed concerns reckoning.

In Yūnus 10:21, it concerns planning and counter-planning.

Asraʿ retains the shared sense of comparison in speed, while the subject differs.

In each verse, it should therefore be understood with the word to which it is connected.

The Quran twice applies asraʿ to The God: once in reckoning and once in countering human plots.

9. Sirāʿan: human haste at the resurrection

Qāf 50:44 states:

يَوْمَ تَشَقَّقُ الْأَرْضُ عَنْهُمْ سِرَاعًا ذَٰلِكَ حَشْرٌ عَلَيْنَا يَسِيرٌ

yawma tashaqqaqu al-arḍu ʿanhum sirāʿan dhālika ḥashrun ʿalaynā yasīrun

Qāf 50:44

Translation:

“The day the earth splits open from over them and they emerge swiftly; that gathering is easy for Us.”

Al-Maʿārij 70:43 also states:

يَوْمَ يَخْرُجُونَ مِنَ الْأَجْدَاثِ سِرَاعًا كَأَنَّهُمْ إِلَىٰ نُصُبٍ يُوفِضُونَ

yawma yakhrujūna mina al-ajdāthi sirāʿan ka-annahum ilā nuṣubin yūfiḍūna

Al-Maʿārij 70:43

Translation:

“The day they emerge swiftly from the graves, as though rushing toward an erected marker.”

In both verses, sirāʿan describes people while they emerge and move.

What is the grammatical function of sirāʿan?

Sirāʿ is the plural of sarīʿ.

Its accusative form functions circumstantially in these verses.

Its meaning is therefore “swiftly” or “in a swift condition.”

In Qāf 50:44, it refers to those from whom the earth splits open as they emerge.

In Al-Maʿārij 70:43, it refers to the subject of yakhrujūna.

Both concern the speed of human movement.

Does swift emergence mean fear?

Fear and dread also occur in various resurrection passages.

Sirāʿan itself does not mean being afraid.

It expresses rapid movement.

Any fear must be identified through the words and context of the particular verse.

Qāf 50:44 describes people emerging and being gathered.

Al-Maʿārij 70:43 compares their haste to rushing toward something erected. Nuṣub has also been understood as an idol or cult object; this question is separate from the meaning of sirāʿan.

Both describe rapid movement, but their imagery differs.

Does sirāʿan describe The God’s own speed?

No.

These verses attribute speed to human beings.

Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb and asraʿ al-ḥāsibīn, by contrast, concern The God.

The uses should not be merged merely because they share a root.

In Qāf 50:44, The God says that this gathering is easy for Him.

Ease comes from yasīrun.

Human swiftness comes from sirāʿan.

Human speed and the ease of gathering for The God are distinct meanings expressed in one verse.

10. Speed, rash haste, precedence, and initiative

S R ʿ (surʿa) and ʿ J L (ʿajala)

Arabic ʿajal concerns haste and doing something soon.

S R ʿ also concerns speed.

The families overlap, although certain uses show a meaningful difference.

ʿAjala can describe acting before the proper time or without sufficient thought.

Surʿa more directly emphasizes short duration and absence of slowness.

The distinction should not become an absolute boundary.

In Ṭā Hā 20:84, Moses says:

وَعَجِلْتُ إِلَيْكَ رَبِّ لِتَرْضَىٰ

wa-ʿajiltu ilayka rabbi li-tarḍā

Ṭā Hā 20:84

And I hastened to You, my Lord, that You might be pleased.

Here, haste accompanies the intention to seek the Lord’s approval.

In Al-Isrāʾ 17:11, human hastiness occurs in a critical context.

Even ʿ J L therefore does not have one fixed moral value in every use.

The distinction must follow construction and context.

Āl ʿImrān 3:133 praises hastening toward forgiveness.

Al-Māʾidah 5:62 criticizes hastening in sin and aggression.

Within S R ʿ itself, the value depends on the action’s direction.

S R ʿ (musāraʿa) and S B Q (sabq)

Sabq concerns going ahead and preceding.

S R ʿ concerns speed of movement or action.

Both occur together in Al-Muʾminūn 23:61.

Their combination shows that speed and precedence can accompany one another without being identical.

Someone may act quickly while another has already reached the result.

Someone may begin before others but move more slowly.

Speed and precedence are different aspects of movement and action.

Al-Ḥadīd 57:21 uses sābiqū for competing toward forgiveness and the Garden.

Āl ʿImrān 3:133 uses sāriʿū.

The commands have related destinations but different verbal imagery.

S R ʿ (musāraʿa) and B D R (mubādara)

Arabic mubādara concerns prompt initiative and swift action.

Musāraʿa is also explained through this idea.

The closeness is clear.

In Qur’anic use, however, the construction and object of musāraʿa must remain visible.

Sāriʿū ilā maghfiratin concerns hastening toward forgiveness.

Yusāriʿūna fī al-ithmi concerns hastening in sin.

Mubādara can explain musāraʿa, but neither initiative nor speed alone supplies an action’s moral value.

S R ʿ (sarīʿ) and Kh F F (khiffa)

Khiffa concerns lightness and ease of movement.

A light object may move faster.

Speed and lightness nevertheless differ.

A light object may move slowly.

A heavy object can also move very quickly.

Being lightly burdened can facilitate rapid human movement, but S R ʿ does not itself mean low weight.

S R ʿ (sarīʿ) and Y S R (yasīr)

Y S R concerns ease and lack of difficulty.

S R ʿ concerns absence of slowness and short duration.

An easy task may deliberately be performed calmly over a long time.

A difficult task may also be performed rapidly.

Sirāʿan and yasīrun occur together in Qāf 50:44.

The former expresses people’s speed as they emerge.

The latter describes the ease of gathering them for The God.

They should not be treated as synonyms.

11. Translation in Forqan

Persian equivalents for speed and haste suit many forms in this family.

Several distinctions matter: an imperative differs from a divine adjective; asraʿ is comparative or superlative; and sirāʿan is a plural adjective functioning circumstantially.

Main meanings of the forms

Saruʿa: was swift; happened quickly.

Asraʿa: hastened; accelerated; performed something quickly.

Sāraʿa: hastened and took prompt action.

Surʿa: speed; absence of slowness in movement or work.

Musāraʿa: hastening and taking initiative.

Sarīʿ: swift; quickly accomplished.

Asraʿ: faster; fastest in comparison.

Sirāʿ: swift; plural of sarīʿ.

Sarʿāna: an expression of how soon something occurs. Saraʿānu al-qawmi: the first members of a group; the construction and vocalization differ.

Uses requiring special explanation

In Al-Muʾminūn 23:56, nusāriʿu lahum fī al-khayrāti is part of a question about an assumption, not an independently affirmed statement of divine haste in providing them with good things.

In Yūnus 10:21, asraʿ makran is an elative with an accusative specification, describing divine superiority in swiftly countering human plots.

In Qāf 50:44 and Al-Maʿārij 70:43, sirāʿan means swiftly and describes people while emerging and moving.

The eight uses of sarīʿ al-ḥisāb concern swift reckoning, not necessarily final judgment immediately after each act.

The two uses of sarīʿ al-ʿiqāb accompany reminders of divine forgiveness and mercy.

Brief root meaning and verse-specific meanings

For the short root box:

“Speed and swiftness; movement or work in a short time without slowness.”

For verses about hastening in good:

“Hastening and taking prompt action in good deeds; their value follows from the direction and object of the action.”

For verses about hastening in disbelief, sin, and aggression:

“Hastening to adopt or perform the blameworthy conduct specified by the verse.”

For Al-Muʾminūn 23:56:

“Hastening to provide good things; here, part of an assumption that the Quran rejects.”

For sarīʿ al-ḥisāb:

“Swiftness and absence of slowness in divine reckoning, without the adjective itself specifying its exact starting time.”

For sarīʿ al-ʿiqāb:

“Divine swiftness in punishment, in a context that also recalls forgiveness and mercy.”

For asraʿ al-ḥāsibīn:

“The swiftest of reckoners; divine superiority in the speed of reckoning.”

For asraʿ makran:

“Swifter in counter-planning against human plots.”

For sirāʿan:

“Swiftly; the condition of people emerging and moving at the resurrection.”

Verse boxes should preserve the exact word form, including attached particles.

The brief root meaning should remain separate from the meaning specific to that verse.

12. Conclusion

Early Arabic S R ʿ concerns speed and haste.

The verse attributed to al-Nābigha uses sarīʿ al-tamām for a youth reaching maturity quickly.

This shows that pre-Qur’anic speed was not limited to running and bodily movement.

The verse attributed to Bishr also uses sarʿāna for a prompt action, although its transmitted readings differ.

Speed stands opposite slowness and applies to movement, speech, work, and prompt initiative.

The root occurs 23 times in the Quran.

Nine are Form III verbs, ten are sarīʿ, two are the elative asraʿ, and two are sirāʿan.

Āl ʿImrān 3:114, Āl ʿImrān 3:133, Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:90, and Al-Muʾminūn 23:61 praise hastening in good.

Āl ʿImrān 3:176, Al-Māʾidah 5:41, Al-Māʾidah 5:52, and Al-Māʾidah 5:62 describe hastening in blameworthy conduct.

Speed is therefore not intrinsically a virtue or a sin.

Its value depends on the direction, purpose, and quality of the action.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:56 places nusāriʿu lahum fī al-khayrāti within a question rejecting an assumption about wealth and children.

Detached from its question, it should not become definitive approval of material prosperity.

Eight verses describe The God as swift in reckoning.

This expresses that divine reckoning is not constrained by human slowness and limitations.

It does not alone determine precisely when final reckoning begins.

Two verses describe swift punishment alongside ghafūr raḥīm.

Swift punishment does not necessarily conflict with respite or forgiveness.

Al-Anʿām 6:62 calls The God the swiftest of reckoners.

Yūnus 10:21 describes His superior speed in countering human plots.

Qāf 50:44 and Al-Maʿārij 70:43 use sirāʿan for people emerging swiftly at the resurrection.

Here, speed concerns human movement, not divine reckoning.

S R ʿ denotes speed in movement or action: accomplishment in a short time without slowness. This remains across its Qur’anic uses, while moral value, agent, object, and consequences follow from each verse’s context.

The central finding is that the Quran neither praises nor condemns speed unconditionally.

Hastening to correct errors, do good, respect rights, and seek forgiveness is praised.

Hastening in disbelief, aggression, corruption, and illicit gain is condemned.

Divine swiftness does not mean rashness, carelessness, or inability to judge.

In these Qur’anic uses, speed is valuable when joined to right direction and responsibility; swift divine reckoning expresses the absence of slowness and incapacity in divine judgment.
