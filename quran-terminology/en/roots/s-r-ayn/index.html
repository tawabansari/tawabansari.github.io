1. Introducing the root and its Qur’anic forms

The triliteral Arabic root «س ر ع» (S R ʿ) conveys speed or the quicker performance of a movement or action, as opposed to slowness. This speed may concern bodily movement, speaking, fulfilling a responsibility, deciding, or bringing about a result.

In the Qur’an, this root extends beyond physical movement. People may hasten in good deeds, hurry into disbelief and sin, or emerge quickly from their graves. Elsewhere, speed is attributed to The God in connection with reckoning, punishment, and responding to deceptive schemes. The root occurs twenty-three times in twenty-three verses across fourteen surahs: nine Form III verbs, ten occurrences of sarīʿ, two elative occurrences of asraʿ, and two of sirāʿan.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: twenty-three occurrences in twenty-three verses across fourteen surahs

This range shows that speed in its lexical meaning is neither a virtue nor a sin. Its value is determined by the action’s subject, direction, and consequences.

Important forms in this family include:

  • سَرُعَ / يَسْرُعُ (saruʿa / yasruʿu): to be swift in movement or action. Qur’anic use: no, in this verbal form.
  • سَرِعَ / يَسْرَعُ (sariʿa / yasraʿu): to become swift or move and act quickly; another lexical Form I pattern. Qur’anic use: no.
  • سَرِيع (sarīʿ): swift in performing or bringing about something. Qur’anic use: yes, ten times.
  • أَسْرَع (asraʿ): swifter or swiftest, according to the comparative construction. Qur’anic use: yes, twice.
  • سَارَعَ / يُسَارِعُ (sāraʿa / yusāriʿu): to hasten or take prompt initiative toward doing or reaching something. Qur’anic use: yes, nine times in various Form III inflections.
  • سَارِعُوا (sāriʿū): hasten; the plural imperative. Qur’anic use: yes, in Āl ʿImrān 3:133.
  • سِرَاع (sirāʿ): swift, moving quickly; plural of sarīʿ, describing rapid movement. Qur’anic use: yes, as the accusative sirāʿan in Qāf 50:44 and Al-Maʿārij 70:43.
  • سُرْعَة (surʿah): speed or swiftness in movement or action. Qur’anic use: no, as an independent noun.
  • إِسْرَاع (isrāʿ): moving or acting quickly. Qur’anic use: no, in this verbal-noun form.
  • أَسْرَعَ / يُسْرِعُ (asraʿa / yusriʿu): to increase speed, act quickly, or hasten toward something. Qur’anic use: no, as a Form IV verb.
  • مُسَارَعَة (musāraʿah): hastening and taking prompt initiative. Qur’anic use: no, as an independent verbal noun, although its verb occurs.
  • تَسَارَعَ (tasāraʿa): to hasten; in some uses, several people or events proceeding quickly in succession. Qur’anic use: no.
  • تَسَرَّعَ (tasarraʿa): to act hastily; in later use, sometimes acting without sufficient deliberation. Qur’anic use: no.
  • مُسْرِع (musriʿ): someone moving or acting swiftly. Qur’anic use: no.
  • مُسَارِع (musāriʿ): someone hastening or taking prompt initiative. Qur’anic use: no, as an independent active participle.
  • سَرَعَان / سَرْعَان / سُرْعَان (saraʿān / sarʿān / surʿān): in sarʿān al-nās, the foremost or those who act early; in sarʿāna mā faʿala, an expression of how soon an action occurs. Qur’anic use: no.
  • سَرَعْرَع (saraʿraʿ): in early use, a tender, moist shoot, and also a supple young person. This meaning alone does not establish rapid growth. Qur’anic use: no.
  • سَرَّعَ / مُسَرِّع (sarraʿa / musarriʿ): to act swiftly, and someone hastening; Form II and its active participle in Ibn Aḥmar’s poetic witness. Qur’anic use: no.

Arabic Language Academy dictionary: verb forms and the plural of sarīʿ

All Qur’anic verbs of this root are Form III. The bare verb saruʿa, Form IV asraʿa, and Form V tasarraʿa do not occur in the Qur’anic text. The imperative sāriʿū is included in the nine verbs, rather than being an additional occurrence.

The absence of these forms does not itself prove their deliberate exclusion for a particular purpose. It establishes only that the forms are not attested in Qur’anic usage.

2. The semantic core: speed as opposed to slowness

Surʿah: less time in movement or completing an action

Arabic al-surʿah is opposed to al-buṭʾ: slowness.

Someone covering a route quickly takes less time than someone moving more slowly. Likewise, when a task is performed quickly, less time separates its beginning from its completion.

Speed therefore extends beyond a body moving through space.

For example, one person may speak quickly, another decide quickly, and a third finish a task quickly.

In all three cases, speed concerns how an action proceeds or reaches completion.

Speed itself, however, does not determine whether the action is performed correctly.

One person may work quickly and achieve an accurate, desirable result. Another may perform the same task without sufficient consideration and make a mistake.

Speed therefore differs from reprehensible rashness.

Speed concerns the timing or pace of an action; blameworthy rashness concerns an unsound decision or action made without the necessary consideration.

This distinction is essential to understanding Qur’anic uses.

Does speed always describe perceptible movement?

No.

In a basic physical use, a person, horse, or other animal can be described as moving quickly.

Arabic also uses speed for performing tasks, answering, and bringing about a result.

The Qur’anic sarīʿ al-ḥisāb is a clear instance of this extension.

Reckoning is not a body moving on feet. Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb therefore cannot be turned into spatial movement of the reckoning itself.

Here, speed concerns carrying out and completing the reckoning.

The root’s semantic field thus extends beyond bodily movement while preserving speed and its opposition to slowness.

The physical image: rapid movement and arriving before others

The clearest physical image is a person or animal covering a route quickly.

Two people may head toward a destination: one proceeds slowly, while the other moves faster and arrives sooner.

In that situation, speed can lead to getting ahead.

Nevertheless, being swift and getting ahead are distinct.

Two people may both move quickly without either passing the other. Someone may also arrive before another by starting earlier, even while moving more slowly.

This distinction matters when examining yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāt and wa-hum lahā sābiqūn in Al-Muʾminūn 23:61.

The entire meaning of S R ʿ should therefore not be restricted to outstripping others.

3. Early Arabic usage and linguistic evidence

Speed in movement, speech, and action

Early Arabic uses saruʿa al-rajul for someone who moves or acts quickly.

Asraʿa fī al-sayr describes increasing speed while travelling.

Lexical accounts also record saruʿa fī kalāmihi wa-fiʿālihi for speed in speech and action.

These uses show that speed was not restricted to walking.

Sībawayh, as reported by Ibn Sīdah in Al-Muḥkam, distinguishes saruʿa from asraʿa: the bare verb may highlight a person’s habitual or natural speed, whereas Form IV may highlight an effort to perform the action more quickly.

Al-Muḥkam: Sībawayh on saruʿa and asraʿa

That distinction can be explained in some uses, but it should not become an absolute rule.

Arabic asraʿa and saruʿa may be close in some contexts.

The Form IV pattern also does not establish that every asraʿa necessarily involves strain or forced effort.

A swift horse in a poetic witness with differing attributions

A witness attributed by Ibn Barrī to ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib reads:

حَتَّى تَرَوْهُ كَاشِفًا قِنَاعَهْ
تَعْدُو بِهِ سَلْهَبَةٌ سُرَاعَهْ

ḥattā tarawhu kāshifan qināʿah / taʿdū bihi salhabatun surāʿah

English translation

Until you see him with his covering cast aside, borne along by a tall, swift mare.

Here, surāʿah describes the horse as swift; its vocalization differs from sarrāʿah.

The subject is an animal’s actual movement, rather than quick thinking or decision-making.

The witness shows that the S R ʿ family was used for a swift horse and physical movement.

Tāj al-ʿArūs records both Ibn Barrī’s attribution to ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib and Ibn Durayd’s transmission from the wife of Qays ibn Rawāḥah. The attribution is therefore not uniform, and the witness should not be called securely pre-Qur’anic without independently establishing its date.

Tāj al-ʿArūs: surāʿah and the horse witness’s differing attributions

Speed and achieving a result in Ibn Aḥmar’s poetry

A verse attributed to Ibn Aḥmar reads:

أَلَا لَا أَرَى هَذَا الْمُسَرِّعَ سَابِقًا
وَلَا أَحَدًا يَرْجُو الْبَقِيَّةَ بَاقِيًا

alā lā arā hādhā al-musarriʿa sābiqan / wa-lā aḥadan yarjū al-baqiyyata bāqiyā

English translation

Indeed, I do not see this hurrying person getting ahead, nor anyone who hopes to remain actually remaining.

In Al-Muḥkam and Tāj al-ʿArūs, al-musarriʿ is written with doubled rāʾ: the active participle of sarraʿa. This word appears alongside sābiqan in the witness.

Al-Muḥkam and Tāj al-ʿArūs: al-musarriʿ in Ibn Aḥmar’s verse

Their juxtaposition matters because it distinguishes having speed from getting ahead.

Someone may move quickly yet fail to reach the desired result or outstrip others.

Speed itself therefore does not guarantee success.

This witness too is recorded in early Arabic, but should not be called securely pre-Islamic without independently establishing its date.

Saraʿān and sarʿān: those who set out earlier

Arabic uses saraʿān al-nās, with an open rāʾ, and sarʿān al-nās, with a vowelless rāʾ, for those who move or act before others.

Here, speed is connected with earlier timing and taking the lead.

La-sarʿāna mā faʿala is also attested as an expression of how soon something was done.

Physical movement is unnecessary here: the focus is the prompt occurrence of the action itself.

Saraʿraʿ: a moist shoot and suppleness of body

Early lexicons record saraʿraʿ for a shoot that remains moist, fresh, and tender, and also for a supple young person.

This witness establishes a botanical and bodily descriptive branch in the sources; by itself it does not denote rapid growth.

The name therefore establishes neither that softness in plants is the core of S R ʿ nor that every saraʿraʿ necessarily expresses speed.

A historical connection between this use and speed requires independent evidence. The basic Qur’anic definition remains speed as opposed to slowness.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ and Tāj al-ʿArūs: the meaning of saraʿraʿ

Overall, the early evidence confirms a clear core: speed in movement, action, or bringing about a result, as opposed to slowness.

4. Hastening in good: speed praised by the Qur’an

Āl ʿImrān 3:133: hastening toward forgiveness

Verse text

وَسَارِعُوا إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ وَجَنَّةٍ عَرْضُهَا السَّمَاوَاتُ وَالْأَرْضُ أُعِدَّتْ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ

wa-sāriʿū ilā maghfiratin min rabbikum wa-jannatin ʿarḍuhā al-samāwātu wa-al-arḍu uʿiddat li-al-muttaqīna

English translation

And hasten toward forgiveness from your Lord and a Garden as wide as the heavens and the earth, prepared for those mindful of The God.

Examining the use

Sāriʿū is a second-person plural imperative in Form III.

Here, the addressees are commanded to hasten.

The goal of this haste is also explicit:

«إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ وَجَنَّةٍ» (ilā maghfiratin min rabbikum wa-jannah).

The Qur’an therefore specifies the direction and destination, rather than merely saying to be quick.

This matters because speed without knowing the destination carries no particular moral value in itself.

Here, the goal is the Lord’s forgiveness and the Garden.

This does not mean that people can reach forgiveness through faster bodily movement.

Forgiveness is not an earthly location toward which one walks or runs.

Speed here therefore concerns prompt initiative in conduct and actions that place people on the path of forgiveness and a good outcome.

What do the following verses clarify?

Āl ʿImrān 3:134 describes people who spend in ease and hardship, restrain anger, and pardon others.

Āl ʿImrān 3:135 also describes those who, after committing an indecency or wronging themselves, remember The God, seek forgiveness, and do not knowingly persist in wrongdoing.

Hastening toward forgiveness in this passage is therefore connected with actual conduct.

For example, someone who has wronged another and recognized the error can promptly act to put matters right.

This fits sāriʿū, because the person does not delay right action.

The verb itself does not establish that every quick decision is commendable, even without recognizing the truth or considering others’ rights.

The verse concerns hastening on the path of forgiveness, rather than rashness in every decision.

Āl ʿImrān 3:114: hastening in good deeds

Verse text

يُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ وَيُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَأُولَٰئِكَ مِنَ الصَّالِحِينَ

yuʾminūna bi-Allāhi wa-al-yawmi al-ākhiri wa-yaʾmurūna bi-al-maʿrūfi wa-yanhawna ʿani al-munkari wa-yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāti wa-ulāʾika mina al-ṣāliḥīna

English translation

They believe in The God and the Last Day, enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong, and hasten in good deeds; they are among the righteous.

Examining the use

Yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāt describes people characterized by belief, enjoining what is right, and forbidding what is wrong.

Here, speed is connected with good deeds.

The point extends beyond quick movement: they show prompt initiative and earnestness in doing good.

Linguistically, al-khayrāt is a plural of khayr and encompasses a broad range of good and beneficial deeds.

Yusāriʿūna itself, however, does not determine which action is good.

Someone may rush into an action thought to be good while actually harming others.

Recognition of good through the Qur’an, truth, and justice should therefore precede haste to perform it.

This fits the context, where hastening in good accompanies belief and right conduct, rather than being detached from them.

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:90: hastening in good alongside prayer

Verse text

فَاسْتَجَبْنَا لَهُ وَوَهَبْنَا لَهُ يَحْيَىٰ وَأَصْلَحْنَا لَهُ زَوْجَهُ إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَيَدْعُونَنَا رَغَبًا وَرَهَبًا وَكَانُوا لَنَا خَاشِعِينَ

fa-istajabnā lahu wa-wahabnā lahu Yaḥyā wa-aṣlaḥnā lahu zawjahu innahum kānū yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāti wa-yadʿūnanā raghaban wa-rahaban wa-kānū lanā khāshiʿīna

English translation

So We answered him, granted him Yaḥyā, and set his wife’s condition right for him. They used to hasten in good deeds, call upon Us with hope and fear, and remain humble before Us.

Examining the use

The same expression, yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāt, appears here.

This time, it occurs in the context of Zakariyyā’s prayer and the divine response.

Prayer with hope and fear, and humility before The God, follow.

Hastening in good here is therefore connected with a person’s relationship with The God and conduct.

This verse does not, however, establish that everyone who hastens in good will necessarily receive the same specific response as Zakariyyā.

It describes a particular event, while the description of hastening in good has a broader semantic range.

5. Hastening and getting ahead: related but distinct concepts

Al-Muʾminūn 23:61

Verse text

أُولَٰئِكَ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَهُمْ لَهَا سَابِقُونَ

ulāʾika yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāti wa-hum lahā sābiqūna

English translation

Those are the ones who hasten in good deeds, and they are foremost in them.

Examining the use

This verse is particularly useful for distinguishing the two roots.

Yusāriʿūna belongs to S R ʿ.

Sābiqūna belongs to S B Q.

Treating them as completely synonymous would obscure their semantic distinction in the sentence.

Musāraʿah emphasizes haste and prompt initiative in action, while sabq concerns getting ahead and taking precedence.

They can occur together, but are distinct.

Someone may act quickly to do good while another person has already done the same thing.

Conversely, someone may begin before others but proceed slowly afterward.

Speed and precedence therefore each identify a separate feature.

Here, both describe a group characterized in the preceding verses by fear of their Lord, belief in His signs, and avoiding association of partners with Him.

Hastening and taking the lead in good thus form part of their broader conduct and orientation.

Does musāraʿah always mean racing against others?

No.

Form III can express a relationship or participation between parties, but its pattern alone does not establish a direct contest between two or more people in every use.

For example, sāriʿū ilā maghfirah in Āl ʿImrān 3:133 expresses hastening toward forgiveness.

It does not establish that forgiveness reaches only the person who moves faster than everyone else and leaves others behind.

Hastening in good likewise does not mean competing to humiliate others or gain public recognition.

The main point is doing good without delay.

This distinction prevents the moral value of musāraʿah from being reduced to winning a competition.

6. Hastening in disbelief, sin, and harmful relationships

Āl ʿImrān 3:176: hastening in disbelief

Verse text

وَلَا يَحْزُنْكَ الَّذِينَ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْكُفْرِ إِنَّهُمْ لَنْ يَضُرُّوا اللَّهَ شَيْئًا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ أَلَّا يَجْعَلَ لَهُمْ حَظًّا فِي الْآخِرَةِ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ

wa-lā yaḥzunka alladhīna yusāriʿūna fī al-kufri innahum lan yaḍurrū Allāha shayʾan yurīdu Allāhu allā yajʿala lahum ḥaẓẓan fī al-ākhirati wa-lahum ʿadhābun ʿaẓīmun

English translation

Do not let those who hasten in disbelief grieve you. They will never harm The God at all. The God wills to give them no share in the Hereafter, and a tremendous punishment awaits them.

Examining the use

The same verb, yusāriʿūna, appears here as in the verses concerning good.

Its object of engagement, however, has changed:

«فِي الْكُفْرِ» (fī al-kufr).

Lexically, speed remains speed. What changes is the subject in which people hasten.

The criticism therefore comes from the haste being directed toward disbelief and from the verse’s context, rather than from S R ʿ itself.

Speed is thus a feature of action that can serve opposing orientations.

This expression also does not establish that everyone who comments quickly on a religious subject necessarily hastens in disbelief.

Identifying disbelief, its object, a person’s awareness, and their circumstances requires separate examination.

Al-Māʾidah 5:41 also describes hastening in disbelief. Alongside that description, it discusses groups listening to falsehood and acting in particular ways toward divine speech and its proper places.

Both verses should therefore be examined in context, rather than turning a shared expression into a judgment on every intellectual disagreement.

Al-Māʾidah 5:62: hastening in sin and aggression

Verse text

وَتَرَىٰ كَثِيرًا مِنْهُمْ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْإِثْمِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ وَأَكْلِهِمُ السُّحْتَ لَبِئْسَ مَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

wa-tarā kathīran minhum yusāriʿūna fī al-ithmi wa-al-ʿudwāni wa-aklihimu al-suḥta la-biʾsa mā kānū yaʿmalūna

English translation

You see many of them hastening in sin, aggression, and consuming illicit gain. How evil is what they used to do.

Examining the use

Here, yusāriʿūna is connected with three subjects: sin, aggression, and consuming illicit gain.

These terms belong to other roots, and their meanings should not be merged into speed itself.

Ithm concerns sin and responsibility arising from wrongdoing.

ʿUdwān concerns overstepping limits and aggression.

Suḥt here concerns consuming impure or illicit wealth.

The criticism concerns people hastening in such conduct.

Someone may commit a sin, while repetition or increasing speed in that conduct adds a further feature to the action.

Yusāriʿūna itself, however, determines neither the number of offences nor a specified time in which they occur.

The word expresses the pace and prompt inclination toward action, rather than its exact frequency.

Al-Māʾidah 5:52: hastening into association with a group

Verse text

فَتَرَى الَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ مَرَضٌ يُسَارِعُونَ فِيهِمْ يَقُولُونَ نَخْشَىٰ أَنْ تُصِيبَنَا دَائِرَةٌ فَعَسَى اللَّهُ أَنْ يَأْتِيَ بِالْفَتْحِ أَوْ أَمْرٍ مِنْ عِنْدِهِ فَيُصْبِحُوا عَلَىٰ مَا أَسَرُّوا فِي أَنْفُسِهِمْ نَادِمِينَ

fa-tarā alladhīna fī qulūbihim maraḍun yusāriʿūna fīhim yaqūlūna nakhshā an tuṣībanā dāʾiratun fa-ʿasā Allāhu an yaʾtiya bi-al-fatḥi aw amrin min ʿindihi fa-yuṣbiḥū ʿalā mā asarrū fī anfusihim nādimīna

English translation

You see those with sickness in their hearts hastening among them, saying, “We fear that an adverse turn may strike us.” Perhaps The God will bring victory or a command from Him, and they will become regretful over what they concealed within themselves.

Examining the use

This verse has yusāriʿūna fīhim.

Fī combines with the pronoun -him and describes people’s conduct toward another group.

The surrounding verses concern relationships, loyalty, and reliance upon other groups.

Hastening here therefore extends beyond bodily movement toward a gathering: it concerns quickly establishing ties and turning toward them.

Yusāriʿūna itself, however, does not mean loyalty, protective affiliation, or betrayal.

These concepts are established by context and other verbs.

The verse also reports the people’s stated reason: they fear an adverse turn of events.

Haste alone therefore does not reveal all their intentions; their statements, conduct, and circumstances require examination.

This verse thus illustrates speed extending from movement or action to decision-making and social orientation.

7. Nusāriʿu lahum fī al-khayrāt: does material prosperity establish The God’s approval?

Al-Muʾminūn 23:55 and Al-Muʾminūn 23:56

Verse texts

أَيَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّمَا نُمِدُّهُمْ بِهِ مِنْ مَالٍ وَبَنِينَ

a-yaḥsabūna annamā numidduhum bihi min mālin wa-banīna

نُسَارِعُ لَهُمْ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ بَلْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ

nusāriʿu lahum fī al-khayrāti bal lā yashʿurūna

English translation

Do they suppose that, through the wealth and children We supply them with,

We are hastening good things to them? Rather, they do not perceive.

Examining the use

Nusāriʿu is a first-person plural imperfect verb in Form III.

Here, hastening is attributed to The God, but the construction is a critical question.

The passage concerns people supplied with wealth and children who may consider their prosperity proof of definite good for themselves.

The Qur’an’s question challenges that assumption.

Nusāriʿu lahum fī al-khayrāt should therefore not be detached from the preceding question to conclude that everyone’s wealth and children establish The God’s particular approval.

Bal lā yashʿurūn also indicates that this group’s perception and judgment of their condition are unsound.

Comparing this use with Al-Muʾminūn 23:61

A few verses later, the Qur’an says of another group:

«أُولَٰئِكَ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَهُمْ لَهَا سَابِقُونَ» (ulāʾika yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāti wa-hum lahā sābiqūn).

In the first passage, people suppose that The God is hastening good things to them, but that supposition is questioned and criticized.

In the second, the conduct of people themselves hastening in good is described.

The distinction is fundamental.

Possessing material resources differs from actually hastening in good deeds.

Someone may possess great wealth yet use it for oppression and corruption. Another may have limited means yet remain faithful to truth and justice within those limits.

The amount of wealth and number of children therefore provide no independent measure of musāraʿah in good.

Linguistically too, nusāriʿu in Al-Muʾminūn 23:56 takes lahum, whereas yusāriʿūna in Al-Muʾminūn 23:61 describes the people’s own action.

This difference in subject and construction should not be overlooked.

8. Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb: swiftness in divine reckoning

Al-Baqarah 2:202

Verse text

أُولَٰئِكَ لَهُمْ نَصِيبٌ مِمَّا كَسَبُوا وَاللَّهُ سَرِيعُ الْحِسَابِ

ulāʾika lahum naṣībun mimmā kasabū wa-Allāhu sarīʿu al-ḥisābi

English translation

Those have a share of what they have earned, and The God is swift in reckoning.

Examining the use

Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb is a construct phrase.

Sarīʿ is an adjective from S R ʿ, while al-ḥisāb belongs to Ḥ S B.

The construction relates speed to reckoning.

Divine reckoning should nevertheless be distinguished from human reckoning.

A person examining a matter may need time, tools, documents, counting, and recollection of information.

The Qur’an, by comparison, describes The God’s knowledge as comprehensive.

Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb therefore should not imply that The God, like a person, first faces slowness in calculation and then strives to become faster.

Conversely, speed should not be removed altogether from the expression and replaced merely with knowledge.

The verse explicitly uses sarīʿ.

Semantically, the emphasis is thus on swiftness in reckoning and the absence of limiting delay or difficulty.

Other uses of sarīʿ al-ḥisāb

The expression also occurs in Āl ʿImrān 3:19 and Āl ʿImrān 3:199, Al-Māʾidah 5:4, Al-Raʿd 13:41, Ibrāhīm 14:51, Al-Nūr 24:39, and Ghāfir 40:17.

In total, eight of the ten occurrences of sarīʿ concern divine reckoning.

Their contexts, however, differ.

Āl ʿImrān 3:19 concerns disbelief in The God’s signs.

Āl ʿImrān 3:199 describes the reward of a group among the People of the Book who believe in The God and revelation.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:4, the expression concludes an instruction concerning food and hunting.

Al-Nūr 24:39 describes disbelievers finding their deeds like a mirage, then encountering divine reckoning.

Ghāfir 40:17 likewise places the expression in the context of recompense for deeds and the absence of injustice on the Day of Reckoning.

Swiftness in reckoning therefore appears alongside reward and examination of deeds, as well as punishment.

This prevents sarīʿ al-ḥisāb from being restricted to hastening punishment.

Reckoning and punishment are related but distinct concepts.

Does sarīʿ al-ḥisāb mean reckoning within a very short time?

The expression conveys speed, but specifies no duration in minutes, hours, or days.

Nor can a physical theory of the passage of time for The God be derived from it.

Linguistically, sarīʿ genuinely conveys swiftness; its realization in relation to The God should nevertheless be kept distinct from bodily imagery and human limitations.

“Swift in reckoning” therefore preserves the word’s meaning while avoiding unsubstantiated details.

9. Asraʿ al-ḥāsibīn and asraʿ makran

Al-Anʿām 6:62: the swiftest of reckoners

Verse text

ثُمَّ رُدُّوا إِلَى اللَّهِ مَوْلَاهُمُ الْحَقِّ أَلَا لَهُ الْحُكْمُ وَهُوَ أَسْرَعُ الْحَاسِبِينَ

thumma ruddū ilā Allāhi mawlāhumu al-ḥaqqi alā lahu al-ḥukmu wa-huwa asraʿu al-ḥāsibīna

English translation

Then they are returned to The God, their true Protector. Surely judgment belongs to Him, and He is the swiftest of reckoners.

Examining the use

Asraʿ is an elative form from S R ʿ.

Asraʿ al-ḥāsibīn describes The God as the swiftest of reckoners.

The comparison should not become a bodily race between The God and human beings.

Arabic elative constructions can emphasize superiority and incomparability in the quality described.

The expression highlights swiftness in divine reckoning alongside the limitations of other reckoners.

The word itself, however, does not specify how many moments the reckoning takes.

The preceding expression also states:

«أَلَا لَهُ الْحُكْمُ» (alā lahu al-ḥukm).

This juxtaposition connects reckoning with divine judgment, but asraʿ itself does not mean sovereignty or judging.

Yūnus 10:21: asraʿ makran

Verse text

وَإِذَا أَذَقْنَا النَّاسَ رَحْمَةً مِنْ بَعْدِ ضَرَّاءَ مَسَّتْهُمْ إِذَا لَهُمْ مَكْرٌ فِي آيَاتِنَا قُلِ اللَّهُ أَسْرَعُ مَكْرًا إِنَّ رُسُلَنَا يَكْتُبُونَ مَا تَمْكُرُونَ

wa-idhā adhaqnā al-nāsa raḥmatan min baʿdi ḍarrāʾa massat-hum idhā lahum makrun fī āyātinā quli Allāhu asraʿu makran inna rusulanā yaktubūna mā tamkurūna

English translation

When We let people taste mercy after adversity has touched them, suddenly they scheme against Our signs. Say, “The God is swifter in scheming; Our messengers record what you scheme.”

Examining the use

Asraʿ is again an elative here, this time with makran.

Speed is thus connected with scheming and responding to people’s deceptive plans.

Makr belongs to the independent root M K R and requires examination in that root’s article.

For S R ʿ, the clear point is that The God is described here with asraʿ.

This should not imply that The God, like a person, is forced by fear, surprise, or limited knowledge to make a new decision hurriedly.

Nor should divine makr be equated with unjust human deception without considering context.

The verse itself describes people scheming against The God’s signs while their actions are recorded.

Asraʿ makran in this construction therefore concerns the superiority and swiftness of divine planning in response to their schemes.

Nevertheless, asraʿ itself expresses speed and its comparison. The nature of makr, justice, and its consequences are established through that separate root and context.

10. Sarīʿ al-ʿiqāb: swiftness in punishment alongside forgiveness

Al-Anʿām 6:165

Verse text

وَهُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَكُمْ خَلَائِفَ الْأَرْضِ وَرَفَعَ بَعْضَكُمْ فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ دَرَجَاتٍ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ فِي مَا آتَاكُمْ إِنَّ رَبَّكَ سَرِيعُ الْعِقَابِ وَإِنَّهُ لَغَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ

wa-huwa alladhī jaʿalakum khalāʾifa al-arḍi wa-rafaʿa baʿḍakum fawqa baʿḍin darajātin li-yabluwakum fī mā ātākum inna rabbaka sarīʿu al-ʿiqābi wa-innahu la-ghafūrun raḥīmun

English translation

He is the One who made you successors on earth and raised some of you above others in degrees, to test you through what He has given you. Your Lord is swift in punishment, and He is indeed most forgiving and merciful.

Examining the use

Sarīʿ al-ʿiqāb uses the same adjective sarīʿ, but relates it to punishment rather than reckoning.

ʿIqāb concerns punishment and the consequences of wrongdoing.

Swiftness in reckoning and swiftness in punishment should therefore be distinguished.

The same verse places swiftness in punishment alongside The God’s forgiveness and mercy.

That juxtaposition matters.

Sarīʿ al-ʿiqāb does not establish that The God immediately punishes everyone in every case, with no respite or opportunity for repentance.

Nor should the description of forgiveness render the warning of swift punishment ineffective.

The verse presents both.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:167

Al-Aʿrāf 7:167 likewise states:

«إِنَّ رَبَّكَ لَسَرِيعُ الْعِقَابِ وَإِنَّهُ لَغَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ» (inna rabbaka la-sarīʿu al-ʿiqābi wa-innahu la-ghafūrun raḥīm).

This closely resembles Al-Anʿām 6:165, with the emphatic lām added in la-sarīʿ.

In both, swiftness in punishment accompanies forgiveness and mercy.

These passages show that swift punishment does not mean injustice, rashness, or absence of mercy.

How punishment, respite, repentance, and mercy fit together, however, requires the verses concerning each subject.

S R ʿ itself conveys only speed.

11. Sirāʿan: swift movement in the resurrection scene

Qāf 50:44

Verse text

يَوْمَ تَشَقَّقُ الْأَرْضُ عَنْهُمْ سِرَاعًا ذَٰلِكَ حَشْرٌ عَلَيْنَا يَسِيرٌ

yawma tashaqqaqu al-arḍu ʿanhum sirāʿan dhālika ḥashrun ʿalaynā yasīrun

English translation

On the day the earth splits open from around them as they emerge swiftly: that gathering is easy for Us.

Examining the use

Sirāʿan here describes the speed of people’s movement.

Whereas sarīʿ al-ḥisāb concerns reckoning, this use concerns movement in a resurrection scene.

Grammatically, sirāʿan is circumstantial, describing the people as they emerge.

“Swiftly” is therefore a suitable translation.

The word alone, however, does not determine whether they hurry willingly, fearfully, under compulsion, or hopefully.

Those features require the context of other verses.

Al-Maʿārij 70:43

Verse text

يَوْمَ يَخْرُجُونَ مِنَ الْأَجْدَاثِ سِرَاعًا كَأَنَّهُمْ إِلَىٰ نُصُبٍ يُوفِضُونَ

yawma yakhrujūna mina al-ajdāthi sirāʿan kaʾannahum ilā nuṣubin yūfiḍūna

English translation

On the day they emerge swiftly from the graves, as though rushing toward a raised marker.

Examining the use

Sirāʿan appears here too.

This time, yakhrujūna and mina al-ajdāth explicitly identify emergence from the graves.

The following comparison makes their swift movement clearer.

Yūfiḍūna belongs to a different root and concerns moving quickly toward something.

Several linguistic elements thus combine to create the image of haste in movement.

Sirāʿan nevertheless should not be defined as resurrection, gathering, or emergence from a grave.

Those are contextual events; sirāʿan describes only the speed of movement.

Does swift movement indicate eagerness?

Not necessarily.

Someone may hurry eagerly toward something; another may move quickly from fear, pressure, or obligation.

These two verses concern resurrection and the gathering of people.

Speed alone therefore does not establish that everyone is eager for that event or approaches it in the same psychological condition.

Such judgments require independent examination of context.

12. Distinctions from related roots and translation proposals

S R ʿ (saruʿa) compared with ʿ J L (ʿajila)

Study of ʿ J L

ʿ J L concerns haste, bringing forward, or wanting and doing something early.

S R ʿ emphasizes speed in movement or action as opposed to slowness.

Their fields overlap, but are distinct.

The Qur’an criticizes human haste in some situations, as in Al-Isrāʾ 17:11.

Āl ʿImrān 3:133, however, commands hastening.

Criticism of haste in one verse therefore does not establish that every kind of speed is blameworthy.

Nor should the roots be restricted to the absolute rule that speed is always good and haste always bad.

Linguistically, both can concern acting sooner. Context must establish their value.

S R ʿ (saruʿa) compared with S B Q (sabaqa)

Study of S B Q

Sabq concerns getting ahead and taking precedence.

S R ʿ concerns speed and promptness in action.

They occur together in Al-Muʾminūn 23:61, where their distinction is clear.

Someone may move quickly without overtaking another, or get ahead by starting earlier without moving faster.

Sābiq and sarīʿ are therefore not complete synonyms.

S R ʿ (saruʿa) compared with B D R (bādara)

Study of B D R

Arabic mubādarah concerns taking the initiative and acting early.

Musāraʿah can also cover this field.

Speed concerns the pace of an action, whereas mubādarah gives greater prominence to beginning and not delaying it.

This is a tendency, rather than an absolute rule.

S R ʿ (saruʿa) compared with S ʿ Y (saʿā)

Study of S ʿ Y

Qur’anic saʿy concerns movement, effort, and pursuing an activity.

Speed can accompany effort, but not every saʿy is necessarily quick.

Someone may work steadily for years toward a sound goal.

Saʿy therefore concerns effort and movement toward a goal, while speed concerns its pace.

S R ʿ (saruʿa) compared with ʿ D W (ʿadā)

Study of ʿ D W

Some Arabic uses of ʿ D W concern running and rapid movement, while another field concerns transgression and enmity.

S R ʿ itself, however, conveys neither transgression nor enmity.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:62, al-ithm and al-ʿudwān accompany yusāriʿūna, but this juxtaposition should not merge the roots.

S R ʿ (saruʿa) compared with S R B (saraba)

Study of S R B

S R B concerns going or movement and, in some uses, a route or path.

It nevertheless differs in root from S R ʿ.

Movement may be fast or slow; movement itself therefore does not equal speed.

S R ʿ compared with Ṣ R ʿ (ṣaraʿa)

Study of Ṣ R ʿ

Ṣ R ʿ begins with ṣād and concerns falling or throwing someone to the ground.

It differs from S R ʿ.

The two shared final letters should not transfer falling or wrestling into the meaning of speed.

S R ʿ compared with S ʿ R (saʿara)

Study of S ʿ R

S ʿ R concerns kindling and blazing fire.

It too is independent of S R ʿ.

Saʿīr should not be treated as a derivative of speed because of similar spelling.

Translation proposals for Forqan

The basic equivalent of sarīʿ is “swift.”

For sarīʿ al-ḥisāb, “swift in reckoning” is natural and precise.

For sarīʿ al-ʿiqāb, “swift in punishment” is suitable.

Asraʿ al-ḥāsibīn can be rendered “the swiftest of reckoners,” and asraʿ makran as “swifter in scheming” or “swifter in counter-planning.” The latter adds contextual explanation and should not replace the direct wording without reason.

For sāriʿū ilā maghfirah, “hasten toward forgiveness” is suitable.

Yusāriʿūna fī al-khayrāt naturally becomes “they hasten in good deeds.”

Yusāriʿūna fī al-kufr should retain “they hasten in disbelief.”

For yusāriʿūna fīhim in Al-Māʾidah 5:52, “they hasten among them” closely follows the construction. Social relationships and loyalty should be explained separately in reflection.

For sirāʿan in Qāf 50:44 and Al-Maʿārij 70:43, “swiftly” is suitable.

Translation should not automatically turn speed into outstripping others, reprehensible haste, fear, eagerness, or bodily movement.

Those concepts may be discussed in particular contexts when supported by other wording.

13. Final synthesis

Arabic S R ʿ conveys speed in movement or performing an action, as opposed to slowness.

Its Qur’anic uses cover several fields: hastening in good, hastening in disbelief and sin, prompt social affiliation, swift divine reckoning and punishment, and people’s rapid movement at resurrection.

These uses show that speed itself has no fixed moral value.

The Qur’an commands hastening toward forgiveness and identifies haste in good as a quality of the righteous.

It criticizes, by comparison, hastening in disbelief, sin, aggression, and improper affiliations.

The value of speed is therefore established by its direction and subject.

Musāraʿah is also close to sabq but distinct: speed concerns the pace of movement or action; sabq concerns getting ahead and taking precedence over another.

In uses concerning The God, speed is connected with reckoning, punishment, and responding to human schemes.

That description should not become human rashness, limited knowledge, or dependence on physical processes.

Conversely, avoiding such images should not remove the actual meaning of speed from the Qur’anic expression.

In Qāf 50:44 and Al-Maʿārij 70:43, speed occurs in the scene of people emerging from graves. This use is closer to physical movement, but the word itself does not specify its psychological cause.

Final definition: S R ʿ concerns speed, or the quicker performance of movement, action, and the realization of a result, as opposed to slowness. In Form III, this may extend to prompt initiative and hastening toward a goal; context determines the goal and its value.

Understanding each use requires three matters to be clear: who or what is swift, in which action or direction, and how that action relates to truth, justice, and responsibility.

Speed can help someone advance in good or carry them further along a path of error and oppression.

Qur’anic virtue therefore lies in recognizing truth and hastening toward it, rather than in unrestricted speed.
