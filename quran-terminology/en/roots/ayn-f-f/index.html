Central meaning: restraining oneself and abstaining from a particular act or benefit. This often concerns improper conduct, but in Al-Nur 24:60 includes choosing greater restraint within an explicit permission. The subject and limits of restraint depend on context.

1. The root and its meanings in early Arabic

ʿ F F is a doubled Arabic root: its second and third consonants are identical. Its principal branch concerns self-restraint and abstention, often from what is improper or unworthy. This qualification must not erase contexts in which abstention is preferable while the alternative remains permitted.

Early Arabic ʿaffa ʿani al-shayʾ means abstaining from something. ʿIffah and ʿafāf name restraint of desire and avoidance of conduct from which one should refrain.

ʿAfīf describes a self-restrained person; its feminine form is ʿafīfah. The quality applies to both men and women and is not restricted to sexual relations.

Someone may have access to another's property and the opportunity to take it, yet refrain because it is not theirs.

Another person may be in need but refrain from repeated or demeaning requests.

In another setting, a person may experience sexual desire but abstain from an improper relationship.

The subjects differ, but share a feature: despite opportunity, need, or desire, a person restrains a particular behavior.

Restraint does not mean having no desires. Mere lack of interest does not necessarily constitute self-restraint.

Its meaning becomes clearer when someone faces desire, opportunity, or pressure and refrains from conduct they should avoid.

Nor should restraint mean depriving people of every natural and legitimate desire.

Eating, owning property, marrying, and seeking needed help do not inherently conflict with restraint. Conduct, others' rights, and relevant limits matter.

The main sense may therefore be defined as:

Restraining oneself and abstaining from a particular act or benefit, where abstention is required or preferable in context.

Abstention is the semantic core; its object is determined by context.

2. Early Arabic poetic witnesses

A recognized witness appears in a verse attributed to Aws ibn Hajar, a pre-Islamic poet:

يَا عَيْنُ جُودِي عَلَى عَمْرِو بْنِ مَسْعُودِ
أَهْلِ الْعَفَافِ وَأَهْلِ الْحَزْمِ وَالْجُودِ

yā ʿaynu jūdī ʿalā ʿamri bni masʿūdin / ahli al-ʿafāfi wa-ahli al-ḥazmi wa-al-jūd

“O eye, weep generously for ʿAmr ibn Masʿūd, a man of restraint, resolve, and generosity.”

Al-ʿafāf appears alongside al-ḥazm and al-jūd.

Ḥazm concerns firmness of decision and foresight; jūd concerns generosity.

The poet recalls someone combining restraint, firmness in decisions, and generosity toward others.

The verse places restraint among broader qualities of character. It does not itself specify what the person refrained from; the nonsexual range is established more clearly by the next witness and the Quranic financial uses.

Generosity and restraint remain distinct concepts.

A generous person may fail to restrain improper desires; a restrained person may neglect helping others.

Their conjunction in poetry does not make their meanings identical.

The verse is recorded under Aws ibn Hajar, a pre-Islamic poet. Its exact date has not been independently established in this study; attribution and dating a particular verse should be distinguished.

Restraint in poverty

Another verse is attributed to ʿAmr ibn al-Ahtam:

جُرْثُومَةٌ أُنُفٌ يَعْتَفُّ مُقْتِرُهَا
عَنِ الْخَبِيثِ وَيُعْطِي الْخَيْرَ مُثْرِيهَا

jurthūmatun unufun yaʿtaffu muqtiruhā / ʿani al-khabīthi wa-yuʿṭī al-khayra muthrīhā

“A noble, proud stock: its poor refrain from the vile, and its wealthy give what is good.”

Yaʿtaffu is a verbal form belonging to ʿ F F.

Muqtir denotes someone in straitened circumstances; muthrī denotes someone wealthy.

The poet contrasts a poor person refraining from improper conduct with a wealthy person sharing good from their resources.

This contrast clarifies restraint.

It is not reserved for the wealthy or those without need. Even someone facing poverty may avoid improper ways of satisfying their wants.

Wealth is not itself the praised virtue: the wealthy person's generosity is praised separately.

ʿAmr ibn al-Ahtam lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods. This study has not established this verse's date. It supports early economic and ethical usage, without proving a pre-Quranic date.

Together, these witnesses connect the family with avoiding unworthy desires, wrongful appropriation or conduct, and maintaining honorable character.

The Quran also employs this sense in four different contexts.

3. Lexical branches and forms

Important Arabic forms include ʿaffa, yaʿiffu, ʿiffah, ʿafāf, ʿafāfah, ʿafīf, ʿafīfah, taʿaffafa, taʿaffuf, istaʿfafa, istiʿfāf, and iʿtaffa.

Their functions and uses differ.

ʿAffa and yaʿiffu are basic-stem verbs concerning abstention and self-restraint.

ʿIffah and ʿafāf name restraint and avoidance of improper conduct.

ʿAfīf and ʿafīfah describe a man or woman possessing this restraint.

Form V taʿaffafa concerns exercising restraint and endeavoring to avoid the relevant desire or behavior.

Form X istaʿfafa likewise concerns seeking or practicing restraint and abstaining from something.

Iʿtaffa also appears in early witnesses for abstaining from the unworthy.

ʿIffah and taʿaffuf

ʿIffah may name the state or quality of restraint.

Taʿaffuf emphasizes practicing restraint and applying oneself to abstention.

Someone may have developed integrity from childhood and be known for restraint.

Another may face temptation or pressure in a difficult situation and strive to avoid misconduct.

Both involve restraint, with practical exertion more prominent in taʿaffuf.

The Form V pattern does not establish that taʿaffuf always means pretending to be restrained.

It can convey exertion and self-application without hypocrisy or deception.

In Al-Baqarah 2:273, taʿaffuf describes genuine restraint by people in need, not a pretense of integrity.

ʿIffah and istiʿfāf

Istiʿfāf belongs to Form X.

That pattern can express seeking, but does not automatically mean requesting something from another person in every verb.

Here, istiʿfāf concerns endeavoring to exercise and maintain restraint.

Someone entrusted with another's property may refrain from benefiting personally from it.

Another person may resist an improper sexual desire.

Both concern the person's conduct, not necessarily asking someone else for restraint.

The verb and the object of abstention determine the relevant meaning.

Other lexical branches

Arabic sources also record ʿuffah and ʿufāfah for a little milk remaining in an animal's udder after milking.

Another explanation uses ʿufāfah for a small amount of milk sucked by a young animal.

This differs from ethical restraint.

One might imagine a connection between a small remaining quantity and not consuming it all, but the evidence examined here does not establish one historical origin for both branches.

ʿIffān also occurs for the time or season of something and has been treated as a form close to iffān.

It should not be connected to self-restraint without historical and morphological examination.

These branches show why not every word containing these consonants can be explained by one ethical definition.

4. Quranic forms

The Quran contains two main formations from this family:

Al-taʿaffufi — Al-Baqarah 2:273

Form V verbal noun: practicing restraint.

Fa-l-yastaʿfif — Al-Nisa 4:6

Form X imperfect in a command with lām, concerning a financially independent guardian of an orphan's property.

Wa-l-yastaʿfifi — Al-Nur 24:33

The same verbal construction, concerning those unable to marry.

Yastaʿfifna — Al-Nur 24:60

Form X imperfect, third-person feminine plural, concerning elderly women and clothing.

Al-taʿaffufi is this family's sole Quranic verbal noun; the other three occurrences belong to Form X.

Fa-l-yastaʿfif and wa-l-yastaʿfifi contain the commanding lām.

The final i of wa-l-yastaʿfifi in Al-Nur 24:33 facilitates connection to the following word, avoiding two adjacent vowelless consonants. It does not change the root or verbal form.

The final nūn in yastaʿfifna is the feminine plural subject pronoun, distinguishing it from the other singular forms.

Quranic restraint is not confined to sexual relations.

Two occurrences concern finances; two concern marriage and clothing.

A Quranic definition must account for all four.

5. Mina al-taʿaffuf: restraint among people in need

The first Quranic occurrence appears in Al-Baqarah:

لِلْفُقَرَاءِ الَّذِينَ أُحْصِرُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ لَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ ضَرْبًا فِي الْأَرْضِ يَحْسَبُهُمُ الْجَاهِلُ أَغْنِيَاءَ مِنَ التَّعَفُّفِ تَعْرِفُهُمْ بِسِيمَاهُمْ لَا يَسْأَلُونَ النَّاسَ إِلْحَافًا وَمَا تُنْفِقُوا مِنْ خَيْرٍ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ بِهِ عَلِيمٌ

li-al-fuqarāʾi alladhīna uḥṣirū fī sabīli Allāhi lā yastaṭīʿūna ḍarban fī al-arḍi yaḥsabuhumu al-jāhilu aghniyāʾa mina al-taʿaffufi taʿrifuhum bi-sīmāhum lā yasʾalūna al-nāsa ilḥāfan wa-mā tunfiqū min khayrin fa-inna Allāha bihi ʿalīm

Al-Baqarah 2:273

Al-Baqarah 2:273

“For the poor who are constrained in The God's way and cannot travel about the land. The uninformed suppose them wealthy because of their restraint. You recognize them by their signs; they do not ask people insistently. Whatever good you spend, The God surely knows it.”

Al-taʿaffuf describes people in need whose restraint conceals their poverty from the uninformed. Their abstention from asking, or from insistent asking, is discussed below.

Their circumstances prevent them from traveling freely to find suitable means of livelihood.

Their poverty need not result from laziness or unwillingness to work.

For example, illness, insecurity, war, confinement, or other difficulties can prevent work or travel to earn a living. These illustrate possible constraints, rather than identifying every historical circumstance of this verse.

Despite their circumstances, they refrain from repeatedly displaying their need and insistently requesting aid.

The role of mina al-taʿaffuf

Mina al-taʿaffuf explains the uninformed observer's mistaken impression.

يَحْسَبُهُمُ الْجَاهِلُ أَغْنِيَاءَ

yaḥsabuhumu al-jāhilu aghniyāʾ

Al-Baqarah 2:273

Someone unaware of their real circumstances supposes them wealthy.

Their restraint produces that mistaken impression.

A person might dress neatly, behave respectfully, and never discuss financial difficulties, yet lack money for essential food or medicine.

Someone judging only appearances might think they need no help.

Restraint has concealed their true situation from others.

Thus, restraint here does not mean financial independence: it is conduct that may hide genuine need.

Neat appearance, silence, or not asking is insufficient evidence that a person has no need.

Does the Quran prohibit asking for help?

The verse continues:

لَا يَسْأَلُونَ النَّاسَ إِلْحَافًا

lā yasʾalūna al-nāsa ilḥāfā

Al-Baqarah 2:273

That is: “They do not ask people insistently.”

Ilḥāfan belongs to L Ḥ F and here concerns insistence in asking.

Two interpretations are recorded: denial of insistent asking, or complete abstention from asking, expressed emphatically. This article's translation retains the explicit qualifier ilḥāfan without dismissing the other interpretation.

The verse describes particular people in need. Even on the complete-abstention interpretation, it does not by itself establish a universal prohibition on requesting necessary help.

Someone in an emergency may ask for assistance without demeaning or excessive insistence.

Requesting help for genuine need is not inherently contrary to restraint.

The verse should not be taken to require hunger, illness, or severe deprivation rather than requesting essential help.

Neither the root itself nor ilḥāfan yields that conclusion.

The responsibility of those able to help

This verse belongs to a passage about giving.

It does more than praise the poor person's restraint: it draws the attention of those able to assist.

The Quran says:

تَعْرِفُهُمْ بِسِيمَاهُمْ

taʿrifuhum bi-sīmāhum

Al-Baqarah 2:273

That is: “You recognize them by their signs.”

Recognizing need does not always depend on an explicit request.

Someone may feel embarrassed to disclose genuine need or fear losing dignity before others.

An able person need not merely wait for a request, but can respectfully learn the circumstances and act to meet the need.

A family may struggle to obtain food or medicine without wanting its circumstances publicly exposed.

Someone aware of that need can help while preserving dignity and privacy.

Al-Baqarah 2:271 describes concealed charity to the poor as better.

This connection cautions against turning assistance into humiliation or exposure of someone's need.

Dignity and ways of helping emerge from the wider giving passages; the direct meaning of taʿaffuf remains restraint.

6. Fa-l-yastaʿfif: refraining from using an orphan's property

The second occurrence appears in Al-Nisa:

وَابْتَلُوا الْيَتَامَىٰ حَتَّىٰ إِذَا بَلَغُوا النِّكَاحَ فَإِنْ آنَسْتُمْ مِنْهُمْ رُشْدًا فَادْفَعُوا إِلَيْهِمْ أَمْوَالَهُمْ وَلَا تَأْكُلُوهَا إِسْرَافًا وَبِدَارًا أَنْ يَكْبَرُوا وَمَنْ كَانَ غَنِيًّا فَلْيَسْتَعْفِفْ وَمَنْ كَانَ فَقِيرًا فَلْيَأْكُلْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ فَإِذَا دَفَعْتُمْ إِلَيْهِمْ أَمْوَالَهُمْ فَأَشْهِدُوا عَلَيْهِمْ وَكَفَىٰ بِاللَّهِ حَسِيبًا

wa-ibtalū al-yatāmā ḥattā idhā balaghū al-nikāḥa fa-in ānastum minhum rushdan fa-dfaʿū ilayhim amwālahum wa-lā taʾkulūhā isrāfan wa-bidāran an yakbarū wa-man kāna ghaniyyan fa-l-yastaʿfif wa-man kāna faqīran fa-l-yaʾkul bi-al-maʿrūfi fa-idhā dafaʿtum ilayhim amwālahum fa-ashhidū ʿalayhim wa-kafā bi-Allāhi ḥasībā

Al-Nisāʾ 4:6

Al-Nisa 4:6

“Test the orphans until they reach marriageable age; then, if you discern sound judgment in them, hand over their property. Do not consume it extravagantly and hastily before they grow up. Whoever is financially independent should abstain; whoever is poor should consume according to what is right. When handing over their property, have witnesses present; The God suffices as Reckoner.”

Fa-l-yastaʿfif concerns someone responsible for preserving and administering an orphan's property.

The guardian is not its owner.

The property belongs to the orphan; the guardian administers it until the conditions for handing it over are met.

That distinction is essential here.

Access to another's property does not necessarily confer a right to spend it for personal wants.

Imagine someone administering a child's assets, with access to accounts and responsibility for necessary expenses and safeguarding funds.

That access does not turn the child's assets into the guardian's property.

Financial restraint matters in this situation.

The role of wa-man kāna ghaniyyan

The Quran says:

وَمَنْ كَانَ غَنِيًّا فَلْيَسْتَعْفِفْ

wa-man kāna ghaniyyan fa-l-yastaʿfif

Al-Nisāʾ 4:6

That is: “Whoever is financially independent should abstain.”

The financially independent guardian should refrain from personal use of the orphan's property.

This does not prohibit administering the assets or spending them on the orphan's own needs.

The subject is personal consumption of another's property.

Istiʿfāf here therefore concerns refraining from personally benefiting from the orphan's assets.

This makes the nonsexual range clear: financial integrity and restraint from improper personal benefit also fall within this field.

The role of wa-man kāna faqīran

The verse continues:

وَمَنْ كَانَ فَقِيرًا فَلْيَأْكُلْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

wa-man kāna faqīran fa-l-yaʾkul bi-al-maʿrūf

Al-Nisāʾ 4:6

That is: “Whoever is poor should consume according to what is right.”

The verse distinguishes the financially independent guardian from the poor guardian.

The former receives the command to abstain.

The latter's use is discussed within the limits of maʿrūf, what is right and recognized as proper.

Restraint should therefore not be defined as condemning every receipt or use of property under all circumstances.

The poor guardian's use is qualified. Exegetical and legal accounts differ about its amount, its relation to compensation or borrowing, and possible repayment; istiʿfāf alone does not settle those details.

Bi-al-maʿrūf does not grant unlimited discretion to take any amount.

The manner and extent of use must respect actual circumstances, the guardian's responsibility, and the owner's rights.

Ethical restraint concerns rights and proper limits, rather than absolute deprivation.

Restraint and oversight of entrusted assets

The verse ends by commanding witnesses when property is handed over to its owners.

This relates to trustworthiness and preventing financial disputes or misuse.

Restraint here is not merely a feeling; it affects how another's assets are administered.

Someone might claim integrity and restraint while using another's property without clear accounting or records.

A claim of restraint cannot replace financial responsibility and respect for rights.

That conclusion arises from the whole verse; fa-l-yastaʿfif itself commands restraint.

7. Wa-l-yastaʿfifi: restraint when marriage is unavailable

The third occurrence appears in Al-Nur:

وَلْيَسْتَعْفِفِ الَّذِينَ لَا يَجِدُونَ نِكَاحًا حَتَّىٰ يُغْنِيَهُمُ اللَّهُ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ وَالَّذِينَ يَبْتَغُونَ الْكِتَابَ مِمَّا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ فَكَاتِبُوهُمْ إِنْ عَلِمْتُمْ فِيهِمْ خَيْرًا وَآتُوهُمْ مِنْ مَالِ اللَّهِ الَّذِي آتَاكُمْ وَلَا تُكْرِهُوا فَتَيَاتِكُمْ عَلَى الْبِغَاءِ إِنْ أَرَدْنَ تَحَصُّنًا لِتَبْتَغُوا عَرَضَ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَمَنْ يُكْرِهْهُنَّ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ مِنْ بَعْدِ إِكْرَاهِهِنَّ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ

wa-l-yastaʿfifi alladhīna lā yajidūna nikāḥan ḥattā yughniyahumu Allāhu min faḍlihi wa-alladhīna yabtaghūna al-kitāba mimmā malakat aymānukum fa-kātibūhum in ʿalimtum fīhim khayran wa-ātūhum min māli Allāhi alladhī ātākum wa-lā tukrihū fatayātikum ʿalā al-bighāʾi in aradna taḥaṣṣunan li-tabtaghū ʿaraḍa al-ḥayāti al-dunyā wa-man yukrihhunna fa-inna Allāha min baʿdi ikrāhihinna ghafūrun raḥīm

Al-Nūr 24:33

Al-Nur 24:33

“Those unable to marry should remain chaste until The God enriches them from His bounty. Those among the people your right hands possess who seek a contract: make a contract with them if you know good in them, and give them from The God's wealth that He has given you. Do not force your enslaved women into prostitution, if they desire chastity, seeking the goods of worldly life. Whoever forces them: after their being coerced, The God is Forgiving, Merciful.”

Wa-l-yastaʿfifi concerns those unable to marry.

The subject of restraint is therefore sexual relations and marriage.

The root itself does not mean remaining unmarried or having no sexual desire.

Such a definition would not explain the earlier uses concerning orphans' property and people in need.

Self-restraint remains the core; here its object is improper sexual conduct when marriage is unavailable.

Restraint does not negate sexual desire

Someone may want marriage and a family but currently face economic, social, or other barriers.

Desire and need have not vanished.

The Quran describes those who lā yajidūna nikāḥan: cannot find the means or opportunity for marriage.

Being unable to marry does not necessarily mean not wanting marriage.

The command here concerns abstaining from improper conduct while those circumstances persist.

Chastity should not be turned into total suppression of natural desire or condemnation of marriage.

The preceding verse, Al-Nur 24:32, commands arranging marriage for the unmarried and the worthy among those in servitude.

The passage thus addresses facilitating marriage as well as individual restraint.

For example, if excessive costs or unreasonable social expectations obstruct marriage, individual restraint cannot be discussed while ignoring the role of families and society in creating those barriers.

This example is an application drawn from Al-Nur 24:32 and Al-Nur 24:33 together, not the lexical meaning of istiʿfāf alone.

The role of ḥattā yughniyahumu Allāhu min faḍlih

The verse continues:

حَتَّىٰ يُغْنِيَهُمُ اللَّهُ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ

ḥattā yughniyahumu Allāhu min faḍlih

Al-Nūr 24:33

That is: “Until The God enriches them from His bounty.”

The command concerns a situation in which marriage is not yet available.

It should not be made a recommendation to avoid marriage forever or remain deprived of legitimate relations.

Nor does it specify a time by which every individual will become very wealthy.

Ighnāʾ here concerns sufficiency or relief that removes the obstacle.

The manner and timing of that relief are not contained in istiʿfāf itself.

Restraint and the prohibition of sexual coercion

The same verse later prohibits forcing enslaved women into prostitution.

The connection matters: individual restraint is followed by others' abuse of power in sexual relations.

Chastity cannot be treated solely as the vulnerable person's responsibility while the powerful person's conduct is ignored.

A woman may refuse an improper relationship while someone controlling her economic or social circumstances threatens or pressures her.

Responsibility for coercion must not be concealed by blaming her or merely advising her to restrain herself.

The verse explicitly prohibits coercion. If they desire chastity does not license coercion in other circumstances. Its ending is understood as mercy toward the coerced women, not automatic exoneration of those coercing them.

Ikrāh belongs to K R H; coercion is not part of the lexical definition of ʿ F F.

Istiʿfāf is self-restraint; ikrāh is forcing another against their will. The context connects them without merging their roots or meanings.

8. Wa-an yastaʿfifna: restraint concerning clothing

The fourth occurrence also appears in Al-Nur:

وَالْقَوَاعِدُ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ اللَّاتِي لَا يَرْجُونَ نِكَاحًا فَلَيْسَ عَلَيْهِنَّ جُنَاحٌ أَنْ يَضَعْنَ ثِيَابَهُنَّ غَيْرَ مُتَبَرِّجَاتٍ بِزِينَةٍ وَأَنْ يَسْتَعْفِفْنَ خَيْرٌ لَهُنَّ وَاللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ

wa-al-qawāʿidu mina al-nisāʾi allātī lā yarjūna nikāḥan fa-laysa ʿalayhinna junāḥun an yaḍaʿna thiyābahunna ghayra mutabarrijātin bi-zīnatin wa-an yastaʿfifna khayrun lahunna wa-Allāhu samīʿun ʿalīm

Al-Nūr 24:60

Al-Nur 24:60

“Elderly women who no longer expect marriage incur no blame for laying aside their garments without displaying adornment; and exercising restraint is better for them. The God is Hearing, Knowing.”

Yastaʿfifna concerns elderly women who no longer expect marriage.

Its subject differs from the previous three uses.

Al-Baqarah 2:273 concerned restraint among poor people and their asking for help.

Al-Nisa 4:6 required a financially independent guardian to abstain from personal use of the orphan's property.

Al-Nur 24:33 called those unable to marry to restraint.

Here, restraint concerns clothing and setting garments aside in a specified situation.

The meaning of yastaʿfifna here

The opening removes blame from a specified group of women regarding laying aside garments.

That permission carries a qualification:

غَيْرَ مُتَبَرِّجَاتٍ بِزِينَةٍ

ghayra mutabarrijātin bi-zīnah

Al-Nūr 24:60

That is: “Without displaying adornment.”

It then says:

وَأَنْ يَسْتَعْفِفْنَ خَيْرٌ لَهُنَّ

wa-an yastaʿfifna khayrun lahunna

Al-Nūr 24:60

That is: “And exercising restraint is better for them.”

Restraint here means refraining from using the stated concession concerning clothing.

Without the preceding clause, one might wrongly translate it as sexual abstinence or refraining from asking for money.

The verse identifies the relevant conduct.

Distinguishing absence of blame from a better choice

The verse contains two different evaluations.

First:

فَلَيْسَ عَلَيْهِنَّ جُنَاحٌ

fa-laysa ʿalayhinna junāḥ

Al-Nūr 24:60

That is: “They incur no blame.”

Then:

وَأَنْ يَسْتَعْفِفْنَ خَيْرٌ لَهُنَّ

wa-an yastaʿfifna khayrun lahunna

Al-Nūr 24:60

That is: “And exercising restraint is better for them.”

This distinction matters.

Setting aside garments under specified conditions is declared blameless, while greater restraint is described as better.

Khayrun lahunna should not cancel the permission already given.

Someone may choose not to use an available permission.

That choice can be commendable without making the permitted alternative sinful.

The permission must also be distinguished from its qualifications: here, laying aside garments accompanies non-display of adornment.

One cannot cite absence of blame while discarding ghayra mutabarrijātin bi-zīnah.

Does istiʿfāf mean covering the entire body?

ʿ F F does not itself mean covering the body.

Thiyāb is an independent word naming garments or clothing.

The verse discusses setting garments aside in specified circumstances.

Istiʿfāf alone does not determine the precise type, number, or shape of garments.

Nor should an yaḍaʿna thiyābahunna be read as removing all clothing and complete nudity without examining context.

The non-display qualification and other clothing passages must inform interpretation.

Here, istiʿfāf means greater restraint concerning clothing; details derive from other words and related verses.

Is restraint specific to women?

Al-Nur 24:60 uses a feminine plural verb because its subject is elderly women.

That grammatical form does not make the root inherently female-specific.

Al-Nur 24:33 concerns those unable to marry.

Al-Nisa 4:6 addresses a guardian of an orphan's assets.

Al-Baqarah 2:273 concerns people in need.

Grammatical gender follows each sentence's subject, rather than restricting the root to one sex.

9. The shared meaning of the four Quranic uses

The four uses share a semantic core despite differing subjects.

In Al-Baqarah 2:273, a poor person restrains asking, or insistent asking, according to the interpretation discussed above.

In Al-Nisa 4:6, the financially independent guardian abstains from personal use of the orphan's property.

In Al-Nur 24:33, someone unable to marry refrains from improper sexual conduct.

In Al-Nur 24:60, an elderly woman chooses greater restraint concerning clothing.

In each case, a person refrains from a particular act.

The object of restraint nevertheless differs.

Knowing that someone is restrained is insufficient: from what, under what circumstances, and for what reason do they abstain?

Someone refraining from another's property may be acting rightly.

But if they refuse wages owed to them and needlessly deprive their family, the lexical meaning of restraint alone does not make that choice commendable.

Someone needing essential help does not necessarily lose restraint by asking respectfully.

Likewise, sexual restraint does not condemn natural desire or legitimate marriage.

Thus, Quranic restraint does not mean absolute deprivation, suppression of every desire, or surrender of every right.

Its value and limits depend on rights, recognized propriety, and context.

Restraint and economic circumstances

Three Quranic uses connect restraint with need or financial independence.

In Al-Baqarah 2:273, the poor exercise restraint despite poverty.

In Al-Nisa 4:6, an independent guardian abstains from personal use, while the poor guardian's use is qualified by maʿrūf.

Al-Nur 24:33 calls those unable to marry to restraint pending relief.

Economic circumstances can therefore affect the conduct expected.

This does not make poverty inherently virtuous or require those in need always to waive legitimate rights and wants.

Nor is wealth itself evidence of restraint: a wealthy person may still encroach on others' property.

Restraint concerns conduct, not simply the amount owned.

10. Distinguishing related roots

ʿ F F (ʿaffa) and K F F (kaffa)

K F F concerns restraining, holding back, and preventing action.

Kaffa yadahu can describe holding one's hand back from an act.

ʿAffa likewise involves restraining oneself.

Kaff has a broader preventative range, including stopping someone else or checking a force.

The principal ʿiffah branch more directly concerns self-restraint and avoidance of unworthy conduct.

Preventing someone from entering a place may be described using kaff without implying ethical self-restraint.

Abstaining from taking another's property is closer to ʿiffah.

This is a tendency, not an absolute boundary dividing every use.

ʿ F F (ʿaffa) and Ḥ Ṣ N (ḥaṣana)

Ḥ Ṣ N concerns enclosure, protection, and a protected condition.

Quranic iḥṣān and taḥaṣṣun sometimes concern chastity and protection from improper sexual relations.

ʿIffah and iḥṣān are nevertheless different words.

Self-restraint is prominent in ʿiffah.

Protection is prominent in iḥṣān.

Al-Nur 24:33 begins with wa-l-yastaʿfifi and later uses taḥaṣṣunan concerning women and the prohibition of sexual coercion.

The families may meet in one subject while retaining distinct structures and meanings.

Their coexistence does not restrict every instance of restraint to sexuality.

ʿ F F (ʿaffa) and Ṣ B R (ṣabara)

Ṣabr concerns endurance, perseverance, and holding oneself steady under pressure or difficulty.

ʿIffah may likewise require restraint under difficulty.

Someone tempted to appropriate another's assets may need perseverance to resist.

The concepts are not identical.

Ṣabr may concern illness, persistence in work, or enduring hardship; ʿiffah focuses on abstaining from a particular act or desire.

Nor is every perseverance commendable: someone may persist in wrongdoing.

Their value in a particular situation depends on their object and direction.

ʿ F F (ʿaffa) and Z H D (zahida)

Zuhd concerns limited interest or lack of desire for something.

Al-zāhidīn in Yusuf 12:20 describes those with little interest in Joseph.

Restraint does not mean lack of interest.

Someone may desire something yet refrain because it is not theirs by right.

Someone with no interest may avoid it without particular effort.

Limited desire and self-restraint are different states, although they may coexist.

ʿ F F (ʿaffa) and Ḥ Y Y (ḥayiya)

Some uses of Ḥ Y Y concern modesty or shame.

Modesty may deter a person from acting before others.

Restraint does not depend on observers.

Someone alone and unseen may refrain from taking another's property.

That conduct can still exemplify restraint.

ʿIffah should therefore not be confined to social embarrassment or fear of judgment. Nor is ḥayāʾ itself confined to others' presence; this comparison concerns one possible motive.

Shame should not obstruct necessary help, defense of rights, or speaking truth.

ʿ F F (ʿaffa) and ʿ F W (ʿafā)

Their written forms resemble one another, but the roots differ.

ʿ F F concerns restraint and abstention.

Quranic ʿ F W includes pardon and forgiveness, and in some uses surplus or what remains beyond need.

Fa-ʿfū in Al-Baqarah 2:109 belongs to ʿ F W, not ʿ F F.

Al-ʿafwa in Al-Baqarah 2:219 belongs to that same family.

Resemblance does not make ʿiffah mean forgiving another's fault.

Forgiveness and restraint may coexist in ethical conduct, but their lexical meanings and Quranic uses remain distinct.

11. Translation proposals for Forqan

Self-restraint is a suitable general equivalent across the four Quranic occurrences.

Each verse must nevertheless clarify its object.

Al-taʿaffufi — Al-Baqarah 2:273

Root: ʿ F F

Word type: definite Form V verbal noun, genitive after min.

Brief root meaning: restraining oneself and abstaining from particular conduct, as required or preferred in context.

Contextual meaning: restraint among the poor that conceals their need from an uninformed observer, in the context of asking for help.

Proposed translation:

يَحْسَبُهُمُ الْجَاهِلُ أَغْنِيَاءَ مِنَ التَّعَفُّفِ

yaḥsabuhumu al-jāhilu aghniyāʾa mina al-taʿaffuf

Al-Baqarah 2:273

“The uninformed suppose them wealthy because of their restraint.”

This accords with the basic self-restraint rendering; more detailed commentary belongs separately from the direct translation.

Here, taʿaffuf should not be translated solely as sexual chastity.

Fa-l-yastaʿfif — Al-Nisa 4:6

Word type: Form X imperfect, third-person masculine singular, jussive after the commanding lām.

Brief root meaning: abstention and restraining oneself from something to be avoided.

Contextual meaning: the financially independent guardian refraining from personal use of the orphan's assets.

Proposed translation:

وَمَنْ كَانَ غَنِيًّا فَلْيَسْتَعْفِفْ

wa-man kāna ghaniyyan fa-l-yastaʿfif

Al-Nisāʾ 4:6

“Whoever is financially independent should abstain.”

This works because the surrounding clauses clarify the object of abstention.

Wa-l-yastaʿfifi — Al-Nur 24:33

Word type: Form X imperfect, third-person masculine singular, jussive after the commanding lām; the final kasrah facilitates connected pronunciation.

Brief root meaning: practicing restraint and avoiding improper conduct.

Contextual meaning: abstaining from improper sexual behavior when marriage is unavailable.

Proposed translation:

وَلْيَسْتَعْفِفِ الَّذِينَ لَا يَجِدُونَ نِكَاحًا

wa-l-yastaʿfifi alladhīna lā yajidūna nikāḥā

Al-Nūr 24:33

“Those unable to marry should remain chaste.”

Chaste is natural here, but should not define the entire root.

Yastaʿfifna — Al-Nur 24:60

Word type: Form X imperfect, third-person feminine plural, built on sukūn because of the feminine plural nūn, in the accusative syntactic position after an.

Brief root meaning: self-restraint and abstention from a specified action.

Contextual meaning: choosing greater restraint regarding setting aside garments.

Proposed translation:

وَأَنْ يَسْتَعْفِفْنَ خَيْرٌ لَهُنَّ

wa-an yastaʿfifna khayrun lahunna

Al-Nūr 24:60

“And exercising restraint is better for them.”

This preserves the distinction between permission in the first part and a preferable choice in the second.

Chastity is suitable in some contexts, but self-restraint and abstention as required or preferred in context is broader and more accurate for a brief root definition.

Conclusion

ʿ F F concerns self-restraint and abstention, whether required or preferred in the particular setting.

Early witnesses show a range beyond sexuality: character, avoidance of unworthy conduct, and restraint in poverty and need.

The Quran uses it in four different settings.

Al-Baqarah 2:273 concerns poor people whose restraint may make others suppose them wealthy; the interpretation of asking and insistent asking was distinguished above.

Al-Nisa 4:6 concerns the financially independent guardian abstaining from personal use of an orphan's property.

Al-Nur 24:33 concerns those unable to marry.

Al-Nur 24:60 concerns elderly women's clothing and identifies greater restraint as better.

These uses do not mean absence of desire, deprivation, unwillingness to marry, or surrender of all personal rights.

Someone may be needy and have a legitimate request, experience sexual desire and want marriage, or have access to property without a right to use it personally.

The relevant restraint depends on rights, recognized propriety, and real circumstances.

The semantic core is restraining oneself and abstaining from a particular act or benefit, where abstention is required or preferable in context.

Self-restraint is a suitable basic Forqan equivalent, with each derivative interpreted in its verse. Taʿaffuf and istiʿfāf concern regulating one's conduct, not denying natural desires, depriving people of legitimate rights, or reducing every use to sexual chastity.
