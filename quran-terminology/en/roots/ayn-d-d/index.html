Linguistic Note

The core of ع د د (ʿ D D) can be seen as bringing something out of vagueness through counting, order, and preparation. When something is counted, its amount becomes clear. When something is prepared, it leaves disorder and is placed for a defined purpose.

There is a natural connection between number and preparation. Preparing for something is not mere intention; it involves assessing capacity, tools, time, strength, and path. Preparation without measure is incomplete. Counting, in turn, is not only dealing with figures; it is a way of ordering reality and becoming ready for decision, worship, appointment, travel, defense, or judgment.

So ʿ D D should not be reduced to “number” in a narrow mathematical sense. In the Qur’an, this root concerns years and months, appointed periods, preparing strength, prepared reward or punishment, and counting that brings human opportunity toward its end.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of ʿ D D is count, measure, order, and preparation: something leaves vagueness and disorder, becomes counted and arranged, and is prepared for an appointed time, reckoning, action, or outcome.

In this root, counting and preparation are not separate. What is prepared for a goal is usually counted, measured, and arranged. What is counted enters the realm of order, responsibility, and reckoning.

Material Image

The material image is someone counting things one by one before an undertaking, separating them, arranging them, and preparing them for use. A traveler considers water, food, mount, path, and timing. Someone preparing for defense measures strength, tools, position, and capacity.

In this image, number is not merely a figure. It is a way of ordering things. What is counted becomes known. What is prepared takes its place. From here, ʿ D D is close both to counting and to readiness.

ʿAdad: Count and Measurability

One clear use of this root is number and count: something leaves vagueness and its measure becomes known. In 10:5, the moon and its stations are set so that people may know عَدَدَ السِّنِينَ وَالْحِسَابَ (ʿadada al-sinīna wa-al-ḥisāb).

In 17:12, night and day are also connected with knowing the count of years and reckoning. The Qur’an places count within the order of creation, time, and practical human life.

Number and Divine Encompassing

The Qur’an also uses number to express the encompassing knowledge of The God. In 72:28, it says: وَأَحْصَىٰ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ عَدَدًا (wa-aḥṣā kulla shayʾin ʿadadā).

This is not mere counting. Nothing falls outside divine measure, number, and preservation. Iḥṣāʾ presents counting together with encompassing, recording, and keeping the truth of each thing from being lost or forgotten.

ʿIddah: Appointed Duration and Readiness for the Next Stage

One important form of this root is ʿiddah. It can point to an appointed period: a duration whose count is known and upon which practical rulings depend.

In 2:184, about fasting, the Qur’an says: فَعِدَّةٌ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ (fa-ʿiddatun min ayyāmin ukhar). Here ʿiddah means a fixed count of other days to make up what was missed. It is not only “number,” but an appointed compensating period.

In 65:1, ʿiddah appears with iḥṣāʾ: فَطَلِّقُوهُنَّ لِعِدَّتِهِنَّ وَأَحْصُوا الْعِدَّةَ (fa-ṭalliqūhunna li-ʿiddatihinna wa-aḥṣū al-ʿiddah). The period must be kept carefully. It is not merely a calendar count; it is an ordered interval in which rights, home, possible return, clarity of status, and readiness for the next stage are preserved.

ʿIddat al-Shuhūr: The Count of Months

In 9:36, the Qur’an says: إِنَّ عِدَّةَ الشُّهُورِ عِندَ اللَّهِ اثْنَا عَشَرَ شَهْرًا (inna ʿiddata al-shuhūri ʿinda Allāhi ithnā ʿashara shahrā). Here ʿiddah concerns the ordered count of months.

This count is not a matter of human preference; it belongs to the order of creation and the Book of The God. Again, ʿ D D is tied to order, measure, and structure.

Aʿiddū: Purposeful Preparation

The second major field of this root is preparation. In 8:60, the Qur’an says: وَأَعِدُّوا لَهُم مَّا اسْتَطَعْتُم مِّن قُوَّةٍ (wa-aʿiddū lahum mā isṭaʿtum min quwwah).

Aʿiddū means prepare, provide, and arrange. Preparation here is not merely possessing tools; it involves measuring capacity, arranging resources, and directing them toward deterrence. The verse is in the context of threat and war, but the root meaning of aʿiddū is not limited to war. Form IV here conveys purposeful preparation.

Aʿtadnā and Uʿiddat: Prepared Outcome

The form أَعْتَدْنَا (aʿtadnā) often appears in the Qur’an for something The God has prepared for a group, whether punishment for wrongdoers and coverers of truth, or reward for believers. In 18:29, it says: إِنَّا أَعْتَدْنَا لِلظَّالِمِينَ نَارًا (innā aʿtadnā lil-ẓālimīna nārā). In 76:4, it says: إِنَّا أَعْتَدْنَا لِلْكَافِرِينَ سَلَاسِلَ وَأَغْلَالًا وَسَعِيرًا (innā aʿtadnā lil-kāfirīna salāsila wa-aghlālan wa-saʿīrā).

In this use, divine preparation means that the outcome is ready for its people. What a human being moves toward does not remain without consequence in the order of truth.

The Qur’an also uses the passive form أُعِدَّتْ (uʿiddat). In 3:131, the Fire is prepared for the coverers of truth; in 3:133, the Garden is prepared for the muttaqīn. This preparedness is not arbitrary; each is connected to its people.

Naʿuddu Lahum ʿAddā: Precise Counting of Opportunity

In 19:84, the Qur’an says: فَلَا تَعْجَلْ عَلَيْهِمْ إِنَّمَا نَعُدُّ لَهُمْ عَدًّا (fa-lā taʿjal ʿalayhim innamā naʿuddu lahum ʿaddā).

Their respite is not left unreckoned. This counting is not emotionally neutral; it suggests an opportunity moving toward its end. Translation should not insert a specific object such as “breaths” or “days” as if it were in the Arabic wording. Explanation may clarify that the verse creates an image of precise counting of a remaining opportunity.

ʿAddadahu: Counting Wealth and Imagined Security

Surah Al-Humazah says: الَّذِي جَمَعَ مَالًا وَعَدَّدَهُ (alladhī jamaʿa mālan wa-ʿaddadah) in 104:2.

ʿAddadahu is not simple counting. A person gathers wealth and keeps counting it, as though repeated counting and storage could secure his future. The next verse exposes the illusion: يَحْسَبُ أَنَّ مَالَهُ أَخْلَدَهُ (yaḥsabu anna mālahu akhladah). Counting, when severed from truth, can become an illusion of control and permanence.

Number Against Vagueness

Another important layer of ʿ D D is bringing matters out of vagueness. When days, months, years, or things become numbered, they are no longer boundaryless or unreckoned. Number gives order to the world, and that order makes responsibility, worship, contract, waiting, and reckoning possible.

Distinguishing Qur’anic Uses

The Qur’anic uses of ʿ D D should be kept distinct. In 10:5 and 17:12, ʿadad concerns the count of years and the reckoning of time. In 2:184 and 65:1, ʿiddah concerns an appointed and countable period. In 9:36, ʿiddah concerns the count of months in the divine order.

In 8:60, aʿiddū concerns practical and purposeful preparation. In 18:29 and 76:4, aʿtadnā concerns prepared outcome. In 19:84, naʿuddu lahum ʿaddā concerns precise counting and the approach of an appointed end. In 104:2, ʿaddadahu concerns counting wealth and relying on stored material security.

If every use is translated as “count,” the preparation in aʿiddū and aʿtadnā disappears. If every use is translated as “prepare,” the counting and reckoning in ʿadada al-sinīn or naʿuddu lahum ʿaddā disappears.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In لِتَعْلَمُوا عَدَدَ السِّنِينَ وَالْحِسَابَ (litaʿlamū ʿadada al-sinīna wa-al-ḥisāb), number is the object of knowing: the heavenly order enables knowledge of the count of years and reckoning. In فَعِدَّةٌ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ (fa-ʿiddatun min ayyāmin ukhar), ʿiddah points to a compensating count of other days.

In أَحْصُوا الْعِدَّةَ (aḥṣū al-ʿiddah), iḥṣāʾ stands beside ʿiddah. Iḥṣāʾ is more precise than simple counting; the period must be kept carefully and not neglected.

In وَأَعِدُّوا لَهُم (wa-aʿiddū lahum), the imperative is from Form IV and means preparing and providing. Lahum gives the direction of preparation, and مَّا اسْتَطَعْتُم (mā isṭaʿtum) ties the measure of preparation to real human capacity.

In نَعُدُّ لَهُمْ عَدًّا (naʿuddu lahum ʿaddā), the verbal noun after the verb intensifies the precision and completeness of the counting. In جَمَعَ مَالًا وَعَدَّدَهُ (jamaʿa mālan wa-ʿaddadah), gathering and counting stand together: first the wealth is collected, then repeatedly counted and held in the mind.

Difference From Nearby Words

ʿ D D is close to ḥisāb, but they are not identical. ʿAdad points to number and amount, while ḥisāb concerns reckoning, calculation, assessment, and conclusion based on count. Number is the raw material of reckoning, but reckoning goes beyond number.

ʿ D D is also close to aḥṣā. ʿAdd focuses on counting and separating units, while iḥṣāʾ is precise counting with encompassing, recording, and preventing loss. In the Qur’an, The God does not merely count; He encompasses in complete knowledge.

ʿ D D differs from qadar, hayyaʾa, jahhaza, amad, and jamaʿa. Qadar concerns measuring and determining limits. Hayyaʾa is more about preparing a suitable state. Jahhaza is closer to equipping outward supplies. Amad concerns duration or the end of a span. Jamaʿa means gathering, while ʿadad means count and ordered quantity.

Forqan Translation Method

Forqan should not translate ʿ D D always as “number” or “count.” In some verses, “count” is best, as in ʿadada al-sinīn. In other verses, “appointed period” or “a count of days” is better, as in ʿiddatun min ayyāmin ukhar. In verses of preparation, “prepare,” “provide,” or “make ready” is necessary, as in aʿiddū and aʿtadnā.

ʿIddah and ʿadad should also be distinguished. ʿAdad is more directly the number itself, while ʿiddah can be a fixed count, an appointed period, or something prepared. Depending on context, “count,” “period,” “readiness,” or “what has been prepared” may be the better rendering.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

  • ʿadada al-sinīna wa-al-ḥisāb: the count of years and reckoning; or the count of years and the measuring of time.
  • fa-ʿiddatun min ayyāmin ukhar: then a count of other days; or an appointed period of other days, to the same count.
  • aḥṣū al-ʿiddah: count the ʿiddah carefully; or keep the appointed period accurately.
  • inna ʿiddata al-shuhūr: indeed, the count of the months.
  • wa-aʿiddū lahum: prepare for them; or make ready for facing them.
  • mā isṭaʿtum min quwwah: whatever you can of strength; or whatever measure of strength you are able to provide.
  • innā aʿtadnā lil-ẓālimīn: indeed, We have prepared for the wrongdoers.
  • uʿiddat lil-muttaqīn: prepared for the muttaqīn; or made ready for the people of taqwa.
  • innamā naʿuddu lahum ʿaddā: We are counting for them with precise counting; or We are counting their opportunity toward its end.
  • aḥṣā kulla shayʾin ʿadadā: He has encompassed every thing in count; or He has brought every thing, in count, under encompassing record.
  • jamaʿa mālan wa-ʿaddadah: he gathered wealth and counted it repeatedly.

Summary

The root ʿ D D points to count, measure, order, and preparation. At the material level, it evokes someone counting, arranging, and making things ready for a purpose. From this, two major fields emerge: ʿadad as number and amount, and iʿdād as preparing and making ready.

In the Qur’an, this root appears for the count of years, reckoning of time, days of fasting, divorce ʿiddah, the count of months, preparation of strength, prepared punishment or reward, The God’s encompassing of every thing in count, and the repeated counting of wealth out of attachment.

In short: ʿ D D in the Qur’an points to count, measure, appointed duration, and ordered preparation for a defined purpose; something leaves vagueness and disorder, becomes counted and arranged, and is prepared for an appointed time, reckoning, action, or outcome.
