1. Introducing the root

The triliteral Arabic root ج ن ب concerns the flank, side, proximity, and keeping a distance. In bodily usage, جَنْب means a human or animal flank: one of the body’s two sides. This tangible meaning is related to broader uses: being beside something, accompanying another person, moving away from a position, and setting something aside from one’s course of life or conduct.

At first, “being beside” and “being distant” may seem opposed. In this family, however, both describe a relationship between things.

Someone beside a person is spatially close to them, yet separate from those on the other side. Similarly, when people distance themselves from something, they set it aside from their direct course and place themselves on another side.

This semantic field connects verbs such as جَنَّبَ, to keep away; تَجَنَّبَ, to avoid; and اجْتَنَبَ, to stand aside or refrain.

These forms nevertheless do not all have the same meaning.

جَنْب can name a bodily flank. جَانِب indicates a side or direction. اجْتَنَبَ describes deliberately keeping away. In some uses جُنُب describes a distant person or stranger; in another it identifies the state of janābah.

A shared root therefore does not make every جَنْب a sin, every instance of avoidance a matter of impurity, or every جُنُب a statement of geographical distance.

The Qur’an uses this root 33 times in 31 verses, in seven morphological categories.

Quranic Arabic Corpus:33 occurrences in seven categories

Its uses are varied: human and animal flanks; locations such as the side of a mountain; neighbors and close companions; avoidance of wrongful conduct; the state of janābah; and the figurative expression فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ.

This diversity prevents reducing ج ن ب to a religious term such as janābah. Even ijtināb, important in Qur’anic moral and religious commands, is only one branch of this family.

Methodologically, bodily and spatial senses should first be understood; morphology and context then determine whether a particular use concerns a flank, proximity, distance, avoidance, or another state.

2. Semantic cores in early Arabic

“Side” and “distance”

Ibn Fāris writes in Maqāyīs al-Lughah:

الْجِيمُ وَالنُّونُ وَالْبَاءُ أَصْلَانِ مُتَقَارِبَانِ: أَحَدُهُمَا النَّاحِيَةُ، وَالْآخَرُ الْبُعْدُ.

al-jīmu wa-al-nūnu wa-al-bāʾu aṣlāni mutaqāribāni: aḥaduhumā al-nāḥiyatu wa-al-ākharu al-buʿdu

Translation: “Jīm, nūn, and bā have two closely related principles: one is a side, the other distance.”

Maqāyīs:side and distance

This definition identifies the root’s two principal branches. Brief root meaning: Flank, side, and lateral position; distancing, keeping away, and avoidance. In purification contexts, junub names a state requiring purification.

نَاحِيَة indicates a side, edge, or part of a place or body.

بُعْد concerns being distant or moving away.

Ibn Fāris considers these related, without treating them as entirely identical.

That distinction matters: something beside a person may be spatially very close.

Conversely, a person or thing may be distant without occupying a particular bodily side.

Flank and distance are thus related semantic fields, not interchangeable words in every use.

جَنْب: the bodily flank

In Arabic, جَنْب names the flank of a person or animal.

The flank lies beside the chest, abdomen, and back, forming one of the body’s two sides.

جَنْب can therefore describe a person lying down or standing.

In a sleeping context, a person may lie on their side.

With livestock, the animal’s flank may be described when it falls to the ground or in other positions.

Its familiar plural is جُنُوب.

The Qur’an uses this plural for human and animal flanks.

The separately vocalized جَنُوب also names the south wind and the southern direction. It should be distinguished from جُنُوب, the plural of جَنْب in bodily passages.

All the Qur’anic plural forms جُنُوب examined here concern bodily flanks, not geographical south.

جَانِب: side and direction

جَانِب belongs to the same family and indicates a side, direction, or particular part of a place.

For example, someone may be on the right side of a road or the western side of an area.

A bodily flank is not necessarily intended.

Jānib can describe a mountain, land, sea, or any environment in which a direction or side can be identified.

In the Qur’an, جَانِبِ الطُّورِ concerns the side of Mount Ṭūr.

جَانِبَ الْبَرِّ likewise concerns a part or side of the land.

The relationship between a bodily side and the side of a place is therefore familiar in Arabic; this semantic relationship alone does not establish a definite historical sequence of derivation.

جُنُب: a distant person or stranger

Lisān al-ʿArab records:

وَرَجُلٌ جَانِبٌ وَجُنُبٌ: غَرِيبٌ.

wa-rajulun jānibun wa-junubun: gharībun

Translation: “A man described as jānib or junub: a stranger.”

Lisān:jānib and junub as stranger

Here the distance is social or familial.

Someone may belong to another tribe or family while living among people to whom they are not related by descent.

Such a person has been called جُنُب or جَانِب.

This matters in Al-Nisāʾ 4:36, where the Qur’an mentions الْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ.

A neighbor may be spatially close yet distant in kinship or social affiliation.

“Distant” in this expression does not necessarily mean the neighbor’s home is far away.

This distinction is fundamental to studying the root.

3. Important forms and derivatives

جَنْب and جُنُوب

جَنْب names a flank or side.

Its plural is جُنُوب.

The Qur’an has eight occurrences of this noun, in forms including جُنُوبِهِمْ, جُنُوبِكُمْ, جُنُوبُهَا, and جَنْبِ.

Most concern a human or animal’s physical flank.

In Al-Zumar 39:56, جَنْبِ اللَّهِ occurs in a figurative expression examined separately below.

جَانِب

Arabic جَانِب indicates the side or edge of something.

This form occurs nine times in the Qur’an.

Its uses are mostly spatial: the side of Ṭūr, the western side, and the side of the land.

Two verses also contain نَأَىٰ بِجَانِبِهِ, which, in the context of human withdrawal, means more than a geographical direction.

جَنَبَ and جَنَّبَ

In some uses جَنَبَ concerns setting something aside or keeping it away.

Form II جَنَّبَ can mean keeping someone or something away from a particular condition or danger.

Al-Layl 92:17 contains the passive يُجَنَّبُهَا.

It concerns being kept away from the Fire.

أَجْنَبَ

أَجْنَبَ is Form IV.

One use concerns keeping someone or something away.

Another relates to janābah.

أَجْنَبَ can also describe turning southward or entering the south wind.

These uses must be distinguished.

The received Qur’anic reading used on this site has no Form IV أَجْنَبَ. A variant reading أَجْنِبْنِي is nevertheless recorded for Ibrāhīm 14:35 in lexical sources.

Lisān:the recorded ajnibnī reading

تَجَنَّبَ

Form V تَجَنَّبَ concerns standing aside, moving away, or avoiding.

In Al-Aʿlā 87:11, يَتَجَنَّبُهَا describes someone avoiding the reminder.

It shows that keeping away can be deliberate.

The verb itself does not determine whether the thing avoided is good or bad.

People may move away from a real danger; elsewhere they may turn away from a reminder of truth.

The object and context determine the conduct’s value.

اجْتَنَبَ

اجْتَنَبَ is Form VIII.

It occurs nine times in the Qur’an.

Qur’anic uses concern avoiding sin, ṭāghūt, false speech, and other reprehensible conduct.

Ijtināb here describes choosing to keep one’s distance from something.

The Form VIII pattern alone does not establish an identical religious ruling for every instance, irrespective of its object.

جَانَبَ and مُجَانَبَة

Arabic جَانَبَ can concern distancing oneself and taking another side.

مُجَانَبَة means avoidance or keeping a distance.

Related مُجَانِب can describe someone who distances themselves from another.

These forms do not occur in the Qur’an.

جَنَاب and جَنَابَة

جَنَاب names a vicinity or side.

In some early uses جَنَابَة concerns distance and being a stranger.

In purification terminology, janābah also names a familiar state accompanied in the Qur’an by a command to wash or purify oneself.

Lexicographers connect the two uses through the idea of distance; that explanation does not make the uses identical or establish their complete historical development.

جُنُب in the state of janābah

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:43 and Al-Māʾidah 5:6, جُنُبًا describes a person who needs purification for ṣalāh.

Here it does not merely mean a stranger or someone far from home.

جُنُب identifies a state relating to purification.

Without evidence, that designation must not be turned into “sinful person,” “morally contaminated person,” or someone lacking human dignity.

جَنِيب and جَنَائِب

Arabic جَنِيب can name an animal led alongside a person or another animal.

جَنَائِب can be the plural of animals led alongside the principal group.

This use directly concerns the semantic field of being beside something.

The issue is neither moral avoidance nor janābah, but the animal’s position relative to its handler.

الْجَنُوب: south and the south wind

جَنُوب also names the southern direction and the south wind.

Early sources describe this wind’s climatic and behavioral characteristics.

Its name alone does not imply that every south wind is warm, moist, or rain-bearing everywhere.

The wind’s properties depend on geography and actual conditions.

This form does not occur in the Qur’an as the name of geographical south.

جَنُوب, geographical south, should also be distinguished from جُنُوب, the plural “flanks.”

Lisān:janūb the wind and junūb the flanks

4. Early Arabic witnesses and pre-Islamic poetry

جَنَابَة as distance: ʿAlqamah ibn ʿAbadah

ʿAlqamah ibn ʿAbadah, known as ʿAlqamah al-Faḥl, is a recognized pre-Islamic poet.

Addressing Al-Ḥārith ibn Jabalah while seeking the release of his brother Shaʾs, he says:

وَفِي كُلِّ حَيٍّ قَدْ خَبَطْتَ بِنِعْمَةٍ
فَحَقٌّ لِشَأْسٍ مِنْ نَدَاكَ ذَنُوبُ

wa-fī kulli ḥayyin qad khabaṭta bi-niʿmatin / fa-ḥaqqun li-Shaʾsin min nadāka dhanūbu

Translation: “Your generosity has reached every tribe; Shaʾs too deserves a share of your bounty.”

He then says:

فَلَا تَحْرِمَنِّي نَائِلًا عَنْ جَنَابَةٍ
فَإِنِّي امْرُؤٌ وَسْطَ الْقِبَابِ غَرِيبُ

fa-lā taḥrimannī nāʾilan ʿan janābatin / fa-innī imruʾun wasṭa al-qibābi gharību

Translation: “Do not withhold a favor from me after this distance and estrangement, for I am a stranger among these tents.”

Lisān:ʿAlqamah seeking Shaʾs’s release

جَنَابَة here means distance and being away from one’s people.

The poet presents himself among people to whom he has no close kinship connection.

This agrees with Ibn Fāris’s analysis of distance.

It also shows that janābah in early Arabic was not solely a purification term following sexual intercourse.

The witness does not, however, make جُنُبًا in Al-Nisāʾ 4:43 merely a traveler or stranger: that verse’s context concerns purification and washing.

Forms and uses must remain distinct.

جَنَابَة in Al-Aʿshā’s poetry

Explaining Al-Nisāʾ 4:36, Al-Ṭabarī quotes this line from Al-Aʿshā:

أَتَيْتُ حُرَيْثًا زَائِرًا عَنْ جَنَابَةٍ
فَكَانَ حُرَيْثٌ فِي عَطَائِي جَامِدَا

ataytu Ḥuraythan zāʾiran ʿan janābatin / fa-kāna Ḥuraythun fī ʿaṭāʾī jāmidā

Translation: “I came from afar as a stranger to visit Ḥurayth, but Ḥurayth was unyielding in giving to me.”

Al-Ṭabarī:Al-Aʿshā and distance in kinship

عَنْ جَنَابَةٍ concerns distance and being a stranger.

The poet describes someone arriving from elsewhere who is a stranger relative to his host.

This witness helps explain الْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ in Al-Nisāʾ 4:36.

A neighbor may be spatially close yet a stranger in descent or social affiliation.

Al-Aʿshā is a recognized poet of the late pre-Islamic period. Knowing his lifetime alone does not establish when this particular line was composed.

The witness’s direct value is its attestation of distance and estrangement in early Arabic.

Distance in human relationships

Lisān al-ʿArab also quotes this line:

وَإِنِّي لِمَا قَدْ كَانَ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَهَا
لَمُوفٍ وَإِنْ شَطَّ الْمَزَارُ الْمُجَانِبُ

wa-innī li-mā qad kāna baynī wa-baynahā / la-mūfin wa-in shaṭṭa al-mazāru al-mujānibu

Translation: “I remain faithful to what once lay between us, even though the place of meeting has grown distant.”

الْمُجَانِب here concerns distance and separation.

Spatial distance does not mean the end of fidelity.

The poet says that despite the distance, he preserves the earlier bond or commitment.

This witness shows that distance in Arabic need not imply hostility or lack of affection.

The precise attribution and composition date are not established by the lexical passage examined; it cannot therefore be presented as securely dated pre-Islamic poetry.

Lisān:fidelity despite distance

The witnesses and limits of inference

The ʿAlqamah and Al-Aʿshā witnesses clarify distance and estrangement within ج ن ب.

The third also shows that mujānabah can concern spatial separation in human relationships.

These uses do not exhaust the root’s meaning.

Understanding جَنْب as a flank, جَانِب as a side, and جُنُب in janābah requires evidence relevant to each form.

5. Bodily flanks in the Qur’an

Āl ʿImrān 3:191: remembrance in different positions

الَّذِينَ يَذْكُرُونَ اللَّهَ قِيَامًا وَقُعُودًا وَعَلَىٰ جُنُوبِهِمْ وَيَتَفَكَّرُونَ فِي خَلْقِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ رَبَّنَا مَا خَلَقْتَ هَٰذَا بَاطِلًا سُبْحَانَكَ فَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ

alladhīna yadhkurūna Allāha qiyāman wa-quʿūdan wa-ʿalā junūbihim wa-yatafakkarūna fī khalqi al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍi rabbanā mā khalaqta hādhā bāṭilan subḥānaka fa-qinā ʿadhāba al-nāri

Proposed translation for this study: “Those who remember God standing, sitting, and on their sides, and reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth: Our Lord, You did not create this in vain. Glory be to You; protect us from the punishment of the Fire.”

جُنُوبِهِمْ refers to their flanks.

Three bodily positions appear together: standing, sitting, and being on one’s side.

The sequence shows that remembering God is not confined to one bodily posture.

جُنُوب itself does not mean remembrance or reflection.

Remembering comes from يَذْكُرُونَ; reflecting comes from يَتَفَكَّرُونَ.

جُنُوب expresses only the bodily aspect of part of this picture.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:103: remembrance after ṣalāh

فَإِذَا قَضَيْتُمُ الصَّلَاةَ فَاذْكُرُوا اللَّهَ قِيَامًا وَقُعُودًا وَعَلَىٰ جُنُوبِكُمْ ۚ فَإِذَا اطْمَأْنَنتُمْ فَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ ۚ إِنَّ الصَّلَاةَ كَانَتْ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ كِتَابًا مَّوْقُوتًا

fa-idhā qaḍaytumu al-ṣalāta fa-udhkurū Allāha qiyāman wa-quʿūdan wa-ʿalā junūbikum fa-idhā iṭmaʾnantum fa-aqīmū al-ṣalāta inna al-ṣalāta kānat ʿalā al-muʾminīna kitāban mawqūtan

Proposed translation for this study: “When you have completed ṣalāh, remember God standing, sitting, and on your sides. When you are secure, establish ṣalāh. Ṣalāh is indeed a prescribed obligation for the believers at appointed times.”

جُنُوبِكُمْ again refers to flanks.

The root’s meaning is close to its use in Āl ʿImrān 3:191.

The context differs, however.

Al-Nisāʾ concerns remembering God after ṣalāh and returning to a condition of security.

Similar phrasing does not make all the circumstances of the two verses identical.

The flank here concerns bodily position, not a geographical direction or janābah.

Al-Tawbah 9:35: foreheads, flanks, and backs

Al-Tawbah 9:35 describes gold and silver being heated and used to brand people’s foreheads, flanks, and backs.

جُنُوبُهُمْ refers to bodily flanks.

Its position alongside جِبَاهُهُمْ and ظُهُورُهُمْ makes the physical meaning clear.

The root ج ن ب does not itself mean punishment or branding.

Those actions come from the verbs and other words in the verse.

Yūnus 10:12: calling while lying down

وَإِذَا مَسَّ الْإِنسَانَ الضُّرُّ دَعَانَا لِجَنْبِهِ أَوْ قَاعِدًا أَوْ قَائِمًا فَلَمَّا كَشَفْنَا عَنْهُ ضُرَّهُ مَرَّ كَأَن لَّمْ يَدْعُنَا إِلَىٰ ضُرٍّ مَّسَّهُ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ زُيِّنَ لِلْمُسْرِفِينَ مَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

wa-idhā massa al-insāna al-ḍurru daʿānā li-janbihi aw qāʿidan aw qāʾiman fa-lammā kashafnā ʿanhu ḍurrahu marra ka-an lam yadʿunā ilā ḍurrin massahu kadhālika zuyyina li-al-musrifīna mā kānū yaʿmalūna

Proposed translation for this study: “When harm touches the human being, he calls upon Us on his side, sitting, or standing. When We remove his harm, he goes on as though he had never called upon Us about harm that touched him. Thus what the excessive used to do was made attractive to them.”

لِجَنْبِهِ concerns the person’s position on his side.

The verse does not condemn lying on one’s side.

It concerns someone who calls upon God in difficulty but, after the danger is removed, behaves as though he had never asked for help.

That conclusion comes from context, not from جَنْب itself.

Al-Sajdah 32:16: flanks leaving their beds

تَتَجَافَىٰ جُنُوبُهُمْ عَنِ الْمَضَاجِعِ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُمْ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ

tatajāfā junūbuhum ʿani al-maḍājiʿi yadʿūna rabbahum khawfan wa-ṭamaʿan wa-mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūna

Existing Persian Forqan translation, rendered here in English: “Their sides draw away from their beds; they call upon their Lord in fear and hope, and spend from what We have provided them.”

جُنُوبُهُمْ comes from ج ن ب, whereas تَتَجَافَىٰ comes from ج ف و.

This distinction must be preserved.

جُنُوب names the flanks.

تَتَجَافَىٰ describes their moving away from beds.

Two different roots thus concern the flanks and their movement in one sentence.

ج ن ب should consequently not be reduced to rising or moving away from bed.

Al-Ḥajj 22:36: the flanks of livestock

وَالْبُدْنَ جَعَلْنَاهَا لَكُم مِّن شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ لَكُمْ فِيهَا خَيْرٌ ۖ فَاذْكُرُوا اسْمَ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهَا صَوَافَّ ۖ فَإِذَا وَجَبَتْ جُنُوبُهَا فَكُلُوا مِنْهَا وَأَطْعِمُوا الْقَانِعَ وَالْمُعْتَرَّ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ سَخَّرْنَاهَا لَكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

wa-al-budna jaʿalnāhā lakum min shaʿāʾiri Allāhi lakum fīhā khayrun fa-udhkurū isma Allāhi ʿalayhā ṣawāffa fa-idhā wajabat junūbuhā fa-kulū minhā wa-aṭʿimū al-qāniʿa wa-al-muʿtarra kadhālika sakhkharnāhā lakum laʿallakum tashkurūna

Existing Persian Forqan translation, rendered here in English: “We have made the sacrificial camels among the signs of The God for you; in them is good for you. So mention the name of The God over them as they stand in rows. When their flanks have fallen to the ground, eat from them and feed the contented and the petitioner. Thus We have subjected them to you, so that you may give thanks.”

جُنُوبُهَا refers to the animals’ flanks.

وَجَبَتْ comes from و ج ب and here concerns the flanks falling and coming to rest on the ground.

“Legal obligation” must therefore not replace the sense of falling.

جُنُوب names the animal’s flanks, not an independently named stage of ritual.

Sacrifice, feeding others, and sacred rites require examination of the verse’s complete wording and related passages.

6. Side and position in spatial uses

The side of Ṭūr

In Maryam 19:52, Ṭā Hā 20:80, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:29, and Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:46, جَانِب concerns Mount Ṭūr.

It indicates a side or part of a place.

For example, جَانِبِ الطُّورِ الْأَيْمَنِ concerns Ṭūr’s right side.

جَانِب alone does not establish an exact compass direction.

Right and left also depend on the frame of orientation and what the description modifies.

The word alone cannot produce a definite map of Moses’s position or the historical event’s exact location.

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:44: the western side

وَمَا كُنتَ بِجَانِبِ الْغَرْبِيِّ إِذْ قَضَيْنَا إِلَىٰ مُوسَى الْأَمْرَ وَمَا كُنتَ مِنَ الشَّاهِدِينَ

wa-mā kunta bi-jānibi al-gharbiyyi idh qaḍaynā ilā Mūsā al-amra wa-mā kunta mina al-shāhidīna

Proposed translation for this study: “You were not on the western side when We conveyed the command to Moses, and you were not among the witnesses.”

بِجَانِبِ concerns being on the side of a place.

الْغَرْبِيِّ specifies the direction.

جَانِب alone does not mean west; the accompanying description supplies “western.”

The verse also describes the addressee’s absence from that position when the event occurred.

That construction does not make jānib mean witnessing or historical knowledge.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:68: the side of the land

Al-Isrāʾ 17:68 contains جَانِبَ الْبَرِّ.

جَانِب indicates a part or side of the land.

The word itself does not specify the danger or event mentioned in the verse.

Nor does it establish that the side of the land always means a seashore: the precise meaning depends on context.

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:8: from every side

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:8 contains مِنْ كُلِّ جَانِبٍ.

The expression indicates different sides.

جَانِب has a broad spatial meaning here, unrelated to a bodily organ or janābah.

نَأَىٰ بِجَانِبِهِ: human withdrawal

Al-Isrāʾ 17:83 and Fuṣṣilat 41:51 contain نَأَىٰ بِجَانِبِهِ.

نَأَىٰ comes from ن أ ي and concerns moving away.

بِجَانِبِهِ concerns the person’s side.

In these verses, the human being turns away when enjoying favor but reacts differently when difficulty arrives.

Bodily withdrawal can thus depict withdrawal in conduct.

جَانِب itself does not mean arrogance or ingratitude.

Those meanings come from أَعْرَضَ and the verses’ context.

7. جُنُب: distance, estrangement, and social proximity

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:11: seeing from a distance

وَقَالَتْ لِأُخْتِهِ قُصِّيهِ ۖ فَبَصُرَتْ بِهِ عَن جُنُبٍ وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ

wa-qālat li-ukhtihi quṣṣīhi fa-baṣurat bihi ʿan junubin wa-hum lā yashʿurūna

Existing Persian Forqan translation, rendered here in English: “She said to his sister: Follow him. Then she saw him from a distance, while they were unaware.”

عَن جُنُبٍ concerns observing from a distance.

Moses’s sister follows him without others recognizing her relationship to the child.

جُنُب therefore does not mean janābah here.

Nor does the word establish her exact distance from Moses.

“From a distance” is natural and appropriate in this context.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:36: the distant neighbor and companion at one’s side

وَاعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَلَا تُشْرِكُوا بِهِ شَيْئًا ۖ وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا وَبِذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْيَتَامَىٰ وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَالْجَارِ ذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ وَالصَّاحِبِ بِالْجَنْبِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ وَمَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ مَن كَانَ مُخْتَالًا فَخُورًا

wa-uʿbudū Allāha wa-lā tushrikū bihi shayʾan wa-bi-al-wālidayni iḥsānan wa-bi-dhī al-qurbā wa-al-yatāmā wa-al-masākīni wa-al-jāri dhī al-qurbā wa-al-jāri al-junubi wa-al-ṣāḥibi bi-al-janbi wa-ibni al-sabīli wa-mā malakat aymānukum inna Allāha lā yuḥibbu man kāna mukhtālan fakhūran

Proposed translation for this study: “Worship The God and associate nothing with Him; show kindness to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbor, the distant neighbor, the companion at your side, the stranded traveler, and those whom your right hands possess. The God does not love anyone conceited and boastful.”

ج ن ب occurs twice in this verse.

First in الْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ.

Second in وَالصَّاحِبِ بِالْجَنْبِ.

Despite sharing a root, these expressions differ in meaning and social role.

الْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ: a neighbor outside close kinship

الْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ appears in contrast to الْجَارِ ذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ.

Lexical sources and early commentaries explain جُنُب here as a neighbor without close kinship, or belonging to another family or group.

This reading relates to Arabic جُنُب meaning a stranger or outsider.

Two houses may therefore stand next to each other while their residents are not close in kinship or social affiliation.

The neighbor is spatially close but junub in descent.

“Distant neighbor” can express the distinction, but in Persian may suggest only physical separation between houses.

The root explanation should therefore clarify that distance can be familial or social, rather than necessarily geographical.

The command to show kindness still applies.

Different kinship or social affiliation does not remove a neighbor’s rights.

Does الْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ mean only a non-Muslim neighbor?

Some early explanations also apply the expression to a Jewish or Christian neighbor.

جُنُب itself does not mean non-Muslim.

It can describe someone from another family, tribe, or group.

Restricting it to one religious category without verbal evidence narrows its meaning.

A neighbor with a different belief can nevertheless be one instance.

الصَّاحِبِ بِالْجَنْبِ: the close companion

This expression concerns someone beside a person who accompanies them.

Early explanations identify different instances: a traveling companion, spouse, close associate, or someone accompanying a person through work or social relations.

These differences indicate the expression’s broader lexical capacity.

Al-Ṭabarī:distant neighbor and companion at one’s side

الصَّاحِب concerns companionship.

بِالْجَنْبِ emphasizes proximity or being beside someone.

Limiting it to a spouse alone or a traveling companion alone, without independent evidence, does not capture its full verbal range.

The proposed Persian phrase “companion at one’s side” is suitable: it preserves companionship and proximity without arbitrarily specifying one instance.

8. جُنُب and the state of janābah

Al-Nisāʾ 4:43

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَقْرَبُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَأَنتُمْ سُكَارَىٰ حَتَّىٰ تَعْلَمُوا مَا تَقُولُونَ وَلَا جُنُبًا إِلَّا عَابِرِي سَبِيلٍ حَتَّىٰ تَغْتَسِلُوا ۚ وَإِن كُنتُم مَّرْضَىٰ أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ أَوْ جَاءَ أَحَدٌ مِّنكُم مِّنَ الْغَائِطِ أَوْ لَامَسْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَلَمْ تَجِدُوا مَاءً فَتَيَمَّمُوا صَعِيدًا طَيِّبًا فَامْسَحُوا بِوُجُوهِكُمْ وَأَيْدِيكُمْ ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَفُوًّا غَفُورًا

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā taqrabū al-ṣalāta wa-antum sukārā ḥattā taʿlamū mā taqūlūna wa-lā junuban illā ʿābirī sabīlin ḥattā taghtasilū wa-in kuntum marḍā aw ʿalā safarin aw jāʾa aḥadun minkum mina al-ghāʾiṭi aw lāmastumu al-nisāʾa fa-lam tajidū māʾan fa-tayammamū ṣaʿīdan ṭayyiban fa-imsaḥū bi-wujūhikum wa-aydīkum inna Allāha kāna ʿafuwwan ghafūran

Existing Persian Forqan translation, rendered here in English: “Believers, do not approach ṣalāh while intoxicated until you know what you are saying; nor in janābah, except when passing along a way, until you wash yourselves. If you are ill or traveling, or one of you comes from relieving himself, or you have touched women and find no water, turn to clean earth and wipe your faces and hands. The God is indeed greatly pardoning and forgiving.”

جُنُبًا indicates a state relating to purification.

Religious terminology calls it janābah.

The family’s lexical meaning must nevertheless be distinguished from the name of this state.

Early Arabic جُنُب can mean distant or foreign. In purification usage it names a state in which a person refrains from certain acts of worship until washing.

Lexicographers explain the designation through being aside or kept away from certain acts of worship.

That derivational explanation does not itself establish a person’s moral impurity.

Janābah and moral contamination

جُنُب in Al-Nisāʾ 4:43 identifies a person’s state.

The text commands washing or bathing.

It does not say the person in janābah is morally sinful or of lesser human worth.

Nor does the state imply that everything such a person touches necessarily becomes physically contaminated.

These are separate matters, and each ruling requires evidence.

إِلَّا عَابِرِي سَبِيلٍ

Commentaries differ over “passing along a way.”

One reading concerns passing through a place of ṣalāh or a mosque.

Another concerns a traveler.

The two readings differ in their referent.

In both, جُنُبًا still concerns janābah.

Al-Ṭabarī:two readings of ʿābirī sabīl

The disagreement over عَابِرِي سَبِيلٍ must not be turned into an invented disagreement over the root ج ن ب itself.

Al-Māʾidah 5:6

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا قُمْتُمْ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ فَاغْسِلُوا وُجُوهَكُمْ وَأَيْدِيَكُمْ إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ وَامْسَحُوا بِرُءُوسِكُمْ وَأَرْجُلَكُمْ إِلَى الْكَعْبَيْنِ ۚ وَإِن كُنتُمْ جُنُبًا فَاطَّهَّرُوا ۚ وَإِن كُنتُم مَّرْضَىٰ أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ أَوْ جَاءَ أَحَدٌ مِّنكُم مِّنَ الْغَائِطِ أَوْ لَامَسْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَلَمْ تَجِدُوا مَاءً فَتَيَمَّمُوا صَعِيدًا طَيِّبًا فَامْسَحُوا بِوُجُوهِكُمْ وَأَيْدِيكُمْ مِّنْهُ ۚ مَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيَجْعَلَ عَلَيْكُم مِّنْ حَرَجٍ وَلَٰكِن يُرِيدُ لِيُطَهِّرَكُمْ وَلِيُتِمَّ نِعْمَتَهُ عَلَيْكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū idhā qumtum ilā al-ṣalāti fa-ighsilū wujūhakum wa-aydiyakum ilā al-marāfiqi wa-imsaḥū bi-ruʾūsikum wa-arjulakum ilā al-kaʿbayni wa-in kuntum junuban fa-iṭṭahharū wa-in kuntum marḍā aw ʿalā safarin aw jāʾa aḥadun minkum mina al-ghāʾiṭi aw lāmastumu al-nisāʾa fa-lam tajidū māʾan fa-tayammamū ṣaʿīdan ṭayyiban fa-imsaḥū bi-wujūhikum wa-aydīkum minhu mā yurīdu Allāhu li-yajʿala ʿalaykum min ḥarajin wa-lākin yurīdu li-yuṭahhirakum wa-li-yutimma niʿmatahu ʿalaykum laʿallakum tashkurūna

Existing Persian Forqan translation, rendered here in English: “Believers, when you rise for ṣalāh, wash your faces and hands up to the elbows, and wipe your heads and feet up to the two ankles. If you are in janābah, purify yourselves. If you are ill or traveling, or one of you comes from relieving himself, or you have touched women and find no water, turn to clean earth and wipe your faces and hands with it. The God does not wish to impose hardship upon you; He wishes to purify you and complete His favor upon you, so that you may give thanks.”

جُنُبًا again concerns janābah.

فَاطَّهَّرُوا expresses the command to become pure.

The continuation gives instructions for tayammum in the circumstances of illness, travel, and lack of water.

The Qur’an thus distinguishes a person’s state from the means of purification.

جُنُب names the state.

The other verbs and expressions establish how purification occurs.

Does janābah mean sexual sin?

No. The designation itself does not imply that.

In purification usage, janābah relates to sexual matters and rules of washing.

Lawful sexual relations can also produce the state called janābah in religious language.

Junub must therefore not be defined as adulterer or sexual sinner.

The causes of janābah, a person’s duties, and washing conditions must be examined using the relevant verses and evidence.

An independent study of ṣalāh and purification is the more appropriate place for that discussion.

9. Ijtināb: deliberate avoidance of an act or condition

The basic meaning of ijtināb

اجْتَنَبَ indicates standing aside and keeping away.

In this construction a person seeks to stay away from something or not enter it.

The distance may be physical, as when moving away from danger.

Qur’anic uses of ijtināb, however, are mostly moral and behavioral.

Avoiding ṭāghūt, for example, does not mean taking a few steps away from a physical object.

People can avoid following, obeying, or serving something while remaining in the same social environment.

The object of avoidance must therefore determine its meaning in each verse.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:31: avoiding major sins

إِن تَجْتَنِبُوا كَبَائِرَ مَا تُنْهَوْنَ عَنْهُ نُكَفِّرْ عَنكُمْ سَيِّئَاتِكُمْ وَنُدْخِلْكُم مُّدْخَلًا كَرِيمًا

in tajtanibū kabāʾira mā tunhawna ʿanhu nukaffir ʿankum sayyiʾātikum wa-nudkhilkum mudkhalan karīman

Proposed translation for this study: “If you avoid the major sins among what you are forbidden, We will remove your misdeeds from you and admit you to an honorable place.”

تَجْتَنِبُوا concerns avoiding major sins.

Its object is conduct that the Qur’an forbids.

Avoidance can thus mean refraining from an act, rather than merely moving physically away from its location.

The root itself does not define a major sin.

That requires كَبَائِرَ and the related network of verses about sin.

Al-Naḥl 16:36: avoiding ṭāghūt

Al-Naḥl 16:36 contains the command وَاجْتَنِبُوا الطَّاغُوتَ.

It appears alongside the command to serve God.

Avoiding ṭāghūt here concerns distancing oneself from its service or following.

The word alone does not require physical avoidance of every person or group holding an incorrect belief.

The central concern is the relationship of worship and allegiance.

In Al-Zumar 39:17, avoiding ṭāghūt is likewise accompanied by not worshiping it and returning to God.

This association clarifies the behavioral meaning of ijtināb.

Al-Māʾidah 5:90: avoiding defilement

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنَّمَا الْخَمْرُ وَالْمَيْسِرُ وَالْأَنصَابُ وَالْأَزْلَامُ رِجْسٌ مِّنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَانِ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū innamā al-khamru wa-al-maysiru wa-al-anṣābu wa-al-azlāmu rijsun min ʿamali al-shayṭāni fa-ijtanibūhu laʿallakum tufliḥūna

Proposed translation for this study: “Believers, khamr, maysir, anṣāb, and azlām are defilement from Satan’s work. Avoid it, so that you may prosper.”

فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ commands avoidance.

The singular pronoun هُ follows a set of things described as رِجْسٌ.

Grammatical analysis can examine its reference to rijs or to the named set treated collectively as reprehensible.

Lexically, ijtināb concerns keeping away.

In this context the distance is practical abstention, not merely physical separation from an object.

The root ج ن ب itself does not determine the precise referents of khamr, maysir, anṣāb, and azlām.

Those concepts require their own studies.

Nor should the meaning of avoidance alone yield additional rulings about every form of association, observation, or contact with related objects.

The command’s object and other Qur’anic evidence establish its scope.

Al-Ḥajj 22:30: two commands of avoidance

This root appears twice in Al-Ḥajj 22:30:

فَاجْتَنِبُوا الرِّجْسَ مِنَ الْأَوْثَانِ.

وَاجْتَنِبُوا قَوْلَ الزُّورِ.

The first commands avoidance of the defilement associated with idols.

The second commands avoidance of false and wrongful speech.

The subjects differ, but the Qur’an uses the verb of avoidance for both.

This demonstrates that ijtināb is not restricted to physical distance.

False speech is not a material object avoided simply by moving away.

Here avoidance concerns refraining from uttering or participating in false speech.

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:12: avoiding much suspicion

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اجْتَنِبُوا كَثِيرًا مِّنَ الظَّنِّ إِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ إِثْمٌ ۖ وَلَا تَجَسَّسُوا وَلَا يَغْتَب بَّعْضُكُم بَعْضًا ۚ أَيُحِبُّ أَحَدُكُمْ أَن يَأْكُلَ لَحْمَ أَخِيهِ مَيْتًا فَكَرِهْتُمُوهُ ۚ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَوَّابٌ رَّحِيمٌ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū ijtanibū kathīran mina al-ẓanni inna baʿḍa al-ẓanni ithmun wa-lā tajassasū wa-lā yaghtab baʿḍukum baʿḍan a-yuḥibbu aḥadukum an yaʾkula laḥma akhīhi maytan fa-karihtumūhu wa-ittaqū Allāha inna Allāha tawwābun raḥīmun

Existing Persian Forqan translation, rendered here in English: “Believers, avoid much suspicion; some suspicion is sin. Do not spy, and do not backbite one another. Would any of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother? You would detest it. Be mindful of The God; The God is indeed accepting of repentance and possessed of special mercy.”

اجْتَنِبُوا concerns much suspicion.

It is immediately followed by إِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ إِثْمٌ.

The Qur’an thus distinguishes all conjectures from those that are sinful.

Avoidance must be understood through the object and qualifications of the particular verse.

The continuation also says وَلَا تَجَسَّسُوا.

Tajassus comes from ج س س, not ج ن ب.

Two distinct behaviors are involved: avoiding much suspicion, and refraining from spying or covert investigation of others’ affairs.

The two roots must not be merged because they occur in one verse.

Avoiding indecencies and major sins

Al-Shūrā 42:37 and Al-Najm 53:32 describe people who avoid major sins and indecencies.

In both, يَجْتَنِبُونَ describes abstaining conduct.

فواحش and كبائر الإثم each have their own semantic field.

The verb of avoidance alone does not mean all sins have the same degree or every reprehensible act the same rulings.

10. Keeping away and being kept away: other Qur’anic forms

Ibrāhīm 14:35: asking to be kept from serving idols

وَإِذْ قَالَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ رَبِّ اجْعَلْ هَٰذَا الْبَلَدَ آمِنًا وَاجْنُبْنِي وَبَنِيَّ أَن نَّعْبُدَ الْأَصْنَامَ

wa-idh qāla Ibrāhīmu rabbi ijʿal hādhā al-balada āminan wa-ujnubnī wa-baniyya an naʿbuda al-aṣnāma

Proposed translation for this study: “When Abraham said: My Lord, make this land secure, and keep me and my children away from serving idols.”

وَاجْنُبْنِي is an imperative used in prayer.

Abraham asks his Lord to keep him and his children from serving idols.

The distance concerns worship and conduct.

It is not a matter of taking a few steps away from a stone or wooden object.

The verb alone does not guarantee that all Abraham’s descendants in every generation will necessarily remain immune to idolatry.

The verse reports a request and prayer; historical outcomes or individual destinies require other evidence.

Al-Aʿlā 87:11: avoiding the reminder

وَيَتَجَنَّبُهَا الْأَشْقَى

wa-yatajannabuhā al-ashqā

Proposed translation for this study: “And the most wretched will avoid it.”

يَتَجَنَّبُهَا concerns avoiding the reminder.

The preceding verses establish the reference of هَا.

Here avoiding something positive and guiding is blameworthy.

Not every instance of tajannub or ijtināb in the Qur’an is therefore a virtue.

The value of avoidance depends on what is avoided and the setting of the act.

Al-Layl 92:17: being kept away from the Fire

وَسَيُجَنَّبُهَا الْأَتْقَى

wa-sa-yujannabuhā al-atqā

Proposed translation for this study: “And the most mindful will be kept away from it.”

يُجَنَّبُهَا is a passive Form II verb.

هَا refers to the fire mentioned in the preceding verses.

Here the person is kept away from the Fire.

This differs from Al-Aʿlā 87:11.

In Al-Aʿlā, the person avoids the reminder.

In Al-Layl, the person is kept away from the Fire.

One verb concerns the person’s own conduct; the other concerns being distanced from something.

Translation must preserve that distinction.

11. فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ: a figurative expression in Al-Zumar 39:56

أَن تَقُولَ نَفْسٌ يَا حَسْرَتَىٰ عَلَىٰ مَا فَرَّطتُ فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ وَإِن كُنتُ لَمِنَ السَّاخِرِينَ

an taqūla nafsun yā ḥasratā ʿalā mā farraṭtu fī janbi Allāhi wa-in kuntu la-mina al-sākhirīna

Proposed translation for this study: “Lest someone say: Alas for what I neglected concerning The God; I was indeed among those who mocked.”

This is one of the Qur’an’s most important figurative uses of جَنْب.

In ordinary usage جَنْب can mean a bodily flank.

In فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ, however, the sentence cannot be assigned a bodily meaning without examining context.

فَرَّطتُ فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ

فَرَّطتُ comes from ف ر ط and here concerns neglect and failure in responsibility.

فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ identifies the object of that neglect.

Early lexical and exegetical explanations offer related expressions: neglecting one’s duty to God, obedience to Him, His way, or what brings a person closer to His approval.

Al-Ṭabarī:neglect of God’s command and obedience

These differ in detail but share a concern with human responsibility in relation to God.

“I neglected my duty to The God” is therefore natural and consistent with context.

Does جَنْبِ اللَّهِ mean God’s bodily flank?

The expression itself does not establish that conclusion.

In Arabic, names for bodily parts or sides can figuratively express proximity, rights, standing, or relationship to someone.

For example, a person may be said to stand on the side of right. Such expressions need not denote a fixed physical location.

Al-Zumar 39:56 likewise concerns regret over neglecting one’s duty to God.

Surrounding verses concern returning to God, following revelation, and regretting denial and arrogance.

Context thus supports a reading about religious responsibility.

This does not make every جَنْب in the Qur’an figurative.

The physical flank is clear in verses such as Al-Tawbah 9:35 and Al-Ḥajj 22:36.

جَنْب and one’s due

Arabic فِي جَنْبِ can mean “in relation to” or “concerning one’s due.”

If someone is said to have fallen short regarding a friend, the intended meaning may be neglecting the friend’s rights or their relationship.

In Al-Zumar 39:56, this usage concerns human responsibility toward God.

The root ج ن ب must not therefore be reduced in its entirety to “a right.”

That is a contextual rendering of an expression, not the general meaning of all derivatives.

12. Distinguishing related roots

ج ن ب (جَنْب) and ج س م (جِسْم)

Study of J S M

جِسْم concerns the physical body and its structure.

جَنْب is a flank, one of the body’s two sides.

A janb can thus be part of a jism without being the entire body.

Al-Tawbah 9:35 names the flanks alongside the forehead and back.

The construction shows that جُنُوب names a particular bodily region there.

ج ن ب (جَنْب) and ب د ن (بَدَن)

Study of B D N

بَدَن concerns the body or frame.

جَنْب indicates the flank.

Al-Ḥajj 22:36 brings sacrificial camels and their flanks into one picture.

جُنُوبُهَا does not itself mean the animals’ entire bodies: it identifies the part emphasized when they fall to the ground.

ج ن ب (جَنْب) and ظ ه ر (ظَهْر)

Study of Ẓ H R

Arabic ظَهْر names the back.

جَنْب is the flank.

These are physically adjacent but not identical.

Al-Tawbah 9:35 names them separately.

جُنُوب must therefore not be translated “backs” there.

ج ن ب (جَانِب) and ن ح و (نَاحِيَة)

نَاحِيَة concerns a side or particular part of a place.

جَانِب also has this use.

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris identifies “side” as one of the two principles of ج ن ب.

The words’ proximity does not make all their derivatives identical.

ج ن ب (اجْتِنَاب) and ب ع د (بُعْد)

Study of B ʿ D

بُعْد concerns distance and separation.

اجْتِنَاب can also mean keeping away.

In Qur’anic ethical uses, however, ijtināb usually involves deliberately refraining from entering a behavior or relationship.

Spatial distance, by contrast, can exist without a person choosing it.

Ijtināb can therefore be a voluntary and behavioral form of distancing.

ج ن ب (اجْتِنَاب) and ت ر ك (تَرْك)

Study of T R K

تَرْك concerns leaving something or not doing it.

اجْتِنَاب can likewise concern refraining from conduct.

In ijtināb, however, the image of standing aside and maintaining distance is prominent.

In legal and ethical uses, both may yield the same outcome, such as not doing something forbidden.

Different lexical imagery must nevertheless not generate an additional, uniform ruling for every instance.

ج ن ب (اجْتِنَاب) and ن ب ذ (نَبْذ)

Study of N B DH

نَبْذ concerns throwing or casting aside.

Ijtināb concerns keeping away.

Someone may cast something aside and then avoid it, but the acts are not identical.

In ijtināb, the person can move away; in nabdh, throwing or casting aside is central.

ج ن ب (اجْتِنَاب) and ه ج ر (هَجْر)

هَجْر concerns leaving and separation.

Ijtināb also relates to keeping a distance.

Hajr can end a relationship or involve leaving a place, whereas Qur’anic ijtināb can concern avoiding acts, suspicions, and ṭāghūt.

Not every hajr is therefore avoidance of sin, nor is every ijtināb a complete ending of a human relationship.

ج ن ب (جُنُب) and ج ن ن (جِنّ)

Study of J N N

These roots share their first two letters but differ in the third.

ج ن ب concerns the flank, side, and distance.

A principal branch of ج ن ن concerns concealment and being hidden.

جُنُب in janābah consequently belongs to ج ن ب, not ج ن ن.

Written similarity must not merge the two families.

ج ن ب (جَنْب) and ج ن ح (جَنَاح)

Study of J N Ḥ

Arabic جَنَاح names a wing and, in some uses, a side.

جَنْب likewise concerns a flank or side.

They overlap in the field of direction and lateral position.

A bird’s wing, a person’s flank, and a place’s side nevertheless differ in their referents.

Their roots also differ and must not be merged merely because of semantic similarity.

ج ن ب (جَنْب) and ج ن ف (جَنَف)

Arabic جَنَف concerns leaning or deviation from a straight course.

جَنْب concerns the side.

In bodily imagery, moving aside and leaning can be close.

Ethical and legal جَنَف can nevertheless concern deviation and injustice, whereas جَنْب itself does not mean oppression or moral deviation.

The roots must therefore be studied separately.

13. Questions of understanding and translation

Do all uses of this root concern a bodily flank?

No.

The bodily flank is a principal use, but the root also concerns a place’s side, social distance, avoidance, and janābah.

In Al-Zumar 39:56, فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ is figurative, understood through the context of human responsibility toward God.

Does ijtināb always mean physical withdrawal?

No.

The Qur’an speaks of avoiding ṭāghūt, false speech, and much suspicion.

These concern human choice and conduct.

Ijtināb can thus be practical and moral distancing.

Does every ijtināb establish the same religious ruling?

No.

The root itself concerns avoidance.

The command’s force, scope, and subject require its object and other relevant verses.

For example, avoiding much suspicion in Al-Ḥujurāt 49:12 is qualified by كَثِيرًا.

The same verse identifies some suspicion as sin.

Different instances of ijtināb cannot therefore be equated without considering their complete wording.

Does جُنُب always mean someone needing a ritual bath?

No.

That sense applies in Al-Nisāʾ 4:43 and Al-Māʾidah 5:6.

In Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:11, however, عَن جُنُبٍ means “from a distance.”

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:36, الْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ concerns a neighbor considered distant in kinship or social affiliation.

The same form can therefore have different uses in different contexts.

Is a person in janābah morally impure?

The word itself does not establish that conclusion.

In the relevant verses, janābah is a state associated with purification for ṣalāh.

It must not be turned into sinfulness or loss of human dignity.

Does الْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ mean only a neighbor whose house is distant?

Not necessarily.

Early Arabic جُنُب can describe a stranger or someone from another family or tribe.

A neighbor’s home may therefore be close while their descent is distant.

The word’s analysis should explain this distinction.

Does جَنْبِ اللَّهِ have a necessarily bodily meaning?

No.

In Al-Zumar 39:56 it occurs in the context of regret for neglecting one’s duty to God.

Language and context support readings concerning God’s due, obedience, and way.

The expression itself cannot establish a claim about God’s bodily structure.

Is avoidance always good conduct?

No.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:31 commends avoiding major sins.

Al-Aʿlā 87:11, however, attributes avoidance of the reminder to someone called الْأَشْقَى, “the most wretched.”

The value of avoidance depends on its object and direction.

14. Translation proposals for Forqan

For bodily جَنْب, the principal equivalent is “flank” or “side.”

For جُنُوب, “flanks” or “sides” is appropriate.

This meaning should be preserved in Āl ʿImrān 3:191, Al-Nisāʾ 4:103, Al-Tawbah 9:35, Yūnus 10:12, Al-Ḥajj 22:36, and Al-Sajdah 32:16.

For جَانِب, equivalents such as “side” and “edge” are appropriate.

In the Mount Ṭūr passages, “the side of Ṭūr” or “the side of Mount Ṭūr” is natural.

In Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:44, “the western side” expresses the word’s clear meaning.

For نَأَىٰ بِجَانِبِهِ in Al-Isrāʾ 17:83 and Fuṣṣilat 41:51, a direct translation can retain the image of withdrawing and distancing, while analysis explains the behavioral sense of turning away.

The principal equivalents for اجْتَنَبَ are “kept away” or “avoided.”

In Qur’anic imperatives such as اجْتَنِبُوا and فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ, “keep away” or “avoid it” is natural.

Without evidence, however, the command’s object must neither be omitted nor expanded with new referents.

For وَاجْنُبْنِي in Ibrāhīm 14:35, “keep me away” is appropriate.

For يَتَجَنَّبُهَا in Al-Aʿlā 87:11, “he avoids it” is appropriate.

For يُجَنَّبُهَا in Al-Layl 92:17, “he is kept away from it” is more precise because it preserves the passive.

For جُنُبًا in Al-Nisāʾ 4:43 and Al-Māʾidah 5:6, “in janābah” or “junub” is appropriate.

In Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:11, عَن جُنُبٍ means “from a distance.”

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:36, الْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ can be rendered “the distant neighbor,” but explanation should identify familial or social distance among the expression’s important senses.

In the same verse, الصَّاحِبِ بِالْجَنْبِ is “the companion at one’s side.”

For فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ in Al-Zumar 39:56, “regarding one’s duty to The God” is precise and contextually appropriate.

جَنْب must not first be translated separately as a bodily flank and that sense then imposed on the figurative expression.

For non-Qur’anic uses, جَنِيب can name an animal led alongside someone, جَنَائِب its plural, and جَنُوب geographical south or the south wind.

These meanings must not be transferred to Qur’anic uses without evidence.

15. Conclusion

The Arabic root ج ن ب connects two related semantic fields: side and lateral position, and distance or withdrawal.

Ibn Fāris presents them as distinct but related.

In the first branch, جَنْب names the flank and جَانِب a side or direction.

In the second, forms such as جَنَّبَ, تَجَنَّبَ, and اجْتَنَبَ concern keeping away, moving away, and avoidance.

Related جُنُب can describe a distant person or stranger, while another use identifies janābah.

Poetic witnesses transmitted from ʿAlqamah ibn ʿAbadah and Al-Aʿshā attest جَنَابَة as distance and estrangement in early Arabic; the dating limits discussed for individual lines still apply.

These uses matter because they prevent reducing the entire root to janābah in jurisprudence and purification.

The Qur’an uses the root 33 times.

In some verses جُنُوب identifies human or animal flanks.

Elsewhere جَانِب concerns the side of a mountain, land, or another place.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:36 has two important social uses: a neighbor outside close kinship and a companion at one’s side.

In Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:11, عَن جُنُبٍ describes seeing from a distance.

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:43 and Al-Māʾidah 5:6, جُنُبًا concerns a state relating to purification.

In Al-Zumar 39:56, فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ figuratively concerns human neglect of duty to God.

In avoidance passages, the same family extends from the spatial field to voluntary human conduct.

People can avoid major sins, ṭāghūt, false speech, and much suspicion. A related verb also describes avoiding the reminder in Al-Aʿlā 87:11.

Distance or avoidance is not inherently virtuous: its value depends on its object and direction.

Ibrāhīm 14:35 asks for protection from serving idols, while Al-Layl 92:17 concerns being kept from the Fire.

Translation must preserve the difference between avoiding something, keeping another away, and being kept away.

For Forqan, “flank” or “side” is the principal equivalent for bodily جَنْب; “side” suits جَانِب; and “keep away” or “avoid” suits ijtināb.

In purification usage جُنُب is the familiar designation of janābah; it must not be turned into personal sinfulness or moral contamination.

For فِي جَنْبِ اللَّهِ, “regarding one’s duty to The God” expresses the contextual sense better than a bodily flank.

Being beside something and keeping a distance provide a defensible way to relate this family’s meanings: flank, side, companionship, estrangement, keeping away, and avoidance. This conceptual relationship alone does not establish the historical order in which the meanings arose; each takes its sense from its own construction and context.

Preserving these distinctions allows the Qur’anic uses of ج ن ب to be understood without reducing them all to janābah, turning every avoidance into an identical ruling, or imposing bodily senses on figurative expressions.
