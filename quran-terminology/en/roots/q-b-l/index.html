The Qur’anic Root Q B L

Facing, orientation, what comes before, and acceptance

Qur’anic forms of this root

The root ق ب ل (Q B L) does not only mean “to accept.” In this root, turning toward, facing, being before, taking direction, and accepting are tied together. The forms below show the main Qur’anic branches of this semantic field.

The central meaning of the root

The root ق ب ل (Q B L) is one of the important roots of the Qur’an. It does not only mean “to accept.” Several meanings are joined in it: turning toward, facing, being in front, being before, taking direction, and accepting.

At first glance, قَبْل (qabl), meaning “before,” قِبْلَة (qiblah), meaning a direction of turning, and قَبُول (qabūl), meaning acceptance, may seem separate. But all these branches return to one central image: something stands before the human being, or the human being turns toward something, or something has already come before him in the path, or a matter comes toward him and he either accepts it or rejects it.

So Q B L is the root of facing and turning toward. From this facing, the meaning of direction can appear; sometimes the meaning of prior time; sometimes acceptance; and sometimes mutual confrontation or two sides standing before one another.

The central meaning can be understood as this: something being placed in the direction of facing, or turning toward something that stands before the human being. From this angle, qabl is not only a time-word. What is before you has come earlier in the order of events. Qiblah is not merely a geographic side; it is the direction toward which the human being turns so that servanthood and salat are ordered around a unified axis.

Qabūl is also not merely hearing or seeing. It means that something enters the circle of acceptance before the human being or before God. Muqābalah, confrontation or encounter, also belongs to this field: two sides stand before each other. Therefore this root does not leave the human being neutral and directionless. A direction, a face-to-face relation, a prior history, or an acceptance is always involved.

Why qabl and qabūl come from one root

At first it may seem strange that a word related to past time, such as qabl, and a word related to acceptance, such as qabūl, come from the same root. But the link is hidden in the central meaning of Q B L: facing, encounter, and the direction of the eye.

In Arabic, many abstract concepts, including time, are understood through sensory and spatial images. The human being is imagined as moving along the path of time. What happened before him has come earlier in the chain of time; it stands before his view. From this angle, qabl means before or prior to.

On the other side, qabūl means that something is placed before the human being and, instead of turning away or pushing it back, the human being turns toward it and brings it into the circle of reception.

So qabl and qabūl are not from two separate worlds. Both return to one image: something stands before the human being. If that thing has already come earlier in the chain of time, it is qabl. If it is presented to the human being and he accepts it, it is qabūl. From here, the connection between qubul, qabl, iqbāl, and qabūl becomes clearer.

For this reason, God in the Qur’an is قَابِلُ التَّوْب (qābilu al-tawb), the Accepter of repentance. The servant returns toward God, and God accepts the path of return. The Qur’an also repeatedly speaks of مِنْ قَبْلُ (min qabl), from before: history and the past are placed before the human being so that he may take warning.

Qabl: before us, in front of history

The word قَبْل (qabl) appears very frequently in the Qur’an. Its meaning is before, prior to, or from the past.

In Al-Baqarah 2:4, the Qur’an speaks of those who believe in what has been sent down upon the Prophet and what was sent down before him. Here qablika shows that the Muhammadan revelation did not come in a vacuum. Before it, there was also a flow of guidance, prophets, books, and communities.

In Al-Baqarah 2:254, the human being is warned to spend from what God has provided before a day comes in which trade and intercession will not benefit. Here qabl points the human being to the opportunity before the end. Before that day arrives, action is still possible.

So qabl is not only the past. Sometimes it is a reminder of the past; sometimes a warning about the time that remains; and sometimes a sign that the human being lives inside a historical chain, not separate from earlier communities and signs.

Qabila: acceptance and rejection

The verb قَبِلَ (qabila) and forms such as يَقْبَلُ (yaqbal) mean to accept. But in the Qur’an, acceptance is not simple or automatic.

In At-Tawbah 9:104, it is said that God is the One who accepts repentance from His servants. Here acceptance is tied to mercy, return, and the repair of the relation between the human being and God. The human being repents, but final acceptance belongs to God.

In An-Nur 24:4, a command is given that the testimony of slanderers should not be accepted. Here acceptance is tied to social justice and the protection of human honor. Not every statement gains the right to enter the system of justice simply because it has been spoken.

In At-Tawbah 9:54, the Qur’an speaks of people whose expenditures are not accepted because inwardly they are corrupted in relation to God, the Messenger, salat, and giving. This shows that outward action without a correct inner direction does not guarantee acceptance.

So in the Qur’an, acceptance is not mere receiving. Acceptance means that something becomes worthy to enter the circle of truth, mercy, justice, or validity.

Taqabbala: acceptance with favor and growth

The form تَقَبَّلَ (taqabbala) is semantically deeper than simple acceptance. It usually shows an acceptance accompanied by care, approval, purity of intention, giving value, and divine attention.

In Al-Baqarah 2:127, Abraham and Ishmael, after raising the foundations of the House, pray:

رَبَّنَا تَقَبَّلْ مِنَّا إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ

Our Lord, accept from us; You are the Hearing, the Knowing. This prayer is very important. Even when the action is great, even when it is the building of the House and service to God, the human being still does not own its acceptance. The action must be presented to God, and God must accept it.

In Al-Ma’idah 5:27, the offering of one of Adam’s two sons is accepted, while the other is not accepted. The answer is clear:

إِنَّمَا يَتَقَبَّلُ اللَّهُ مِنَ الْمُتَّقِينَ

God accepts only from the people of taqwa. This verse is the heart of taqabbul. God does not accept an action only according to its outward form. The criterion is taqwa: inner direction, truthfulness, purity of intention, and standing in a right relation with God.

In Al-Imran 3:37, regarding Mary, it is said that her Lord accepted her with قَبُولٍ حَسَنٍ (qabūlin ḥasan) and caused her to grow with a good growth. Here acceptance is not merely accepting a vow; it is the acceptance of a soul, a path, and a divine nurturing.

So taqabbul in the Qur’an is living and divine acceptance: an acceptance that gives lasting value, blessing, and growth to the action or the soul of the human being.

Aqbala: turning toward and drawing near

The form أَقْبَلَ (aqbala) means to turn toward, draw near, or direct attention toward something or someone.

In Al-Qasas 28:31, Moses is told:

يَا مُوسَىٰ أَقْبِلْ وَلَا تَخَفْ

O Moses, come forward and do not fear. Here aqbil is not merely a bodily movement. Moses must pass through fear and turn toward the divine command. Turning toward can be the first step into a mission.

In As-Saffat 37:27 and similar verses, groups turn toward one another and question each other. This shows that aqbala can also show conversation, encounter, and mutual attention.

So aqbala belongs to practical orientation. The human being does not merely see something; he turns toward it, draws near, and enters an encounter.

Qiblah: the direction of salat and the channel of servanthood

The word قِبْلَة (qiblah) is one of the most important branches of this root. The qiblah is not simply “the direction of prayer” in a shallow geographic sense. It is the direction of turning: the direction through which the human being, the body, the community, and salat are ordered.

Salat is not merely bodily movement. Salat is standing in the relation of servanthood, remembrance, submission, and connection to God. The qiblah channels this turning through the body and the community; it orders scattered and directionless servanthood toward a unified axis of tawhid.

From this view, Makkah and the House belong to all monotheists and believers, not to the identity property of one people, tribe, or sect. In Al-Hajj 22:25, the Sacred Mosque is placed for the people, and equality is shown between the resident and the visitor from outside. This verse does not allow the House of God to be turned into the closed property of one group.

In Al-Hajj 22:26, the place of the House is appointed for Abraham, and the command comes to purify the House for those who circle it, stand, bow, and prostrate. This shows that from the beginning the House is tied to tawhid, purity, salat, standing, bowing, prostration, and the presence of God’s servants.

In Al-Hajj 22:27, the call to Hajj is proclaimed to the people. This is not the call of a closed tribe; it is a public call toward a House that must be the axis of servanthood and remembrance of God.

Therefore the qiblah is not a direction of religious ownership. It is the direction that channels servanthood toward God. The qiblah moves the body toward one side, but it does not imprison God in one place. The qiblah is the practical order of the believers, not the limitation of God in space.

Qiblah and going beyond outward direction

The Qur’an itself warns that outward direction, if separated from inward truth, is not enough. In Al-Baqarah 2:177, it says:

لَيْسَ الْبِرَّ أَنْ تُوَلُّوا وُجُوهَكُمْ قِبَلَ الْمَشْرِقِ وَالْمَغْرِبِ وَلَكِنَّ الْبِرَّ مَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ...

Righteousness is not that you turn your faces toward the east or the west; rather, righteousness is faith in God and the realities that follow. This verse criticizes pure outwardism in relation to qiblah. Turning the face, if it is not joined with faith, giving, fulfilling covenants, patience, and taqwa, becomes a shell without a core.

So the qiblah must become a ground for whole-person turning toward God, not a preoccupation with spatial coordinates. The outward direction must also take the heart, action, ethics, society, and justice toward God.

In Al-Baqarah 2:145, the question of qiblah is also not merely a simple side. When it is said that some of the People of the Book do not follow the qiblah of others, qiblah gains a deeper meaning: it can indicate the practical course, communal identity, and civilizational direction of a community.

Therefore qiblah in the Qur’an is not only a side. It is a living direction of encounter with God, establishing salat, and ordering the community of believers.

Qubul: what is front-facing and visible before the eyes

The word قُبُل (qubul) means the front, what is before one, or what is face-to-face.

In Yusuf 12:26, regarding Joseph’s shirt, it is said that if it was torn from qubul, meaning from the front, it would have one meaning; and if it was torn from behind, it would have another. Here qubul points to the front side of the body and visible facing.

In Al-Kahf 18:55, the Qur’an speaks of punishment coming qubulan: as something face-to-face, manifest, and undeniable.

So qubul is a branch that shows the meaning of facing in a clearer and more visible form. Something stands before the eyes and in front of the human being.

Qibal: side and capacity to face

The word قِبَل (qibal) in the Qur’an sometimes means side, direction, or face, and sometimes carries the meaning of capacity to face or resist.

In An-Naml 27:37, it speaks of forces before whom the other side has no qibal, no capacity to stand before them. Here qibal means the ability to face a force.

In Al-Hadid 57:13, a wall is described whose inner side contains mercy and from qibalihi, meaning from its outward side, there is punishment. Here the word points to side and direction.

In Al-Baqarah 2:177, qibal appears in the criticism of outward direction: righteousness is not merely turning faces toward the east or west. This verse shows that outward direction, without inward direction, is not enough.

Thus qibal shows both direction and the capacity to stand before something. The human being either has the ability to face, or does not; either joins direction to truth, or becomes trapped in the appearance of direction.

Qabīl and qabā’il: groups and mutual recognition

The word قَبَائِل (qabā’il) appears in Al-Hujurat 49:13:

وَجَعَلْنَاكُمْ شُعُوبًا وَقَبَائِلَ لِتَعَارَفُوا

God made human beings into peoples and tribes so that they may know one another. This verse is very important. Tribe and human grouping are not for arrogance, superiority, fanaticism, or hostility. The aim is taʿāruf: mutual recognition.

If tribe leads the human being to recognition, responsibility, connection, and justice, it remains in its proper place. But if tribe becomes an idol of identity, it carries the human being away from taqwa. The same verse gives the criterion clearly: the most honored before God is the one with the most taqwa, not the one most attached to blood, race, tribe, or group.

In Al-Aʿraf 7:27, the Qur’an also speaks of Satan and qabīluhu, his group or faction. This shows that qabīl does not always have a positive meaning. Being a group has no value by itself; the direction of the group matters.

In Al-Isra 17:92, the deniers ask the Prophet to bring God and the angels qabīlan. Here the word can be understood as a kind of tangible face-to-face presence, visible guarantee, or direct encounter. They want unseen truth to become a forced sensory sight so that faith becomes compelled observation.

This use again returns to the central meaning of the root: facing and encounter. But the Qur’an shows that true faith is not made by forced seeing or sensory compulsion.

Qābil: the Accepter of repentance

The word قَابِل (qābil) appears regarding God in Ghafir 40:3:

غَافِرِ الذَّنْبِ وَقَابِلِ التَّوْبِ

Forgiver of sin and Accepter of repentance. This expression is very tender. God is not only Commander and Reckoner; He is the Accepter of repentance. He leaves the path of return open and accepts true repentance from the servant.

But accepting repentance does not mean playing with sin. Repentance has meaning when the human being truly turns back: from corruption to reform, from arrogance to humility, and from insistence upon falsehood to return toward truth.

So God being qābil al-tawb is both mercy and responsibility. It is mercy because the way is open; it is responsibility because repentance must be truthful.

Mutaqābilīn: facing without humiliation

The word مُتَقَابِلِين (mutaqābilīn) appears regarding the people of Paradise, such as in Al-Hijr 15:47 and As-Saffat 37:44, where they are described upon couches facing one another.

This is a beautiful image. Facing in this world is sometimes a field of hostility, competition, envy, and superiority. But in Paradise, facing means clarity, security, nearness, and the absence of turning away.

Mutaqābilīn means people who are before one another, but not for war: for intimacy, honor, and equal presence. No one has turned his back on another; no one looks down from above; and no one is in a state of concealment or distance.

This branch of the root shows that facing is not always conflict. Sometimes facing one another is the fullest form of love and peace.

Mustaqbil: what comes from the front

The word مُسْتَقْبِل (mustaqbil) appears in Al-Ahqaf 46:24, where the people of ʿAd see something like a cloud coming toward their valleys.

Its primary meaning in this verse is clear: something that comes from the front, something with which the human being is about to be faced.

This meaning matters ethically. The human being is always faced by things: mercy, warning, opportunity, punishment, truth, or the result of his own actions. The question is whether, when something comes toward him, he recognizes it correctly or is deceived by its appearance.

The people of ʿAd thought it was a rain cloud, but what was coming toward them was punishment. So not every approaching thing is mercy; discernment and taqwa are necessary.

Accepted action and the criterion of taqwa

One of the most important lessons of the root Q B L is that not every action is accepted.

A human being may perform an action, give wealth, offer a sacrifice, recite a prayer, or apparently worship; but the Qur’an repeatedly shows that acceptance is not limited to the outward form of the action.

In Al-Ma’idah 5:27, two offerings look outwardly similar, but one is accepted and the other is not. The criterion is taqwa.

This principle is very important. Taqwa means that the human being has the right direction before God, truth, justice, and purity of intention. Action without right direction may be only outward movement. But action that comes out of taqwa becomes capable of acceptance.

So acceptance in the Qur’an is not only the result of action; it is the result of the direction of the action.

Qiblah and qabūl: outer direction and inner direction

There is a beautiful connection between قِبْلَة (qiblah) and قَبُول (qabūl). The qiblah organizes the outward direction of the human being; acceptance is connected to the inward direction of the action.

The human body may face the qiblah, while the heart faces power, reputation, fanaticism, display, or a lifeless inherited religion. In such a state, the direction of the body is correct, but the inner direction is wounded.

On the other side, when the qiblah is understood correctly, it reminds the human being that servanthood must have direction. A believer is not directionless. He orders his body, heart, action, and society toward God.

Al-Baqarah 2:177 makes this connection clear. The Qur’an says that righteousness is not merely turning faces toward the east and the west; rather, it is faith, giving, fulfilling covenants, patience, and taqwa. So the qiblah is not for displaying identity; it is for training direction. And acceptance is not for confirming the outward form; it is for testing the inner truth of the action.

The past, turning toward, and present responsibility

In the root Q B L, there is a reflective relation between qabl and aqbala. Qabl turns the human being toward the past and the people before him; aqbala brings the human being into turning and encounter in the present moment.

The Qur’an repeatedly speaks of earlier communities. This reminder is not storytelling. The past in the Qur’an is a mirror of present responsibility. The human being must see how those before him faced truth, what they accepted, what they rejected, and what their outcome was.

But the human being must not become imprisoned in the past. He must now turn toward truth. Knowing what happened qabl, before, should prepare him for iqbāl, turning toward truth.

So this root places the human being between two directions: looking at what has passed before him, and deciding toward which direction he will now turn.

Suggested rendering in Forqan

For the root Q B L, one fixed translation is not enough. The form of the word and the context of the verse must be considered.

  • قَبْل (qabl): before, prior to, earlier.
  • قَبِلَ / يَقْبَلُ (qabila / yaqbal): he accepted, he accepts.
  • تَقَبَّلَ / يَتَقَبَّلُ (taqabbala / yataqabbal): he graciously accepted, accepted with good favor.
  • أَقْبَلَ (aqbala): he turned toward, drew near, directed himself toward something or someone.
  • قِبْلَة (qiblah): direction of turning, direction for establishing salat, practical axis of servanthood.
  • قَبُول (qabūl): good acceptance, acceptance accompanied by favor.
  • قُبُل (qubul): front, before one, face-to-face.
  • قِبَل (qibal): side, direction, or capacity to face.
  • قَبِيل (qabīl): group, faction, directionally aligned group, or tangible face-to-face presence in some contexts.
  • قَبَائِل (qabā’il): tribes, human groupings for mutual recognition.
  • قَابِل (qābil): accepter.
  • مُتَقَابِلِين (mutaqābilīn): facing one another, in mutual presence.
  • مُسْتَقْبِل (mustaqbil): coming from the front, approaching from before.

Summary

The root ق ب ل (Q B L) is the root of direction, facing, anteriority, and acceptance.

In this root, the human being is always faced by something: a past that came before him, a direction toward which he must turn, an action that may be accepted or rejected, a repentance returning toward God, and a truth standing before him.

The qiblah shows the direction of the body and the community; but in truth, it is the direction of establishing salat and channeling servanthood toward God. Acceptance also reveals the direction of the heart and action. Qabl turns the human being toward history and his limited opportunity; iqbāl brings him into the moment of decision. Taqabbul shows that the value of action is not in its outward form, but in taqwa, truthfulness, and inward direction.

So Q B L teaches the human being not to live without direction. He must know where he is facing, what past he learns from, what he accepts, what he rejects, and whether his action has the capacity to be accepted before God.
