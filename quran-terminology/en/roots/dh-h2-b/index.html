Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root DH H B

Going, leaving, passing away, disappearing from presence, and being taken out of reach

Important Forms from This Root

ذَهَبَ / يَذْهَبُ

dhahaba / yadhhabu; went, left, passed away, or moved out of reach.

ذَهَاب

dhahāb; going, departure, passing away, disappearance.

ذَهَبَ بِـ

dhahaba bi-; took something away, removed it from the scene, carried it out of reach.

أَذْهَبَ / يُذْهِبُ

adhhaba / yudhhibu; removed, took away, drove off, erased, or dispelled.

مَذْهَب

madhhab; a way, path, direction; later a method, school, or intellectual route.

ذَهَب

dhahab; gold, a well-known noun from the same root, to be handled carefully.

Central Meaning

The root DH H B means going, leaving, moving away from one’s place, passing away, disappearing, and being taken away from a person. The word ذَهَبَ means he went, departed, moved away, or went out of reach.

This root does not only indicate physical movement. Sometimes a person goes from one place to another. Sometimes a state is lifted from a person. Sometimes light or power disappears. Sometimes time, opportunity, or blessing passes away. In all these uses, one thing is shared: what was present is no longer in the same place or within the same reach.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of DH H B is going and leaving presence. Something that dhahaba separates from its current place. A human being may leave a place, light may disappear, fear or grief may subside, or power and blessing may move beyond a person’s reach.

In this root, “going” does not always mean complete destruction. Sometimes something simply moves away from a person. Sometimes it exits its present state. Sometimes its effect is removed. Sometimes it truly disappears. For this reason, translation must follow context. Dhahaba may mean “went,” but in some verses “disappeared,” “passed away,” “subsided,” “was removed,” or “was taken away” is more precise.

Material Image

The material image of this root is the movement of something from near a person toward distance. Someone stands near you, then begins to go. Something is in your hand, then leaves your hand. Light is around you, then it is extinguished or gone. A state is inside you, then it is lifted from you.

In all these images, presence becomes absence. Dhahaba means that something which was there is no longer in the same place or within the same reach. This image matters for Qur’anic usage because the Qur’an may speak of a body going, but also of light, fear, grief, blessing, or the effect of something going.

Another image is a flood carrying something away. In such a case, not only movement occurs; the previous traces may be washed away and the scene may be left empty. In this use, dhahaba bi- is stronger: something is pulled from its place and removed from the field of presence.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, dhahaba is one of the common verbs of movement. It is used for a person going, a group departing, leaving a place, and moving away from a previous position. The root then expanded naturally from physical departure into disappearance, fading, passing away, and removal of an effect.

The word madhhab also belongs to this root. Originally it means a way or place of going. Later it came to mean method, direction, intellectual way, or school. This shows that “going” in this root is not only movement by foot. A person also goes by thought, decision, value, and way of life.

The word dhahab, gold, is also well known in Arabic. Its connection with the root should be explained cautiously. Some lexicographers mention possible explanations, such as gold moving from hand to hand because of its value, or impurities going away during refining. These may illuminate the field of the root, but gold should not be imposed on every derivative of DH H B.

Dhahaba in al-Baqarah

One clear use of this root appears in Surat al-Baqarah:

فَلَمَّا أَضَاءَتْ مَا حَوْلَهُ ذَهَبَ اللَّهُ بِنُورِهِمْ 2:17

Here, ذَهَبَ اللَّهُ بِنُورِهِمْ means The God took away their light. The expression does not merely say that the light went out. It says that the light was taken from them and moved out of their reach.

The bāʾ in بِنُورِهِمْ is important. In Arabic, dhahaba bi-shayʾ means to take something away, remove it, or carry it out of the scene. Thus ذَهَبَ النُّورُ would mean “the light went” or “the light disappeared,” while ذَهَبَ اللَّهُ بِنُورِهِمْ means The God took their light away and removed it from the horizon of their access.

Qur’anic Usage

Physical Going and Leaving Presence

Dhahaba can mean going from one place to another. This is the most basic meaning of the root. At this level, a person or group leaves a place and no longer remains in the former presence.

Dhahaba Bi- as Taking Away

In many uses, dhahaba comes with bi- and means taking something away or removing it from the scene. ذَهَبَ بِهِ means he took him away; ذَهَبَ بِالشَّيْءِ means he took the thing away or removed it from the field. In 2:17, this structure shows that the light did not merely lessen or go out; it was taken away from them.

Adhhaba as Removing or Dispelling

The Qur’an also uses this root in Form IV: إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ الرِّجْسَ 33:33. Here yudhhiba emphasizes removing, driving away, and cleansing a disliked thing.

There is a subtle difference between dhahaba bi- and adhhaba. Dhahaba bi- more strongly suggests taking something from the scene or out of reach. Adhhaba emphasizes removing, dispelling, or erasing the effect of something from someone.

The Going of a State

The Qur’an also speaks of inner states going away: فَلَمَّا ذَهَبَ عَنْ إِبْرَاهِيمَ الرَّوْعُ 11:74. Here dhahaba is not physical movement. It means that fear went away, subsided, or was lifted from Abraham.

This use shows that DH H B can be used for inner states. Fear, grief, agitation, or worry may “go,” meaning its presence and effect end.

Taking Away Perceptive Power

In 2:20, the Qur’an says: وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَذَهَبَ بِسَمْعِهِمْ وَأَبْصَارِهِمْ. Here dhahaba bi- means taking away the faculties of perception: removing hearing and seeing from access and function.

This is structurally close to ذَهَبَ اللَّهُ بِنُورِهِمْ. In both, something that enables perception and movement is removed from human reach.

The Going of Opportunity, Blessing, or Effect

DH H B may also be used for something taken from a person or whose opportunity of presence has ended. Blessing, power, light, or opportunity may “go,” meaning it has left its presence and effect. The meaning comes from the root itself: something that was with a person is no longer within reach.

Difference from Nearby Roots

M SH Y concerns walking and movement by foot, while DH H B is broader and may indicate any kind of going, leaving, or disappearance. S Y R is closer to movement along a path, travel, or ordered journey, while DH H B emphasizes going away and leaving presence.

M R R concerns passing by or passing through. Z W L is closer to decline and removal. F N Y is closer to annihilation or perishing, while DH H B is not always annihilation. Sometimes it is only moving away, being taken, or going out of reach.

GH Y B concerns hiddenness and absence. DH H B may lead to absence, but the root itself emphasizes movement out of presence. KH Ṭ F concerns sudden snatching; dhahaba bi- may show complete taking away, but not necessarily suddenness.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, dhahaba should not always be translated only as “went.” If it concerns a person or group, “went” or “departed” may be suitable. If it comes with bi-, as in ذَهَبَ اللَّهُ بِنُورِهِمْ, “took away,” “removed from reach,” or “stripped from their horizon” may be more precise.

If the root appears in Form IV, as in يُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ الرِّجْسَ, then “remove,” “drive away,” “cleanse away,” or “dispell” is often better. If it concerns an inner state, “subsided,” “was lifted,” “went away,” or “disappeared” may be more accurate. If it concerns opportunity or blessing, “passed away,” “was lost,” or “was taken” may fit the context.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

ذَهَبَ (dhahaba): went, departed, disappeared, passed away, subsided.

يَذْهَبُ (yadhhabu): goes, disappears, moves away, passes away.

ذَهَاب (dhahāb): going, departure, disappearance, passing away.

أَذْهَبَ (adhhaba): removed, took away, drove away, dispelled, erased.

يُذْهِبُ (yudhhibu): removes, takes away, dispels, cleanses away.

ذَهَبَ بِـ (dhahaba bi-): took something away, removed it from reach, carried it out of presence.

مَذْهَب (madhhab): way, path, method, intellectual direction.

ذَهَب (dhahab): gold; carefully treated as a well-known noun from the same root.

Linguistic Summary

The root DH H B denotes going, moving away, leaving presence, and disappearance. Materially, dhahaba means something leaves its place and is no longer within former reach. Semantically, light, grief, fear, opportunity, blessing, or a state may also “go,” meaning its effect and presence end.

In 2:17, ذَهَبَ اللَّهُ بِنُورِهِمْ does not mean that the light simply went away by itself. The structure dhahaba bi- shows that The God took their light away, removed it from their reach, and stripped their horizon of brightness.

The main image of this root is this: something that was present leaves presence. It either goes by itself, is taken away, or its effect disappears.
