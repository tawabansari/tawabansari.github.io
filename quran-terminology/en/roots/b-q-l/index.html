Soft edible greens and plants that grow from the earth.

1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ب ق ل in Arabic and Qur'anic usage points to soft plants and earth-grown vegetation, especially those plants that are edible and are consumed as greens or fresh growth.

This root pays attention mostly to the natural and living aspect of the plant: something that grows from the earth, is soft, and is used as food or for direct human benefit.

The semantic core of the root can be stated as follows:

ب ق ل denotes the growing of soft edible plants from the earth.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

بَقْل

A soft plant, green, or edible growth of the earth.

بَقْلِهَا

Its greens and edible plants; the greens of that earth or land.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The core of this root rests on a few simple elements:

growing from the earth;

the softness and tenderness of the plant;

being edible or usable for human beings.

Therefore, بقل does not point to large trees or hard woody plants. It relates more to greens and soft fresh plants.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is green and soft plants that grow from the soil and can be harvested and consumed quickly.

This image includes things such as:

edible greens;

fresh and soft plants;

growths that do not require complex processing.

In this image, the elements of freshness and closeness to the earth are prominent.

6. Use of the Root in Arabic

In Arabic, بقل generally refers to greens and soft plants, especially what is harvested for eating or daily use.

This word stands in contrast to tree-like plants or harder products, and points more to simple and direct growths of the earth.

7. Qur'anic Use of the Root

وَإِذْ قُلْتُمْ يَا مُوسَىٰ لَنْ نَصْبِرَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامٍ وَاحِدٍ فَادْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ يُخْرِجْ لَنَا مِمَّا تُنْبِتُ الْأَرْضُ مِنْ بَقْلِهَا وَقِثَّائِهَا وَفُومِهَا وَعَدَسِهَا وَبَصَلِهَا

And when you said: O Moses, we will not be patient with one kind of food; so call upon your Lord for us, that He may bring out for us from what the earth grows: its greens, its cucumbers, its garlic or wheat, its lentils, and its onions.

Al-Baqarah 2:61

In this verse, بَقْلِهَا appears alongside other products of the earth and points specifically to greens and soft edible plants.

This usage shows that بقل is part of the wider set of earth-grown things, but it focuses especially on simple and everyday greens and plants.

8. Semantic Notes

بقل points to soft plants and greens, not to trees or hard products;

the word emphasizes natural growing from the earth;

in Qur'anic usage, بقل appears beside other products and shows part of the variety of earthly foods.

9. Conclusion

The root ب ق ل in the Qur'an points to soft edible plants of the earth: growths that come from the soil and are used directly for human nourishment or benefit.

The final definition of the root is:

ب ق ل denotes soft edible greens and plants that grow from the earth.
