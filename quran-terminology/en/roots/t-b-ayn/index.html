The Qur'anic Root T B AYN (T B ʿ)

Following, pursuing, going behind something, and moving along the path of a forerunner

Foundational Meaning

The root ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) fundamentally points to following, pursuing, coming behind something, and moving along the path of a forerunner. In this root, something stands ahead and something else comes after it: sometimes by choice, sometimes by habit, sometimes through deception, and sometimes as consequence and pursuit.

Important Forms from This Root

Important Qur’anic forms of this root include تبع (tabiʿa), يتبع (yattabiʿu / yatbaʿu), اتبع (ittabaʿa), يتبعون (yattabiʿūn), اتبعوا (ittabaʿū), اتباع (ittibāʿ), أتباع (atbāʿ), متبع (muttabiʿ), تابع (tābiʿ), اتبعهم (ittabaʿahum), أتبعهم (atbaʿahum), and أتبعوا (utbiʿū / atbaʿū, according to form and reading context). The simple verb تبع (tabiʿa) is close to following or pursuing. Form VIII, اتبع (ittabaʿa), is especially important in the Qur’an and often points to accepted following, taking a path, or placing oneself in the extension of a way. اتباع (ittibāʿ) is the verbal noun of following. أتباع (atbāʿ) are followers or those who come behind. أتبع (atbaʿa) in some contexts points to following after, pursuing, or setting out behind someone.

Central Meaning

The linguistic core of ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) is posterior movement along the path of a forerunner. This root is not simply “obedience.” Obedience is more closely tied to accepting a command, but اتباع (ittibāʿ) carries an image of movement: someone or something has gone ahead, and the human being walks behind it. The followed thing can be guidance, revelation, the Messenger, light, desire, Satan, assumption, forefathers, or false leaders. Therefore, the value of following does not come from the root itself; it comes from what the human being follows.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is shadow-like movement behind a guide, a footprint, a caravan, or a living being that has gone ahead. One may imagine an animal following its young or its owner, or a group moving along the path of its leader. In this image, the follower does not merely stand in place and think; the follower enters a path, takes steps, and aligns direction with the one or the thing ahead.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic usage, ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) is connected with coming behind, following, pursuing, joining, and continuing the path of another. Sometimes it is used for physical movement, and sometimes for social, intellectual, or religious following. In the final version, if a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or early Arabic usage is found, it can be added.

Following Guidance

قُلْنَا اهْبِطُوا مِنْهَا جَمِيعًا ۖ فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم مِّنِّي هُدًى فَمَن تَبِعَ هُدَايَ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

We said: Descend from it, all together. Then if guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows My guidance: no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve.

Al-Baqarah 2:38

In this ayah, تبع هداي (tabiʿa hudāya) means following the guidance of The God. Guidance is not only heard or known; it must be followed. The human being must place himself within its path.

The same meaning appears in Ta-Ha:

فَمَنِ اتَّبَعَ هُدَايَ فَلَا يَضِلُّ وَلَا يَشْقَىٰ

So whoever follows My guidance will not go astray and will not be wretched.

Ta-Ha 20:123

In this ayah, following guidance has a result: neither misguidance nor wretchedness. Following guidance is therefore not merely a mental belief; it is becoming aligned with guidance in life.

Following What The God Sent Down

اتَّبِعُوا مَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكُم مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا مِن دُونِهِ أَوْلِيَاءَ

Follow what has been sent down to you from your Lord, and do not follow allies besides Him.

Al-A'raf 7:3

In this ayah, the object of following is clear: ما أنزل إليكم من ربكم (mā unzila ilaykum min rabbikum), what has been sent down to you from your Lord. The Qur’an makes revelation the criterion of following. A human being must not follow every forerunner, tradition, power, or social authority; what has been sent down from The God must be the measure of the path.

The same principle appears in the critique of blind following of forefathers:

وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمُ اتَّبِعُوا مَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ قَالُوا بَلْ نَتَّبِعُ مَا وَجَدْنَا عَلَيْهِ آبَاءَنَا

And when it is said to them, “Follow what The God has sent down,” they say, “Rather, we follow what we found our forefathers upon.”

Luqman 31:21

In this ayah, two kinds of following stand opposite one another: following revelation and following the inherited way of forefathers. The Qur’an does not reject the past merely because it is past. The problem begins when the past takes the place of revelation and the human being hands his path over to it.

Following the Messenger and the Sent-Down Light

الَّذِينَ يَتَّبِعُونَ الرَّسُولَ النَّبِيَّ الْأُمِّيَّ

Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet.

Al-A'raf 7:157

In the same ayah it says:

وَاتَّبَعُوا النُّورَ الَّذِي أُنزِلَ مَعَهُ

And they followed the light that was sent down with him.

Al-A'raf 7:157

This connection is very important. Following the Messenger in the Qur’an is not detached from the light sent down with him. The Messenger is not an independent forerunner set against The God; the Messenger carries the message and the path illuminated by divine light. Therefore, following the Messenger, in its depth, is becoming aligned with the message and the light he brought.

Following Satan

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ كُلُوا مِمَّا فِي الْأَرْضِ حَلَالًا طَيِّبًا وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا خُطُوَاتِ الشَّيْطَانِ

O people, eat from what is in the earth, lawful and pure, and do not follow the footsteps of Satan.

Al-Baqarah 2:168

The expression خطوات الشيطان (khuṭuwāt al-shayṭān), the footsteps of Satan, shows that following Satan is often step by step. The human being does not always fall through one large movement; small steps, gradual normalization, justification, and moral numbness can place him on Satan’s path.

In another ayah it says:

وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا خُطُوَاتِ الشَّيْطَانِ ۚ إِنَّهُ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ مُّبِينٌ

And do not follow the footsteps of Satan; indeed, he is for you a clear enemy.

Al-Baqarah 2:208

Following Satan here is following a gradual path. The danger is not only in obvious evil; it is in steps that slowly change the human being’s direction.

Following Desire

وَلَا تَتَّبِعِ الْهَوَىٰ فَيُضِلَّكَ عَن سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ

And do not follow desire, lest it misguide you from the way of The God.

Sad 38:26

الهوى (al-hawā) here is unleashed desire and unrestrained pull. When the human being follows desire, desire sits in the position of the forerunner and the human being moves behind it. The result is departure from سبيل الله (sabīl Allāh), the way of The God. In this usage, ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) shows that the human being is always taking direction: either he follows guidance, or he follows desire.

Following Assumption

إِن يَتَّبِعُونَ إِلَّا الظَّنَّ وَمَا تَهْوَى الْأَنفُسُ

They follow nothing but assumption and what the souls desire.

An-Najm 53:23

In this ayah, following assumption is joined with what the souls desire. When assumption becomes the criterion of movement, it separates the human being from knowledge and guidance. Following without knowledge places the human being on a groundless path.

In another ayah it says:

وَإِن تُطِعْ أَكْثَرَ مَن فِي الْأَرْضِ يُضِلُّوكَ عَن سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ ۚ إِن يَتَّبِعُونَ إِلَّا الظَّنَّ

And if you obey most of those on the earth, they will misguide you from the way of The God. They follow nothing but assumption.

Al-An'am 6:116

Here, being the majority is not the criterion of truth. If most people follow assumption, following them takes the human being out of the way of The God.

Followers and Leaders on the Day of Resurrection

إِذْ تَبَرَّأَ الَّذِينَ اتُّبِعُوا مِنَ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوا وَرَأَوُا الْعَذَابَ وَتَقَطَّعَتْ بِهِمُ الْأَسْبَابُ

When those who were followed disown those who followed, and they see the punishment, and the bonds between them are cut off.

Al-Baqarah 2:166

In this ayah, there are two groups: those who were followed, and those who followed. In worldly life there was connection, trust, influence, and following between them. But on the Day of Resurrection these bonds are cut. The Qur’an shows that the follower cannot transfer responsibility for his direction onto the one he followed.

The continuation says:

وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوا لَوْ أَنَّ لَنَا كَرَّةً فَنَتَبَرَّأَ مِنْهُمْ كَمَا تَبَرَّءُوا مِنَّا

And those who followed said, “If only we had a return, we would disown them as they disowned us.”

Al-Baqarah 2:167

Here, blind following becomes regret. Following in worldly life is not merely an intellectual choice; it builds a bond of destiny. If that bond is upon falsehood, its end is disavowal and the cutting of ties.

Physical Pursuit

فَأَتْبَعُوهُم مُّشْرِقِينَ

So they pursued them at sunrise.

Ash-Shu'ara 26:60

Here following has a clear spatial meaning: Pharaoh’s people set out after the Children of Israel. This usage shows the material layer of the root: moving behind a target.

In another ayah it says:

فَأَتْبَعَهُمْ فِرْعَوْنُ بِجُنُودِهِ فَغَشِيَهُم مِّنَ الْيَمِّ مَا غَشِيَهُمْ

So Pharaoh pursued them with his soldiers, then there covered them from the sea that which covered them.

Ta-Ha 20:78

In this usage, following points to active pursuit. Pharaoh was not merely opposed; he moved, pursued, and perished on that same path.

Being Followed by Consequence or Effect

فَأَتْبَعَهُ الشَّيْطَانُ فَكَانَ مِنَ الْغَاوِينَ

So Satan followed him, and he became of those who go astray.

Al-A'raf 7:175

In this ayah, a person detaches himself from the signs, and Satan follows him. This usage shows that sometimes the human being follows falsehood, and sometimes, after separating from truth, falsehood and Satan come behind him and do not leave him alone.

About ʿĀd it says:

وَأُتْبِعُوا فِي هَٰذِهِ الدُّنْيَا لَعْنَةً وَيَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ

And they were followed in this worldly life by a curse, and on the Day of Resurrection as well.

Hud 11:60

Here following points to a consequence that comes behind the people. The curse, meaning distance from mercy, follows them as an effect and consequence. Thus ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) is not only the movement of a human being behind something; sometimes the effect of action moves behind the human being.

Following with Ihsan

وَالَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوهُم بِإِحْسَانٍ

And those who followed them with ihsan.

At-Tawbah 9:100

In this ayah, following is qualified by إحسان (iḥsān). Following the earlier righteous ones has value when it is with ihsan: goodness, carefulness, moral excellence, and rightness. This condition shows that the Qur’an does not make blind following the criterion; even in following the righteous, the quality of following matters.

Important Distinctions

Different usages of ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) must be distinguished. Following guidance means placing oneself on the path of The God’s guidance. Following revelation means taking what The God sent down as the criterion. Following the Messenger means becoming aligned with the message and the light sent down with him. Following forefathers becomes a problem when it replaces revelation. Following Satan is gradual and step by step. Following desire means desire becomes the forerunner. Following assumption means assumption takes the place of knowledge. Pharaoh’s pursuit shows the physical meaning of the root. Being followed by a curse or by Satan shows that consequences can also pursue the human being.

Structural Note

The object of the verb is very important in this root. اتبع الهدى (ittabaʿa al-hudā) means he followed guidance. اتبع الرسول (ittabaʿa al-rasūl) means he followed the Messenger in the path of the message. اتبع ما أنزل الله (ittabaʿa mā anzala Allāh) means he made what The God sent down the criterion of movement. اتبع الهوى (ittabaʿa al-hawā) means he made desire the forerunner. اتبع خطوات الشيطان (ittabaʿa khuṭuwāt al-shayṭān) means he followed the gradual footsteps of Satan. اتبع الظن (ittabaʿa al-ẓann) means he made assumption the criterion. The moral meaning of following becomes clear from what is being followed.

Difference from Nearby Roots

ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) differs from several nearby roots. ط و ع (ṭ-w-ʿ) is closer to obedience, becoming compliant, and accepting a command; but ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) more strongly carries the image of moving behind a forerunner or path. ق ف و (q-f-w) is close to following a trace, track, or something without knowledge, as in ولا تقف ما ليس لك به علم (wa lā taqfu mā laysa laka bihi ʿilm) in Al-Isra 17:36. لحق (l-ḥ-q) points to reaching and joining, while following may continue without full arrival. اقتدى (iqtadā) is close to taking someone as an example or leader. سلك (s-l-k) points to entering and traversing a path, while following emphasizes that something or someone ahead has determined the direction. مشى (m-sh-y) means walking, but following is walking behind something or in the extension of something.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

For Forqan translation, ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) must be translated by context. تبع (tabiʿa) and اتبع (ittabaʿa) can be translated as “followed,” “pursued,” “took up,” “went behind,” “took his way,” or “went after it.” اتبع الهدى (ittabaʿa al-hudā) is better rendered as “followed guidance” or “went in pursuit of guidance.” اتبع الرسول (ittabaʿa al-rasūl) is “followed the Messenger,” but in explanation it must be connected to the message and the light sent down. اتبع الهوى (ittabaʿa al-hawā) is “followed desire” or “followed his desire.” اتبع خطوات الشيطان (ittabaʿa khuṭuwāt al-shayṭān) is “followed the footsteps of Satan.” أتباع (atbāʿ) can be “followers” or “those who came behind.” الذين اتبعوا (alladhīna ittabaʿū) means “those who followed,” while الذين اتبعوا (alladhīna uttubiʿū), according to passive reading and context, means “those who were followed”; in English the difference must be made clear through grammar. أتبع (atbaʿa) in pursuit contexts is better translated as “followed,” “went after them,” or “pursued.”

Linguistic Summary

Linguistic summary: the root ت ب ع (t-b-ʿ) points to following, pursuing, going behind something, and continuing the path of a forerunner. Its material image is continuous movement behind a guide, caravan, footprint, or target. In the Qur’an, this root extends from following guidance and revelation to following the Messenger and the light, from following Satan and desire to following assumption and forefathers, and from the relationship between followers and leaders to physical pursuit and pursuing consequences. Its difference from obedience is that obedience focuses on accepting a command, while following emphasizes becoming aligned with and moving along the path of what is followed.
