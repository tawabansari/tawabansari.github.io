Short Introduction to the Root

The root moves in the field of following, going behind something, moving along its trace or path, and one thing coming after another.

In the root’s foundational image, something stands ahead and something else comes after it. At times this relation is spatial and movement-based, as when an army pursues a group. At times it is intellectual, religious, or social, as when someone follows guidance, a messenger, a way, desire, or assumption. At other times it is a relation between an act and what follows it, as when harm comes after charity or a curse follows a people in this world and the next.

, therefore, is not merely knowing, mental agreement, or hearing. In voluntary uses, the follower aligns direction with what stands ahead and continues along its course.

Important Forms and Derivatives

The root occurs in the Qur’an in a variety of verbal and nominal forms. Its principal forms include:

تَبِعَ (tabiʿa)

This is a form I verb indicating following, coming behind, or taking a position after something.

فَمَنْ تَبِعَ هُدَايَ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

“Then whoever follows My guidance—no fear will be upon them, nor will they grieve.”

Al-Baqarah 2:38

This form can describe voluntary human following, but at times it also expresses an involuntary sequence or something coming afterward:

تَتْبَعُهَا الرَّادِفَةُ

“And the following one comes after it.”

An-Naziʿat 79:7

اتَّبَعَ (ittabaʿa)

This is a form VIII verb and the most frequent form of this root in the Qur’an. It indicates going after something, taking its way, following it, or placing oneself along its course.

اتَّبِعُوا مَا أُنْزِلَ إِلَيْكُمْ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ

“Follow what has been sent down to you from your Rabb.”

Al-Aʿraf 7:3

وَلَا تَتَّبِعِ الْهَوَىٰ

“And do not follow desire.”

Sad 38:26

The verb itself carries neither a positive nor a negative value. Its value becomes clear from the object, direction, and context of following.

أَتْبَعَ (atbaʿa)

This is a form IV verb. In the Qur’an it can indicate going after someone, pursuing, catching up, or bringing one thing after another.

فَأَتْبَعَهُمْ فِرْعَوْنُ بِجُنُودِهِ

“Then Pharaoh went after them with his forces.”

Ta-Ha 20:78

In some uses, the subject places one thing after another:

فَأَتْبَعْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ بَعْضًا

“Then We brought some of them after others.”

Al-Mu’minun 23:44

There is overlap between and the other forms of this root. A fixed causative or pursuit-based meaning should therefore not be imposed upon it in every ayah.

تَبَع (tabaʿ)

This noun can refer to a follower or a group of followers. In the Qur’an, it is used both collectively and for a body of followers:

إِنَّا كُنَّا لَكُمْ تَبَعًا

“We were followers of yours.”

Ibrahim 14:21

The familiar plural is known in Arabic, but this form does not occur in the text of the Qur’an.

تَابِع (tābiʿ)

This active participle indicates one who follows or is a follower:

وَمَا أَنْتَ بِتَابِعٍ قِبْلَتَهُمْ

“And you are not a follower of their qiblah.”

Al-Baqarah 2:145

In An-Nur 24:31, appears in the context of people attached to and accompanying a household.

تَبِيع (tabīʿ)

This noun from the same family can indicate a pursuer, claimant, or someone who comes after a matter to demand and recover a right:

ثُمَّ لَا تَجِدُوا لَكُمْ عَلَيْنَا بِهِ تَبِيعًا

“Then you will find no one for yourselves against Us to pursue and demand it.”

Al-Isra 17:69

Here, the image of following extends to pursuing a claim or demanding accountability.

مُتَتَابِعَيْن (mutatabiʿayn)

This is the dual active participle of form VI and indicates two months following one another without interruption:

فَصِيَامُ شَهْرَيْنِ مُتَتَابِعَيْنِ

“Then fasting two consecutive months.”

An-Nisa 4:92

In this form, temporal succession without a gap is prominent.

اتِّبَاع (ittibaʿ)

This is the verbal noun of form VIII and indicates the act of following, going after, or pursuing:

إِلَّا اتِّبَاعَ الظَّنِّ

“Except following assumption.”

An-Nisa 4:157

In Al-Baqarah 2:178, concerns pursuing a right in a recognized and fitting manner, not doctrinally following a person.

مُتَّبَعُون (muttabaʿun)

This is the passive participle of form VIII:

إِنَّكُمْ مُتَّبَعُونَ

“You will surely be pursued.”

Ash-Shuʿara 26:52

Here, those who leave at night will become the objects of pursuit.

Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of rests upon a relation between what is ahead and what comes after it. One stands farther forward, while another takes a place along its trace, direction, movement, or position.

This relation is not always between two human beings. What is followed can be a person, way, guidance, revelation, book, light, reminder, shariʿah, command, approval, truth, falsehood, assumption, desire, passion, or the footsteps of Satan. Even a consequence can follow an act.

For this reason, “following” is suitable in many ayat but does not convey the root’s entire field. In English, following may sometimes be understood only as an intellectual or obedient relation, while also preserves the image of movement and coming afterward.

Ittibaʿ is also not identical with obedience. A person may obey a command because it has been accepted or because the person submits to it. In ittibaʿ, the focus is on which person, way, or direction the human being places ahead and then moves along.

The two fields can be joined in one behavior, but their images are not identical. The Qur’an at times places the two verbs beside one another:

فَاتَّبِعُونِي وَأَطِيعُوا أَمْرِي

“Then follow me and obey my command.”

Ta-Ha 20:90

Their appearance together shows that ittibaʿ and obedience overlap but are not the same word or image.

Material Image of the Root

The root’s material image is movement behind a trace, footprint, caravan, guide, or living being that has gone ahead.

One may picture a person moving ahead while another follows the first person’s footprints. The follower does not independently make a new direction; the existing path is continued. If the forerunner changes direction, the follower’s direction also changes so long as the relation of following continues.

Lexical sources mention a tangible example in explaining this root: can refer to a young calf in its first year that moves behind its mother. This use makes the root’s image clear: a living being whose movement and direction are aligned with the advance of another.

The Qur’an makes the same image visible in the expression :

وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا خُطُوَاتِ الشَّيْطَانِ

“And do not follow the footsteps of Satan.”

Al-Baqarah 2:168

The subject is not merely accepting a belief; it is placing one’s foot where another foot has been and continuing along a path.

A second image is one thing coming after another:

قَوْلٌ مَعْرُوفٌ وَمَغْفِرَةٌ خَيْرٌ مِنْ صَدَقَةٍ يَتْبَعُهَا أَذًى

“A fitting word and forgiveness are better than charity followed by harm.”

Al-Baqarah 2:263

Here, harm does not decide to “follow” charity; it comes after it and accompanies its effect. This use shows that sequence, continuity, and coming afterward belong to the root’s core.

Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic lexical sources, and are used of someone who walks behind another, or when another passes by and the person sets out along with him.

This family is also used for several nearby fields: following a trace or track; catching up with someone who moved earlier; things coming one after another; accompanying and agreeing with someone in an act; pursuing something gradually; and claiming a right that remains attached to a person or property.

The form in Arabic is close to searching and pursuing step by step: a person follows something part by part or trace by trace. This form does not occur in the Qur’an, but it helps clarify the linguistic extension of the root.

The form is also used in Arabic for a consequence, liability, claim, or right that remains after an act or property. This use shows that the image of coming afterward extended from bodily movement to the relation between an act and its consequence.

Lexical sources do not give one single account of the exact difference among , and . Some treat them as overlapping in certain uses, while others give a more prominent sense of catching up, pursuing, or bringing one thing after another.

Each Qur’anic use must therefore be examined within its own context; a mechanical meaning should not be imposed on it from morphological form alone.

History of Understanding, Restriction, or Semantic Confusion

One common restriction is to translate always and only as “obedience.” In some contexts that may convey part of the result, but it reduces the image of moving after a person or path.

For example:

فَاتَّبِعُوهُ وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا السُّبُلَ

“Then follow it, and do not follow the other ways.”

Al-Anʿam 6:153

The image of one way and several ways is present in this ayah. “Obey” by itself does not convey this spatial and directional structure well.

Another restriction is to make always mean “caused to follow.” This form is causative in some ayat, but in others the subject itself goes after another:

فَأَتْبَعَهُمْ فِرْعَوْنُ

“Then Pharaoh went after them.”

Nor should always be treated as conscious and approved following. The Qur’an uses the same verb for following desire, assumption, passion, Satan, falsehood, and Pharaoh’s command.

Conversely, following the Messenger in the Qur’an should not be reduced merely to bodily accompaniment or personal attachment. In Al-Aʿraf 7:157, following the Messenger is placed beside following the light sent down with him:

الَّذِينَ يَتَّبِعُونَ الرَّسُولَ النَّبِيَّ الْأُمِّيَّ

And then:

وَاتَّبَعُوا النُّورَ الَّذِي أُنْزِلَ مَعَهُ

This pairing places following the Messenger in relation to the message and the sent-down light. Yet the root by itself does not resolve every question concerning the Messenger’s authority; that discussion must be examined from the ayat as a whole.

Methodological Note

Several kinds of relation must be distinguished when examining this root.

First is , such as following guidance, revelation, a messenger, a way, desire, or assumption. In this use, a person places something ahead as a forerunner.

Second is , such as Pharaoh and his forces moving after Moses and those with him.

Third is , such as a later event coming after the first or two months placed consecutively.

Fourth is , such as harm that follows charity or a curse placed after a people.

Fifth is , as with and , where the root is not limited to personal or doctrinal following.

These uses extend from one shared image, but they should not all be covered by one fixed translation.

The ethical value of ittibaʿ also does not come from the verb itself. What is followed and the direction of following determine whether it guides or misleads.

Qur’anic Uses of the Root

Following Guidance

فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُمْ مِنِّي هُدًى فَمَنْ تَبِعَ هُدَايَ فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

“Then if guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows My guidance—no fear will be upon them, nor will they grieve.”

Al-Baqarah 2:38

Guidance in this ayah is not something merely received or affirmed; the human being must take a place after it. The verb forms a relation between the coming of guidance and human movement along its course.

The same structure appears in Ta-Ha:

فَمَنِ اتَّبَعَ هُدَايَ فَلَا يَضِلُّ وَلَا يَشْقَىٰ

“Then whoever follows My guidance will neither go astray nor reach misery.”

Ta-Ha 20:123

Following guidance here stands against dalal. Guidance shows the direction; ittibaʿ is human movement in that direction.

Following What Has Been Sent Down

اتَّبِعُوا مَا أُنْزِلَ إِلَيْكُمْ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا مِنْ دُونِهِ أَوْلِيَاءَ

“Follow what has been sent down to you from your Rabb, and do not follow protectors or authorities apart from Him.”

Al-Aʿraf 7:3

The first object is : what has been sent down to you. In contrast stands following awliya apart from Him. The ayah places what has been sent down as the criterion of movement.

The same contrast appears concerning inherited ancestral ways:

وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمُ اتَّبِعُوا مَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ قَالُوا بَلْ نَتَّبِعُ مَا وَجَدْنَا عَلَيْهِ آبَاءَنَا

“And when it is said to them, ‘Follow what The God has sent down,’ they say, ‘Rather, we follow what we found our fathers upon.’”

Luqman 31:21

The ayah does not reject everything received from earlier generations merely because it is old. The contrast concerns the final criterion: does what was sent down stand ahead, or has inherited practice taken its place?

Following the Book, Reminder, Shariʿah, and Way

The Qur’an does not restrict the object of ittibaʿ to one form:

وَهَٰذَا كِتَابٌ أَنْزَلْنَاهُ مُبَارَكٌ فَاتَّبِعُوهُ

“And this is a blessed Book We have sent down; so follow it.”

Al-Anʿam 6:155

إِنَّمَا تُنْذِرُ مَنِ اتَّبَعَ الذِّكْرَ

“You warn only the one who follows the Reminder.”

Ya-Sin 36:11

ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَاكَ عَلَىٰ شَرِيعَةٍ مِنَ الْأَمْرِ فَاتَّبِعْهَا

“Then We placed you upon a clear way from the command; so follow it.”

Al-Jathiyah 45:18

وَاتَّبِعْ سَبِيلَ مَنْ أَنَابَ إِلَيَّ

“And follow the way of one who turns back to Me.”

Luqman 31:15

In each case, something is placed as a path, direction, or forerunner, and the addressee is told to align movement with it.

Following the Messenger, Messengers, and Those Sent

الَّذِينَ يَتَّبِعُونَ الرَّسُولَ النَّبِيَّ الْأُمِّيَّ

“Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered prophet.”

Al-Aʿraf 7:157

The same ayah says:

وَاتَّبَعُوا النُّورَ الَّذِي أُنْزِلَ مَعَهُ

“And they followed the light that was sent down with him.”

These two phrases place following the Messenger and following the sent-down light within one structure. The Messenger carries and points to a path illuminated by the light sent down.

Ya-Sin likewise says:

اتَّبِعُوا الْمُرْسَلِينَ ۝ اتَّبِعُوا مَنْ لَا يَسْأَلُكُمْ أَجْرًا وَهُمْ مُهْتَدُونَ

“Follow those sent; follow those who ask no payment from you and are themselves guided.”

Ya-Sin 36:20–21

In this context, not asking payment and being guided are signs that clarify the fitness of those being followed.

Following the Prophet Beside Obedience

فَاتَّبِعُونِي وَأَطِيعُوا أَمْرِي

“Then follow me and obey my command.”

Ta-Ha 20:90

Their placement together shows two related angles. Ittibaʿ creates an image of movement after the person; obedience focuses on accepting the person’s command.

Another ayah says:

أَلَّا تَتَّبِعَنِ أَفَعَصَيْتَ أَمْرِي

“Why did you not follow me? Did you disobey my command?”

Ta-Ha 20:93

Failure to follow is linked in context with disobeying the command, while the two verbs retain their distinct structures.

Following Satan and His Footsteps

وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا خُطُوَاتِ الشَّيْطَانِ

“And do not follow the footsteps of Satan.”

Al-Baqarah 2:168

The expression strengthens the root’s movement-based image. Satan is pictured as one who lays out a course or moves ahead, and the human being is prohibited from placing one foot after those footsteps.

The expression by itself does not prove that every form of misguidance necessarily develops gradually in a psychological sense, but the image of footsteps contains the possibility of movement by stages.

Another ayah makes Satan himself the object:

وَيَتَّبِعُ كُلَّ شَيْطَانٍ مَرِيدٍ

“And he follows every rebellious satan.”

Al-Hajj 22:3

Here, a person directly takes a misleading being or force as the one to be followed.

Following Desire and Passions

وَلَا تَتَّبِعِ الْهَوَىٰ فَيُضِلَّكَ عَنْ سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ

“And do not follow desire, lest it lead you away from the way of The God.”

Sad 38:26

In this structure, desire takes the position ahead. Instead of measuring inclination against truth, the person receives direction from inclination.

فَإِنْ لَمْ يَسْتَجِيبُوا لَكَ فَاعْلَمْ أَنَّمَا يَتَّبِعُونَ أَهْوَاءَهُمْ

“Then if they do not respond to you, know that they only follow their own desires.”

Al-Qasas 28:50

And:

فَخَلَفَ مِنْ بَعْدِهِمْ خَلْفٌ أَضَاعُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَاتَّبَعُوا الشَّهَوَاتِ

“Then successors came after them who neglected salat and followed passions.”

Maryam 19:59

and are not one word, but in both cases an inward pull takes the position of forerunner.

Following Assumption

إِنْ يَتَّبِعُونَ إِلَّا الظَّنَّ وَمَا تَهْوَى الْأَنْفُسُ

“They follow nothing except assumption and what the selves desire.”

An-Najm 53:23

Assumption and the inclination of the selves are placed together. Assumption becomes the path of movement in place of knowledge and guidance.

إِنْ يَتَّبِعُونَ إِلَّا الظَّنَّ وَإِنَّ الظَّنَّ لَا يُغْنِي مِنَ الْحَقِّ شَيْئًا

“They follow nothing except assumption, while assumption does not suffice against truth in anything.”

An-Najm 53:28

The issue is not merely that assumption exists in the mind; the issue is placing it in the position of criterion and forerunner.

Following Truth and Falsehood

ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا اتَّبَعُوا الْبَاطِلَ وَأَنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّبَعُوا الْحَقَّ مِنْ رَبِّهِمْ

“That is because those who committed kufr followed falsehood, while those who had iman followed the truth from their Rabb.”

Muhammad 47:3

This ayah clearly shows that the act of following is neither positive nor negative in itself. The difference lies in what is followed: falsehood or truth from their Rabb.

Following the Command and Way of Leaders

فَاتَّبَعُوا أَمْرَ فِرْعَوْنَ وَمَا أَمْرُ فِرْعَوْنَ بِرَشِيدٍ

“Then they followed Pharaoh’s command, while Pharaoh’s command was not rightly directed.”

Hud 11:97

Here, is placed ahead. The followers did not merely admire Pharaoh; they followed his direction and command.

وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّبِعُوا سَبِيلَنَا

“And those who committed kufr said to those who had iman, ‘Follow our way.’”

Al-ʿAnkabut 29:12

Ittibaʿ can express attachment to a person, a command, or a way that person presents.

The Relation Between Those Followed and Those Who Followed

إِذْ تَبَرَّأَ الَّذِينَ اتُّبِعُوا مِنَ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوا وَرَأَوُا الْعَذَابَ وَتَقَطَّعَتْ بِهِمُ الْأَسْبَابُ

“When those who were followed disown those who followed, and they see the punishment, and the bonds among them are cut.”

Al-Baqarah 2:166

The ayah makes a clear distinction between two forms:

: those who were followed.

: those who followed.

In this world, there was a relation of leadership, influence, and following between them. At judgment, the forerunners disown the followers, and the bonds that sustained the relation are cut.

وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوا لَوْ أَنَّ لَنَا كَرَّةً فَنَتَبَرَّأَ مِنْهُمْ

“And those who followed say, ‘If only we had a return, then we would disown them.’”

Al-Baqarah 2:167

The ayah does not remove the follower’s responsibility merely because a forerunner existed. Following is itself a direction and choice.

Tabaʿ: Followers in Relation to Forerunners

إِنَّا كُنَّا لَكُمْ تَبَعًا فَهَلْ أَنْتُمْ مُغْنُونَ عَنَّا مِنْ عَذَابِ اللَّهِ مِنْ شَيْءٍ

“We were followers of yours; can you now ward anything of the punishment of The God away from us?”

Ibrahim 14:21

here refers to the body of followers. They turn to those who had stood ahead of them in this world, but the relation of following cannot transfer or erase responsibility.

The same construction appears in Ghafir 40:47.

Following With Ihsan

وَالَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوهُمْ بِإِحْسَانٍ

“And those who followed them with ihsan.”

At-Tawbah 9:100

The qualifier specifies the quality of following. The ayah does not treat every form of attaching oneself to earlier people as equal; the praised following is accompanied by ihsan.

Yet in this phrase does not lexically mean “criticizing” or “independent evaluation.” Its presence shows that the quality and manner of following matter, but the full meaning of ihsan should not be derived from alone.

Physical Pursuit

فَأَتْبَعُوهُمْ مُشْرِقِينَ

“Then at sunrise they went after them.”

Ash-Shuʿara 26:60

فَأَتْبَعَهُمْ فِرْعَوْنُ بِجُنُودِهِ

“Then Pharaoh went after them with his forces.”

Ta-Ha 20:78

In these uses, spatial and movement-based pursuit is clear. Pharaoh and those with him move behind a group that has already gone away from them.

Following a Means or Route

فَأَتْبَعَ سَبَبًا

“Then he followed a means or route.”

Al-Kahf 18:85

This expression occurs three times in the account of Dhu al-Qarnayn. can include a route, means, or possibility of access. The verb shows that he took that means and moved along it.

This use should not be translated only as “pursuing an enemy”; its object is not a person but a .

A Human Being Followed by Satan or a Piercing Flame

فَأَتْبَعَهُ الشَّيْطَانُ فَكَانَ مِنَ الْغَاوِينَ

“Then Satan went after him, and he became one of those who went astray.”

Al-Aʿraf 7:175

Here, Satan is the subject and the human being is the object of pursuit. The expression can convey Satan catching up, clinging, or going after him.

فَأَتْبَعَهُ شِهَابٌ ثَاقِبٌ

“Then a piercing flame follows him.”

As-Saffat 37:10

Again, something comes after something else and follows it.

An Act Followed by Its Consequence

ثُمَّ لَا يُتْبِعُونَ مَا أَنْفَقُوا مَنًّا وَلَا أَذًى

“Then they do not follow what they spent with reminders of generosity or harm.”

Al-Baqarah 2:262

Reminders and harm can come after spending and attach themselves to it. Here the root expresses a relation between an act and a later consequence.

وَأُتْبِعُوا فِي هَٰذِهِ الدُّنْيَا لَعْنَةً وَيَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ

“And in this world a curse was made to follow them, and on the Day of Resurrection as well.”

Hud 11:60

In this passive construction, the curse is presented as a consequence placed after them.

Al-Qasas 28:42 uses an active construction:

وَأَتْبَعْنَاهُمْ فِي هَٰذِهِ الدُّنْيَا لَعْنَةً

“And in this world We placed a curse after them.”

The active and passive constructions both show the relation between a people and a consequence that comes after them and remains attached to them.

Temporal Succession

فَصِيَامُ شَهْرَيْنِ مُتَتَابِعَيْنِ

“Then fasting two consecutive months.”

An-Nisa 4:92

indicates that the two months are intended to come one after another without interruption.

Here, the root concerns neither following a person nor pursuing an enemy; temporal relation and continuity between two units are prominent.

Ittibaʿ as Pursuing a Right

فَاتِّبَاعٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَأَدَاءٌ إِلَيْهِ بِإِحْسَانٍ

“Then pursuit should be in a recognized and fitting manner, and payment to him with ihsan.”

Al-Baqarah 2:178

In this ayah, concerns pursuing and claiming a right. The person entitled to the right should pursue it according to maʿruf, while the other party should pay it with ihsan.

The general translation “following” is therefore unsuitable here. “Pursuit,” “claiming in a fitting manner,” or “following up the right according to maʿruf” is closer to context.

Important Qur’anic Derivatives

The Difference Between تَبِعَ and اتَّبَعَ

Both forms can indicate following and going after.

is the root’s simpler form. In the Qur’an it occurs both for voluntary following:

فَمَنْ تَبِعَ هُدَايَ

and for something coming after without volition:

تَتْبَعُهَا الرَّادِفَةُ

has a very wide range and is used for following guidance, a messenger, desire, assumption, falsehood, truth, a way, and people.

It cannot be said that is in every case more conscious or deeper than . Such a difference may be felt in some contexts, but it does not emerge as a fixed rule from every Qur’anic use.

The Difference Between أَتْبَعَ and اتَّبَعَ

In many Qur’anic uses, highlights pursuit or direct and rapid movement after something:

فَأَتْبَعَهُمْ فِرْعَوْنُ

But at times it is causative:

فَأَتْبَعْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ بَعْضًا

“We brought some of them after others.”

In some ayat, its boundary with ordinary following is also narrow:

فَأَتْبَعَ سَبَبًا

Form IV in this root can therefore both transitivize and express the subject’s own movement after something else.

Passive اتُّبِعُوا and Active اتَّبَعُوا

The vocalization in Al-Baqarah 2:166 is very important:

الَّذِينَ اتُّبِعُوا

“Those who were followed.”

In contrast with:

الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوا

“Those who followed.”

Without vowel marks, the difference in vowel and doubling can be missed, but structurally and semantically the phrases identify two entirely different groups.

تَبَع and تَابِع

can be a collective noun or a noun that includes one or more followers:

إِنَّا كُنَّا لَكُمْ تَبَعًا

is the clearer active participle:

وَمَا أَنْتَ بِتَابِعٍ قِبْلَتَهُمْ

The form is a recognized Arabic plural, but it does not occur in the Qur’an.

تَبِيع (tabīʿ)

comes from the image of someone going after a matter. In Al-Isra 17:69, claimant, pursuer, or one who demands accountability is more suitable than an ordinary “follower.”

This use shows that pursuing a right and pursuing a consequence also lie within the root’s field.

مُتَتَابِعَيْن

Form VI here shows a reciprocal or consecutive relation between two units. Two months stand one after another.

Suitable translations include “consecutive,” “continuous,” or “one after another,” not “two following months” in the sense of followers.

اتِّبَاع

The verbal noun points to the act of following, while its object and context determine the precise meaning.

In:

إِلَّا اتِّبَاعَ الظَّنِّ

it means following assumption.

In:

فَاتِّبَاعٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

it means pursuing and claiming according to maʿruf.

مُتَّبَعُون

is a passive participle, not “followers”:

إِنَّكُمْ مُتَّبَعُونَ

It means “you will be pursued” or “they will come after you.”

This form should not be confused with , “those who follow.”

Grammatical and Structural Notes

The Importance of the Verb’s Object

With the root , the verb’s object or complement is very important for understanding its precise meaning:

: followed guidance.

: followed the Messenger.

: followed what was sent down.

: placed desire ahead as the forerunner.

: made assumption the criterion of movement.

: took a way and continued along it.

: placed step after step.

The verb itself does not change fundamentally; the object clarifies the kind of path.

اتَّبَعَ With a Direct Object

In most uses, what is followed comes without a preposition and as a direct object:

اتَّبِعُوا مَا أُنْزِلَ

وَلَا تَتَّبِعِ الْهَوَىٰ

اتَّبَعُوا الْحَقَّ

This structure shows direct proximity between follower and what is followed.

اتَّبَعَ With عَلَىٰ

In Moses’ conversation with the knowledgeable servant, it says:

هَلْ أَتَّبِعُكَ عَلَىٰ أَنْ تُعَلِّمَنِ

“May I follow you on the basis that you teach me from what you have been taught?”

Al-Kahf 18:66

clarifies the condition, basis, or aim of this accompaniment.

أَتْبَعَ With Two Objects

In constructions such as:

فَأَتْبَعْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ بَعْضًا

one group is placed after another. This construction reveals the causative capacity of form IV.

The same pattern appears in:

وَأَتْبَعْنَاهُمْ فِي هَٰذِهِ الدُّنْيَا لَعْنَةً

A curse is placed after them.

Passive أُتْبِعُوا

وَأُتْبِعُوا فِي هَٰذِهِ الدُّنْيَا لَعْنَةً

The agent is not mentioned in the passive construction; the focus is on the people after whom the curse came.

بِإِحْسَانٍ and بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

The qualifiers and prepositional phrases clarify the quality of the act:

اتَّبَعُوهُمْ بِإِحْسَانٍ

Following with ihsan.

فَاتِّبَاعٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

Pursuit according to maʿruf.

The presence of in both constructions does not make the two uses identical. One concerns following earlier people, while the other concerns pursuing a right.

لَا تَتَّبِعُوا السُّبُلَ

وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا السُّبُلَ فَتَفَرَّقَ بِكُمْ عَنْ سَبِيلِهِ

“And do not follow the ways, lest they scatter you away from His way.”

Al-Anʿam 6:153

The plural stands against the singular . Following multiple ways is connected with being scattered from the principal way. The path image should be preserved in translation.

Comparison With Nearby Roots

T B ʿ Compared With T W ʿ: Ittibaʿ and Obedience

The root moves in the field of accepting, being pliant and ready, and carrying out a command without compulsion. Obedience focuses more on a relation with command and compliance.

preserves the image of taking a position after a person, way, or direction.

A person who follows a Messenger may also obey the Messenger’s command, but linguistically one emphasizes movement along the course of a forerunner, while the other emphasizes acceptance of that person’s command.

T B ʿ Compared With Q F W: Ittibaʿ and Qafw

The root also indicates going after a trace and moving behind something. In:

وَلَا تَقْفُ مَا لَيْسَ لَكَ بِهِ عِلْمٌ

Al-Isra 17:36

the nearby meaning is not following what one has no knowledge of.

more clearly preserves the image of following a trace or track. has a wider field and can include religious, social, intellectual, movement-based, and consequence-based following.

The boundary is not absolute, but qafw is closer to following a trace.

T B ʿ Compared With L H Q: Ittibaʿ and Catching Up

The root indicates reaching someone or something ahead and joining it.

Ittibaʿ can continue before reaching and throughout the movement. A person is after another even if the first has not yet been reached.

Lahaq highlights the moment or result of joining; ittibaʿ highlights the route and relation of following.

T B ʿ Compared With Q D W: Ittibaʿ and Iqtida

The root , in the form , indicates taking something as an example and shaping one’s behavior according to a model or leader.

Ittibaʿ is broader. A person may pursue someone, follow a way, or bring one thing after another without necessarily taking it as a behavioral model.

Iqtida places greater emphasis on emulation and taking a leader as an example.

T B ʿ Compared With S L K: Ittibaʿ and Suluk

The root indicates inserting or entering into a path, passage, or thread and traversing it.

In suluk, the path itself and entry into it are prominent. In ittibaʿ, besides the path, something or someone stands ahead and determines the movement’s direction.

A person may traverse a way, but when the same way, person, or command is taken as forerunner, the field of ittibaʿ becomes more prominent.

T B ʿ Compared With M SH Y: Ittibaʿ and Walking

The root expresses the act of walking and moving itself.

Ittibaʿ is a relation between the one moving and something ahead. Every movement of following may involve walking, but not every act of walking follows something.

T B ʿ Compared With R D F: Ittibaʿ and Ridf

The root indicates coming behind, occupying the rear position, or arriving immediately after something.

In:

تَتْبَعُهَا الرَّادِفَةُ

the two fields stand together: the second event comes after the first and arrives from behind it.

Ridf emphasizes the posterior position and being behind; ittibaʿ emphasizes the relation of coming after or moving along a trace.

T B ʿ Compared With T L W: Ittibaʿ and Tilw

The root indicates coming after something and also reading or reciting. In its primary image, one thing takes a position after another.

Tilw and ittibaʿ overlap in sequence and coming afterward. Yet tilw in the Qur’an has also developed the particular field of reciting ayat and reading in succession; ittibaʿ more often indicates following a person, path, way, intellectual object, or consequence.

T B ʿ Compared With T R D: Ittibaʿ and Pursuit

The root is closer to driving away, expelling, and forcing something to move from behind.

In pursuit with , the subject moves after the target or places something after it. In tard, pressure to drive the target away is more prominent.

T B ʿ Compared With A TH R: Ittibaʿ and Following a Trace

The root concerns a sign, remainder, trace, or mark that something leaves behind.

Ittibaʿ can be following that same trace, but the root focuses on the act of following; athar is what makes such following possible.

Summary

The root indicates following, going after something, moving behind it, and one thing taking a position after another. Its foundational image is a relation between a forerunner and what comes along that person’s or thing’s route, trace, or direction.

In the Qur’an, this root is not used only for religious following or obedience to a person. Guidance, revelation, a book, a messenger, light, reminder, shariʿah, way, truth, falsehood, desire, passion, assumption, command, fathers, and Satan’s footsteps can all be objects of ittibaʿ.

The same root also expresses Pharaoh’s spatial pursuit, a piercing flame coming after something, peoples coming one after another, a curse remaining after a people, harm coming after charity, two consecutive months, and pursuing a right.

The forms and have broad semantic overlap. In many ayat, gives greater prominence to pursuit, catching up, or placing one thing after another, but this distinction is not uniform and mechanical in every context.

is the Qur’anic form for a follower or followers; the familiar plural does not occur in the Qur’an. occurs in the context of pursuit and demand, in the meaning of consecutiveness, and for those who will be pursued.

Ittibaʿ and obedience are also not identical. Obedience emphasizes accepting a command; ittibaʿ emphasizes movement along the course of a person, way, or direction that stands ahead. They can be joined within one relation but should not replace one another in every translation.

The root overlaps with roots such as , and , but its central feature is the follower’s relation to the person, thing, trace, or path that stands ahead.
