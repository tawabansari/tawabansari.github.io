Short Introduction to the Root

The root S B T moves in the semantic field of stopping, ceasing, cutting off the continuation of activity, and leaving work.

This root in the Qur’an is not related only to the Sabbath day. In addition to السَّبْت and the verb يَسْبِتُونَ, the word سُبَاتًا also appears twice about sleep. This use about sleep shows that the core of the root is wider than the name of one day and is connected with cutting off or stopping the flow of activity.

“Resting” may be one result of this stopping, but it is better not to identify the core of the root directly with “rest.” The Qur’an itself distinguishes among the different forms of this family: in one place it speaks of the Sabbath day, in another of sabting, and elsewhere of sleep as subat.

The short meaning of this root may be stated as follows:

stopping, ceasing, cutting activity, leaving work.

Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

The root S B T appears in the Qur’an in three main forms:

السَّبْت / سَبْتِهِم Sabbath; a day or temporal order connected with ceasing from a prescribed activity.

يَسْبِتُونَ They were sabting; a verb that appears in Al-Araf 163 and shows that “Sabbath” is not only the name of a day, but also has an active meaning behind it.

سُبَاتًا Subat; twice about sleep, in Al-Furqan 47 and An-Naba 9.

These three forms are very important for understanding the root, because together they show that the field of S B T goes beyond one historical name and relates to ceasing and cutting off activity itself.

The Semantic Core of the Root

The defensible core of S B T may be stated as follows:

cutting off or stopping the continuation of an activity or flow.

This definition is more precise than “calmness.” Calmness is a state, while sabt focuses originally on the cutting off or stopping of what was in motion.

On the Sabbath day, an activity is stopped.

In the verb yasbitun, a group enters the condition of Sabbath or leaves the prescribed activity.

In nawmukum subatan, sleep is introduced as a condition that stops or cuts off the ordinary activity of the human being.

As a result, the connection between “Sabbath” and “sleep” becomes understandable from the root itself, without having to make Sabbath equal to sleep or rest.

The Material Image of the Root

The simple image of this root is an activity that is continuing and then stops continuing.

A hand is working and then stops working.

Daily movement and occupation stop.

The waking human being is active, and with sleep, a large part of his voluntary and ordinary activity is cut off.

What is shared among these images is not “calmness,” but the breaking of the flow of activity.

This material image is a tool for explanation and should not become a mechanical definition of all derivatives.

Use in Arabic

In Arabic lexical sources, the family S B T is connected with cutting and ceasing. From this field, both the name of the Sabbath day and the expression subat for sleep can be explained.

However, a Qur’anic article should not bring every meaning recorded in dictionaries into the text. The main criterion is the actual usage of the Qur’an.

The Qur’anic uses themselves show that this family has at least three areas:

  • stopping activity on the Sabbath day;
  • the act of sabting;
  • sleep as subat.

Subat: Sleep as Cutting Off Activity

وَهُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ اللَّيْلَ لِبَاسًا وَالنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًا وَجَعَلَ النَّهَارَ نُشُورًا

“And He is the One who made the night a covering for you, and sleep subat, and made the day a time of rising and spreading.”

Al-Furqan, 47

And:

وَجَعَلْنَا نَوْمَكُمْ سُبَاتًا

“And We made your sleep subat.”

An-Naba, 9

These two verses are among the most important witnesses for the core of the root.

Subatan is not sleep itself; sleep is the subject in the sentence, and “subat” is what sleep has been made as.

Therefore, the translation “rest” can convey part of the effect of sleep, but it does not fully show the image of the root. “Cutting off activity” or “ceasing” is closer to the core of the word.

Even so, one should not conclude from this expression that sleep completely stops all bodily activities. The verse is not speaking in biological or medical language. What is clear from the word and human experience is the cutting off and reduction of ordinary and voluntary activity in sleep.

Sabbath and Transgressing the Limit

وَلَقَدْ عَلِمْتُمُ الَّذِينَ اعْتَدَوْا مِنكُمْ فِي السَّبْتِ

“And you certainly knew those among you who transgressed in the Sabbath.”

Al-Baqarah, 65

In this verse, Sabbath itself is not the sin. The verb iʿtadaw expresses transgression, and fi as-sabt specifies the field or time of that transgression.

This distinction is important:

as-sabt: Sabbath.

iʿtadaw: they transgressed.

Thus concepts such as disobedience, sin, and transgression should not be placed inside the lexical meaning of the root S B T.

“Do Not Transgress in the Sabbath”

وَقُلْنَا لَهُمْ لَا تَعْدُوا فِي السَّبْتِ

“And We said to them: do not transgress in the Sabbath.”

An-Nisa, 154

In this structure too, the Sabbath itself is not the object of prohibition; the prohibition comes from la taʿdu.

The verse shows that the Sabbath had a prescribed limit, but all the details of that limit are not obtained from the word Sabbath itself.

This distinction prevents later historical laws of Sabbath from being poured into the base translation of the Qur’an.

The Community Beside the Sea and the Verb “Yasbitun”

وَاسْأَلْهُمْ عَنِ الْقَرْيَةِ الَّتِي كَانَتْ حَاضِرَةَ الْبَحْرِ إِذْ يَعْدُونَ فِي السَّبْتِ إِذْ تَأْتِيهِمْ حِيتَانُهُمْ يَوْمَ سَبْتِهِمْ شُرَّعًا وَيَوْمَ لَا يَسْبِتُونَ لَا تَأْتِيهِمْ

“And ask them about the town that was beside the sea, when they were transgressing in the Sabbath; when their fish came to them openly on the day of their Sabbath, and on the day they did not sabt, they did not come to them.”

Al-Araf, 163

This verse brings three forms of one family side by side:

فِي السَّبْتِ in the Sabbath.

يَوْمَ سَبْتِهِم the day of their Sabbath.

لَا يَسْبِتُونَ they did not sabt.

The verb yasbitun is an important witness showing that “Sabbath” has an active backing and is not merely the calendar name of a day.

Still, yasbitun should not be translated without evidence as “they stopped all kinds of work.” The verse itself does not name the exact activity inside this verb. The safer meaning is: they were in the condition of Sabbath / they were sabting / they were ceasing from the activity connected with Sabbath.

“The People of the Sabbath”

أَوْ نَلْعَنَهُمْ كَمَا لَعَنَّا أَصْحَابَ السَّبْتِ

“Or We curse them as We cursed the people of the Sabbath.”

An-Nisa, 47

In this construction, ashab as-sabt is a name for a group known through the event of Sabbath.

But Sabbath is not the name of a people. “Ashab” specifies the group, and “Sabbath” is the event or temporal order through which they became known.

“The Sabbath Was Only Made...”

إِنَّمَا جُعِلَ السَّبْتُ عَلَى الَّذِينَ اخْتَلَفُوا فِيهِ

“The Sabbath was only made upon those who differed about it.”

An-Nahl, 124

In this verse, juʿila shows that Sabbath was placed upon a group as something prescribed.

From this verse one cannot extract the whole history or all details of Sabbath rulings, but it is clear that the Qur’an leaves a real distinction between the lexical meaning of the root and its legislative and historical form.

The root is connected with stopping and ceasing; for whom and with what limits this ceasing was prescribed is obtained from the legislative context.

Exceptional Section: Comparison With Hebrew

In the case of S B T, comparison with Hebrew has special value, because the same three-letter structure ש־ב־ת in Hebrew is also connected with ceasing from work.

In Genesis 2:2, after the completion of the work of creation, the verb וַיִּשְׁבֹּת appears; that is, he ceased from the work that was being done. In the next verse, the cognate form שָׁבַת appears again in connection with ceasing from work.

In texts related to the day of Shabbat, the word שַׁבָּת also appears with the command not to do work. Therefore, in Hebrew too a clear connection is seen between the name of the day and the concept of ceasing from activity.

This Semitic data has two benefits for Forqan:

First, it shows that the connection of “Sabbath/Shabbat” with ceasing from work is not limited to a Persian analysis or later impression.

Second, it helps prevent “Shabbat/Sabbath” from being treated as a meaningless name of a day.

But Hebrew must not determine the meaning of the Qur’anic verses. Its role is only historical comparison and showing a shared semantic field. The final meaning of each verse is extracted from Arabic and the context of the Qur’an itself.

Sabbath and Resting

“Sabbath” and “resting” are close to one another, but they are not identical.

When activity stops, calmness and rest may result. But the result of an act is not the same as the meaning of the act itself.

This difference is also seen in Semitic comparison: Hebrew texts may use different verbs for “ceasing” and “resting.” Therefore, the automatic translation Sabbath = rest removes part of the semantic structure.

For the Forqan article, it is better to keep:

Sabbath ← ceasing, cutting activity.

resting / repose ← a result or contextual translation where the text requires it.

In subatan too, “rest” is a smooth translation, but in the explanation of the root it must be made clear that the foundational image is connected with cutting off and stopping activity.

Comparison With Nearby Roots

S B T Compared With Q T A: Sabbath and Cutting

Qataʿ focuses on cutting, separating, or severing the continuation of something.

Sabt in Qur’anic usage leans toward stopping or ceasing the flow of activity.

These two fields overlap, but they are not complete synonyms. One may say that “cutting” has the more general image of severing, while “sabt” in the Qur’an is connected specifically with stopping activity and the Sabbath day.

S B T Compared With N W M: Subat and Sleep

Nawm is sleep itself.

Subat is a description or state by which sleep is introduced.

In:

وَجَعَلْنَا نَوْمَكُمْ سُبَاتًا

if these two words were complete synonyms, the structure of the verse would lose an important part of its precision. “Sleep” is the subject, and “subat” is the quality or function expressed for it.

S B T Compared With R A H / Rest

The field of rest focuses more on a state of ease.

Sabt focuses on stopping action.

Rest may be the consequence of ceasing, but the two should not replace one another in every use.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

فِي السَّبْتِ

In:

اعْتَدَوْا فِي السَّبْتِ

fi as-sabt specifies the field or time of the act. Transgression comes from the verb iʿtadaw.

يَوْمَ سَبْتِهِم

In:

يَوْمَ سَبْتِهِم

Sabbath is attached to their pronoun: “the day of their Sabbath.”

This genitive attachment shows Sabbath’s connection with that community, not that the lexical meaning of the word has changed.

لَا يَسْبِتُونَ

In this structure, the imperfect verb from this family appears. This verb is the most important witness for the living verbal meaning of the root in the Qur’an.

The translation “non-Sabbath day” conveys the temporal result of the phrase, but it hides the linguistic relation between Sabbath / yasbitun.

نَوْمَكُمْ سُبَاتًا

In:

وَجَعَلْنَا نَوْمَكُمْ سُبَاتًا

nawmukum is the first object of jaʿalna, and subatan in the sentence structure is what sleep has been made as.

Semantically, this structure does not allow subat to be taken merely as a direct synonym of nawm.

Challenges of Understanding and Rendering

The first error is that Sabbath is always translated only as “Saturday” and the living meaning of the root becomes hidden.

“Saturday” in English is the name of a day of the week, but by itself it does not show its connection with stopping activity.

The second error is fully equating Sabbath with “rest.” Rest can be the result of sabt; the root core is closer to stopping.

The third error is ignoring subatan. This derivative shows that the root has been used outside the story of the Children of Israel too, and that the field of stopping and cutting activity is also active concerning sleep.

The fourth error is turning a biological interpretation of sleep into the meaning of the root. The Qur’an does not say that all functions of the body stop in sleep; therefore the article should not make such a claim either.

The fifth error is inserting all historical Jewish laws into the meaning of as-sabt. The details of rulings are not obtained from the root itself.

The sixth error is using Hebrew as a ruling authority over the Qur’an. Hebrew is only a comparative witness.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For السَّبْت:

“Sabbath”

In the explanation of the first use:

“Sabbath; the day of ceasing from prescribed activity.”

For:

اعْتَدَوْا فِي السَّبْتِ

“They transgressed in the Sabbath.”

For:

يَوْمَ سَبْتِهِم

“The day of their Sabbath.”

For:

لَا يَسْبِتُونَ

“They did not sabt.”

And in explanation:

“They were not in the condition of Sabbath that day / they were not ceasing from the related activity.”

For:

وَجَعَلْنَا نَوْمَكُمْ سُبَاتًا

The base translation may be:

“And We made your sleep a means of ceasing.”

Or, in a smoother translation:

“And We made your sleep a means of repose.”

In the second case, the root explanation must clarify that “repose” is the result of the field of stopping and ceasing.

Short Root Meaning for Forqan

Short root meaning: stopping, ceasing, cutting activity, leaving work.

“Resting” is better placed in the second level as a result or contextual translation.

Conclusion

The root S B T in the Qur’an moves around the axis of stopping and ceasing from the continuation of activity.

This core is not obtained only from the story of the Sabbath day. The Qur’an twice calls sleep subatan, and this use shows that the field of the root is alive independently of the name of the day.

In the story of the Children of Israel, as-sabt is the name of a prescribed day or order, yasbitun shows the verb connected with it, and iʿtadaw expresses transgression of the limit. These elements should not be confused with one another.

In the use about sleep, nawm is sleep itself and subat is the stopping or cutting of activity by which sleep is described. Therefore “rest” may be a smooth translation, but it does not by itself represent the lexical core.

The exceptional comparison with Hebrew also strengthens the ancient connection between this Semitic family and ceasing from work, without making Hebrew rule over the meaning of the Qur’an.

The final definition of this root may be stated as follows:

S B T expresses stopping, ceasing, or cutting off the continuation of activity. This field is seen in the Qur’an in the Sabbath of the Children of Israel, in the verb “yasbitun,” and in “subat” as a description of sleep.
