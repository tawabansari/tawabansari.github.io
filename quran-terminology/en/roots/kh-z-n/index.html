Keeping, safeguarding, and administering what is entrusted: from earthly storehouses to divine treasuries and the keepers of the Hereafter

The Arabic root خ ز ن concerns keeping, safeguarding, and placing something somewhere to prevent loss or uncontrolled access. خَزْن is the act of keeping, خِزَانَة a repository, and خَازِن the person responsible for safeguarding or administering it.

In daily Arabic life, this family concerned storing wealth, food, and goods, but was not restricted to physical objects. It also described keeping secrets, withholding words, and watching what one says.

A common concept runs through these usages: something is available or accessible, but is kept to preserve it, prevent dispersal, or manage access to it.

Custody, ownership, and unrestricted power to consume or distribute are nevertheless distinct. Someone may store their own wealth; another may guard public or governmental property without personally owning it. A place’s keeper likewise does not necessarily decide its occupants’ fate.

This distinction becomes particularly important in the Qur’anic usages.

The Qur’an mentions خَزَائِنِ الْأَرْضِ in Joseph’s story, خَزَائِنِ اللَّهِ, and خَزَائِنِ رَحْمَةِ رَبِّكَ. Al-Ḥijr concerns the treasuries of things and human storage of water. In passages about Hell and Paradise, خَزَنَة are the keepers responsible for those places.

These share a semantic family but differ in subject and scope. Joseph’s earthly storehouses cannot be equated with the Lord’s unlimited treasuries; shared root letters likewise do not make a keeper of Paradise or Hell its independent owner.

1. Root Forms and Basic Meaning

خ ز ن occurs thirteen times in the Qur’an in three main forms: eight occurrences of خَزَائِن, four of خَزَنَة, and one of خَازِنِين.

خَزَائِن is the plural of خِزَانَة, denoting treasuries or repositories.

خَزَنَة is the plural of خَازِن, describing those responsible for guarding and keeping.

خَازِنِين is also a sound masculine plural of خَازِن. In Al-Ḥijr, however, it concerns human custody of water, rather than naming a group of keepers in the Hereafter.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: eight khazāʾin, four khazanah, and one khāzinīn

Other important Arabic forms include:

  • خَزَنَ ـ يَخْزُنُ: kept, safeguarded, or placed something in a repository.
  • خَزْن: keeping and storage.
  • خِزَانَة: a place for keeping wealth, goods, or valuables; also the responsibility or work of a treasurer.
  • خَزَائِن: plural of treasury; repositories or stored resources.
  • خَازِن: keeper, guard, or person responsible for a treasury.
  • خَزَنَة: plural of keeper; guards or custodians.
  • خُزَّان: another plural of خَازِن.
  • مَخْزَن: a place for storing goods and property.
  • مَخْزُون: something kept or stored.
  • اِخْتَزَنَ: stored something or kept it for oneself.
  • اِسْتَخْزَنَ: in one usage, asked another to keep something.
  • خَزِين: in an early usage, something stored; in a different branch, food or meat whose smell has changed.

These are recorded in lexical sources, but do not all occur in the Qur’an.

Semantic Core

Ibn Fāris writes in Maqāyīs al-Lughah:

الْخَاءُ وَالزَّايُ وَالنُّونُ أَصْلٌ يَدُلُّ عَلَى صِيَانَةِ الشَّيْءِ.

al-khāʾu wa-al-zāyu wa-al-nūnu aṣlun yadullu ʿalā ṣiyānati al-shayʾi

The root denotes safeguarding and taking care of something.

He illustrates this with keeping money and preserving a secret.

Lisān al-ʿArab also states:

خَزَنَ الشَّيْءَ يَخْزُنُهُ خَزْنًا وَاخْتَزَنَهُ: أَحْرَزَهُ وَجَعَلَهُ فِي خِزَانَةٍ.

khazana al-shayʾa yakhzunuhu khaznan wa-ikhtazanahu aḥrazahu wa-jaʿalahu fī khizānatin

That is, keeping something, securing it, and placing it in a repository.

Both definitions emphasize protection and custody.

Keeping something does not necessarily mean hiding it.

Food may be stored in a known warehouse. Everyone knows it exists, but its contents are preserved against loss and for use at a suitable time.

Conversely, someone may keep a secret inwardly and not wish others to know it.

Concealment may therefore characterize a treasury, but is not essential to every usage.

Nor does خَزْن necessarily mean permanently refusing to give or consume. Something can be kept for future use and distributed when needed.

The basic meaning may therefore be stated as:

Keeping and safeguarding something in a particular custody or place, with its access, preservation, or use under care and administration.

Particular usages may emphasize the store itself, its place, or the person responsible.

Maqāyīs al-Lughah and Lisān al-ʿArab: safeguarding, treasury, and keeper

2. Keeping Wealth and Secrets: Early Arabic Witnesses

Treasuries and Storehouses in Daily Life

Arabic خَزَنْتُ الْمَالَ means keeping wealth.

Someone may put money, a crop, or valuable goods somewhere for use when needed.

That place is called خِزَانَة or مَخْزَن.

A treasury need not be a closed box or a room with a lock: it can concern a place for collecting and administering considerable resources.

This matters in Joseph’s story, where خَزَائِنِ الْأَرْضِ concerns resources and property to be managed, rather than necessarily a few small boxes or rooms.

خَزَنَ itself does not judge property’s quantity, value, or lawful origin.

Someone may keep their own wealth for the future, administer public property under a particular responsibility, or wrongly conceal something from its rightful holders.

All three involve keeping, but their ethical and legal value differs.

A Pre-Islamic Witness: Guarding the Tongue in Imruʾ al-Qays’s Poetry

Imruʾ al-Qays says in a verse from his collected poetry:

إِذَا الْمَرْءُ لَمْ يَخْزُنْ عَلَيْهِ لِسَانُهُ
فَلَيْسَ عَلَى شَيْءٍ سِوَاهُ بِخَزَّانِ

idhā al-marʾu lam yakhzun ʿalayhi lisānuhu / fa-laysa ʿalā shayʾin siwāhu bi-khazzāni

Translation:

“If a person’s tongue does not keep speech safe for him, he will be no keeper of anything else.”

يَخْزُنْ here concerns the tongue.

What is kept is neither money nor physical goods, but speech and what should be safeguarded in speaking.

This shows an early Arabic extension from physical things to preserving speech and secrets.

Just as wealth may be protected from theft or destruction, words can be kept back from inappropriate disclosure.

The comparison does not mean that every statement should remain hidden.

Trustworthiness may require preserving a personal secret; elsewhere, concealing truth or testimony can be improper.

خَزْن itself therefore does not validate every concealment.

Imruʾ al-Qays’s verse concerns careful speech, rather than a universal command to remain silent before every truth.

The verse appears in his collected poetry and early works such as Ibn Qutaybah’s Al-Shiʿr wa-al-Shuʿarāʾ and Ibn Durayd’s Jamharat al-Lughah.

Its ending varies between بِخَزَّانِ and بِخَازِنِ. The former appears in the collected poetry and some early reports; Lisān al-ʿArab records the latter.

Both concern keeping and care, but differ morphologically: خَزَّان is intensive, while خَازِن is an active participle. Reports also differ in the verb’s vocalization and the case of لسانه; their shared theme is guarding the tongue and speech.

Al-Thaʿālibī’s Lubāb al-Ādāb: the witness attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays and its grammatical readings

خَزَنْتُ السِّرَّ: Keeping a Secret

Lexical sources record:

خَزَنْتُ السِّرَّ

I preserved or concealed the secret.

This clarifies that what is kept need not be physical or tangible.

A secret is knowledge or speech someone can retain.

Lisān al-ʿArab metaphorically calls a person’s heart a treasury and their tongue its keeper.

The image is understandable: people retain some knowledge and feelings inwardly, and speech can reveal or conceal them.

It does not medically or physically define the heart as a storehouse of all human information.

This is a linguistic and ethical comparison.

Nor does it establish that Qur’anic خَزَائِنُ اللَّهِ must exclusively mean secrets or hidden knowledge. Each verse’s object must be examined separately.

A Different Usage: Meat Changing in Smell

Lexical sources also record:

خَزِنَ اللَّحْمُ

The meat’s smell changed, or it spoiled.

Though visually close to خَزْن, Ibn Fāris separates it from safeguarding.

He relates it to خَنِزَ and treats it as a case of rearranged root letters.

Ṭarafah ibn al-ʿAbd says:

ثُمَّ لَا يَخْزَنُ فِينَا لَحْمُهَا
إِنَّمَا يَخْزَنُ لَحْمُ الْمُدَّخِرْ

thumma lā yakhzanu fīnā laḥmuhā / innamā yakhzanu laḥmu al-muddakhir

Translation:

“Its meat does not remain among us until it spoils; only the hoarder’s stored meat goes bad.”

The poet praises generosity and consumption: meat is given away or eaten rather than remaining for a long time.

This literary image does not establish that every stored piece of meat necessarily spoils.

The comparison concerns generosity and prolonged food storage.

This verse also varies. Some editions of Ṭarafah’s collected poetry record يَحْزُنُ, while dictionaries quote يَخْزَنُ.

The meat-odor sense is therefore recorded, but this verse alone cannot settle the original reading.

It does not occur in the Qur’an, and spoilage must not be transferred to خَزَائِن, خَازِنِين, or خَزَنَة.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ, Lisān al-ʿArab, and Maqāyīs: Ṭarafah’s witness and meat changing in smell

3. خَزَائِنِ الْأَرْضِ: Responsibility for Resources in Joseph’s Story

قَالَ اجْعَلْنِي عَلَىٰ خَزَائِنِ الْأَرْضِ إِنِّي حَفِيظٌ عَلِيمٌ

qāla ijʿalnī ʿalā khazāʾini al-arḍi innī ḥafīẓun ʿalīmun

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“He said: Appoint me over this land’s storehouses; I am a knowledgeable keeper.”

Yūsuf 12:55

خَزَائِنِ occurs in خَزَائِنِ الْأَرْضِ.

This is a particularly clear earthly and administrative usage.

What Are the Land’s Storehouses?

Earlier passages concern the king’s dream, seven fertile years, and seven difficult years.

In Yūsuf 12:47, Joseph advises cultivating during the fertile years and leaving much of the crop in its ears.

Yūsuf 12:48 describes the difficult years consuming the earlier reserves.

His request in Yūsuf 12:55 therefore belongs to the administration of a land’s resources and stores.

They may include crops, food reserves, and resources needed to survive the difficult years.

خَزَائِنِ الْأَرْضِ itself nevertheless does not restrict them to wheat or one particular crop.

الْأَرْض here concerns the land where Joseph is present and requests administration of its storehouses.

It should not be taken without evidence as all the planet’s treasuries.

Nor does the word establish that Joseph personally owned all that land’s property.

اجْعَلْنِي عَلَىٰ: Administrative Authority, Not Necessarily Ownership

اِجْعَلْنِي requests appointment to a particular position.

عَلَىٰ خَزَائِنِ الْأَرْضِ shows Joseph seeking responsibility or supervision over the stores.

عَلَىٰ here relates to administration and oversight.

It does not establish a transfer to his personal ownership.

The financial distinction is clear.

A government or institution’s treasurer may receive, safeguard, and distribute resources without being entitled to spend them as personal wealth.

Joseph’s request likewise arises amid a public need for considered resource preservation.

إِنِّي حَفِيظٌ عَلِيمٌ: Custody and Knowledge

Joseph states two qualities explaining his fitness:

حَفِيظٌ and عَلِيمٌ.

حَفِيظ belongs to ح ف ظ and concerns protection and custody.

عَلِيم belongs to ع ل م and concerns knowledge.

They are not identical.

Someone may faithfully preserve a trust but lack the knowledge to administer complex resources.

Another may possess extensive knowledge but be untrustworthy with entrusted property.

Joseph claims both qualities.

Trustworthiness and expertise in administration can therefore be distinguished.

خَزَائِن should not, however, be translated as knowledge or trustworthiness: the verse states those separately.

Nor should this request become a universal rule that everyone must pursue office and power in every circumstance.

It describes a particular social need and Joseph’s stated ability.

Al-Ṭabarī: the land’s stores, responsibility, ḥafīẓ, and ʿalīm

4. خَزَائِنُ اللَّهِ: Divine Treasuries and the Limits of Prophetic Authority

Al-Anʿām 6:50: The Messenger Does Not Claim to Possess Divine Treasuries

قُلْ لَا أَقُولُ لَكُمْ عِنْدِي خَزَائِنُ اللَّهِ وَلَا أَعْلَمُ الْغَيْبَ وَلَا أَقُولُ لَكُمْ إِنِّي مَلَكٌ إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰ إِلَيَّ قُلْ هَلْ يَسْتَوِي الْأَعْمَىٰ وَالْبَصِيرُ أَفَلَا تَتَفَكَّرُونَ

qul lā aqūlu lakum ʿindī khazāʾinu allāhi wa-lā aʿlamu al-ghayba wa-lā aqūlu lakum innī malakun in attabiʿu illā mā yūḥā ilayya qul hal yastawī al-aʿmā wa-al-baṣīru a-fa-lā tatafakkarūna

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“Say: I do not tell you that the treasuries of The God are with me, nor do I know the unseen, nor do I tell you that I am an angel. I follow only what is revealed to me. Say: Are the blind and the seeing equal? Will you not reflect?”

Al-Anʿām 6:50

The messenger disclaims three assertions:

possessing the divine treasuries, knowing the unseen, and being an angel.

He then explains his position through following revelation.

This construction matters for خَزَائِنُ اللَّهِ.

The treasuries appear beside unseen knowledge and being an angel, but the three are not identical.

Directly translating خَزَائِنُ اللَّهِ as “knowledge of the unseen” would erase the distinction between two clauses.

Early lexical explanations suggest that divine treasuries may include matters unseen and concealed from people. That can relate to part of the word’s field.

The verse nevertheless states two separate claims.

The basic translation should therefore retain “the treasuries of The God,” with their scope discussed separately.

Lisān al-ʿArab: Ibn al-Anbārī’s explanation of unseen matters and treasuries

Hūd 11:31: The Same Principle in Noah’s Words

وَلَا أَقُولُ لَكُمْ عِنْدِي خَزَائِنُ اللَّهِ وَلَا أَعْلَمُ الْغَيْبَ وَلَا أَقُولُ إِنِّي مَلَكٌ وَلَا أَقُولُ لِلَّذِينَ تَزْدَرِي أَعْيُنُكُمْ لَنْ يُؤْتِيَهُمُ اللَّهُ خَيْرًا اللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا فِي أَنْفُسِهِمْ إِنِّي إِذًا لَمِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

wa-lā aqūlu lakum ʿindī khazāʾinu allāhi wa-lā aʿlamu al-ghayba wa-lā aqūlu innī malakun wa-lā aqūlu li-alladhīna tazdarī aʿyunukum lan yuʾtiyahumu allāhu khayran allāhu aʿlamu bi-mā fī anfusihim innī idhan la-mina al-ẓālimīna

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“I do not tell you that the treasuries of The God are with me, nor do I know the unseen, nor do I say I am an angel. Nor do I say of those your eyes disdain that The God will give them no good. The God knows best what is within them; I would then be among the wrongdoers.”

Hūd 11:31

Noah likewise disclaims possessing divine treasuries and knowledge of the unseen.

His continuation clarifies another point: he does not declare that The God will give no good to people his opponents consider worthless.

The verse both limits prophetic claims about resources and the unseen and prevents groundless judgments of people’s worth.

خَزَائِن itself does not mean judging people. That comes from other clauses.

Does This Negation Deny Every Prophetic Blessing or Authority?

No.

عِنْدِي خَزَائِنُ اللَّهِ concerns having divine treasuries with oneself.

The messenger does not make that claim.

This does not establish that prophets had no property, facilities, responsibilities, or authority in life.

Joseph’s story shows a prophet holding a particular responsibility over earthly stores.

Limited human resources and responsibilities must therefore be distinguished from a claim to independent access and dominion over divine treasuries.

Comparing Yūsuf 12:55 with Al-Anʿām 6:50 and Hūd 11:31 makes this distinction clear.

5. Treasuries of Mercy, Creation’s Resources, and Distribution

Al-Ḥijr 15:21: Every Thing’s Treasuries Are with The God

وَإِنْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ إِلَّا عِنْدَنَا خَزَائِنُهُ وَمَا نُنَزِّلُهُ إِلَّا بِقَدَرٍ مَعْلُومٍ

wa-in min shayʾin illā ʿindanā khazāʾinuhu wa-mā nunazziluhu illā bi-qadarin maʿlūmin

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“There is nothing whose treasuries are not with Us, and We send it down only in a known measure.”

Al-Ḥijr 15:21

This verse uses خَزَائِنُهُ.

هُ refers to شَيْءٍ, a thing.

وَإِنْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ إِلَّا expresses broad scope: nothing lacks its treasuries with The God.

Sending down in a known measure follows.

خَزَائِنُهُ relates to sources or places of keeping.

نُنَزِّلُهُ concerns sending down.

قَدَرٍ concerns measure and determination.

Having divine treasuries is therefore accompanied by measured provision.

This does not establish that every thing was previously stored as a complete physical object in a material warehouse.

Some early exegetical explanations understand treasuries figuratively as divine power to create and give.

Another reading emphasizes resources and means in His control, from which He brings forth or provides in a known measure.

Both must remain distinct from claims of unstated details about the universe’s structure.

The verse states treasuries and measured sending down, rather than the physical location of every being or its exact mode of emergence.

Al-Ḥijr 15:22: وَمَا أَنْتُمْ لَهُ بِخَازِنِينَ

وَأَرْسَلْنَا الرِّيَاحَ لَوَاقِحَ فَأَنْزَلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَأَسْقَيْنَاكُمُوهُ وَمَا أَنْتُمْ لَهُ بِخَازِنِينَ

wa-arsalnā al-riyāḥa lawāqiḥa fa-anzalnā mina al-samāʾi māʾan fa-asqaynākumūhu wa-mā antum lahu bi-khāzinīna

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“We sent the winds as bearers of fertilization, then sent down water from the sky and provided it for you to drink; you are not its keepers.”

Al-Ḥijr 15:22

بِخَازِنِينَ is the sound masculine plural of خَازِن.

وَمَا أَنْتُمْ لَهُ بِخَازِنِينَ denies the addressees’ custody of the water.

Its preceding verse and subject must nevertheless be considered.

Al-Ḥijr 15:21 places everything’s treasuries with The God.

Al-Ḥijr 15:22 mentions winds, descending water, and its provision for drinking. فَأَسْقَيْنَاكُمُوهُ concerns making water available to drink, rather than simply reporting that all the water was actually drunk.

This negation therefore concerns the source, provision, and continuing availability of water. Al-Ṭabarī also explains it as lacking the power to withhold water from those The God supplies. “Ultimate authority” here is a contextual explanation, rather than the independent lexical meaning of خَازِن.

Al-Ṭabarī: providing drinking water and denying control over withholding it

Can People Not Store Water?

Such an interpretation conflicts with human experience.

People have long kept water in jars, pools, cisterns, and other repositories.

The verse should therefore not deny every human ability to store water temporarily.

Limited storage differs from ultimate authority over the emergence, quantity, and continuity of its sources.

People can collect some existing water, but do not independently control every source, rainfall amount, or natural supply’s continuation.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:18 also states:

وَأَنْزَلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً بِقَدَرٍ فَأَسْكَنَّاهُ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَإِنَّا عَلَىٰ ذَهَابٍ بِهِ لَقَادِرُونَ

wa-anzalnā mina al-samāʾi māʾan bi-qadarin fa-askannāhu fī al-arḍi wa-innā ʿalā dhahābin bihi la-qādirūna

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“We sent down water from the sky in a measure and settled it in the earth, and We are certainly able to take it away.”

Al-Muʾminūn 23:18

This shows water’s retention in the earth and possible loss within the Qur’anic network of verses.

وَمَا أَنْتُمْ لَهُ بِخَازِنِينَ should nevertheless not be translated directly as “you cannot construct water reservoirs.”

Nor does it supply a medical theory that the human body stores no water.

Its clear subject is descending water and the human relationship to its sources.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:100: Possessing Treasuries and Fearing Expenditure

قُلْ لَوْ أَنْتُمْ تَمْلِكُونَ خَزَائِنَ رَحْمَةِ رَبِّي إِذًا لَأَمْسَكْتُمْ خَشْيَةَ الْإِنْفَاقِ وَكَانَ الْإِنْسَانُ قَتُورًا

qul law antum tamlikūna khazāʾina raḥmati rabbī idhan la-amsaktum khashyata al-infāqi wa-kāna al-insānu qatūran

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“Say: If you possessed the treasuries of my Lord’s mercy, you would hold back for fear of depletion through spending; the human being is tightfisted.”

Al-Isrāʾ 17:100

تَمْلِكُونَ, “you possess,” is explicit.

This is more than having a storage place: it hypothesizes ownership of mercy’s treasuries.

أَمْسَكْتُمْ and خَشْيَةَ الْإِنْفَاقِ then concern withholding for fear of expenditure. Early commentaries also explain khashyata al-infāq as fear of poverty or depletion; the feared consequence of spending therefore matters.

This clarifies the distinction between custody and withholding.

خَزْن can preserve something for appropriate use.

Here, however, withholding accompanies fear of expenditure and the trait قَتُور, tightfistedness.

The criticism therefore concerns restrictive refusal to spend and give in this context, rather than keeping resources itself.

Joseph’s story clarifies the difference.

Storing crops for difficult years forms part of considered resource protection there.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:100 instead censures withholding mercy’s treasuries for fear of spending.

Responsible storage and miserliness are not identical.

Al-Ṭabarī: khashyata al-infāq and fear of poverty or depletion

Ṣād 38:9 and Al-Ṭūr 52:37: Who Holds the Lord’s Treasuries?

Ṣād 38:9 states:

أَمْ عِنْدَهُمْ خَزَائِنُ رَحْمَةِ رَبِّكَ الْعَزِيزِ الْوَهَّابِ

am ʿindahum khazāʾinu raḥmati rabbika al-ʿazīzi al-wahhābi

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“Or are the treasuries of your Lord’s mercy with them, the Mighty, the Abundant Giver?”

Al-Ṭūr 52:37 also states:

أَمْ عِنْدَهُمْ خَزَائِنُ رَبِّكَ أَمْ هُمُ الْمُصَيْطِرُونَ

am ʿindahum khazāʾinu rabbika am humu al-muṣayṭirūna

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“Or are your Lord’s treasuries with them, or are they the controllers?”

Both question possession of the Lord’s treasuries.

Al-Ṭūr pairs this with الْمُصَيْطِرُونَ: those exercising dominion and control.

This makes authority and dominion clearer.

Treasuries and control are nevertheless not exact synonyms.

Treasuries concern resources or repositories.

Control concerns power and command.

The verse therefore critically connects alleged possession of divine resources with a claim to independent control.

These questions do not establish that all human material resources are worthless or imaginary. They concern claims of independent authority over the Lord’s treasuries.

Al-Munāfiqūn 63:7: Real Resources and Economic Pressure

هُمُ الَّذِينَ يَقُولُونَ لَا تُنْفِقُوا عَلَىٰ مَنْ عِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ حَتَّىٰ يَنْفَضُّوا وَلِلَّهِ خَزَائِنُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَلَٰكِنَّ الْمُنَافِقِينَ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ

humu alladhīna yaqūlūna lā tunfiqū ʿalā man ʿinda rasūli allāhi ḥattā yanfaḍḍū wa-li-allāhi khazāʾinu al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍi wa-lākinna al-munāfiqīna lā yafqahūna

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“They are the ones who say: Do not spend on those with the messenger of The God until they disperse. Yet the treasuries of the heavens and earth belong to The God, but the hypocrites do not understand.”

Al-Munāfiqūn 63:7

خَزَائِن occurs here in a social and economic matter.

A group proposes ending financial assistance to those with the messenger so they will disperse.

The Qur’an responds by attributing the heavens’ and earth’s treasuries to The God.

Holders of limited financial resources should therefore not consider themselves the ultimate masters of people’s provision and livelihoods.

This does not deny economic pressure’s real effects on human lives.

Ending assistance, depriving people of resources, and exploiting financial dependence to enforce obedience can have real consequences.

The verse raises this pressure tactic and recalls that the heavens’ and earth’s treasuries are not restricted to that group.

Nor does it promise unconditionally that every deprived person will become wealthy at a specified time without effort or changed circumstances.

It concerns resources and ultimate divine ownership in relation to human attempts at financial pressure.

6. خَزَنَة: Keepers of Hell and Paradise

Qur’anic خَزَنَة is the plural of خَازِن.

It occurs four times, always referring to keepers of places in the Hereafter.

What is said of Hell’s keepers nevertheless differs from what is said of Paradise’s.

They share responsibility for guarding and administering a place; each verse identifies their particular speech and actions.

Al-Zumar 39:71: Hell’s Keepers Recall the Earlier Warning

وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا إِلَىٰ جَهَنَّمَ زُمَرًا حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا فُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا وَقَالَ لَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَا أَلَمْ يَأْتِكُمْ رُسُلٌ مِنْكُمْ يَتْلُونَ عَلَيْكُمْ آيَاتِ رَبِّكُمْ وَيُنْذِرُونَكُمْ لِقَاءَ يَوْمِكُمْ هَٰذَا قَالُوا بَلَىٰ وَلَٰكِنْ حَقَّتْ كَلِمَةُ الْعَذَابِ عَلَى الْكَافِرِينَ

wa-sīqa alladhīna kafarū ilā jahannama zumaran ḥattā idhā jāʾūhā futiḥat abwābuhā wa-qāla lahum khazanatuhā a-lam yaʾtikum rusulun minkum yatlūna ʿalaykum āyāti rabbikum wa-yundhirūnakum liqāʾa yawmikum hādhā qālū balā wa-lākin ḥaqqat kalimatu al-ʿadhābi ʿalā al-kāfirīna

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“Those who disbelieved will be driven to Hell in groups. When they reach it, its gates will open and its keepers will say: Did messengers from among you not come to you, reciting your Lord’s signs and warning you of meeting this day? They say: Yes, but the word of punishment has become binding upon the disbelievers.”

Al-Zumar 39:71

خَزَنَتُهَا refers to Hell’s keepers.

هَا refers to Hell.

The keepers ask those arriving whether divine messengers came and warned them.

They are therefore more than silent gatekeepers in this scene: they speak to arrivals and recall the message and warning reaching them.

This speaking role nevertheless does not make them Hell’s owners or independent arbiters of punishment.

They are called keepers, rather than independent owners or rulers.

Al-Zumar 39:73: Paradise’s Keepers Welcome Arrivals

وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا رَبَّهُمْ إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ زُمَرًا حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا وَفُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا وَقَالَ لَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَا سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ طِبْتُمْ فَادْخُلُوهَا خَالِدِينَ

wa-sīqa alladhīna ittaqaw rabbahum ilā al-jannati zumaran ḥattā idhā jāʾūhā wa-futiḥat abwābuhā wa-qāla lahum khazanatuhā salāmun ʿalaykum ṭibtum fa-udkhulūhā khālidīna

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“Those mindful of their Lord will be led to Paradise in groups, until they reach it and its gates are opened and its keepers say: Peace be upon you; you have been good and pure, so enter it, remaining there.”

Al-Zumar 39:73

خَزَنَتُهَا appears again, but هَا now refers to Paradise.

Its keepers receive arrivals with greetings of peace and welcome.

خَزَنَة therefore is not a title exclusive to Hell’s keepers.

It also describes Paradise’s keepers.

It cannot invariably be translated as “agents of punishment.”

One context concerns Hell, another Paradise.

The word denotes the responsibility of keeping, rather than punishing or rewarding.

Ghāfir 40:49: A Request to Hell’s Keepers

وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ فِي النَّارِ لِخَزَنَةِ جَهَنَّمَ ادْعُوا رَبَّكُمْ يُخَفِّفْ عَنَّا يَوْمًا مِنَ الْعَذَابِ

wa-qāla alladhīna fī al-nāri li-khazanati jahannama udʿū rabbakum yukhaffif ʿannā yawman mina al-ʿadhābi

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“Those in the Fire say to Hell’s keepers: Call upon your Lord to lighten our punishment for a day.”

Ghāfir 40:49

The expression is لِخَزَنَةِ جَهَنَّمَ.

The possessive construction identifies them as keepers of Hell.

Those in the Fire ask them to call upon their Lord for relief.

The request shows the sufferers addressing the keepers.

It does not establish the keepers’ independent authority to reduce punishment.

Their reply in Ghāfir 40:50 recalls messengers and clear signs.

The sufferers’ request must therefore be distinguished from authority the Qur’an actually grants the keepers.

خَزَنَة itself also does not mean intercessors or intermediaries with independent power.

It names their custodial responsibility; prayer, its response, and the addressees’ authority follow from the full exchange.

Al-Mulk 67:8: Keepers Asking about the Warning

تَكَادُ تَمَيَّزُ مِنَ الْغَيْظِ كُلَّمَا أُلْقِيَ فِيهَا فَوْجٌ سَأَلَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَا أَلَمْ يَأْتِكُمْ نَذِيرٌ

takādu tamayyazu mina al-ghayẓi kullamā ulqiya fīhā fawjun saʾalahum khazanatuhā a-lam yaʾtikum nadhīrun

Proposed translation in this study, without changing the installed verse translation:

“It almost bursts apart with rage. Whenever a group is cast into it, its keepers ask: Did no warner come to you?”

Al-Mulk 67:8

The keepers ask groups cast into the Fire whether a warning reached them.

This resembles Al-Zumar 39:71.

Both exchanges concern the earlier warning.

In Al-Mulk 67:9, the arrivals answer that a warner came but they denied the message.

This relates to human responsibility toward received warning and truth.

The title خَزَنَة alone, however, does not make them independent judges of every person’s knowledge and intention without any further process.

The verse reports a conversation, rather than every stage of accounting or all custodial powers.

Are Hell’s Keepers Angels?

Al-Taḥrīm 66:6 describes stern angels over the Fire who do not disobey The God and carry out what they are commanded.

This matters for understanding the agents responsible for Hell.

Without further examination, it does not establish that every detail about angels applies identically to every being called خَازِن or خَزَنَة.

Al-Zumar 39:73, for example, mentions Paradise’s keepers without specifying their nature or number in that verse.

Explicit statements about one group of agents in the Hereafter must therefore be distinguished from information inferred with other verses.

Nor does the plural خَزَنَة establish the exact number of keepers of each place.

7. Distinctions from Related Roots

خ ز ن (KH Z N, Khazn) and ح ف ظ (Ḥ F Ẓ, Ḥifẓ): Keeping and Protection

حِفْظ concerns keeping, care, and preventing loss.

خَزْن also concerns protection, but often emphasizes placing something in a repository for preservation or administration.

Someone may, for example, keep important documents in a treasury and protect them.

Custody and protection combine there.

Not every protection, however, is storage.

Protecting a person’s life or watching a route may involve no physical treasury.

Yūsuf 12:55 clarifies this: خَزَائِن are administered resources, while حَفِيظ describes Joseph’s ability and trustworthiness in keeping them.

Study of Ḥ F Ẓ

خ ز ن (KH Z N, Khazn) and ك ن ز (K N Z, Kanz): Storage and Amassing Wealth

Qur’anic كَنْز concerns collecting and keeping wealth.

Al-Tawbah 9:34 describes people hoarding gold and silver without spending it in the way of The God.

خَزْن has a broader field of custody, repositories, and their keepers.

A treasury may preserve public property for responsible distribution.

Conversely, someone may accumulate wealth personally and refuse to spend where needed.

Not every storage can therefore be called censured hoarding.

Joseph’s story and Al-Isrāʾ 17:100 show the importance of purpose and use.

خ ز ن (KH Z N, Khazn) and م ل ك (M L K, Milk): Custody and Ownership

مِلْك concerns ownership and authority.

A خَازِن may nevertheless be responsible for something they do not own.

A treasurer can safeguard institutional property without personally owning it.

Yūsuf 12:55 concerns responsibility over storehouses.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:100 states تَمْلِكُونَ separately, hypothesizing ownership of mercy’s treasuries.

Custody and ownership should therefore not be made identical.

Study of M L K

خ ز ن (KH Z N, Khazn) and م س ك (M S K, Imsāk): Keeping and Withholding

إِمْسَاك concerns holding and restraining.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:100 connects it with fear of expenditure.

خَزْن itself is not miserliness or refusal to give.

Something may be stored for later use and distributed when needed.

An ethical judgment therefore requires examining why someone keeps it and whether another’s right is being violated.

خ ز ن (KH Z N, Khazn) and ر ز ق (R Z Q, Rizq): Resources and Provision

رِزْق concerns what reaches beings for life and benefit.

خَزَائِن concerns available resources or repositories from which something is provided or distributed.

Al-Ḥijr 15:21 and Al-Ḥijr 15:22 place treasuries and descending water in one context.

Al-Munāfiqūn 63:7 likewise sets the heavens’ and earth’s treasuries against attempts to deprive others of financial support.

Treasuries and provision are therefore related, but خَزَائِن itself does not mean رِزْق.

Study of R Z Q

خ ز ن (KH Z N, Khazn) and خ ن ز (KH N Z, Khanaz): Keeping and Food Spoilage

Lexical sources record خَزِنَ اللَّحْمُ for meat changing in smell or spoiling.

Ibn Fāris relates this branch to خَنِزَ, distinguishing it from basic preservation.

Visual similarity should therefore not make Qur’anic خَزْن mean spoilage or becoming foul-smelling.

Divine treasuries, earthly stores, and keepers in the Hereafter are never explained in the Qur’an through meat spoilage.

8. Translation Proposals for Forqan

Clear, natural Persian equivalents exist for the main Qur’anic derivatives.

خَزَائِن usually becomes khazāʾin or ganjīnah-hā, “treasuries.”

Khazāʾin is familiar in Persian and can be retained in expressions such as “divine treasuries” or “treasuries of mercy.”

Ganjīnah-hā is also natural, but can suggest precious possessions or jewels in some contexts.

“The land’s storehouses” or “the land’s reserves” suits Joseph’s story, which concerns resources and crops.

خَازِنِين in Al-Ḥijr 15:22 becomes “keepers.” The explanation should distinguish ultimate authority over water sources from limited human storage abilities.

خَزَنَة in passages about Paradise and Hell becomes “keepers.”

“Treasurers” could misleadingly suggest financial boxes and property, whereas the context concerns those guarding a place.

Brief Root Meaning and Qur’anic Usages

خَزَائِنُ اللَّهِ — Al-Anʿām 6:50 and Hūd 11:31

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: خَزَائِن is the broken plural of خِزَانَة, in a possessive construction with اللَّه.

Brief root meaning: Keeping and safeguarding; a treasury is a repository or stored resources.

Meaning here: Divine treasuries and resources that prophets do not claim to hold independently.

خَزَائِنِ الْأَرْضِ — Yūsuf 12:55

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: A broken plural, genitive after عَلَىٰ and in a possessive construction with الْأَرْض.

Brief root meaning: Storehouses and places for keeping resources.

Meaning here: A land’s resources and stores for which Joseph seeks administrative and protective responsibility.

خَزَائِنُهُ — Al-Ḥijr 15:21

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: A broken plural with attached هُ.

Brief root meaning: Treasuries, repositories, or stored resources.

Meaning here: Every thing’s treasuries, placed with The God and accompanied by sending down in a known measure.

بِخَازِنِينَ — Al-Ḥijr 15:22

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: A sound masculine plural active participle of خَازِن; formally genitive after the emphatic bāʾ in a negative construction, functioning as the predicate of mā.

Brief root meaning: A keeper responsible for safeguarding something.

Meaning here: Humans do not ultimately control the custody and provision of the descending water’s sources.

خَزَائِنَ رَحْمَةِ رَبِّي — Al-Isrāʾ 17:100

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: An accusative broken plural, in a possessive construction with رَحْمَة.

Brief root meaning: Treasuries and stored resources.

Meaning here: The Lord’s mercy treasuries, hypothetical human ownership of which is contrasted with fear of expenditure.

خَزَائِنُ رَحْمَةِ رَبِّكَ — Ṣād 38:9

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: A broken plural in a possessive construction with رَحْمَة.

Brief root meaning: Treasuries and resources.

Meaning here: The Lord’s mercy resources, whose possession by the addressees is critically questioned.

خَزَائِنُ رَبِّكَ — Al-Ṭūr 52:37

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: A broken plural in a possessive construction with رَبِّكَ.

Brief root meaning: Treasuries and resources.

Meaning here: The Lord’s treasuries, whose possession is raised alongside alleged dominion and control.

خَزَائِنُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ — Al-Munāfiqūn 63:7

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: A broken plural in a possessive construction with السَّمَاوَات.

Brief root meaning: Treasuries and stored resources.

Meaning here: The heavens’ and earth’s resources, whose ownership is attributed to The God.

خَزَنَتُهَا — Al-Zumar 39:71, Al-Zumar 39:73, and Al-Mulk 67:8

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: A broken plural of خَازِن with attached هَا.

Brief root meaning: Keepers or those responsible for custody.

Meaning here: Keepers of Hell or Paradise, depending on هَا’s referent and each verse’s context.

لِخَزَنَةِ جَهَنَّمَ — Ghāfir 40:49

Root: خ ز ن (KH Z N).

Grammar: A broken plural of خَازِن, genitive after lām and in a possessive construction with جَهَنَّم.

Brief root meaning: Keepers and custodians.

Meaning here: Hell’s keepers, whom those in the Fire ask to call upon their Lord for lessened punishment.

These forms share a basic meaning, but each translation must follow its object and construction.

9. Conclusion

Arabic خ ز ن concerns keeping, protecting, and placing something in a repository for preservation and administration.

Early witnesses apply it to wealth, secrets, speech, and storage places. Imruʾ al-Qays’s verse shows the extension to watching one’s tongue.

A separate usage concerns meat changing in smell, which Ibn Fāris distinguishes from basic safeguarding.

The Qur’an uses three main forms.

خَزَائِن concerns resources and repositories, whether Joseph’s earthly stores or divine treasuries of mercy.

خَازِنِين in Al-Ḥijr 15:22 concerns human custody and authority over descending water.

خَزَنَة names the keepers of Hell and Paradise.

Three concepts must remain distinct: keeping something, owning it, and having independent authority over its distribution or fate.

Joseph requests administration of a land’s stores, but the verse does not give him personal ownership of all those resources.

Prophets in Al-Anʿām 6:50 and Hūd 11:31 do not claim to hold divine treasuries; this does not deny every earthly responsibility or resource.

Al-Ḥijr 15:21 and Al-Ḥijr 15:22 relate treasuries and water to divine measure and provision, while humans can use and store existing resources in limited ways.

The keepers in the Hereafter are likewise custodians; the word alone does not grant independent ownership or final authority over punishment and reward.

Nor is keeping property inherently miserly. Storage for future needs, as in Joseph’s planning, differs from restrictive refusal to spend and give, as criticized in Al-Isrāʾ 17:100.

Final definition: The root خ ز ن denotes keeping and safeguarding something, whether physically, through preserving speech and secrets, or through custodial and resource-management responsibilities. خَزَائِن denotes repositories and stored resources, خَازِن their keeper, and خَزَنَة keepers. In the Qur’an, ownership, authority, storage’s purpose, and each keeper’s responsibilities follow from the subject, object, and context, rather than the root alone. Brief root meaning: Keeping and safeguarding; a treasury is a repository or resources in custody, and a khāzin is their keeper.
