The Qur’anic Root AYN M H (ʿ M H)

General meaning of the root AYN M H

The root ع م ه (AYN M H / ʿ M H) points to bewilderment, wandering, hesitation, and weak wayfinding. In this root, the human being does not stand before a clear path. He does not see the way, does not recognize the direction, and remains confused while moving within the field.

This bewilderment is not only a mental state. A person may be confused in thought, but still active in deed. ʿAmah shows exactly this condition: movement without insight, advance without a path, and motion without right direction.

In this meaning, ʿamah is tied to path and direction. The person in ʿamah does not merely lack information; he does not know which way to go. The problem is inward blindness, hesitation, and inability to recognize the path of rescue.

Usage of the root in Arabic

Arabic says عَمِهَ الرجلُ يَعْمَهُ عَمَهًا (ʿamiha al-rajulu yaʿmahu ʿamahan): the man became bewildered and confused, not knowing where to turn.

Lexically, العَمَه (al-ʿamah) is bewilderment and hesitation, especially hesitation within misguidance or in a path where the person does not know from which direction to proceed. A عَامِه (ʿāmih) person is one who cannot find his path and destination.

For this reason, it has been said that ʿamah in insight is like ʿamā in eyesight. Just as blindness of the eye prevents seeing the outer road, ʿamah prevents seeing the inner and moral road.

The material image of ʿamah: a featureless land

One important image of this root is a vast desert or land with no signs by which one can find direction. Arabic uses expressions such as الأرض العَمْهاء (al-arḍ al-ʿamhāʾ) or مَعَامِه (maʿāmih) for land that is wide, uniform, featureless, and difficult for wayfinding.

In such a land, the traveler may walk a long distance without knowing whether he has advanced or merely circled around himself. There is no mountain, tree, footprint, contrast, or sign that clarifies direction. There is movement, but the signs of direction are weak or absent.

This image helps us understand Qur’anic ʿamah. The person in ʿamah moves through an inner featureless land. When remembrance, revelation, honesty, taqwā, and the signs of truth are removed from his inner world, life becomes a field without markers. He is active, but not rightly guided.

The linguistic image of ʿamah: directionless movement

The main image of this root is a human being moving in a dark or featureless space without knowing where to go. He stops, turns back, goes again, but has no clear route. There is movement, but no guidance.

ʿAmah is not stillness; it is directionless motion. A person in ʿamah may be highly active, speak often, make plans, possess power, and even imagine that he is progressing. But if he cannot see the direction of truth, his motion belongs to the field of ʿamah.

In the Qur’an, this image is tied to rebellion, heedlessness of the Hereafter, sakrah, and the adornment of deeds. When the human being separates from the standards of truth, he may see his action as beautiful while actually moving in bewilderment.

Examples outside the Qur’an

Arabic says عَمِهَ في الطريق (ʿamiha fī al-ṭarīq): he became lost and bewildered on the road, not knowing which way to go.

It says عَمِهَ في أمره (ʿamiha fī amrih): he became confused and directionless in his affair and did not find the right decision.

It says رجلٌ عَمِهٌ (rajulun ʿamih) or رجلٌ عامه (rajulun ʿāmih): a bewildered, wandering, wayless man.

For land, expressions such as أرض عمهاء (arḍ ʿamhāʾ) or معامه (maʿāmih) are used: land where signs of direction are scarce and the traveler becomes confused.

Morphological and semantic forms

The simple form is عَمِهَ / يَعْمَهُ (ʿamiha / yaʿmahu): he became bewildered, wandered, and failed to find the path.

The prominent Qur’anic form is يَعْمَهُونَ (yaʿmahūn): they remain bewildered, move blindly, or wander without direction in the field of misguidance.

عَمَه (ʿamah) is the verbal noun and points to the state of bewilderment and hesitation itself. عَمِه (ʿamih) or عَامِه (ʿāmih) describes one who is inwardly blind and confused in recognizing the right path, destination, and direction.

Yaʿmahūn and ongoing bewilderment

An important point is that the root ع م ه (ʿ m h) appears in the Qur’an as يَعْمَهُونَ (yaʿmahūn), a present-tense verb. In Arabic, the present tense can indicate continuity, renewal, and an ongoing state.

The Qur’an does not say that they became bewildered only once. It says yaʿmahūn: their bewilderment is in motion, continues, and is reproduced through new choices and behaviors.

This matters for understanding hypocrisy. The hypocrite is not still. He changes tactics, changes speech, changes face, says one thing to the believers and another in private. But this motion is not guidance; it is renewed bewilderment and directionlessness.

The difference between ʿamah, ʿamā, and ḍalāl

ʿAmā points mainly to blindness: either blindness of the eye or blindness of the heart, depending on context. Its center is not seeing.

Ḍalāl points to losing the way, missing the path, or departing from guidance. Its center is the loss of the path.

But ʿamah is bewilderment inside misguidance. The person in ʿamah has not only lost the road; he turns and wanders within that lostness, not knowing where to face. One may say: ʿamā is darkness of seeing, ḍalāl is departure from the road, and ʿamah is bewildered movement within the field of waylessness.

Usage of the root in the Qur’an

In the Qur’an, the root ع م ه (AYN M H / ʿ M H) appears only in the form يَعْمَهُونَ (yaʿmahūn). This is important because the Qur’an presents this root as a continuing state, not merely a fixed label.

Yaʿmahūn means that they continue moving in bewilderment. This word is connected in the Qur’an with rebellion, disbelief in the Hereafter, the adornment of deeds, and sakrah. So ʿamah is not accidental confusion; it is the result of moving away from the standards of guidance.

In several verses, the expression فِي طُغْيَانِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ (fī ṭughyānihim yaʿmahūn) appears: they wander blindly in their rebellion. Rebellion pushes the path beyond its right limit, and ʿamah shows that inside this crossing of limits, clear direction no longer remains.

Yaʿmahūn in the verse of hypocrisy

In 2:15, it says: اللَّهُ يَسْتَهْزِئُ بِهِمْ وَيَمُدُّهُمْ فِي طُغْيَانِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ (allāh yastahziʾu bihim wayamudduhum fī ṭughyānihim yaʿmahūna). The word yaʿmahūn is the end of a behavioral chain.

Before it, the hypocrites take on the face of faith in the field of laqū. Then, in the field of khalaw, they return to their devils. Then they say: innamā naḥnu mustahziʾūn.

This means they imagine they understand the game, but in reality they have lost direction. Their mockery is not mastery; it is inward blindness. They move, perform roles, change language, but they are not on a clear path.

ʿAmah and rebellion

In the Qur’an, ʿamah appears several times with ṭughyān, rebellion. Ṭughyān means crossing the limit, overflowing, and going beyond the right boundary. When a human being crosses the boundary of truth and insists on it, he gradually loses the ability to recognize direction.

In 6:110, 7:186, and 10:11, this link between rebellion and bewilderment appears.

So فِي طُغْيَانِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ (fī ṭughyānihim yaʿmahūna) means they wander blindly within their rebellion and boundary-crossing. Rebellion makes the field appear without limits, and ʿamah shows that false limitlessness does not bring guidance; it brings bewilderment.

ʿAmah and extended respite

The connection between yamudduhum and يَعْمَهُونَ (yaʿmahūn) is precise. Madd means extending, granting respite, and leaving a field open. But if that field is not on the right path, its extension does not produce growth; it deepens bewilderment.

If the human being receives time on the path of guidance, that time can become repentance, reform, and growth. But if he receives time in rebellion and uses it to continue the same path, the wider field only moves him farther from the signs of return.

For this reason, يَمُدُّهُمْ فِي طُغْيَانِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ (yamudduhum fī ṭughyānihim yaʿmahūna) means they are given extension in the path of rebellion, but this respite does not become light. Within that extension, their wandering and inward blindness continue.

ʿAmah and sakrah

In the Qur’an, يَعْمَهُونَ (yaʿmahūn) also appears with sakrah. In 15:72, it says: إِنَّهُمْ لَفِي سَكْرَتِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ (innahum lafī sakratihim yaʿmahūna).

Sakrah is a state that covers reason, balance, and discernment: like intoxication, daze, or the dominance of a state that prevents a person from seeing reality.

When ʿamah comes with sakrah, the image becomes clearer. The human being is under the covering force of desire, pride, fear, anger, or heedlessness, and within that condition he moves without direction.

ʿAmah and adorned actions

In 27:4, the Qur’an says that for those who do not believe in the Hereafter, their deeds are adorned for them, so they يَعْمَهُونَ (yaʿmahūn).

When wrong action becomes beautiful in a person’s eyes, bewilderment becomes more dangerous. The person no longer thinks he is lost; he sees his own path as correct and beautiful. This is a severe form of ʿamah: he moves in misguidance while seeing his motion as polished and defensible.

Here ʿamah becomes tied to self-deception. If the human being sees the ugliness of his action, awakening is still nearer. But when ugly action is adorned in his own eyes, bewilderment appears as false confidence.

Translation suggestions for Forqan

عَمِهَ / يَعْمَهُ (ʿamiha / yaʿmahu): became bewildered; wandered; failed to find the path; moved without direction.

يَعْمَهُونَ (yaʿmahūn): they remain bewildered; they move blindly; they wander in waylessness; they are inwardly blind and confused; they struggle in a field without signs.

فِي طُغْيَانِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ (fī ṭughyānihim yaʿmahūna): they wander blindly in their rebellion; in their boundary-crossing, they fail to find the right path.

وَيَمُدُّهُمْ فِي طُغْيَانِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ (wayamudduhum fī ṭughyānihim yaʿmahūna): and He grants them respite in their rebellion while they wander blindly; He extends the field of their rebellion, and they keep moving in the same directionlessness.

فِي سَكْرَتِهِمْ يَعْمَهُونَ (fī sakratihim yaʿmahūna): they wander in their intoxicated covering; they fail to find the way under the dominance of that state.

فَهُمْ يَعْمَهُونَ (fahum yaʿmahūna): so they wander blindly; so they move in directionlessness.

Summary

The root ع م ه (AYN M H / ʿ M H) in Arabic points to bewilderment, hesitation, wandering, and weak wayfinding. It is tied to inward blindness and inability to recognize the right direction.

In the Qur’an, this root appears only as يَعْمَهُونَ (yaʿmahūn). The Qur’an presents it as an ongoing movement. They do not stand still; they move. But the movement is not guided and clear. It is blind, directionless, and bewildered.

In the verses of hypocrisy, yaʿmahūn is the end of a chain: outward meeting, private withdrawal to devils, mockery, extension in rebellion, and finally inward bewilderment. They imagine they understand, but in reality they have lost the road.
