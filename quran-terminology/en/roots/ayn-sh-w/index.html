Central meanings: dim vision and looking without sufficient clarity; in directional constructions, looking toward or away from something. The temporal branch concerns the latter part of the day and the onset of night.

1. The root in early Arabic

ع ش و relates to dim sight, looking in inadequate light, moving in darkness, and indistinct perception. Related words also name the latter part of the day and approaching night.

عَشَا يَعْشُو is recorded for looking with weak or unclear sight: seeing something, but less distinctly than someone with clear vision.

عَشِيَ يَعْشَى describes impaired sight, especially difficulty seeing in darkness. أَعْشَى describes someone with this impairment; عَشْوَاء is its feminine form.

Lexical accounts distinguish the constructions: عَشِيَ يَعْشَى primarily concerns the condition, whereas عَشَا يَعْشُو can express looking with weak sight or acting like someone who sees poorly.

Dim sight is not necessarily total blindness. Conversely, someone deliberately looking away may have unimpaired physical vision.

Imagine someone traveling a dark road at night, seeing a distant light without clearly identifying its source. They may move toward it for guidance or turn elsewhere.

عَشَا إِلَى النَّارِ can mean approaching a fire glimpsed in darkness. عَشَا عَنِ الشَّيْءِ can mean turning one's gaze away or disregarding something.

The preposition matters: إِلَى marks orientation toward something, while عَنْ marks turning away.

The temporal branch includes عَشِيّ, عَشِيَّة, and عِشَاء, naming late day and the transition into night.

Dim vision and fading daylight form an intelligible association, but that resemblance does not prove that every temporal term historically derived from an eye condition.

Two important fields should therefore be distinguished: dim sight and orientation of the gaze; and late day with the onset of night.

2. Early poetry and its dating

A famous witness appears in the Mu'allaqah attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Zuhayr ibn Abi Sulma:

رَأَيْتُ الْمَنَايَا خَبْطَ عَشْوَاءَ مَنْ تُصِبْ
تُمِتْهُ وَمَنْ تُخْطِئْ يُعَمَّرْ فَيَهْرَمِ

raʾaytu al-manāyā khabṭa ʿashwāʾa man tuṣib / tumithu wa-man tukhṭiʾ yuʿammar fa-yahrami

“I have seen deaths strike like a poorly sighted she-camel: whoever they hit dies; whoever they miss lives on into old age.”

عَشْوَاء describes a she-camel unable to see clearly, striking the ground without accurately discerning what lies before it.

The poet uses this image for death as humans experience it: arrivals whose pattern is not readily predictable.

The verse illustrates the early sense through a tangible image of impaired sight and movement without clear discernment.

This is a famous transmitted witness attributed to Zuhayr. Its lexical illustration should be distinguished from any theological judgment about death: a poetic image alone does not establish a universal doctrine about the order of events.

Another witness is attributed to al-Hutay'ah, who lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods:

مَتَى تَأْتِهِ تَعْشُو إِلَى ضَوْءِ نَارِهِ
تَجِدْ خَيْرَ نَارٍ عِنْدَهَا خَيْرُ مُوقِدِ

matā taʾtihi taʿshū ilā ḍawʾi nārihi / tajid khayra nārin ʿindahā khayru mūqidi

“Whenever you come to him, looking toward his fire's light, you will find the best fire beside the best fire-kindler.”

تَعْشُو إِلَى ضَوْءِ نَارِهِ describes approaching firelight: someone glimpses a fire in darkness and heads toward it.

A fire could signal human presence and hospitality to travelers. Someone traveling at night might approach it for assistance or welcome.

Hospitality belongs to the poem's setting and description of the host, not to the verb's lexical meaning.

Al-Hutay'ah lived before and after Islam. This particular verse's date has not been independently established in this review, so it illustrates early usage without proving a pre-Quranic date.

The two witnesses illustrate related but distinct uses: unclear sight and movement, and orientation toward light in darkness.

تَعَاشَى is also recorded for pretending to see poorly or acting as though one does not see; figuratively, it can express feigned ignorance.

This branch helps illuminate Quranic يَعْشُ without making it identical in form to تَعَاشَى.

3. Lexical and Quranic forms

Important forms include عَشَا, يَعْشُو, عَشْو, عُشُوّ, عَشِيَ, يَعْشَى, أَعْشَى, عَشْوَاء, تَعَاشَى, عَشِيّ, عَشِيَّة, and عِشَاء.

Their structures and uses differ despite semantic relationships.

عَشَا يَعْشُو concerns dim or unclear seeing and, with prepositions, can express turning toward or away.

عَشِيَ يَعْشَى primarily describes impaired vision.

أَعْشَى and عَشْوَاء describe a person or animal with poor sight.

تَعَاشَى can express feigned poor sight and, figuratively, feigned ignorance.

عَشِيّ and عَشِيَّة name the latter part of the day.

عِشَاء concerns the onset of night and darkness after daytime.

The Quranic classification followed here groups four forms under ع ش و: يَعْشُ, عِشَاء, عَشِيّ, and عَشِيَّة.

Some dictionaries discuss عَشِيَ and temporal forms under ع ش ي or jointly under waw and ya. Classification differences should not obscure exact morphology or differences in meaning.

The Quran has one يَعْشُ, two عِشَاء occurrences, ten عَشِيّ occurrences, and one عَشِيَّة: fourteen altogether in this classification.

Semantically, they fall into sight and turning away, or late day and the beginning of night.

4. يَعْشُ: turning away from God's remembrance

The sole verbal occurrence is in al-Zukhruf:

وَمَنْ يَعْشُ عَنْ ذِكْرِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ نُقَيِّضْ لَهُ شَيْطَانًا فَهُوَ لَهُ قَرِينٌ

wa-man yaʿshu ʿan dhikri al-Raḥmāni nuqayyiḍ lahu shayṭānan fa-huwa lahu qarīn

Al-Zukhruf 43:36

Al-Zukhruf 43:36

“Whoever turns away from the remembrance of the Entirely Merciful, We appoint a satan for him, who becomes his companion.”

يَعْشُ comes from عَشَا يَعْشُو. Conditional مَنْ makes the imperfect jussive; the final waw is consequently omitted.

The wording uses يَعْشُ rather than يَعْمَ.

The lexical field includes dim looking and acting like someone who sees poorly. عَنْ here directs that sense toward turning away from something.

يَعْشُ عَنْ ذِكْرِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ thus concerns turning away from God's remembrance, not a judgment about a bodily visual disability.

Imagine someone deliberately looking away from a sign on their path. The sign remains, and their eyes need not be blind; they withhold attention.

Turning away and impaired discernment meet in this use.

The verb alone does not establish complete knowledge of every aspect of truth followed by hostile rejection. Knowledge, motives, and responsibility require contextual examination.

The explicit object of turning away is ذِكْرِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ.

Here remembrance concerns divine reminder and message, directing attention toward truth, responsibility, and the right path.

The verse then states the consequence: a satan becomes the person's companion.

The next verse clarifies that consequence:

وَإِنَّهُمْ لَيَصُدُّونَهُمْ عَنِ السَّبِيلِ وَيَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُهْتَدُونَ

wa-innahum la-yaṣuddūnahum ʿani al-sabīli wa-yaḥsabūna annahum muhtadūn

Al-Zukhruf 43:37

Al-Zukhruf 43:37

“They surely turn them away from the way, while those people think they are guided.”

The passage moves from turning away from remembrance to satanic companions obstructing the way and the people imagining themselves guided.

Someone whose discernment is impaired or who turns away may still think they are taking the right path.

Being astray does not always entail awareness of being astray.

Someone may regard their decisions as entirely right after setting aside the criteria by which to assess them.

This whole process should not be inserted into يَعْشُ itself. Imagined guidance and obstruction of the way are stated in al-Zukhruf 43:37.

يَعْشُ and يَعْشَ

Alongside the reading with dammah on shin followed here, commentaries report يَعْشَ with fathah. It is mentioned for linguistic comparison, not substituted for the text used in this study.

The first is associated with عَشَا يَعْشُو, dim looking and turning away.

The second is associated with عَشِيَ يَعْشَى and the condition of impaired vision.

The vowel difference affects the underlying verb and semantic emphasis; it does not by itself establish a precise degree of physical blindness.

Given the reading followed here, “turns away from God's remembrance” is preferable to automatically translating total blindness. “Turns a blind eye to” can retain the visual metaphor.

5. عَشِيّ: the latter part of the day

The second main branch concerns time.

عَشِيّ denotes the latter part of the day. Lexical accounts sometimes extend it from after midday to sunset and sometimes define a narrower period toward day's end.

It should not be fixed to an exact hour in every context.

The Quran often pairs it with morning terms such as بُكْرَة, إِبْكَار, and غَدَاة.

For example, God addresses Zechariah:

قَالَ رَبِّ اجْعَلْ لِي آيَةً ۖ قَالَ آيَتُكَ أَلَّا تُكَلِّمَ النَّاسَ ثَلَاثَةَ أَيَّامٍ إِلَّا رَمْزًا ۗ وَاذْكُرْ رَبَّكَ كَثِيرًا وَسَبِّحْ بِالْعَشِيِّ وَالْإِبْكَارِ

qāla rabbi ijʿal lī āyatan qāla āyatuka allā tukallima al-nāsa thalāthata ayyāmin illā ramzan wa-udhkur rabbaka kathīran wa-sabbiḥ bi-l-ʿashiyyi wa-l-ibkār

Āl ʿImrān 3:41

Al Imran 3:41

“He said: My Lord, appoint a sign for me. He said: Your sign is that you will not speak to people for three days except by gesture. Remember your Lord much, and glorify Him at day's end and in the early morning.”

الْعَشِيّ appears beside الْإِبْكَار.

Together they identify the beginning and latter part of the day.

Maryam 19:11 likewise calls Zechariah's people to glorification بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا.

Ghafir 40:55 also joins بِالْعَشِيِّ وَالْإِبْكَارِ with glorification and seeking forgiveness.

عشي functions as a temporal designation for the latter day.

The word alone does not name a particular prayer or determine its units or form. The action comes from verbs such as سَبِّحْ and the whole construction.

عشي and recurring remembrance

Al-An'am states:

وَلَا تَطْرُدِ الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُمْ بِالْغَدَاةِ وَالْعَشِيِّ يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُ ۖ مَا عَلَيْكَ مِنْ حِسَابِهِمْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ وَمَا مِنْ حِسَابِكَ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ فَتَطْرُدَهُمْ فَتَكُونَ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

wa-lā taṭrudi alladhīna yadʿūna rabbahum bi-l-ghadāti wa-l-ʿashiyyi yurīdūna wajhahu mā ʿalayka min ḥisābihim min shayʾin wa-mā min ḥisābika ʿalayhim min shayʾin fa-taṭrudahum fa-takūna mina al-ẓālimīn

Al-Anʿām 6:52

Al-An'am 6:52

“Do not drive away those who call upon their Lord in the morning and at day's end, seeking His face. None of their account rests upon you, nor any of your account upon them, that you should drive them away and become one of the wrongdoers.”

بِالْغَدَاةِ وَالْعَشِيِّ accompanies calling upon the Lord.

The paired times may suggest recurring devotion throughout life, but عشي itself does not mean continuity or prayer.

Al-Kahf 18:28 uses the same construction for those calling upon their Lord morning and late day, seeking His face.

Their conduct's value comes from their invocation, purpose, and ethical context, not the time word alone.

عشي in ordinary events

The Quran does not reserve عشي for invocation or glorification.

In Solomon's story it says:

إِذْ عُرِضَ عَلَيْهِ بِالْعَشِيِّ الصَّافِنَاتُ الْجِيَادُ

idh ʿuriḍa ʿalayhi bi-l-ʿashiyyi al-ṣāfinātu al-jiyād

Ṣād 38:31

Sad 38:31

“When the poised, swift horses were presented to him at day's end.”

بِالْعَشِيِّ specifies when the horses were presented.

It is an ordinary time word, without an inherent meaning of worship or sacred action.

The action and context determine its subject and value.

Sad 38:18 describes the mountains' glorification with David بِالْعَشِيِّ وَالْإِشْرَاقِ. Al-Rum 30:18 places عَشِيًّا among temporal expressions of praise.

The time meaning remains the latter day; the activity varies.

6. عِشَاء: the onset of night

عِشَاء concerns the time after sunset and entry into nighttime darkness.

Lexical definitions do not always draw identical boundaries; some emphasize the beginning of darkness, others a broader period.

The usual distinction places عَشِيّ primarily in the latter day and عِشَاء in the darkness following it.

The distinction is also visible in Quranic usage.

عِشَاء in Joseph's story

The Quran says of Joseph's brothers:

وَجَاءُوا أَبَاهُمْ عِشَاءً يَبْكُونَ

wa-jāʾū abāhum ʿishāʾan yabkūn

Yūsuf 12:16

Yusuf 12:16

“They came to their father at night, weeping.”

عِشَاءً specifies their arrival time.

يَبْكُونَ describes their condition as they arrived.

عِشَاء itself means neither concealment nor deception. Their false account emerges from the story, not from the time word.

One person may return at night because work lasted late; another may choose darkness to hide conduct. The time word remains the same, while motives require other evidence.

“At night” is appropriate without inserting the brothers' conduct into the temporal meaning.

عِشَاء in al-Nur

The second occurrence is in al-Nur:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لِيَسْتَأْذِنْكُمُ الَّذِينَ مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ وَالَّذِينَ لَمْ يَبْلُغُوا الْحُلُمَ مِنْكُمْ ثَلَاثَ مَرَّاتٍ ۚ مِنْ قَبْلِ صَلَاةِ الْفَجْرِ وَحِينَ تَضَعُونَ ثِيَابَكُمْ مِنَ الظَّهِيرَةِ وَمِنْ بَعْدِ صَلَاةِ الْعِشَاءِ ۚ ثَلَاثُ عَوْرَاتٍ لَكُمْ ۚ لَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ وَلَا عَلَيْهِمْ جُنَاحٌ بَعْدَهُنَّ ۚ طَوَّافُونَ عَلَيْكُمْ بَعْضُكُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ لَكُمُ الْآيَاتِ ۗ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū li-yastaʾdhinkumu alladhīna malakat aymānukum wa-alladhīna lam yablughū al-ḥuluma minkum thalātha marrātin min qabli ṣalāti al-fajri wa-ḥīna taḍaʿūna thiyābakum mina al-ẓahīrati wa-min baʿdi ṣalāti al-ʿishāʾi thalāthu ʿawrātin lakum laysa ʿalaykum wa-lā ʿalayhim junāḥun baʿdahunna ṭawwāfūna ʿalaykum baʿḍukum ʿalā baʿḍin kadhālika yubayyinu Allāhu lakumu al-āyāti wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm

Al-Nūr 24:58

Al-Nur 24:58

“You who believe, let those whom your right hands possess and those among you who have not reached puberty ask your permission at three times: before dawn salat, when you lay aside your clothes at midday, and after night salat. These are three times of privacy for you. Outside them there is no blame upon you or them, as you move about among one another. Thus The God makes the signs clear to you; The God is Knowing, Wise.”

الْعِشَاءِ occurs in صَلَاةِ الْعِشَاءِ.

It names a recognized period with which the salat is associated.

The root alone does not determine all details of the prayer's form, units, or precise timing. It identifies time; other features require their own evidence.

The verse concerns privacy at three times. Night salat helps identify a period of household privacy and rest.

This use accords with the temporal meaning.

عِشَاء and عَشَاء

عِشَاء with kasrah names nighttime; عَشَاء with fathah can name the meal eaten late in the day or early at night.

They are temporally connected but distinct.

Yusuf 12:16 gives the brothers' arrival time, not their dinner.

Al-Nur 24:58 names salat associated with nighttime, not eating.

7. عَشِيَّة: part of a day

عَشِيَّة occurs once in the Quran.

It denotes the latter part of a day, including an evening or a particular day's late hours.

The Quran states:

كَأَنَّهُمْ يَوْمَ يَرَوْنَهَا لَمْ يَلْبَثُوا إِلَّا عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَاهَا

ka-annahum yawma yarawnahā lam yalbathū illā ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:46

Al-Nazi'at 79:46

“On the day they see it, it will be as though they had remained only an evening or its day's forenoon.”

عَشِيَّةً is paired with ضُحَاهَا.

ضُحَى denotes the bright morning after sunrise, whereas عشيّة denotes late day. The pronoun in ضُحَاهَا is understood through the day containing that evening: its forenoon, not a forenoon within the evening itself.

The contrast conveys how brief their stay seems when they encounter that day.

It does not establish that actual life or the interval between death and resurrection lasted only a few earthly hours.

كَأَنَّهُمْ introduces the comparison, presenting it as though that were their duration.

The temporal noun keeps its meaning; the perceived brevity comes from the sentence as a whole.

عشيّة names part of the day's structure, not absolute darkness or the entire night.

8. عشي in descriptions of the hereafter

Two further occurrences matter for descriptions beyond earthly life.

Maryam states:

لَا يَسْمَعُونَ فِيهَا لَغْوًا إِلَّا سَلَامًا ۖ وَلَهُمْ رِزْقُهُمْ فِيهَا بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا

lā yasmaʿūna fīhā laghwan illā salāman wa-lahum rizquhum fīhā bukratan wa-ʿashiyyā

Maryam 19:62

Maryam 19:62

“They hear no idle speech there, but peace; and their provision is theirs there, morning and at day's end.”

بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا names two familiar parts of the day.

It may convey regular, recurring provision without independently explaining paradise's temporal cycle or establishing an earthly sun and sunset there.

Neither an identical earthly day-night cycle nor the absence of any real temporal meaning follows from the phrase alone.

Ghafir states:

النَّارُ يُعْرَضُونَ عَلَيْهَا غُدُوًّا وَعَشِيًّا ۖ وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ السَّاعَةُ أَدْخِلُوا آلَ فِرْعَوْنَ أَشَدَّ الْعَذَابِ

al-nāru yuʿraḍūna ʿalayhā ghuduwwan wa-ʿashiyyan wa-yawma taqūmu al-sāʿatu adkhilū āla Firʿawna ashadda al-ʿadhāb

Ghāfir 40:46

Ghafir 40:46

“The Fire: they are exposed to it morning and at day's end. On the day the Hour arises: Admit Pharaoh's people to the severest punishment.”

غُدُوًّا وَعَشِيًّا again names two periods.

It does not by itself offer a physical theory of sunlight, day length, or time after death.

The verse describes exposure to the Fire and then the event accompanying the Hour. The temporal meaning remains without importing further details.

9. Related roots and distinctions

ع ش و and ع م ي

ع م ي concerns blindness, inability to see, or loss of perception and discernment.

The visual branch of ع ش و emphasizes dim or unclear looking.

One person may see nothing; another may distinguish objects indistinctly.

Figurative usage can likewise distinguish failure to see truth from inattentiveness or looking away.

In al-Zukhruf 43:36, construction and context favor turning away from remembrance, rather than automatically requiring complete blindness.

This is a tendency, not an absolute rule excluding blindness from عشو or restricting عمي to complete physical loss of sight.

ع ش و and ب ص ر

بصر concerns sight and discernment.

The visual branch of عشو concerns seeing weakly or indistinctly.

Someone may therefore possess sight without sufficient clarity to distinguish something accurately.

In a guidance context, this image can distinguish outwardly seeing signs from understanding them correctly.

Not every intellectual mistake is automatically عشو; figurative interpretation requires actual usage.

ع ش و and غ ف ل

غ ف ل concerns heedlessness and lack of attention.

عشو in al-Zukhruf 43:36 concerns turning away from divine remembrance.

Both may involve inattention to truth, but their emphases differ.

Heedlessness may follow forgetting or distraction; عَشَا عَنْ here retains the image of averted vision and failed or withheld attention.

“Becomes heedless” is therefore not always a complete equivalent of يَعْشُ.

ع ش و and غ ش و

غ ش و, discussed alongside غ ش ي in some classifications, concerns covering and enveloping.

غِشَاوَة can denote a covering obstructing sight.

عشو instead concerns dim or unclear looking itself.

A covering over the eyes illustrates غشاوة; looking with impaired or indistinct sight illustrates عشو.

A possible cause should not be identified with the condition it may produce.

ع ش و and ظ ل م

Here ظُلْمَة, darkness, should be distinguished from ظُلْم, wrongdoing, in other branches.

Dark surroundings can hinder sight, but visual عشو concerns looking and discernment rather than simply naming environmental darkness.

Someone may struggle to see because the surroundings are dark or because their own sight is impaired despite adequate light.

Poor sight and environmental darkness are distinguishable, although connected in experience.

ع ش و and م س و: أَمْسَى

أَمْسَى belongs to م س و, not أ م س, and can mean entering or being in the evening.

عَشِيّ and عِشَاء also name late-day and nighttime periods.

A shared temporal field does not make their constructions or uses identical.

أَمْسَى is a verb expressing entry into evening; عَشِيّ and عِشَاء are temporal nouns.

Translation should preserve that morphological and syntactic difference.

10. Translation proposals for Forqan

Equivalents should distinguish the visual and temporal branches.

يَعْشُ — al-Zukhruf 43:36

Brief root meaning: Dim seeing, unclear looking, and orientation of the gaze.

Contextual meaning: Turns away from God's remembrance.

Proposed phrase translation:

وَمَنْ يَعْشُ عَنْ ذِكْرِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ

wa-man yaʿshu ʿan dhikri al-Raḥmān

Al-Zukhruf 43:36

“Whoever turns away from the remembrance of the Entirely Merciful.”

“Turns a blind eye to” is another rendering that retains more of the visual image while conveying disregard.

عِشَاءً — Yusuf 12:16

Brief temporal meaning: The onset of nighttime darkness.

Contextual meaning: At night.

Proposed translation:

“They came to their father at night, weeping.”

الْعِشَاءِ — al-Nur 24:58

Contextual meaning: Nighttime, in the expression for night salat.

Proposed translation:

“After night salat.”

عَشِيّ / عَشِيًّا / بِالْعَشِيِّ

Brief temporal meaning: The latter part of the day, approaching night.

Contextual meanings: Late day, afternoon, or evening, as appropriate.

Proposed rendering of بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا:

“Morning and at day's end.”

عَشِيَّةً — al-Nazi'at 79:46

Brief root meaning: The latter part of the day.

Contextual meaning: An evening or the latter part of one day.

Proposed translation:

“As though they had remained only an evening or its day's forenoon.”

عشي and عشاء should not invariably receive the same equivalent: the former usually concerns latter day, the latter darkness following it.

In the adopted reading of al-Zukhruf 43:36, “turns away” is preferred to “becomes blind,” while the visual background remains in the explanation.

Conclusion

ع ش و has two important fields: dim, unclear sight; and late day with the onset of night.

عَشَا يَعْشُو can describe weak looking; its prepositions can indicate turning toward or away.

Al-Zukhruf 43:36 concerns turning away from divine remembrance. The following verse describes satanic companionship, obstruction, and imagined guidance.

عَشِيّ and عَشِيَّة concern late day, while عِشَاء more often concerns the onset of nighttime darkness. Quranic uses locate ordinary events, invocation, glorification, salat, and descriptions of the hereafter.

Semantic proximity does not prove historical derivation, nor justify reducing every branch to blindness, darkness, or evening.

Keep two connected but distinct definitions: dim or unclear looking, with directional constructions toward or away; and late day with the onset of night. Morphology, preposition, and context determine each Quranic occurrence's meaning.
