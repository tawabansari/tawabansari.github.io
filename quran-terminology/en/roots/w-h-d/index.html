Short Introduction to the Root

The root و ح د moves in the semantic field of being one, being single, being alone, and treating something as one. In the Qur'an, this root is used both in ordinary counting and in expressing the oneness of The God. Even so, not every derivative in this family performs the same function, and وَاحِد, أَحَد, and وَحِيد should not be interpreted identically without attention to structure and context.

وَاحِد may be the number “one,” may specify one person or one thing among others, and in expressions such as إِلَهٌ وَاحِدٌ or الْوَاحِدُ الْقَهَّارُ it expresses the oneness of The God against partnership and multiplicity.

أَحَد in most of its Qur'anic uses appears in negative, conditional, interrogative, or general structures, and can mean “no one,” “none,” or “one person.” But in Surat al-Ikhlas, in the affirmative sentence اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ, it takes on an exceptional and very compressed use.

وَحِيد denotes being alone and appears only once in the Qur'an. Its grammatical attachment in the verse of al-Muddaththir must be examined carefully, and one analysis should not be declared certain without evidence.

Therefore, the core of this root is not merely “the number one.” In different uses, it extends from oneness in counting to oneness in divinity, absence of partner, absence of equal, and the aloneness of one person.

Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

The most important Qur'anic forms of this family are:

وَاحِد

One, single, or one individual from among a group. It is used both numerically and as a description of The God.

وَاحِدَة

The feminine form of واحد; used for one thing, one soul, one cry, one community, or one event.

أَحَد

One person, one, no one, or none, depending on sentence structure. In Surat al-Ikhlas it comes as a special description for The God.

إِحْدَى

One of two or more feminine matters, such as “one of the two daughters.”

إِحْدَاهُمَا / إِحْدَاكُمَا

One of the two, or one of you two.

وُحْدَان / فُرَادَى are not from this root and should not be mixed with it merely because of semantic closeness.

وَحِيد

Alone, solitary, or without companion. It occurs only in al-Muddaththir 74:11.

الْوَاحِد

A definite divine description that appears in several verses together with الْقَهَّار.

أَحَدًا

The accusative form of أحد, frequently seen in negative, conditional, or prohibitive structures.

Sharing a root or semantic field does not mean that all these forms express exactly the same degree of oneness or aloneness. Grammatical structure and the position of the word in the sentence play an essential role.

The Semantic Core of the Root

The shared core of this family can be stated as follows:

Something or someone being placed in the position of “one,” in such a way that it is set apart from multiplicity, partner, companion, or an equal of the same rank.

This core appears differently in each derivative.

In واحد, the main axis is being one in counting or being one against multiplicity.

In أحد, depending on structure, it may consider an unspecified individual, or in a negative sentence include all possible individuals, as in “no one.”

In وحيد, aloneness and lack of companion become prominent.

In descriptions of The God, واحد and أحد are both connected with oneness, but the precise difference between them must be examined from the actual Qur'anic uses, not from a prebuilt theological definition.

The Material and Linguistic Image of the Root

Unlike roots that have a concrete image such as cutting, covering, or splitting, و ح د is connected above all with counting and distinguishing one unit from other units.

When one says “one seed,” “one soul,” “one cry,” or “one community,” the mind separates one case from a set. This basic use allows the word to extend into other fields as well: one reference point, one worshiped one, one source of command, or one being without companion.

But one should not immediately conclude from the image of “one unit” that all divine uses are also numerical. In the phrase إِلَهٌ وَاحِدٌ, number and description are connected with the negation of partnership in divinity. In اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ, the sentence structure and the context of the surah create a meaning more compressed than simple counting.

On the Relation Between “Wahid” and “Ahad”

There is a clear historical and lexical connection between واحد and أحد, but the exact nature of this connection should not be made more certain than the evidence allows. Some lexicographers and linguists consider them two related forms from an ancient family, and some have proposed a phonetic development between waw and hamzah. Data from Semitic languages may also indicate kinship or antiquity in this semantic field.

Even so, similarity among Semitic forms is not by itself enough to extract all the grammatical and theological distinctions of the Qur'an from it. What is more certain for this article is the functional difference within the Qur'an itself:

  • واحد is used broadly in counting, description, and positive structures.
  • أحد in general use usually appears in negative, conditional, and interrogative structures.
  • اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ is an exceptional use in an affirmative sentence.

This functional difference is real; but conclusions such as “Ahad intrinsically negates every kind of philosophical composition” should be presented as reflection on the structure of the surah and the network of uses, not as a definitive morphological definition of the word.

Qur'anic Uses of “Wahid”

One Worshiped One

وَإِلَٰهُكُمْ إِلَٰهٌ وَاحِدٌ لَّا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الرَّحْمَٰنُ الرَّحِيمُ

“And your worshiped one is one worshiped one; there is no worshiped one except Him, The Merciful, The Compassionate.”

Al-Baqarah 2:163

In this verse, واحد comes in the expression إِلَهٌ وَاحِدٌ, and the following sentence clarifies its meaning: لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا هُوَ. Oneness here is directly connected with negating every other worshiped one.

The meaning “absence of partner in divinity” comes from the whole structure of the verse, not from the word واحد alone. واحد itself may also simply mean “one” in non-divine uses.

Address to the People of the Book

يَا أَهْلَ الْكِتَابِ لَا تَغْلُوا فِي دِينِكُمْ ... إِنَّمَا اللَّهُ إِلَٰهٌ وَاحِدٌ سُبْحَانَهُ أَنْ يَكُونَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ

“O People of the Book, do not exceed bounds in your religion... The God is only one worshiped one; exalted is He above having a child.”

An-Nisa 4:171

In this verse, إِلَهٌ وَاحِدٌ appears in a context that rejects attributing a child and excess concerning the Messiah. Therefore, oneness here is joined both with negation of partner and with negation of a structure that divides divinity among multiple persons or relations.

A detailed historical theory about every form of Trinity or every Christian current should not be built from this verse. What the verse itself explicitly says is the rejection of excess, the rejection of a child for The God, and the affirmation of one worshiped one.

One Worshiped One for All

قُلْ إِنَّمَا أَنَا بَشَرٌ مِّثْلُكُمْ يُوحَىٰ إِلَيَّ أَنَّمَا إِلَٰهُكُمْ إِلَٰهٌ وَاحِدٌ

“Say: I am only a human like you; it is revealed to me that your worshiped one is one worshiped one.”

Al-Kahf 18:110

In this verse, واحد is introduced as the center of revelation's message. The messenger identifies himself as a human being and sees the main difference in revelation; revelation that emphasizes the oneness of the worshiped one.

Do Not Make Three One

لَقَدْ كَفَرَ الَّذِينَ قَالُوا إِنَّ اللَّهَ ثَالِثُ ثَلَاثَةٍ وَمَا مِنْ إِلَٰهٍ إِلَّا إِلَٰهٌ وَاحِدٌ

“Those who said The God is the third of three have covered truth; while there is no worshiped one except one worshiped one.”

Al-Maidah 5:73

In this verse, إِلَهٌ وَاحِدٌ stands openly against the structure “third of three.” This is one of the clearest Qur'anic uses of واحد in negating multiplicity in divinity.

Here too, it is better for the text of the verse to be understood as it is. Turning it into “a political statement against Najran” or a specific theory about the history of sects requires external evidence and is not part of the meaning of the root.

Al-Wahid Al-Qahhar

قُلْ مَن رَّبُّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ قُلِ اللَّهُ ... قُلِ اللَّهُ خَالِقُ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ وَهُوَ الْوَاحِدُ الْقَهَّارُ

“Say: Who is the Lord of the heavens and the earth? Say: The God... Say: The God is the Creator of every thing, and He is The One, The Overwhelming.”

Ar-Rad 13:16

In this verse, الواحد is joined with القهار. Oneness comes from the root و ح د, and comprehensive overpowering mastery comes from ق ه ر. The meaning of overpowering and mastery should not be inserted into the definition of “one,” but the co-occurrence of these two descriptions shows that divine oneness in this context is joined with the absence of a rival in creation and management.

The same combination appears in other verses as well:

لِمَنِ الْمُلْكُ الْيَوْمَ لِلَّهِ الْوَاحِدِ الْقَهَّارِ

“To whom belongs dominion today? To The God, The One, The Overwhelming.”

Ghafir 40:16

أَأَرْبَابٌ مُّتَفَرِّقُونَ خَيْرٌ أَمِ اللَّهُ الْوَاحِدُ الْقَهَّارُ

“Are scattered lords better, or The God, The One, The Overwhelming?”

Yusuf 12:39

In the verse of Yusuf, the contrast between “scattered lords” and الواحد is direct. This context clarifies that واحد here is not merely a number, but the negation of scattered authority and lordship.

Numerical and General Uses of “Wahid”

One Food

لَن نَّصْبِرَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامٍ وَاحِدٍ

“We will not endure one food.”

Al-Baqarah 2:61

In this verse, واحد is simply a number and description of “one kind.” No special theological or philosophical meaning is taken from it.

One Soul

مَّا خَلْقُكُمْ وَلَا بَعْثُكُمْ إِلَّا كَنَفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ

“Your creation and your resurrection are only like one soul.”

Luqman 31:28

واحدة is the feminine form of واحد and agrees with نفس. The meaning of comparison and the ease of collective creation comes from the full structure of the verse; the word itself only conveys “one.”

One Cry

إِن كَانَتْ إِلَّا صَيْحَةً وَاحِدَةً

“It was nothing but one cry.”

This structure appears in several verses. واحدة expresses the number of the event. Intensity, suddenness, or the result of the cry comes from صيحة and the context.

One Community

إِنَّ هَٰذِهِ أُمَّتُكُمْ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً

“This community of yours is one community.”

Al-Anbiya 21:92؛ Al-Muminun 23:52

Here واحد points to the unity of the community, but the type of this unity must be understood from context. The verse does not necessarily speak of one political organization or sameness of all customs and laws. Connection to one Lord and the path of servitude is closer to the context.

Qur'anic Uses of “Ahad”

Use in Negation and Exhaustiveness

فَمَا مِنكُم مِّنْ أَحَدٍ عَنْهُ حَاجِزِينَ

“Then none of you could have withheld him from it.”

Al-Haqqah 69:47

In this structure, مِنْ أَحَدٍ comes in a negative context and includes all possible individuals. The meaning “no one” is formed by the combination of negation and the exhaustive extra من with أحد, not from أحد alone.

No One From Them Is to Be Obeyed

فَلَا تُطِعْ مِنْهُمْ آثِمًا أَوْ كَفُورًا ... وَلَا تُطِعْ مِنْهُمْ أَحَدًا

In prohibitive or negative structures, أحدًا can mean “no one” or “none.” The comprehensive force comes from the structure of the sentence.

One of the Women

يَا نِسَاءَ النَّبِيِّ لَسْتُنَّ كَأَحَدٍ مِّنَ النِّسَاءِ

“O wives of the prophet, you are not like any one of the women...”

Al-Ahzab 33:32

Here أحد comes with من النساء. Grammatically, the word functions in a comparative and negative structure. The natural translation is “like none of the women” or “like no one among the women.”

This verse shows that أحد can also be used for a feminine group in a fixed form and does not necessarily agree with grammatical gender like an ordinary adjective.

One of the Two

قَالَتْ إِحْدَاهُمَا يَا أَبَتِ اسْتَأْجِرْهُ

“One of the two women said: O my father, hire him.”

Al-Qasas 28:26

إحداهما is the feminine construct form of this family and means “one of the two.” This use shows that the field of أحد is not restricted to negation and theology.

Do Not Call Upon Anyone Along With Him

وَأَنَّ الْمَسَاجِدَ لِلَّهِ فَلَا تَدْعُوا مَعَ اللَّهِ أَحَدًا

“And the places of worship belong to The God, so do not call upon anyone along with The God.”

Al-Jinn 72:18

أحدًا in a prohibitive structure negates every possible one who might be called upon. Here the root serves the negation of partner in supplication and worship.

“The God Is Ahad” in Surat al-Ikhlas

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ۝ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ

“Say: He is The God, One and Unique; The God, The Self-Sufficient Refuge; He neither begot nor was begotten; and no one is equal to Him.”

Al-Ikhlas 112:1-4

The use of أحد at the opening of the surah is exceptional, because the word appears in an affirmative sentence and without a qualifier as a description of The God. At the end of the surah, أحد also appears in the negative structure وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ.

There is a clear connection between these two uses:

At the beginning, The God is introduced as أحد.

At the end, the existence of any أحد who could be equal to Him is denied.

From the complete structure of the surah one can understand that أحد here is not merely “one as opposed to two.” The following verses negate begetting, being begotten, and equality, while الصمد also highlights His independent and need-free finality.

But three levels must be distinguished:

Lexical meaning: Ahad is connected with one and one individual, and in negative structures it can negate all individuals.

Contextual analysis: In Surat al-Ikhlas, Ahad, together with as-Samad, negation of begetting, and negation of any equal, expresses the incomparable oneness of The God.

Philosophical reflection: One may say that this oneness does not place Him in a class with individuals of the same kind and rejects the idea of “one among several examples.” This conclusion comes from the whole surah and the tawhidi network of the Qur'an, not from the form or pattern of the word alone.

Therefore, translating اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ as “The God is One and Unique” is more fitting than “The God is one.” But explanations such as “without parts, indivisible, and outside every category” should not be presented without distinction as a definitive morphological definition of Ahad.

The Use of “Wahid” in Al-Muddaththir

ذَرْنِي وَمَنْ خَلَقْتُ وَحِيدًا ۝ وَجَعَلْتُ لَهُ مَالًا مَّمْدُودًا ۝ وَبَنِينَ شُهُودًا

“Leave Me with the one whom I created alone; and I gave him extended wealth and sons present.”

Al-Muddaththir 74:11-13

The grammatical attachment of وحيدًا is a matter of discussion.

The common analysis is that وحيدًا is a circumstantial accusative from the object of خلقت: the one whom I created alone. In that case, the following verses show that wealth and children were then given to him. But from this structure alone, details such as “naked in his mother's womb” or “necessarily without wealth and children at the moment of birth” cannot be inserted as a definitive lexical meaning.

Another analysis takes وحيدًا as a circumstantial accusative from the subject, that is, The God: I created him while I alone was the one creating him. This possibility has been raised grammatically, but the distance of the circumstantial accusative from the subject and the natural direction of the context mean that it should not be declared certain or superior without evidence.

Some have also taken وحيدًا as a description of that person's standing, meaning singular and prominent among his people. Since several grammatical and semantic possibilities exist, the article should report them and avoid making one certain.

A cautious rendering can be:

“Leave Me with the one whom I created alone.”

Then the explanation should mention that the attachment of وحيدًا to the object, the subject, or the person's standing is debated.

Divine Descriptions in This Root

الْوَاحِد

الواحد is an explicit description of The God in the Qur'an. This description appears in verses with القهار and stands against scattered lords, partners, and claimants to independent power.

Its base meaning is “The One.” The negation of partner in lordship, creation, or dominion comes from the context of the verses.

أَحَد

أحد in اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ is an explicit and special description for The God. Unlike الواحد, this form appears without the definite article.

Its grammatical indefiniteness here does not mean unknown or one among several cases. An indefinite predicate in an Arabic nominal sentence is a normal structure. The special meaning is understood from the sentence itself, its place in the surah, and the continuation of the verses.

Therefore, the mere absence of the definite article should not by itself be made the basis of a philosophical theory. What is stronger is the co-occurrence of أحد with الصمد, the negation of begetting, and the negation of every equal.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

Wahid as Number and Adjective

واحد can function like an ordinary number and adjective: it may accompany a noun, have masculine and feminine forms, take dual and plural forms, and appear in various structures. This feature makes it suitable for counting and general description.

Ahad in Negative Structures

In sentences such as:

مَا جَاءَنِي أَحَدٌ

The meaning “no one” comes from negation. أحد outside negation is not always “no one”; the examples اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ and إحداهما make this clear.

Therefore, it is better to say that Ahad in negative structures has the capacity to exhaust all individuals, not that it is inherently and in every use a general noun of negation.

Ahad and Grammatical Gender

In some general structures, Ahad can be used without change for masculine and feminine, as in لَسْتُنَّ كَأَحَدٍ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ. But its family also has the feminine form إحدى. Therefore, an absolute rule about the absence of a feminine form should not be built from one use.

The Indefiniteness of “Ahad”

In اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ, Ahad is an indefinite predicate. Indefiniteness in this structure does not mean “one unspecified,” because the subject is definite and the context is specific. This grammatical point should be kept separate from philosophical reflections.

Comparison With Nearby Roots

و ح د Compared with ف ر د

ف ر د in the Qur'an tends toward coming alone, being left alone, or a person being separated:

وَلَقَدْ جِئْتُمُونَا فُرَادَىٰ

“And you have come to Us alone.”

Al-Anam 6:94

وَكُلُّهُمْ آتِيهِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ فَرْدًا

“And every one of them will come to Him on the Day of Rising alone.”

Maryam 19:95

In these uses, فرد focuses on the aloneness of a person against companions, possessions, or worldly ties. واحد focuses more on being one and negating multiplicity, and أحد in specific structures denotes one individual or comprehensive negation of individuals.

One should not say “fard can never be used for The God.” Qur'anic study can only say that the Qur'an has not used فرد as a direct divine description. Uses outside the Qur'an are not the subject of this article, and Qur'anic absence by itself does not produce a universal ruling about all religious language.

و ح د Compared with ش ر ك

ش ر ك denotes making a partner, considering something to share, or connecting divine power and right to another. In verses such as إِلَهٌ وَاحِدٌ, wahid stands against the multiplicity of worshiped ones and partners.

Even so, wahid and shirk are not opposites in every use. In “one food” or “one cry,” there is no theological contrast with shirk. This contrast takes shape only in tawhidi contexts.

و ح د Compared with ص م د

In Surat al-Ikhlas, أحد and الصمد appear together. Ahad expresses oneness, and Samad has another semantic field connected with being the final refuge, needlessness, and being intended.

The meaning of Samad should not be inserted into Ahad, nor the reverse. The surah creates a fuller picture by placing the two descriptions together.

و ح د Compared with ك ف أ

كُفْء at the end of Surat al-Ikhlas refers to an equal, counterpart, or one of the same rank. The absence of an equal is understood from كفوا and the negative structure. أحد alone is not the word “equal.”

This distinction is important: although اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ and لَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ explain one another, the negation of equality comes from the complete sentence at the end of the surah.

Challenges of Understanding and Translation

One error is to treat واحد and أحد as completely synonymous and translate both in all cases only as “one.” This hides the structural difference between Surat al-Ikhlas and the verses of إِلَهٌ وَاحِدٌ.

The opposite error is to turn the difference between the two into a fixed philosophical system and place all concepts such as negation of part, negation of composition, negation of genus, and negation of category directly inside the lexical meaning of Ahad. Some of these conclusions may be compatible with the whole surah and the tawhidi logic of the Qur'an, but they should be presented as analysis and reflection, not as a definitive dictionary definition.

Likewise, وحيد in al-Muddaththir should not be translated and interpreted with certainty on the basis of one grammatical possibility.

Historical claims such as saying that الواحد was directly a political statement against Sasanian Manichaeism or the Trinity of Najran should not enter the root article without independent historical evidence. The Qur'an certainly confronts polytheism, scattered lords, attribution of child, and the structure “third of three”; these Qur'anic evidences are enough for analysis of the root.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For واحد in counting: “one.”

For واحدة: “one,” with natural agreement in English.

For إِلَهٌ وَاحِدٌ: “one worshiped one.”

For الواحد as a divine description: “The One” or “One.”

For اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ: “The God is One and Unique,” or in a more compressed structure, “The God is uniquely One.”

For أحد in negative structures: “no one,” “none,” or “one person,” depending on the sentence.

For إحداهما: “one of the two.”

For وحيدًا in al-Muddaththir 74:11: “alone,” together with explanation about the disputed grammatical attachment.

In translating اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ, it is better not to place the whole philosophical analysis inside one long equivalent. “One and Unique” is the base rendering, and the explanation of incomparability and negation of every likeness comes in the analysis of the surah.

Conclusion

The root و ح د in the Qur'an begins from being one and one individual, but in different contexts it expands into oneness, unity, aloneness, and negation of partner.

واحد is the broader and more ordinary form of this family. In counting it means “one,” and in expressions such as إِلَهٌ وَاحِدٌ and الواحد القهار it expresses the oneness of The God against multiple worshiped ones, scattered lords, and partners.

أحد in negative structures can include all possible individuals and be translated as “no one” or “none.” But in اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ it appears exceptionally in an affirmative sentence. The deep meaning of this use is obtained from the whole of Surat al-Ikhlas: The God is One and Unique, Samad, neither begotten nor begetting, and has no equal.

وحيد denotes aloneness, but its grammatical attachment in al-Muddaththir 74:11 is not certain and valid possibilities should remain open.

The final definition of this root can be stated as follows:

و ح د expresses being one or being placed in the position of one; a meaning that, depending on structure and context, can convey numerical counting, oneness, unity, aloneness, or negation of partner and equal. The difference between واحد, أحد, and وحيد is understood from the actual uses of each form in the Qur'an, not from a prebuilt philosophical or morphological rule.
