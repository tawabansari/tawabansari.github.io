1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root س ق ي moves in the semantic field of giving water, giving drink, bringing a drink, providing watering, and asking for water.

The center of this root includes three elements:

1. a drink or liquid that can be drunk;

2. someone or something that brings it or makes it available;

3. a human being, animal, or land that needs it.

This root is not limited to water. The Qur'an uses it for water, milk, drinks of Hell, and a pure drink of Paradise. Therefore its basic meaning is better understood as giving drink or bringing a drink, not only giving water.

Important forms include سَقَى, أَسْقَى, اسْتَسْقَى, and سِقَايَة. Differences between سَقَى and أَسْقَى are reported in lexical sources and in some uses, but this difference should not become an absolute law in which سَقَى must always mean directly placing drink in the mouth and أَسْقَى must always mean building an indirect source or system. The exact meaning of each form must be known from its objects, syntax, and context.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

سَقَى / يَسْقِي

He brought water or drink to someone or something and gave it to drink.

This form appears in the Qur'an for different cases:

  • shepherds watering flocks;
  • Musa watering the flock of two women;
  • giving a drink of Paradise;
  • giving hard and unpleasant drinks in punishment.

Thus سَقَى by itself is neither positive nor negative. The value and quality of the drink are known from the object and context.

أَسْقَى / يُسْقِي

He brought water or drink to someone, placed it at his disposal, or caused him to drink.

This form is used in the Qur'an for water sent down from the sky and milk from livestock. In these uses, the drink is made available through a natural source or process. This pattern may show a tendency toward making drink available or causing watering, but it should not become an absolute rule for all Arabic usage.

اسْتَسْقَى

He asked for water or sought watering for someone else.

This form appears in the story of Musa when he requested water for his people.

سِقَايَة

This word has two Qur'anic uses:

  • a vessel or cup used for drinking;
  • the work, responsibility, or service of providing water to pilgrims.

Its exact referent is known from context.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core may be stated as:

Bringing or making available a drinkable liquid for someone or something that benefits from it.

This core looks at the giver or provider. By contrast, the root ش ر ب focuses on the act of the drinker.

In a simple event there may be two separate verbs:

  • someone brings the drink: سَقَى;
  • another drinks it: شَرِبَ.

Saqy does not necessarily mean complete quenching of thirst. Someone may be given drink, but the amount may be small, or the drink may even be unpleasant and punitive. Therefore quenching is suitable in some verses, but should not replace giving drink everywhere.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The simple material image of this root is bringing a vessel or flow of drink to a needy recipient.

This can happen in several ways:

  • a person gives a vessel to another;
  • a shepherd brings an animal to water;
  • someone draws water from a well;
  • rain makes water available to earth and people;
  • milk is produced in livestock and becomes available to the drinker.

These images show that the root concerns the relation between source, provider, and recipient. But this relation should not be turned into terms such as cellular pumping, hydraulic network, frequency transfer, or information flow. These are modern scientific terms and are not present in the base meaning of the root.

6. Use of the Root in Arabic

In Arabic, سَقَاهُ مَاءً means he gave him water or gave him water to drink. The verb may take two objects: the one who is given drink, and the drink given to him.

For example:

سَقَاهُمْ شَرَابًا

He gave them a drink.

The verb can also be used for animals even when water is not placed directly into their mouths. One who brings the flock to water and makes drinking possible has also performed saqy.

This shows that defining سَقَى as directly placing a cup to the mouth is too narrow. In the story of Madyan, the people and Musa water flocks; they do not pour water by hand into the mouth of every animal.

Lexical sources sometimes distinguish between سَقَى and أَسْقَى, such as one leaning toward actual giving of drink and the other toward providing water or the means of drinking. Yet sources and Arabic usage do not always maintain one identical and absolute boundary between the two. The article should speak of Qur'anic tendencies, not unchanging morphological law.

7. Challenges in Understanding and Translation

Restricting the root to water

The Qur'an uses this root for milk, pure heavenly drink, and drinks of punishment. Giving drink is therefore broader and often more precise than giving water.

Making saqy the same as drinking

Saqy is the act of the giver or provider; shurb is the act of the drinker. They may be related in one event, but speak from two different angles.

Absolutizing the difference between سَقَى and أَسْقَى

In some Qur'anic uses, سَقَى is closer to the act of giving drink, while أَسْقَى is linked with drink being made available through rain or livestock. This is important, but one cannot say:

  • سَقَى is always direct;
  • أَسْقَى always builds infrastructure or a network;
  • سَقَى is always superior or more intimate;
  • أَسْقَى is always earthly while سَقَى is always heavenly or otherworldly.

These conclusions are not proven by the morphological form itself.

Loading the root with positive meaning

Water and drink are often connected with life and ease, but not every use of the root is positive. The Qur'an also speaks of being given boiling water, pus-like liquid, and hard drinks.

Thus positive or negative meaning comes from the type of drink and context, not from the root alone.

Turning سِقَايَة into a technical term

In the story of Yusuf, سِقَايَة may refer to a cup or drinking vessel, and in At-Tawbah 9:19 to the service of providing water to pilgrims. Translations such as distribution system, resource platform, or hydraulic logistics are outside natural language and make the meaning of the verse heavy.

8. Methodological Note

In each verse these questions should be asked separately:

  • Who gives drink?
  • Is the recipient human, animal, or land?
  • What is the drink?
  • Is the drink explicitly mentioned in the sentence?
  • Does the verb focus on bringing the drink or asking for it?
  • From which word is the positive or negative nature of the event known?
  • Does the morphological form truly prove a difference, or does the difference mostly come from context?

These levels should also be distinguished:

Direct meaning: bringing or providing drink.

Result: relief of thirst, growth of land, ease, pain, or punishment.

Contextual reflection: water as a sign of mercy, social service, or heavenly drink.

Result and reflection should not be merged into the basic translation of the root.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

Watering flocks in Madyan

Al-Qasas 28:23 says:

وَلَمَّا وَرَدَ مَاءَ مَدْيَنَ وَجَدَ عَلَيْهِ أُمَّةً مِنَ النَّاسِ يَسْقُونَ وَوَجَدَ مِنْ دُونِهِمُ امْرَأَتَيْنِ تَذُودَانِ قَالَ مَا خَطْبُكُمَا قَالَتَا لَا نَسْقِي حَتَّىٰ يُصْدِرَ الرِّعَاءُ وَأَبُونَا شَيْخٌ كَبِيرٌ

When he reached the water of Madyan, he found there a community of people watering, and apart from them he found two women holding back. He said: What is your matter? They said: We do not water until the shepherds return, and our father is an elderly old man.

Al-Qasas 28:23

يَسْقُونَ and نَسْقِي concern watering flocks. This use shows that سَقَى is not only bringing a cup to a mouth. The shepherd brings the flock to the source of water, draws water from the well, or makes it possible for the animals to drink.

In the next verse:

فَسَقَىٰ لَهُمَا ثُمَّ تَوَلَّىٰ إِلَى الظِّلِّ

So he watered for the two of them; then he turned back toward the shade.

Al-Qasas 28:24

The pronoun لَهُمَا shows that Musa did this for the benefit of the two women. The direct object is omitted, but the context makes clear that he watered their flock.

Asking for water for the people

Al-Baqarah 2:60 says:

وَإِذِ اسْتَسْقَىٰ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِ فَقُلْنَا اضْرِبْ بِعَصَاكَ الْحَجَرَ

And when Musa asked for water for his people, We said: Strike the stone with your staff.

Al-Baqarah 2:60

اسْتَسْقَىٰ is Form X and in this verse means asking for water. The lām in لِقَوْمِهِ shows that the request was for his people.

Heavenly water being made available

Al-Hijr 15:22 says:

وَأَرْسَلْنَا الرِّيَاحَ لَوَاقِحَ فَأَنْزَلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَأَسْقَيْنَاكُمُوهُ وَمَا أَنْتُمْ لَهُ بِخَازِنِينَ

And We sent the winds as fertilizing, then sent down water from the sky and gave it to you to drink, and you are not its keepers.

Al-Hijr 15:22

In فَأَسْقَيْنَاكُمُوهُ two object pronouns are present: كُم, you, and هُ, that water. The simple meaning is that We brought that water to you or gave it to you to drink.

Milk of livestock

An-Nahl 16:66 says:

وَإِنَّ لَكُمْ فِي الْأَنْعَامِ لَعِبْرَةً نُسْقِيكُمْ مِمَّا فِي بُطُونِهِ مِنْ بَيْنِ فَرْثٍ وَدَمٍ لَبَنًا خَالِصًا سَائِغًا لِلشَّارِبِينَ

And for you in the livestock there is surely a lesson: from what is in their bellies, from between digestive matter and blood, We give you pure milk, easy for the drinkers.

An-Nahl 16:66

The drink is specified by لَبَنًا خَالِصًا, and سَائِغًا لِلشَّارِبِينَ describes its being easy and pleasant for drinkers.

Pure drink in Paradise

Al-Insan 76:21 says:

عَالِيَهُمْ ثِيَابُ سُنْدُسٍ خُضْرٌ وَإِسْتَبْرَقٌ وَحُلُّوا أَسَاوِرَ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ وَسَقَاهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ شَرَابًا طَهُورًا

Upon them are garments of green fine silk and brocade, and they are adorned with bracelets of silver, and their Lord gives them a pure drink.

Al-Insan 76:21

The structure is clear: the subject is their Lord; the recipients are them; the drink is شَرَابًا طَهُورًا. طَهُورًا is the description of the drink.

Drinks of punishment

Ibrahim 14:16 says:

مِنْ وَرَائِهِ جَهَنَّمُ وَيُسْقَىٰ مِنْ مَاءٍ صَدِيدٍ

Before him is Jahannam, and he is given to drink from pus-like water.

Ibrahim 14:16

Muhammad 47:15 says:

وَسُقُوا مَاءً حَمِيمًا فَقَطَّعَ أَمْعَاءَهُمْ

And they are given boiling water to drink, and it cuts their intestines.

Muhammad 47:15

Al-Ghashiyah 88:5 says:

تُسْقَىٰ مِنْ عَيْنٍ آنِيَةٍ

They are given drink from a boiling spring.

Al-Ghashiyah 88:5

These verses show that س ق ي does not inherently indicate mercy, life-giving, pleasantness, or quenching. The type of drink and its result are determined by words such as صَدِيد, حَمِيم, and آنِيَة.

سِقَايَة in the story of Yusuf

Yusuf 12:70 says:

فَلَمَّا جَهَّزَهُمْ بِجَهَازِهِمْ جَعَلَ السِّقَايَةَ فِي رَحْلِ أَخِيهِ

When he prepared them with their equipment, he placed the siqayah in the baggage of his brother.

Yusuf 12:70

Here سِقَايَة is an object that can be placed in baggage. It therefore points to a vessel, cup, or drinking implement.

سِقَايَة الحاج

At-Tawbah 9:19 says:

أَجَعَلْتُمْ سِقَايَةَ الْحَاجِّ وَعِمَارَةَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ كَمَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَجَاهَدَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ

Have you made providing water to the pilgrims and maintaining the Sacred Mosque like one who has faith in The God and the Last Day and strives in the path of The God?

At-Tawbah 9:19

In this verse, سِقَايَة is not an object, but the work and service of providing water.

10. Examination of Important Qur'anic Forms

سَقَى

In Qur'anic uses it may mean watering an animal, giving drink to a human, being given drink in the passive, giving a heavenly drink, or giving a drink of punishment. Its shared meaning is bringing a drink, not absolute directness or pleasantness.

أَسْقَى

In clear verses it appears with heavenly water and the milk of livestock. These cases show a tendency toward making drink available through a source or process. But since the verb still takes both person and drink as objects, translations such as giving drink or bringing to drink remain natural.

اسْتَسْقَى

It is seeking or asking for water. In Al-Baqarah 2:60, Musa asks for water for his people.

سِقَايَة

A noun with two context-dependent uses: a drinking vessel, or the act and responsibility of providing water.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

Two-object structure

In sentences such as:

فَأَسْقَيْنَاكُمُوهُ

سَقَاهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ شَرَابًا

the verb is connected with the recipient and the drink.

But this structure alone does not prove transfer of ownership, complete absorption into the body, or direct injection. Syntax clarifies who gives drink, who receives it, and what is given.

Omission of the object

In يَسْقُونَ, لَا نَسْقِي, and فَسَقَىٰ لَهُمَا, the drink or animal being watered is not explicitly named, but the context of the well and flocks clarifies it.

The lām in لِقَوْمِهِ

In اسْتَسْقَىٰ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِ, the lām shows that Musa's request was for the benefit of his people.

Passive structure

In punishment verses, forms such as يُسْقَىٰ, سُقُوا, and تُسْقَىٰ appear. The passive directs attention to being given drink and to the type of drink, not to naming the agent.

12. The Difference between سَقَى and أَسْقَى

A usage tendency can be seen between the two forms.

سَقَى

It more often highlights the act of giving drink or watering itself. Examples include Musa watering the flock, the Lord giving drink to the people of Paradise, and the giving of boiling or pus-like water in punishment.

أَسْقَى

In prominent Qur'anic uses, it is connected with making drink available from a natural source: water sent down from the sky, and milk within livestock.

But this difference must be stated as a tendency, not an absolute rule. سَقَى in the story of Madyan is also not necessarily placing water by hand into the mouth; it is watering a flock. أَسْقَى can also take personal and drink objects in a fully transitive structure. Lexical sources do not always report one fixed boundary between the two.

A cautious conclusion is:

In the Qur'an, سَقَى more often highlights the act of giving drink itself, while أَسْقَى in clear examples is associated with bringing or making drink available from a source; but the boundary is not absolute or mechanical.

13. س ق ي Compared with Near Roots

س ق ي compared with ش ر ب

س ق ي speaks from the angle of the giver or provider. ش ر ب speaks from the angle of the drinker. In An-Nahl 16:66, The God gives drink and humans are drinkers: نُسْقِيكُمْ ... لِلشَّارِبِينَ.

س ق ي compared with ر و ي

Saqy is the act of bringing drink. Rayy and riwā' are connected with quenching thirst and reaching satiation. Someone may be given drink without being fully quenched, and the drink may not be for relief of thirst, as in punishment verses.

س ق ي compared with غ ي ث

Ghayth concerns rain or relief that comes at a time of need. Saqy concerns bringing or giving liquid to drink.

س ق ي compared with م ط ر

Matar focuses on raining or rain. Saqy focuses on the effect of giving drink or watering. Rain may cause watering of land, humans, or animals, but the two words speak about different parts of the event.

س ق ي compared with ن ه ل

Nahal in Arabic is connected with drinking and benefiting from water. Saqy highlights the act of bringing the drink. The exact boundaries should be studied from actual uses, not absolutized from short dictionary definitions.

14. Siqayah as Social Service

Providing water in dry environments is an essential service. The Qur'an in Al-Qasas 28:23 to 24 shows a scene where access to water is linked with turn-taking, physical power, and the presence of shepherds.

The two women keep their flock back and cannot water before the shepherds return. Musa waters for them without asking for payment and then returns to the shade.

From this context one can reflect on helping the needy, responsible use of strength, and opening access to a public resource. But these are contextual messages. The verb سقى alone does not contain all of these ethical meanings.

In At-Tawbah 9:19, providing water to pilgrims is also presented as a known and important act, but the Qur'an does not allow the social status of a service alone to be counted as the criterion of faith-based superiority.

15. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

سَقَى

Suitable equivalents:

  • gave drink
  • gave water
  • provided water
  • quenched

The choice depends on the recipient and the drink.

For a human and a drink: gave drink.

For an animal or flock: gave water or watered.

Where the result of relief from thirst is clear: quenched.

أَسْقَى

Suitable equivalents:

  • gave drink
  • brought to drink
  • made available for you to drink
  • gave you to drink from it

The base translation should not use phrases such as created a water-distribution network, unless separately explained with clear evidence.

اسْتَسْقَى

  • asked for water
  • requested water
  • asked for water for others

In Al-Baqarah 2:60:

Musa asked for water for his people.

سِقَايَة in Yusuf 12:70

  • cup
  • drinking cup
  • drinking vessel

سِقَايَة in At-Tawbah 9:19

  • providing water to the pilgrims
  • giving water to the pilgrims
  • the service of providing water to the pilgrims

Al-Insan 76:21

And their Lord gives them a pure drink.

Al-Hijr 15:22

Then We sent down water from the sky and gave it to you to drink; and you are not its keepers.

Or more explanatory:

Then We sent down water from the sky and made it available for you to drink; and you are not its storers.

An-Nahl 16:66

From what is in the bellies of livestock, We give you pure and pleasant milk to drink.

Al-Qasas 28:24

So Musa watered the flock of the two women; then he turned back toward the shade.

16. Final Definitions of the Derivatives

سَقَى

He brought water or drink to a human, animal, or another recipient and gave him drink or water.

أَسْقَى

He caused drink to reach someone or made it available to him; in Qur'anic usage it is sometimes connected with a natural source of water or milk.

اسْتَسْقَى

He requested water or asked for water for another.

سِقَايَة

A drinking vessel, or the work and responsibility of providing water.

17. Conclusion

The root س ق ي in the Qur'an indicates bringing, providing, or requesting drink. Its center is the relation between drink, provider, and recipient.

This root is not only about water. The Qur'an uses it for water, milk, pure heavenly drink, and drinks of punishment. Therefore giving drink is often broader than giving water.

Qur'anic uses show that people and Musa water flocks; Musa asks for water for his people; The God makes heavenly water and milk of livestock available to humans; the Lord gives the people of Paradise a pure drink; in punishment verses people are given hot and painful drinks; and سِقَايَة can mean either a drinking cup or the service of providing water.

Between سَقَى and أَسْقَى a tendency can be seen: سَقَى more often focuses on the act of giving drink, while أَسْقَى in clear examples is linked with bringing drink through a source or process. But this difference is not absolute and must not be turned into a theory of direct transfer, networks, pumping, frequency, or infrastructure.

A final cautious definition is:

س ق ي indicates bringing or making water and other drinks available for a human, animal, or needy recipient. In Form X it indicates asking for water, and in سِقَايَة it can mean a drinking vessel or water-providing service. The kind of drink, the manner of giving it, and its result are known from the structure and context of each verse, not from the root alone.
