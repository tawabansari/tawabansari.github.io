1. Introduction to the Root

The central word of the material ي د ي in the Qur'an is يَد. Its first and material meaning is "hand": the limb by which a human being takes, gives, makes, writes, strikes, holds back, gives generously, makes a pledge, and turns a decision into action.

Yet يَد in the Qur'an is not always limited to the physical hand. Because the hand is one of the clearest instruments for carrying out human will, this word expands in Arabic and in Qur'anic usage into several related fields: ability to do something; practical power; authority and possibility of disposal; ownership or being in one's control; human action and responsibility; generosity and favor; support and protection; covenant and commitment; and, in certain constructions, what is before, present, or prior.

The proposed short meaning is: hand; and by semantic extension, the instrument and sign of ability, action, authority, disposal, and giving.

These extensions are not scattered or unrelated. They all grow from one central image: the hand is the instrument by which will becomes action in the world. A person may want something, decide something, or hold an intention inwardly; but when that will becomes outward action, the hand is one of its most important means.

For this reason, language can use the hand in place of the action itself, the ability to perform the action, the authority to act, or the responsibility that results from action.

At the same time, not every use of يَد should be reduced to one figurative meaning such as "power." In each verse one must ask whether the intended meaning is the physical hand, ability to act, an action already performed, authority and ownership, generosity and favor, or a special image created by that verse's construction.

2. Note on the Construction of the Root

يَد is a very old and short word in Arabic. In the morphological sources there is some disagreement about the deleted original letter and the historical reconstruction of its material. Many root dictionaries place it under ي د ي, while some analyses explain its older structure in another way.

For the three-letter root system of Forqan, the heading ي د ي can be used. But it should be remembered that the Qur'anic word يَد itself is an ancient short noun, and not all of its features should be explained by simple analogy with regular three-letter verbs.

The main forms are:

يَد: one hand.

يَدَانِ: two hands in the nominative state.

يَدَيْنِ: two hands in the accusative or genitive state.

أَيْدِي: hands.

أَيْدٍ: an indefinite and tanwin-bearing form of the plural in some grammatical positions.

يَدِي، يَدُهُ، يَدِهِ، يَدَكَ، يَدَيْكَ and similar forms: forms attached to pronouns.

The word يَد is grammatically feminine in Arabic by convention. That means that, although it has no visible feminine ending, adjectives and verbs related to it may come in feminine form.

In Al-Ma'idah 5:64 it says:

بَلْ يَدَاهُ مَبْسُوطَتَانِ

مَبْسُوطَتَانِ is a dual feminine adjective, because it returns to يَدَاهُ.

Al-Ma'idah 5:64

Knowing that يَد is grammatically feminine is important for understanding the syntax of the verses. But this grammatical feature, by itself, does not create a symbolic or theological meaning.

3. Difference between أَيْدِي and أَيْد

One of the most important morphological points in this article is the distinction between two forms that appear similar:

أَيْدِي, the plural of يَد.

أَيْد, from the material أ ي د, meaning strength and power.

In Adh-Dhariyat 51:47 it says:

وَالسَّمَاءَ بَنَيْنَاهَا بِأَيْدٍ

In this verse, بِأَيْدٍ means "with strength and power" and belongs to the material أ ي د, not to the plural of the word يَد.

Adh-Dhariyat 51:47

How then does the written similarity with some states of the plural of يَد arise?

The known plural of يَد is أَيْدِي. This word has a final ya. When such a noun appears in an indefinite form in a grammatical position where the final ya is dropped, it can appear as أَيْدٍ, just as قَاضِي in an indefinite nominative or genitive state can appear as قَاضٍ. In that state, the final ya is omitted in writing and tanwin replaces it.

On the other hand, أَيْد in the material أ ي د is an independent word meaning strength, power, and capacity. It is not the plural of يَد and there is no omitted final ya in it.

Therefore, two words from two different roots may sometimes appear in the same written form أَيْدٍ:

أَيْدٍ: hands, from the plural of يَد.

أَيْدٍ: strength and power, from the material أ ي د.

The correct meaning must be recognized from structure, context, and root. For this reason, Adh-Dhariyat 51:47 is not direct evidence that يَد in that verse means power, because the word of that verse can fundamentally belong to another material. This distinction is required for scholarly trustworthiness.

4. Main Forms and Structures

يَد: hand; the known bodily limb. In expanded uses it can signal ability, authority, action, disposal, or giving.

يَدَانِ: two hands in the nominative state.

يَدَيْنِ: two hands in the accusative or genitive state.

أَيْدِي: plural of يَد; hands. In some verses actual human hands are intended, and in others "hands" represent actions human beings have done.

يَدِي: my hand.

يَدُهُ and يَدِهِ: his hand, with the final vowel changing according to grammatical position.

يَدَكَ: your hand.

يَدَيْكَ: your two hands.

أَيْمَان is the plural of يَمِين, not the direct plural of يَد. يَمِين may mean right hand, oath, right side, or in some constructions a sphere of authority and possession. This word should be examined independently in the article on ي م ن.

بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ is a fixed construction. Literally it is "between his two hands," but in usage it may mean: before him; in front of him; what is present before him; or what has already come before him.

بِمَا قَدَّمَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ

Because of what their hands have sent ahead; that is, because of the actions they themselves performed.

Al-Baqarah 2:95

سُقِطَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ

An idiomatic expression for regret, helplessness, or the collapse of confidence after error becomes clear.

عَنْ يَدٍ

A disputed expression whose meaning must be examined separately with attention to the language and the context of At-Tawbah 9:29.

5. Semantic Core

The material core of the word is this: the hand is the limb by which a human being acts directly in the world.

This definition does not merely describe the bodily shape of the hand; it also shows its role. The hand is the instrument of taking, the instrument of releasing, the instrument of making, the instrument of giving, the instrument of restraining, the instrument of writing, the instrument of fighting, the instrument of covenant, and one of the clearest instruments by which decision is carried out.

From this practical role, semantic expansion arises:

physical hand -> performing an action -> ability to perform an action -> authority in action -> domain of disposal -> ownership -> responsibility for action -> giving and favor -> support and authority.

This path should not be treated as a rigid historical formula. It is a semantic network that explains the relation between the different uses of the word. The center of this network is not abstract "power" alone; it is an ability that can become action.

For this reason, يَد in some constructions differs from words such as قُدْرَة or قُوَّة. Power can be a general and hidden capacity. The hand often gives an image of ability in execution or of having the instrument of action in one's possession. This distinction is a tendency, not an absolute rule.

6. Hand as the Instrument through which Will Becomes Action

One of the most fundamental features of يَد is that it stands between will and action. A person first wants, then decides, and then turns the decision into outward behavior. In many human acts, the hand is the means of this transfer.

Therefore, in language the hand can represent more than a limb.

When someone intends to do something but lacks the means, authority, or possibility to carry it out, language may say: "his hand does not reach it," or "it does not come from his hand." In these expressions, the point is not the physical shortness of the hand; it is the absence of practical ability or access.

When it is said that a matter is "in his hand," it means that the decision, administration, or possibility of changing it is within his domain of authority.

When the Qur'an speaks of what hands have sent ahead, the hand becomes a representative of the set of voluntary actions of the human being.

Thus the hand is not only an organ. In the language of action, it is a sign of the transition from intention to effect.

7. Use in Arabic

In Arabic, يَد is first used for the physical hand. But because of the central role of the hand in action, it appears in many expressions.

مَا لِي بِهِ يَدٌ

I have no hand in it; that is, I have no role, authority, or involvement in it.

لَهُ عَلَيَّ يَدٌ

He has a hand over me; that is, he has done me a favor or has a claim of kindness upon me.

الأَمْرُ بِيَدِهِ

The matter is in his hand; that is, it is in his authority or disposal.

These expressions show that the semantic expansion of يَد from the body to action, authority, favor, and control is a natural expansion of Arabic usage.

But for Qur'anic study, ordinary Arabic expressions alone are not enough. The decisive criterion is the Qur'anic context itself.

8. Semitic Cognates

Words related to hand are also found in other Semitic languages. This shows that the word is ancient and belongs to the common linguistic heritage of the region.

For example, forms close to y-d / yad are known in Hebrew and Aramaic with the meaning hand. These comparisons help show the antiquity of the word, but they should not replace Qur'anic analysis.

The Qur'anic meaning must be understood first from Qur'anic usage, then from Arabic, and only after that from broader historical comparison.

9. Distinguishing Levels of Meaning

In studying يَد, several levels must be distinguished:

First: the physical level, meaning the bodily hand, as in washing the hands in wudu or the hand of Moses.

Second: the level of action, where the hand represents what a person has done.

Third: the level of ability and authority, where the hand means practical power or the domain of disposal.

Fourth: the level of ownership and possession, as in expressions related to what is in one's control.

Fifth: the level of giving and favor, where the hand becomes an image of generosity or withholding.

Sixth: the level of covenant and pledge, where placing hands together becomes a sign of commitment.

Seventh: the level of idiom and orientation, such as بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ and سُقِطَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ.

Confusing these levels causes mistranslation. Translating every occurrence as physical hand is wrong; translating every occurrence as power is also wrong.

10. Methodological Note

When examining any verse with يَد, one should ask:

Is the bodily limb intended?

Is the hand representing an action done by the person?

Is the meaning ability, authority, or possibility of action?

Is the meaning ownership or disposal?

Is the meaning giving, favor, or withholding?

Is this a fixed idiom?

Is the word being attributed to The God, and if so, what does the context itself indicate?

This method prevents two opposite errors: crude literalism that turns every usage into a bodily limb, and excessive abstraction that erases the Qur'an's own image and replaces it everywhere with "power."

11. يَد as Ability to Act

In some contexts, يَد points to ability to act. It is not merely hidden power, but practical capacity: having the means, access, or domain of action.

For this reason, phrases such as "it is in his hand" in Arabic and Persian often mean that he has control over the matter. The hand is the place where ability meets implementation.

This does not mean that every hand in the Qur'an means power. It means that the hand can naturally expand toward practical ability because the hand is the instrument through which ability is displayed.

The difference with قُدْرَة is important. Qudrah can be general capacity. یَد usually keeps a concrete image of the instrument or domain of action.

The difference with قُوَّة is also important. Quwwah can be strength itself. The hand can be a place where strength is applied, but it is not identical with strength.

12. The Physical Hand

Some Qur'anic uses clearly refer to the physical hand.

وَاضْمُمْ يَدَكَ إِلَىٰ جَنَاحِكَ

This is about the physical hand of Moses.

Ta-Ha 20:22

فَاغْسِلُوا وُجُوهَكُمْ وَأَيْدِيَكُمْ إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ

This is about washing hands up to the elbows.

Al-Ma'idah 5:6

فَلَمَّا رَأَىٰ أَيْدِيَهُمْ لَا تَصِلُ إِلَيْهِ

This is about the hands of the guests of Ibrahim not reaching the food.

Hud 11:70

In these places, replacing hand with power would damage the meaning. The verse is speaking about an actual bodily hand or bodily action.

In Ta-Ha 20:22 it says:

وَاضْمُمْ يَدَكَ إِلَىٰ جَنَاحِكَ تَخْرُجْ بَيْضَاءَ مِنْ غَيْرِ سُوءٍ آيَةً أُخْرَىٰ

And draw your hand to your side; it will come out white, without harm, as another sign.

Ta-Ha 20:22

In Al-Qasas 28:32 it says:

اسْلُكْ يَدَكَ فِي جَيْبِكَ تَخْرُجْ بَيْضَاءَ مِنْ غَيْرِ سُوءٍ

Put your hand into your garment opening; it will come out white, without harm.

Al-Qasas 28:32

In Al-Ma'idah 5:6 it says:

فَاغْسِلُوا وُجُوهَكُمْ وَأَيْدِيَكُمْ إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ

Wash your faces and your hands up to the elbows.

Al-Ma'idah 5:6

In Hud 11:70 it says:

فَلَمَّا رَأَىٰ أَيْدِيَهُمْ لَا تَصِلُ إِلَيْهِ نَكِرَهُمْ وَأَوْجَسَ مِنْهُمْ خِيفَةً

When he saw that their hands did not reach it, he found them strange and felt fear of them within himself.

Hud 11:70

These examples establish the bodily sense of يَد clearly.

13. Hand as Human Action

One of the broad Qur'anic uses of hands is the representation of actions people themselves perform.

In Al-Baqarah 2:95:

وَلَنْ يَتَمَنَّوْهُ أَبَدًا بِمَا قَدَّمَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ

They will never wish for it, because of what their hands have sent ahead.

Al-Baqarah 2:95

Here hands represent deeds. The hands did not literally send something ahead in a bodily sense; the actions of the people are described through the image of hands.

In Ar-Rum 30:41:

ظَهَرَ الْفَسَادُ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِي النَّاسِ

Corruption has appeared on land and sea because of what the hands of people have earned.

Ar-Rum 30:41

In Ash-Shuraa 42:30:

وَمَا أَصَابَكُمْ مِنْ مُصِيبَةٍ فَبِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِيكُمْ وَيَعْفُو عَنْ كَثِيرٍ

Whatever affliction reaches you is because of what your hands have earned, and He pardons much.

In Al-Baqarah 2:79:

فَوَيْلٌ لِلَّذِينَ يَكْتُبُونَ الْكِتَابَ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ ثُمَّ يَقُولُونَ هَٰذَا مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ

Woe to those who write the book with their own hands, then say: this is from The God.

Here the hand may be literal writing, but the moral emphasis is responsibility: they themselves produced the writing and then falsely attributed it.

14. Hand, Action, and Responsibility

The expression "what their hands have sent ahead" shows the close relation between hand and responsibility. The hand is the visible instrument of the deed, and therefore it can stand for the deed itself.

This does not remove inner intention. Rather, it shows that intention, when it becomes action, becomes accountable.

In Qur'anic language, human beings are not judged only by claims, wishes, or inward states. Their actions appear, and those actions are described as what their hands have sent, earned, written, or made.

Thus hand becomes a sign of responsibility: the act is not alien to the person; it came from the person's own field of action.

15. Hand and Making

The hand is also linked with making and fabrication. When a person makes something by hand, the hand shows direct involvement.

In Al-Baqarah 2:79, writing with hands is not merely technical writing. It points to deliberate production, then false attribution.

This use shows that the hand can mark the difference between something received from The God and something produced by human beings.

16. Important Notes

أَيْدِي is the plural of يَد.

أَيْد can belong to the separate root أ ي د, meaning strength.

Not every occurrence of a similar written form should be forced into the root ي د ي.

Not every occurrence of يَد should be translated as power.

Not every occurrence should be kept as a physical hand without explanation.

The Qur'anic context decides which part of the semantic field is active.

17. The Idiom سُقِطَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ

One of the most image-rich Qur'anic uses of يَد appears in Al-A'raf 7:149:

وَلَمَّا سُقِطَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ وَرَأَوْا أَنَّهُمْ قَدْ ضَلُّوا قَالُوا لَئِنْ لَمْ يَرْحَمْنَا رَبُّنَا وَيَغْفِرْ لَنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ الْخَاسِرِينَ

When it fell into their hands, and they saw that they had gone astray, they said: if our Lord does not have mercy on us and forgive us, we will surely be among the losers.

Al-A'raf 7:149

The expression سُقِطَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ is idiomatic. It does not mean that some physical object literally fell into their hands. It points to a state of deep regret, helplessness, or collapse after the truth of their error became clear.

The image is powerful: what they had done returns upon them; their confidence falls; the matter is now in their own hands as a burden of regret.

A natural translation may be: they were overcome with regret; they became helpless and remorseful; their matter collapsed in their own hands.

18. بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ: from Bodily Position to What Is Before and What Is Prior

The expression بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ literally means "between his two hands." In bodily space, something between the hands is before a person, present in front of him, and within the field of attention.

From this image, the expression expands to several meanings: before him; in his presence; in front of him; in his immediate reach; or what has come before him in time.

In Al-Baqarah 2:97 it says:

مُصَدِّقًا لِمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ

Confirming what is before it.

In such contexts, بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ often refers to what came before, especially earlier revelation. The image of what is before the hands becomes a way to speak about what stands before a text historically or evidentially.

Thus the expression should not always be translated literally. Its meaning must be determined by context.

19. مِنْ بَيْنِ أَيْدِيهِمْ: from Before Them

In Ya-Sin 36:9:

وَجَعَلْنَا مِنْ بَيْنِ أَيْدِيهِمْ سَدًّا وَمِنْ خَلْفِهِمْ سَدًّا فَأَغْشَيْنَاهُمْ فَهُمْ لَا يُبْصِرُونَ

We placed a barrier before them and a barrier behind them, then covered them, so they do not see.

Here مِنْ بَيْنِ أَيْدِيهِمْ is contrasted with مِنْ خَلْفِهِمْ. The contrast shows the spatial meaning: in front of them and behind them.

This is different from contexts where بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ refers to something prior in time. Again, context is decisive.

20. Hand as Authority, Ownership, and Domain of Disposal

The hand can also represent authority, ownership, or a domain of disposal. Something that is "in one's hand" is within one's reach, possession, or control.

This does not mean that يَد itself always means ownership. Rather, because the hand is the instrument of holding, taking, and disposing, it naturally expands into this field.

Expressions such as "what your right hands possess" belong to this wider field and must be studied with care in their historical and legal context. They cannot be reduced to a simple physical hand, nor should they be turned into a timeless rule without examining the whole Qur'anic framework of justice, responsibility, and human relations.

21. عَنْ يَدٍ in At-Tawbah 9:29

In At-Tawbah 9:29 it says:

حَتَّىٰ يُعْطُوا الْجِزْيَةَ عَنْ يَدٍ

Until they give the jizyah عَنْ يَدٍ.

At-Tawbah 9:29

This expression is disputed. Possible explanations include direct payment, payment from ability, payment under authority, payment while acknowledging the governing order, or other context-based readings.

A root article alone should not settle every historical and legal debate around this phrase. It should clarify that يَد here is connected with the field of action, giving, ability, and authority, but the exact translation must be determined by a fuller study of the verse and its legal-historical context.

For a research translation, it may be better to preserve عَنْ يَدٍ and explain the main possibilities in a note rather than impose one definitive interpretation too quickly.

22. Hand as Favor and Goodness

In Arabic, hand can also point to favor and benefaction. If someone has a "hand" over another person, it may mean that he has done him a favor.

This meaning arises naturally from giving. The hand gives, extends, and bestows. Therefore يَد can enter the field of favor and goodness.

This does not make every hand a favor. The meaning depends on context.

23. The Bound Hand and the Open Hand

The image of a bound hand and an open hand is one of the clearest images of withholding and giving.

A bound hand cannot give. An open hand can give. From this bodily image, language forms the meaning of stinginess and generosity.

This image appears both in human instruction and in the Qur'anic response to the accusation made against The God.

24. Al-Ma'idah 5:64: the Charge of a Bound Hand

In Al-Ma'idah 5:64 it says:

وَقَالَتِ الْيَهُودُ يَدُ اللَّهِ مَغْلُولَةٌ غُلَّتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَلُعِنُوا بِمَا قَالُوا بَلْ يَدَاهُ مَبْسُوطَتَانِ يُنْفِقُ كَيْفَ يَشَاءُ

The Jews said: the hand of The God is bound. May their hands be bound, and they were distanced from mercy because of what they said. Rather, His two hands are outspread; He gives as He wills.

Al-Ma'idah 5:64

The context itself explains the image. The issue is not a bodily limb. The issue is giving and withholding.

The accusation "the hand of The God is bound" means that they attributed restriction, withholding, or inability to divine giving. The response says:

بَلْ يَدَاهُ مَبْسُوطَتَانِ

Rather, His two hands are outspread.

Al-Ma'idah 5:64

Then the verse explains:

يُنْفِقُ كَيْفَ يَشَاءُ

He gives as He wills.

Thus the context points to giving, generosity, and unrestricted divine bestowal. At the same time, the Qur'anic image should not be erased. A careful translation preserves the image of hand, while the explanation rejects bodily likeness.

25. Hand in Human Giving

In Al-Isra 17:29 it says:

وَلَا تَجْعَلْ يَدَكَ مَغْلُولَةً إِلَىٰ عُنُقِكَ وَلَا تَبْسُطْهَا كُلَّ الْبَسْطِ فَتَقْعُدَ مَلُومًا مَحْسُورًا

Do not make your hand bound to your neck, and do not spread it out completely, lest you sit blameworthy and exhausted.

Al-Isra 17:29

Here the hand is an image of spending and withholding. A hand bound to the neck is an image of extreme withholding; a hand spread out completely is an image of uncontrolled giving. The verse teaches balance.

This human example helps clarify the image in Al-Ma'idah 5:64: binding and spreading the hand belong to the field of giving and withholding.

26. Hand in Covenant and Pledge

The hand also appears in the field of covenant and pledge. In pledging allegiance, hands are placed together or one hand is placed upon another. The hand becomes a sign of commitment.

In Al-Fath 48:10 it says:

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يُبَايِعُونَكَ إِنَّمَا يُبَايِعُونَ اللَّهَ يَدُ اللَّهِ فَوْقَ أَيْدِيهِمْ

Those who pledge allegiance to you are only pledging allegiance to The God; the hand of The God is above their hands.

Al-Fath 48:10

The context is covenant and pledge. The verse does not invite bodily imagination about The God. It shows that the pledge with the messenger is not merely a social gesture; it is a covenant before The God. His authority, witness, and binding command stand above their hands.

The image should be preserved, but bodily likeness should be rejected.

27. Expressions of يَد Regarding The God

When يَد is attributed to The God in the Qur'an, the method must be careful.

On one side, the Qur'anic wording should not be erased. On the other side, The God is not like creation:

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ

Nothing is like Him.

Therefore, one should not attribute bodily shape, size, direction, organs, or human physical quality to The God.

At the same time, every divine use should not be flattened into the single word "power" without attention to context. In Al-Ma'idah 5:64 the context is giving. In Al-Fath 48:10 the context is pledge and covenant. In Sad 38:75 the context is the creation of Adam. In Ya-Sin 36:71 the context is creating cattle for human beings.

Each passage must be explained from its own context.

28. Sad 38:75: لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَيَّ

In Sad 38:75 it says:

قَالَ يَا إِبْلِيسُ مَا مَنَعَكَ أَنْ تَسْجُدَ لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَيَّ أَسْتَكْبَرْتَ أَمْ كُنْتَ مِنَ الْعَالِينَ

He said: O Iblis, what prevented you from bowing to what I created with My two hands? Did you become arrogant, or were you among the high ones?

Sad 38:75

This verse is about the special creation of Adam and the arrogance of Iblis. The expression بِیَدَيَّ should not be interpreted as a human-like body. But it should also not be erased without explanation.

The safest method is to preserve the Qur'anic expression in translation and explain that the verse points to a special divine act of creation, while bodily likeness and the question of "how" are not affirmed.

29. Ya-Sin 36:71: مِمَّا عَمِلَتْ أَيْدِينَا

In Ya-Sin 36:71 it says:

أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا أَنَّا خَلَقْنَا لَهُمْ مِمَّا عَمِلَتْ أَيْدِينَا أَنْعَامًا فَهُمْ لَهَا مَالِكُونَ

Have they not seen that We created for them, from what Our hands made, cattle, and they are owners of them?

Ya-Sin 36:71

The verse points to divine making and creating of cattle for human use. The context emphasizes favor, creation, and ownership granted to human beings:

فَهُمْ لَهَا مَالِكُونَ

They are owners of them.

Again, the image must be kept without bodily interpretation. The phrase does not prove a created bodily form. It expresses divine making in Qur'anic language.

30. Is يَد One of the Divine Attributes?

In later theological discussions, يَد was often treated as one of the divine attributes. A root article should not turn into a full theological treatise, but it must state the methodological boundary.

The Qur'an uses expressions of يَد regarding The God in specific contexts. These expressions must be read with the principle that nothing is like Him. They must also be read with attention to the immediate context: giving, covenant, creation, or making.

Therefore, the article should neither deny the Qur'anic wording nor turn it into bodily description.

31. The Right Hand and يَمِين

يَمِين is related to the right hand, but it is not simply another form of يَد. It has its own semantic network: right side, right hand, oath, blessing, strength, and domain of authority in some constructions.

For this reason, أَيْمَان is the plural of يَمِين, not the plural of يَد.

Where the Qur'an speaks of "right hands," the discussion belongs primarily to the article on ي م ن, although it intersects with the field of hand, authority, and possession.

32. Hand and Covenant in Relation to يمين

Because hands are used in pledge, and because يمين can mean oath, the fields of hand and oath can meet. But they should not be confused.

A hand can become the visible sign of a pledge. An oath can bind speech and responsibility. Both belong to covenantal life, but they are not the same root and not the same word.

33. Comparison with Near Roots and Concepts

ي د ي compared with ق د ر: Qudrah is capacity and ability. Yad is the hand and, by expansion, practical ability or domain of action. They overlap, but they are not identical.

ي د ي compared with ق و ي: Quwwah is strength. The hand may apply strength, but يَد is broader and can include giving, writing, pledge, and action.

ي د ي compared with ع م ل: Amal is the act performed. The hand may be the instrument or image of the act, but the meaning of hand and action is not the same.

ي د ي compared with ك س ب: Kasb relates to acquiring the result of action. In كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِيكُمْ the hands are the image-bearing subject of earning, but hand and earning are not identical.

ي د ي compared with م ل ك: Mulk denotes ownership, dominion, and authority. Something in someone's hand may be in his control or ownership, but يَد is not always ملك.

ي د ي compared with ب ط ش: Batsh relates to hard seizing, assault, or forceful application of power. The hand can be the instrument of batsh, but يَد is much wider and includes giving, writing, and covenant.

ي د ي compared with ذ ر ع: Dhira' is the part of the arm from elbow to fingertips or a unit of measure. يَد may cover different ranges of the limb depending on language and context. Where a physical limit is needed, a phrase such as إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ clarifies the boundary.

ي د ي compared with ك ف ف: Kaff may mean palm of the hand or restraining. The palm is part of the hand, but يَد is the more general word for the hand.

ي د ي compared with ي م ن: Yamin may be the right hand and then expands to oath, right side, and sphere of authority. يَد is the general word hand; yamin is a particular branch with an independent semantic network.

34. Challenges in Understanding

Translating every occurrence as a physical hand makes expressions such as بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ, سُقِطَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ, and بِمَا قَدَّمَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ unclear or wrong.

Translating every occurrence as power also erases the difference between the hand of Moses, the hands of wudu, human deeds, giving, pledge, ownership, and creation.

One should not confuse figurative expansion with the primary meaning. Ability, authority, action, and giving are semantic extensions, but the first material meaning remains hand.

One must avoid bodily interpretation in divine uses. Attributing shape, size, direction, organ, or human physical quality to The God is not obtained from the text.

One must also avoid deleting the image in the name of transcendence. Transcendence does not mean cleansing the Qur'an of its own images. One can preserve the word, reject bodily likeness, and explain the contextual meaning.

أَيْدِي and أَيْد must not be conflated. That error mixes the plural of hand with the root of strength and power.

عَنْ يَدٍ should not be made definitive too quickly. The expression is disputed, and a root article alone cannot solve all historical and legal disagreements around it.

Historical constructions such as مَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ appear in a particular social structure. Understanding them requires a comprehensive study of verses, justice, and the transformation of human relations.

One should not build theology from morphology alone. Singular, dual, or plural forms of يَد are linguistic realities, but by themselves they do not establish a final conclusion about the reality of the divine essence or the mode of divine action.

35. Principles for Forqan Translation

When the physical hand is intended, the main translation should be "hand," as in يَدَكَ, أَيْدِيَكُمْ إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ, and أَيْدِيَهُمْ لَا تَصِلُ إِلَيْهِ.

When the hand represents action, the image should preferably be preserved and an explanation added. For example:

بِمَا قَدَّمَتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ

Because of what their hands have sent ahead.

Al-Baqarah 2:95

Explanation: because of the deeds they themselves performed.

When the construction is idiomatic, the translation should convey the idiom:

سُقِطَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ

They became deeply regretful and helpless.

بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ

Depending on context: before him; what is before him; what is present before him.

مِنْ بَيْنِ أَيْدِيهِمْ

From before them.

When giving and spending are in view, the image of bound and open hands should be preserved:

بَلْ يَدَاهُ مَبْسُوطَتَانِ

Rather, His two hands are outspread.

Al-Ma'idah 5:64

Then explain: His giving is not restricted.

When the expression is attributed to The God, the recommended method is: preserve the word "hand" in the main translation; explain the contextual meaning; reject bodily likeness; avoid claiming the modality.

When عَنْ يَدٍ appears, a definitive interpretive translation should not be inserted before a full study of the verse. In a research translation, the phrase may be preserved as عَنْ يَدٍ and the main views explained in a note.

36. Definition of the Main Forms

يَد: hand; the bodily limb and direct instrument of action. In expanded use it may represent ability, authority, disposal, action, giving, or support.

يَدَانِ / يَدَيْنِ: two hands, physically or figuratively according to context.

أَيْدِي: hands; and in some constructions, the actions, practical ability, or domain of human action.

أَيْدٍ: it may be an indefinite grammatical form of the plural أَيْدِي, or an independent word from the material أ ي د meaning strength. Its identification depends on root and context.

بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ: before him, in his presence, or what stands before him.

سُقِطَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ: they became deeply regretful and helpless after realizing their error.

عَنْ يَدٍ: a disputed expression about manner of payment, directness, ability, or a state of authority and control; its meaning must be determined from context.

37. Final Definition of the Root

Short definition:

Hand; and by semantic expansion, the instrument and sign of ability, action, authority, disposal, and giving.

Detailed definition:

ي د ي denotes the hand: a limb that is one of the primary instruments by which human will becomes action in the world. From this material role, the word expands in Qur'anic language to practical ability, authority, disposal, ownership, action, responsibility, favor, support, and covenant. In idiomatic constructions it may also indicate what is before, regret, or a mode of action. The precise meaning of each use must be known from the structure and context of that verse.

38. Final Summary

The word يَد in the Qur'an is a clear example of a material word with a broad semantic network.

Its first meaning is the physical hand. But the hand is not merely a body part; it is one of the main instruments of human action. With the hand, a person takes, gives, makes, writes, restrains, pledges, and turns will into outward effect.

From this role, several semantic extensions arise: hand as ability to act; hand as authority and domain of disposal; hand as action and responsibility; hand as ownership; hand as favor and benefaction; hand as support and authority; and hand in idiomatic and directional constructions.

The Qur'anic study showed that يَد is grammatically feminine; its plural is أَيْدِي; the form أَيْدٍ may coincide in writing with the independent material أ ي د meaning strength; these two roots must not be confused; يَد in some verses is fully physical; in some verses it represents human deeds; in some constructions it conveys practical ability and authority; بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ usually points to what is before or what is prior; سُقِطَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ is an idiom of regret and helplessness; عَنْ يَدٍ is disputed and should not be limited to one meaning without study; bound and open hands in the context of spending are images of withholding and giving; in pledge, the hand is connected with covenant, witness, and commitment; uses of يَد regarding The God must not be understood bodily; at the same time, they should not all be changed to "power" without contextual examination.

The backbone of the meaning is this:

The hand is the instrument by which will becomes action.

From here the semantic network is formed:

hand -> action -> practical ability -> authority -> disposal -> ownership -> responsibility -> giving -> support -> covenant.

But this network does not mean that all these meanings are present in every verse. Each use activates one part of the field.

The final definition is:

ي د ي denotes the hand: the direct instrument of action and the visible emergence of will. From this material meaning arise uses for ability, authority, disposal, ownership, action, responsibility, giving, support, and covenant. In every verse it must be determined whether the intended meaning is the physical hand, practical ability, an action performed, a domain of authority, or an idiomatic expression. In divine attributions, the Qur'anic image must be preserved without bodily interpretation and without hastily deleting the contextual meaning.
