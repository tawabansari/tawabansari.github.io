Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root W Z N

Weighing, measuring with a scale, establishing balance, and recognizing quantity or value through a criterion

The root field; measuring according to balance and criterion.

Weighed; measured; possessing measure, proportion, and balance.

Central Meaning

The root W Z N refers to weighing, measuring, determining the amount of something through a criterion and a scale. Its central meaning is measurement through balance: something is placed against a criterion so that its amount, heaviness, value, or proportion becomes clear.

This root begins in the physical field of weight, but in the Qur’an and Arabic it reaches a wider meaning: mīzān, justice, balance, correct measurement, and avoiding short-giving or false exaggeration.

Linguistic Note

The root W Z N is tied to the idea of criterion. Weight does not only mean that something is heavy or light. Weight means that the thing is measured by a criterion and its place becomes clear.

For this reason, mīzān is not only a scale. Mīzān is anything by which right, amount, proportion, and balance are known. In the market, mīzān is an instrument of economic justice. In ethics, it is the criterion for measuring behavior. In the world, it is a sign of order and balance in creation.

So this root moves from a simple market tool to a large cosmological concept.

Main Image of the Root

The main image of this root is the scale. Something is placed in the pan or measuring instrument, and its relation to the criterion becomes clear. As long as the scale is moving, the final result is not yet clear. When the mīzān settles and balance is established, the true amount becomes visible.

So weight means that surface movements must settle before the truth of measure becomes clear. If the scale is sound, right becomes clear. If the scale is manipulated, justice is destroyed.

The person who weighs does not merely give a number. He is responsible for showing reality correctly.

Weight and Justice

The Qur’an repeatedly emphasizes completing weight and measure. This shows that justice is not only in intention or speech; it must also appear in the instruments of measurement.

If the criterion is corrupted, everything is corrupted, because people trade, trust, price, and judge according to that criterion. When the scale shows less or more than the truth, right is displaced.

So weight in the Qur’an is not only a market issue. It is an issue of social trust.

Mīzān

The word مِيزَان comes from this root. Mīzān means an instrument or criterion of measurement. Outwardly, it is a scale. In a deeper layer, every correct and just criterion is a mīzān.

The Qur’an speaks of mīzān beside creation and justice. In 55:7 through 55:9, it says: وَوَضَعَ الْمِيزَانَ ... أَلَّا تَطْغَوْا فِي الْمِيزَانِ ... وَأَقِيمُوا الْوَزْنَ بِالْقِسْطِ. This shows that the world is built upon balance, and the human being must not disrupt that balance.

When it says أَلَّا تَطْغَوْا فِي الْمِيزَانِ, it means do not transgress in the mīzān; do not step beyond the limit of just measurement. Mīzān is therefore not only number; it is limit and justice.

Measured in Creation

The Qur’an says about the earth and its growth: وَأَنْبَتْنَا فِيهَا مِنْ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ مَوْزُونٍ. This appears in 15:19 and shows that mawzūn is not merely something weighed on a scale. It is something that has exact measure, proportion, composition, and balance.

In creation, everything has its place, size, proportion, and amount. If these relations are disturbed, life is harmed. So mawzūn in this layer means measured, proportioned, and placed in its correct measure.

From here it becomes clear that weight is not only the market; it is a principle of order in existence.

Weight and Loss

The root W Z N is directly connected with KH S R. The Qur’an says: وَلَا تُخْسِرُوا الْمِيزَانَ: do not make the mīzān deficient.

This means corrupting weight and the scale is a form of producing loss. When a person makes the criterion short or crooked, he does not only reduce people’s property; he harms the balance of society.

So short-giving in weight is not merely an economic error. It is the breaking of trust and justice.

Weight and Measure by Volume

The root W Z N is semantically close to K Y L, but they are not the same. Kayl is more connected to measuring by volume, such as grain, liquid, or things measured by a container. Wazn is connected to measuring heaviness and balance.

The Qur’an places the two beside each other because both are instruments of economic justice. In 7:85, it says: فَأَوْفُوا الْكَيْلَ وَالْمِيزَانَ.

Kayl is justice in measured volume; wazn is justice in weighing and balance.

Weight and Today’s Economy

This root is also very important for understanding today’s economy. In the past, weight and mīzān were mostly connected to scales and goods. Today, the criteria of measurement have become more complex: money, rates, inflation, indexes, contracts, credit, and nominal value.

If the criterion of measurement is corrupted, justice is harmed even if physical scales are accurate. For example, if the value of money decreases and people see the same number while their purchasing power falls, the economic criterion of measurement has been disturbed.

From this angle, manipulating criteria, hidden inflation, reducing the real value of money, or creating indexes that do not show people’s lived reality accurately can semantically approach corruption of mīzān.

Of course, this must be said carefully. Not every economic change is transgression in the mīzān. But every structure that separates measurement from reality and quietly reduces people’s right can be evaluated through the Qur’an’s warnings about mīzān.

Weight in the Hereafter

The Qur’an also speaks of weight and mīzān in the Hereafter. Human deeds are weighed. This shows that weight does not belong only to goods; the truth of action also has weight.

When the Qur’an speaks of ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ, it means that some deeds have heaviness in the mīzān of truth. This wording appears in 101:6. This heaviness does not depend only on the number or appearance of deeds; it is tied to truth, sincerity, effect, and relation to right.

On the other hand, a deed may look large outwardly but be light in the mīzān of truth. A light deed is one that does not have sufficient substance and truth. It may have volume, but no weight.

So Qur’anic weight is not only quantity. Quality, truth, and relation to right also affect weight.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

وَزَنَ / يَزِنُ (wazana / yazinu): weighed; measured; measured by a criterion.

وَزْن (wazn): weight; measured amount; heaviness; measure.

مِيزَان (mīzān): scale; criterion of measurement; mīzān; instrument for knowing balance and justice.

مَوْزُون (mawzūn): measured; weighed; possessing measure, proportion, and balance.

أَوْفُوا الْوَزْنَ: complete the weight; perform measurement fully and correctly.

أَقِيمُوا الْوَزْنَ بِالْقِسْطِ: establish weight with justice; uphold measurement upon equity.

وَلَا تُخْسِرُوا الْمِيزَانَ: do not make the mīzān deficient; do not drag the balance of measurement into loss and short-giving.

أَلَّا تَطْغَوْا فِي الْمِيزَانِ: do not transgress in the mīzān; do not exceed the limit of justice in measurement.

مِنْ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ مَوْزُونٍ: from every thing measured and possessing measure and balance.

ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ: his scales became heavy; his record gained weight and truth in the mīzān of right.

Summary

The root W Z N refers to weighing, measuring with a scale, and recognizing quantity through a criterion. Its central meaning is measurement on the basis of balance.

This root begins in the market, but reaches justice, criterion, order, creation, and the Hereafter. In the Qur’an, weight and mīzān are not only economic tools; they are the material language of justice and order.

If the mīzān is sound, right becomes clear. If the mīzān is corrupted, right is hidden or displaced. For this reason, preserving weight and mīzān is one of the foundations of trust, economic justice, and human responsibility.

In the Qur’an’s balance sheet, weight means that every thing must be measured by the correct criterion: not shown as less than its right, and not shown as more than its truth.
