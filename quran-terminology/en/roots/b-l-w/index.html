1. Short Introduction to the Root

The word family B L W in the Qur'an moves in the semantic field of testing, placing someone in a situation, and making the true quality of a person or action appear.

Testing in this root is not merely a question asked in order to know an unknown answer. A person is placed in a real situation, and his reaction, choice, and action become manifest. For this reason, ibtila may come through hardship, fear, hunger, and loss of wealth and life; but blessing, power, possession, and ease can also be instruments of ibtila.

Beside this family, the near root B L Y denotes becoming old, wearing out, and being subject to decay, as in:

مُلْكٍ لَا يَبْلَىٰ

A dominion that does not wear out.

Ta Ha 20:120

These two families are close in form and semantic field, but they should not be treated as completely identical without explanation:

  • B L W is more closely tied to testing and the disclosure of truth in the field of action;
  • B L Y more directly denotes oldness, wearing out, and decay through time or use.

One may see an imagistic relation between testing and wearing down: the situations of life rub against the covering of claims and make a person's real quality appear. But “wearing down the body” is not the full definition of ibtila, because some tests occur through blessing, choice, and power, not through bodily pressure.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

بَلَا / يَبْلُو

He tested; he placed in a situation and made the quality of action appear.

نَبْلُو / نَبْلُوَكُمْ

We test, or We test you; that is, We bring you into situations in which the direction of your choice and action becomes manifest.

ابْتَلَىٰ / يَبْتَلِي

He tested and placed in a situation of ibtila.

The form ifti'al in this family emphasizes entering into, or being placed within, a process of testing, but by itself it should not be restricted to the meaning of a long and wearing test.

ابْتُلِيَ

The passive form: he was tested, or was placed in the field of testing.

مُبْتَلِي

A tester, or one who places another in a condition of ibtila.

بَلَاء

A test, a revealing situation, or that through which a human being is measured by good or evil.

In some contexts, bala comes close to hardship and suffering; in others, it is joined with blessing, victory, or opening.

بَلَاءٌ حَسَنٌ

A good test, or an effective blessing that should disclose the truth of a person's response and gratitude.

الْبَلَاءُ الْمُبِينُ

The clear and great test; a situation that makes the truth of surrender and truthfulness plain.

تَبْلَى السَّرَائِرُ

Inner secrets and hidden matters are tested and made manifest.

يَبْلَىٰ

It becomes old and wears out. This form belongs to the family B L Y.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of B L W may be stated as follows:

Placing a person or thing in a real situation in which its hidden quality, capacity, truthfulness, or result of action becomes manifest.

This disclosure may be for others, for the person himself, or in the field where action is realized and recorded. The God does not test the human being in order to remove His own ignorance; His knowledge is comprehensive even before occurrence.

Then why does ibtila occur?

Because divine knowledge becomes realized in the world of responsibility through choice and action. The human being turns what is possible, intended, or claimed into action, and the result of his action becomes manifest in reality.

Ibtila is the field of realization, not a tool for removing ignorance from God.

For this reason, the Qur'an sometimes states the purpose of ibtila in this way:

لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا

So that He may test you as to which of you is best in action.

The subject of the verse is not merely knowing; it is action. The quality of a human being appears in what he does.

5. The Material Image of the Root

One of the known images in the near family of this word is a garment that becomes old through use and the passing of time:

بَلِيَ الثَّوْبُ

The garment became old and worn.

When the outer covering is rubbed and worn, the real weave and quality of the thing becomes more visible. This image can help explain ibtila: real situations remove the covering of claims and make what is inside a person appear.

But one must distinguish between an explanatory image and a definition of the root. Not every ibtila means wearing something down. Wealth, ease, power, and victory can also be tests, even though they do not necessarily wear down a person's body or possessions.

Another image is testing a metal in order to know its quality. Metal is exposed to heat or striking, and its impurity or strength becomes apparent. This image helps explain testing, but the image of a furnace and smelting belongs more closely to the field of the root F T N.

Therefore, the more suitable image for B L W is this:

Something is placed in the field of use and reality so that its quality is known not by claim, but by action.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, balawtuhu was used in the sense: I tested him and came to know his quality.

This testing could occur by assigning a task, placing someone in a difficult situation, or observing his behavior under real conditions.

The expression baliya al-thawb belongs to the wearing out of a garment and to the family B L Y. The phonetic and semantic closeness of these two families led lexicographers to see a relation between the disclosure of quality through use and the meaning of testing.

Baliyyah in the language may refer to calamity or a hard event. It is also reported as a term for a pre-Islamic custom involving a camel tied near a grave. If this historical usage is correct and established, it is a cultural example of the word; but it should not be made the certain origin of the entire concept of ibtila.

The word bal in expressions such as:

أَصْلَحَ بَالَهُمْ

should not be connected to B L W in root analysis without evidence. Bal refers to a person's condition, state, or affair. A similarity of wording is not enough to build a relation such as “a person's bal is repaired through bala,” even though conceptually a person may be reformed through a test.

7. History of Understanding, Misuse, or Semantic Restriction

One common restriction is comparing divine ibtila to the examination of a teacher who does not know the student's answer and asks a question in order to gain information.

This image is not compatible with the comprehensive knowledge of The God. Ibtila is not for God to learn something unknown; it is for the realization of choice, the disclosure of action, the formation of responsibility, and the deserving of outcome in the real world.

Another error is restricting ibtila to suffering and calamity. The Qur'an explicitly says:

وَنَبْلُوكُمْ بِالشَّرِّ وَالْخَيْرِ فِتْنَةً

We test you with evil and good.

So good is also a test. Wealth, power, health, knowledge, security, and victory can disclose the truth of a person just as poverty and defeat can.

A third restriction is making ibtila completely bodily. Many tests have material consequences, but their subject can be truthfulness, patience, gratitude, justice, loyalty, fear, greed, or moral choice.

On the other side, making it purely mental is also wrong. Ibtila in the Qur'an happens in real life: in war, wealth, life, family, power, poverty, and decision.

So ibtila is neither merely mental nor necessarily bodily; it is a real situation that discloses the truth of action.

8. Methodological Note

In studying this family, several levels should be kept distinct.

First, divine knowledge, which is prior and comprehensive.

Second, the realization of human choice in time and in the world of action.

Third, the disclosure of the quality of action for the person himself, for others, and for the field of judgment.

Fourth, the educative or punitive result of the test, which differs according to the person's response.

Fifth, the wearing out of B L Y, a near but distinct family from the testing of B L W.

Also, ibtila should not always be treated as positive or negative. A single situation may become a cause of growth for one person and a cause of fall for another. The result forms from the human response to that situation.

The word bala by itself also does not only mean calamity. The adjective and context determine whether the test comes with good, evil, victory, a difficult command, or enemy pressure.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

a. Ibtila with Good and Evil

كُلُّ نَفْسٍ ذَائِقَةُ الْمَوْتِ وَنَبْلُوكُمْ بِالشَّرِّ وَالْخَيْرِ فِتْنَةً وَإِلَيْنَا تُرْجَعُونَ

Every soul will taste death, and We test you with evil and good, and to Us you are returned.

Al-Anbiya 21:35

This verse gives one of the clearest Qur'anic definitions of ibtila. Both evil and good can be instruments of testing.

In hardship, a person's patience, steadfastness, and trust become manifest.

In ease, gratitude, justice, generosity, and avoidance of arrogance become manifest.

The letter bi in bil-sharri wal-khayr introduces the means or field of the test; but evil and good are not necessarily “hardware bodies.” They include a range of real material, psychological, and social conditions.

b. Testing for the Disclosure of the Best Action

الَّذِي خَلَقَ الْمَوْتَ وَالْحَيَاةَ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا

The One who created death and life so that He may test you as to which of you is best in action.

Al-Mulk 67:2

The subject of the test is not “the most action,” but ahsan 'amalan: better action.

This structure shows that ibtila does not only measure the ability to bear pressure; moral quality and direction of action are also its subject.

c. Ibtila in What Has Been Given to the Human Being

لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ فِي مَا آتَاكُمْ

So that He may test you in what He has given you.

Al-An'am 6:165

What has been given may include differences in ability, means, position, and blessing.

Ibtila in what is given shows that possessing a blessing is not a sign of final approval or inherent superiority. Every gift creates responsibility, and the quality of its use discloses the person's truth.

d. Ibtila with Fear, Hunger, and Decrease

وَلَنَبْلُوَنَّكُمْ بِشَيْءٍ مِنَ الْخَوْفِ وَالْجُوعِ وَنَقْصٍ مِنَ الْأَمْوَالِ وَالْأَنْفُسِ وَالثَّمَرَاتِ

And We will surely test you with something of fear, hunger, and decrease in wealth, lives, and fruits.

Al-Baqarah 2:155

This verse makes the material and social instances of ibtila clear.

But the expression bishay'in min is also important: something and a measure of these hardships, not necessarily the total destruction of all possessions.

The continuation of the verses identifies the desired response as patience, awareness of belonging to The God, and return to Him.

e. The Testing of the Believers in Al-Ahzab

هُنَالِكَ ابْتُلِيَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَزُلْزِلُوا زِلْزَالًا شَدِيدًا

There the believers were tested, and they were shaken with a severe shaking.

Al-Ahzab 33:11

This verse describes an intense military and psychological situation. Enemies have come from several sides and fear has reached its height.

Ibtila here makes the truth of positions appear: some remain upon their covenant, while some fall into suspicion and hypocrisy.

But ibtila is not merely a “sieve of the physical body.” Speech, intention, trust, fear, and loyalty also become manifest.

f. Bala Hasan

وَلِيُبْلِيَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ مِنْهُ بَلَاءً حَسَنًا

And so that He may test the believers from Himself with a good test.

Al-Anfal 8:17

The context of the verse concerns battle and divine support. Bala hasanan may denote blessing, victory, and a situation in which the believers are tested while also receiving opening.

The word hasan should not be removed, and bala should not be reduced to wearing down. A good test can also come through victory and blessing.

This expression shows that bala is not inherently calamity.

g. Bala Mubin in the Story of Ibrahim

إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَهُوَ الْبَلَاءُ الْمُبِينُ

Indeed, this was the clear test.

As-Saffat 37:106

The test of Ibrahim became manifest in his readiness to carry out the vision and to surrender before what he understood as divine command.

Mubin may point to the clear and manifest greatness of the test. This situation made Ibrahim's truthfulness appear.

But bala should not be translated only as “a material crisis at the edge of severing attachment.” The center of the verse is the clear and great test of surrender.

h. Great Bala in the System of Pharaoh

وَفِي ذَٰلِكُمْ بَلَاءٌ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ عَظِيمٌ

And in that there was for you a great test from your Lord.

Al-Baqarah 2:49

The pronoun dhalikum may refer to the whole complex of Pharaonic torment and deliverance from it. Therefore, bala may include both great hardship and a test joined with deliverance and blessing.

This verse shows that bala is not always a simple event with a one-sided value; a historical situation may hold suffering, deliverance, and responsibility at the same time.

i. The Disclosure of Inner Secrets

يَوْمَ تُبْلَى السَّرَائِرُ

The Day when the inner secrets are tested and made manifest.

At-Tariq 86:9

Sara'ir are hidden matters, intentions, and inward secrets.

On that Day, what remained covered in the world is placed under examination and disclosure.

Translation only as “the secrets are worn out” is incorrect. The meaning of the verse is that hidden matters are tested and exposed.

j. Dominion That Does Not Wear Out

هَلْ أَدُلُّكَ عَلَىٰ شَجَرَةِ الْخُلْدِ وَمُلْكٍ لَا يَبْلَىٰ

Shall I guide you to the tree of everlastingness and a dominion that does not wear out?

Ta Ha 20:120

This use belongs to the family B L Y.

Satan promises Adam permanence and freedom from decay. La yabla means it does not become old, wear out, or pass away.

This verse shows that the meaning of wearing out is real, but it should not be transferred to all forms of B L W.

10. Examination of Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

بَلَاء

Bala is a situation of testing and disclosure.

It may come with hardship:

بَلَاءٌ عَظِيمٌ

And it may come with good and opening:

بَلَاءً حَسَنًا

So the fixed translation “calamity” or “wearing down” is wrong for all cases.

يَبْلُو

The subject places another in a situation where the quality of his action becomes manifest.

In divine usage, this verb does not mean acquiring new knowledge. Divine knowledge is realized in the field of choice and responsibility.

ابْتَلَىٰ

The form ifti'al emphasizes being placed in a process of testing, or testing.

One should not infer from the form itself that the test must be longer, harder, or more bodily than the simple triliteral form. The precise difference is determined by context.

تَبْلَى

In:

تُبْلَى السَّرَائِرُ

it means being tested and made manifest.

يَبْلَىٰ

In:

مُلْكٍ لَا يَبْلَىٰ

it means becoming worn out and passing away, and belongs to B L Y.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

بَلَا with a Direct Object

لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ

The pronoun kum is the object; you are placed in the field of testing.

بَلَا with بِـ

نَبْلُوكُمْ بِالشَّرِّ وَالْخَيْرِ

The letter bi introduces the instrument, field, or thing through which the test occurs.

بَلَا with فِي

لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ فِي مَا آتَاكُمْ

The letter fi shows the field and sphere of testing: in what He has given you.

أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا

This expression explains the aim or result of the test. The subject is the disclosure of the quality of action.

بَلَاءً حَسَنًا

Bala'an is an object or explanatory verbal noun, and hasanan is its adjective. The positive adjective prevents bala from always being translated as calamity.

الْبَلَاءُ الْمُبِينُ

The definite article introduces the known and specific test in the story. Mubin emphasizes its greatness and clarity.

12. B L W (Bala) Compared with F T N (Fitnah)

The root F T N in its material use is connected with placing metal in fire so impurity may be separated. In the Qur'an, fitnah can mean test, turmoil, pressure, seduction, or a condition in which a human being is diverted from his direction.

Bala emphasizes being placed under testing and the disclosure of the quality of action.

Fitnah can more strongly denote intensity of conflict, disturbance, deception, or a situation in which separation and slipping occur.

The two appear together in one verse:

وَنَبْلُوكُمْ بِالشَّرِّ وَالْخَيْرِ فِتْنَةً

Testing is the action, and fitnah may describe the nature of the testing situation.

The difference should not be reduced to the rigid formula “bala is gradual wearing down and fitnah is sudden smelting.” Both can be long or severe, and their precise difference is known from context.

13. B L W (Bala) Compared with M H N (Mihnah)

The root M H N is connected with testing, refining, and knowing quality through pressure or examination.

This root appears verbally in the Qur'an concerning migrant women:

فَامْتَحِنُوهُنَّ

Examine and test them.

Al-Mumtahanah 60:10

Imtihan in this context is a kind of examination to identify the truthfulness of the claim of migration.

Bala is broader and can include the whole condition of life and action; imtihan can be more specific and targeted examination.

14. B L W (Bala) Compared with M S B (Musibah)

A musibah is an event that reaches a human being and afflicts him with loss, sadness, or hardship.

Bala is the structure of testing itself and can occur through calamity or blessing.

So:

Musibah: a hard event that reaches a person; Bala: a situation in which the person's reaction and quality are tested and made manifest.

Every musibah can be a bala, but not every bala is a musibah.

15. Specialized Discussion and Structural Note: Divine Knowledge, Realization of Action, and Disclosure of Inner Secrets

The central question in the theology of ibtila is this: if The God knows everything, why does He test the human being?

The answer should not be sought in God's need to gain information. The Qur'an presents His knowledge as comprehensive. Ibtila is not for turning ignorance into knowledge in His essence.

There is a difference between knowing a possibility and that possibility being realized in a human being's chosen action. The human being must choose, act, and bring the result of his choice into the world of responsibility.

The God knows what the human being will do; but judgment attaches to an action that has occurred, not to a claim that never found room for realization. Ibtila is the field of realization for that action.

For this reason, the Qur'an repeatedly connects the aim with action:

لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا

In this structure, the test is not for disclosing the best slogan, group identity, or claim of faith; the criterion is better action.

Testing also reveals the truth of a person to himself. A person may imagine himself brave, patient, just, or generous, but only in a real situation does it become known how far these qualities have actually been realized in him.

The same is true for society. Crisis, power, or blessing reveals hidden structures:

  • who remains upon the covenant;
  • who withdraws out of fear;
  • who uses power for justice;
  • and who turns blessing into arrogance and corruption.

The peak of this disclosure is seen in the verse:

يَوْمَ تُبْلَى السَّرَائِرُ

On that Day, not only outward behavior but also the inner secrets will be exposed to disclosure.

This verse shows that ibtila is not merely the wearing down of the body before the material world. Its subject can be intention, secret, motive, and inward truth.

At the same time, the inner secrets are not judged merely as mental claims. What is inside is connected with action, direction, and choice. On the Day when the inner secrets are disclosed, the relation between inward reality and behavior becomes clear.

The relation of B L W with B L Y must also remain limited and precise. A garment becomes old and its weave becomes apparent; a human being also makes his quality apparent in situations. This similarity is useful for explanation, but one cannot conclude from it that God tests the human being in order to wear him down.

The aim of ibtila in all cases is not erosion. A person may grow in the test, become purer, show gratitude, or use blessing rightly. He may also fall and reveal his inward corruption.

So ibtila may be understood as follows:

The human being is placed in the real field of choice, so that what exists in possibility and claim becomes action and the truth of his direction becomes manifest.

17. Conclusion

The family B L W in the Qur'an denotes testing in the field of reality and the disclosure of the quality of action.

Ibtila is not for removing ignorance from The God. He knows beforehand, but the human being must realize his choice in the world of action, and his responsibility is formed on the basis of what he has done.

Bala is not only hardship. The Qur'an speaks of testing through evil and good. Poverty and fear test the human being, but wealth, power, victory, and blessing do so as well.

Bala hasan shows that testing may come with good and opening. Bala mubin is a test that reveals the truth of surrender. Ibtuliya al-mu'minun speaks of a severe situation in which truthfulness and hypocrisy become visible. Tubla al-sara'ir shows that even the inner secrets are exposed to disclosure.

Beside this family, B L Y denotes oldness and wearing out, as in mulkin la yabla. The closeness of these two families allows an explanatory image: just as use reveals the quality of a garment, the situations of life reveal the quality of the human being. But ibtila should not be restricted to bodily wearing down.

Also, bal in “aslaha balahum” and the pre-Islamic custom of baliyyah should not be turned into the Qur'anic core of bala without evidence.

The shortest statement for this family is this:

Placing the human being in a real situation in which his choice, quality of action, and hidden truth are realized and disclosed; while the near family B L Y denotes oldness and wearing out.
