Principal meanings: ascending; limping and uneven movement of the legs; bending and changing direction; and, in another branch, a herd of camels. Quranic uses concern ascent, ways of ascending, and a person with a walking impairment.

1. The root in early Arabic

The three-letter root ع ر ج has several recognized semantic branches. Three important ones are:

First, ascending and reaching a higher level or position.

Second, limping and uneven movement of the legs.

Third, bending, turning, and departing from a straight course.

Lexicons also record عَرْج for a herd of camels, a separate branch within the family.

In the first branch, عَرَجَ يَعْرُجُ عُرُوجًا denotes ascending or moving toward a higher position.

It can, for example, describe someone climbing a ladder.

In the second, عَرِجَ concerns an uneven gait or difficulty walking because of the condition of a leg or legs.

In the third, اِنْعَرَجَ describes bending or turning: a path may follow a mountain and then turn right or left.

Changing direction can connect these meanings conceptually, but historical evidence is needed to reduce them to one origin.

Ascending, limping, and turning are different conditions. Upward movement does not require limping, and someone limping is not necessarily ascending.

The Quran distinguishes them clearly: يَعْرُجُ describes ascent, while الْأَعْرَجِ describes a person's walking condition.

Distinguishing the lexical meanings

Early lexicographers, including Ibn Faris, distinguish several semantic foundations:

Inclination and deviation;

Number and a herd of camels;

And height and ascent.

Even early lexicographers thus did not derive all uses from one simple meaning.

Limping may relate to uneven or deviating movement, and a bending road to inclination.

Ascent, however, foregrounds movement to a higher position.

A person can climb a straight ladder without moving in a spiral or deviating from it.

عروج should therefore not always be understood as curved, circular, or spiral movement.

First poetic witness: turning aside and stopping

A poem transmitted under the name of Umm al-Dahhak al-Muharibiyyah reads as follows. This transmission alone does not establish a pre-Islamic date for the line:

يَا أَيُّهَا الرَّاكِبُ الْغَادِي لِطِيَّتِهِ

yā ayyuhā al-rākibu al-ghādī li-ṭiyyatihi

عَرِّجْ أَبُثَّكَ عَنْ بَعْضِ الَّذِي أَجِدُ

ʿarrij abuththaka ʿan baʿḍi alladhī ajidu

Translation: Rider setting out toward your destination, turn aside and stop, that I may tell you some of what I feel.

عَرِّجْ is a Form II imperative.

The poet asks a traveler to pause or turn toward her to hear her words.

تعريج here involves a stop and attention to something along the journey, beyond a simple bend in the road.

عَرَّجَ عَلَى الْمَنْزِلِ is recorded for stopping at a place during a journey.

This relates to diverting one's course toward a place.

Early literary collections transmit the verse. Some have أُنَبِّئْكَ instead of أَبُثَّكَ and attribute it to Zaynab bint Farwah al-Murriyyah.

The attribution varies, but the use of عَرِّجْ for turning aside and stopping remains in these transmissions.

The witness shows that تعريج could denote stopping during a journey or turning toward another place.

Second witness: camel herds in pre-Islamic poetry

A poem attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Tarafah ibn al-Abd reads:

يَوْمَ تُبْدِي الْبِيضُ عَنْ أَسْوُقِهَا

yawma tubdī al-bīḍu ʿan aswuqihā

وَتَلُفُّ الْخَيْلُ أَعْرَاجَ النَّعَمْ

wa-taluffu al-khaylu aʿrāja al-naʿam

Translation: On the day when the fair women uncover their shins and the horses surround the camel herds.

أَعْرَاج is the plural of عَرْج, a herd or group of camels.

النَّعَم here denotes livestock, particularly camels.

The word concerns groups of camels, not limping camels or camels ascending a mountain.

Lexicons give varying herd sizes, from about eighty or ninety to several hundred.

This branch names a herd without fixing the same exact number in every usage.

The line occurs in Tarafah's poem beginning سَائِلُوا عَنَّا الَّذِي يَعْرِفُنَا.

It witnesses a branch concerning camel groupings in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period.

It does not make Quranic مَعَارِج mean camel herds or their numbers.

A witness concerning limping

A verse by al-Shammakh ibn Dirar reads:

فَبِتُّ كَأَنِّي مُتَّقٍ رَأْسَ حَيَّةٍ

fa-bittu ka-annī muttaqin raʾsa ḥayyatin

لِحَاجَتِهَا إِنْ تُخْطِئِ النَّفْسَ تُعْرِجِ

li-ḥājatihā in tukhṭiʾi al-nafsa tuʿriji

Approximate translation: I spent the night as though guarding against a snake's head intent on striking: if it misses life itself, it leaves one limping. The image concerns injury from a snake.

تُعْرِجِ here means causing someone to limp, rather than merely limping oneself.

It is a causative form from أَعْرَجَ, to make someone lame, not the verb of ascent.

Al-Shammakh lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods. The exact date of this line was not independently established here; it is early Arabic evidence without an assured pre-Quranic date.

Its usage agrees with lexical accounts of عَرَج and أَعْرَج.

Historical conclusion

Arabic evidence establishes several distinct branches in actual usage.

One concerns ascending to a higher position.

Another concerns limping and uneven movement.

A third concerns bending a route and stopping during travel.

Another uses عَرْج for camel herds.

This range cannot be reduced to a narrow meaning such as bending or ascending alone.

2. Word family and morphological forms

Recorded forms include عَرَجَ, عَرِجَ, عَرَج, عُرُوج, عَرَجَان, أَعْرَج, عَرْجَاء, عُرْج, عَرِيج, عَارِج, مَعْرَج, مِعْرَج, مِعْرَاج, مَعَارِج, عَرَّجَ, تَعْرِيج, تَعَرَّجَ, اِنْعَرَجَ, and مُنْعَرَج.

عَرَجَ يَعْرُجُ: ascending

In the ascent branch, عَرَجَ يَعْرُجُ عُرُوجًا means going up to a higher position.

عَرَجَ فِي السُّلَّمِ describes climbing a ladder.

عَرَجَ إِلَى الْمَوْضِعِ describes ascending toward a higher place.

The verb can apply to people or other beings.

Quranic ascent concerns people going through a heavenly gate, angels and the Spirit, the affair, and broadly whatever ascends in heaven.

The branch's basic meaning is ascending or moving to a higher level or position.

The verb alone specifies neither speed, means, nor the exact shape of the route.

Ascent may use a ladder, stairs, or another route.

It need not involve a strictly vertical or spiral path or a particular device.

عُرُوج: ascent

عُرُوج is the verbal noun of عَرَجَ in its ascending sense.

It names the act of ascent itself.

Material examples include climbing a ladder or reaching an elevated place.

In heavenly or unseen contexts, the manner of ascent may remain unexplained to human beings.

The lexical meaning is clear, but knowing the verb's meaning differs from knowing every physical feature of an event.

عَرِجَ: developing a limp

عَرِجَ commonly concerns becoming lame or having an uneven gait.

Lexicons distinguish some verbal forms connected with limping.

عَرِجَ يَعْرَجُ is particularly associated with an established condition that may be lasting.

عَرَجَ يَعْرُجُ is also recorded for limping caused by injury or a temporary condition.

This distinction should not be made exceptionless across every dialect and period.

Both concern uneven leg movement and difficulty walking in the usual way.

أَعْرَج and عَرْجَاء: a person who limps

أَعْرَج is masculine and عَرْجَاء feminine.

The plurals عُرْج and عُرْجَان are also recorded.

رَجُلٌ أَعْرَجُ describes a man who walks with a limp.

امْرَأَةٌ عَرْجَاءُ describes the corresponding condition in a woman.

The adjective alone does not identify the cause.

The condition may be present from birth or follow injury or illness.

People also differ in the degree of their mobility limitation.

أعرج describes walking, not the entirety of someone's physical, intellectual, or moral capabilities.

The Quran places it alongside blindness and illness in contexts of responsibility and freedom from blame or hardship.

عَرَجَان and عُرْجَة: limping gait

عَرَجَان denotes the gait of someone who limps.

عُرْجَة is also recorded for the location or condition of lameness in a leg.

تَعَارَجَ can mean imitating a limping gait.

A bodily condition must therefore be distinguished from deliberate imitation.

One person may genuinely find walking difficult, while another mimics that movement for performance or imitation.

Different forms express these different circumstances.

مَعْرَج, مِعْرَج, and مِعْرَاج: means or route of ascent

مَعْرَج and مِعْرَج are recorded for a place or route of ascending.

مِعْرَاج can mean a ladder or means of ascent.

In material usage, it is a means of reaching a higher place.

A ladder or staircase permits movement from a lower level to a higher one.

The name of the means or route is connected with the act of ascending.

The singular noun مِعْرَاج does not occur independently in the Quran.

The plural مَعَارِج does occur.

مَعَارِج: ways and places of ascent

Lexicons relate this plural to مَعْرَج and مِعْرَج. The more regular plural of مِعْرَاج is مَعَارِيج, although lexical discussions also connect it with مَعَارِج.

The word denotes ways, steps, or means used to ascend.

It can concern stairways and routes upward within a building.

Other uses concern ascending ways or levels.

Its two Quranic occurrences have clearly different contexts.

Al-Zukhruf 43:33 concerns ways or steps used to ascend in houses.

Al-Maarij 70:3 describes The God, followed by the ascent of angels and the Spirit.

Both concern ascent, while their type and setting come from context.

عَرَّجَ and تَعْرِيج: turning aside and stopping

عَرَّجَ is Form II.

It can denote turning aside, stopping at a lodging place, or remaining somewhere for a time.

عَرَّجَ عَلَى الْمَنْزِلِ means stopping at a place during travel.

عَرَّجَ عَلَى فُلَانٍ can concern stopping by someone or attending to that person along the way.

This relates to changing course and inclining toward something.

تعريج in this sense differs from ascent.

A traveler can stop on level ground without climbing anywhere.

اِنْعَرَجَ and مُنْعَرَج: a turning route

اِنْعَرَجَ concerns bending and turning.

مُنْعَرَج is a bend or turning place.

A river may flow along a course and then turn right or left.

That curved part can be called a منعرج.

A mountain road may also bend repeatedly because of the terrain.

This resembles اِعْوِجَاج from ع و ج, but the roots have different letters and derivational families.

Not every use of عروج should therefore be interpreted as turning or becoming crooked.

عَرْج: a camel herd

In a different branch, عَرْج names a group of camels.

أَعْرَاج and عُرُوج are recorded plurals.

Tarafah's أَعْرَاجَ النَّعَمْ uses this meaning.

Lexicographers differ over the number of camels constituting a herd of this name.

It is therefore a herd designation, not an invariably fixed numerical unit.

This usage is absent from the Quran and should not be confused with Quranic معارج or ascent.

3. Quranic occurrences

The root occurs nine times in nine verses across eight surahs.

The occurrences fall into three morphological groups:

  • Five Form I verbs: يَعْرُجُونَ once, يَعْرُجُ three times, and تَعْرُجُ once.
  • الْأَعْرَجِ twice.
  • مَعَارِج twice.

The Quranic verb denotes ascending.

الأعرج describes someone with a limping gait.

معارج denotes ways or means of ascent.

These groups must remain distinct.

A. يَعْرُجُونَ: ascending through a gate in heaven

وَلَوْ فَتَحْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ بَابًا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ فَظَلُّوا فِيهِ يَعْرُجُونَ

wa-law fataḥnā ʿalayhim bāban mina al-samāʾi fa-ẓallū fīhi yaʿrujūn

Al-Ḥijr 15:14

And if We opened for them a gate from heaven and they continued ascending through it,

Al-Hijr 15:14

يَعْرُجُونَ is the plural imperfect of عَرَجَ يَعْرُجُ.

It means they go up or ascend.

فِيهِ relates to the gate opened in heaven.

The ascent is along a route associated with that gate.

Ascent and the audience's response

The verse presents a hypothetical situation.

If a heavenly gate were opened and they continued ascending through it, how would they respond?

The next verse answers:

لَقَالُوا إِنَّمَا سُكِّرَتْ أَبْصَارُنَا بَلْ نَحْنُ قَوْمٌ مَسْحُورُونَ

la-qālū innamā sukkirat abṣārunā bal naḥnu qawmun masḥūrūn

Al-Ḥijr 15:15

They would say: Our sight has only been blocked or altered; rather, we are an enchanted people.

Al-Hijr 15:15

The passage concerns both ascent and people's response to a conspicuous sign.

Even experiencing ascent, they might attribute it to impaired sight or enchantment.

Experiencing an event thus differs from accepting the truth to which it points.

يَعْرُجُونَ itself expresses only ascending.

It does not mean believing, witnessing truth, or accepting a sign.

Does ascent mean spiral motion?

يَعْرُجُونَ denotes going up here.

The verb does not require a spiral, circular, or precisely angled trajectory.

Arabic uses ascent for climbing a ladder or following an upward route.

The exact motion, speed, and mechanism cannot be inferred from the root alone.

Lexical meaning should not be equated with a theory of heavenly structure or travel.

The verse hypothesizes a gate and ascent without specifying the event's physical details.

B. يَعْرُجُ إِلَيْهِ: the affair ascends to The God

يُدَبِّرُ الْأَمْرَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ إِلَى الْأَرْضِ ثُمَّ يَعْرُجُ إِلَيْهِ فِي يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ مِمَّا تَعُدُّونَ

yudabbiru al-amra mina al-samāʾi ilā al-arḍi thumma yaʿruju ilayhi fī yawmin kāna miqdāruhu alfa sanatin mimmā taʿuddūn

Al-Sajdah 32:5

He directs the affair from heaven to earth; then it ascends to Him in a day whose measure is a thousand years of your reckoning.

Al-Sajdah 32:5

يَعْرُجُ is masculine singular imperfect.

الْأَمْرَ appears earlier in the verse.

The natural reading relates the ascending subject to the affair.

The God directs the affair from heaven to earth, then it ascends to Him.

Direction and ascent

يُدَبِّرُ belongs to د ب ر and concerns directing and ordering the affair.

يَعْرُجُ belongs to ع ر ج and concerns ascent.

They do not mean the same thing.

Direction from heaven to earth is followed by ascent to The God.

A relationship is expressed between directing the affair and its return or ascent.

Ascent itself does not mean returning.

Return may be inferred from the contextual relationship, whereas يعرج lexically means going up.

فِي يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ

The final phrase describes a day measured as a thousand years of human reckoning.

It relates to ascent in that sentence.

It should not automatically become a particular physical speed for heavenly beings or messages.

The verse supplies neither a numerical distance between two points nor a means of movement.

Dividing an assumed distance by a thousand years cannot establish the actual speed of ascent.

The verb does not itself mean a thousand years elapsing; the time comes from the separate phrase.

C. يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا: whatever ascends in heaven

يَعْلَمُ مَا يَلِجُ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَمَا يَخْرُجُ مِنْهَا وَمَا يَنْزِلُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ وَمَا يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا ۚ وَهُوَ الرَّحِيمُ الْغَفُورُ

yaʿlamu mā yaliju fī al-arḍi wa-mā yakhruju minhā wa-mā yanzilu mina al-samāʾi wa-mā yaʿruju fīhā wa-huwa al-raḥīmu al-ghafūr

Sabaʾ 34:2

He knows what enters the earth and what emerges from it, what descends from heaven and what ascends in it; and He is the Especially Merciful, the Forgiving.

Saba 34:2

يَعْرُجُ is contrasted with يَنْزِلُ.

يَنْزِلُ belongs to ن ز ل and denotes descending.

يَعْرُجُ denotes ascending.

This contrast clarifies the meaning of ascent.

The verse concerns what enters and emerges from the earth, descends from heaven, or ascends in it.

مَا: the breadth of divine knowledge

مَا appears repeatedly.

مَا يَلِجُ, مَا يَخْرُجُ, مَا يَنْزِلُ, and مَا يَعْرُجُ.

The verse does not restrict its subject to one being or kind of movement.

Without contextual evidence, مَا يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا should not be translated exclusively as angels or human deeds.

Commentary may discuss different examples, but the wording has a broader scope.

Whatever ascends in it is suitable for direct translation.

The parallel in Al-Hadid

هُوَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ اسْتَوَىٰ عَلَى الْعَرْشِ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا يَلِجُ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَمَا يَخْرُجُ مِنْهَا وَمَا يَنْزِلُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ وَمَا يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا ۖ وَهُوَ مَعَكُمْ أَيْنَ مَا كُنْتُمْ ۚ وَاللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ

huwa alladhī khalaqa al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa fī sittati ayyāmin thumma istawā ʿalā al-ʿarshi yaʿlamu mā yaliju fī al-arḍi wa-mā yakhruju minhā wa-mā yanzilu mina al-samāʾi wa-mā yaʿruju fīhā wa-huwa maʿakum ayna mā kuntum wa-llāhu bi-mā taʿmalūna baṣīr

Al-Ḥadīd 57:4

He is the One who created the heavens and earth in six days, then established Himself upon the Throne. He knows what enters the earth and what emerges from it, what descends from heaven and what ascends in it. He is with you wherever you are, and The God sees what you do.

Al-Hadid 57:4

The same four constructions of entry, emergence, descent, and ascent appear.

يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا therefore has the same meaning in both verses.

Al-Hadid also connects it with creation, the Throne, divine presence with people, and knowledge of their deeds.

Ascent is one of the movements and events encompassed by divine knowledge.

Does ascent locate The God within a particular place?

Al-Sajdah 32:5 and Al-Maarij 70:4 include إِلَيْهِ.

This expresses ascent's direction or relation toward The God.

The verb alone cannot establish a material location, distance from earth, or confinement of The God to a cosmic point.

Al-Hadid 57:4 adds وَهُوَ مَعَكُمْ أَيْنَ مَا كُنْتُمْ.

Al-Shura 42:11 also states لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ.

The verse's language of direction must be distinguished from imagining The God as a spatially confined body.

Ascent lexically means going up; one motion verb cannot determine the unseen world's nature and its relation to The God.

D. تَعْرُجُ: angels and the Spirit ascend

تَعْرُجُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ وَالرُّوحُ إِلَيْهِ فِي يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُ خَمْسِينَ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ

taʿruju al-malāʾikatu wa-l-rūḥu ilayhi fī yawmin kāna miqdāruhu khamsīna alfa sanah

Al-Maʿārij 70:4

The angels and the Spirit ascend to Him in a day whose measure is fifty thousand years.

Al-Maarij 70:4

تَعْرُجُ is the imperfect of Form I عَرَجَ.

Its subject is الْمَلَائِكَةُ وَالرُّوحُ.

Unlike Saba 34:2 and Al-Hadid 57:4, the ascending beings are named explicitly.

The angels and the Spirit ascend to The God.

تَعْرُجُ and يَعْرُجُ

Both are Form I imperfect verbs meaning ascending.

Their difference concerns grammatical form and agreement with the subject or referent.

الملائكة is a broken plural. A preceding verb can be feminine singular with a collective interpretation; this does not classify angels as non-rational beings. In the reading used here, تَعْرُجُ has this grammatical form.

تَعْرُجُ does not designate a distinct kind of ascent.

The initial letter alone cannot make one ascent bodily and the other non-bodily.

الرُّوحُ and the limits of the wording

الرُّوحُ appears alongside الْمَلَائِكَةُ.

The verb of ascent does not identify the Spirit.

The root ر و ح and other Quranic usages must be studied for that question.

This root article should not silently replace the Spirit with a particular person's or angel's name.

Retaining Spirit in direct translation is more precise.

Fifty thousand years and a thousand years

Al-Sajdah 32:5 describes a day measured as a thousand years.

Al-Maarij 70:4 gives fifty thousand years.

The different numbers do not alone prove either an error or that both verses describe precisely the same temporal event.

Their subjects and contexts differ.

Al-Sajdah concerns directing the affair from heaven to earth and its ascent.

Al-Maarij concerns the ascent of angels and the Spirit, alongside a description of The God and the passage's discussion of judgment.

Equating the two days requires independent justification; it is not a lexical conclusion from ع ر ج.

Calculating angels' speed from these numbers without distance and conditions of movement is not justified by the wording or by physical calculation.

Ascent means going up; time and the event's nature must be examined through the separate phrases of each verse.

4. مَعَارِج: ascending ways and stairways

A. مَعَارِج: stairways in houses

وَلَوْلَا أَنْ يَكُونَ النَّاسُ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً لَجَعَلْنَا لِمَنْ يَكْفُرُ بِالرَّحْمَٰنِ لِبُيُوتِهِمْ سُقُفًا مِنْ فِضَّةٍ وَمَعَارِجَ عَلَيْهَا يَظْهَرُونَ

wa-lawlā an yakūna al-nāsu ummatan wāḥidatan la-jaʿalnā li-man yakfuru bi-l-raḥmāni li-buyūtihim suqufan min fiḍḍatin wa-maʿārija ʿalayhā yaẓharūn

Al-Zukhruf 43:33

Were it not that people would become one community, We would have made for those who disbelieve in the Entirely Merciful silver roofs for their houses and stairways on which they ascend.

Al-Zukhruf 43:33

مَعَارِجَ is plural, denoting ways or means of ascending.

The context concerns houses, roofs, and material amenities.

Here it denotes stairways or routes to higher parts of a building.

عَلَيْهَا يَظْهَرُونَ further clarifies this.

يَظْهَرُونَ, from ظ ه ر, means climbing up and reaching a higher surface in this construction.

معارج names the means or route; the following verb describes using it.

The tangible meaning of معارج

This supplies a concrete example from human life.

Stairs in a two-story house connect the lower floor with the upper.

They may be straight, curved, or divided into sections.

Their exact shape is not essential to the meaning.

The word foregrounds the possibility and route of ascent.

Stairways or ways of ascending are suitable equivalents here.

Does material wealth prove divine approval?

The verse envisages costly amenities for those who disbelieve.

Al-Zukhruf 43:34 and Al-Zukhruf 43:35 continue with doors, couches, and adornments.

Al-Zukhruf 43:35 calls these مَتَاعُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا.

Silver roofs and luxurious stairs do not independently prove an owner's righteousness or moral superiority.

This comes from context; معارج itself does not mean wealth, arrogance, or disbelief.

Stairs are a means of ascent and can exist in anyone's home.

Moral worth concerns conduct and choices, not the presence of stairs or building materials.

B. ذِي الْمَعَارِجِ: Possessor of the ascending ways

مِنَ اللَّهِ ذِي الْمَعَارِجِ

mina allāhi dhī al-maʿārij

Al-Maʿārij 70:3

From The God, Possessor of the ascending ways.

Al-Maarij 70:3

الْمَعَارِجِ occurs within ذِي الْمَعَارِجِ.

ذِي means possessing or having and qualifies The God here.

Under the reading adopted here, الْمَعَارِجِ denotes ascending ways or stations; early explanations also include elevated degrees, greatness, and levels of beneficence.

The following verse describes angels and the Spirit ascending to The God.

This connection helps clarify معارج.

Verse 3 describes The God as Possessor of معارج.

Verse 4 describes angels and the Spirit ascending to Him.

Two words from one family occur consecutively and illuminate one another.

Can معارج mean degrees and ranks?

Ascending ways can relate conceptually to higher levels.

Stairs, for example, take someone from one level to another.

Some lexical and exegetical explanations therefore connect معارج with degrees, ranks, or elevated stations when describing The God.

The following تَعْرُجُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ وَالرُّوحُ إِلَيْهِ emphasizes ascent.

This article prefers Possessor of the ascending ways to preserve that verbal connection; it is a translation choice, not a claim that other transmitted explanations lack evidence.

Elevated degrees and levels of beneficence are recorded alternatives and should be considered separately rather than excluded as lexically impossible.

Material and unseen ascending ways

Al-Zukhruf 43:33 concerns building stairways.

Under the interpretation adopted here, Al-Maarij 70:3 concerns ascending ways in a description of The God.

Both relate to ascent, but their contextual nature differs.

Building stairs are material means on which people move bodily.

The passage about angels and the Spirit does not specify the construction or substance of their routes.

Heavenly ways should not be imagined as architectural stairs or a particular material without evidence.

The word itself does not specify their number, shape, or distances.

Their relationship to ascent is what the root makes clear.

5. الْأَعْرَجِ: walking impairment and responsibility

الْأَعْرَجِ occurs twice in the Quran.

The references are Al-Nur 24:61 and Al-Fath 48:17.

Both place it alongside الْأَعْمَىٰ and الْمَرِيضِ while negating حَرَج.

A. الْأَعْرَجِ in Al-Nur

لَيْسَ عَلَى الْأَعْمَىٰ حَرَجٌ وَلَا عَلَى الْأَعْرَجِ حَرَجٌ وَلَا عَلَى الْمَرِيضِ حَرَجٌ وَلَا عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَنْ تَأْكُلُوا مِنْ بُيُوتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ آبَائِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أُمَّهَاتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ إِخْوَانِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أَخَوَاتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أَعْمَامِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ عَمَّاتِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ أَخْوَالِكُمْ أَوْ بُيُوتِ خَالَاتِكُمْ أَوْ مَا مَلَكْتُمْ مَفَاتِحَهُ أَوْ صَدِيقِكُمْ ۚ لَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ أَنْ تَأْكُلُوا جَمِيعًا أَوْ أَشْتَاتًا ۚ فَإِذَا دَخَلْتُمْ بُيُوتًا فَسَلِّمُوا عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِكُمْ تَحِيَّةً مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ مُبَارَكَةً طَيِّبَةً ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ لَكُمُ الْآيَاتِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ

Al-Nūr 24:61

There is no blame upon the blind person, the person who limps, or the sick person; nor upon yourselves for eating from your houses, your fathers' houses, your mothers' houses, your brothers' houses, your sisters' houses, your paternal uncles' houses, your paternal aunts' houses, your maternal uncles' houses, your maternal aunts' houses, those whose keys you possess, or your friend's house. There is no sin upon you for eating together or separately. When you enter houses, greet one another with a blessed, wholesome greeting from The God. Thus The God makes the signs clear to you, so that you may reason.

Al-Nur 24:61

الْأَعْرَجِ describes a person who walks with a limp.

It appears alongside blindness and illness.

حَرَج belongs to ح ر ج and concerns difficulty, constraint, and, in responsibility contexts, blame or sin.

لَيْسَ عَلَى الْأَعْرَجِ حَرَجٌ removes such blame or burden from the person concerned.

Walking impairment and social relationships

The verse discusses eating in specified homes, together or separately, and greeting people upon entry.

This article relates the opening to that social context. Early explanations also connect the opening exemption with fighting; the interpretation adopted here should not be presented as the only transmitted account.

Social life and interaction are clearly also addressed.

The Quran names people with differing bodily conditions before discussing food and family and social relationships.

Read in this social context, the passage supports the principle that physical limitations should not in themselves exclude a person from social participation and its benefits.

The verse alone does not settle every detail of access to homes or quantities of another person's food. These depend on its other phrases, relationships, and respect for one another's rights.

Does الأعرج mean entirely incapable?

No.

أعرج describes walking.

Someone may have difficulty walking while being highly capable in thinking, teaching, writing, management, and many other activities.

The degree of impairment also varies.

The word does not imply incapacity in every domain or a need for guardianship.

Removing blame does not devalue the person's other abilities.

B. الْأَعْرَجِ in Al-Fath

لَيْسَ عَلَى الْأَعْمَىٰ حَرَجٌ وَلَا عَلَى الْأَعْرَجِ حَرَجٌ وَلَا عَلَى الْمَرِيضِ حَرَجٌ ۗ وَمَنْ يُطِعِ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ يُدْخِلْهُ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ وَمَنْ يَتَوَلَّ يُعَذِّبْهُ عَذَابًا أَلِيمًا

laysa ʿalā al-aʿmā ḥarajun wa-lā ʿalā al-aʿraji ḥarajun wa-lā ʿalā al-marīḍi ḥarajun wa-man yuṭiʿi allāha wa-rasūlahu yudkhilhu jannātin tajrī min taḥtihā al-anhāru wa-man yatawalla yuʿadhdhibhu ʿadhāban alīmā

Al-Fatḥ 48:17

There is no blame upon the blind person, the person who limps, or the sick person. Whoever obeys The God and His Messenger, He will admit into gardens beneath which rivers flow; whoever turns away, He will punish with painful punishment.

Al-Fath 48:17

الأعرج again accompanies blindness and illness.

Al-Fath's context differs from Al-Nur's.

Al-Fath 48:16 describes a call to confront people of formidable military strength.

Verse 17 then addresses the conditions of blind, limping, or sick people.

The exemption relates to participation in a demanding wartime situation.

Actual inability and voluntary refusal

Actual physical capacity matters in determining responsibility.

Someone unable to walk as a demanding mission requires is not situated identically to someone with that capacity.

This does not exempt a person who limps from every human and moral responsibility.

The person may assist, teach, decide, or undertake another task suited to their ability.

Exemption from a task prevented by physical limitation does not remove all responsibilities.

Conversely, someone genuinely unable to perform a task should not be blamed for that inability.

This accords with Al-Baqarah 2:286, which speaks of no soul being burdened beyond its capacity.

The two occurrences compared

This article reads Al-Nur 24:61 in relation to social interaction, eating, and relief from blame in that setting, while acknowledging the alternative explanation of its opening.

Al-Fath 48:17 places the exemption within a call to demanding confrontation.

The lexical meaning is the same in both: a person who limps.

The relevant responsibility and setting of exemption differ.

The root's meaning must not be equated with each verse's particular ruling.

أعرج names a walking condition; the responsibility comes from context.

6. Comparison with related roots

ع ر ج and ص ع د: ascending

ص ع د concerns going up toward a height.

The ascent branch of عَرَجَ also denotes going up.

The verbs are close in many uses.

Both can describe climbing a ladder.

ع ر ج also has branches involving limping, curved routes, and ways of ascent.

Their derivatives are therefore not all identical.

تعرج الملائكة should not be restricted to spiral movement or a bending course merely because of its root.

Its clear meaning is ascent.

ع ر ج and ر ق ي: reaching a higher position

ارتقاء, from ر ق ي, concerns ascending to a higher level or place.

Al-Isra 17:93 contains تَرْقَىٰ فِي السَّمَاءِ.

It is semantically close to يَعْرُجُونَ in Al-Hijr 15:14.

Both concern going up.

This shared meaning does not make every detail of the passages identical.

Al-Isra 17:93 places the request within demands for particular signs.

Al-Hijr 15:14 hypothesizes opening a heavenly gate and the audience ascending.

The motion is similar, but the construction and purpose differ.

ع ر ج and ن ز ل: ascent and descent

ن ز ل concerns descending.

Saba 34:2 and Al-Hadid 57:4 place يَنْزِلُ and يَعْرُجُ in one sentence.

They form a clear semantic contrast.

Descent is downward movement or coming down.

Ascent is upward movement or going up.

The Quran can apply them to different subjects.

Their contrast does not require everything descending to ascend again by the same route and in the same manner.

ع ر ج and ظ ل ع: limping

ظ ل ع concerns limping and uneven leg movement.

أعرج likewise describes a person with a limping gait.

Their bodily uses are close.

ع ر ج additionally includes ascent and ascending ways.

Its whole range cannot be understood through ظ ل ع.

Limping does not necessarily mean losing all mobility.

A person may walk more slowly or differently while remaining able to move around.

ع ر ج and ع و ج: turning and crookedness

ع و ج concerns curvature and crookedness.

اِنْعَرَجَ likewise concerns bending and turning in its route-related branch.

A road may bend at a certain point.

That part may be called a منعرج.

اِعْوِجَاج has a broader range of material and figurative crookedness.

The roots overlap in curvature without being complete synonyms.

Quranic عُرُوج and أَعْرَج especially cannot be translated simply as crookedness.

ع ر ج and د ر ج: ascending ways and degrees

One branch of د ر ج concerns steps, degrees, and movement by stages.

مَعَارِج can also denote stairways and upward routes.

Building stairs fit Al-Zukhruf 43:33.

درجات can also describe different ranks of people, deeds, or positions.

The way upward and the level reached are related but distinct concepts.

For Al-Maarij 70:3, this article therefore proposes Possessor of the ascending ways, while treating degrees and ranks as recognized explanatory alternatives.

ع ر ج and ع ر ج ن: different formations

عُرْجُون occurs in Ya-Sin 36:39.

It names the curved main stalk of a date cluster.

The site's Quranic root index lists it separately under the four-letter family ع ر ج ن.

Lexicographers differ over whether its final n is historically radical or additional.

Some treat it as quadriliteral, while others connect the stalk's curvature with ع ر ج.

That issue belongs to the independent study of ع ر ج ن.

In either analysis, عرجون differs in meaning from معارج and عروج.

عرجون names part of a date palm.

معارج denotes ascending ways or means.

عروج names ascent itself.

Their resemblance does not make عرجون a ladder or climbing device.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

The ascent, limping, and bending branches must remain distinct.

عُرُوج: ascent; going up.

عَارِج: ascending; someone or something going up.

عَرِجَ: became lame; developed an uneven gait.

عَرَج: lameness or a limping gait; عَرْج, with a different vowel pattern, denotes a camel herd.

أَعْرَج: a person who walks with a limp.

عَرْجَاء: the feminine of أعرج.

عَرَجَان: a limping gait.

مَعْرَج and مِعْرَج: an ascending route or place.

مِعْرَاج: a ladder or means of ascent.

مَعَارِج: ways, stairs, or means of ascending.

عَرَّجَ: stopped along the way; turned toward a person or place.

تَعْرِيج: stopping during travel; elsewhere, bending or changing course.

اِنْعَرَجَ: bent; turned; inclined away from a straight course.

مُنْعَرَج: a bend or turning place.

أَعْرَاج: camel herds; plural of عَرْج in this usage.

8. Conclusion

The root has recognized branches concerning ascent, limping, a turning route, and camel herds.

In the poem attributed to Umm al-Dahhak, عَرِّجْ concerns turning aside and stopping briefly during travel.

Tarafah's أَعْرَاجُ النَّعَمْ concerns camel herds.

Al-Shammakh's verse also witnesses the branch concerning lameness and injury.

Together with early lexical records, these demonstrate more than one narrow meaning.

عَرَجَ يَعْرُجُ denotes ascent, and مَعَارِج names its ways or means.

أَعْرَج describes a person with an uneven or impaired gait.

اِنْعَرَجَ describes a turning route and مُنْعَرَج its bend.

عَرْج names a group of camels in a separate branch.

The Quran uses the root nine times.

All five verbal occurrences concern ascent.

Al-Hijr 15:14 hypothesizes people ascending through a heavenly gate.

Al-Sajdah 32:5 concerns the affair ascending to The God.

Saba 34:2 and Al-Hadid 57:4 contrast ascent with descent among events encompassed by divine knowledge.

Al-Maarij 70:4 describes angels and the Spirit ascending to The God.

In none of these does the root alone determine trajectory, speed, or means of travel.

Ascent should not be converted without evidence into spiral motion, a particular device, or a process with a calculable speed.

مَعَارِج occurs twice.

Al-Zukhruf 43:33 concerns building stairways or upward routes.

Al-Maarij 70:3 calls The God Possessor of معارج, followed by the ascent of angels and the Spirit.

This relates the word to ascending ways and levels without revealing their unseen nature from the word alone.

The other two occurrences are الْأَعْرَجِ.

Al-Nur 24:61 and Al-Fath 48:17 place a person who limps alongside blind and sick people and remove blame.

Al-Nur's context includes social relationships and eating in homes; this article adopts the social reading of the opening while acknowledging alternatives.

Al-Fath concerns participation in a demanding confrontation.

These usages show that physical limitations must be considered when assessing actual capacity and responsibility.

أعرج does not alone mean total incapacity or absence of other human abilities.

The site also distinguishes ع ر ج from ع ر ج ن. عُرْجُون in Ya-Sin 36:39 names the curved date-cluster stalk and has a separate quadriliteral index entry. Its historical connection with curvature is debated, but its Quranic meaning comes from the word and context.

Not every use reduces to bending, nor does every use reduce to ascent.

Final definition: ع ر ج denotes ascent in one principal motion-related branch, limping and uneven leg movement in another, and bending, turning, or stopping in route-related uses. A separate branch names camel herds. In the Quran, يَعْرُجُ and related verbs concern ascent, مَعَارِج its ways or means, and الْأَعْرَجِ a person who walks with a limp. Each form must be understood through morphology and context, without imposing one branch's meaning on another without evidence.
