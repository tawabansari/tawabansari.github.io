Core meaning: a matter becoming difficult and entangled, making release or progress hard; in human relationships, obstructing, hindering, and constraining someone so that they cannot attain what they seek or exercise a right.

1. The root and its meaning in early Arabic

The triliteral ع ض ل concerns difficulty, constriction, complexity, and obstruction. It describes situations from which escape or resolution is difficult, as well as deliberate human behavior that blocks another person's path or places them under constraint.

Early Arabic records عَضَلَ عَلَيْهِ and عَضَّلَ عَلَيْهِ for constraining someone and preventing them from attaining what they seek. This involves more than disagreement: a person uses their position or authority to make another's path difficult or closed.

أَعْضَلَ الْأَمْرُ describes a matter becoming difficult, complex, or exceedingly hard to resolve. أَمْرٌ مُعْضِلٌ is a problem for which a solution is not readily found.

دَاءٌ عُضَالٌ describes a severe illness difficult to treat, which may defeat the efforts of healers.

عَضَّلَتِ الْمَرْأَةُ بِوَلَدِهَا also describes difficult childbirth, where the child becomes obstructed in emerging and delivery does not proceed easily.

No one necessarily intends to prevent birth in this use. The difficulty arises from a bodily condition.

In interpersonal uses, such as obstructing a woman's marriage, someone may consciously use power to block another's path.

We must therefore distinguish difficulty arising without human intent from deliberate obstruction intended to restrict another person.

عَضَلَة also names a thick, cohesive portion of flesh and bodily tissue. Early Arabic uses it for firm, fleshy parts of the upper arm and lower leg.

Modern medical usage gives “muscle” a specific technical meaning, but that definition should not be projected onto every early use without examination.

Firm, cohesive flesh has an intelligible association with hardness and constriction. This resemblance alone does not establish that all the root's meanings arose from the bodily term.

The definition must therefore accommodate several attested branches: hardness and constriction, difficulty in passage or resolving a problem, and preventing another from attaining a right or desired end.

The third branch is central to the Quranic uses.

2. Early poetic evidence and attribution

An early example occurs in poetry attributed to Dhu al-Isba al-Adwani, a pre-Islamic poet:

وَاحِدَةٌ أَعْضَلَكُمْ أَمْرُهَا
فَكَيْفَ لَوْ دُرْتُ عَلَى أَرْبَعِ

wāḥidatun aʿḍalakum amruhā / fa-kayfa law durtu ʿalā arbaʿi

“The matter of one woman has proved so difficult for you; what if I had turned to four?”

The account accompanying the verse says that the poet sought his relatives' help in providing a marriage payment, but they did not comply. He mocked the difficulty they had raised over one marriage.

أَعْضَلَكُمْ أَمْرُهَا concerns a matter proving difficult and entangled.

أَعْضَلَ here does not mean biting, bodily injury, or strong muscles. It describes something that has become difficult for people to accomplish.

The example also has social interest, since it concerns marriage and difficulties involving those around the person seeking it.

The lexical meaning is not restricted to marriage; the expression can apply to any complex and difficult matter.

Early sources attribute the verse to Dhu al-Isba, but transmit different wording, including أَعْضَلَكُمْ شَأْنُهَا and variants of the second half. It is useful lexical evidence, but neither every textual detail nor the date of composition has been independently established here.

Another example is attributed to Aws ibn Hajar, a pre-Islamic poet:

تَرَى الْأَرْضَ مِنَّا بِالْفَضَاءِ مَرِيضَةً
مُعَضِّلَةً مِنَّا بِجَمْعٍ عَرَمْرَمِ

tarā al-arḍa minnā bi-al-faḍāʾi marīḍatan / muʿaḍḍilatan minnā bi-jamʿin ʿaramrami

“You see the open land made ill by us, constrained by our immense gathering.”

مُعَضِّلَة describes land crowded with people, its space seeming constricted.

The poet depicts the land as a living being under pressure.

The image illustrates the use of عضل for constricted space and difficulty of movement.

The land has not literally contracted a human illness. مَرِيضَة and مُعَضِّلَة figuratively express the intensity of crowding.

This is not deliberate obstruction, but a difficult and compressed condition caused by a large gathering.

Early evidence thus attests difficulty, constriction, and obstacles to accomplishing something. The Quran draws on this linguistic capacity to describe obstruction in human relationships.

Without independent historical evidence, one cannot insist that the marriage sense arose after the senses involving illness or bodily obstruction.

3. Lexical and Quranic forms

Important forms include عَضَلَ, يَعْضُلُ, عَضْل, عَضِلَ, عَضَلَة, عُضَال, أَعْضَلَ, مُعْضِل, عَضَّلَ, and تَعْضِيل.

These forms belong to several semantic branches.

عَضَلَ: To obstruct, prevent, or constrain, depending on syntax and context.

عَضْل: Obstruction and hindrance; particularly preventing someone from exercising a right they seek to enjoy.

عَضِلَ: In some uses, being muscular or having prominent muscles.

عَضَلَة: A thick, cohesive portion of bodily flesh.

عُضَال: Describing a very severe illness or difficulty.

أَعْضَلَ: A matter becoming difficult, complex, or overwhelming; in some constructions, confronting someone with a difficult problem.

مُعْضِل: Difficult and complex; not readily resolved.

عَضَّلَ: To constrain, obstruct, or place under pressure; also used of obstruction and difficult childbirth.

تَعْضِيل: The Form II verbal noun in uses concerning constraint and obstruction.

Severe illness and incurability

دَاءٌ عُضَالٌ describes a severe illness whose treatment is difficult or beyond physicians' ability. Some lexical explanations expressly call it incurable.

These explanations should be preserved, but the phrase alone does not establish a medical judgment that treatment is impossible forever under every condition.

An illness may be extremely difficult to treat at a particular time or with particular resources, while a treatment becomes available later.

The sense in each instance depends on its description and context. “Severe and difficult to treat” is a cautious general equivalent; “incurable” should be used where that stronger sense is intended.

The Quranic form

The Quran uses only تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ from this root, once in Al-Baqarah 2:232 and once in Al-Nisa 4:19.

Both belong to Form I عَضَلَ يَعْضُلُ, not Form II.

Both are second-person plural imperfect verbs: و marks the subject and هُنَّ is the feminine plural object suffix. In Al-Baqarah 2:232, the verb is jussive after prohibitive لَا. In Al-Nisa 4:19, both this analysis and a subjunctive analysis coordinated with أَنْ تَرِثُوا have been proposed; either way, the conduct is prohibited.

Both verses thus address people in a position to prevent women from attaining a desired end or right.

The obstruction differs between the verses:

Al-Baqarah 2:232 concerns preventing women from marrying.

Al-Nisa 4:19 concerns constraining women to recover property.

The Quranic use of عَضَلَ is therefore not limited to one particular behavior, such as preventing a marriage contract.

4. فَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ: prohibiting obstruction of marriage

The first occurrence in Quranic order is in Surah Al-Baqarah:

وَإِذَا طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَبَلَغْنَ أَجَلَهُنَّ فَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ أَنْ يَنْكِحْنَ أَزْوَاجَهُنَّ إِذَا تَرَاضَوْا بَيْنَهُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۗ ذَٰلِكَ يُوعَظُ بِهِ مَنْ كَانَ مِنْكُمْ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ ۗ ذَٰلِكُمْ أَزْكَىٰ لَكُمْ وَأَطْهَرُ ۗ وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنْتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

wa-idhā ṭallaqtumu al-nisāʾa fa-balaghna ajalahunna fa-lā taʿḍulūhunna an yankiḥna azwājahunna idhā tarāḍaw baynahum bi-al-maʿrūfi dhālika yūʿaẓu bihi man kāna minkum yuʾminu bi-Allāhi wa-al-yawmi al-ākhiri dhālikum azkā lakum wa-aṭharu wa-Allāhu yaʿlamu wa-antum lā taʿlamūn

Al-Baqarah 2:232

Al-Baqarah 2:232

“When you divorce women and they reach the end of their term, do not prevent them from marrying their husbands when they mutually agree in a proper manner. This admonishes whoever among you believes in The God and the Last Day. That is more wholesome and purer for you; The God knows, while you do not know.”

تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ here concerns obstructing women's marriage.

The issue is not merely verbal disagreement, but preventing a step the parties wish to take by mutual consent after the prescribed period.

أَنْ يَنْكِحْنَ أَزْوَاجَهُنَّ specifies what others must not prevent: the women's marrying.

Within the divorce passage, women have separated from their husbands and remarriage is contemplated after the prescribed period ends.

The woman and former husband may wish to remarry after disagreement and separation.

The verse prohibits obstructing that marriage when mutual consent within what is recognized as right is present.

Who can obstruct marriage?

The verse begins with the plural address طَلَّقْتُمُ and continues with the plural فَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ.

Linguistically, the verb addresses those able or positioned to obstruct women's marriage.

Such people may include relatives, family guardians, former husbands, or others who interfere through influence or authority.

Early interpretation particularly connects the verse with a guardian preventing a woman's remarriage. This fits the verse, but تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ itself does not mean specifically “fathers must not prevent” or “brothers must not prevent.”

The prohibition focuses on obstruction; situation and context identify the obstructing person.

Imagine a divorced woman whose prescribed period has ended. She and her former husband resolve their differences and decide to remarry.

Her father or brother, however, says he will not allow it because of hostility toward the man's family.

The third party places his own wish above the couple's decision.

This clearly illustrates the obstruction under discussion.

It need not be confined to fathers or brothers. A former husband might obstruct a woman's subsequent marriage through threats, rumors, or abuse of social influence.

Both involve using power to restrain another person.

The role of إِذَا تَرَاضَوْا بَيْنَهُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

The verse states:

إِذَا تَرَاضَوْا بَيْنَهُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

idhā tarāḍaw baynahum bi-al-maʿrūf

Al-Baqarah 2:232

This phrase specifies mutual consent and the framework of recognized right conduct.

تَرَاضَوْا is Form VI of the family of رِضَا and here expresses mutual consent.

The man's consent alone, or that of the two families, is not sufficient to describe this mutual agreement.

The woman and man wishing to marry must themselves consent to the union.

بِالْمَعْرُوفِ places that consent within proper conduct and legitimate rights.

The qualification prevents treating every relationship as this case merely because one party claims consent, without regard to other rules and rights.

Conversely, recognized right conduct should not be reduced to relatives' preferences and prejudices.

A community may, for example, consider remarriage to a former husband unacceptable even when both spouses wish to repair their relationship.

That custom cannot by itself nullify the Quran's clear instruction.

A family may also have genuine concerns about a woman's safety or rights. Evidence-based concern, goodwill, and advice differ from imposing a decision or arbitrarily obstructing her choice.

The prohibition of عضل does not ban all advice or sincere warning; it targets wrongful obstruction.

Connection with divorce limits

Al-Baqarah 2:230 sets a restriction on immediate reunion after the third divorce.

Al-Baqarah 2:232 should therefore not be understood to allow every formerly married couple to remarry in all circumstances regardless of prescribed limits.

It rejects human obstruction where remarriage is possible within divine limits and mutual consent.

Wrongful human prevention must thus be distinguished from a limit established by the Quran itself.

This distinction comes from the passage and its provisions, not from a lexical definition that independently divides every form of prevention into lawful and unlawful categories.

5. وَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ: pressuring women to recover property

The second Quranic occurrence is in Surah Al-Nisa:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا يَحِلُّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَرِثُوا النِّسَاءَ كَرْهًا ۖ وَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ لِتَذْهَبُوا بِبَعْضِ مَا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ إِلَّا أَنْ يَأْتِينَ بِفَاحِشَةٍ مُبَيِّنَةٍ ۚ وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۚ فَإِنْ كَرِهْتُمُوهُنَّ فَعَسَىٰ أَنْ تَكْرَهُوا شَيْئًا وَيَجْعَلَ اللَّهُ فِيهِ خَيْرًا كَثِيرًا

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā yaḥillu lakum an tarithū al-nisāʾa karhan wa-lā taʿḍulūhunna li-tadhhabū bi-baʿḍi mā ātaytumūhunna illā an yaʾtīna bi-fāḥishatin mubayyinatin wa-ʿāshirūhunna bi-al-maʿrūfi fa-in karihtumūhunna fa-ʿasā an takrahū shayʾan wa-yajʿala Allāhu fīhi khayran kathīrā

Al-Nisāʾ 4:19

Al-Nisa 4:19

“You who believe, it is not lawful for you to inherit women against their will. Do not constrain them to take back part of what you have given them, unless they commit a manifest indecency. Live with them honorably. If you dislike them, you may dislike something in which The God places much good.”

تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ occurs again, but the matter differs from Al-Baqarah 2:232.

Al-Baqarah concerned obstructing marriage.

Al-Nisa concerns constraining women for a financial objective.

That objective is expressed by:

لِتَذْهَبُوا بِبَعْضِ مَا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ

li-tadhhabū bi-baʿḍi mā ātaytumūhunna

Al-Nisāʾ 4:19

“To take back part of what you have given them.”

لِـ in لِتَذْهَبُوا marks the purpose of the obstruction.

Someone may pressure a woman to recover part of the property previously given to her.

This is more than an ordinary financial disagreement: it uses constraint to induce someone to relinquish a right.

Obstruction without imprisonment

عضل does not necessarily mean confining someone in an enclosed place.

A woman may be physically free while her living conditions become so oppressive that she surrenders financial rights to escape.

For example, a man may no longer want to remain with his wife, yet instead of ending the relationship properly, repeatedly humiliate her, neglect his responsibilities, or make continued life together intolerable.

He may then say: “If you want to be released from this situation, return your marriage payment or part of your property to me.”

She may appear to have a choice, but imposed pressure limits her freedom to decide.

This fits the semantic field of عضل: creating constraint makes another person's path to a decent life or release from a relationship difficult.

“Do not constrain them” therefore conveys Al-Nisa 4:19 better than the much narrower “do not imprison them.”

Connection with لَا يَحِلُّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَرِثُوا النِّسَاءَ كَرْهًا

The opening prohibits inheriting women against their will.

It indicates that women should not be transferred like property or have control over their lives placed in heirs' hands.

The prohibition of عضل follows.

Both instructions address abuse of position and power in relations with women.

In one situation, someone seeks control over a woman as though she were inherited property.

In the other, he pressures her to surrender financial rights.

Both conflict with respect for her rights and choices, but they are not identical acts.

تَرِثُوا belongs to و ر ث and concerns inheritance.

تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ belongs to ع ض ل and concerns obstruction and constraint.

Each retains its separate semantic contribution.

The role of إِلَّا أَنْ يَأْتِينَ بِفَاحِشَةٍ مُبَيِّنَةٍ

The prohibition is followed by:

إِلَّا أَنْ يَأْتِينَ بِفَاحِشَةٍ مُبَيِّنَةٍ

illā an yaʾtīna bi-fāḥishatin mubayyinah

Al-Nisāʾ 4:19

“Unless they commit a manifest indecency.”

This introduces an exception that cannot be removed from the verse.

Its scope should not be expanded without evidence. Early interpretations differ over فَاحِشَة here, including sexual misconduct and serious marital misconduct; membership in this root alone cannot resolve the dispute.

فَاحِشَة does not simply name every disliked behavior or family disagreement. مُبَيِّنَة emphasizes its manifest character.

A man's dissatisfaction, differences of taste, diminished affection, or an unproven accusation cannot simply replace فَاحِشَةٍ مُبَيِّنَةٍ.

Nor does the exception by itself grant unrestricted permission for violence, humiliation, or arbitrary seizure of property. Its scope and application must be considered alongside Quranic instructions on justice, financial rights, and honorable marital conduct.

Al-Nisa 4:20 and Al-Nisa 4:21 likewise prohibit wrongful recovery of a woman's property at separation.

An allegation of offensive conduct cannot simply set all these principles aside.

The role of وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

Following the prohibition, The God says:

وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

wa-ʿāshirūhunna bi-al-maʿrūf

Al-Nisāʾ 4:19

“Live with them honorably.”

This sets proper conduct against obstruction and pressure.

Marriage may involve disagreement, dissatisfaction, or hardship. These do not by themselves authorize violating the other person's rights.

The verse continues:

فَإِنْ كَرِهْتُمُوهُنَّ

fa-in karihtumūhunna

Al-Nisāʾ 4:19

“If you dislike them.”

Even dislike of a spouse must not automatically become oppression, pressure, or deprivation of rights.

Lexically, عضل denotes obstruction and constraint; the context shows how such behavior can become a means of imposing financial demands in family relationships.

6. Comparing the Quranic uses

Al-Baqarah 2:232 and Al-Nisa 4:19 use one verb for two different forms of obstruction.

In Al-Baqarah, a woman wishes to marry by mutual consent and in a proper manner after the prescribed period, but others may obstruct her.

In Al-Nisa, a woman is pressured to return some property given to her.

The first concerns restricting marital choice.

The second concerns using pressure to obtain property.

Both place an obstacle before a woman and limit her ability to attain a right or legitimate objective.

The form of the obstacle need not be identical.

In Al-Baqarah, it may involve relatives' opposition, abuse of family authority, or interference in a marriage decision.

In Al-Nisa, it may involve mistreatment, neglected duties, imposed uncertainty, or conditions that compel a woman to surrender property for release.

عضل is therefore not restricted to one outward form of obstruction.

A person can directly block another's path or create conditions that practically prevent the exercise of choice and rights.

For example, an official who unjustifiably delays someone's lawful entitlement to obtain a bribe behaves in a conceptually similar way.

A family may also use threats and social pressure to frustrate a member's legitimate decision.

These examples illustrate conceptual extension in human relationships; they are not all direct, literal instances of the two verses concerning women.

Both verses also concern women's rights and freedom of decision.

The Quran calls on those able to intervene in their lives not to turn that power into pressure.

Family authority, social standing, and economic power do not by themselves authorize preventing someone from exercising a right.

7. Is every kind of prevention عضل?

Lexically, عضل concerns prevention, but not every obstacle in life is necessarily the condemned conduct prohibited in these two verses.

For example, preventing an invalid transaction that takes another's property without consent differs from unjustly obstructing a person's rights.

Parents may know of a real danger in a relationship and warn their child. Truthful information, evidence, and sincere advice are not inherently coercive obstruction.

Blocking another's legitimate decision without clear grounds, merely from hostility, prejudice, or personal preference, is closer to the conduct under discussion.

The moral value of prevention is not determined by the root alone.

In Al-Baqarah 2:232, إِذَا تَرَاضَوْا بَيْنَهُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ specifies the situation.

In Al-Nisa 4:19, لِتَذْهَبُوا بِبَعْضِ مَا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ specifies the financial purpose of the pressure.

These qualifications identify particular conduct. The root alone should not become an unlimited ruling about every form of prevention.

Obstruction and free choice

Preventing others' abuse does not mean compelling a woman to marry or remain married.

Al-Baqarah 2:232 prohibits others from obstructing mutual consent; it does not require a woman to marry her former husband.

Her own unwillingness to marry is a different matter.

Likewise, Al-Nisa 4:19 does not require a woman to remain married under every circumstance simply because it prohibits financial coercion.

We must distinguish free choice, legitimate advice and assessment, and imposing a preference through obstruction or pressure.

The third is what the two Quranic uses condemn.

8. Comparison with neighboring roots

ع ض ل and م ن ع

م ن ع concerns preventing, restraining, or withholding something.

ع ض ل also involves prevention in human relationships, but emphasizes making the path difficult, creating difficulty, and imposing constraint.

Someone may openly refuse another's request; منع can express that act.

Using power and influence to make access to a right difficult more clearly illustrates عضل.

This distinction is not absolute. عضل can directly mean prevention, and need not always involve a complicated, prolonged scheme.

ع ض ل and ح ب س

حبس concerns holding and preventing movement or departure.

عضل may also involve restraint, but the Quranic uses are not limited to physically imprisoning women.

A person may obstruct a woman's marriage without confining her in a house.

A woman may likewise be physically free while financial and family pressure restrict her choices.

Confinement thus describes some possible instances of عضل, not its entire meaning.

ع ض ل and ض ي ق

ض ي ق concerns narrowness and restricted space or possibilities.

عضل can describe difficulty in accomplishing something or an obstructed path.

These semantic fields are related.

Al-Talaq 65:6 states:

وَلَا تُضَارُّوهُنَّ لِتُضَيِّقُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ

wa-lā tuḍārrūhunna li-tuḍayyiqū ʿalayhinna

Al-Ṭalāq 65:6

“Do not harm them in order to constrain them.”

This is conceptually close to Al-Nisa 4:19: both concern pressure on women and abuse of their situation.

تُضَيِّقُوا belongs to ض ي ق, while تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ belongs to ع ض ل.

One emphasizes narrowing conditions, the other obstructing or constraining someone. A single behavior may exhibit both.

ع ض ل and ض ر ر

ضرر concerns harm or injury to a person or thing.

عضل in the two verses is a form of obstruction or pressure that can cause harm.

A man constraining his wife to recover her property may both cause psychological harm and violate her financial rights.

عضل describes the conduct; harm may be its consequence.

Not every harm is عضل. A natural event can injure someone without another person deliberately erecting an obstacle.

ع ض ل and ع ن ت

ع ن ت concerns hardship, suffering, and being caught in difficulty.

عضل also involves difficulty, but its Quranic uses concern obstructing women and restricting their choices.

عنت can describe the painful condition endured, whereas عضل in these verses focuses on the act of obstruction or constraint.

They may coincide in an event without becoming interchangeable.

ع ض ل and ع ض د

ع ض د concerns the upper arm and figuratively a helper or support.

ع ض ل concerns difficulty, constriction, and obstruction.

Despite sharing two initial consonants, these families are distinct.

عَضُد in Al-Qasas 28:35 and Al-Kahf 18:51 belongs to ع ض د and is not directly the verb تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ.

ع ض ل and ع ض ض

ع ض ض concerns biting and pressing something with the teeth.

Its forms in Al Imran 3:119 and Al-Furqan 25:27 concern biting fingertips and hands.

ع ض ل instead describes obstructing and constraining women in the Quran.

Similar sounds or a shared image of pressure do not make the roots identical.

ع ض ل and ع ض و

عِضِينَ in Al-Hijr 15:91 is commonly analyzed in connection with ع ض و, although its derivation has also been disputed.

Shared initial consonants do not make it a form of ع ض ل.

Actual root, morphological structure, and contextual use distinguish these families.

9. Translation proposals for Forqan

In both Quranic uses, تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ concerns obstructing and constraining.

The translation must nevertheless suit the subject of each verse.

تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ — Al-Baqarah 2:232

Word: تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ

Root: ع ض ل

Word type: Second-person plural imperfect, Form I, with a feminine plural object suffix, in a prohibitive construction.

Brief root meaning: Making a matter difficult and entangled; preventing and obstructing.

Meaning here: Preventing women from marrying.

Suggested translation:

فَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ أَنْ يَنْكِحْنَ أَزْوَاجَهُنَّ

fa-lā taʿḍulūhunna an yankiḥna azwājahunna

Al-Baqarah 2:232

“Do not prevent them from marrying their husbands.”

“Do not prevent” renders the verb in its prohibitive construction.

أَنْ يَنْكِحْنَ أَزْوَاجَهُنَّ identifies the action being obstructed.

“Do not confine” or “do not imprison” would unnecessarily narrow the verse's scope.

تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ — Al-Nisa 4:19

Word: تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ

Root: ع ض ل

Word type: Second-person plural imperfect, Form I, with a feminine plural object suffix, in a context prohibiting the conduct; both jussive and subjunctive analyses are recorded.

Brief root meaning: Obstructing, hindering, and constraining.

Meaning here: Pressuring and constraining women.

Suggested translation:

وَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ لِتَذْهَبُوا بِبَعْضِ مَا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ

wa-lā taʿḍulūhunna li-tadhhabū bi-baʿḍi mā ātaytumūhunna

Al-Nisāʾ 4:19

“Do not constrain them to take back part of what you have given them.”

This distinguishes the obstruction from its financial purpose.

“Do not constrain” expresses the verb.

“To take back part of what you have given them” comes from the separate phrase لِتَذْهَبُوا بِبَعْضِ مَا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ.

تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ alone does not mean taking a woman's marriage payment or property. Obtaining property is the objective specified for the conduct here.

Other equivalents in the family

For non-Quranic uses, consider:

أَمْرٌ مُعْضِلٌ: A difficult, complex matter.

دَاءٌ عُضَالٌ: A severe illness difficult to treat.

أَعْضَلَ الْأَمْرُ: The matter became difficult and entangled.

عَضَّلَتِ الْمَرْأَةُ بِوَلَدِهَا: The woman experienced obstructed childbirth.

عَضَلَ الْمَرْأَةَ عَنِ التَّزَوُّجِ: He prevented the woman from marrying.

عَضَلَة: Muscle; a thick, cohesive portion of bodily flesh.

These equivalents show that sharing a root does not make all derivatives identical.

Conclusion

ع ض ل concerns difficulty, constriction, a matter becoming hard to accomplish, and obstruction.

Bodily uses can describe difficulty emerging through a passage, as in obstructed childbirth.

أَمْرٌ مُعْضِلٌ describes a difficult problem; دَاءٌ عُضَالٌ describes a severe illness difficult to treat.

In human relationships, the root can describe obstruction and constraint, particularly using power or authority to prevent another from exercising a right or pursuing a legitimate objective.

The Quran uses only تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ from this root, in two verses.

Al-Baqarah 2:232 prohibits obstructing women's remarriage when mutual consent and recognized right conduct are present.

Al-Nisa 4:19 prohibits constraining women to recover part of the property given to them, while expressly stating an exception whose limits require separate examination.

Obstruction may interfere with a person's choices or create hardship to induce the surrender of rights.

The root concerns a matter becoming difficult and entangled, or making another's path difficult; its Quranic uses concern obstructing and constraining women.

Context identifies the obstacle, the person obstructed, and the objective. One verse concerns marital choice, the other prevention of financial abuse.

Forqan should preserve obstruction and constraint without limiting the root to physical confinement, obstruction of marriage alone, or taking property.
