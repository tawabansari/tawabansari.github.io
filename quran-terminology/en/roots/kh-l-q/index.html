Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root KH L Q

Creating, bringing into being, measuring, shaping, forming, and bringing something forth in a specific form, measure, and order

Important Forms from This Root

خَلَقَ / يَخْلُقُ (khalaqa / yakhluqu)

khalaqa / yakhluqu; created, brought forth, shaped, or brought into a measured form.

خَلْق (khalq)

khalq; creation, created structure, formed being, or outward constitution.

خَالِق / خَلَّاق (khāliq / khallāq)

khāliq; creator, shaper, bringer forth. Khallāq; intensely and continuously creating.

مَخْلُوق (makhlūq)

makhlūq; created being, dependent on creation.

خُلُق (khuluq)

khuluq; character, inward form, moral constitution.

Method Note

The root KH L Q is often translated simply as “to create.” In many verses that is correct. But if “create” is always understood as “to bring out of absolute nothing,” part of the Qur’anic use becomes distorted. The Qur’an uses KH L Q for The God, and in some contexts also for other than The God. Therefore the lexical meaning cannot be limited to creation from nothing.

In the Qur’an, KH L Q is tied to measure, form, structure, proportion, and bringing something into a defined shape. When The God is Creator, He is the final origin of existence, matter, law, possibility, life, and measure. But when a human being or another created agent “creates,” that creation is lower and dependent: shaping, making, forming, or bringing something forth from materials and possibilities that the agent does not ultimately own.

For this reason, فَتَبَارَكَ اللَّهُ أَحْسَنُ الْخَالِقِينَ (fatabāraka allāhu aḥsanu al-khāliqīn) is important. The Qur’an does not say that the word creator can never be used in any sense for anyone other than The God. It identifies The God as the best of creators. Divine transcendence here is preserved not by erasing all other linguistic uses, but by showing the absolute superiority of The God’s creating.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of KH L Q is bringing something forth in a specific measure, form, and structure. Khalq means that a thing is not left formless and unrelated; it receives form, limit, measure, and function. In this sense, KH L Q is close to Q D R, because Qur’anic creation is rarely understood apart from measured determination.

When the Qur’an says The God created, it does not merely say that something appeared. It says that the thing was brought forth with measure, composition, order, and suitable placement. This is why وَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ فَقَدَّرَهُ تَقْدِيرًا (wakhalaqa kulla shayʾin faqaddarahu taqdīrā) immediately joins creation to measured determination 25:2. In the Qur’an, khalq and taqdīr are deeply connected.

Human creating must be distinguished from divine creating. If a human being creates something, he works within materials, laws, and possibilities already placed in the world. The God, as Creator, is not merely shaping pre-owned matter. Matter, law, possibility of form, life, and measure belong to His ownership and command.

Material Image

The material image is someone shaping a material according to a known measure and design: forming something from clay, cutting and sewing leather, or making a tool from raw material with a precise measure. The act is not merely the presence of matter. It is the giving of form, limit, relation, and function.

This image is clear in the verse about Jesus: أَنِّي أَخْلُقُ لَكُم مِّنَ الطِّينِ كَهَيْئَةِ الطَّيْرِ (annī akhluqu lakum mina al-ṭīni kahayʾati al-ṭayr) 3:49. Jesus forms from clay something like the shape of a bird. This is not independent creation from nothing; it is forming existing material, by The God’s permission.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, KH L Q is connected not only with creating, but also with measuring and cutting according to measure. One may say خَلَقَ الْأَدِيمَ (khalaqa al-adīm), meaning he measured and cut the leather in order to make something from it. This material image matters because it shows that KH L Q has long been tied to measure, pattern, and shaping.

From here, خَلْق (khalq) comes near outward structure, while خُلُق (khuluq) comes near inward moral structure. Both relate to being formed. One concerns the outer form or created constitution; the other concerns the inner form of behavior and character.

Qur’anic Usage

The God as Creator of the Heavens and the Earth

In many verses, The God is presented as the Creator of the heavens and the earth, such as خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بِالْحَقِّ (khalaqa al-samāwāti wal-arḍa bil-ḥaqq) 64:3. This is independent divine creation. The God is not merely shaping matter outside His ownership. He owns the root of existence, matter, law, time, measure, and possibility.

The phrase بِالْحَقِّ (bil-ḥaqq) is also important. Divine creation is upon truth: not vain, not purposeless, and not without order. The God’s creating is joined to truth, measure, and wisdom. This is the highest level of KH L Q.

The Creation of the Human Being Through Material Stages

In وَلَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا الْإِنسَانَ مِن سُلَالَةٍ مِّن طِينٍ (walaqad khalaqnā al-insāna min sulālatin min ṭīn) and the following verses of Surat al-Muʾminūn, human creation is presented in stages: extract of clay, drop, clinging form, chewed-like form, bones, the clothing of bones with flesh, and then another creation 23:12 to 23:14.

These verses are central for understanding the root. The creation of the human being is staged, measured, and structured. The God brings the human being forth through matter, measure, form, and life. The sequence ends with: فَتَبَارَكَ اللَّهُ أَحْسَنُ الْخَالِقِينَ (fatabāraka allāhu aḥsanu al-khāliqīn).

Aḥsan al-Khāliqīn and Divine Transcendence

The phrase أَحْسَنُ الْخَالِقِينَ (aḥsanu al-khāliqīn), the best of creators, shows that the word khāliq can linguistically be used beyond The God, while The God remains the best and highest Creator 23:14. If the Qur’an meant that no one other than The God can ever be called a creator in any sense, this phrase would be difficult.

This does not mean associating partners with The God. The real distinction is independence and level. The God’s creating is independent, foundational, life-giving, and owner of all causes. Human creating is dependent, limited, instrumental, and takes place inside materials and laws The God has already brought into being.

Jesus Creating from Clay by The God’s Permission

In أَنِّي أَخْلُقُ لَكُم مِّنَ الطِّينِ كَهَيْئَةِ الطَّيْرِ فَأَنفُخُ فِيهِ فَيَكُونُ طَيْرًا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (annī akhluqu lakum mina al-ṭīni kahayʾati al-ṭayri fa-anfukhu fīhi fayakūnu ṭayran bi-idhni allāh), Jesus says that he forms for them from clay something like the shape of a bird, then breathes into it, and it becomes a bird by The God’s permission 3:49.

This verse clearly shows that khalq can be used for other than The God, but the phrase بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (bi-idhni allāh) preserves tawḥīd. Jesus shapes from clay, but life and realized effect occur by The God’s permission. At Jesus’ level, khalq is dependent shaping; at The God’s level, creation includes possibility, life, and final realization.

The Inability of False Objects of Worship to Create

The Qur’an says of false objects of worship: أَفَمَن يَخْلُقُ كَمَن لَّا يَخْلُقُ (afaman yakhluqu kaman lā yakhluq); is the one who creates like one who does not create? 16:17. Creation becomes an important measure distinguishing The God from powerless objects of worship.

Another verse says: لَا يَخْلُقُونَ شَيْئًا وَهُمْ يُخْلَقُونَ (lā yakhluqūna shayʾan wahum yukhlaqūn); they create nothing, while they themselves are created 16:20. The relation of dependence is clear: a created being that has no independent power to bring forth cannot be lord or deity.

Khalq and Its Relation to Jaʿl

KH L Q is close to J AYN L, but they are not identical. Khalq focuses more on bringing forth structure and form. Jaʿl focuses more on placing, assigning a role, or setting something within a specific state or function. For example, خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ (khalaqa al-samāwāti wal-arḍ) concerns the bringing forth of structure, while جَعَلَ الظُّلُمَاتِ وَالنُّورَ (jaʿala al-ẓulumāti wal-nūr) may point to placing darknesses and light within the order of the world.

The two roots can come near each other because creation and role-assignment are not completely separate. But in Forqan translation, the distinction should be preserved: khalq is forming and bringing structure into being; jaʿl is placing in a role, station, or condition.

Khalq and Taswiyah

In الَّذِي خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ (alladhī khalaqa fasawwā), creation is joined with taswiyah 87:2. The God created, then proportioned and set in balance. This pairing shows that divine creation is not compatible with disorder or defective formation.

Taswiyah is a layer of completing proportion. Khalq is the bringing forth of structure; taswiyah is straightening, harmonizing, and proportioning it. The difference should appear in translation.

Khalq and Taqdīr

In وَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ فَقَدَّرَهُ تَقْدِيرًا (wakhalaqa kulla shayʾin faqaddarahu taqdīrā), creation is immediately joined to measured determination 25:2. The God created every thing and set for it a measure. This verse shows that Qur’anic creation is bound to measure.

So when we say that The God creates, we should not see only the moment of emergence. We must also see the measure, path, capacity, and relation of that created thing within the whole order. Khalq without qadr is incomplete in Qur’anic logic.

Khalq and Khuluq

In وَإِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍ (wa-innaka laʿalā khuluqin ʿaẓīm), khuluq refers to the Messenger’s moral constitution and character 68:4. Though this word is not identical to khalq as creation, the root relationship shows that ethics also has a kind of inner form and structure.

Khuluq is not merely passing behavior. It is the stable inward shape of a person. As khalq is near outward form and created structure, khuluq is near inward form and behavioral structure.

Difference from Nearby Words

Faṭr is opening, splitting, and initiating from the beginning. Its focus is the first opening. Badʿ is bringing forth without a previous example. Khalq focuses more on forming, measure, structure, and proportion of parts, even where some material already exists.

Jaʿl is placing in a role, station, or condition. Ṣawr is closer to giving visible form. Sawwā points to straightening, proportioning, and setting in balance. Baraʾa is bringing forth and separating a being into its own sound existence. Anshaʾa is closer to initiating and developing in stages.

KH L Q shares a field with all of these, but its core is measured forming and bringing into structured existence.

Translation Method

“Create” is suitable in many verses, but the reader should not always hear it as creation from nothing. In some verses, “bring forth,” “form,” “shape,” “create in measured form,” or “bring into structure” may be more accurate.

For verses about The God, “create” is usually right because The God is the final origin of creation. But in the verse about Jesus, “I create” needs explanation: he forms from clay, and the effect of life occurs by The God’s permission. In أَحْسَنُ الْخَالِقِينَ (aḥsanu al-khāliqīn), the translation should show that The God’s creating is superior to every dependent and limited kind of making or forming.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بِالْحَقِّ (khalaqa al-samāwāti wal-arḍa bil-ḥaqq): He created the heavens and the earth with truth; He brought the heavens and the earth forth upon truth.

وَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ فَقَدَّرَهُ تَقْدِيرًا (wakhalaqa kulla shayʾin faqaddarahu taqdīrā): He created every thing and set for it a precise measure; He brought every thing forth and made it measured.

فَتَبَارَكَ اللَّهُ أَحْسَنُ الْخَالِقِينَ (fatabāraka allāhu aḥsanu al-khāliqīn): blessed is The God, the best of creators; the highest of all shapers and bringers forth.

أَخْلُقُ لَكُم مِّنَ الطِّينِ كَهَيْئَةِ الطَّيْرِ (akhluqu lakum mina al-ṭīni kahayʾati al-ṭayr): I form for you from clay something like the shape of a bird; I shape for you from clay a form like a bird.

أَفَمَن يَخْلُقُ كَمَن لَّا يَخْلُقُ (afaman yakhluqu kaman lā yakhluq): is the one who creates like one who does not create? is the bringer forth like one unable to bring forth?

لَا يَخْلُقُونَ شَيْئًا وَهُمْ يُخْلَقُونَ (lā yakhluqūna shayʾan wahum yukhlaqūn): they create nothing, while they themselves are created; they bring nothing forth and are themselves created.

الَّذِي خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ (alladhī khalaqa fasawwā): the One who created and proportioned; the One who brought forth and set in balance.

Summary

The root KH L Q in the Qur’an does not only mean creation from nothing. Its core is bringing forth in a defined form, measure, and structure. The God is Creator because He is the final origin of existence, matter, law, life, and measure. If a human being or another agent “creates” at some level, that act is dependent: shaping, making, or combining within a world whose materials, laws, and possibilities come from The God.

In Forqan’s reading, the main difference between divine creating and human creating is independence, life-giving, ownership of all causes, and perfect measure. The God is أَحْسَنُ الْخَالِقِينَ (aḥsanu al-khāliqīn), not because the word khalq can never be used for anyone else, but because every creation besides His is limited and dependent, while His creating is the highest, most complete, and the origin of all forms and measures.
