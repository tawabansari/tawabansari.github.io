Gathering, separating, and bringing the resulting substance or outcome to light

The Arabic root ح ص ل concerns gathering, separating things, and identifying what remains afterward. Words from this family can describe separating grain from chaff, extracting valuable metal from ore, or determining the result of a calculation.

حاصل names what has been obtained or remains after other things are removed; تحصيل can express separating, gathering, and obtaining it; and حصيلة concerns the outcome or remainder resulting from that process.

The root occurs only once in the Qur’an: وَحُصِّلَ in Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10, describing a time when what is in people’s breasts is made clear. Understanding it requires examining how taḥṣīl relates to gathering, separating, extracting, and disclosure in Arabic.

Qur’anic form: حُصِّلَ, a form II passive past verb, accompanied by the conjunction وَ in وَحُصِّلَ.

حَصَلَ, حَاصِل, تَحْصِيل, حَصِيلَة, حُصَالَة, مَحْصُول, مُحَصِّل, and تَحَصَّلَ are recorded in Arabic, but none of these forms occurs in the Qur’an.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: the sole occurrence, ḥuṣṣila in Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10

1. The semantic core and differences between derivatives

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris gives gathering something as the root’s basis. He also connects a bird’s crop with it because food gathers there.

Kitāb al-ʿAyn explains حَصَلَ الشَّيْءُ as something remaining and becoming established while other things disappear or are removed. It explains taḥṣīl as distinguishing what has resulted.

These explanations show two related but distinct aspects.

Something is obtained from a collection, gathered, or left behind; and it becomes identifiable by being separated from other elements.

For example, many figures and operations may contribute to a calculation, but its ḥāṣil is the final result rather than all the calculations themselves.

Separating grain from chaff likewise distinguishes the original mixture, the separating process, and what is obtained afterward.

Gathering and separating can thus lead to a common outcome: something is separated from other elements and gathered distinctly. This does not mean every use of the root simultaneously conveys all these stages.

Kitāb al-ʿAyn: outcome, remaining, and distinction

Ḥaṣala: emerging and remaining

In early usage, حَصَلَ الشَّيْءُ describes something left or established after other elements have been removed.

Ḥāṣil names what has been obtained or remains; maḥṣūl also denotes an outcome or result in lexical usage.

حَاصِلُ الْكَلَامِ means the gist or outcome of speech: the meaning derived from it rather than every sentence in the discourse.

In extended usage, حَصَلَ لَهُ الشَّيْءُ can mean that something arose for someone or came into their possession. The modern general meaning “happened” should not be imposed on all early or Qur’anic uses without contextual evidence.

Ḥaṣṣala: separating and obtaining

حَصَّلَ is form II. Lexically, it can express separating something from accompanying elements, gathering it, and identifying the result.

حَصَّلَ الذَّهَبَ مِنْ حَجَرِ الْمَعْدِنِ means extracting gold from ore. The gold already exists in the material, but extraction distinguishes it from accompanying elements.

Taḥṣīl here is neither creating gold nor merely exposing its surface. What was already within the mixture is separated and obtained.

تَحْصِيلِ الْكَلَامِ likewise concerns reducing speech to its outcome or gist. The word can therefore concern meaning and thought as well as physical objects.

Taḥaṣṣala: gathering and becoming established

تَحَصَّلَ الشَّيْءُ describes something gathering and becoming established, emphasizing the resulting condition rather than necessarily identifying its producer.

Its structure differs from حَصَّلَ: the latter can name an agent gathering or separating something, while تَحَصَّلَ emphasizes gathering or the emergence of a result.

Ḥaṣīlah and maḥṣūl: outcome and remainder

حَصِيلَة names what has resulted or remains after a process; its plural is حَصَائِل.

Early dictionaries also record مَحْصُول for an outcome or remainder. Agricultural produce is a familiar modern use, but not the root’s original and exclusive meaning.

حُصَالَة has another use: what remains after cleaning and separating grain, including fragments, impurities, and low-value material removed from it.

The remainder is not necessarily the most valuable component. The root does not inherently mean pure and valuable. What is retained or discarded depends on the process and context.

2. Concrete uses and early linguistic witnesses

Extracting metal from ore

A concrete early use of taḥṣīl is extracting gold or silver from ore and accompanying earth.

The valuable material already exists in the stone but is obtained by separation. This explains the connection with extraction, distinction, and obtaining a result.

Ibn Fāris reports that some lexicographers considered metal extraction the basis of taḥṣīl. He finds that compatible with gathering, but transmits it as their view. It does not establish historically that metal extraction was the root’s sole earliest meaning.

Ibn Fāris, Maqāyīs al-Lughah: gathering and the reported metal-extraction explanation

A poetic witness also quoted by Sībawayh contains مُحَصِّلَة, which lexicographers explain as a woman separating valuable material from ore-bearing earth.

It supports a use involving extraction and separation. The line’s precise date and some attribution details nevertheless remain insufficiently secure for presenting it unconditionally as a pre-Qur’anic witness.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ and Lisān al-ʿArab: the muḥaṣṣilah witness and Sībawayh’s transmission

Grain and chaff: separating the yield from impurities

Dictionaries also illustrate separating wheat from chaff.

The desired substance is mixed with other materials. Cleaning and separation distinguish usable grain from chaff and accompanying fragments.

Within this usage, حُصَالَة names what remains after grain is cleaned.

This helps explain taḥṣīl as more than indiscriminately collecting objects. In some uses, distinguishing the yield proceeds through separating components.

Al-Rāghib, Al-Mufradāt: separating grain from chaff and metal from ore

Ḥawṣalah: where food gathers

حَوْصَلَة names a part of a bird’s digestive system where food gathers and is temporarily stored: the crop.

Dictionaries discuss it within the family ح ص ل; Ibn Fāris also uses it to explain gathering as a semantic basis.

The salient feature is collecting and retaining material in a particular place, rather than separating pure from impure material.

A bird’s crop illustrates gathering, but its name alone does not establish that every meaning historically developed from that organ’s name.

Modern Persian ḥawṣalah can also mean patience, psychological capacity, or willingness to do something. These Persian meanings should not enter the translation of Qur’anic حُصِّلَ.

Uses concerning dates and plants

Early dictionaries record حَصَل for early stages of date fruit before it fully develops.

أَحْصَلَ النَّخْلُ is also used of a palm whose fruit has appeared at such a stage.

This concerns fruit forming and becoming apparent. Its precise historical relationship to the other branches requires caution; shared root letters do not prove one account of how every meaning originated.

A poetic witness about the outcome of deeds

Some early dictionaries transmit the following line attributed to Labīd ibn Rabīʿah:

وَكُلُّ امْرِئٍ يَوْمًا سَيُعْلَمُ سَعْيُهُ
إِذَا حُصِّلَتْ عِنْدَ الْإِلَهِ الْحَصَائِلُ

wa-kullu imriʾin yawman sa-yuʿlamu saʿyuhu / idhā ḥuṣṣilat ʿinda al-ilāhi al-ḥaṣāʾilu

Translation following the supplied passive reading: “Every person’s endeavor will one day be known, when the outcomes are distinguished and made clear before God.”

حُصِّلَت accompanies الحصائل, both concerning disclosure of the outcome of deeds. In سَيُعْلَمُ سَعْيُهُ, the verb is passive and سَعْيُهُ its grammatical passive subject. “Each person will know his endeavor” does not match that vocalization; it requires the active reading سَيَعْلَمُ سَعْيَهُ, found in some transmissions.

Lisān al-ʿArab: the passive reading sa-yuʿlamu saʿyuhu

A version of Labīd’s Dīwān instead reads إِذَا كُشِّفَتْ عِنْدَ الْإِلَهِ الْمَحَاصِلُ, “when the outcomes are unveiled before God.” The variant matters because it does not contain حُصِّلَت.

Labīd lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods. Without establishing the line’s date and reliable reading, it cannot securely attest this precise form before the Qur’an.

The transmission matters for lexical history, but the principal argument about Qur’anic meaning should not depend on its disputed wording.

Kitāb al-ʿAyn editorial note: ḥuṣṣilat versus kushshifat in Labīd’s Dīwān

3. Qur’anic usage: Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10

وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ

wa-ḥuṣṣila mā fī al-ṣudūri

Suggested translation: “And what is in the breasts is disclosed and made clear.”

Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10

This is the root’s sole Qur’anic occurrence.

حُصِّلَ is a form II passive past verb. The construction does not name the agent, and what is in the breasts is the subject matter of the action.

In وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ, مَا means what. It therefore concerns what lies within generally, rather than a single particular feeling or thought. Grammatically, مَا is a relative pronoun functioning as the passive subject; فِي الصُّدُورِ supplies its relative clause with an understood omitted predicate.

Qur’anic language attributes many inward states to the breasts: thoughts, intentions, worries, and concealed matters. حُصِّلَ itself does not mean intention, thought, or secret; these are understood through مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ.

Study of Ṣ D R

The preceding verse: Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:9

أَفَلَا يَعْلَمُ إِذَا بُعْثِرَ مَا فِي الْقُبُورِ

a-fa-lā yaʿlamu idhā buʿthira mā fī al-qubūri

Suggested translation: “Does he not know when what is in the graves is overturned and brought out?”

Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:9

In Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:9, بُعْثِرَ concerns what is in the graves; in Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10, حُصِّلَ concerns what is in the breasts.

The verbs belong to different roots and should not be assumed synonymous.

بُعْثِرَ concerns overturning and bringing out what lies in the graves, while حُصِّلَ concerns distinguishing and making inward realities clear.

The contrast between الْقُبُورِ and الصُّدُورِ also matters: graves conceal something, while breasts in Qur’anic expression concern what people hold within.

In the first verse, what is hidden beneath the earth is brought out; in the second, what is concealed within people is disclosed and distinguished.

The connection extends beyond bodies emerging from graves. Inward human reality also enters the scene of reckoning.

Their juxtaposition does not establish a physical or biological mechanism for extracting thoughts from the chest. The passage concerns disclosure of inward realities in the Hereafter, rather than a medical procedure.

Gathering, separating, or disclosing?

Lexicographers give several closely related explanations of حُصِّلَ here.

Al-Farrāʾ explains it as بُيِّنَ, made clear or disclosed. مُيِّزَ, distinguished, is also recorded, as is جُمِعَ, gathered, in some sources.

These should be considered alongside the root’s familiar uses.

Gathering alone underplays distinguishing and making the outcome clear. Disclosure alone may obscure the difference between حُصِّلَ and ordinary verbs of appearing or revealing.

Lexically, taḥṣīl can concern a process in which components are gathered, separated, and their outcome identified. This range supports understanding Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10 as making what is in the breasts clear and distinct.

This does not mean the verse specifies three independent consecutive actions. It uses one verb whose appropriate meaning follows from context.

Nor does حُصِّلَ establish that only evil concealed matters are disclosed. مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ does not linguistically restrict the inward contents to evil.

Good and evil matters, beliefs, intentions, and the inward reality of deeds can fall within its scope. These are contextual examples, however, and should not all replace the word حُصِّلَ in translation.

Lisān al-ʿArab and Al-Mufradāt: clarified, distinguished, and gathered

The following verse: Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:11

إِنَّ رَبَّهُمْ بِهِمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ لَخَبِيرٌ

inna rabbahum bihim yawmaʾidhin la-khabīrun

Suggested translation: “Their Lord is indeed fully aware of them on that day.”

Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:11

The closing verse relates disclosure of what is in the breasts to the Lord’s awareness.

This should not imply that God was previously unaware of people’s inward reality and only learns it through taḥṣīl.

Other verses describe God’s knowledge of hidden matters. Āl ʿImrān 3:29 states that God knows what people conceal or reveal in their breasts. Ghāfir 40:19 likewise describes His knowledge of what the breasts conceal.

حُصِّلَ therefore cannot be restricted to God acquiring new knowledge. It describes an event within the reckoning in which inward human reality becomes clear and distinct.

God’s knowledge of reality and that reality being disclosed in the reckoning should remain distinct.

The testing of inward secrets

Al-Ṭāriq 86:9 states:

يَوْمَ تُبْلَى السَّرَائِرُ

yawma tublā al-sarāʾiru

Suggested translation: “The day when hidden secrets are tested.”

This also concerns inward matters in the Hereafter. تُبْلَى belongs to ب ل و and expresses being tested; حُصِّلَ belongs to ح ص ل and in Al-ʿĀdiyāt concerns disclosure and distinction.

The statements may relate to the same general scene without being lexically identical. Translation and analysis should preserve their different roots.

4. Distinctions from related roots

Ḥ Ṣ L (حَصَل) and J M ʿ (جَمَع)

Jamʿ basically concerns gathering scattered things.

Ḥaṣl can also concern gathering, but may emphasize what is obtained and distinguished after gathering or separating.

Things may be gathered within taḥṣīl, but not every gathering amounts to extracting an outcome.

Collecting all the grain in a container is not by itself separating sound grain from impurities.

Study of J M ʿ

Ḥ Ṣ L (حَصَل) and KH L Ṣ (خَلَص)

Khulūṣ concerns separation from admixture and attaining a pure condition.

In extracting gold from ore, taḥṣīl and takhlīṣ can describe closely related aspects of one task: separating valuable material from accompanying elements.

Ḥaṣl does not always mean purification. The outcome can be valuable, worthless, good, or bad. حُصَالَة illustrates this through low-value residue after cleaning grain.

In Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10, حُصِّلَ does not mean making inward contents pure or good. The verse concerns disclosure and distinction, rather than the correction or purification of every inward reality.

Study of KH L Ṣ

Ḥ Ṣ L (حَصَل) and Ḥ Ṣ Y (حَصَى)

The roots differ in their final consonant: lām in ح ص ل, yāʾ in ح ص ي.

Iḥṣāʾ concerns counting, recording, and encompassing things numerically. Taḥṣīl need not involve counting; it can concern separation or clarifying an outcome.

Counting and determining an outcome may accompany each other in accounting, but that does not make the roots synonymous.

Ḥ Ṣ L (حَصَل) and Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ (حَصْحَص)

حَصْحَصَ occurs in Yūsuf 12:51:

الْآنَ حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ

al-āna ḥaṣḥaṣa al-ḥaqqu

That is: “Now the truth has become clear.”

The verb has four root consonants and must be distinguished lexically from ح ص ل.

الْحَقُّ is its subject, and the statement concerns a previously concealed or denied truth becoming clear.

In Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10, حُصِّلَ is a form II passive construction concerning مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ.

Both concern clarifying something previously concealed, but their similar contextual outcome does not identify the roots or morphological constructions.

Study of Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ

Ḥ Ṣ L (حَصَل) and B ʿ TH R (بَعْثَر)

This comparison matters particularly because the two verbs occur side by side in Al-ʿĀdiyāt.

Baʿtharah in Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:9 concerns overturning and bringing out what is in the graves. Ḥaṣl in Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10 concerns clarifying and distinguishing what is in the breasts.

Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10 therefore does not merely repeat Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:9 with another word. The subject shifts from graves and their contents to inward human reality, accompanied by a different appropriate verb.

5. Developments in usage and translation proposals

Modern Persian taḥṣīl chiefly concerns education and formal study. Ḥāṣil means result, maḥṣūl a product, and ḥuṣūl something arising or being realized.

These uses relate to the root’s historical branches, but a restricted modern meaning should not determine the Qur’anic translation.

Acquiring knowledge concerns obtaining learning, and agricultural produce concerns the land’s yield. وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ concerns neither education nor economic production.

Several renderings of Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10 merit consideration.

“What is in the breasts is gathered” reflects an attested meaning, but alone inadequately conveys disclosure and distinction.

“What is in the breasts is separated and distinguished” approaches tamyīz, but may leave Persian readers asking what is being separated from what.

“What is in the breasts is disclosed” is fluent and clear, conveying the verb’s contextual outcome. Some difference between taḥṣīl and simple appearance nevertheless becomes less visible.

Among these options, the following suits Forqan’s basic translation:

“And what is in the breasts is disclosed and made clear.”

It conveys the verse’s understandable meaning without replacing مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ with a list of intentions, beliefs, deeds, and secrets.

The root explanation can add that حُصِّلَ concerns separating and distinguishing previously concealed contents, just as an outcome becomes distinct from other components.

Taḥṣīl here does not itself mean final judgment of what is disclosed. Judgment and reckoning belong to the broader context of the Hereafter; the verb need not mean rewarding, punishing, or purifying.

Conclusion

Brief root meaning: ح ص ل concerns gathering, obtaining, distinguishing, and retaining something’s outcome. Different derivatives emphasize different aspects.

حَصَلَ can express remaining and becoming established. حَصَّلَ can concern gathering, separating, and obtaining what is present within a collection. حَصِيلَة and مَحْصُول name an outcome or remainder.

Separating metal from ore or grain from chaff clearly illustrates concrete uses. Neither should be declared the sole historical source of every semantic branch without sufficient evidence.

Only حُصِّلَ occurs in the Qur’an. In Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10 it stands between bringing out what is in the graves and affirming the Lord’s complete awareness.

In this Qur’anic use, the meaning is making clear and distinct what lies within a person. It concerns inward reality being disclosed in the Hereafter, without making حُصِّلَ itself mean intention, sin, reward, punishment, or God acquiring new knowledge.

Taḥṣīl can distinctively gather or separate something from other components so its outcome becomes known. In Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10, this semantic range expresses clarifying what lies in people’s breasts.
