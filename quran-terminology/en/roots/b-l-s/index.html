Foundational Meaning

The root ب ل س fundamentally means despair, being cut off, the closing of the path of response, falling silent from helplessness, and collapsing into a state of being severed and without a way forward.

This root shows the state of a person or group who, after truth becomes clear, punishment descends, or their supports collapse, no longer finds any path for defense, answer, escape, justification, or hope, and falls into silence, shock, and despair.

Important Forms

In the Qur'an, one form from this root is مُبْلِسُونَ: those who have entered the state of iblas, meaning despairing, silent, cut off from response, and helpless.

The verbal form يُبْلِسُ also appears in the Qur'an and points to falling into iblas: the moment when a human being, after judgment, punishment, or final truth arrives, loses the path of remedy and speech.

The name إِبْلِيس is also commonly connected, at the lexical level, with this same field: being cut off, despairing, and falling away from the path of mercy. However, in the Qur'an, إِبْلِيس appears as a proper name: the name of a specific being in the story of Adam who refused the command of sujud. Therefore, in translation and analysis, his name should be preserved and not reduced to a simple adjective.

Linguistic Note

The semantic center of ب ل س turns around being cut off and inwardly collapsing after the path of hope is closed. This root is not merely "becoming sad" or "being afraid." It is stronger: a person sees that no path remains for escape, defense, justification, answer, or rebuilding the situation.

In this state, the tongue closes, inward motion collapses, and the person stands in hopeless silence. In ب ل س, silence usually does not come from peace; it is the silence of helplessness.

This silence resembles the silence of someone who no longer has an argument, hope, or path ahead. For this reason, iblas should be understood as despair joined with shock, lack of answer, and being cut off.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is a person standing before the truth or the consequence of his action, and suddenly every path closes before him. The tongue fails to answer, the body falls into shock, and the person becomes like someone who has reached a sealed wall.

It can be imagined as a road that suddenly ends with no exit. Or as someone whose supports collapse all at once, leaving him with no ability to move, no argument to speak, and no hope of escape.

ب ل س is the image of silent despair: not only lacking hope, but being cut off from the possibility of answer and exit.

Older and Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic, iblas refers to despair, bewilderment, helplessness, and falling silent from intense severance. A person who is mublis is someone who no longer has an answer or way out and has been cut off from hope.

In a later expanded version, a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or early Arabic usage may be added. Without a clear witness, no specific historical or poetic claim should be forced into the text.

Qur'anic Usage

One clear Qur'anic use of this root is the state of criminals when the Hour is established:

وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ السَّاعَةُ يُبْلِسُ الْمُجْرِمُونَ

And on the day the Hour rises, the criminals will become despairing and speechless. Ar-Rum 30:12

In this verse, يُبْلِسُ does not merely mean becoming upset. The criminals stand before a truth against which they no longer have a path for denial, excuse, argument, or ordinary return. Iblas here is the state of falling into despair and losing the path of response.

In Surat al-An'am, this state comes after heedlessness, outward abundance, and sudden seizure:

فَلَمَّا نَسُوا مَا ذُكِّرُوا بِهِ فَتَحْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ أَبْوَابَ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ حَتَّىٰ إِذَا فَرِحُوا بِمَا أُوتُوا أَخَذْنَاهُمْ بَغْتَةً فَإِذَا هُمْ مُبْلِسُونَ

So when they forgot what they had been reminded of, We opened for them the doors of every thing, until, when they rejoiced in what they had been given, We seized them suddenly; then there they were, despairing and silent. Al-An'am 6:44

In this verse, iblas comes after joy and outward opening. They were deceived by abundance and security, but the sudden seizure collapsed every path of justification and reliance. The structure فَإِذَا هُمْ مُبْلِسُونَ shows suddenness: in one moment, they moved from intoxicated confidence into helplessness and silence.

The same meaning appears when a door of severe punishment is opened:

حَتَّىٰ إِذَا فَتَحْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ بَابًا ذَا عَذَابٍ شَدِيدٍ إِذَا هُمْ فِيهِ مُبْلِسُونَ

Until, when We opened upon them a door of severe punishment, suddenly they were in it despairing and helpless. Al-Mu'minun 23:77

Here مُبْلِسُونَ shows the state that occurs after the door of punishment opens. They are no longer in argument, play, denial, or arrogance. They have fallen into severance, helplessness, and silence.

The Name Iblis

The name إِبْلِيس appears in the Qur'an as a proper name:

وَإِذْ قُلْنَا لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ اسْجُدُوا لِآدَمَ فَسَجَدُوا إِلَّا إِبْلِيسَ

And when We said to the angels, "Prostrate for Adam," they prostrated, except Iblis. Al-Baqarah 2:34

Here إِبْلِيس is the name of a specific being who refused the command of The God. If its lexical connection with ب ل س is considered, the name fits the field of being cut off, despairing, and falling away from the path of mercy. But it must be noted that the Qur'an presents him as a narrative figure and real agent.

Therefore, his name should not be turned into an abstract adjective. In translation, إِبْلِيس should remain "Iblis," while the lexical connection may be mentioned only in analysis.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

مُبْلِسُونَ in Al-An'am 6:44 and Al-Mu'minun 23:77 both refer to despair and helplessness, but their contexts differ. In Al-An'am, iblas comes after abundance, joy, and sudden seizure. In Al-Mu'minun, iblas comes after the opening of a door of severe punishment. In both, the semantic center is the same: hope collapses and the path of response closes.

يُبْلِسُ الْمُجْرِمُونَ in Ar-Rum 30:12 shows the state of criminals on the Day of Resurrection. Here iblas has the color of final judgment: truth is exposed, and the path of denial, argument, or defense is closed.

إِبْلِيس must be treated separately and carefully. مُبْلِسُونَ and يُبْلِسُ are descriptive and verbal forms, but إِبْلِيس is a proper name. The resemblance and semantic connection matter, but the proper name must not be reduced without caution to a purely root-based or adjectival analysis.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In فَإِذَا هُمْ مُبْلِسُونَ, the sudden structure فَإِذَا هُمْ matters. It shows that the state of iblas descends upon them suddenly, as though in one moment they move from arrogance, distraction, or apparent security into helplessness and silence.

In إِذَا هُمْ فِيهِ مُبْلِسُونَ, the pronoun فِيهِ returns to the severe punishment. This structure shows that they are mublis within that very state of punishment; despair and having no way out become their inward state inside that field of punishment.

In يُبْلِسُ الْمُجْرِمُونَ, the subject is الْمُجْرِمُونَ. This shows that iblas in this verse is tied to the state of criminals at the Resurrection, not to every general form of sadness or despair.

In إِلَّا إِبْلِيسَ, the word إِبْلِيس appears in a structure of exception. In the Qur'anic story, he is the one who separated from the command of sujud and took the path of rebellion. This narrative role fits the semantic field of severance and being cut off, but the analysis must pass through the verses themselves and the context of the sujud command, not through external legends or claims.

Difference From Nearby Roots

ي أ س relates to despair and loss of hope. ب ل س also contains despair, but it is usually joined with helplessness, silence, and the closing of the path of response. Ya's may be an inward state of hopelessness; iblas is the collapse and severance that follows the exposure of truth or the arrival of punishment.

ق ن ط relates to severe despair and being cut off from mercy or relief. Qunut focuses more on the heart's loss of hope; ب ل س, in addition to despair, also conveys silence, shock, and the closing of the tongue and the path of defense.

ح ي ر relates to confusion and bewilderment. Iblas can include bewilderment, but it is not only bewilderment; it also contains despair and silence.

ب ه ت relates to being stunned and unable to answer. ب ل س is close to this, but its field is more strongly despair and severance. Being stunned may come from surprise or intellectual shock, while iblas comes from the closing of hope and remedy.

خ س ر relates to loss and ruin. Iblas may happen after loss becomes clear, but ب ل س itself points to the psychological and existential state of despair and helplessness, not merely loss.

ق ط ع relates to cutting and severing. ب ل س contains a kind of severance in hope, response, and possibility of exit. But Q T A is broader and can be material, social, or spiritual.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

يُبْلِسُ: becomes despairing and silent, becomes helpless and cut off, falls away from response and hope.

مُبْلِسُونَ: the silently despairing, the helpless, those cut off from response, those fallen into despair.

فَإِذَا هُمْ مُبْلِسُونَ: then suddenly they were helpless and silent / suddenly they fell into despair.

إِذَا هُمْ فِيهِ مُبْلِسُونَ: suddenly they were in it helpless and despairing / in it they fell into silence and despair.

يُبْلِسُ الْمُجْرِمُونَ: the criminals become despairing and speechless / the criminals fall into helplessness.

إِبْلِيس: Iblis; preserve it as a proper name. In lexical explanation one may mention its connection with despair, severance, and falling away from the path of mercy, but it should not be translated simply as "the despairing one."

Linguistic Summary

The root ب ل س indicates despair, severance, the closing of the path of response, and falling into silent helplessness. In its material image, a human being reaches a sealed wall before truth or punishment; his tongue fails to defend, and his hope collapses.

In the Qur'an, this root is used mostly in contexts of criminals, punishment, sudden seizure, and the exposure of judgment. إِبْلِيس also fits this field lexically, but in the Qur'an it is a proper name and must be studied separately from descriptive forms such as مُبْلِسُونَ.

The difference between ب ل س and despair, qunut, bewilderment, being stunned, loss, and severance must be preserved. Iblas is not only hopelessness; it is silent hopelessness cut off from response.
