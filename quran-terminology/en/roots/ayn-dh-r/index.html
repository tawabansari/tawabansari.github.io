Central meaning: offering a reason or explanation to remove blame or responsibility directed at someone; related uses include accepting an excuse, lifting reproach, and establishing a legitimate reason for acting or refraining from action.

1. The root in early Arabic

The three-consonant root ع ذ ر has several semantic branches. Its principal branch concerns offering excuses, defending conduct, removing blame, and accepting a legitimate reason.

عُذْر is a reason or explanation presented to remove blame, responsibility, or an objection to one's conduct. اعْتَذَرَ denotes offering an excuse; عَذَرَهُ denotes accepting someone's excuse or excusing them.

An excuse is a reason that may explain conduct or reduce responsibility; its mere presentation does not establish its validity.

For example, someone unable to meet a deadline because of illness or an obstacle beyond their control may explain why. A real reason proportionate to their responsibility can be a legitimate excuse.

Someone else may have the ability and opportunity to act yet neglect the responsibility and invent a justification afterward. This too is excuse-making, but offering an excuse does not make it true.

Three concepts must therefore be distinguished: offering an excuse, having a legitimate excuse, and having an excuse accepted.

This distinction is recognized in early usage and is fundamental to the Quranic passages.

A witness from pre-Islamic poetry

A poem attributed to Al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī contains:

هَا إِنَّ تَا عِذْرَةٌ إِلَّا تَكُنْ نَفَعَتْ

hā inna tā ʿidhratun illā takun nafaʿat

فَإِنَّ صَاحِبَهَا قَدْ تَاهَ فِي الْبَلَدِ

fa-inna ṣāḥibahā qad tāha fī al-baladi

Translation: Here is my plea; if it has not helped, its author has wandered lost in the land. Fear and distress belong to the poem's context rather than being separate words in this verse.

The poem seeks to dispel Al-Nuʿmān ibn al-Mundhir's displeasure and defend its speaker against an accusation.

تَا is a demonstrative meaning “this,” and عِذْرَة means an excuse or defensive plea. Here إِلَّا combines conditional إِنْ with negative لَا; it is not the particle of exception.

The poet offers his reason but knows that acceptance depends on the addressee. He therefore describes his predicament if the plea proves ineffective.

This transmitted witness shows excuse-making as an attempt to remove displeasure, blame, or accusation in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period.

The plea and its acceptance remain distinct: the speaker presents it without knowing whether it will be accepted.

The verse appears in the attributed collection and early commentaries. A version ending فَإِنَّ صَاحِبَهَا مُشَارِكُ النَّكَدِ is also recorded. The supplied claim of إِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ نَفَعَتْ as an alternative wording has not been independently established in this review.

These transmitted differences do not fundamentally alter عِذْرَة as a plea offered to remove displeasure.

Attribution to a pre-Islamic poet supports its relevance to earlier Arabic usage, although this review has not independently established the exact date of this verse.

Another poetic witness: resistance and difficulty

The Muʿallaqah attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays contains:

وَيَوْمًا عَلَىٰ ظَهْرِ الْكَثِيبِ تَعَذَّرَتْ

wa-yawman ʿalā ẓahri al-kathībi taʿadhdharat

عَلَيَّ وَآلَتْ حَلْفَةً لَمْ تَحَلَّلِ

ʿalayya wa-ālat ḥalfatan lam taḥallali

Translation: One day upon the sand dune she resisted me and swore an oath without making an exception in it.

تَعَذَّرَتْ here concerns refusal, resistance, or difficulty in obtaining what the speaker seeks.

The poet describes a woman refusing his request and affirming her position with an oath.

This differs from apologizing.

The person is not explaining a wrongdoing; rather, a request encounters resistance or refusal.

تَعَذَّرَ الْأَمْرُ likewise describes something becoming difficult or impossible to accomplish.

The verse occurs in established transmissions of the Muʿallaqah, and early commentaries explain the verb through refusal and difficulty.

Thus difficulty or inability to accomplish something is another branch of this family; it must not always be translated as apologizing.

What the early witnesses show

Al-Nābighah's attributed poem illustrates a defensive explanation and a request to remove displeasure.

Imruʾ al-Qays's poem illustrates تَعَذَّرَ in refusal and difficulty.

Together they show that early Arabic did not restrict this family to one narrow sense.

Lexicons record other branches, including the side strap of a bridle, the side of the face, a house's forecourt, bodily uses, and names connected with social occasions.

Even early root analyses do not necessarily derive all these branches from one original meaning.

Accordingly, excuse and removal of blame should be central to the Quranic study, while other lexical branches are recognized separately.

2. Forms and semantic branches

Important forms include عَذَرَ, يَعْذِرُ, عُذْر, أَعْذَار, عِذْرَة, مَعْذِرَة, مَعَاذِير, اعْتَذَرَ, اعْتِذَار, أَعْذَرَ, إِعْذَار, عَذَّرَ, تَعْذِير, مُعَذِّر, تَعَذَّرَ, عِذَار, and عَذْرَاء.

عُذْر: a reason for removing blame

عُذْر is a reason or explanation offered in response to objection, blame, or responsibility.

Its plural is أَعْذَار.

Ordinarily, it can concern doing something wrong or failing to perform an expected responsibility.

The word alone does not establish that the person actually committed a wrong.

For example, someone falsely accused may present a reason in their defense. Their explanation is an excuse or plea, but not necessarily an admission of guilt.

Someone who did commit a wrong may instead acknowledge it and explain the circumstances that contributed to it.

An excuse can therefore be a defense of innocence, an explanation of conduct, or a request to remove blame.

These situations are not always identical.

عَذَرَ: accepting an excuse

One principal use of عَذَرَهُ means accepting someone's excuse or regarding them as excused for an action.

In عَذَرْتُهُ فِيمَا صَنَعَ, the speaker accepts the conduct in view of the person's reasons and circumstances, or refrains from blaming them for it.

مَعْذُور accordingly describes someone with an acceptable excuse for their conduct or omission.

Here being excused differs from offering an excuse.

Someone may have a valid excuse without having stated it yet.

Conversely, someone may offer many excuses without any being acceptable.

The verb اعْتَذَرَ therefore does not by itself imply acceptance.

اعْتَذَرَ: presenting an excuse

اعْتَذَرَ is Form VIII, meaning to offer an excuse or explanation in defense of conduct or to remove blame.

اعْتِذَار is its verbal noun.

In contemporary Persian, the borrowed term often suggests apologizing and acknowledging fault; its Arabic semantic range is wider.

A person can offer a defense without acknowledging wrongdoing.

Someone falsely accused, for instance, may establish that the allegation is untrue.

Someone actually at fault may also offer an excuse to reduce responsibility.

Quranic اعْتَذَرَ should therefore not invariably become “repented” or “confessed a sin.”

Its basic meaning is offering an excuse or defending conduct; the context establishes whether the excuse is truthful.

مَعْذِرَة: an excuse or defensible explanation

مَعْذِرَة denotes an excuse, a defensive explanation, or a reason to remove blame.

It may refer to the excuse someone states.

It may also concern an action taken to discharge a responsibility, giving the person a defensible position when questioned.

For example, someone obliged to warn another of danger may properly do so. Even if the warning is ignored, the person can show that the responsibility was fulfilled.

Here the meaning is not simply saying “sorry,” but having grounds for removing responsibility or blame.

This is particularly important in Al-Aʿrāf 7:164.

مَعَاذِير: excuses and various defenses

مَعَاذِير denotes excuses and explanations offered in self-defense.

Grammatical sources differ over its formation.

Some treat it as a non-regular plural of مَعْذِرَة; others as an independent collective expression for excuses.

Some lexical reports also record مِعْذَار for a curtain or covering and مَعَاذِير for curtains.

A figurative relationship between concealing curtains and excuses that conceal conduct is conceivable, but it does not establish the historical derivation of either sense.

“Curtains” has also been proposed for the Quranic passage; this article prefers “excuses” rather than treating the alternative as linguistically impossible.

Al-Qiyāmah 75:15 concerns the human being and awareness of oneself, making the excuses and defenses a person presents a direct contextual reading.

Another lexical sense alone does not require changing the verse's translation.

أَعْذَرَ and إِعْذَار: establishing grounds and removing blame

Form IV أَعْذَرَ can mean presenting an excuse or doing something so fully that grounds for reproach are removed.

For example, someone may give the necessary warning before a danger and enable others to take precautions.

They have then fulfilled the warning responsibility; others' disregard cannot simply be transferred to them as blame.

The expression أَعْذَرَ مَنْ أَنْذَرَ reflects this field: one who has duly warned has established grounds for being excused.

It links excuse with warning but does not make every outward claim of warning sufficient.

If a warning is unclear, inaccurate, or unsuitable to the audience's circumstances, claiming to have warned does not necessarily remove responsibility.

These considerations concern whether an excuse is substantively justified. The linguistic form إِعْذَار alone does not prove that all relevant responsibilities have actually been fulfilled.

عَذَّرَ and مُعَذِّر: excuse-making or falling short

Form II عَذَّرَ has several recorded uses.

One concerns offering an excuse or trying to establish a justification.

Another describes failing to exert sufficient effort while making it appear that enough has been done.

An excuse may then conceal actual neglect.

مُعَذِّر can be a Form II active participle, but the same surface form can also arise through assimilation from Form VIII مُعْتَذِر.

This matters for Al-Tawbah 9:90.

Without considering the reading and context, the form alone does not establish that a person is lying or genuinely excused.

تَعَذَّرَ: becoming difficult or impossible

تَعَذَّرَ الْأَمْرُ describes something becoming difficult, unattainable, or impossible.

The subject is not necessarily apology.

An action may be prevented by insufficient resources, another person's refusal, or some other obstacle.

This sense also appears in Imruʾ al-Qays's verse.

Yet actual impossibility must be distinguished from claiming impossibility to evade responsibility.

One person may truly be unable to act; another may claim impossibility despite being able to act.

The word alone does not decide whether the claim is true.

Other branches

Arabic records other lexical groups with these consonants.

عِذَار denotes side straps of a bridle running beside a horse's face; it also refers to the side of the face and the hair growing beside the cheek.

عَذْرَاء ordinarily describes a girl or woman who has not had sexual intercourse.

عُذْرَة has distinct recorded senses including a house's forecourt or surrounding space, bodily uses, a throat ailment, and human excrement.

Other forms occur in names for social occasions and associated meals.

These uses do not occur in the Quran.

Lexicographers offer different connections between them, but they cannot all confidently be derived from “apologizing.”

They should therefore be recognized separately rather than imported into Quranic derivatives.

3. Quranic occurrences

In the reading followed here, the root occurs 12 times in 11 verses, grouped under five principal forms.

The Quranic forms are:

  • Form VIII verbs of اعْتَذَرَ, five times: Al-Tawbah 9:66, Al-Tawbah 9:94 twice, Al-Taḥrīm 66:7, and Al-Mursalāt 77:36.
  • عُذْر, twice: Al-Kahf 18:76 and Al-Mursalāt 77:6.
  • مَعْذِرَة, three times: Al-Aʿrāf 7:164, Al-Rūm 30:57, and Ghāfir 40:52.
  • مَعَاذِير, once: Al-Qiyāmah 75:15.
  • الْمُعَذِّرُونَ, once: Al-Tawbah 9:90.

These uses fall into four main settings: grounds for being excused, legitimate or illegitimate pleas, truth exposed against human defenses, and excuses ceasing to benefit at the final reckoning.

A. عُذْرًا: grounds for ending companionship

قَالَ إِنْ سَأَلْتُكَ عَنْ شَيْءٍ بَعْدَهَا فَلَا تُصَاحِبْنِي ۖ قَدْ بَلَغْتَ مِنْ لَدُنِّي عُذْرًا

qāla in saʾaltuka ʿan shayʾin baʿdahā fa-lā tuṣāḥibnī qad balaghta min ladunnī ʿudhrā

Al-Kahf 18:76

He said, “If I ask you about anything after this, do not accompany me further; you have obtained sufficient grounds from me.”

Al-Kahf 18:76

عُذْرًا here denotes legitimate grounds that can justify ending the companionship.

Moses says this after questioning his companion again.

In Al-Kahf 18:67, the companion had said that Moses would not be able to remain patient with him.

Al-Kahf 18:70 had set the condition: Moses should not ask about anything until the companion explained it himself.

Moses nevertheless asks questions after witnessing unexpected events.

In verse 76, he accepts that another such question would justify ending their companionship.

قَدْ بَلَغْتَ مِنْ لَدُنِّي عُذْرًا

بَلَغْتَ, from ب ل غ, means reaching or attaining.

مِنْ لَدُنِّي means “from me” or “on my part.”

عُذْرًا denotes legitimate grounds.

The companion has therefore obtained, from Moses himself, sufficient grounds for ending the companionship.

The excuse belongs to the person entitled to end it; this is not simply Moses asking forgiveness for a specified sin.

The passage shows that an excuse can justify a decision, rather than merely explain a past wrong.

For example, when two people agree to a clear condition and one repeatedly breaks it, the other may have defensible grounds for ending their cooperation.

Here an excuse means grounds for terminating a relationship.

This example clarifies the usage; the word itself does not supply every detail of Moses's relationship with his companion.

B. مَعْذِرَةً: fulfilling responsibility before the Lord

وَإِذْ قَالَتْ أُمَّةٌ مِنْهُمْ لِمَ تَعِظُونَ قَوْمًا اللَّهُ مُهْلِكُهُمْ أَوْ مُعَذِّبُهُمْ عَذَابًا شَدِيدًا ۖ قَالُوا مَعْذِرَةً إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُمْ وَلَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ

wa-idh qālat ummatun minhum lima taʿiẓūna qawman allāhu muhlikuhum aw muʿadhdhibuhum ʿadhāban shadīdan qālū maʿdhiratan ilā rabbikum wa-laʿallahum yattaqūn

Al-Aʿrāf 7:164

And when a group among them said, “Why do you admonish people whom The God will destroy or punish severely?” they said, “To have an excuse before your Lord, and perhaps they will be mindful.”

Al-Aʿrāf 7:164

This is a particularly significant occurrence because مَعْذِرَة does not justify an already committed wrong.

Some people object to others' wrongful conduct and admonish them.

A third group asks why they warn people they believe will ultimately face destruction or punishment.

The admonishers' response has two parts:

First, مَعْذِرَةً إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُمْ.

Second, وَلَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ.

مَعْذِرَةً إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُمْ: discharging responsibility

The admonishers say they act to have grounds before their Lord.

Here excuse is connected with doing what they regard as their responsibility.

Someone who witnesses wrongdoing and can give a truthful, appropriate warning can thereby show they were not indifferent to it.

The defense then rests on an actual action, not merely a verbal claim.

Someone who knows of a danger and clearly informs others may show they fulfilled their duty to communicate even if the warning is rejected.

Someone who does nothing but later claims to have intended a warning is not in the same position.

Thus مَعْذِرَة here concerns actually fulfilling responsibility, rather than inventing a pretext to escape it.

وَلَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ: the possibility of reform

The second part adds a further purpose.

The admonishers do not say their warning is certainly useless.

They say, “Perhaps they will be mindful.”

Two aims stand together: fulfilling responsibility before The God and hoping for the recipients' reform.

مَعْذِرَة therefore does not mean despair of others' improvement.

Nor does the verse say that one must continually reproach others in all circumstances regardless of possible harm or the appropriate manner of dialogue.

It describes a particular situation and states the admonishers' purpose.

The clear distinction is that an audience's failure to accept a warning does not by itself prove that the person warning failed in their responsibility.

The following verse

Al-Aʿrāf 7:165 describes the rescue of those who forbade wrongdoing and the punishment of the wrongdoers.

This continuation establishes the importance of their preventive conduct in the story.

The word مَعْذِرَة itself, however, does not mean rescue.

Rescue and punishment come from the next verse; مَعْذِرَة in verse 164 explains the grounds for fulfilling responsibility.

C. عُذْرًا أَوْ نُذْرًا: establishing grounds or warning

عُذْرًا أَوْ نُذْرًا

ʿudhran aw nudhrā

Al-Mursalāt 77:6

As an establishment of grounds or a warning.

Al-Mursalāt 77:6

This short verse follows the surah's opening verses.

Al-Mursalāt 77:5 refers to those or that which convey a reminder.

Verse 6 gives two purposes or results: عُذْرًا and نُذْرًا.

عُذْر belongs to ع ذ ر.

نُذْر belongs to ن ذ ر and concerns warning.

The roots are distinct in form and meaning but closely connected here.

Grounds and warning

When truth or the consequences of conduct are explained, a person cannot as readily claim to have received no information or warning.

Conveying the message can therefore establish grounds and remove room for that excuse.

نُذْر emphasizes warning of wrongful conduct's consequences.

They can be aspects of one process: conveying truth and warning while providing an opportunity to understand and choose.

The verse does not by itself show that any outward hearing of a message removes every possible excuse.

Hearing sounds or words differs from understanding them correctly.

People differ in language, access to information, cognitive ability, and the quality of the message reaching them.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:15 states that The God does not punish before sending a Messenger.

This gives the conveyance of the message and the grounds of responsibility importance in divine judgment.

عُذْرًا in Al-Mursalāt 77:6 can thus be understood as establishing grounds through the reminder. Other early explanations connect it with the excuse or repentance of those who respond; removing every possible excuse is not the word's only uncontested lexical meaning.

The word alone does not settle the circumstances in which each person's excuse ceases to hold.

4. Excuse-making: genuine grounds and pretexts

الْمُعَذِّرُونَ: Bedouins presenting excuses

وَجَاءَ الْمُعَذِّرُونَ مِنَ الْأَعْرَابِ لِيُؤْذَنَ لَهُمْ وَقَعَدَ الَّذِينَ كَذَبُوا اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ ۚ سَيُصِيبُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

wa-jāʾa al-muʿadhdhirūna mina al-aʿrābi li-yuʾdhana lahum wa-qaʿada alladhīna kadhabū allāha wa-rasūlahu sa-yuṣību alladhīna kafarū minhum ʿadhābun alīm

Al-Tawbah 9:90

And those presenting excuses among the Bedouins came to be granted permission, while those who lied to The God and His Messenger stayed behind. A painful punishment will befall those among them who disbelieved.

Al-Tawbah 9:90

الْمُعَذِّرُونَ identifies those who sought permission by presenting excuses.

There are several lexical analyses of its formation and precise meaning.

مُعَذِّر and مُعْذِر

In the Ḥafṣ reading followed here, الْمُعَذِّرُونَ has a doubled dhāl.

It can be analyzed in two ways.

One derives it from Form VIII الْمُعْتَذِرُونَ through assimilation of tāʾ into dhāl.

On that analysis it means people making excuses, whether valid or invalid.

Another derives مُعَذِّر from Form II, including a usage for someone falling short while making themselves appear justified.

Yaʿqūb's reading has الْمُعْذِرُونَ, with a still ʿayn and undoubled dhāl, from Form IV; it has been explained through exerting oneself in presenting an excuse or offering a valid excuse.

The alternatives show why sincerity or deceit cannot be conclusively assigned to everyone from the form alone, without context.

Those presenting excuses and those who lied

The verse distinguishes two descriptions or behaviors: coming for permission and remaining behind while lying. This contrast alone does not conclusively establish the sincerity of every person in the first group; early explanations differ.

First are those who came with excuses to obtain permission.

Then come those who lied to The God and His Messenger and stayed behind.

The conclusion specifies painful punishment for those among them who disbelieved.

مِنْهُمْ matters: a judgment on specified people should not automatically be extended to every Bedouin or everyone offering an excuse.

Rendering all الْمُعَذِّرُونَ as “lying excuse-makers” can therefore add interpretation beyond the word and construction.

“Those presenting excuses” is the preferred basic rendering here; their sincerity or insincerity should be examined contextually.

The following verses and genuine excuses

Al-Tawbah 9:91 mentions the weak, the sick, and those without means to spend.

It says there is no blame upon them if they are sincere toward The God and His Messenger.

Al-Tawbah 9:92 describes people who requested a means of accompanying the expedition but returned grieving when none could be provided.

By contrast, Al-Tawbah 9:93 reproaches those who ask permission despite their wealth and are content to remain behind.

These three verses matter for understanding excuse.

They distinguish actual inability, illness, and lack of resources from evading responsibility despite being able to act.

Excuse in the Quran is therefore not merely a word for deception or pretext.

A person may truly be excused from a responsibility beyond their capacity.

Another may fabricate an excuse despite being capable.

Both may linguistically offer excuses, while their actual circumstances and moral responsibility differ.

Excuses upon returning

يَعْتَذِرُونَ إِلَيْكُمْ إِذَا رَجَعْتُمْ إِلَيْهِمْ ۚ قُلْ لَا تَعْتَذِرُوا لَنْ نُؤْمِنَ لَكُمْ قَدْ نَبَّأَنَا اللَّهُ مِنْ أَخْبَارِكُمْ ۚ وَسَيَرَى اللَّهُ عَمَلَكُمْ وَرَسُولُهُ ثُمَّ تُرَدُّونَ إِلَىٰ عَالِمِ الْغَيْبِ وَالشَّهَادَةِ فَيُنَبِّئُكُمْ بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ

yaʿtadhirūna ilaykum idhā rajaʿtum ilayhim qul lā taʿtadhirū lan nuʾmina lakum qad nabbaʾanā allāhu min akhbārikum wa-sa-yarā allāhu ʿamalakum wa-rasūluhu thumma turaddūna ilā ʿālimi al-ghaybi wa-l-shahādati fa-yunabbiʾukum bi-mā kuntum taʿmalūn

Al-Tawbah 9:94

They will make excuses to you when you return to them. Say, “Do not make excuses; we will not believe you. The God has already informed us about you. The God and His Messenger will see your deeds; then you will be returned to the Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, and He will inform you of what you used to do.”

Al-Tawbah 9:94

The root occurs twice here:

يَعْتَذِرُونَ: they make excuses.

لَا تَعْتَذِرُوا: do not make excuses.

Both are Form VIII.

The central question is why these excuses are rejected.

The continuation says لَنْ نُؤْمِنَ لَكُمْ قَدْ نَبَّأَنَا اللَّهُ مِنْ أَخْبَارِكُمْ.

Their excuses are confronted by information revealed about their conduct.

Their deeds are then brought into view.

The sequence shows that merely stating an excuse does not replace the truth of one's conduct.

A person may offer a defense while their actions and the evidence expose its falsity.

The rejection therefore concerns these people's particular conduct and situation, not a universal rule against accepting any human excuse.

لَا تَعْتَذِرُوا: a prohibition in a particular situation

Al-Tawbah 9:66 also contains لَا تَعْتَذِرُوا.

Its audience is called to account for conduct described in the preceding context.

The continuation distinguishes groups: some may be pardoned, while others are punished because they were guilty.

Even in this condemnatory context, the qualifications prevent treating everyone indiscriminately.

لَا تَعْتَذِرُوا addresses the excuses of a particular group.

It should not be detached from context and turned into a general ban on explaining conduct, removing misunderstandings, or asking forgiveness.

A truthful explanation can clarify matters and repair human relationships.

What is condemned here is an excuse that has no validity against the actual deeds and particular circumstances.

5. Excuses at the reckoning

مَعَاذِيرَهُ: human defenses against the truth of conduct

بَلِ الْإِنْسَانُ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ بَصِيرَةٌ

bali al-insānu ʿalā nafsihi baṣīrah

Al-Qiyāmah 75:14

Rather, the human being has insight into himself.

Al-Qiyāmah 75:14

وَلَوْ أَلْقَىٰ مَعَاذِيرَهُ

wa-law alqā maʿādhīrah

Al-Qiyāmah 75:15

Even if he presents his excuses.

Al-Qiyāmah 75:15

مَعَاذِيرَهُ here refers to the excuses and explanations a person offers in self-defense.

أَلْقَىٰ belongs to ل ق ي and here means presenting or putting forward.

أَلْقَىٰ مَعَاذِيرَهُ thus means presenting one's excuses.

The second statement continues the first verse.

The Quran describes a person's insight into oneself despite presenting excuses.

Knowing the truth and justifying conduct

Someone may know their real reason for acting yet offer others a different explanation.

For instance, someone may disregard a right for personal gain but later claim the decision served only the public good.

Actual motivation then differs from the explanation offered.

This does not imply that every person always fully understands every hidden motive.

People may be mistaken or unaware about aspects of their own motivation and conduct.

The passage concerns responsibility for deeds and defenses that cannot erase the evident truth of conduct.

Thus offering excuses does not by itself change what happened.

Nor do these verses mean everyone who defends themselves is necessarily lying.

A defense can be true or false. The passage highlights the inadequacy of fabricated excuses against the known truth of conduct.

The alternative sense of curtains

Some lexical sources also record مَعَاذِير as curtains.

An alternative reading therefore evokes a person drawing coverings around themselves: concealing an act from others does not conceal it from oneself.

In the context الْإِنْسَانُ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ بَصِيرَةٌ, this article prefers the more direct reading of excuses and defenses.

This reading also accords with other Quranic uses of مَعْذِرَة.

The proposed basic rendering is therefore “his excuses,” while “curtains” can be discussed as an alternative lexical interpretation.

مَعْذِرَتُهُمْ: an excuse that does not help

فَيَوْمَئِذٍ لَا يَنْفَعُ الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا مَعْذِرَتُهُمْ وَلَا هُمْ يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ

fa-yawmaʾidhin lā yanfaʿu alladhīna ẓalamū maʿdhiratuhum wa-lā hum yustaʿtabūn

Al-Rūm 30:57

On that day, the excuse of those who wronged will not benefit them, nor will they be asked to seek restored favor.

Al-Rūm 30:57

مَعْذِرَتُهُمْ denotes the wrongdoers' excuse or defense.

The verse says that it will not benefit them.

It then uses يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ, from ع ت ب: they will not be asked to remove displeasure and seek restored favor.

These are two different roots.

مَعْذِرَة concerns a reason or defensive explanation.

اسْتِعْتَاب concerns seeking the removal of displeasure and restoration of favor.

Their juxtaposition does not make them identical.

In this afterlife context, the wrongdoers' excuses no longer help, and the ordinary opportunity to seek restored favor is unavailable.

Another occurrence in Ghāfir

يَوْمَ لَا يَنْفَعُ الظَّالِمِينَ مَعْذِرَتُهُمْ وَلَهُمُ اللَّعْنَةُ وَلَهُمْ سُوءُ الدَّارِ

yawma lā yanfaʿu al-ẓālimīna maʿdhiratuhum wa-lahumu al-laʿnatu wa-lahum sūʾu al-dār

Ghāfir 40:52

The day when the wrongdoers' excuse will not benefit them; theirs will be the curse and the evil abode.

Ghāfir 40:52

This verse also contains مَعْذِرَتُهُمْ.

Both passages concern the unhelpful excuses of a specified group: wrongdoers.

They should not be expanded into a claim that every wrongdoer at every time, even after sincere return and reform, is denied acceptance of an excuse.

The verses address the reckoning and the groups in question.

The opportunity to reform in worldly life must be distinguished from the manifestation of deeds' consequences in the afterlife.

لَا تَعْتَذِرُوا الْيَوْمَ: no excuses on that day

Al-Taḥrīm 66:7 tells those who disbelieved not to offer excuses that day: their recompense is for what they did.

The temporal expression الْيَوْمَ is important.

The setting is the specified day of reckoning, not every situation in worldly life.

Al-Mursalāt 77:36 says:

وَلَا يُؤْذَنُ لَهُمْ فَيَعْتَذِرُونَ

wa-lā yuʾdhanu lahum fa-yaʿtadhirūn

Al-Mursalāt 77:36

Nor will permission be given to them to offer excuses.

Al-Mursalāt 77:36

فَيَعْتَذِرُونَ is Form VIII and concerns offering excuses. It is grammatically indicative, with its nūn retained; the explanatory “to” in translation does not make it an Arabic subjunctive purpose construction.

Here excuse-making is associated with absence of permission.

This is related to Al-Rūm 30:57 and Ghāfir 40:52.

One verse says the excuse does not benefit.

Another says permission to offer it is not given.

The statements are not verbally identical, but both concern the final reckoning.

Their difference should not be made a contradiction. Nor do they imply that humans never speak at any stage of the afterlife, since other passages describe their conversations and replies.

What this verse specifically denies is permission for the group in question to offer excuses in that situation.

6. Comparison with related roots

ع ذ ر and ح ج ج: excuse and argument

حُجَّة, from ح ج ج, concerns evidence or argument offered to establish or defend a claim.

عُذْر can also be a reason, but its principal use concerns removing blame or responsibility.

Evidence that an historical event occurred at a certain time may be an argument without being an excuse.

Someone unable to perform a duty because of illness may instead offer a reason to remove blame; that reason can be an excuse.

A valid excuse may have an argumentative dimension, but not every argument is an excuse.

Offering a reason does not make it sound. Both arguments and excuses must be assessed for truth and relevance.

ع ذ ر and ت و ب: excuse-making and returning from wrong

ت و ب concerns returning.

Human repentance concerns returning from wrongdoing and correcting one's course.

اعْتِذَار instead means offering an explanation or reason in response to blame.

They may occur together but are not identical.

Someone may acknowledge a wrong, abandon it, and act to repair the harm. This involves return and reform.

Another person may merely explain the conduct and continue it.

Thus apology without reform is not necessarily repentance.

Conversely, someone may genuinely be excused and have committed no wrong requiring repentance.

This matters when comparing the genuine limitations in Al-Tawbah 9:91 with the rejected excuses in Al-Tawbah 9:94.

ع ذ ر and ع ت ب: a defense and seeking restored favor

ع ت ب concerns displeasure, reproach, and seeking restored favor.

مَعْذِرَة is a reason or explanation offered to remove blame.

اسْتِعْتَاب concerns seeking the removal of displeasure and restoration of favor.

Someone may explain why they acted; that explanation is an excuse.

The injured person may nevertheless remain displeased, and removing that displeasure may require reparation or changed conduct.

Offering an excuse and obtaining acceptance are then different stages.

Al-Rūm 30:57 places مَعْذِرَتُهُمْ and يُسْتَعْتَبُونَ together, illustrating this distinction.

ع ذ ر and ع ف و: excusing and pardoning

ع ف و concerns pardoning, foregoing pursuit of a wrong, and forgiving.

عَذَرَهُ can also concern removing blame.

In accepting an excuse, however, the reasons and circumstances of conduct matter particularly.

Someone may be excused because of inability, illness, or justifiable ignorance.

Someone else may actually have committed a wrong that the injured party pardons.

Pardoning and accepting an excuse can coincide without always being identical.

ع ذ ر and غ ف ر: excuse and forgiveness

مَغْفِرَة, from غ ف ر, concerns forgiveness.

مَعْذِرَة concerns an explanation or grounds for removing blame.

A person may acknowledge a wrong and ask The God for forgiveness.

Asking forgiveness is not the same as offering a justification for conduct.

A confession and request for forgiveness can be made without any justification.

Conversely, a person may offer a defense without accepting that any wrong occurred.

مَعْذِرَة should therefore not become مَغْفِرَة without contextual grounds.

ع ذ ر and ع ذ ب: independent roots

One branch of ع ذ ب concerns punishment and its hardship; the root also has other lexical senses requiring separate study.

ع ذ ر concerns excuses, defensive explanations, and removal of blame.

They can occur together because an excuse may address responsibility that carries a punitive consequence.

Shared subject matter does not make them one root.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:164 contains مَعْذِرَة in the admonishers' response and also mentions another group's possible punishment.

Excuse and punishment nevertheless remain distinct concepts.

ع ذ ر and ن ذ ر: establishing grounds and warning

ن ذ ر concerns warning and making consequences known.

Al-Mursalāt 77:6 places عُذْرًا and نُذْرًا together.

Warning can enable awareness.

On the reading that concerns removing an excuse, the person's opportunity to receive and understand the responsibility must be considered; this is a contextual question, not a complete definition supplied by the word alone.

Warning and removing grounds for excuse are related but distinct.

A message or warning should therefore not automatically become a definitive judgment about every person's awareness and responsibility without considering their circumstances.

7. Morphological and semantic points

Genuine and alleged excuses

The Quran uses the family for legitimate grounds as well as defenses that are not accepted.

In Al-Kahf 18:76, an excuse provides sufficient grounds for ending companionship.

In Al-Aʿrāf 7:164, مَعْذِرَة concerns actually fulfilling responsibility.

In Al-Tawbah 9:94, excuses confront information exposing the conduct at issue.

In Al-Qiyāmah 75:15, excuses stand against awareness and testimony concerning one's deeds.

Thus the word “excuse” does not itself mean a lie or fabricated pretext.

Assessing validity requires examining the reason, circumstances, capacity, awareness, and actual conduct.

Having an excuse does not erase every responsibility

Illness may excuse someone from a particular task while leaving other responsibilities within their capacity.

It may prevent travel without preventing honesty, goodwill, or helping as far as one can.

Al-Tawbah 9:91 also conditions the absence of blame for the weak and sick on sincerity.

A legitimate excuse can change the scope of a responsibility without removing all human responsibilities.

Apology and reparation

In human relationships, acceptance of an excuse may require changed behavior or repair of harm.

If someone takes another's property and merely expresses regret, the other person's claim to it may remain.

If false speech damages a reputation, an explanation or apology may not repair all the consequences.

These examples distinguish stating an excuse, returning from wrongdoing, repairing harm, and obtaining restored favor.

This root family emphasizes offering and accepting grounds for removing blame; the type of reparation depends on the conduct and rights involved.

Excuses before knowledge and truth

One significant use concerns defending conduct against facts that have become clear.

Al-Tawbah 9:94 weighs excuses against knowledge of the audience's conduct.

Al-Qiyāmah 75:14 and Al-Qiyāmah 75:15 contrast awareness of oneself with one's excuses.

These usages show that an excuse does not replace truth.

If reliable evidence contradicts an explanation, repeating the explanation does not change what happened.

Conversely, someone may have a real justification that others do not yet know.

Excuses should therefore neither be accepted without examination nor rejected without understanding the circumstances.

The criterion is the reason's truth and relevance to the responsibility, not merely how often or how emphatically someone apologizes.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

Distinguish presenting an excuse, having valid grounds, and having an excuse accepted.

عُذْر: an excuse or grounds offered to remove blame or responsibility; validity depends on context.

أَعْذَار: excuses, reasons, and defensive explanations.

عَذَرَهُ: accepted his excuse; regarded him as excused.

مَعْذُور: legitimately excused; not blamed for the matter in question.

اعْتَذَرَ: offered an excuse; presented reasons to explain or defend conduct.

اعْتِذَار: excuse-making; according to context, apology or defense of conduct.

مَعْذِرَة: an excuse or grounds for removing blame; also fulfilling a responsibility that provides such grounds.

مَعَاذِير: excuses and various defenses.

أَعْذَرَ: presented an excuse; in a related use, acted fully enough to leave no grounds for reproach.

إِعْذَار: establishing grounds for removing responsibility, or conveying sufficient warning and explanation.

مُعَذِّر: someone presenting an excuse; in some uses, someone concealing neglect with excuses.

تَعَذَّرَ: became difficult or impossible; encountered an obstacle to accomplishment.

عِذَار: a bridle's side strap or the side of the face.

عَذْرَاء: a girl or woman who has not had sexual intercourse.

Brief meanings for word boxes

Brief root meaning: offering reasons to remove blame or responsibility; presenting excuses, accepting excuses, and establishing justification.

Verbs of اعْتِذَار: offering excuses and defensive explanations; the context establishes their truth or falsity.

مَعْذِرَة: an excuse or grounds for removing responsibility; a defense offered against blame.

مَعَاذِير: excuses and various explanations in defense of conduct.

الْمُعَذِّرُونَ: those presenting excuses; people coming to explain or to be excused.

عُذْرًا: depending on context, legitimate grounds for removing blame or an establishment of grounds that removes room for excuse.

9. Conclusion

The root's main branch concerns offering reasons, defending conduct, removing blame, and accepting excuses.

In early Arabic, an excuse could defend against accusation or displeasure. Al-Nābighah's attributed poem illustrates a plea whose acceptance remains uncertain.

Imruʾ al-Qays's تَعَذَّرَتْ concerns resistance and difficulty, showing why the family should not be restricted to apology.

Lexicons also record bridle straps, عَذْرَاء, a house's forecourt, and bodily uses. These do not occur in the Quran, and their entire historical relationship with apology is not established.

The Quran uses the root twelve times.

These occurrences show that an excuse is not always a lying pretext.

In Al-Kahf 18:76, it provides legitimate grounds for ending companionship.

In Al-Aʿrāf 7:164, مَعْذِرَة concerns fulfilling the responsibility to warn and hoping for others' reform.

In Al-Mursalāt 77:6, excuse stands beside warning and can be understood through establishing grounds by conveying the reminder.

Al-Tawbah 9:90 mentions those seeking permission through excuses. Al-Tawbah 9:91, Al-Tawbah 9:92, and Al-Tawbah 9:93 distinguish inability and lack of resources from evasion despite capacity.

In Al-Tawbah 9:94, excuses confront the truth of the audience's conduct and are rejected.

In Al-Qiyāmah 75:15, numerous excuses cannot by themselves change what someone did.

Al-Rūm 30:57, Ghāfir 40:52, Al-Taḥrīm 66:7, and Al-Mursalāt 77:36 describe afterlife situations where excuses no longer help or permission to offer them is withheld.

This range clarifies several distinctions:

Having valid grounds differs from inventing a pretext.

Offering an excuse does not necessarily acknowledge guilt.

Acceptance differs from merely stating an excuse.

Apology, repentance, reparation, and seeking restored favor are related but distinct.

Assessing an excuse therefore requires attention to the real reason, awareness, ability, resources, circumstances, and conduct.

One person may be unable to perform a duty and have an acceptable excuse; another may fabricate an excuse despite being able to act.

Actually fulfilling a responsibility can provide grounds for removing blame even without the hoped-for result. In Al-Aʿrāf 7:164, the admonishers attend both to their responsibility and to the possibility of reform.

An excuse is not automatically a sign of deceit, weakness, or evasion; equally, not every explanation is a valid justification.

Final definition: In its principal branch, ع ذ ر concerns offering a reason or explanation to remove blame or responsibility. عُذْر is the reason, اعْتِذَار the act of presenting it, مَعْذِرَة an excuse or grounds for removing reproach, and عَذَرَهُ accepting the excuse or lifting blame. The family applies to valid excuses and unsound defenses alike. In the Quran, validity is established by context, actual conduct, and the circumstances of responsibility, not by the word alone.
