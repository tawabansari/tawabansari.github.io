Qur'anic Root D R A

1. Introduction to the Root

The root D R A in Arabic points to warding off, pushing back, restraining, and preventing something from reaching or taking effect. In this root, a force, danger, consequence, or claim is directed toward a person or thing, and another factor moves it away from the target or resists it.

Dar'a is not limited to pushing away a material body. Death, punishment, evil, accusation, or responsibility may also be its object. In such uses, the meaning is to prevent their occurrence, effect, or establishment.

This root by itself does not show whether what is warded off is good or bad, or whether the one warding it off is upon truth or not. In the Qur'an, the same root is used for warding off evil with good, for punishment being warded off from a person, and for mutual deflection of responsibility in a dispute.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

The most important forms of this family in Arabic are:

  • دَرَأَ: he warded off, pushed back, or prevented.
  • يَدْرَأُ: he wards off or prevents.
  • اِدْرَأْ: ward off or prevent.
  • دَرْء: warding off, pushing back, or prevention.
  • دَرْأَة: another verbal-noun form for the act of repelling.
  • دَارَأَ: he opposed someone or engaged in mutual repelling.
  • تَدَارَأَ: two or more sides pushed one another back, disputed with one another, or each side pushed claim and responsibility away from itself.
  • تَدَارُؤ: mutual repelling, dispute, or contention.
  • دَارِئ: one who wards off or pushes back.
  • دَرِيئَة: something placed in front for protection or for approaching prey; this use does not occur in the Qur'an.

In the Qur'an, five verbal forms of this root occur:

  • four times from the simple form dar'a;
  • once from the reciprocal form tadar'a, appearing as fa-iddara'tum.

No noun or verbal noun from this root occurs in the Qur'anic text.

3. Qur'anic Forms at a Glance

The Qur'anic forms of the root may be seen in three groups:

  • فَادْرَءُوا: ward death away from yourselves.
  • يَدْرَءُونَ and يَدْرَأُ: they ward off evil or punishment, or it prevents them.
  • فَادَّارَأْتُمْ: you engaged in mutual deflection and dispute.

The first four uses focus on warding something away from a target. The fifth use shows that when the act of warding is reciprocal among several sides, the meaning comes close to dispute, contention, and pushing responsibility away from oneself.

4. Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of D R A may be expressed as:

Placing a restraining force against something so that it is moved away from the target, does not reach it, or its effect does not become established upon it.

This act may occur before the danger reaches, or when its effect is on the edge of taking place. Therefore, dar'a can carry the sense of preventing, pushing back, or removing an approaching effect.

However, the root by itself does not specify the precise time of this act. Only context can show whether something has not yet arrived, is approaching, or has begun to take effect and must be pushed away.

5. Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is a person placing a hand, shield, or tool in front of a force moving forward and pushing it away from himself or someone else. The opposing force wants to reach the target, but it meets a restraining force and its path or effect is stopped.

This image has three parts:

  • something that comes toward the target or acts upon it;
  • a person or thing exposed to it;
  • a force that pushes it back or prevents it from reaching.

In the reciprocal structure tadar'a, each side does this against the other. As a result, instead of a one-sided force, two or more opposing forces appear, each pushing something away from itself.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In early Arabic one would say:

دَرَأَهُ يَدْرَؤُهُ دَرْءًا

That is: he warded it off, pushed it back, or prevented it.

Dar'a 'anhu ash-shay' was used for warding something away from a person, and dar'a ash-shay'a bish-shay' for warding something off by means of something else.

Tadar'a al-qawm was said when a group engaged in dispute and mutual contention; that is, each side pushed the claim, objection, or responsibility away from itself and back toward the other. From this structure, the meaning of dispute and contention arose.

Other uses are also recorded in lexical reports. The sudden movement of a flood, the sudden emergence of a person, the forward advance of fire, and the rapid movement of something could be expressed with forms of this root. The shared point in these uses is propulsion and forward movement.

Dar' is also reported for crookedness in wood, a spear, or a road. This meaning has been explained as a part that protrudes from the straight extension and turns aside from the main line. These uses do not occur in the Qur'an and should not be imposed directly on the verses.

7. The Restriction of Meaning in Later Usage

In later legal and usuli writings, the root D R A became most familiar in expressions such as dar' al-hudud and dar' al-mafasid. In these uses, dar' means preventing the application of a consequence or warding off harm.

These technical expressions preserve their link with the lexical meaning of repelling and preventing, but later idiomatic phrases should not be presented as the Qur'anic definition of the root. In the Qur'an, neither the verbal noun dar' occurs nor is this root restricted to one specific legal field.

Its Qur'anic use is wider: death is warded away from human beings, punishment from a person, evil by good, and responsibility or claim in reciprocal dispute.

8. Methodological Note

Dar'a does not always mean destroying something. Something may be moved away from a person or situation, or its effect may be stopped, without being annihilated.

For example, yadra'una bil-hasanati as-sayyi'ah does not by itself say that evil is erased from the record of deeds or forgiven. It says that evil is pushed back by good. Whether the result is forgiveness, repair of a relationship, the stopping of conflict, or some other consequence must be understood from wider context.

Likewise, the meaning of dispute in fa-iddara'tum should not be separated from the core of repelling. This word is not merely a general name for “difference”; it is a disagreement in which the sides mutually push something away from themselves.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

9.1. Warding Death Away from Oneself

The first Qur'anic use of the root comes in response to those who made claims about the slain, saying that if they had obeyed them, they would not have been killed:

الَّذِينَ قَالُوا لِإِخْوَانِهِمْ وَقَعَدُوا لَوْ أَطَاعُونَا مَا قُتِلُوا قُلْ فَادْرَءُوا عَنْ أَنْفُسِكُمُ الْمَوْتَ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ صَادِقِينَ

Those who sat back and said about their brothers, “If they had obeyed us, they would not have been killed”; say: Then ward death away from yourselves, if you are truthful.

Ali Imran 3:168

Fadra'u is an imperative verb, and al-mawt is its object. The phrase 'an anfusikum shows those from whom death is to be warded away.

The command in this verse is within a challenge and a testing of a claim. The phrase in kuntum sadiqin shows that the claimants must prove their ability to ward death away.

The root in this verse does not mean ordinary postponement or avoiding danger; the explicit object of the sentence is warding death itself away from oneself.

9.2. Warding Off Evil with Good

The same phrase appears in two places in the Qur'an:

وَيَدْرَءُونَ بِالْحَسَنَةِ السَّيِّئَةَ

And they ward off evil with good.

Ar-Ra'd 13:22

And:

وَيَدْرَءُونَ بِالْحَسَنَةِ السَّيِّئَةَ

And they ward off evil with good.

Al-Qasas 28:54

In this structure, as-sayyi'ah is what is warded off, and bil-hasanah states the means or manner by which it is warded away.

The verse does not say that good and evil are the same, or that one is verbally transformed into the other. A good act or response stands against evil and prevents its continuation, spread, or effect.

The imperfect verb yadra'una in both verses appears within the description of their conduct. This structure presents warding off evil with good not merely as an accidental reaction, but as a recurring and recognized behavior in their way of acting. Nevertheless, one cannot infer perpetual or exceptionless continuity from the imperfect form alone; this tendency comes from the combination of the imperfect verb with the descriptive context.

9.3. Punishment Being Warded Off by Testimony

Another use of the root occurs in the legal structure of Surat An-Nur:

وَيَدْرَأُ عَنْهَا الْعَذَابَ أَنْ تَشْهَدَ أَرْبَعَ شَهَادَاتٍ بِاللَّهِ إِنَّهُ لَمِنَ الْكَاذِبِينَ

And what wards the punishment away from her is that she testify four times by The God that he is among the liars.

An-Nur 24:8

In this verse, al-'adhab is what is warded away, and 'anha shows the woman from whom the punishment is warded away. The phrase an tashhada states the factor or cause of this warding away.

Punishment in this structure is still a consequence that is prevented from reaching the woman by the prescribed testimony. This use fits the preventive aspect of the root, but it should not be concluded from this example that dar'a in every Arabic use applies only before something occurs.

The root D R A here does not determine the precise name of the punishment, the nature of the ruling, or the entire legal system of the verses. Its meaning is limited to this: the mentioned consequence is warded away from the woman by the prescribed testimony.

This verse shows that dar'a is not only for bodily resistance; a speech-act and legal act may also prevent a consequence from reaching.

9.4. Reciprocal Deflection in the Case of a Slain Person

The only reciprocal use of this root is the following:

وَإِذْ قَتَلْتُمْ نَفْسًا فَادَّارَأْتُمْ فِيهَا وَاللَّهُ مُخْرِجٌ مَا كُنْتُمْ تَكْتُمُونَ

And when you killed a person, then you engaged in mutual deflection and dispute about it, while The God was bringing out what you used to conceal.

Al-Baqarah 2:72

Fa-iddara'tum does not mean merely that you had different opinions. The reciprocal structure of the verb shows that each side pushed something away from itself and placed it against the other side.

The context of killing a person and concealing the reality brings this mutual deflection close to a dispute over responsibility or the truth of the event. At the same time, the root itself does not name the persons, the form of accusation, or the details of the dispute.

The phrase fiha specifies the field of this dispute: the disagreement and mutual deflection concerned that soul or the event of killing.

10. Specialized Examination of Fa-iddara'tum

10.1. The Original Structure of the Word

The form fa-iddara'tum arose from the structure tadar'a'tum. This verb belongs to Form VI and indicates participation and reciprocity among several sides.

During formation, the initial ta of tadar'a'tum was assimilated into the dal, producing iddara'tum. Since a word cannot begin with a silent doubled consonant, a joining alif was added at the beginning. With fa added before it, the Qur'anic written form became:

فَادَّارَأْتُمْ

The joining alif at the independent beginning of the word helps pronunciation, but when the word is connected to the preceding fa, it is not pronounced. The final hamzah of the root remains preserved in the ra' portion.

10.2. The Role of Form VI

Form VI can show the participation of two or more sides in an action. Here each side performs the act of dar'a; that is, each pushes something away from itself and toward the other side.

From this structure, two layers of meaning arise:

  • mutual deflection;
  • the dispute and contention formed by this mutual deflection.

Therefore, the simple translation “you differed” gives the result of the behavior, but hides the image of the root. The translation “you pushed one another away” may also be understood too physically.

Forms such as these can be more precise:

  • “you engaged in mutual deflection and dispute about it”;
  • “you disputed about it with one another, each side pushing responsibility away from itself”;
  • “you engaged in contention and reciprocal rejection about it.”

The second option contains more contextual explanation and is better used as explanation, not as the base translation.

10.3. Difference from Simple Disagreement

Not every disagreement is tadar'u. Disagreement may be only a difference of view or understanding; but tadar'u contains the element of confrontation and mutual deflection. The sides do not merely hold different views; each pushes something away from itself.

For this reason, the Qur'anic form in verse 72 of Al-Baqarah suits a situation in which a truth has remained concealed and the sides mutually push responsibility or claim away from themselves.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

11.1. The Structure Dar'a 'An

In this structure, the thing being warded away is the object of the verb, and the person from whom that thing is warded away comes after 'an:

فَادْرَءُوا عَنْ أَنْفُسِكُمُ الْمَوْتَ

Death is the object, and their own selves are the field from which death is to be warded away.

The same structure appears in the verse of An-Nur:

وَيَدْرَأُ عَنْهَا الْعَذَابَ

Punishment is warded away, and the pronoun ha shows the person from whom the punishment is warded away.

11.2. The Structure Dar'a Bi

In the phrase:

يَدْرَءُونَ بِالْحَسَنَةِ السَّيِّئَةَ

As-sayyi'ah is the object of the verb, and bil-hasanah is the means, method, or factor by which evil is warded away.

Therefore, good is not what is warded away; good stands in the position of the means for warding off evil.

11.3. The Structure Tadar'a Fi

In:

فَادَّارَأْتُمْ فِيهَا

Fiha states the subject or field of the reciprocal contention. Fi here should not be translated only as physical location; the dispute is about that event or slain soul.

12. Comparison with Nearby Roots

12.1. D F Ayn (Dafa'a) Compared with D R A (Dar'a)

Daf' and dar' both indicate pushing back and preventing, and in some Qur'anic contexts they are very close to one another.

About evil, it says:

ادْفَعْ بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ السَّيِّئَةَ

Repel evil with what is better.

Al-Mu’minun 23:96

And:

وَيَدْرَءُونَ بِالْحَسَنَةِ السَّيِّئَةَ

And they ward off evil with good.

Ar-Ra'd 13:22

For dar'a, a tendency toward prevention and warding something away before its effect becomes settled has sometimes been noted, while for daf'a a broader tendency toward pushing back an existing or approaching force is expressed. The use yadra'u 'anha al-'adhab fits the preventive aspect of dar'a.

However, the use of the two roots in close structures concerning warding off evil shows that this temporal distinction is not absolute. Daf'a too can occur before harm is fully established, and dar'a also is not limited to one temporal stage.

Therefore, the difference between the two is better understood as a tendency: daf'a has a broader field of driving away, removing, and direct confrontation, while dar'a in its Qur'anic uses focuses more on keeping the effect or consequence of something away from the target.

12.2. R D D (Radda) Compared with D R A (Dar'a)

Radda indicates returning something, answering, or sending it back to its previous state or direction. In dar'a, returning to the previous point is not a necessary part of the meaning; it is enough that something is moved away from the target or that its effect is stopped.

Therefore, every radd can be a kind of warding away, but every dar' is not necessarily returning.

12.3. W Q Y (Waqa) Compared with D R A (Dar'a)

Wiqayah indicates guarding and placing protection between a person and danger. In dar'a, the act of pushing back and moving the threatening force away is more prominent.

One may say that wiqayah pays more attention to the state of protection and prevention, while dar'a pays attention to the act of warding off. Still, the two may overlap in practice and should not be turned into two fixed and inseparable stages.

13. Semantic Boundaries of the Root

The root D R A by itself does not mean:

  • complete destruction;
  • erasing sin;
  • forgiving;
  • returning to the starting point;
  • proving innocence;
  • permanently canceling a ruling;
  • simple intellectual disagreement;
  • protection without any restraining action;
  • the goodness or badness of the act of warding off.

Some of these results may accompany dar'a in a context, but they must be understood from the words and full structure of the verse.

14. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

The base translation of this root should be chosen according to structure:

  • دَرَأَ الشَّيْءَ: he warded that thing off or pushed it back.
  • دَرَأَ عَنْهُ الْخَطَرَ: he warded danger away from him.
  • دَرَأَ الشَّيْءَ بِالشَّيْءِ: he warded something off by means of something else.
  • فَادْرَءُوا عَنْ أَنْفُسِكُمُ الْمَوْتَ: then ward death away from yourselves.
  • يَدْرَءُونَ بِالْحَسَنَةِ السَّيِّئَةَ: they ward off evil with good.
  • يَدْرَأُ عَنْهَا الْعَذَابَ: it wards punishment away from her.
  • فَادَّارَأْتُمْ فِيهَا: you engaged in mutual deflection and dispute about it.

“Repelling” is a close and known equivalent for this root, but in smooth English, “warding off,” “pushing back,” “restraining,” and “preventing” may be more precise according to context.

15. Conclusion

The root D R A indicates warding off, pushing back, and preventing something from reaching or taking effect. Its basic image is a force placed against another force, pushing it away from the target.

In the Qur'an, death is warded away from selves, punishment from a woman, and evil by good. In the reciprocal form fa-iddara'tum, several sides perform the act of deflection against one another, and from this situation dispute and reciprocal rejection emerge.

The grammatical structures play an important role in determining meaning: 'an shows the person or field from which something is warded away; ba identifies the means of warding; and fi specifies the subject of reciprocal contention.

A preventive tendency may be recognized in dar'a, but its close uses alongside daf'a do not allow this difference to be turned into an absolute boundary. The time and quality of warding off must be understood in each verse from the context of that verse.

This root by itself does not indicate complete destruction, forgiveness, purification, innocence, or returning. These results are only valid when a separate contextual clue exists.

The compressed definition of the root is:

D R A: placing a restraining force against something in order to move it away from the target, prevent its reaching, or stop its effect.
