Linguistic Note

The core meaning of Ṭ H R is separation from impurity and arrival at a state of purity. The impurity may be material, such as impurity on the body or clothing. It may be ethical, such as fahishah or polluted relations. It may also be inward or social, such as rijs, sin, or wealth that needs cleansing.

In the Qur’an, ṭahārah is not merely washing. Washing may be one path toward purity, but ṭahārah itself is becoming pure and making pure. Sometimes a person purifies himself or herself; sometimes The God purifies; sometimes a house or garment is commanded to be purified; and sometimes purity describes a blessing, a book, water, or a station.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of Ṭ H R is the removal of something foreign, polluted, heavy, or obstructive from the proper state of a thing. Ṭahārah is a return to clarity, soundness, dignity, and proper readiness.

Purity is both a result and a path. When impurity is removed, something is pure; when someone acts to remove impurity, that person is moving in the path of taṭahhur.

Material Image

The simple material image is a body after washing, clothing after being cleaned, a house after impurity is removed, or a vessel cleared of grime. In each image, something foreign and polluting is removed so the thing can return to a sounder state.

Another image is clear air or a bright day after dust and murkiness have passed. This helps explain why ṭahārah is not only surface washing; at times it removes a layer that had hidden clarity and brightness.

Classical Language Note

In classical Arabic, Ṭ H R is tied to purity and the opposite of pollution. A ṭāhir thing is distant from impurity or has been cleansed. Taṭahhur can refer to becoming pure or purifying oneself, while taṭhīr refers to making someone or something pure.

The Qur’anic uses show that this root is not limited to later legal terminology. The Qur’an uses it for body, clothing, house, relations, heart, wealth, water, and the realm of the protected Book.

Bodily Purity and Physical State

In 2:222, the Qur’an says: وَلَا تَقْرَبُوهُنَّ حَتَّىٰ يَطْهُرْنَ فَإِذَا تَطَهَّرْنَ فَأْتُوهُنَّ مِنْ حَيْثُ أَمَرَكُمُ اللَّهُ (wa-lā taqrabūhunna ḥattā yaṭhurna fa-idhā taṭahharna fa'tūhunna min ḥaythu amarakumu Allāh).

The difference between يَطْهُرْنَ (yaṭhurna) and تَطَهَّرْنَ (taṭahharna) matters. First comes becoming pure and the ending of a physical state. Then comes self-purification and completing the state of purity. Purity here is not only a bodily ruling; it is also tied to respect for the body, time, relational limits, and clean conduct.

Purity for Salat and Devotional Presence

In the verse of wudu, purity is connected with readiness for salat. In 5:6, after the commands of washing, wiping, and tayammum, the verse says: مَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيَجْعَلَ عَلَيْكُم مِّنْ حَرَجٍ وَلَٰكِن يُرِيدُ لِيُطَهِّرَكُمْ وَلِيُتِمَّ نِعْمَتَهُ عَلَيْكُمْ (mā yurīdu Allāh li-yajʿala ʿalaykum min ḥaraj wa-lākin yurīdu li-yuṭahhirakum wa-li-yutimma niʿmatahu ʿalaykum).

The ending of the verse is central. The God does not intend hardship; He intends to purify and complete His blessing. In this setting, purity is bodily cleanliness and devotional readiness, not purposeless severity.

Purity of Clothing and Outward Appearance

In 74:4, the Qur’an says: وَثِيَابَكَ فَطَهِّرْ (wa-thiyābaka fa-ṭahhir).

Clothing is the closest outward covering of the human being. Purifying clothing may carry outward cleanliness, readiness, dignity, and distance from pollution. The root meaning is clear: what covers a person should be cleansed of impurity.

Purity of the House and Place of Worship

In 2:125, the Qur’an says: أَن طَهِّرَا بَيْتِيَ لِلطَّائِفِينَ وَالْعَاكِفِينَ وَالرُّكَّعِ السُّجُودِ (an ṭahhirā baytiya li-l-ṭā'ifīna wa-l-ʿākifīna wa-l-rukkaʿi al-sujūd).

In 22:26, the purity of the House appears beside the rejection of shirk: أَن لَّا تُشْرِكْ بِي شَيْئًا وَطَهِّرْ بَيْتِيَ (an lā tushrik bī shay'an wa-ṭahhir baytiya). Purifying a place of worship is therefore not only building hygiene; it is also purification of the direction of worship and removal of shirk and violation.

Ethical Purity and Human Relations

In the story of the people of Lūṭ, purity appears in an ethical and sexual setting. In 11:78, he says: هَٰؤُلَاءِ بَنَاتِي هُنَّ أَطْهَرُ لَكُمْ (hā'ulā'i banātī hunna aṭharu lakum).

أَطْهَرُ لَكُمْ (aṭharu lakum) points to a cleaner and more rightful path in contrast to polluted and violating conduct. Purity here is not only physical; it concerns relational purity, ethical limit, and distance from sexual and social corruption.

Purity of the Heart

In 33:53, the Qur’an says: ذَٰلِكُمْ أَطْهَرُ لِقُلُوبِكُمْ وَقُلُوبِهِنَّ (dhālikum aṭharu li-qulūbikum wa-qulūbihinna).

This shows that purity is not only body and clothing. Social relation, gaze, speech, boundary, and distance can affect the purity of hearts. Purity of heart means moving away from conditions that invite polluted intent, fitnah, unclean looking, or violation of dignity.

Purifying Ahl al-Bayt

In 33:33, the purification of Ahl al-Bayt comes within a passage directly addressing the wives of the Prophet: وَقَرْنَ فِي بُيُوتِكُنَّ وَلَا تَبَرَّجْنَ تَبَرُّجَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ الْأُولَىٰ وَأَقِمْنَ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتِينَ الزَّكَاةَ وَأَطِعْنَ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ ۚ إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ الرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًا (wa-qarna fī buyūtikunna wa-lā tabarrajna tabarruja al-jāhiliyyati al-ūlā wa-aqimna al-ṣalāta wa-ātīna al-zakāta wa-aṭiʿna Allāh wa-rasūlahu innamā yurīdu Allāh li-yudhhiba ʿankumu al-rijsa ahla al-bayti wa-yuṭahhirakum taṭhīran).

In a Qur’anic reading, the nearest referent of أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ (ahl al-bayt) is the Prophet’s household in the context of the address to his wives. In language and custom, the expression may extend to close family as well, but the verse itself does not establish a sacred, exclusive group separated from the Prophet’s wives.

For the root Ṭ H R, the central point is removing rijs and making pure. Purity here concerns cleansing the Prophet’s house from behavioral, ethical, and social pollution. It is a grave responsibility of purity, dignity, and obedience.

Purifying Wealth and Self Through Sadaqah

In 9:103, the Qur’an says: خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا (khudh min amwālihim ṣadaqatan tuṭahhiruhum wa-tuzakkīhim bihā).

Sadaqah is not only the transfer of wealth. It may purify a person from stinginess, consuming another’s right, unhealthy attachment to wealth, and the heaviness of wrongdoing. تُطَهِّرُهُمْ (tuṭahhiruhum) comes before تُزَكِّيهِم (tuzakkīhim): first cleansing, then growth.

Purity Among the People of the Masjid

In 9:108, the Qur’an says: فِيهِ رِجَالٌ يُحِبُّونَ أَن يَتَطَهَّرُوا ۚ وَاللَّهُ يُحِبُّ الْمُطَّهِّرِينَ (fīhi rijālun yuḥibbūna an yataṭahharū wa-Allāh yuḥibbu al-muṭṭahhirīn).

Here taṭahhur is linked to a masjid founded upon taqwa. Purity is not only washing the body; it also relates to taqwa, intent, rightful place, and distance from nifaq.

Purified Azwāj in the Next Life

In the description of the garden in 2:25, the Qur’an says: وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا أَزْوَاجٌ مُّطَهَّرَةٌ وَهُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ (wa-lahum fīhā azwājun muṭahharatun wa-hum fīhā khālidūn).

مُّطَهَّرَةٌ (muṭahharah) means purified and clean. In the next-life setting, azwāj muṭahharah are counterparts purified from worldly, ethical, bodily, or spiritual pollution. The expression should not be reduced to earthly or gendered imagery.

The Book and the Purified

In 56:77-79, the Qur’an says: إِنَّهُ لَقُرْآنٌ كَرِيمٌ فِي كِتَابٍ مَّكْنُونٍ لَّا يَمَسُّهُ إِلَّا الْمُطَهَّرُونَ (innahu la-qur'ānun karīmun fī kitābin maknūnin lā yamassuhu illā al-muṭahharūn).

The immediate setting is the noble Qur’an in a guarded Book. The verse should not be rushed into only a later legal ruling about touching the written muṣḥaf. In its own setting, it speaks of a pure and protected level that none touches except the purified.

Purifying Water

In 25:48, the Qur’an says: وَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً طَهُورًا (wa-anzalnā mina al-samā'i mā'an ṭahūran).

طَهُورًا (ṭahūran) is water that is itself pure and able to purify. This use shows that purity is not only a state; something may carry purifying capacity. Water here is a means of purity, life, and mercy.

Distinguishing Qur’anic Uses

In 2:222, purity concerns the end of menstruation and return to rightful relation. In 5:6, purity is tied to readiness for salat and removal of hardship. In 74:4, clothing is purified. In 2:125 and 22:26, the House of worship is purified.

In 11:78, purity concerns relation and ethical conduct. In 33:53, hearts are purified. In 33:33, purification from rijs is central. In 9:103, sadaqah purifies and causes growth. In 56:79, the purified are mentioned in relation to the noble Qur’an and the guarded Book.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In حَتَّىٰ يَطْهُرْنَ (ḥattā yaṭhurna), the verb is intransitive and points to becoming pure. In فَإِذَا تَطَهَّرْنَ (fa-idhā taṭahharna), Form V points to self-purification or completion of purity.

In لِيُطَهِّرَكُمْ (li-yuṭahhirakum), Form II is used and the subject is The God. In يُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًا (yuṭahhirakum taṭhīran), the verbal noun after the verb intensifies the meaning. In مَاءً طَهُورًا (mā'an ṭahūran), ṭahūr describes water and carries both purity and purifying capacity.

Difference From Nearby Roots

Ṭ H R is close to Z K W, but they are not the same. Ṭahārah emphasizes removing impurity and arriving at purity; zakat and tazkiyah add the sense of sound growth and increase after cleansing.

Ṭ H R is not the same as GH S L. Ghusl is washing; ṭahārah is the result of becoming pure or being made pure. Washing may be a means of purity, but purity is not merely washing.

Ṭ H R stands opposite R J S and N J S. Rijs and najas concern pollution and impurity; taṭhīr is the removal of pollution and making pure. Ṭ H R also differs from Q D S. Quds points to higher sanctity, inviolability, and transcendence, while ṭahārah more directly concerns being cleansed from impurity.

Forqan Translation Method

In Forqan translation, Ṭ H R should not be limited to wudu or later legal purity. Sometimes bodily purity is in view; sometimes purity of a place of worship, purity of the heart, purity of relation and ethics, purification from rijs, or water that is pure and purifying.

The distinction between becoming pure and making pure should be preserved where possible. يَطْهُرْنَ (yaṭhurna) is becoming pure; يُطَهِّرَكُمْ (yuṭahhirakum) is making you pure; تَطَهَّرُوا (taṭahharū) is seeking purity or purifying oneself; مُطَهَّرَة (muṭahharah) is purified and clean; and طَهُور (ṭahūr) is pure and purifying.

Translation Suggestions

  • For حَتَّىٰ يَطْهُرْنَ (ḥattā yaṭhurna): until they become pure; or: until that state of harm ends and they become pure.
  • For فَإِذَا تَطَهَّرْنَ (fa-idhā taṭahharna): then when they have purified themselves; or: when they have completed their purity.
  • For يُحِبُّ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ (yuḥibbu al-mutaṭahhirīn): He loves those who seek purity.
  • For لِيُطَهِّرَكُمْ (li-yuṭahhirakum): to purify you.
  • For وَثِيَابَكَ فَطَهِّرْ (wa-thiyābaka fa-ṭahhir): purify your clothing.
  • For هُنَّ أَطْهَرُ لَكُمْ (hunna aṭharu lakum): they are purer for you; or in context: this path is purer for you.
  • For مَاءً طَهُورًا (mā'an ṭahūran): purifying water; or: water that is pure and purifying.
  • For أَزْوَاجٌ مُّطَهَّرَةٌ (azwājun muṭahharah): purified azwāj; or: purified and clean counterparts.
  • For لَّا يَمَسُّهُ إِلَّا الْمُطَهَّرُونَ (lā yamassuhu illā al-muṭahharūn): none touches it except the purified.

Summary

The root Ṭ H R in the Qur’an points to purity and cleansing from impurity. The impurity may be bodily, tied to clothing or place, tied to relation and ethics, tied to heart and inward state, tied to wealth and society, or tied to rijs and the pure realm of the guarded Book.

The main difference between ṭahārah and ghusl is that ghusl is washing, while ṭahārah is the result of purity and removal of impurity. The difference from tazkiyah is that tazkiyah includes growth and flourishing after cleansing. In brief: Ṭ H R in the Qur’an means removing impurity and reaching purity; it begins with body, clothing, and place, but extends to heart, relation, wealth, society, the House of worship, purifying water, and spiritual purity.
