Core meaning: beauty, adornment, and making something beautiful, whether through its own qualities, added adornment, or making it appear attractive to someone. In thought and conduct, appearing attractive does not necessarily mean being good or right.

1. The root and its meaning in early Arabic

Z Y N concerns beauty, adornment, attractiveness, and something being pleasing.

Lexical usage contrasts al-zayn with al-shayn: beauty or a pleasing quality against a blemish, ugliness, or something detracting from beauty.

Zāna al-shayʾa means making something beautiful or adding to its beauty.

Zayyana al-shayʾa likewise means adorning or beautifying something.

Tazayyana means becoming adorned or adorning oneself.

Al-zīnah denotes adornment or what provides beauty.

To understand pre-Qur’anic usage, dictionary definitions alone are insufficient: evidence attributed to that period must also be examined.

A pre-Islamic witness: a woman’s hair in Imruʾ al-Qays’s poetry

The celebrated Muʿallaqah attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Imruʾ al-Qays describes a woman:

وَفَرْعٍ يَزِينُ الْمَتْنَ أَسْوَدَ فَاحِمٍ
أَثِيثٍ كَقِنْوِ النَّخْلَةِ الْمُتَعَثْكِلِ

wa-farʿin yazīnu al-matna aswada fāḥimin / athīthin ka-qinwi al-nakhlati al-mutaʿathkil

Translation:

“And hair adorning her back, black and coal-dark, thick as a laden, densely clustered palm bunch.”

Farʿ here means the hair of the head.

Al-matn is the back.

Yazīnu describes the hair adding beauty to her back as it falls upon it.

The poet is not discussing gold, jewels, or artificial ornaments: her natural hair supplies the beauty.

This early witness shows that Z Y N was not restricted to wearing jewelry, special clothing, or added ornaments. A natural feature can itself beautify something.

Yazīnu expresses the poet’s perception of visible beauty here, rather than deception, concealing a defect, or making something ugly appear beautiful.

The verse belongs to the transmitted Muʿallaqāt and Imruʾ al-Qays’s collected poetry. Because pre-Islamic poetry survives through later transmission, it is evidence attributed to that period rather than a contemporary dated inscription. Its established transmission nevertheless supports historical lexical study.

Recorded Arabic usages

The lexical family extends beyond natural beauty.

زَانَهُ الْحُسْنُ

zānahu al-ḥusn

Meaning: “Beauty adorned him.”

زَيَّنَ الشَّيْءَ تَزْيِينًا

zayyana al-shayʾa tazyīnan

Meaning: “He adorned and beautified the thing.”

تَزَيَّنَتِ الْأَرْضُ بِالنَّبَاتِ

tazayyanati al-arḍu bi-l-nabāt

Meaning: “The earth became adorned with vegetation.”

Plant growth gives the earth beauty and splendor.

ازْدَانَ

izdāna

This also means “became adorned.”

Tazayyana, izdāna, and izzayyana overlap in describing adornment but differ morphologically.

Their dictionary attestation does not alone establish the precise date of each usage.

A historical definition

The poetic witness and recorded usages support the following:

Z Y N fundamentally concerns beauty, adornment, and what makes a person, thing, or condition more attractive.

Beauty may be natural, as with hair enhancing someone’s appearance.

It may arise through added clothing, jewelry, or grooming.

It may also concern conduct or an idea becoming attractive in someone’s eyes.

The first two concern familiar visible beauty. The third is frequent in the Qur’an, but dating its origin or development requires independent historical evidence.

There is no basis here for claiming that the Qur’an first introduced the sense of making conduct and ideas seem attractive.

2. Important forms and derivatives

زَانَ يَزِينُ — zāna, yazīnu

The family’s basic Form I verb.

It means to beautify, adorn, or add beauty.

زَانَهُ الْحُسْنُ

zānahu al-ḥusn

Meaning: “Beauty adorned him.”

This is the branch illustrated by yazīnu in Imruʾ al-Qays’s verse.

The Form I verb zāna–yazīnu does not occur in the Qur’an.

زَيْن — zayn

Beauty, comeliness, or adornment.

The expression “الزَّيْنُ خِلَافُ الشَّيْنِ” contrasts it with ugliness and blemish.

Zayn can denote beauty itself or a quality contributing beauty.

زِينَة — zīnah

A noun denoting adornment, beauty, or something that beautifies.

It is not the name of one particular object.

It may concern clothing and jewelry, a natural feature, or more broadly the attractions of life.

The Qur’an uses it for human adornment, the earth, the sky, worldly life, possessions, and social display.

زَيَّنَ يُزَيِّنُ — zayyana, yuzayyinu

A Form II verb.

It means to adorn, beautify, or make something seem attractive to someone.

This verb is frequent in the Qur’an.

Its object may be the sky and creation’s visible beauty.

It may be faith becoming pleasing in the heart.

It may also be wrongful conduct, associating partners, deception, or a harmful desire made attractive.

Zayyana alone does not establish whether something is truly good or only appears so.

The agent, object, and context determine that distinction.

تَزْيِين — tazyīn

The verbal noun of zayyana.

It describes adorning, beautifying, or making something appear beautiful.

This verbal noun is known in Arabic but does not occur in the Qur’an in this nominal form.

زُيِّنَ — zuyyina

The passive past of Form II.

It means “was adorned” or “was made attractive.”

For example:

زُيِّنَ لِلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا

zuyyina li-lladhīna kafarū al-ḥayātu al-dunyā

Al-Baqarah 2:212

Meaning: “Worldly life was made attractive to those who disbelieved.”

The agent of adorning is not named in this construction.

The passive does not justify identifying that agent as The God, Satan, or the person themselves without evidence.

تَزَيَّنَ يَتَزَيَّنُ — tazayyana, yatazayyanu

A Form V verb meaning to become adorned or adorn oneself.

It is familiar in general Arabic usage.

ازْدَانَ — izdāna

It means to become adorned or beautiful.

It belongs to Z Y N and Form VIII. The formation involves sound changes, including replacement of the form’s tāʾ with dāl beside zāy; its medial weak consonant also changes.

Izdāna and tazayyana may share the sense of becoming adorned, but their structures differ.

ازَّيَّنَتْ — izzayyanat

This belongs morphologically to Form V and tazayyanat.

The tāʾ assimilates into zāy, and an initial connecting hamzah permits pronunciation when beginning with the word, producing izzayyanat.

Yūnus 10:24 uses this form for the earth.

Forms actually found in the Qur’an

The Qur’anic forms fall into three main groups:

  • Form II verbs, including zayyannā, zayyana, zayyanū, zuyyina, and zayyanahu.
  • The Form V inflection izzayyanat, “became adorned.”
  • The noun zīnah with different cases and attached pronouns, including zīnatakum, zīnatahunna, zīnatihi, and bi-zīnatin.

Form I, the verbal noun tazyīn, and other derivatives exist in Arabic but must not be presented as attested Qur’anic forms.

3. Beauty itself and beauty in human perception

Three situations should be distinguished.

Beauty in the thing itself

Something may be beautiful through its own features.

A starry sky, vegetation, or natural human hair may possess beauty.

Adornment does not necessarily mean adding an artificial covering.

Beauty may arise through form, color, proportion, or a natural quality.

Beauty produced by adornment

Something may be added to a person or object to adorn it.

Clothes, jewelry, grooming, and decorative objects are clear examples.

This does not inherently mean deception.

A beautiful garment may truly enhance someone’s appearance.

Something appearing beautiful in human perception

Sometimes the subject is human appraisal and liking rather than visible beauty itself.

Someone may act wrongly while regarding the action as right, useful, or admirable.

Perceived attractiveness is then distinct from actual rightness.

Fāṭir 35:8 illustrates this through zuyyina alongside fa-raʾāhu ḥasanan.

Conduct described as bad may appear good to its perpetrator.

The root cannot therefore be restricted to deception: many Qur’anic uses concern actual beauty.

Nor should everything described by this root automatically be considered truly good or right.

Beauty and adornment are the shared field; context determines their relation to truth, moral value, and human judgment.

4. Beauty in creation: sky, earth, and living beings

Adorning the sky in Al-Ḥijr 15:16

وَلَقَدْ جَعَلْنَا فِي السَّمَاءِ بُرُوجًا وَزَيَّنَّاهَا لِلنَّاظِرِينَ

wa-laqad jaʿalnā fī al-samāʾi burūjan wa-zayyannāhā li-l-nāẓirīn

Al-Ḥijr 15:16

Translation:

“We have placed constellations in the sky and adorned it for those who look.”

Zayyannāhā is a past Form II verb.

The God is the agent, and the pronoun refers to the sky.

Li-l-nāẓirīn highlights the observer’s perception of beauty.

This concerns the sky’s adornment, not making wrongful conduct attractive.

Al-Naml 27:60 describes gardens possessing bahjah. Although the root differs, both passages connect creation’s visible beauty with human attention.

A sky adorned with stars in Al-Ṣāffāt 37:6

إِنَّا زَيَّنَّا السَّمَاءَ الدُّنْيَا بِزِينَةٍ الْكَوَاكِبِ

innā zayyannā al-samāʾa al-dunyā bi-zīnatin al-kawākib

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:6

Translation:

“We adorned the nearer sky with an adornment: the stars.”

Both zayyannā and zīnah belong to this root.

The verb expresses the act of adorning.

The noun identifies what adorns the sky.

In the Ḥafṣ reading used here, bi-zīnatin has nunation and al-kawākibi is genitive, analyzable as an appositional substitute or explanatory apposition. Another reading has the construct phrase zīnati al-kawākib. Both associate the sky’s adornment with the stars, though their syntax differs.

Fuṣṣilat 41:12 and Al-Mulk 67:5 describe the nearer sky’s adornment through “lamps.”

Qāf 50:6 also speaks of building and adorning the sky.

Together these passages illustrate Z Y N describing creation’s actual beauty.

Beauty does not itself imply uselessness: other functions may be discussed alongside it.

Fuṣṣilat 41:12 places adornment and protection in the same setting. Their meanings differ, but beauty and a protective function can accompany one another.

The root alone does not establish a physical theory of the heavens or the scientific nature of every celestial body.

The earth becoming adorned in Yūnus 10:24

Yūnus 10:24 compares worldly life with vegetation growing and the earth flourishing.

Water and plant growth give the earth its adornment and splendor.

The expression wa-izzayyanat appears in this setting.

It is the assimilated Form V inflection izzayyanat.

It means “became adorned” or “became beautiful.”

The earth is the subject becoming adorned, unlike passages where “We adorned” attributes the action to The God.

Zukhrufahā also occurs before izzayyanat.

The two belong to related fields of beauty but remain distinct expressions.

Zukhruf may emphasize striking splendor, whereas izzayyanat describes the earth becoming adorned.

The condition is also unstable.

The earth flourishes and its people imagine they control its yield, but the following words describe the divine command arriving and the condition changing.

That instability comes from the full context, not because zīnah lexically means disappearance or destruction.

Living creatures, adornment, and human benefit in Al-Naḥl 16:8

Al-Naḥl 16:8 associates horses, mules, and donkeys with riding and adornment.

Wa-zīnatan concerns the beauty and distinction they can bring to human life.

The verse joins practical benefit with adornment.

Zīnah does not itself mean riding.

Riding is expressed by li-tarkabūhā; adornment has its own word.

Beauty and usefulness can coexist without becoming identical lexical meanings.

5. Human adornment: clothing, jewelry, and appearance

Adornment in Al-Aʿrāf 7:31

يَا بَنِي آدَمَ خُذُوا زِينَتَكُمْ عِنْدَ كُلِّ مَسْجِدٍ وَكُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا وَلَا تُسْرِفُوا إِنَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ الْمُسْرِفِينَ

yā banī Ādama khudhū zīnatakum ʿinda kulli masjidin wa-kulū wa-shrabū wa-lā tusrifū innahu lā yuḥibbu al-musrifīn

Al-Aʿrāf 7:31

Translation:

“Children of Adam, take your adornment at every place of worship, and eat and drink, but do not be excessive; He does not love the excessive.”

Zīnatakum is the noun with a second-person plural possessive pronoun.

Khudhū commands the addressees to take or wear their adornment.

Human adornment can generally include clothing, a clean appearance, and suitable ornaments.

The root specifies no particular garment, color, fabric, or style.

“At every place of worship” must be examined within the command’s context and the meaning of masjid. Zīnah alone cannot supply every detail of worship attire.

Eating and drinking follow, together with prohibition of excess.

These commands show that adornment and enjoying provisions are not inherently condemned.

Who prohibited divine adornment? Al-Aʿrāf 7:32

قُلْ مَنْ حَرَّمَ زِينَةَ اللَّهِ الَّتِي أَخْرَجَ لِعِبَادِهِ وَالطَّيِّبَاتِ مِنَ الرِّزْقِ قُلْ هِيَ لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا خَالِصَةً يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ كَذَٰلِكَ نُفَصِّلُ الْآيَاتِ لِقَوْمٍ يَعْلَمُونَ

qul man ḥarrama zīnata Allāhi allatī akhraja li-ʿibādihi wa-l-ṭayyibāti mina al-rizqi qul hiya li-lladhīna āmanū fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā khāliṣatan yawma al-qiyāmati kadhālika nufaṣṣilu al-āyāti li-qawmin yaʿlamūn

Al-Aʿrāf 7:32

Translation:

“Say: Who has prohibited The God’s adornment which He brought forth for His servants, and good things of provision? Say: They are for those who believe in worldly life, exclusively theirs on the day of resurrection. Thus We explain the signs for people who know.”

Zīnata Allāh refers to adornment The God has brought forth for His servants.

It recognizes human enjoyment of beauty among divine gifts.

“Who has prohibited?” raises a question of authority: who may prohibit in His name what He has provided?

Adornment is not intrinsically a sign of worldliness, corruption, or lack of reverence.

Restrictions concerning excess, injustice, boasting, or other conduct require their own evidence.

Someone may own expensive jewelry without using it to humiliate others, while another may boast using something inexpensive.

An object’s cost or material does not alone determine its owner’s ethics.

Nor does this verse independently settle every clothing and grooming question; conduct must be examined through the relevant passages and commands.

Women’s adornment in Al-Nūr 24:31

Zīnatahunna occurs twice and zīnatihinna once in Al-Nūr 24:31.

These occurrences accompany instructions about revealing adornment and its limits.

First comes not revealing adornment except what is apparent.

Then specified groups receive a different treatment concerning its disclosure.

Finally, an action making hidden adornment known is prohibited.

Lexically, zīnah refers here to women’s beauty and adornment.

The root alone does not determine whether all instances are only gold and jewels, or whether every natural bodily beauty falls under precisely the same ruling.

Scope requires examining accompanying words, exceptions, sentence structure, and related clothing passages.

The clause about moving the feet mentions “what they conceal of their adornment,” showing that hidden adornment can become known through conduct.

This fits ornaments made perceptible through movement, but a plausible example must not become an exclusive definition without evidence.

Al-Nūr 24:60 also has ghayra mutabarrijātin bi-zīnatin.

It connects adornment with the behavior of displaying it.

Zīnah is not the name of that behavior; tabarruj belongs to B R J and requires separate study.

A root study clarifies the semantic field but cannot derive an entire clothing ruling from this lexical definition alone.

6. Worldly adornment: gifts, attraction, and testing

Worldly life and what attracts people to it form an important Qur’anic field of Z Y N.

What is on the earth adorns it

Al-Kahf 18:7 states:

إِنَّا جَعَلْنَا مَا عَلَى الْأَرْضِ زِينَةً لَهَا لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ أَيُّهُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا

innā jaʿalnā mā ʿalā al-arḍi zīnatan lahā li-nabluwahum ayyuhum aḥsanu ʿamalan

Al-Kahf 18:7

Translation:

“We made what is on the earth an adornment for it, to test them as to which of them is best in deeds.”

Zīnatan concerns the earth’s adornment.

The following words also state the purpose of testing.

Adornment can form part of circumstances in which human conduct and choice become apparent; it does not lexically mean testing.

“Best in deeds” introduces another criterion: the quality of human conduct.

Beauty or material advantage should not be equated with doing good.

Al-Kahf 18:8 speaks of making what is on the earth barren ground.

Present beauty, however real and attractive, is not necessarily enduring.

Its finitude comes from context, not from the word zīnah itself.

Wealth and sons as worldly adornment

الْمَالُ وَالْبَنُونَ زِينَةُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَالْبَاقِيَاتُ الصَّالِحَاتُ خَيْرٌ عِنْدَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًا وَخَيْرٌ أَمَلًا

al-mālu wa-l-banūna zīnatu al-ḥayāti al-dunyā wa-l-bāqiyātu al-ṣāliḥātu khayrun ʿinda rabbika thawāban wa-khayrun amalan

Al-Kahf 18:46

Translation:

“Wealth and sons are the adornment of worldly life; enduring good deeds are better with your Lord in reward and better as a source of hope.”

Al-Kahf 18:46

Wealth and sons, the literal sense of al-banūn, are described as worldly adornment. Their family appeal can be discussed more broadly, but that generalization should remain distinct from the noun’s precise wording.

This neither declares wealth and children bad nor guarantees a good life to everyone possessing them.

It recognizes their attraction and value in life, then introduces enduring good deeds as another measure.

Zīnah here does not mean legal ownership, parental responsibility, or children’s ultimate destiny.

Those questions belong to other subjects.

Life’s adornment and human choice

Hūd 11:15 describes people seeking worldly life and its adornment.

Al-Kahf 18:28 contrasts attention to worldly adornment with keeping company with those calling upon their Lord.

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:60 describes what people receive as worldly enjoyment and adornment.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:28 places worldly life and adornment within a choice offered to the Prophet’s wives.

Al-Ḥadīd 57:20 lists adornment alongside play, diversion, boasting, and rivalry in wealth and children.

Adornment can attract human attention, desire, and effort.

Its mention beside boasting does not make every form of adornment boasting.

Nor do these passages establish that worldly life is wholly false or worthless.

It can be a setting for enjoyment, good deeds, testing, responsibility, and choice.

Criticism concerns reversed values, treating display and advantage as the ultimate measure of worth, or violating others’ rights to obtain them.

Worldly life in the eyes of disbelievers

Al-Baqarah 2:212 states:

زُيِّنَ لِلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا وَيَسْخَرُونَ مِنَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَالَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا فَوْقَهُمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ وَاللَّهُ يَرْزُقُ مَنْ يَشَاءُ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ

zuyyina li-lladhīna kafarū al-ḥayātu al-dunyā wa-yaskharūna mina alladhīna āmanū wa-lladhīna ittaqaw fawqahum yawma al-qiyāmati wa-Allāhu yarzuqu man yashāʾu bi-ghayri ḥisāb

Al-Baqarah 2:212

Translation:

“Worldly life was made attractive to those who disbelieved, and they mock those who believe. Those who are reverent will be above them on the day of resurrection; and The God provides for whom He wills without reckoning.”

Zuyyina is passive.

The verse describes worldly life appearing attractive to a particular group.

It then describes their mockery of believers.

The issue is not merely beauty existing in the world, but human appraisal of life and other people.

Excessive attachment to wealth, position, or display may lead someone to despise another who lacks those advantages.

This does not establish that all disbelievers enjoy genuine prosperity or that The God has made worldly life their paradise.

An attractive appearance differs from the actual goodness of every aspect of a condition.

This distinction is examined more fully in the Forqan study “Trials and the Promise of a Good Life in the Qur’an: Is This World a Prison for Believers?”

Love of desired things in Āl ʿImrān 3:14

Āl ʿImrān 3:14 contains zuyyina li-l-nāsi ḥubbu al-shahawāt.

The grammatical point is that ḥubbu al-shahawāt, love of desired things, is the passive verb’s subject.

Examples of those desires and advantages follow.

The verse concerns their attraction, but zuyyina does not itself mean they are forbidden.

They are called worldly enjoyment, while the best return is with The God.

Having a gift, loving it, and making it life’s highest aim must remain distinct.

7. Making conduct attractive: adornment and mistaken judgment

Many verses connect zayyana with human conduct, beliefs, or decisions.

Here it chiefly means making something appear pleasing.

When bad conduct looks good

Fāṭir 35:8 has zuyyina lahu sūʾu ʿamalihi fa-raʾāhu ḥasanan.

Sūʾu ʿamalihi identifies bad conduct.

Zuyyina says it was made attractive to the person.

Fa-raʾāhu ḥasanan gives the result: the person saw it as good.

This clearly distinguishes an act’s reality from its perpetrator’s judgment.

Someone may commit an error yet rationalize and approve of it.

Zayyana here does not lexically mean the sin, injustice, or corruption itself; it describes making the act attractive.

The act’s badness comes from sūʾu ʿamalihi and the context.

Muḥammad 47:14 likewise describes bad conduct being made attractive.

Ghāfir 40:37 applies the expression to Pharaoh.

Al-Raʿd 13:33 describes disbelievers’ plotting being made attractive to them.

Al-Tawbah 9:37 does so for bad conduct in manipulating the sacred months.

Finding one’s conduct pleasing is not evidence that it is right.

Human conduct in passive constructions

Al-Anʿām 6:122 describes disbelievers’ deeds being made attractive.

Yūnus 10:12 applies the expression to the excessive.

Al-Baqarah 2:212 and Āl ʿImrān 3:14 also use the passive with different subjects.

Al-Fatḥ 48:12 describes a false assumption being made attractive in the addressees’ hearts.

The verbs share a construction, but what is made attractive differs.

It may be worldly life.

It may be love of desired things.

It may be someone’s deeds.

It may be an assumption formed in the heart.

Translating all these as “Satan deceived them” adds an unwarranted agent and is inaccurate.

The passive does not name the agent. Evidence about agency elsewhere in the passage or other verses requires separate examination.

Satan making deeds attractive

Al-Anʿām 6:43 says Satan made people’s conduct attractive to them.

Al-Anfāl 8:48 describes Satan adorning a group’s deeds and encouraging expectations of victory.

Al-Naḥl 16:63 attributes the adornment of earlier communities’ conduct to Satan.

In Al-Naml 27:24, the hoopoe reports that Sheba’s people prostrate to the sun and Satan has made their conduct attractive.

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:38 likewise mentions Satan adorning the deeds of ʿĀd and Thamūd.

These verses explicitly identify the agent.

Unlike unspecified passive constructions, they expressly attribute the adornment of conduct to Satan.

Making something attractive does not itself mean compelling a person.

In Al-Anfāl 8:48, Satan withdraws after adorning their conduct and promising victory.

The context preserves a distinction between Satan’s invitation and deception and human decision and responsibility.

Iblīs’s declaration in Al-Ḥijr 15:39

In Al-Ḥijr 15:39, Iblīs says:

قَالَ رَبِّ بِمَا أَغْوَيْتَنِي لَأُزَيِّنَنَّ لَهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَأُغْوِيَنَّهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ

qāla rabbi bi-mā aghwaytanī la-uzayyinanna lahum fī al-arḍi wa-la-ughwiyannahum ajmaʿīn

Al-Ḥijr 15:39

Translation:

“He said: My Lord, because You caused me to go astray, I will surely make things attractive to them on earth, and I will surely mislead them all.” The object of making attractive is not explicitly named in the Arabic clause.

La-uzayyinanna is a first-person imperfect verb reinforced by emphatic lām and the heavy emphatic nūn.

Iblīs declares his intention to make things attractive.

This is his quoted speech. His attribution of the cause of his own misguidance must not automatically be treated as an independent Qur’anic judgment.

Al-Ḥijr 15:40 then states an exception concerning the chosen servants.

La-uzayyinanna expresses the undertaking to adorn; la-ughwiyannahum expresses misleading.

Even in Iblīs’s speech, the verbs remain distinct.

Making something attractive may be a means of misleading, but tazyīn and ighwāʾ are not exact lexical synonyms.

Alleged partners and misleading companions

In the Ḥafṣ reading of Al-Anʿām 6:137 used here, shurakāʾuhum is the agent making the killing of children attractive to many polytheists.

The conduct is grave, yet appears pleasing under the influence of their alleged partners.

Mental attractiveness can accompany ethically devastating conduct.

Fuṣṣilat 41:25 mentions companions who make things attractive to those with whom they associate.

The agent is therefore not always explicitly called Satan.

Alleged partners, companions, or other influential agents may be specified.

This does not make everyone who agrees with or encourages a person a misleader.

The act, its actual character, and the agent’s role require independent examination.

8. Divine adornment: creation, faith, and divine action

The Qur’an sometimes attributes zayyana directly to The God.

This occurs in different contexts.

Adorning creation

Al-Ḥijr 15:16, Al-Ṣāffāt 37:6, Fuṣṣilat 41:12, Qāf 50:6, and Al-Mulk 67:5 attribute the sky’s adornment to The God.

These concern creation’s beauty.

They do not describe making sin attractive.

Adorning faith in the heart

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:7 states:

وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ فِيكُمْ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ لَوْ يُطِيعُكُمْ فِي كَثِيرٍ مِنَ الْأَمْرِ لَعَنِتُّمْ وَلَكِنَّ اللَّهَ حَبَّبَ إِلَيْكُمُ الْإِيمَانَ وَزَيَّنَهُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ وَكَرَّهَ إِلَيْكُمُ الْكُفْرَ وَالْفُسُوقَ وَالْعِصْيَانَ أُولَئِكَ هُمُ الرَّاشِدُونَ

wa-ʿlamū anna fīkum rasūla Allāhi law yuṭīʿukum fī kathīrin mina al-amri la-ʿanittum wa-lākinna Allāha ḥabbaba ilaykumu al-īmāna wa-zayyanahu fī qulūbikum wa-karraha ilaykumu al-kufra wa-l-fusūqa wa-l-ʿiṣyāna ulāʾika humu al-rāshidūn

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:7

Translation:

“Know that The God’s messenger is among you. If he obeyed you in many matters, you would suffer hardship. But The God made faith beloved to you and adorned it in your hearts, and made disbelief, defiance, and disobedience hateful to you. Those are the rightly guided.”

Zayyanahu is attributed to The God.

Its object pronoun refers to faith.

The setting is “in your hearts.”

The object is therefore neither sky, clothing, nor jewelry, but faith becoming attractive within a person.

The sentence also uses ḥabbaba.

Ḥabbaba means making beloved; zayyanahu means adorning.

They are related but not necessarily identical in meaning.

Faith can be loved and perceived as beautiful in the heart.

Here adornment connects with sound guidance.

Tazyīn cannot therefore always mean deception or deviation.

Adorning communities’ deeds in Al-Anʿām 6:108

After prohibiting abuse of those whom others invoke apart from The God, Al-Anʿām 6:108 states:

كَذَٰلِكَ زَيَّنَّا لِكُلِّ أُمَّةٍ عَمَلَهُمْ

kadhālika zayyannā li-kulli ummatin ʿamalahum

Al-Anʿām 6:108

Thus We made each community’s conduct attractive to it.

The God is the agent of zayyannā.

The object is ʿamalahum, each community’s conduct.

The context concerns communal beliefs and conduct, and the danger of provoking reciprocal hostility and abuse.

People may regard their own community’s deeds and beliefs as attractive even when others judge them wrong.

This does not establish that The God declares every community’s actions morally right or beloved to Him.

It describes deeds being made attractive in relation to each community; their ultimate truth or falsehood is another question.

Adorning deeds in Al-Naml 27:4

Al-Naml 27:4 likewise says The God made the deeds of those disbelieving in the hereafter attractive to them.

It shares divine agency with Al-Ḥujurāt 49:7, but the objects and outcomes differ.

In Al-Ḥujurāt, faith is adorned and sound guidance is described.

In Al-Naml, the deeds of those disbelieving in the hereafter are adorned, followed by their wandering in confusion.

Divine agency does not by itself imply moral approval of everything adorned.

These passages require distinguishing divine action and the created order, human orientation and choice, and the act’s moral value.

Their relationship must be studied through guidance, misguidance, responsibility, and choice passages; Z Y N does not itself mean guidance or misguidance.

9. Adornment in social and historical scenes

The day of adornment in Ṭā Hā 20:59

قَالَ مَوْعِدُكُمْ يَوْمُ الزِّينَةِ وَأَنْ يُحْشَرَ النَّاسُ ضُحًى

qāla mawʿidukum yawmu al-zīnati wa-an yuḥshara al-nāsu ḍuḥan

Ṭā Hā 20:59

Translation:

“He said: Your appointment is the day of adornment, and that the people be gathered in the morning light.”

Yawm al-zīnah names or describes the day selected for a public gathering.

The expression is associated with a festival, adornment, and public attendance.

It does not independently establish the precise festival’s historical name or ceremonial details.

The context emphasizes people gathering and the event’s visibility.

Zīnah is part of the description of a public occasion, rather than one individual’s adornment.

The people’s adornment in Ṭā Hā 20:87

Ṭā Hā 20:87 contains zīnati al-qawm.

It speaks of burdens taken from the people’s adornment and then cast down.

In context these are material ornaments.

Zīnah can thus refer to actual portable objects.

The word itself does not mean gold, silver, or a particular ornament.

Material and shape require independent evidence.

Pharaoh’s adornment in Yūnus 10:88

In Yūnus 10:88, Moses’s prayer mentions adornment and wealth The God gave Pharaoh and his chiefs in worldly life.

Zīnatan appears alongside amwālan, wealth.

They may be related, but remain two expressions.

Adornment can concern powerful people’s visible splendor.

The prayer continues with misleading and departure from The God’s way.

That significance comes from the prayer’s full context.

Zīnah itself does not mean oppression, political power, or misguidance.

Qārūn and his adornment in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:79

فَخَرَجَ عَلَىٰ قَوْمِهِ فِي زِينَتِهِ قَالَ الَّذِينَ يُرِيدُونَ الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا يَا لَيْتَ لَنَا مِثْلَ مَا أُوتِيَ قَارُونُ إِنَّهُ لَذُو حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍ

fa-kharaja ʿalā qawmihi fī zīnatihi qāla alladhīna yurīdūna al-ḥayāta al-dunyā yā layta lanā mithla mā ūtiya Qārūnu innahu la-dhū ḥaẓẓin ʿaẓīm

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:79

Translation:

“He came out before his people in his adornment. Those who desired worldly life said: If only we had the like of what Qārūn was given! He certainly possesses a great share.”

Zīnatihi describes Qārūn’s adornment and outward splendor.

The verse then reports spectators’ responses.

Some wish to possess similar wealth.

Adornment can affect social judgments about success and personal worth.

The noun itself does not mean wrongful accumulation or arrogance.

Qārūn’s conduct requires examining the rest of his story and statements.

The spectators’ reaction shows adornment’s effect in a specific situation without changing its lexical meaning.

10. Comparison with neighboring roots

Z Y N (zayn) and Ḥ S N (ḥusn)

Ḥusn concerns goodness or beauty and may describe appearance, conduct, speech, or moral value.

Zayn concerns beauty and adornment, but can describe wrongful behavior being made attractive.

Fāṭir 35:8 clearly illustrates their relationship.

Bad conduct is made attractive, and the person sees it as good.

Zayyana can describe becoming attractive, while ḥasan in that sentence expresses the judgment the person makes about their conduct.

This is a contextual distinction, not a universal boundary across all Arabic usages.

Z Y N (zīnah) and J M L (jamāl)

Jamāl denotes beauty and pleasing appearance.

Al-Naḥl 16:6 describes livestock through “you have beauty in them.”

Al-Naḥl 16:8 uses wa-zīnatan concerning horses, mules, and donkeys.

Both belong to visible beauty and attractiveness.

Zīnah also names things that adorn, while zayyana occurs in contexts of belief and conduct.

Jamāl must likewise be studied through its own usages.

Their difference does not mean jamāl is always natural and zīnah always artificial.

The poetic witness and the sky’s adornment do not support that boundary.

Z Y N (zīnah) and Ḥ L Y (ḥilyah)

Ḥ L Y concerns ornaments and adorning through decorative objects.

The Qur’an mentions actual ornaments such as bracelets.

Zīnah has a broader field and need not name an ornament.

The sky’s adornment, worldly attractions, and faith’s beauty in the heart cannot all be reduced to wearable jewelry.

Where portable objects are described, as in Ṭā Hā 20:87, zīnah can approximate material ornaments.

Context determines the distinction.

Z Y N (zīnah) and Z KH R F (zukhruf)

Zukhruf concerns splendor, adornment, and striking decoration.

Some usages describe material ornament and luxury.

Yūnus 10:24 places zukhruf and izzayyanat together in describing the earth.

They are related within the field of beauty and splendor.

This does not establish that zukhruf always means deception and zīnah always truth, or the reverse.

Each object and context requires examination.

Z Y N (zīnah) and B H J (bahjah)

Bahjah concerns delightful beauty and pleasing appearance.

Al-Naml 27:60 describes gardens as dhāta bahjatin.

This concerns natural beauty.

Zīnah covers that field but also human ornaments and conduct or belief becoming attractive.

No fixed boundary for every usage follows from this difference.

Z Y N (zīnah) and B R J (tabarruj)

Tabarruj concerns displaying oneself; it is a Form V derivative of B R J, not a four-consonant root beginning with tāʾ.

Zīnah denotes beauty, adornment, or what provides them.

Al-Nūr 24:60 combines the two.

Something may be adornment, while displaying it is a separate behavior.

The lexical meaning of zīnah should not be equated with the nature or ruling of tabarruj.

Z Y N (zayn) and Z N Y (zinā)

Despite similar letters, their order and meanings differ.

Z Y N concerns beauty and adornment.

Z N Y concerns illicit sexual intercourse in its familiar usage.

Visual or phonetic resemblance does not establish a semantic relationship.

11. Grammar and structure

Active and passive verbs

Zayyana can explicitly identify an agent.

Some passages name The God; others name Satan or alleged partners.

Zuyyina does not name the agent in the passive construction.

Context and other evidence are needed to identify agency.

It should not automatically be attributed to Satan or The God.

Lām in adornment constructions

In “Satan made their deeds attractive to them,” lām with the pronoun identifies those to whom the conduct appears attractive.

The construction relates what is adorned to those perceiving it as beautiful.

Lām does not itself imply moral approval or ownership.

Fī in the adornment of faith

In Al-Ḥujurāt 49:7, fī qulūbikum identifies the sphere of faith’s adornment.

Adornment can concern inward inclination and preference.

Fī alone supplies no bodily or neurological mechanism.

Bāʾ in the sky’s adornment

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:6 and Fuṣṣilat 41:12 use bi-zīnatin and bi-maṣābīḥa.

Bāʾ connects adornment with its means or constituent.

Each verse’s syntax requires examination; a preposition does not mechanically perform one identical function everywhere.

The noun and the act of adorning

Zīnah can denote beauty itself or what adorns.

Zayyana is the act of adorning.

Izzayyanat describes becoming adorned.

These must not be interchanged in translation.

Zīnatu al-ḥayāti al-dunyā differs grammatically from zuyyina li-lladhīna kafarū al-ḥayātu al-dunyā.

The former calls something worldly life’s adornment.

The latter describes worldly life being made attractive to a particular group.

13. Conclusion

Z Y N concerns beauty, adornment, and beautifying.

Imruʾ al-Qays’s transmitted verse describes natural hair beautifying a woman’s back, showing that early usage was not restricted to artificial ornaments.

Lexical accounts also record natural beauty, adding ornaments, and becoming beautiful.

The root occurs 46 times in 43 Qur’anic verses, across several clear contexts.

The sky is adorned with stars.

The earth becomes beautiful with vegetation.

People benefit from clothing and adornment.

Wealth, sons, and worldly attractions are called adornment.

Faith becomes adorned and beloved in the heart.

Conversely, bad conduct may appear attractive until its perpetrator regards it as good and right.

Some verses attribute adorning to The God.

Others name Satan, alleged partners, or companions.

Others use the passive without naming the agent in the clause.

These distinctions must survive translation and analysis.

Z Y N denotes beauty and adornment, and verbally, making something beautiful or attractive. This may concern visible beauty or a person’s appraisal of conduct, belief, or a condition.

What looks attractive is not necessarily truly good; equally, not every beauty is deceptive or blameworthy.

The Qur’an recognizes creation’s beauty and permissible adornment while warning that people may be deceived by finding their own bad conduct attractive.

Forqan’s renderings should therefore select “adornment,” “adorning,” or “making attractive” according to each construction and context, without automatically equating beauty with moral goodness or adornment with deception.
