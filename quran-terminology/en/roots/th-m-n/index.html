Core Meaning

The root ث م ن, in its main semantic branch, points to price, value in exchange, and something taken in return for something else. Thaman is what stands on the opposite side of a transaction: one thing is given, and an exchange is taken in return.

In the Qur'an, this root is not merely an economic word. The Qur'an also uses thaman for moral and faithful evaluation. The core question is: what does the human being take in exchange for what? Does he exchange truth for a passing benefit? Does he lose signs, covenant, faith, or human dignity in exchange for wealth, position, social safety, or people's approval?

For this reason, thaman in the Qur'an is one of the important words for understanding wrongful exchange. When something immeasurably valuable, such as the signs of The God or the divine covenant, is placed against a worldly benefit, whatever is placed on the other side is small and insignificant in relation to that truth. This is the Qur'anic logic of ثمن قليل.

Alongside this branch, the root ث م ن also has a numerical branch: ثمانية, ثمانين, and ثامنهم, meaning eight, eighty, and their eighth. This branch is important in the Qur'an, but it must not be mixed with the meaning of price and value. Their root-level connection can be understood at the level of order, counting, and placement in a measured structure; but without clear evidence, no fixed symbolic or geometric meaning should be built for the number eight.

Important Forms

ثمن means price, value, or the exchange taken in return for something. In the Qur'an, this word mostly appears in a critical context, especially when the signs of The God, divine covenant, oaths, or truth are sold for a lower benefit.

وَلَا تَشْتَرُوا بِآيَاتِي ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا

And do not sell My signs for a small price.

Al-Baqarah 2:41

ثمن قليل is a very important Qur'anic phrase. It does not simply mean “little money.” The point is that whatever exchange a human being takes in return for truth, the signs of The God, or divine covenant is small in relation to that truth, even if it appears large and impressive from a worldly view.

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَشْتَرُونَ بِعَهْدِ اللَّهِ وَأَيْمَانِهِمْ ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا

Those who sell the covenant of The God and their oaths for a small price.

Ali 'Imran 3:77

ثمن بخس is another phrase, appearing in the story of Joseph. Here thaman is in a clear transactional meaning, but with a severe moral weight:

وَشَرَوْهُ بِثَمَنٍ بَخْسٍ دَرَاهِمَ مَعْدُودَةٍ

And they sold him for a diminished and wrongful price, a few counted dirhams.

Yusuf 12:20

ثمانية means eight:

وَيَحْمِلُ عَرْشَ رَبِّكَ فَوْقَهُمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ ثَمَانِيَةٌ

And on that Day, eight will carry the Throne of your Lord above them.

Al-Haqqah 69:17

ثمانين means eighty:

فَاجْلِدُوهُمْ ثَمَانِينَ جَلْدَةً

Then lash them eighty lashes.

Al-Nur 24:4

ثامنهم means their eighth:

وَيَقُولُونَ سَبْعَةٌ وَثَامِنُهُمْ كَلْبُهُمْ

And they say: seven, and their eighth was their dog.

Al-Kahf 18:22

In the Qur'an, the transactional usage of this root appears with the singular form ثمن. Therefore Forqan should avoid building a Qur'anic discussion on the plural أثمان, except as general Arabic explanation while making clear that this form does not appear in the verses under discussion.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of thaman in the transactional branch is exchange-value. Something is placed against something else so that the transaction becomes complete. In the market, the commodity stands on one side and the price on the other. Thaman is what is accepted in return for losing or handing over something.

But the Qur'an takes this simple market image into a deeper field. In the Qur'an, the human being does not only sell commodities. Sometimes he spends signs, sometimes covenant, sometimes oaths, sometimes he lowers human dignity, and in return he takes something that may look profitable from a worldly angle but is insignificant in truth.

For this reason, thaman in the Qur'an relates to the weighing of values. In every exchange, the issue is not only what was taken. More important is what it was taken in exchange for. If what is lost is truth, covenant, faith, or human dignity, whatever is taken in return is low in rank and reality.

Here the meaning of ثمن قليل becomes clearer. The smallness of this thaman is not always numerical or financial. A person may gain wealth, power, position, or wide influence in return for concealing truth; but because he took it in exchange for the signs of The God or the divine covenant, it is qalil in relation to that truth.

Material Image

The material image of thaman comes from the market and the scale. In a transaction, something is given from one side and its exchange is taken from the other. If the scale is sound, there is proportion between what has been handed over and what has been received.

But the Qur'an shows that not all scales are sound. Sometimes a human being places something immeasurably valuable on one side, then takes a passing benefit on the other. Outwardly, a transaction has happened; in reality, the scale is broken. What was taken has no proportion with what was lost.

The image of Joseph is very clear here. A human being with dignity and a great future is sold for a few counted dirhams. The market prices him, but that price has no proportion with his reality. For this reason the Qur'an says ثمن بخس: a diminished, wrongful price below true worth.

The same image applies to signs and covenant. When the signs of The God or divine covenant are exchanged for worldly benefit, the scale is false from the beginning, because a limited thing has been placed against a truth far beyond it.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, thaman points to the price of a commodity and the financial exchange in a transaction. The word is used in the field of buying and selling, valuation, pricing, and exchange. From the same family, numerical forms related to eight also appear, such as thamaniyah and thamanin.

Thaman and qimah are close, but not identical. Thaman is more specifically what is paid or received inside the transaction. Qimah can also point to general valuation or estimating worth. In Persian, “baha” is sometimes better than “qimat,” because it carries the transactional sense and also preserves the moral weight of the verses more naturally.

The exact relation between the numerical branch and the transactional branch should be handled carefully. One may say that both stand in the same root-level linguistic field, but one should not claim without reliable evidence that the number eight in the Qur'an always indicates a fixed geometric balance or special structure. At this stage, the numerical branch is best introduced separately, and the transactional branch analyzed separately.

Thaman Against the Signs of The God

One of the most prominent Qur'anic uses of thaman is the prohibition against trading the signs of The God for a lower benefit:

وَلَا تَشْتَرُوا بِآيَاتِي ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا

And do not sell My signs for a small price.

Al-Baqarah 2:41

In this verse, the issue is not only receiving money for religious text. The structure of the verse shows that the signs of The God must not become tools for exchange with lower benefits. If someone conceals the signs, distorts them, reads them selectively, or sells truth to protect position and benefit, he has exchanged the signs for a small thaman.

The letter باء in بآياتي is important. The signs of The God stand in the position of what the human being trades with. The signs are spent so that something may be taken in exchange. This displacement is dangerous in Qur'anic sight, because the signs should be a criterion of guidance, not a tool for gaining benefit.

The same verse then mentions taqwa:

وَلَا تَشْتَرُوا بِآيَاتِي ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا وَإِيَّايَ فَاتَّقُونِ

And do not sell My signs for a small price, and toward Me alone have taqwa.

Al-Baqarah 2:41

This connection shows that wrongful exchange with the signs comes from weakness of taqwa and displacement of the reference of vigilance. When a human being accounts more for people, power, market, government, or his own position than for The God, he sells the signs for a small price.

Thaman Against the Covenant of The God and Oaths

In Ali 'Imran, thaman comes beside covenant and oaths:

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَشْتَرُونَ بِعَهْدِ اللَّهِ وَأَيْمَانِهِمْ ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا أُولَٰئِكَ لَا خَلَاقَ لَهُمْ فِي الْآخِرَةِ

Those who sell the covenant of The God and their oaths for a small price, those will have no share in the Hereafter.

Ali 'Imran 3:77

Here thaman is not only a financial price. A person may gain benefit by breaking covenant, lying in an oath, or betraying responsibility. The Qur'an calls that benefit thamanan qalilan because the covenant of The God and the trust of truthfulness cannot be weighed against worldly benefit.

This verse shows that thaman in the Qur'an is directly connected with the ethics of covenant. Breaking covenant is not only an individual mistake; it is a kind of transaction. A human being gives loyalty and truthfulness, and in return he takes a gain. The Qur'an exposes the worth of that transaction: a small price.

Thaman Against Concealing the Book and Truth

In Al-Baqarah, the Qur'an criticizes those who conceal part of the Book and take an exchange for it:

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَكْتُمُونَ مَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ وَيَشْتَرُونَ بِهِ ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا أُولَٰئِكَ مَا يَأْكُلُونَ فِي بُطُونِهِمْ إِلَّا النَّارَ

Those who conceal what The God has sent down from the Book and purchase with it a small price, they eat nothing into their bellies except Fire.

Al-Baqarah 2:174

In this verse, concealment and thaman are tied together. Truth is concealed so that benefit may be obtained. That benefit may be financial, religious, social, or political. With the intense image “they eat nothing into their bellies except Fire,” the Qur'an shows that such thaman, though outwardly a gain, is in reality Fire.

So thaman here is not merely price. It is an exchange that the human being brings into himself by selling truth. What is gained through concealing truth appears outwardly as thaman, but inwardly it is Fire and corruption.

Thamanan Qalilan: Numerically Small or Rank-Small?

The phrase ثمن قليل in the Qur'an must be understood carefully. “Qalil” here does not necessarily mean a small amount of money. The smallness is more relational and rank-based. Whatever exchange is taken for the signs of The God, divine covenant, or truth is small in relation to what was lost.

اشْتَرَوْا بِآيَاتِ اللَّهِ ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا فَصَدُّوا عَنْ سَبِيلِهِ

They sold the signs of The God for a small price, so they turned away from His path.

Al-Tawbah 9:9

In this verse, selling the signs is not only individual corruption. It has a social consequence: فصدوا عن سبيله. When signs are sold for benefit, the path of The God is blocked for others. Wrongful exchange with truth harms not only the seller; it also pollutes the path of guidance in society.

Therefore thamanan qalilan means an exchange that may outwardly look like profit, but on the scale of truth is insignificant. Even if the whole world were placed against the signs of The God, in relation to truth it would still be a small price.

Thaman and Selling a Human Being

In the story of Joseph, thaman is used about selling Joseph:

وَشَرَوْهُ بِثَمَنٍ بَخْسٍ دَرَاهِمَ مَعْدُودَةٍ وَكَانُوا فِيهِ مِنَ الزَّاهِدِينَ

And they sold him for a diminished and wrongful price, a few counted dirhams; and they were among those who had little desire for him.

Yusuf 12:20

This verse is one of the clearest examples of thaman in a transactional meaning. Joseph, a human being of dignity and a great future, is sold for thaman bakhs. بخس means reduced, wrongfully diminished, and below true worth.

Here thaman shows the ugly face of a transaction in which a human being is turned into a commodity. A few counted dirhams are placed against human dignity and truth. The phrase وكانوا فيه من الزاهدين also shows that the sellers had no desire for Joseph; that is, they did not know his true value, or did not want to know it.

So thaman bakhs is not only a low price; it is a sign of inability to recognize the true value of a human being.

Thaman and the Wider Logic of Moral Trade

Although the word thaman itself does not appear in some verses, the field of buying, selling, profit, and loss helps us understand thaman:

أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ اشْتَرَوُا الضَّلَالَةَ بِالْهُدَىٰ فَمَا رَبِحَتْ تِجَارَتُهُمْ

Those are the ones who purchased misguidance at the price of guidance; so their trade did not profit.

Al-Baqarah 2:16

The word thaman does not appear here, but the logic of thaman is present. Guidance is given away; misguidance is taken. The Qur'an calls this trade, but a profitless trade. This verse helps show that thaman in the Qur'an belongs to a wider language of moral exchange: the human being is always choosing and trading in life, but not every transaction is profitable.

The Number Branch: Eight, Eighty, Eighth

The root ث م ن also has a numerical branch in the Qur'an. This branch must be separated from the meaning of price, but it should not be ignored in a full root entry.

An example of ثمانية:

وَيَحْمِلُ عَرْشَ رَبِّكَ فَوْقَهُمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ ثَمَانِيَةٌ

And on that Day, eight will carry the Throne of your Lord above them.

Al-Haqqah 69:17

In this verse, ثمانية is a number. Without decisive evidence, it should not be reduced to a specific symbolic meaning. The verse speaks of the number eight in an unseen and Resurrection context, and its final reality belongs to unseen and ambiguous matters.

An example of ثمانين:

فَاجْلِدُوهُمْ ثَمَانِينَ جَلْدَةً

Then lash them eighty lashes.

Al-Nur 24:4

Here thamanin means eighty and appears in a social/legal ruling. Its meaning is numerical and should not be mixed with thaman as price.

An example of ثامنهم:

وَيَقُولُونَ سَبْعَةٌ وَثَامِنُهُمْ كَلْبُهُمْ

And they say: seven, and their eighth was their dog.

Al-Kahf 18:22

Here thaminuhum means their eighth. This is also an ordinal and numerical usage. In the same context, the Qur'an discourages fruitless dispute about their number; therefore one should not lean on unsupported number-building.

Similar Uses

Thaman in the Qur'an is not always only a financial price. In Yusuf 12:20, thaman is close to a transaction price: a few dirhams for selling Joseph. But in Al-Baqarah 2:41, thaman comes against the signs of The God and takes on a moral and religious meaning.

Thamanan qalilan also does not always mean little money. In exchange with signs, covenant, faith, or truth, whatever a person takes is small in relation to what he has lost. So the smallness here is relational and rank-based, not merely numerical.

Thaman shares a field with shira' and bay', but it is not identical with them. Shira' and bay' point to the act of buying and selling; thaman points to the exchange or price taken in that transaction. The Qur'an may speak about the transaction itself, or focus on thaman: what a person took in exchange for truth.

Thaman in the numerical branch must also be understood separately. Thamaniyah, thamanin, and thaminuhum relate to number and must not be confused with the meaning of price, even if they belong to the same root family.

Grammar and Structure

In ولا تشتروا بآياتي ثمنا قليلا, the letter باء in بآياتي is very important. It shows that the signs of The God are placed as what is traded with. The signs are spent so that thaman may be taken. This structure shows that the problem is not only taking thaman; the problem is that the signs of The God have been placed as a tool of exchange.

In يشترون بعهد الله وأيمانهم ثمنا قليلا, the covenant of The God and the oaths are what the human being uses to obtain thaman. A person spends covenant and oath to gain benefit. The structure exposes the ugliness of the transaction.

In ثمنا قليلا, the adjective قليل should be understood relationally. The thaman is small in relation to the truth that was sold. If a person sells the signs of The God for political power, religious position, or abundant wealth, it is still thamanan qalilan in Qur'anic sight because its relation to truth is insignificant.

In ثمن بخس, the word بخس shows wrongful lowering and reduction of worth. Joseph was sold for a price that had no proportion to his reality and dignity.

In numerical forms such as ثمانية، ثمانين، ثامنهم, the structure of the verse is numerical and should not be interpreted through the transactional meaning of thaman. These should be translated as eight, eighty, and their eighth.

Nearby Roots

ب ي ع points to buying, selling, and transaction. Bay' is the act of transaction itself; thaman is the exchange or price taken in that transaction.

ش ر ي in the Qur'an is used for buying and, in some structures, selling. Shira' shows the movement of exchange; thaman is what is taken in return for the other side.

ق و م / قيمه is close to standing, establishing, and valuation. Qimah can be an estimation of the worth of a thing; thaman is more specifically the exchange paid or received in a transaction.

أ ج ر points to wage, reward, or compensation for work. Ajr often comes in relation to action, service, or divine reward. Thaman is more in the field of transaction and exchange, especially when something is sold for something else.

ج ز ي / جزاء points to response, recompense, punishment, or reward corresponding to action. Jaza' is the result and response of an action; thaman is the price of a transaction.

ع و ض points to replacement or substitute. 'Iwad can be anything that comes in place of something else. Thaman is an exchange placed within the framework of price and transaction.

ب د ل points to replacing and substituting. Badal is the replacement itself or the act of switching; thaman is what is taken in return for selling or exchanging.

خ س ر points to loss. Thamanan qalilan often leads to moral loss, but khusran is the result of a bad transaction, while thaman is the exchange taken in the transaction.

ب خ س points to withholding, wrongful reduction, or lowering someone's right and value. Thaman bakhs means a diminished, wrongful price below true worth.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

ثمن: price, value, transactional exchange, what is taken in return for something else.

ثمن قليل: small price, insignificant exchange, low-value benefit in exchange for truth, a small price against truth.

ولا تشتروا بآياتي ثمنا قليلا: do not sell My signs for a small price; do not make My signs a means for taking an insignificant exchange; do not trade My signs for a low benefit.

بعهد الله وأيمانهم ثمنا قليلا: they sell the covenant of The God and their oaths for a small price; by spending the covenant of The God and their oaths, they take an insignificant benefit.

ثمن بخس: a diminished and wrongful price, a low price, a price below true worth.

دراهم معدودة: a few counted dirhams; counted and few dirhams.

ثمانية: eight.

ثمانين: eighty.

ثامنهم: their eighth.

Summary

The root ث م ن, in its main branch, points to price, value, and exchange taken in return for something else. At the material level, its image comes from the market, the scale, buying and selling, and determining the price of a transaction. But the Qur'an moves this field into moral evaluation: what does a human being take in exchange for what?

If a commodity is exchanged for money, thaman has an economic meaning. But if signs, covenant, faith, truthfulness, or human dignity are placed against worldly benefit, thaman enters the moral and religious field. In such a context, every exchange taken in return for truth is ثمن قليل, because in relation to truth it is insignificant.

The difference between thaman and bay' is that bay' is the transaction itself, while thaman is the price taken in the transaction. Its difference from ajr is that ajr is wage or reward for action, while thaman is the exchange of a transaction. Its difference from jaza' is that jaza' is the response and result of action, while thaman is what is taken in return for selling or exchanging.

In Forqan translation, thaman should not always be treated as a dry economic “price.” In many verses, “price,” “exchange,” “benefit taken,” or “small price in return for truth” is more precise. The numerical branch of the root, such as thamaniyah, thamanin, and thaminuhum, must be understood separately as numerical usage and not mixed with the meaning of price.
