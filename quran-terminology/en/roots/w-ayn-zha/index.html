Short Introduction to the Root

The root W ʿ Ẓ in the Qur’an moves in the semantic field of admonishing, giving counsel, warning, and making the listener attentive to conduct and its consequences.

In classical lexical sources, two close directions appear for this root. Khalil emphasizes reminding of good and of something before which the heart softens, while Raghib al-Isfahani describes waʿẓ as restraint and rebuke accompanied by causing fear. These two definitions show that waʿẓ should not be equated only with “gentle speech” or only with “frightening.”

The Qur’anic uses also show a broader field: waʿẓ can be a father’s sincere counsel to his son, a direct warning, a reminder of consequences, a rebuke meant to prevent the repetition of an error, or even the admonishing function of an event.

Therefore, “softening of the heart,” “remembrance,” and “reform” are better understood as possible effects or aims of admonition, not the fixed meaning of the root itself.

Short root meaning:

admonishing, counseling, warning, and reminding of consequences in order to make one attentive and to reform conduct.

Qur'anic Forms and Derivatives

The root W ʿ Ẓ appears twenty-five times in the Qur’an in three main forms:

وَعَظَ / يَعِظُ and its inflected forms

It appears fifteen times as a Form I verb, such as:

يَعِظُكُمْ

He admonishes you.

وَعِظْهُمْ

Admonish them.

فَعِظُوهُنَّ

Then admonish them.

أَعِظُكَ

I admonish and warn you.

مَوْعِظَة

It appears nine times as مَوْعِظَة.

This noun can be used for speech, a divine message, a writing, or even an event that has an admonishing function for the listener.

الْوَاعِظِينَ

It appears once in Ash-Shuara 136:

الْوَاعِظِينَ

admonishers.

The Semantic Core of the Root

The defensible core of W ʿ Ẓ may be stated as follows:

making the listener attentive to the good and evil of conduct and its consequences through counsel, reminder, or warning, with an orientation toward holding back from error or reforming conduct.

Several elements appear in this core:

Having an addressee

Waʿẓ is usually directed toward someone.

Drawing attention

It is not merely the transfer of news or information; the listener is to become attentive to his conduct or path.

Consequence and outcome

Reminding of consequences is prominent in the lexical field and in many Qur’anic uses.

A reforming direction

Waʿẓ can hold the listener back from error or call him to better conduct.

But acceptance and actual reform are not part of the definition. Admonition may take place and the listener may not accept it.

Two Lexical Sides: Softening the Heart and Warning

In the lexical tradition, two important expressions appear concerning waʿẓ.

One direction is reminding of good and of matters before which the heart softens.

The other direction is restraint with causing fear; that is, an admonition that holds the listener back from a path and warns him about its consequence.

These two should not be turned into two completely separate meanings. Both can stand within one broader field: directed speech that seeks to awaken the listener to conduct and its consequence.

The Qur’an also sometimes shows the tone of admonition as gentle and sincere, and sometimes as firm and warning.

Therefore:

Softening the heart ← a possible effect of admonition.

Causing fear and restraint ← one of the modes or manifestations of admonition.

Counsel and drawing attention to conduct and consequence ← the broader and more defensible field of the root.

The Material Image of the Root

For W ʿ Ẓ, there is no clear and provable material image available by which one could establish the historical origin of the root’s meaning.

Therefore, we should not construct images such as “striking the heart,” “shaking the heart,” or “softening a body” for this root.

The field of the word is clear from its linguistic use itself: admonition, warning, reminder, and drawing attention to outcome.

With this root, the absence of a definite material image is better than inventing an imagined etymology.

Direct Warning to Noah

قَالَ يَا نُوحُ إِنَّهُ لَيْسَ مِنْ أَهْلِكَ إِنَّهُ عَمَلٌ غَيْرُ صَالِحٍ فَلَا تَسْأَلْنِ مَا لَيْسَ لَكَ بِهِ عِلْمٌ إِنِّي أَعِظُكَ أَن تَكُونَ مِنَ الْجَاهِلِينَ

“He said: O Noah, he is not of your family; indeed, it is an unrighteous act. So do not ask Me for that about which you have no knowledge. I admonish and warn you lest you be among the ignorant.”

Hud, 46

This verse shows that waʿẓ is not necessarily soft and calm speech.

Here أَعِظُكَ comes after correcting a request and together with a direct warning.

But aʿiẓuka itself does not mean “I threaten you.” The warning is obtained from the content of the sentence and the context; waʿẓ is the act of admonishing and drawing attention.

Divine Admonition Concerning Trusts and Justice

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تُؤَدُّوا الْأَمَانَاتِ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهَا وَإِذَا حَكَمْتُم بَيْنَ النَّاسِ أَن تَحْكُمُوا بِالْعَدْلِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ نِعِمَّا يَعِظُكُم بِهِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا

“Indeed, The God commands you to deliver trusts to their people, and when you judge between people, to judge with justice. How excellent is that by which The God admonishes you. Indeed, The God is Hearing, Seeing.”

An-Nisa, 58

In this verse, admonition concerns thoroughly practical and social matters: trusts and justice.

Therefore, waʿẓ is not only emotional speech or a religious sermon; it can make the human being attentive to real social responsibilities.

Command, Prohibition, and Admonition in One Verse

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُ بِالْعَدْلِ وَالْإِحْسَانِ وَإِيتَاءِ ذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَيَنْهَىٰ عَنِ الْفَحْشَاءِ وَالْمُنكَرِ وَالْبَغْيِ يَعِظُكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ

“Indeed, The God commands justice, ihsan, and giving to relatives, and forbids indecency, munkar, and transgression; He admonishes you, so that perhaps you may remember.”

An-Nahl, 90

This verse is very important for setting the boundary of the root.

The Qur’an uses three verbs separately:

يَأْمُرُ

He commands.

يَنْهَىٰ

He forbids.

يَعِظُكُمْ

He admonishes you.

So waʿẓ is neither command itself nor prohibition itself.

Command and prohibition state the content of demand and restraint. Waʿẓ places that guidance in the position of drawing attention, admonition, and effect upon the listener.

The ending of the verse:

لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ

also shows that remembrance is the expected result of admonition, not its complete synonym.

Admonition to Prevent Return to Error

يَعِظُكُمُ اللَّهُ أَن تَعُودُوا لِمِثْلِهِ أَبَدًا إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ

“The God admonishes and warns you that, if you are believers, you never return to the like of it again.”

An-Nur, 17

This verse is one of the clearest restraining uses of the root.

The content of the admonition is explicit:

أَن تَعُودُوا لِمِثْلِهِ أَبَدًا

that is, not returning to a similar conduct.

Therefore, warning and holding back from repeating error is one clear manifestation of waʿẓ.

But “holding back” should not be taken physically; waʿẓ acts through address and drawing attention.

Luqman and Sincere Counsel

وَإِذْ قَالَ لُقْمَانُ لِابْنِهِ وَهُوَ يَعِظُهُ يَا بُنَيَّ لَا تُشْرِكْ بِاللَّهِ إِنَّ الشِّرْكَ لَظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌ

“And when Luqman said to his son, while admonishing him: O my son, do not associate partners with The God; indeed, shirk is a great injustice.”

Luqman, 13

This verse shows the other side of the field of waʿẓ.

Luqman speaks with the address:

يَا بُنَيَّ

and he also gives a reason for his prohibition:

إِنَّ الشِّرْكَ لَظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌ

So waʿẓ can be accompanied by sincerity, explanation, and reason.

But paternal love comes from the context of this verse; the root W ʿ Ẓ itself does not mean “love.”

“Then Admonish Them” in Family Dispute

الرِّجَالُ قَوَّامُونَ عَلَى النِّسَاءِ بِمَا فَضَّلَ اللَّهُ بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ وَبِمَا أَنفَقُوا مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ فَالصَّالِحَاتُ قَانِتَاتٌ حَافِظَاتٌ لِّلْغَيْبِ بِمَا حَفِظَ اللَّهُ وَاللَّاتِي تَخَافُونَ نُشُوزَهُنَّ فَعِظُوهُنَّ وَاهْجُرُوهُنَّ فِي الْمَضَاجِعِ وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ فَإِنْ أَطَعْنَكُمْ فَلَا تَبْغُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ سَبِيلًا إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيًّا كَبِيرًا

“Men are standing in responsibility over women, because of what The God has given some of them over others, and because of what they spend from their wealth. So righteous women are devoted, guarding in the unseen what The God has guarded. And those women whose nushuz you fear, admonish them, and distance yourselves from them in the beds, and strike them; then if they obey you, do not seek a way against them. Indeed, The God is High, Great.”

An-Nisa, 34

In this verse فَعِظُوهُنَّ is a verb independent from the verbs that follow it.

Therefore, waʿẓ itself should not be turned into leaving the bed, threat, or striking.

Its meaning is the same: admonishing and making the listener attentive to the matter and its consequence.

Analysis of the other verbs and of the whole structure of the verse is a separate subject and should not be placed inside the definition of the root W ʿ Ẓ.

Admonition Even When Acceptance Seems Unlikely

وَإِذْ قَالَتْ أُمَّةٌ مِّنْهُمْ لِمَ تَعِظُونَ قَوْمًا اللَّهُ مُهْلِكُهُمْ أَوْ مُعَذِّبُهُمْ عَذَابًا شَدِيدًا قَالُوا مَعْذِرَةً إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُمْ وَلَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ

“And when a group among them said: Why do you admonish a people whom The God will destroy or punish with a severe punishment? They said: As an excuse before your Lord, and perhaps they may have taqwa.”

Al-Araf, 164

This verse shows that acceptance of admonition is not part of its definition.

The admonishers continue to admonish even in conditions where others see no hope of effect.

The phrase:

وَلَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ

states the possible aim: perhaps they may have taqwa.

So taqwa and reform are hoped-for results of admonition, not the lexical meaning of waʿẓ itself.

Admonition Being Rejected

قَالُوا سَوَاءٌ عَلَيْنَا أَوَعَظْتَ أَمْ لَمْ تَكُن مِّنَ الْوَاعِظِينَ

“They said: It is the same for us whether you admonish or are not among the admonishers.”

Ash-Shuara, 136

This verse is one of the most important witnesses for separating the act of the admonisher from the effect on the listener.

They explicitly say that admonishing or not admonishing is the same for them.

So:

waʿẓ ≠ softening of the heart

waʿẓ ≠ accepting guidance

Waʿẓ is an act performed by the admonisher; the listener’s response is a separate matter.

Mawʿizah as a Description of the Divine Message

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ قَدْ جَاءَتْكُم مَّوْعِظَةٌ مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ وَشِفَاءٌ لِّمَا فِي الصُّدُورِ وَهُدًى وَرَحْمَةٌ لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

“O people, there has truly come to you an admonition from your Lord, and a healing for what is in the chests, and guidance and mercy for the believers.”

Yunus, 57

The Qur’an places four concepts beside one another in this verse:

مَوْعِظَة

شِفَاءٌ لِّمَا فِي الصُّدُورِ

هُدًى

رَحْمَة

These should not be made synonyms of one another without reason.

This structure is especially important for the issue of “softening the heart.” If inward effect on the human being were the whole meaning of mawʿizah, the separation of healing for what is in the chests from it would lose significance.

Mawʿizah is admonition and drawing attention; healing, guidance, and mercy are other layers of that same message.

Mawʿizah Beside Clarification and Guidance

هَٰذَا بَيَانٌ لِّلنَّاسِ وَهُدًى وَمَوْعِظَةٌ لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ

“This is a clarification for people, and guidance and admonition for the mindful.”

Ali 'Imran, 138

In this verse too, three separate words appear:

بَيَان

clarification.

هُدًى

guidance.

مَوْعِظَة

admonition and drawing attention concerning path and conduct.

So mawʿizah is not merely the transfer of information or explanation. Something can be “clarification” and at the same time have the function of “admonition” for a group.

“The Good Admonition”

ادْعُ إِلَىٰ سَبِيلِ رَبِّكَ بِالْحِكْمَةِ وَالْمَوْعِظَةِ الْحَسَنَةِ وَجَادِلْهُم بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ إِنَّ رَبَّكَ هُوَ أَعْلَمُ بِمَن ضَلَّ عَن سَبِيلِهِ وَهُوَ أَعْلَمُ بِالْمُهْتَدِينَ

“Call to the path of your Lord with wisdom and good admonition, and debate them with what is better. Indeed, your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best the guided.”

An-Nahl, 125

In this verse mawʿizah comes with the attribute:

الْحَسَنَةِ

This point is important, because all features of “being good” should not be inserted into the meaning of mawʿizah itself.

The Qur’an says admonition should be hasanah; therefore, the quality and manner of admonition can also be evaluated.

Admonition to One Specific Matter

قُلْ إِنَّمَا أَعِظُكُم بِوَاحِدَةٍ أَن تَقُومُوا لِلَّهِ مَثْنَىٰ وَفُرَادَىٰ ثُمَّ تَتَفَكَّرُوا مَا بِصَاحِبِكُم مِّن جِنَّةٍ إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا نَذِيرٌ لَّكُم بَيْنَ يَدَيْ عَذَابٍ شَدِيدٍ

“Say: I admonish you only with one thing: that you stand for The God, two by two and individually, then reflect. There is no madness in your companion. He is only a warner to you before a severe punishment.”

Saba, 46

This verse has an important structure:

أَعِظُكُم بِوَاحِدَةٍ

The admonisher can admonish the listener with one specific matter and then explain its content.

Here admonition is connected with reflection, but reflection itself is not the meaning of waʿẓ; it is part of the content of that admonition.

An Event as Mawʿizah

فَجَعَلْنَاهَا نَكَالًا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهَا وَمَا خَلْفَهَا وَمَوْعِظَةً لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ

“So We made it a nakal for what was before it and what was behind it, and an admonition for the mindful.”

Al-Baqarah, 66

This verse shows that mawʿizah is not necessarily oral speech.

An event too can be made:

مَوْعِظَةً لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ

Therefore, it is better not to restrict mawʿizah to a sermon.

Here “admonition” means something that takes on the function of counsel and drawing attention for the mindful.

Translations like “awakening event” may explain its effect, but they should not replace the base meaning of mawʿizah.

Nakal and Mawʿizah: Two Separate Functions

The same verse of Al-Baqarah 66 places two words beside each other:

نَكَالًا ... وَمَوْعِظَةً

Nakal highlights the restraining and pushing-back aspect of the event.

Mawʿizah highlights its admonishing and attention-drawing aspect.

So one event can be both nakal and mawʿizah, without the two words being synonyms.

This example is important for the method of Forqan: the co-occurrence of two words should not cause the meaning of one to be poured into the other.

Is Mawʿizah Only Speech?

The Qur’anic uses answer no.

The verb waʿaẓa usually shows speech and address from an agent to an addressee, such as Luqman and his son or the warning to Noah.

But the noun mawʿizah can be used for:

  • a divine message,
  • a writing and guidance,
  • or a historical event.

So it is better to understand mawʿizah as “something with the function of admonition and drawing attention,” not merely a speech.

Comparison With Nearby Roots

W ʿ Ẓ (Mawʿizah) Compared With DH K R (Dhikr)

Dhikr has a broad field of mentioning, remembering, reminding, and bringing to mind.

Mawʿizah has a more prominent practical and ethical direction and makes the listener attentive to conduct and its consequence.

This difference is a tendency, and to set the full boundary of DH K R, that root must be studied independently.

W ʿ Ẓ (Waʿẓ) Compared With N Ṣ Ḥ (Nush)

Nush tends toward sincere counsel, purity of intention, and truthful guidance.

Waʿẓ highlights the form of admonition, warning, or drawing attention to the outcome.

Admonition can come from nush, but these two roots are not complete synonyms.

W ʿ Ẓ (Waʿẓ) Compared With N DH R (Indhar)

Indhar focuses more on warning against danger and future consequence.

Waʿẓ is broader and can include warning, counsel, reminding of good, and drawing attention to outcome.

Therefore, Raghib’s definition of waʿẓ as “restraint accompanied by causing fear” shows one important direction, but all Qur’anic uses should not be reduced to “frightening.”

W ʿ Ẓ Compared With A M R (Amr) and N H Y (Nahy)

Amr demands doing.

Nahy restrains from doing.

Waʿẓ addresses the listener through counsel and drawing attention to conduct and its consequence.

An-Nahl 90 is the best witness for the separation of these three fields.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

The verb وَعَظَ in Qur’anic usage is often transitive and takes a direct addressee:

يَعِظُكُمْ

“He admonishes you.”

وَعِظْهُمْ

“Admonish them.”

فَعِظُوهُنَّ

“Admonish them.”

يَعِظُكُم بِهِ

In the structure:

يَعِظُكُم بِهِ

the letter ba identifies the thing by which, or with which, admonition is performed.

أَعِظُكَ أَن تَكُونَ

In Hud 46, the phrase after aʿiẓuka clarifies the content of the warning and admonition.

يَعِظُكُمُ اللَّهُ أَن تَعُودُوا

In An-Nur 17 as well, the structure after the verb specifies the content of the admonition.

أَعِظُكُم بِوَاحِدَةٍ

In Saba 46, ba introduces a specific admonition, and then its content is explained.

These structures show that waʿẓ itself does not contain a specific content such as “prohibition,” “command,” or “threat”; the sentence determines the precise content.

Challenges of Understanding and Rendering

First, waʿẓ should not always be restricted to a “religious sermon” or a formal address. A father can admonish his son, and an event can also be an admonition.

Second, softening the heart should not be turned into the inherent and fixed meaning of the root. Ash-Shuara 136 shows that admonition may produce no softening in the listener.

Third, defining waʿẓ as “causing fear” should not cover the whole field either. Warning is an important part of it, but Luqman 13 and other uses also show its sincere and instructional field.

Fourth, mawʿizah is not identical with huda, bayan, shifa, nakal, dhikr, amr, and nahy. The Qur’an repeatedly brings these concepts separately beside each other.

Fifth, reform, taqwa, remembrance, and softening of the heart can be aims or effects of admonition, but their occurrence is not guaranteed.

Sixth, in events such as Al-Baqarah 66, it is better to translate mawʿizah as “admonition” or “a means of admonition,” while “awakening” remains in the contextual explanation.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For وَعَظَ / يَعِظُ:

admonished / admonishes

or in warning contexts:

admonished and warned / admonishes and warns

For مَوْعِظَة:

admonition

counsel

and where an event or text has this function:

a means of admonition

For الْمَوْعِظَةِ الْحَسَنَةِ:

good admonition

or:

good counsel

For الْوَاعِظِينَ:

admonishers

or, in a suitable context:

warning givers

But “warning givers” should not become the fixed equivalent of waʿiẓin, because waʿẓ is broader than indhar.

Short Root Meaning for Forqan

Short root meaning:

admonishing, counseling, warning, and reminding of consequences in order to make one attentive and to reform conduct.

In explanation one may add:

Admonition can call the listener to re-examine his conduct by reminding him of good or of the consequence of error.

“Softening the heart,” “remembrance,” and “reform” are possible effects and aims, not the full core of the root.

Conclusion

The root W ʿ Ẓ in the Qur’an moves around admonition and drawing the listener’s attention to conduct, path, and consequence.

Waʿẓ can be sincere and gentle, like Luqman’s counsel to his son:

وَهُوَ يَعِظُهُ

and it can be warning and firm, like:

إِنِّي أَعِظُكَ أَن تَكُونَ مِنَ الْجَاهِلِينَ

It can have a restraining aim:

يَعِظُكُمُ اللَّهُ أَن تَعُودُوا لِمِثْلِهِ أَبَدًا

and even an event can be made:

مَوْعِظَةً لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ

At the same time, admonition does not necessarily take effect:

سَوَاءٌ عَلَيْنَا أَوَعَظْتَ

Therefore, waʿẓ cannot be equated with “softening of the heart,” “being guided,” or “being reformed.”

The final definition of this root may be stated as follows:

W ʿ Ẓ expresses admonition, warning, and directed attention to conduct and its outcome, with the aim of holding back from error or calling to reform. Softening of the heart, remembrance, taqwa, and reform can be among its effects, but they are not necessary parts of the meaning of the root itself.
