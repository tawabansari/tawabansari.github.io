1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ع ي ن in the Qur'an belongs to a broad family of meanings connected with ʿayn. Its two main and clear uses are:

  • عَيْن as eye;
  • عَيْن as spring and source of water.

The same word is also used in other constructions and contexts for care, direct seeing, tears, heavenly blessings, and water that is visible and accessible.

Between eye and spring, one may see an imaginal similarity: both are specific and visible points. The eye is the place of seeing and of reactions such as tears, and the spring is the place where water emerges from the earth. Yet this similarity should not be turned into the fixed definition "an opening for the exit of every kind of flow." عَيْن is a polysemous Arabic word, and its exact meaning is known from construction and context.

The initial and cautious definition of the root is:

In the Qur'an, ع ي ن mostly points to eye, spring, and something clear and specific. In expressions attributed to The God, ʿayn is imaginal language for care, attention, and protection, not proof of a bodily organ.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

عَيْن

A singular form which, according to context, can mean eye or spring.

عَيْنَان

The dual of ʿayn; in the Qur'an it is used both for two eyes and for two springs.

أَعْيُن

One plural of ʿayn, used in the Qur'an mostly for eyes.

عُيُون

A plural used in the Qur'an mostly for springs and sources of water.

عَيْنُ الْيَقِين

A knowledge-related construction that expresses direct observation and encounter with a certain reality.

مَعِين

In phrases such as مَاءٍ مَعِينٍ and كَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ, it points to water or drink that is flowing, visible, and accessible.

The derivational analysis of مَعِين should be handled carefully. Its link with ʿayn and the meaning of visible or flowing water is known in lexical sources, but a fixed and complex morphological analysis should not be built only on outward pattern.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

Finding one definition that covers every use of ع ي ن without strain is difficult. "Eye" and "spring" are two established meanings of one word, and one does not have to derive one from the other.

Still, several shared features appear among the main uses:

  • ʿayn is a specific and recognizable point;
  • in the meaning eye, seeing and observation take place through it;
  • in the meaning spring, water appears and flows from it;
  • in some wider Arabic uses, ʿayn can indicate the thing itself, the original, a specific person, or something present and visible.

In the Qur'an, this range must be studied verse by verse. One cannot say that the fixed core of all forms is "the exit of a flow", because the eye is more the organ of receiving sight than a path of exit. Tears emerge from the eye, but that is not the main function of the word in every verse.

A more cautious definition is:

ʿAyn is something specific and visible that in one context points to the organ of seeing, and in another context to the source of water. The imaginal link between the two lies in a visible point of appearance or reception, not in one shared physical mechanism.

5. The Material Image of the Root

This root has two main material images.

The eye

An organ in the face through which seeing occurs. The eye can also reveal the inner state of a person:

  • it becomes white from grief;
  • it overflows with tears;
  • through its look, it shows reaction, fear, recognition, or attention.

The spring

A point in the earth where water appears and flows. A spring can be singular, dual, or multiple, and in the Qur'an it appears in descriptions of earth and Paradise.

The outward similarity between eye and spring can be seen in roundness, visibility, and their relation to liquid, but one should not claim that this similarity is necessarily the historical origin of the naming unless independent linguistic evidence is offered.

6. Use of the Root in Arabic

ʿAyn in Arabic is a polysemous word. Among its known uses are:

  • eye;
  • spring;
  • a lookout or informant;
  • the self or original of a thing;
  • something present and specific;
  • ready cash as opposed to debt.

This semantic spread likely emerged from prominence, presence, observability, or the concreteness of something. But a Qur'anic article should not bring every lexical use outside the Qur'an into the analysis of verses without need.

The center of Qur'anic usage remains eye and spring. Other meanings should be raised only when they have a real role in understanding a construction such as عَيْنَ الْيَقِينِ or مَاءٍ مَعِينٍ.

7. Challenges in Understanding and Translation

Making one mechanical definition for eye and spring

The phrase "an opening for the exit of flow" is partly natural for spring, but incomplete for eye, because the eye is also the place of receiving light and seeing. Turning both into a "portal for transfer of fluid or information" imports modern scientific terms into Qur'anic language.

Embodying divine expressions

In phrases such as:

  • بِأَعْيُنِنَا
  • عَلَىٰ عَيْنِي

one cannot assume for The God a biological organ like created beings. The Qur'an itself says:

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ

Nothing is like Him.

Ash-Shuraa 42:11

Therefore these expressions must be understood within imaginal language of care, attention, knowledge, and protection.

Removing the Qur'anic image completely from translation

On the other side, translating both expressions only as "care" may completely hide the image of the verse. In natural translation one may say "under Our eye" or "under Our care", while explaining that the Arabic text uses the image of ʿayn.

Claiming a fixed difference between the two plurals

In the Qur'an, أَعْيُن is used mostly for eyes, and عُيُون for springs. This is a clear Qur'anic pattern.

But from this one should not conclude that Arabic in its essence and in every period assigns these two plurals without exception only to these two meanings. It is better to speak of Qur'anic usage distinction.

Confusing مَعِين with مُعِين

مَعِين in the phrase مَاءٍ مَعِينٍ relates to flowing and accessible water.

مُعِين from the root ع و ن can mean helper.

In unvowelled writing, these two may look the same, but their form, pronunciation, and meaning are different.

8. Methodological Note

In studying each verse, one should ask:

  • Is ʿayn in this verse an eye or a spring?
  • Is it singular, dual, or plural?
  • Is the use bodily, spatial, knowledge-related, or imaginal?
  • If it is attributed to The God, what meaning fits transcendence and context?
  • Does the accompanying verb or adjective create the meaning of flow, tears, seeing, or care?
  • Does visibility come from ʿayn itself, or from the adjective and context?

One should also distinguish three levels:

Direct meaning: eye or spring.

Imaginal use: care, attention, or direct observation.

Root reflection: the similarity of eye and spring in prominence, visibility, or relation to appearance.

Root reflection must not replace direct meaning.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

ʿAyn as Spring

Twelve springs from the stone

Al-Baqarah 2:60 says:

فَانْفَجَرَتْ مِنْهُ اثْنَتَا عَشْرَةَ عَيْنًا

Then twelve springs burst forth from it.

Al-Baqarah 2:60

عَيْنًا states what came out from the stone, while فَانْفَجَرَتْ expresses the event of bursting forth and flowing.

This verse clarifies the relation between ف ج ر and ع ي ن:

  • ف ج ر focuses on opening and flow;
  • ع ي ن focuses on the spring itself that appeared.

Still, ʿayn is not always the result of the verb fajara. A spring can be mentioned in a verse without this root being present.

Twelve springs in Al-A'raf

Al-A'raf 7:160 says:

فَانْبَجَسَتْ مِنْهُ اثْنَتَا عَشْرَةَ عَيْنًا

Then twelve springs flowed forth from it.

Al-A'raf 7:160

In this verse ʿayn is paired with the root ب ج س, not ف ج ر. This shows that ʿayn names the source, while the accompanying verb specifies how the water came out.

A flowing spring in Paradise

Al-Ghashiyah 88:12 says:

فِيهَا عَيْنٌ جَارِيَةٌ

In it is a flowing spring.

Al-Ghashiyah 88:12

جَارِيَةٌ expresses the flow of the spring. Flowing is not inside the meaning of ʿayn itself; it comes from its adjective.

Two flowing springs

Ar-Rahman 55:50 says:

فِيهِمَا عَيْنَانِ تَجْرِيَانِ

In the two of them are two springs flowing.

Ar-Rahman 55:50

The verb تَجْرِيَانِ expresses the flow of the two springs. The dual عَيْنَانِ only shows their number.

Two gushing springs

Ar-Rahman 55:66 says:

فِيهِمَا عَيْنَانِ نَضَّاخَتَانِ

In the two of them are two gushing springs.

Ar-Rahman 55:66

The adjective نَضَّاخَتَانِ expresses the quality of gushing or overflowing. This quality should not be moved into the basic definition of ʿayn.

Heavenly springs

In several verses, عُيُون is mentioned with gardens, fruits, and blessings, such as:

إِنَّ الْمُتَّقِينَ فِي جَنَّاتٍ وَعُيُونٍ

Indeed the mindful are in gardens and springs.

Al-Hijr 15:45

In this construction, عُيُون points to an environment blessed with sources of water. Their quality must be known from the context of those verses.

ʿAyn as Eye

Two eyes as blessing

Al-Balad 90:8 says:

أَلَمْ نَجْعَلْ لَهُ عَيْنَيْنِ

Did We not make for him two eyes?

Al-Balad 90:8

عَيْنَيْنِ is the dual of ʿayn and means two organs of sight. The verse then mentions tongue and two lips and recalls the basic gifts of perception and expression.

Ya'qub's eyes becoming white

Yusuf 12:84 says:

وَابْيَضَّتْ عَيْنَاهُ مِنَ الْحُزْنِ

And his two eyes became white from grief.

Yusuf 12:84

The whitening of the eyes is understood from the verb ابْيَضَّتْ. The verse states Ya'qub's bodily condition after intense grief.

The translation "he became blind" is an interpretive result of this condition and is better placed in explanation, not as a full replacement of the verse's image.

Eyes overflowing with tears

Al-Ma'idah 5:83 says:

تَرَىٰ أَعْيُنَهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ الدَّمْعِ مِمَّا عَرَفُوا مِنَ الْحَقِّ

You see their eyes overflowing with tears because of what they recognized of the truth.

Al-Ma'idah 5:83

Here:

  • أَعْيُنَهُمْ is the organ of sight;
  • تَفِيضُ expresses overflowing;
  • مِنَ الدَّمْعِ specifies the flowing matter.

This verse can create a literary association between eye and spring, but the verse itself does not turn the eye into a spring. It speaks of tears flowing from the eye.

Eyes pouring tears from grief

At-Tawbah 9:92 also says:

تَوَلَّوْا وَأَعْيُنُهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ الدَّمْعِ حَزَنًا

They turned back while their eyes were overflowing with tears from grief.

At-Tawbah 9:92

In this verse too, the overflowing comes from the verb, and the tears from the complement of the sentence, not from the word ʿayn itself.

Expressions of Divine Care

Nuh's ship under the care of The God

Hud 11:37 says:

وَاصْنَعِ الْفُلْكَ بِأَعْيُنِنَا وَوَحْيِنَا

Build the ship under Our eye and by Our revelation.

Hud 11:37

Al-Mu'minun 23:27 also has a close structure:

أَنِ اصْنَعِ الْفُلْكَ بِأَعْيُنِنَا وَوَحْيِنَا

In these verses, بِأَعْيُنِنَا appears beside وَوَحْيِنَا. The context speaks of building the ship according to divine guidance and care.

Al-Mu'minun 23:27

Natural translation:

under Our eye

or:

under Our care

The ship moving under care

Al-Qamar 54:14 says:

تَجْرِي بِأَعْيُنِنَا جَزَاءً لِمَنْ كَانَ كُفِرَ

It was moving under Our eye, as a recompense for one who had been denied.

Al-Qamar 54:14

The verse does not say that each wave was neutralized by an unknown system. بِأَعْيُنِنَا expresses The God's protection and care, while its mode is not explained.

Musa being raised under the eye of The God

Ta-Ha 20:39 says:

وَأَلْقَيْتُ عَلَيْكَ مَحَبَّةً مِنِّي وَلِتُصْنَعَ عَلَىٰ عَيْنِي

I cast upon you love from Me, so that you would be formed under My eye.

Ta-Ha 20:39

In this context, لِتُصْنَعَ points to Musa being formed, raised, and shaped along the path of divine protection.

عَلَىٰ عَيْنِي can be translated:

  • under My eye;
  • under My care;
  • before My attention.

This phrase should not be turned into physiological growth under an error-correction system or frequency supervision.

Being patient under divine care

At-Tur 52:48 says:

وَاصْبِرْ لِحُكْمِ رَبِّكَ فَإِنَّكَ بِأَعْيُنِنَا

Be patient for the judgment of your Lord, for you are under Our eye.

At-Tur 52:48

In this verse, بِأَعْيُنِنَا assures the Prophet that he is not abandoned or outside care.

The meaning of care is clear from context and does not require assuming a bodily organ.

ʿAyn al-Yaqin

At-Takathur 102:7 says:

ثُمَّ لَتَرَوُنَّهَا عَيْنَ الْيَقِينِ

Then you will surely see it with the eye of certainty.

At-Takathur 102:7

This construction comes after the verb of seeing. عَيْنَ الْيَقِينِ expresses a level of direct encounter and certain observation.

It can be understood as:

  • direct and certain seeing;
  • certainty itself in the form of observation;
  • an observation that leaves no room for doubt.

Still, "seeing through a visual portal" is unnatural. "ʿAyn al-yaqin" is a knowledge-related and established construction.

Alongside it, the Qur'an also speaks of عِلْمَ الْيَقِينِ and حَقُّ الْيَقِينِ. The distinction among these constructions should be studied independently in an article on certainty.

Ma'in Water

Al-Mulk 67:30

قُلْ أَرَأَيْتُمْ إِنْ أَصْبَحَ مَاؤُكُمْ غَوْرًا فَمَنْ يَأْتِيكُمْ بِمَاءٍ مَعِينٍ

Say: Have you considered, if your water were to sink away, who would bring you flowing and accessible water?

Al-Mulk 67:30

غَوْرًا expresses water that has sunk and gone out of reach. In contrast, مَعِينٍ is water that is visible, flowing, or accessible.

Suitable translations:

  • flowing water;
  • visible and accessible water;
  • spring water.

One should not extract from this word the technical structure "a pressurized outlet channel."

Ma'in drink

As-Saffat 37:45 says:

يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِكَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ

A cup from a flowing drink is passed around among them.

As-Saffat 37:45

Al-Waqi'ah 56:18 also speaks of كَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ.

مَعِين in these verses describes a flowing and running drink. Its full type and quality are known from the heavenly context, not from the root ʿayn alone.

10. Examination of Important Forms in the Qur'an

عَيْن

In the Qur'an it appears with two main meanings:

  • eye;
  • spring.

Recognizing the meaning is usually not difficult, because the verbs, adjectives, and number around it make it clear.

عَيْنَان

A dual form that can be two eyes or two springs:

  • عَيْنَيْنِ in Al-Balad 90:8: two eyes;
  • عَيْنَانِ in Ar-Rahman: two springs.

The dual itself does not determine the type of ʿayn; context determines it.

أَعْيُن

In Qur'anic use it mostly relates to eyes:

  • human eyes;
  • eyes overflowing with tears;
  • imaginal expressions of divine care.

In divine use, the plural must not be interpreted as a number of bodily organs. The plural in this expression can show breadth and perfection of care in imaginal language.

عُيُون

In the Qur'an it is used for springs. This plural appears in descriptions of:

  • earthly springs;
  • gardens;
  • heavenly blessings;
  • lands blessed with water.

This distinction between أَعْيُن and عُيُون is clear in the Qur'an, but should not be generalized to all Arabic usage without examination.

مَعِين

It points to water or drink that is flowing and accessible. In this use it must be distinguished from مُعِين meaning helper.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

Genitive attachment to pronouns

Forms such as:

  • عَيْنَاهُ
  • أَعْيُنَهُمْ
  • أَعْيُنِنَا
  • عَيْنِي

express possession, relation, or attachment.

In أَعْيُنِنَا and عَيْنِي, attachment to a divine pronoun must not be interpreted as bodily ownership of an organ. The kind of use is known from imaginal context and the principle of divine transcendence.

The bā' in بِأَعْيُنِنَا

The bā' in this construction can express accompaniment, encompassing, or being within the field of care. Translating "with Our eyes" preserves the literal image, but in today's English it can suggest an incorrect bodily meaning.

More natural translation:

under Our eye

ʿalā in عَلَىٰ عَيْنِي

عَلَىٰ in this expression indicates being within the field of attention and care. It should not be treated as a physical location on an organ.

Adjectives of ʿayn

In spring verses, the accompanying adjective or verb states the quality of the spring:

  • جَارِيَةٌ: flowing;
  • تَجْرِيَانِ: they flow;
  • نَضَّاخَتَانِ: gushing;
  • آنِيَةٍ: very hot.

Thus flow, gushing, or heat is not part of the fixed meaning of ʿayn.

The construction عَيْنَ الْيَقِينِ

ʿAyn is attached to certainty. This genitive construction expresses a type of certainty associated with direct observation and encounter.

It should not be translated literally as "the eye of certainty" except in linguistic explanation.

12. ع ي ن Compared with Near Roots

ع ي ن compared with ب ص ر

ʿAyn in bodily use is the eye.

Baṣar relates to seeing, the capacity of visual distinction, and sometimes perception and insight.

However, the expression "ʿayn is the vessel and baṣar is what is inside it" is too simple. Seeing in the Qur'an is connected with eye, sight, heart, and perception in different ways.

Tendency difference:

  • ʿayn is more the organ or place of seeing;
  • baṣar highlights seeing itself and the capacity to distinguish.

ع ي ن compared with ن ظ ر

Naẓar is the act of looking, paying attention, or reflecting on something.

ʿAyn is the organ through which bodily looking is carried out.

Someone may have eyes but not look; or may look but not grasp the truth.

ع ي ن compared with ف ج ر

ف ج ر in water-related use focuses on opening a path and flowing.

ʿAyn is the name of the spring or source itself.

In Al-Baqarah 2:60:

  • infijār is the event of bursting forth;
  • ʿayn is the spring that emerged.

But ʿayn is not always the product of fajr, and fajr does not always produce ʿayn; for example, seas too are described with ف ج ر.

ع ي ن compared with ن ب ع

Nabaʿ focuses on water emerging.

ʿAyn indicates the source or place of water itself.

This difference too is a tendency and should not make nabaʿ always calm or ʿayn always gushing.

ع ي ن compared with ر أ ي

ر أ ي expresses the act of seeing or perceiving.

ʿAyn is the organ or place connected with bodily sight.

In At-Takathur 102:7, the verb لَتَرَوُنَّهَا comes from ر أ ي, while عَيْنَ الْيَقِينِ expresses the quality and level of observation. The pairing of the two shows that ʿayn and ru'yah are not identical.

ع ي ن compared with ع و ن

ع ي ن relates to eye, spring, and connected uses.

ع و ن relates to help, assistance, and support.

مَعِين meaning flowing water and مُعِين meaning helper must not be mixed.

13. Expressions of ʿAyn about The God

The uses of بِأَعْيُنِنَا and عَلَىٰ عَيْنِي are among the most important methodological cases of this root.

Two errors must be set aside at the same time:

Embodiment

One should not imagine that The God has an eye, limit, body, or biological apparatus like created beings.

Erasing the language of the verse

One should not remove the image of ʿayn so completely that it seems as if the Qur'an used only an abstract word such as "awareness."

The language of the verse pictures care that is close, active, and reassuring. The translation "under Our eye" preserves this image in natural English without attributing a bodily organ.

In these expressions, one may consider these meanings:

  • complete knowledge and awareness;
  • care;
  • protection;
  • special attention;
  • the commissioned servant not being abandoned on the path of duty.

But the mode of this care belongs to divine reality and must not be compared or fixed with monitoring systems, radar, scanning, frequency fields, or informational mechanisms.

14. Eye and Tears; The Place of Reception and Manifest Reaction

The eye is paired with tears in Al-Ma'idah 5:83 and At-Tawbah 9:92. These verses show that the eye is not only the organ that receives images; the inner reaction of the human being also becomes visible through it.

In Al-Ma'idah 5:83, recognition of the truth is accompanied by tears flowing:

تَرَىٰ أَعْيُنَهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ الدَّمْعِ مِمَّا عَرَفُوا مِنَ الْحَقِّ

The order of meaning is:

Al-Ma'idah 5:83

  • they recognized something of the truth;
  • this recognition produced a reaction in them;
  • their eyes overflowed with tears.

One can see a literary similarity between a crying eye and a spring, but the verse does not present a theory about the conversion of an "information gateway" into a "fluid gateway."

A cautious reflection is:

The eye is both the place of seeing and one of the places where the truth of a person's inner state becomes visible.

16. Final Definitions of Important Forms

عَيْن

Eye or spring; the exact meaning is known from context.

أَعْيُن

Eyes; in divine expressions, imaginal language of care and protection.

عُيُون

Springs and sources of water.

عَيْنُ الْيَقِينِ

Direct observation and encounter at the level of certainty.

مَعِين

Water or drink that is flowing, visible, and accessible.

17. Conclusion

The root ع ي ن in the Qur'an is used above all in two meanings: eye and spring. The eye is the organ of sight and the place where reactions such as tears become visible; the spring is a source from which water emerges.

One may see an imaginal similarity between the two, but they should not be reduced to a mechanical definition such as "portal for the exit of flow." The eye is not only a place of exit, but the organ of seeing and reception. The spring is also not merely an opening, but a source and place of water-flow.

Qur'anic uses show:

  • ʿayn can be eye or spring;
  • its dual is used for two eyes and two springs;
  • أَعْيُن in the Qur'an is mostly for eyes, and عُيُون for springs;
  • the accompanying adjective and verb determine the quality of the eye or spring;
  • بِأَعْيُنِنَا and عَلَىٰ عَيْنِي express divine care and protection through imaginal language;
  • عَيْنُ الْيَقِينِ is the level of certain observation;
  • مَعِين in water and drink constructions conveys flowing and accessibility;
  • ع ي ن must not be mixed with ع و ن and the concept of help.

The final and cautious definition is:

In the Qur'an, ع ي ن relates to eye, spring, and in broader uses to observation, care, and something visible and accessible. The exact meaning of each form is known from construction, plural form, adjective, accompanying verb, and verse context. Expressions attributed to The God must be understood with preservation of the Qur'anic image and full transcendence: as care and protection, not as a bodily organ.
