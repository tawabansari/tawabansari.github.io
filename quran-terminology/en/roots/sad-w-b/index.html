Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root Ṣ W B

Descending, pouring down, reaching, striking, and something hitting its target or proper place

Important Forms from This Root

صَابَ / يَصُوبُ

ṣāba / yaṣūbu; descended, reached, hit the target, struck.

صَوْب / صَيِّب

ṣawb / ṣayyib; direction, descent, rainfall; heavy pouring rain from the sky.

صَوَّبَ / تَصْوِيب

ṣawwaba / taṣwīb; directed, lowered, aimed toward a target, judged correct.

أَصَابَ / يُصِيبُ

aṣāba / yuṣību; made reach, struck, afflicted, hit a target, or intended in some contexts.

إِصَابَة / مُصِيب

iṣābah / muṣīb; hitting, reaching the target, correct contact; one who hits or reaches rightly.

مُصَاب / مُصِيبَة / صَوَاب

muṣāb / muṣībah / ṣawāb; one struck; an event that reaches a person; correctness that hits the proper target.

Central Meaning

The root Ṣ W B means descending, pouring down, reaching, striking, and something hitting its target or proper place. In al-Baqarah 2:19, the word صَيِّب points to a heavy pouring rain from the sky, a rainfall that descends from above and is accompanied by darknesses, thunder, and lightning.

This root is not limited to rain. From the image of descending and reaching arise meanings such as striking, an event reaching someone, an arrow hitting its target, direction toward a destination, and the correctness of speech or judgment. Something is ṣawāb because it reaches its proper place, like an arrow hitting the target.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of Ṣ W B is the movement of something toward its point of contact or proper destination. Several images gather around this root: descending from above to below, like rain; reaching a target or striking the intended place, like an arrow or blow; and directing something toward a destination, like lowering the head or aiming something downward toward a target.

From here, the meaning of صَوَاب becomes clear. Ṣawāb means that speech, judgment, or action has hit the correct target and has not gone into error. Its correctness is not only abstract “correctness”; it comes from the image of hitting the proper place.

Thus Ṣ W B is tied to direction, descent, reaching, aiming, and precise contact.

Material Image

The first material image is heavy rain pouring down from the sky. Clouds gather above. Water comes from above to below. Rainfall reaches the earth and strikes its surface. If the rain is severe, it is not a soft drizzle; it is pouring descent. This is the image present in صَيِّب.

The second image is an arrow or object hitting its target. Until the arrow reaches the target, it is merely in motion. When it strikes, its movement has reached the intended place. The third image is direct and vertical movement downward, like a bucket descending into a well or something coming down without deviation toward its destination. This helps explain ṣawāb: speech or judgment that has not deviated, but has reached its proper place.

The fourth image is lowering or inclining something toward a target, like an animal lowering its head toward water or a bird descending toward prey. This image clarifies صَوَّبَ and taṣwīb: directing something toward a destination or setting a ruling upon its proper case.

The fifth image is an event descending upon a human being. Muṣībah belongs to this field: something strikes a person, reaches him, or comes down upon him. In Ṣ W B, rain, arrow, direction, intention, and event all gather around one semantic center.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, صَابَ and أَصَابَ are used for reaching, striking, hitting a target, and being affected by something. These uses were natural in old Arab life: rain, archery, war, hunting, travel, wells, and sudden events.

صَيِّب is close to rain or pouring rainfall. It is tied to the image of sky, cloud, rain, thunder, and water descending upon the earth. أَصَابَ also belongs to the field of arrow, target, contact, and reaching what was intended. When something reaches the intended place correctly, it has struck the target.

From this image, ṣawāb comes to mean the correctness of speech or judgment, because true speech hits the target of meaning and truth. صَوَّبَ is also connected with directing and lowering toward a destination. Later uses such as approving a law or judgment can be understood from this field: a ruling or judgment is directed toward its case and settled upon it.

Qur’anic Usage

Ṣayyib: Pouring Rain from the Sky

Surat al-Baqarah says: أَوْ كَصَيِّبٍ مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ فِيهِ ظُلُمَاتٌ وَرَعْدٌ وَبَرْقٌ; or like a pouring rain from the sky, within it darknesses, thunder, and lightning 2:19.

Here, صَيِّب is not simple “rain.” The context shows heavy rainfall from the sky, accompanied by darknesses, thunder, and lightning. The word ṣayyib fits the root’s sense of descending and pouring down. Rain reaches the earth from the sky and overwhelms the environment.

Muṣībah: Something That Reaches a Person

The Qur’an says: الَّذِينَ إِذَا أَصَابَتْهُم مُّصِيبَةٌ قَالُوا إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ; those who, when a muṣībah reaches them, say: we belong to The God, and to Him we are returning 2:156.

مُصِيبَة comes from this root. Muṣībah is something that reaches a human being and descends upon him. In common usage it often means disaster, but root-wise it is an event that has struck the person. Such an event is usually heavy, painful, or harmful, but the core meaning lies in reaching and striking.

Striking by Permission of The God

Surat al-Taghābun says: مَا أَصَابَ مِن مُّصِيبَةٍ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ; no muṣībah strikes except by permission of The God 64:11.

In this verse, both the verb أَصَابَ and the noun مُصِيبَة appear. Their combination shows that, in Qur’anic language, muṣībah is something that reaches or strikes. The verse is not a simple discussion of compulsion. Root-wise, the point is that an event is not placeless or relationless; it reaches the human being within the order of divine permission.

Good and Harm Reaching a Person

The Qur’an also speaks of good and harm reaching someone: إِن تُصِبْكَ حَسَنَةٌ تَسُؤْهُمْ وَإِن تُصِبْكَ مُصِيبَةٌ يَقُولُوا قَدْ أَخَذْنَا أَمْرَنَا مِن قَبْلُ; if a good thing reaches you, it displeases them; and if a muṣībah reaches you, they say: we had already taken our matter beforehand 9:50.

Here, aṣāba is not only about calamity. A good thing can also تُصِبْ, meaning it reaches a person. Whatever reaches a human being, whether welcome or unwelcome, may enter the field of iṣābah. The core is reaching and contact; the context determines whether what reaches is good, harm, blessing, or injury.

Muṣībah and Human Actions

The Qur’an sometimes connects the striking of muṣībah with human actions: وَمَا أَصَابَكُم مِّن مُّصِيبَةٍ فَبِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِيكُمْ; whatever muṣībah reaches you is because of what your hands have earned 42:30.

Here, muṣībah is not a loose, unrelated event; it is connected with human earning. Root-wise, something has struck the human being, but the Qur’an also directs attention to the relation between event and action. This should not be oversimplified; not every hardship should be assigned to a specific sin without knowledge.

Ḥaythu Aṣāba: Direction of Will and Destination

In Surat Ṣād, about the wind placed at Solomon’s command, the Qur’an says: فَسَخَّرْنَا لَهُ الرِّيحَ تَجْرِي بِأَمْرِهِ رُخَاءً حَيْثُ أَصَابَ; We subjected the wind to him, flowing gently by his command wherever he intended or aimed 38:36.

Here, aṣāba does not mean calamity or rain. It is close to intention, direction, and aiming: the wind moved by his command toward the direction he intended and targeted. This use shows an important layer of the root: Ṣ W B is not only contact after movement; it may also refer to aiming the will toward a destination.

Ṣawāb: Correct Speech That Hits the Target

Surat al-Nabaʾ says: إِلَّا مَنْ أَذِنَ لَهُ الرَّحْمَٰنُ وَقَالَ صَوَابًا; except one whom the Merciful permits and who says ṣawāb 78:38.

Ṣawāb here means correct, truthful, and fitting speech. Root-wise, ṣawāb is speech that hits the target of truth and does not deviate from the proper place. It is not merely dry “correctness.” It is speech that lands where it should, strikes the target of right, and stays outside falsehood.

Ṣayyib and Maṭar

صَيِّب is not identical with maṭar. Maṭar more generally refers to rain or raining, and depending on context it may be mercy or punishment. Ṣayyib emphasizes heavy pouring descent from above. In al-Baqarah 2:19, the context of darkness, thunder, and lightning shows that ṣayyib is heavy, surrounding rainfall.

For Forqan translation, ṣayyib should not be rendered only as plain “rain.” “Heavy pouring rain,” “a downpour from the sky,” or “a sky-borne downpour” is more precise.

Difference from Nearby Roots

N Z L emphasizes descending from above. Ṣ W B includes descent, but also reaching and striking the proper place. H Ṭ L concerns continuous raining or rainfall; ṣayyib emphasizes heavy pouring descent. W B L may indicate heavy rain or a heavy consequence; ṣayyib is understood from descent and striking.

M Ṭ R is the more general word for rain. Ṣayyib carries a stronger image of pouring descent and sky-borne impact. W Q AYN (W Q ʿ) concerns falling, occurring, or happening; iṣābah emphasizes precise reaching or striking a target. L Q Y is meeting or encountering, more mutual; iṣābah is more one-directional, something reaches someone or something.

R M Y is throwing or casting; iṣābah is the result of the thrown thing reaching the target. KH Ṭ A is error, missing the target. Ṣawāb and iṣābah stand opposite error: ṣawāb means hitting the target of truth. S D D is straightness and sound direction; sadīd is firm, correct speech, while ṣawāb is speech that strikes truth. Q Ṣ D emphasizes intention and direction toward a goal; iṣābah also includes reaching or aiming at the target.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, Ṣ W B should not be forced into one English word. In صَيِّب, the heavy pouring descent should be preserved. In أَصَابَ, depending on context, “reached,” “struck,” “afflicted,” “hit,” “intended,” or “aimed” may fit.

In مُصِيبَة, one should not translate only as “disaster.” Muṣībah is an event that reaches a human being, usually a hard or unwelcome event. In صَوَاب, correctness should be kept together with the idea of hitting the target. In some contexts, “correct speech,” “fitting speech,” or “speech aligned with truth” is suitable. In حَيْثُ أَصَابَ, it should not be confused with calamity; the meaning is closer to direction of will and aiming at a destination.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

صَيِّب (ṣayyib): heavy pouring rain, downpour from the sky, sky-borne downpour.

صَوْب (ṣawb): direction, side, descent, rainfall, reaching from a specified direction.

صَوَّبَ / تَصْوِيب (ṣawwaba / taṣwīb): directed, lowered, aimed, judged correct; directing, judging correct, setting a ruling upon its case.

أَصَابَ / يُصِيبُ (aṣāba / yuṣību): struck, reached, afflicted, hit the target, intended or aimed in a specific context.

إِصَابَة (iṣābah): striking, reaching, correct contact.

مُصِيبَة (muṣībah): a calamity, a reaching event, an event that strikes a person, hardship.

صَوَاب (ṣawāb): correct, fitting, aligned with truth, hitting the target of truth.

أَوْ كَصَيِّبٍ مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ: or like a heavy downpour from the sky.

حَيْثُ أَصَابَ: wherever he intended; or whichever direction he aimed.

وَقَالَ صَوَابًا: and spoke correctly; or spoke fittingly, aligned with truth.

Linguistic Summary

The root Ṣ W B denotes descending, pouring down, reaching, striking, direction toward a destination, and hitting the target. At the material level, ṣayyib is rain that pours from the sky and reaches the earth. Aṣāba means that something strikes or reaches a target, place, or person. Muṣībah is an event that descends upon or strikes a human being. Ṣawāb is understood from the same field: speech or judgment that hits the target of truth and settles in its proper place.

Its difference from nuzūl is that nuzūl mainly shows descent, while Ṣ W B also carries reaching and striking. Its difference from khaṭaʾ is that khaṭaʾ misses the target, while ṣawāb hits the target of meaning and truth. The central image is this: something comes from a direction toward its place, descends, reaches, and strikes its target or proper position.
