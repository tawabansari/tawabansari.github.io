Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root W Q D

Kindling, igniting, the rising of fire from fuel, and the persistence of flame

Important Forms from This Root

وَقَدَ / يَقِدُ (waqada / yaqidu)

waqada / yaqidu; fire kindled, became lit, or took flame.

أَوْقَدَ / يُوقِدُ (awqada / yūqidu)

awqada / yūqidu; kindled, lit, set aflame.

وَقُود (waqūd)

waqūd; fuel, the material by which fire remains alive and sustained.

وَقِيد (waqīd)

waqīd; kindled fire, visible flame, or state of burning.

مُوقَدَة (mūqadah)

mūqadah; kindled, inflamed, kept actively burning.

استَوْقَدَ (astawqada)

istawqada; sought or kindled fire; tried to obtain light.

Central Meaning

The root W Q D means kindling, setting aflame, the rising of fire, the lighting of combustible matter, the persistence of flame, and the effort to bring light.

This root is connected with living fire: fire that comes out of hidden material, rises, produces light and heat, and sometimes, if it crosses the proper limit, burns and destroys.

So W Q D is not merely “turning something on.” It contains the movement from darkness to flame, from hiddenness to appearance, from stored material to active energy, and from a small spark to a sustained fire.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of W Q D is the activation of fire from within combustible material. Fire in this root is not simply light. Fire must seize something, consume something, and turn it into flame.

Without fuel, fire does not remain. For this reason, وَقُود (waqūd) is a crucial word: it is what feeds fire and makes the continuity of flame possible.

From this view, W Q D is tied to light and heat, the consumption of material and its transformation into flame, and the persistence of ignition. Not every kindling is merciful. Sometimes fire is for light; sometimes for punishment, war, trial, testing, or destruction.

Material Image

The material image of this root is clear: someone prepares wood, oil, metal, or a material able to be affected by heat. Then fire reaches it, the fire rises, flame appears, light and heat emerge, and the material either burns, gives light, or changes state under heat.

Several stages appear in this image: there is a material with capacity for burning or heat; a spark or fire reaches it; the material receives flame or heat; the fire becomes alive and rises; and if fuel continues, the fire remains.

From here we can see that W Q D does not only point to the instant of lighting. It points to the whole process of ignition and the continuation of fire.

Classical Arabic Note

In the life of ancient Arabs, fire was vital. It provided warmth, cooking, night light, security, guidance for travelers, and sometimes a sign of hospitality. Kindling fire at night could signal presence, life, shelter, and direction.

But the same fire could also signal war, threat, and destruction. A fire lit for a guest is not the same as a fire lit for trial or war.

In older Arabic usage, this root is also connected with steadiness of flame and intensity of heat. Such evidence may illuminate the linguistic atmosphere of the root, but it should not restrict Qur’anic meaning.

Qur’anic Usage

The Qur’an uses W Q D in several important fields: material fire and its fuel, the fire of punishment, fire of war and trial, fire used for melting and separating impurity, light and a lamp kindled from pure material, and the human effort to light a path.

One important verse concerns fuel:

فَاتَّقُوا النَّارَ الَّتِي وَقُودُهَا النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَةُ (fāttaqūā al-nāra allatī waqūduhā al-nāsu wal-ḥijārah) 2:24

Here, وَقُود (waqūd) is not simply fire; it is the fuel of fire, the thing by which the fire continues. The image is weighty: human beings and stones stand in the place of the material that feeds the fire.

Istawqada: Seeking Light

One important form of this root is استَوْقَدَ (astawqada):

مَثَلُهُمْ كَمَثَلِ الَّذِي اسْتَوْقَدَ نَارًا (mathaluhum kamathali alladhī astawqada nāran) 2:17

Istawqada is not only mechanical lighting. In this form, seeking and effort are also present. Someone wants to light his surroundings by fire. But in the continuation of the verse, the light is taken from him and he remains in darkness.

The human being may seek light, but if that light is separated from truth, it does not remain. He may create a flame, yet that flame may not carry him to true light.

Fire of War and Trial

The Qur’an also speaks of kindling fire for war:

كُلَّمَا أَوْقَدُوا نَارًا لِّلْحَرْبِ أَطْفَأَهَا اللَّهُ (kullamā awqadū nāran lilḥarbi ʾaṭfahā allāh) 5:64

Here fire is not only physical fire. It is the image of war, hostility, trial, and the inflaming of violence. People light the fire of war: they gather the materials of hostility, strike the spark, and seek to pull society into flame.

Against this kindling, The God extinguishes. Not every flame is light; some flames are only destruction.

Fire for Melting and Purifying

One important Qur’anic use of this root connects it with melting and the separation of impurity:

وَمِمَّا يُوقِدُونَ عَلَيْهِ فِي النَّارِ ابْتِغَاءَ حِلْيَةٍ أَوْ مَتَاعٍ زَبَدٌ مِّثْلُهُ (wamimmā yūqidūna ʿalayhi fī al-nāri abtighāʾa ḥilyah aw matāʿin zabadun mithluhu) 13:17

In this verse, kindling fire is not only for burning. It is for melting, separating, and revealing impurities. Metal is placed under heat, its inner material becomes manifest, and foam or scum rises.

Fire may destroy, but it may also reveal the nature of material. Heat can test something, bring impurity to the surface, and make the truth of the material visible.

Light, Oil, and Pure Kindling

In the Verse of Light, the root W Q D appears in one of the Qur’an’s most beautiful images:

يُوقَدُ مِن شَجَرَةٍ مُّبَارَكَةٍ زَيْتُونَةٍ (yūqadu min shajarah mubārakah zaytūnah) 24:35

Here kindling is connected with light, pure oil, the blessed tree, and the lamp. This is not the fire of war or punishment. This fire serves light.

This verse shows that W Q D may also have a very delicate field: a light that rises from a pure and blessed material. This kindling is not destructive; it is guiding.

Kindled Fire and Hearts

In Surat al-Humazah, kindled fire draws near to the inner human being:

نَارُ اللَّهِ الْمُوقَدَةُ (nāru allāh al-mūqadah) 104:6

The following verse says: الَّتِي تَطَّلِعُ عَلَى الْأَفْئِدَةِ (allatī taṭṭaliʿu ʿalā al-afʾidah) 104:7

مُوقَدَة (mūqadah) shows a fire that has been kindled and remains in its burning. It is not cold or extinguished; it is active, flaming, and sustained.

Here fire does not only burn the body. It reaches the afʾidah, the inner center of perception and orientation. This use shows that W Q D can be connected with the human interior.

Difference from Nearby Roots

N W R is closer to light, manifestation, and guidance; light does not necessarily burn. Ḍ W ʾ is closer to radiance and shining. SH ʿ L is closer to flame and tongues of fire. Ḥ R Q is closer to burning and consuming. S ʿ R is closer to intense ignition, especially in punishment or severe fire. Ṭ F ʾ concerns extinguishing fire or light. Ṣ H R is closer to melting and liquefying under heat.

W Q D emphasizes the kindling and activation of fire from fuel: the moment and process by which fire becomes alive, takes something as its fuel, and remains if the fuel continues.

W Q D and Guidance

This root distinguishes several kinds of light and fire in the Qur’an: light that rises from truth and pure material; light the human being makes by himself but that does not remain; fire kindled for war and trial; fire used for testing and purification; and fire of punishment that takes human beings and stones as its fuel.

In Surat al-Baqarah, someone kindles a fire but his light is taken. In Surat al-Nūr, a lamp is kindled from a blessed tree. In Surat al-Raʿd, fire is used to separate scum and impurity. In Surat al-Māʾidah, the fire of war is kindled and The God extinguishes it.

These images must not be collapsed into one. Not every brightness is guidance. Not every fire is merciful light. Not every flame leads the human being to the path.

Connection to Life Today

Today W Q D can be seen in energy and fuel, media that inflame public thought, wars kindled by words, fear, propaganda, and hatred, industry and technology, and the human interior.

The root W Q D teaches that lighting something is not always good. We must ask: what is being kindled, what fuel keeps it alive, is it lasting or only a passing spark, and is its result light or burning?

If fire is for guidance, it may show the way. If it is for war, envy, arrogance, usury, domination, or trial, it burns society. If it is for testing and purification, it brings impurity to the surface so truth and falsehood can be separated.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

وَقَدَ (waqada): kindled; took flame; became lit.

أَوْقَدَ (awqada): kindled; lit; set aflame.

يُوقَدُ (yūqadu): is kindled; is lit; takes flame.

يُوقِدُونَ عَلَيْهِ (yūqidūna ʿalayhi): they kindle fire upon it; they heat it in the fire.

وَقُود (waqūd): fuel; material of fire; what keeps fire alive and sustained.

مُوقَدَة (mūqadah): kindled; flaming; living and active fire; kept burning.

استَوْقَدَ (istawqada): kindled a fire; sought to kindle fire; tried to obtain light.

Summary

The root W Q D shows that fire is not only a natural phenomenon; it is a pattern of meaning. Fire can give light, show the way, warm, and make life possible. But the same fire can burn, produce war, reach the heart, and turn the human being into the fuel of punishment.

In this root, we must ask what is being kindled, what its fuel is, whether the flame is lasting or passing, whether the fire is for guidance, war, punishment, or purification, and whether its result is light or destruction.

The main question of this root is therefore: is the fire we have kindled a lamp, a blaze, or a fire for being purified?
