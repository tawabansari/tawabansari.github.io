Foundational Meaning

The root A Y Y appears in the Qur'an mostly in the forms āyah and āyāt. Its foundational meaning is a sign, a clear marker, a remarkable indication, and something that guides the human being beyond itself toward a greater truth. In the Qur'an, an āyah is not only a sentence of the Arabic Qur'anic text or a number in the muṣḥaf. An āyah is any sign which, if seen correctly, carries the human being beyond itself and guides them toward truth, power, lordship, guidance, accounting, or the message of The God.

Important Qur'anic Forms

Important forms of this root in the Qur'an include āyah, āyāt, āyātunā, āyātihi, āyātikum, and āyāt Allāh. Āyah is singular and points to one clear sign or guiding marker. Āyāt is its plural and can refer to the signs of The God in nature, history, the human self, the fates of peoples, the miracles of messengers, and also the revealed parts of revelation.

Linguistic Core

The linguistic core of āyah is a sign that indicates something greater than itself. The āyah itself is not the end of meaning; it is a doorway of recognition. When the human being sees an āyah, they should not stop at its surface. They should ask what truth this sign points toward. The sun, night and day, the reviving of the earth, the histories of peoples, the rescue of Moses, the creation of the human being, Jesus and his mother, and the words of revelation can all be āyāt, because each guides the human being toward a truth beyond itself.

Material Image

The material image of this root is a marker on a road. A road sign is not itself the destination, but without it the traveler may lose the way. An āyah is like this: something placed before the eye, ear, intellect, or heart of the human being so that it may carry them toward a meaning beyond it. If the human being sees the sign but does not pass beyond it, they have reduced the āyah to its surface.

Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic usage, āyah is connected with a sign, marker, wondrous matter, clear indication, and something by which recognition is gained. In the Qur'an, this meaning expands, and āyah is used for every sign of The God in the world, history, the human being, and revelation.

Signs in the Horizons and Nature

One foundational field of this root is signs in the horizons and nature:

إِنَّ فِي خَلْقِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَاخْتِلَافِ اللَّيْلِ وَالنَّهَارِ لَآيَاتٍ لِّأُولِي الْأَلْبَابِ

Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for people of deep understanding.

Āl ʿImrān 3:190

In this verse, āyāt is not limited to parts of the Qur'anic text. The creation of the heavens and the earth, and the alternation of night and day, are āyāt. The world itself carries signs. A person of deep understanding moves from seeing order, measure, movement, harmony, and change in the world toward a truth beyond raw matter.

Surat al-Rūm also says:

وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ أَنْ خَلَقَكُم مِّن تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ إِذَا أَنتُم بَشَرٌ تَنتَشِرُونَ

And among His signs is that He created you from dust; then suddenly you are human beings spreading out.

Al-Rūm 30:20

وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ خَلْقُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَاخْتِلَافُ أَلْسِنَتِكُمْ وَأَلْوَانِكُمْ

And among His signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the difference of your languages and your colors.

Al-Rūm 30:22

Here the signs of The God are seen in the creation of the human being, the difference of languages, colors, and the spread of humanity. Human diversity is therefore also an āyah, not a pretext for ethnic or racial superiority.

Signs Within the Human Self

The second field is signs in the human self and inner being:

وَفِي الْأَرْضِ آيَاتٌ لِّلْمُوقِنِينَ

And in the earth are signs for those who have certainty.

Al-Dhāriyāt 51:20

And after it:

وَفِي أَنفُسِكُمْ ۚ أَفَلَا تُبْصِرُونَ

And within yourselves; do you not see?

Al-Dhāriyāt 51:21

The structure of these two verses shows that the earth and the human self are both fields of signs. The human being is not guided only through an outward gaze. Within themselves as well, they have signs of poverty, need, awareness, conscience, order, weakness, growth, death, and return.

Historical Signs

The third field is historical signs and the fates of peoples. The Qur'an repeatedly makes destruction, rescue, victory, collapse, and the destiny of communities into signs. History is not only story; it is āyah. The past should show the boundaries of truth, wrongdoing, transgression, patience, and final outcome.

About Pharaoh it says:

فَأَرَاهُ الْآيَةَ الْكُبْرَىٰ

So he showed him the great sign.

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:20

The āyah here is a clear sign in the encounter between a messenger and tyranny. Yet even the great sign does not lead to faith if the heart is closed.

Signs of Messengership and Miracles

The fourth field is āyāt as signs of messengership and miracles:

وَآتَيْنَا مُوسَىٰ تِسْعَ آيَاتٍ بَيِّنَاتٍ

And We gave Moses nine clear signs.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:101

The signs of Moses were not merely verses of a book; they were clear signs of messengership. This use shows that āyah in the Qur'an can be an extraordinary sign, a historical sign, or a clear proof for the truthfulness of a messenger.

Jesus and His Mother as a Sign

About Jesus it says:

وَجَعَلْنَا ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ وَأُمَّهُ آيَةً

And We made the son of Mary and his mother a sign.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:50

Here Jesus and his mother themselves are an āyah. The āyah is not a text and not a sentence; it is a human and historical reality that points to the power and wisdom of The God. Their being an āyah shows the station of being a sign, not divinity. The danger is always that the human being places the sign where the truth to which the sign should guide them belongs.

Signs of Revelation and the Book

The fifth field is the signs of revelation and the Book:

تِلْكَ آيَاتُ الْكِتَابِ الْمُبِينِ

These are the signs of the clear Book.

Yūsuf 12:1

كِتَابٌ فُصِّلَتْ آيَاتُهُ قُرْآنًا عَرَبِيًّا لِّقَوْمٍ يَعْلَمُونَ

A Book whose signs have been made detailed, an Arabic Qur'an for a people who know.

Fuṣṣilat 41:3

Here āyāt refers to the signs and parts of revealed revelation. Yet even in this use, an āyah is not only a verse number. The signs of the Book are signs of guidance: units of message that bring the human being toward truth, furqān, remembrance, judgment, mercy, and responsibility.

So when we say "a Qur'anic āyah," we should not reduce it to a number or printed unit. A Qur'anic āyah is also a sign: speech that should carry the human being to a truth beyond letters, sound, and script.

Recitation of the Signs

The sixth field is the recitation of the signs:

يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِهِ

He recites His signs to them.

Āl ʿImrān 3:164

Reciting the signs means bringing and reading the signs of revelation to people in sequence. These signs are not only for hearing sound. They are for purification, teaching the Book, wisdom, and bringing the human being out of misguidance. When the āyah is recited, it should leave an effect of guidance in the soul and society.

Denying the Signs

The seventh field is denying the signs:

وَالَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا أُولَـٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ

And those who counted Our signs as false, they are the companions of the Fire.

Al-Baqarah 2:39

In this verse, the signs can refer to the signs of The God and His clear messages. Denying the signs means that the human being counts as untrue a sign that should lead them to truth and breaks its credibility. Thus āyah has an important connection with the root K DH B: the sign comes, but the denier rejects it.

Another verse says:

وَالَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا صُمٌّ وَبُكْمٌ فِي الظُّلُمَاتِ

And those who counted Our signs as false are deaf and mute in darknesses.

Al-Anʿām 6:39

Someone who understands the signs only as "text" makes this field small. The signs of The God are spread through the Qur'an, the world, history, and the human self. Denying the signs is closing the way of receiving truth from all these fields.

Turning Away From the Signs

The eighth field is turning away from the signs:

وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّن ذُكِّرَ بِآيَاتِ رَبِّهِ ثُمَّ أَعْرَضَ عَنْهَا

And who is more unjust than one who was reminded with the signs of his Lord, then turned away from them?

Al-Sajdah 32:22

Turning away from the signs differs from denying them. Denial counts the sign as untrue and breaks its credibility. Turning away means refusing to face the sign and not placing oneself in its field. A human being may hear the āyah and say it is false; that is denial. Or they may hear the āyah and pass by it with indifference; that is turning away. Both cut the relation between the human being and the sign, but by two different paths.

Signs and Types of Reception

The ninth field is the signs and the type of reception among their audiences:

إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَاتٍ لِّقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ

Indeed, in that are signs for a people who reflect.

Al-Raʿd 13:3

إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَاتٍ لِّقَوْمٍ يَعْقِلُونَ

Indeed, in that are signs for a people who use reason.

Al-Rūm 30:24

إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَاتٍ لِّقَوْمٍ يَسْمَعُونَ

Indeed, in that are signs for a people who listen.

Yūnus 10:67

These structures show that an āyah is not only the existence of a sign; right reception is also necessary. Sometimes an āyah requires reflection, because the human being must follow a movement, change, and outcome. Sometimes it requires reason, because the human being must understand relations, measures, and connections. Sometimes it requires listening, because a message or warning must be received with openness. The sign exists, but not everyone benefits from it.

Thus the āyah in the Qur'an creates a two-sided relation: on one side, The God places the sign in the world, history, the self, and revelation; on the other side, the human being must see, hear, reflect, use reason, and pass from the outward form of the sign to its meaning.

Distinguishing Qur'anic Uses

The uses of āyah must be distinguished. An āyah can be a natural sign, such as night and day, rain, earth, sky, and creation. It can be an inner sign, such as the human self and the stages of creation. It can be a historical sign, such as the fate of peoples. It can be a sign of messengership, such as the signs of Moses. It can be a specific person or event, such as Jesus and his mother. It can be the revealed parts of revelation, such as the signs of the clear Book. All of these share one core: something that points toward a truth beyond itself.

Structural Notes

Structurally, the expression āyāt Allāh, the signs of The God, is very important. The signs of The God are not owned by anyone else. Nature is not separate from The God, nor is history, nor are the signs of revelation. When the Qur'an says āyātunā or āyātihi, the signs are attributed to The God. Their authority is from The God, and they should guide toward Him, not toward making an idol out of the sign itself.

Nearby Terms

Āyah differs from several nearby roots and terms. Bayyinah is a clear proof and evident argument; an āyah can be a sign that leads to proof. Burhān is a decisive and clarifying proof; āyah is broader and can be natural, historical, or revelatory. Sulṭān is the force of proof or valid authority; āyah is the sign itself. Mathal is an example and teaching image; āyah is a sign that carries beyond itself. ʿIbrah is the crossing from an event to lesson and meaning; an āyah can be the material of ʿibrah. Dhikr is remembrance and reminder; an āyah can be something that brings the human being to remembrance. Kitāb is a written body, ruling, or revelation; āyāt are its sign-bearing and guiding parts. Furqān is the criterion that separates truth from falsehood; āyāt are signs that guide the human being toward this separation.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

In Forqan translation, āyah should not always be translated as "verse" in the sense of a textual unit. In many contexts, "sign," "clear sign," "marker," "āyah," or "guiding sign" is more fitting. When the context is revelation, "āyāt" may be preserved, but the explanation should make clear that Qur'anic āyāt are also signs of guidance, not merely numbered textual units. Āyāt Allāh is best rendered as "signs of The God" or "āyāt of The God," according to context. Āyah kubrā can be "great sign." Āyāt bayyināt is best rendered as "clear signs" or "clear āyāt."

Linguistic Summary

The root A Y Y in the Qur'an indicates a sign, a clear marker, and something that guides the human being toward a truth beyond itself. An āyah is not only a sentence of the Qur'anic text. An āyah can be in the sky, the earth, the human self, history, the messengership of prophets, wondrous events, and revealed revelation. An āyah always calls the human being to pass from surface to meaning. The great danger is that the human being sees the āyah but does not pass beyond it; or denies it; or turns away from it; or reduces it to text, number, script, or habit. In the Qur'anic view, the world is full of signs and the Qur'an itself is clear signs. Both are so that the human being may find a path from sign toward truth.
