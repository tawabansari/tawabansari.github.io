Qur’anic root س ب ق

Preceding, getting ahead and outstripping: from racing and temporal priority to taking the lead in good deeds and the impossibility of outstripping The God’s will

The three-letter Arabic root س ب ق concerns one thing preceding another. In a race, someone reaching the destination sooner has outstripped the others. In time, an event occurring before another precedes it. In action, someone beginning before others or taking the lead is called sābiq. Getting ahead, however, is not always a virtue: a person can lead in good conduct or precede others in wrongdoing.

The Qur’an uses the root in various settings: racing and running, leading in good deeds, preceding others in faith, temporal priority, anticipating a command, the arrival of an appointed term, and people’s inability to escape The God’s power and judgement. Some verses command people to outstrip others in good deeds; others establish that no one can get ahead of the appointed time or divine will.

The root occurs 37 times in 35 Qur’anic verses, in seven structural groups. Principal forms include سَبَقَ and inflections such as سَبَقَتْ, سَبَقُوا, سَبَقُونَا, يَسْبِقُونَهُ and تَسْبِقُ; Form III سَابِقُوا; Form VIII فَاسْتَبِقُوا, نَسْتَبِقُ, اسْتَبَقَا and اسْتَبَقُوا; the active participle سَابِق and its plurals; السَّابِقَات; the passive participle مَسْبُوقِين; and the verbal noun سَبْقًا.

Arabic also has words such as سِبَاق, مُسَابَقَة, سَابِقَة, تَسَابُق and سَبَق meaning a race prize, but not all these forms occur in the Qur’an.

1. The semantic core and usage in early Arabic

Getting ahead in movement and time

Arabic سَبَقَ فُلَانٌ فُلَانًا means that one person preceded another. This can concern movement and racing, or performing a task both are striving to accomplish.

سَبَقَ الْفَرَسُ describes a horse getting ahead of the others in a race. سَابِق can likewise describe a horse or rider reaching the destination before the others.

Temporal سَبَقَ هَذَا ذَاكَ means this thing existed or occurred before that one. If rain begins before a traveller arrives, for example, the rain precedes the arrival. No race exists between the rain and traveller; only the order of two events is stated.

The same meaning applies to people. Someone present in a task before another, or beginning it first, can be sābiq relative to that person. This priority itself states neither the action’s correctness nor its wrongness, only that it happened earlier.

Arabic lexical sources explain سَبْق through تَقَدُّم, precedence, with getting ahead in movement one clear use. The same field also includes outstripping others in knowledge, generosity, conduct, social standing and other human activities.

A horse’s lead in pre-Islamic poetry

A poem by the pre-Islamic poet al-Nābighah al-Dhubyānī, praising al-Nuʿmān ibn al-Mundhir, says:

«إِلَّا لِمِثْلِكَ أَوْ مَنْ أَنْتَ سَابِقُهُ
سَبْقَ الْجَوَادِ إِذَا اسْتَوْلَى عَلَى الْأَمَدِ»

“Except for someone like you, or someone you outstrip, as a swift horse outstrips others when it reaches the course’s end.”

The poet draws on horse racing to describe one person’s superiority over others. سَابِقُهُ concerns someone preceding another, and سَبْقَ الْجَوَادِ depicts a swift horse’s lead. The verse follows the poem’s exemplum concerning Solomon and its preceding discourse. Commentaries debate exactly how إِلَّا connects with that discourse; nevertheless, سَابِقُهُ clearly denotes outstripping, while سَبْقَ الْجَوَادِ clearly supplies a racing image.

الْأَمَد here denotes the race’s end or limit. Outstripping therefore is more than moving quickly: one participant’s position relative to the others matters.

This witness places two uses together: a horse literally getting ahead in a race, and a person figuratively surpassing others in capacity or standing. A familiar image from Arab life explains the latter.

Horse racing was known in pre-Islamic Arab life. Accounts of the Dāḥis and al-Ghabrāʾ race also show how horse competition, judging precedence and a race prize could connect with social relations and tribal disputes. The details survive in later historical sources, however, and cannot all be treated without examination as direct, undisputed accounts from the pre-Islamic period.

Sabq and sabaq: getting ahead and the race prize

Arabic lexical accounts distinguish two similar forms. سَبْق, with an unvowelled bāʾ, is the verbal noun of سَبَقَ and denotes getting ahead. سَبَق, with a vowel on the bāʾ, can denote a prize or property placed in competition for the race’s winner. Recording the prize sense in later dictionaries does not alone establish when that use first arose.

The act of getting ahead and property potentially awarded for victory are therefore distinct concepts. Someone can race without a prize being set; a race prize itself is not the act of running.

This distinction matters for فَالسَّابِقَاتِ سَبْقًا in Al-Nāziʿāt. سَبْقًا there is a verbal noun concerning outstripping, rather than a race prize.

Precedence and an early beginning

Arabic lexical accounts also apply the sabaq family to date palms bearing earlier than other trees. The tree is not racing another; its fruit appears earlier.

Having سَابِقَة in an activity can likewise mean that someone entered it before others or has a prior record in that field.

These uses show precedence concerning movement, time or action. One thing can precede another even without a race.

The root’s semantic core therefore should not be restricted to running quickly. Speed can cause someone to get ahead, but sabaq concerns precedence and being in front.

2. Istabaqa: racing, getting ahead and hastening towards a destination

The root’s Form VIII occurs five times in the Qur’an. Its uses concern either people’s actual movement or taking the lead in an activity.

Joseph’s brothers racing

After Joseph’s brothers return to their father, Yūsuf 12:17 quotes them saying:

«قَالُوا يَا أَبَانَا إِنَّا ذَهَبْنَا نَسْتَبِقُ وَتَرَكْنَا يُوسُفَ عِنْدَ مَتَاعِنَا فَأَكَلَهُ الذِّئْبُ وَمَا أَنْتَ بِمُؤْمِنٍ لَنَا وَلَوْ كُنَّا صَادِقِينَ»

“They said: ‘Our father, we went to race each other and left Joseph by our belongings, then the wolf ate him. You will not believe us, even if we are truthful.’”

(Yūsuf 12:17)

نَسْتَبِقُ occurs in the brothers’ speech. Its clear meaning is participating in a race or trying to get ahead of each other.

Lexical sources propose a running race or competing in archery. Istabaqa can express competition without itself identifying the equipment or method. The verse does not specify exactly how they competed.

The speakers’ identity also matters. The Qur’an’s story makes clear that they gave their father a false account of Joseph’s fate. The report’s falsehood nevertheless does not change نَسْتَبِقُ’s lexical meaning.

The speakers’ claim must therefore be distinguished from the word they use. They speak of competing; whether a competition actually occurred concerns the truth of their speech.

وَاسْتَبَقَا الْبَابَ: racing with different aims

Yūsuf 12:25 says:

«وَاسْتَبَقَا الْبَابَ وَقَدَّتْ قَمِيصَهُ مِنْ دُبُرٍ وَأَلْفَيَا سَيِّدَهَا لَدَى الْبَابِ»

“Both raced towards the door, trying to reach it ahead of each other; she tore his shirt from behind, and both found her husband at the door.”

(Yūsuf 12:25; part of the verse)

اسْتَبَقَا is a dual verb describing Joseph and the woman. Both move towards one destination: the door.

Their purposes differ, however. Joseph seeks to leave the situation, while the woman pursues him.

This matters for understanding the root. Istibāq denotes competing or getting ahead to reach a destination, without determining people’s intentions.

Two people can hasten towards one point, with one seeking to leave and the other to prevent him.

Istabaqā also does not itself say who arrived first. The verse states both people’s movement; the story’s continuation explains what happened at the door.

Form VIII can therefore express an attempt to outstrip without guaranteeing either party’s success within the verb itself.

فَاسْتَبَقُوا الصِّرَاطَ: movement without sight

Yā Sīn 36:66 says:

«وَلَوْ نَشَاءُ لَطَمَسْنَا عَلَىٰ أَعْيُنِهِمْ فَاسْتَبَقُوا الصِّرَاطَ فَأَنَّىٰ يُبْصِرُونَ»

“Had We willed, We would surely have effaced their eyes; then they would have raced towards the path. How then could they see?”

(Yā Sīn 36:66)

اسْتَبَقُوا concerns people who, in the situation hypothesised, have lost their sight and wish to traverse the path.

Lexically, getting ahead or hastening towards a destination remains present. فَأَنَّىٰ يُبْصِرُونَ, however, shows movement towards the path confronting an inability to see.

Two interpretations concern the effacement of sight: loss of bodily vision and loss of the ability to see the path of truth. This disagreement concerns context and the meanings of effacement and seeing.

In either interpretation, istibāq itself does not mean being guided. Someone can hasten towards a path, but haste alone does not guarantee correctly reaching the destination.

This prevents istibāq being translated in every verse as ethical leadership or successful attainment of a goal.

3. فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ: taking the lead in good deeds

One prominent Qur’anic use commands taking the lead in good deeds.

Good deeds and orientation

Al-Baqarah 2:148 says:

«وَلِكُلٍّ وِجْهَةٌ هُوَ مُوَلِّيهَا فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ أَيْنَ مَا تَكُونُوا يَأْتِ بِكُمُ اللَّهُ جَمِيعًا إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ»

“Each has a direction towards which he turns, so outstrip one another in good deeds. Wherever you are, The God will bring you all together. The God is indeed powerful over everything.”

(Al-Baqarah 2:148)

This occurs among the qiblah verses. The Qur’an discusses orientation and then commands فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ.

الْخَيْرَاتِ identifies what is pursued. Istabiqū itself does not mean goodness; outstripping in good deeds makes this precedence valuable.

Comparison with Yūsuf 12:25 clarifies this. Two people race to the door there with different motives; in Al-Baqarah 2:148, good deeds are the object of striving.

After discussing orientation, the Qur’an calls its audience to action. This connection nevertheless does not make the surrounding qiblah commands merely symbolic or remove their spatial significance. Both orientation and taking the lead in good deeds occur in the same passage.

Racing in good deeds also need not involve only one winner with everyone else excluded from goodness. Several people can take the lead and all act worthily.

The racing image highlights acting and getting ahead. Horse-racing rules, such as every participant except one losing, should not be imposed on good deeds without evidence.

Good deeds amid different paths and laws

Al-Māʾidah 5:48 says:

«لِكُلٍّ جَعَلْنَا مِنْكُمْ شِرْعَةً وَمِنْهَاجًا وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَجَعَلَكُمْ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً وَلَٰكِنْ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ فِي مَا آتَاكُمْ فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ إِلَى اللَّهِ مَرْجِعُكُمْ جَمِيعًا فَيُنَبِّئُكُمْ بِمَا كُنْتُمْ فِيهِ تَخْتَلِفُونَ»

“For each of you We appointed a way and a course. Had The God willed, He would have made you one community, but He intends to test you through what He has given you. So outstrip one another in good deeds. Your return, all together, is to The God; He will then inform you concerning your disagreements.”

(Al-Māʾidah 5:48; part of the verse)

The same فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ appears here, but in a different context from Al-Baqarah 2:148.

Al-Māʾidah discusses شِرْعَةً وَمِنْهَاجًا, a way and course, the possibility of making all people one community, and testing them through what they received. It then commands outstripping in good deeds and recalls everyone’s return to The God.

Taking the lead in goodness is thus placed amid the reality of different paths and human responsibilities.

The root س ب ق nevertheless cannot supply an entire theory of religion, revealed law or human salvation. The verb plainly means getting ahead and taking the lead. Its subject is good deeds; other expressions supply the broader setting.

Different laws and paths also do not make every claim equally right or wrong. The verse itself discusses testing and return to The God for differences to be made clear.

What matters for this root is that both verses direct precedence towards good deeds, rather than dominance based on a group’s name or identity.

سَابِقُوا إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ: racing towards forgiveness

Al-Ḥadīd 57:21 says:

«سَابِقُوا إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ وَجَنَّةٍ عَرْضُهَا كَعَرْضِ السَّمَاءِ وَالْأَرْضِ أُعِدَّتْ لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَرُسُلِهِ»

“Race one another towards forgiveness from your Lord and a garden whose breadth is like the breadth of heaven and earth, prepared for those who believe in The God and His Messengers.”

(Al-Ḥadīd 57:21; part of the verse)

Unlike Form VIII اسْتَبِقُوا in Al-Baqarah and Al-Māʾidah, this verse uses Form III سَابِقُوا.

Both can mean competing and outstripping. The formation alone does not assign one exclusively to worldly activities and the other to the Hereafter, or one to individual and the other to collective competition.

إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ identifies the destination, with the garden alongside it. The verb expresses striving to get ahead; forgiveness and a good outcome identify its purpose.

Āl ʿImrān 3:133 also has the similar وَسَارِعُوا إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ, but that verb belongs to س ر ع. Comparison helps distinguish speed from precedence.

Musāraʿah more strongly highlights hastening, whereas musābaqah can also picture getting ahead and competing to reach a goal. This is a useful tendency, although the two verbs overlap in some contexts.

سَابِقٌ بِالْخَيْرَاتِ: taking the lead in goodness

Fāṭir 35:32 says:

«ثُمَّ أَوْرَثْنَا الْكِتَابَ الَّذِينَ اصْطَفَيْنَا مِنْ عِبَادِنَا فَمِنْهُمْ ظَالِمٌ لِنَفْسِهِ وَمِنْهُمْ مُقْتَصِدٌ وَمِنْهُمْ سَابِقٌ بِالْخَيْرَاتِ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَضْلُ الْكَبِيرُ»

“Then We gave the Book as an inheritance to those of Our servants whom We chose. Some wrong themselves, some are measured in their conduct, and some take the lead in good deeds by The God’s permission. That is the great bounty.”

(Fāṭir 35:32)

سَابِق is an active participle here, alongside ظَالِمٌ لِنَفْسِهِ, one wronging himself, and مُقْتَصِدٌ, one following a measured course.

Sābiq therefore does not simply denote someone born earlier or historically prior. The subject is conduct and taking the lead in good deeds.

بِالْخَيْرَاتِ identifies this precedence’s direction and field. بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ occurs in the same construction, connecting leadership in goodness with The God’s permission.

The verse distinguishes people’s behaviour. The order of its three descriptions, however, should not establish each group’s certain individual fate without further examination. Wronging, following a measured course and taking the lead each have their own meanings; reward and outcome emerge from the relevant verses together.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:61 also says:

«أُولَٰئِكَ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَهُمْ لَهَا سَابِقُونَ»

“Those hasten in good deeds, and they are foremost in them.”

(Al-Muʾminūn 23:61)

يُسَارِعُونَ and سَابِقُونَ stand alongside each other. The first concerns speed in doing good, and the second highlights their leading position.

The expressions reinforce each other without being fully synonymous. Speed can produce precedence, but precedence does not always mean having the greatest speed at every moment.

4. Al-sābiqūn: precedence in faith and human standing

The first forerunners

Al-Tawbah 9:100 says:

«وَالسَّابِقُونَ الْأَوَّلُونَ مِنَ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ وَالْأَنْصَارِ وَالَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوهُمْ بِإِحْسَانٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ وَرَضُوا عَنْهُ»

“The first forerunners among the emigrants and helpers, and those who followed them in goodness: The God was pleased with them, and they were pleased with Him.”

(Al-Tawbah 9:100; part of the verse)

السَّابِقُونَ accompanies الْأَوَّلُونَ, first, highlighting precedence and relating it to the emigrants and helpers.

Al-sābiqūn describes their pioneering position, while al-awwalūn stresses being first. Both belong to one semantic field without playing the same grammatical role.

س ب ق itself identifies neither the forerunners’ names nor the precise time they joined. Historical identification needs separate evidence.

Those following them بِإِحْسَانٍ, in goodness, are mentioned alongside the forerunners. The value presented therefore is not restricted to being earlier in time; goodness in following is also stated.

سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ: temporal precedence in faith

Al-Ḥashr 59:10 says:

«وَالَّذِينَ جَاءُوا مِنْ بَعْدِهِمْ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا اغْفِرْ لَنَا وَلِإِخْوَانِنَا الَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا رَبَّنَا إِنَّكَ رَءُوفٌ رَحِيمٌ»

“Those who came after them say: ‘Our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who preceded us in faith. Place no rancour in our hearts towards those who believe. Our Lord, You are indeed deeply compassionate and merciful.’”

(Al-Ḥashr 59:10)

سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ identifies those who reached faith before the speakers.

The precedence is temporal and concerns faith. The verse nevertheless goes beyond the date of believing: it also includes seeking forgiveness for predecessors and avoiding rancour towards believers.

Sabaqūnā does not establish that everyone believing earlier is superior in all actions and judgements to later believers. Precedence in faith is one particular priority; all human values cannot be reduced to an action’s date.

وَالسَّابِقُونَ السَّابِقُونَ

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:10–11 says:

«وَالسَّابِقُونَ السَّابِقُونَ ۝ أُولَٰئِكَ الْمُقَرَّبُونَ»

“The forerunners, the forerunners: those are the ones brought near.”

(Al-Wāqiʿah 56:10–11)

The preceding verses describe three groups. Then السَّابِقُونَ is repeated and its referents identified as الْمُقَرَّبُونَ.

The repetition highlights their standing. Sābiq itself nevertheless does not mean brought near; the next verse attributes that description to them.

Precedence here connects with a good outcome and nearness. The root alone, however, identifies neither all these forerunners precisely nor whether their precedence is only temporal or also concerns taking the lead in faith and action.

That question needs examination of the full surah and other verses concerning people’s deeds and outcomes.

Is everyone getting ahead superior?

Al-Aḥqāf 46:11 quotes disbelievers saying:

«وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَوْ كَانَ خَيْرًا مَا سَبَقُونَا إِلَيْهِ»

“Those who committed kufr said concerning the believers: ‘Had it been good, they would not have preceded us to it.’”

(Al-Aḥqāf 46:11; part of the verse)

This matters methodologically. The speakers treat another group’s earlier belief as evidence that their faith has no value.

That argument belongs to the speakers quoted, rather than being a conclusion the Qur’an endorses.

سَبَقُونَا plainly means they reached it before us. The argument’s error concerns the conclusion drawn from others’ precedence, rather than the root’s meaning.

Precedence itself therefore establishes neither spiritual superiority nor an action’s wrongness. The action’s value must be assessed independently of who did it first.

This also clarifies positive sābiq. Fāṭir 35:32 concerns good deeds, whereas Al-Aḥqāf 46:11 makes believers’ precedence a pretext for rejecting their faith. Both involve actual priority, but the subject and context determine its evaluation.

5. Temporal priority, the prior word and appointed terms

مَا سَبَقَكُمْ بِهَا مِنْ أَحَدٍ

In Al-Aʿrāf 7:80, Lot tells his people:

«أَتَأْتُونَ الْفَاحِشَةَ مَا سَبَقَكُمْ بِهَا مِنْ أَحَدٍ مِنَ الْعَالَمِينَ»

“Do you commit the indecency in which no one among all peoples preceded you?”

(Al-Aʿrāf 7:80; part of the verse)

A similar construction occurs in Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:28.

سَبَقَكُمْ بِهَا concerns precedence in performing an act. Lot addresses his people concerning conduct they have undertaken.

This shows that sabaqa does not inherently have a positive value. People can pioneer an act condemned in context.

الْفَاحِشَة and other parts of the story convey the act’s wrongness, rather than sabaqa.

مِنْ أَحَدٍ مِنَ الْعَالَمِينَ also specifies the quoted claim’s scope. Identifying precisely the act discussed and assessing its history nevertheless require examining the whole story, rather than expanding precedence’s lexical meaning.

سَبَقَتْ كَلِمَةٌ مِنْ رَبِّكَ

Yūnus 10:19 says:

«وَمَا كَانَ النَّاسُ إِلَّا أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً فَاخْتَلَفُوا وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةٌ سَبَقَتْ مِنْ رَبِّكَ لَقُضِيَ بَيْنَهُمْ فِيمَا فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ»

“People were only one community, then they differed. Had a word from your Lord not preceded, judgement would have been made between them concerning their disagreements.”

(Yūnus 10:19)

سَبَقَتْ describes كَلِمَة, a word or decree that existed previously from the Lord.

This denotes neither racing nor bodily movement. The priority concerns a word or decision preceding the event discussed.

Hūd 11:110, Ṭā Hā 20:129, Fuṣṣilat 41:45 and Al-Shūrā 42:14 also have وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةٌ سَبَقَتْ مِنْ رَبِّكَ or a similar expression.

In these verses the prior divine word relates to delayed judgement or the absence of immediate consequences for human disagreement and conduct. Al-Shūrā 42:14 also includes إِلَىٰ أَجَلٍ مُسَمًّى, highlighting an appointed term. In Hūd 11:40 and Al-Muʾminūn 23:27, إِلَّا مَنْ سَبَقَ عَلَيْهِ الْقَوْلُ in Noah’s story concerns those subject to the prior word who are excepted from the people delivered with him. A preceding divine word therefore does not always mean forgiveness or respite; context determines its content and outcome.

سَبَقَتْ itself means neither granting respite, forgiving nor punishing. It states only the word or decree’s priority. Other parts of the verse convey its content and consequences.

Al-Anfāl 8:68 also says:

«لَوْلَا كِتَابٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ سَبَقَ لَمَسَّكُمْ فِيمَا أَخَذْتُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ»

“Had a prior decree from The God not existed, a great punishment would have touched you for what you took.”

(Al-Anfāl 8:68)

سَبَقَ describes كِتَابٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ. The context connects that writing or decree with preventing punishment from immediately reaching them.

Its precedence is lexically clear. Its precise content is a separate question, however, and cannot be answered solely from sabaqa.

سَبَقَتْ لَهُمْ مِنَّا الْحُسْنَىٰ

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:101 says:

«إِنَّ الَّذِينَ سَبَقَتْ لَهُمْ مِنَّا الْحُسْنَىٰ أُولَٰئِكَ عَنْهَا مُبْعَدُونَ»

“Those for whom goodness has already been decreed from Us are indeed kept far from it.”

(Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:101)

الْحُسْنَىٰ is سَبَقَتْ’s subject. لَهُمْ identifies the people to whom that goodness relates.

The construction approaches sabaqat kalimah: something already decreed or stated by The God.

الْحُسْنَىٰ belongs to ح س ن and supplies goodness to the sentence. Sabaqa itself does not mean goodness or reward.

The phrase also does not alone establish that decree’s exact time or relationship with every human choice. Wider questions concerning The God’s knowledge, will and judgement require their own relevant verses.

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:171–173 also says:

«وَلَقَدْ سَبَقَتْ كَلِمَتُنَا لِعِبَادِنَا الْمُرْسَلِينَ ۝ إِنَّهُمْ لَهُمُ الْمَنْصُورُونَ ۝ وَإِنَّ جُنْدَنَا لَهُمُ الْغَالِبُونَ»

“Our word has indeed already gone forth concerning Our servants sent as Messengers: they are surely the ones aided, and Our host are surely the victors.”

(Al-Ṣāffāt 37:171–173)

The continuation states كَلِمَتُنَا’s content. Sabaqat therefore still denotes the word’s priority; the following expressions convey promised aid and victory.

Precedence in time, without a race

Ṭā Hā 20:99 says:

«كَذَٰلِكَ نَقُصُّ عَلَيْكَ مِنْ أَنْبَاءِ مَا قَدْ سَبَقَ وَقَدْ آتَيْنَاكَ مِنْ لَدُنَّا ذِكْرًا»

“Thus We recount to you some reports of what has already passed, and We have indeed given you a reminder from Us.”

(Ṭā Hā 20:99)

مَا قَدْ سَبَقَ concerns earlier events. No one races another, nor is ethical superiority at issue.

This clearly witnesses the root’s temporal priority: something happened earlier, and its report is now recounted. Yā Sīn 36:40 also has وَلَا اللَّيْلُ سَابِقُ النَّهَارِ, “nor is night an outstripper of day,” describing the order of night and day. Sābiq here is neither human nor ethical; its negation must be understood within this context, rather than as a claim that no night ever comes before a day in their succession.

6. The impossibility of outstripping: appointed time, The God’s will and judgement

مَا تَسْبِقُ مِنْ أُمَّةٍ أَجَلَهَا

Al-Ḥijr 15:5 says:

«مَا تَسْبِقُ مِنْ أُمَّةٍ أَجَلَهَا وَمَا يَسْتَأْخِرُونَ»

“No community can precede its appointed term, nor do they lag behind.”

(Al-Ḥijr 15:5)

The same wording occurs in Al-Muʾminūn 23:43.

تَسْبِقُ concerns temporal precedence. The subject is a community’s أَجَل, appointed term, and it cannot advance beyond or lag behind that time.

The difference from racing is clear. Effort and speed can let someone outstrip another in a race; here the text negates getting ahead of an appointed time.

يَسْتَأْخِرُونَ belongs to أ خ ر, describing lagging behind or being delayed. The contrast between the verbs clarifies sabaqa’s temporal meaning.

This expression alone cannot yield complete conclusions about every community member’s lifespan or the cause of every historical event. The verse concerns communities’ terms; other questions require separate evidence.

لَا يَسْبِقُونَهُ بِالْقَوْلِ

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:26–27 says:

«وَقَالُوا اتَّخَذَ الرَّحْمَٰنُ وَلَدًا سُبْحَانَهُ بَلْ عِبَادٌ مُكْرَمُونَ ۝ لَا يَسْبِقُونَهُ بِالْقَوْلِ وَهُمْ بِأَمْرِهِ يَعْمَلُونَ»

“They said: ‘The All-Merciful has taken a child.’ Glory be to Him; rather, they are honoured servants. They do not precede Him in speech and act by His command.”

(Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:26–27)

يَسْبِقُونَهُ بِالْقَوْلِ concerns anticipating in speech rather than bodily precedence.

وَهُمْ بِأَمْرِهِ يَعْمَلُونَ connects priority in speech with The God’s command and standing. These servants do not speak ahead of Him, and their actions also follow His command.

Sabaqa here can mean taking the initiative in a statement whose permission or command has not yet arrived.

This does not prohibit every instance of speaking before another person in human life. The verse concerns honoured servants and their relationship with The God’s command. Lexical precedence is clear; context supplies speech’s particular significance.

أَنْ يَسْبِقُونَا: can they outstrip The God’s power?

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:4 says:

«أَمْ حَسِبَ الَّذِينَ يَعْمَلُونَ السَّيِّئَاتِ أَنْ يَسْبِقُونَا سَاءَ مَا يَحْكُمُونَ»

“Do those doing evil deeds think they can outstrip Us and escape Our reach? How badly they judge.”

(Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:4)

يَسْبِقُونَا occurs in a critical question concerning people doing evil who think they can escape its consequences.

Al-Anfāl 8:59 also says:

«وَلَا يَحْسَبَنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا سَبَقُوا إِنَّهُمْ لَا يُعْجِزُونَ»

“Those who committed kufr must not think they have outstripped and escaped reach; they certainly cannot render [The God] powerless.”

(Al-Anfāl 8:59)

سَبَقُوا accompanies لَا يُعْجِزُونَ. Outstripping is therefore more than being ahead on a route: the text concerns escaping The God’s power and judgement.

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:39 also has a related use concerning Korah, Pharaoh and Haman:

«فَاسْتَكْبَرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ وَمَا كَانُوا سَابِقِينَ»

“They acted arrogantly in the land, but could not outstrip and escape reach.”

(Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:39; part of the verse)

Precedence in these passages relates to escaping something that encompasses or will reach people.

A comparison is someone trying to flee before a pursuer arrives. Escaping their reach would mean outstripping them in this sense. This image only explains a linguistic relation; these verses do not describe an actual race between The God and people.

وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَسْبُوقِينَ

Al-Wāqiʿah 56:60–61 says:

«نَحْنُ قَدَّرْنَا بَيْنَكُمُ الْمَوْتَ وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَسْبُوقِينَ ۝ عَلَىٰ أَنْ نُبَدِّلَ أَمْثَالَكُمْ وَنُنْشِئَكُمْ فِي مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ»

“We have decreed death among you; no one can outstrip Us in replacing you with your likes and bringing you into being in what you do not know.”

(Al-Wāqiʿah 56:60–61)

مَسْبُوق is a passive participle from سَبَقَ: someone whom another has outstripped.

وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَسْبُوقِينَ denies the possibility of being outstripped; the next verse connects this with the capacity to change and bring into being.

This does not mean The God competes against other beings and moves faster. Such an image would turn a use concerning power and will into bodily movement.

The sentence means no one can anticipate Him so as to frustrate His will and power.

Al-Maʿārij 70:41 also says:

«عَلَىٰ أَنْ نُبَدِّلَ خَيْرًا مِنْهُمْ وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَسْبُوقِينَ»

“To replace them with those better than them; We cannot be outstripped or rendered powerless.”

(Al-Maʿārij 70:41; part of the verse)

مَسْبُوقِينَ again connects with the power to replace. In divine usage this construction emphasises not being rendered powerless or having one’s will frustrated, rather than fast movement or spatial precedence.

7. Particular uses and comparisons with neighbouring roots

فَالسَّابِقَاتِ سَبْقًا: who outstrip?

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:3–5 says:

«وَالسَّابِحَاتِ سَبْحًا ۝ فَالسَّابِقَاتِ سَبْقًا ۝ فَالْمُدَبِّرَاتِ أَمْرًا»

“By those swimming or moving swiftly; then those outstripping in their precedence; then those directing a matter.”

(Al-Nāziʿāt 79:3–5)

السَّابِقَات is a feminine plural active participle, and سَبْقًا the same root’s verbal noun, used to emphasise or describe the action’s manner.

Outstripping is linguistically clear, but this phrase does not explicitly identify السَّابِقَات.

Older explanations propose angels, leading horses, stars and, in some accounts, souls preceding others in movement or departure.

The word itself is therefore insufficient to identify its referents. Feminine plural morphology also does not alone mean female beings; it can relate to an unstated collective designation or noun.

The basic translation should consequently retain outstripping’s image rather than substituting a disputed identification without clear evidence.

سَبْقًا here is a verbal noun and does not mean a race prize.

س ب ق (سَبَقَ) and س ر ع (سَرُعَ)

Speed concerns rapid movement or accomplishing something in a short time. Precedence emphasises getting ahead of another or occupying an earlier place in an order.

Someone can move quickly but arrive later because they started later. Conversely, an earlier start can put someone ahead despite a lower speed.

Speed and precedence therefore are related but distinct meanings.

Both roots appear together in Al-Muʾminūn 23:61: يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ and هُمْ لَهَا سَابِقُونَ. Hastening in goodness and leading in it can coexist without being one and the same meaning.

س ب ق (سَبَقَ) and ع ج ل (عَجِلَ)

ʿAjalah concerns haste and accelerating, sometimes also conveying rashness.

Outstripping nevertheless does not necessarily imply rashness or poor judgement. Someone can be prepared and judicious while taking the lead in right action.

فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ is a positive command, whereas some Qur’anic haste is condemned. Not all haste is good, nor does all precedence mean improper hurry.

The purpose, method and context establish the conduct’s value.

س ب ق (سَبَقَ) and أ و ل (أَوَّل)

Awwal concerns being first in an order. Sabaqa can likewise concern priority in time or action.

Al-Tawbah 9:100 places both expressions together in السَّابِقُونَ الْأَوَّلُونَ.

Being first and outstripping nevertheless are not always identical. Awwal can simply identify a set’s first position; sabaqa can also highlight striving to get ahead or precede another.

Not every sābiq can therefore be translated solely as first, nor does being first always result from a race.

س ب ق (سَبَقَ) and ق د م (قَدَّمَ)

Some qadam uses concern being ahead and sending forward. قَدَّمَ can denote bringing something forward or sending it ahead.

Sabaqa also conveys precedence, but racing and outstripping constructions more strongly highlight the relationship between two people or things.

سَبَقَتْ كَلِمَةٌ, for example, denotes a word that existed earlier, whereas qaddama can elsewhere denote the act of sending something ahead.

These families approach each other in some results without being interchangeable in every sentence.

س ب ق (سَبَقَ) and ف و ت (فَاتَ)

Fawt concerns being lost, passing beyond reach or failing to attain something. Some sabaqa uses likewise approach escaping reach.

In Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:4 and Al-Anfāl 8:59, outstripping relates to escaping The God’s power and judgement. Fawt and sabaqa approach each other in that context.

Sabaqa nevertheless has a wider range, including temporal precedence, leading in good deeds and actual racing. Escaping reach is one contextual use, rather than the root’s primary definition.

Translation suggestions for Forqan

For movement and racing, getting ahead, outstripping and racing are suitable. نَسْتَبِقُ in Yūsuf 12:17 naturally becomes “we were racing,” while وَاسْتَبَقَا الْبَابَ in Yūsuf 12:25 becomes “both raced each other to reach the door.”

فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ suits “outstrip one another in good deeds.” سَابِقُوا إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ can likewise become “race one another towards forgiveness.”

سَابِقٌ بِالْخَيْرَاتِ in Fāṭir 35:32 suits “taking the lead in good deeds,” and الَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ in Al-Ḥashr 59:10 “those who preceded us in faith.”

Temporal مَا قَدْ سَبَقَ in Ṭā Hā 20:99 is better conveyed by “what has already passed.” سَبَقَتْ كَلِمَةٌ مِنْ رَبِّكَ accords with “a word had previously been decreed by your Lord.” Being decreed, however, comes from the divine word and context; sabaqa independently means precedence.

مَا تَسْبِقُ مِنْ أُمَّةٍ أَجَلَهَا suits “no community can precede its appointed term.” لَا يَسْبِقُونَهُ بِالْقَوْلِ is naturally and precisely “they do not precede Him in speech.”

Literal outstripping in أَنْ يَسْبِقُونَا and وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَسْبُوقِينَ may need explanation for English readers. The sense concerns escaping The God’s power or frustrating the execution of His will, without attributing bodily racing to Him.

فَالسَّابِقَاتِ سَبْقًا should retain the image of those outstripping, with their identity examined separately in explanation.

Precedence should not automatically become victory, virtue, exceptional speed, salvation or ethical superiority in any of these uses. They can accompany it in some contexts but are not the root’s fixed meaning.

8. Conclusion

Arabic س ب ق concerns getting ahead and precedence in movement, time, action, faith or standing. Horse racing is one tangible early use, but the root is not restricted to bodily competition.

The Qur’an retains this broad field. Joseph’s story concerns racing and reaching a destination; Al-Baqarah and Al-Māʾidah command taking the lead in goodness; Al-Ḥadīd discusses racing towards forgiveness and the garden; Al-Tawbah and Al-Ḥashr discuss those entering faith earlier.

Elsewhere, سَبَقَتْ كَلِمَةٌ مِنْ رَبِّكَ concerns a word decreed previously; مَا تَسْبِقُ مِنْ أُمَّةٍ أَجَلَهَا negates advancing beyond an appointed time; and وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَسْبُوقِينَ denies being outstripped in The God’s power and will.

One main distinction is speed versus precedence. Being fast can help someone get ahead, but precedence concerns their leading position. Nor does getting ahead always mean ethical superiority. Some verses praise precedence in goodness; others concern priority in wrongdoing or imagining escape from divine judgement.

Another distinction is precedence versus victory. Someone can get ahead in reaching a destination without that priority establishing its correctness or their action’s ultimate value. Racing in goodness also need not resemble a competition where only one benefits and everyone else is excluded.

The semantic core of س ب ق is a person, thing or event getting ahead of or preceding another in movement, time, action or standing; in competitive constructions, it also concerns striving to arrive or act before others.

This definition encompasses material racing, historical precedence, ethical leadership and the prior divine word. Where outstripping divine power or command is negated, the same relation of priority expresses inability to escape, anticipate or frustrate divine will.

Precedence’s value consequently comes from what people lead in, the truth concerning which something precedes, and the relationship the verse establishes between the one outstripping and the destination, rather than from the root alone.
