Core meaning: grapes, the fruit of the vine; in cultivation and garden contexts, grapevines and vineyards

The Arabic lexical family ʿ N B concerns grapes. ʿInab names the familiar fruit borne by vines, whose berries usually grow together in clusters.

ʿInab can denote the fruit itself. In descriptions of gardens, cultivation, and growth, the same fruit name or its plural aʿnāb can refer to grapevines and their plantings.

This distinction matters in translation. A “garden of grapes” is a garden containing grapevines, rather than merely a place filled with harvested fruit.

The root occurs eleven times in eleven Quranic verses. All occurrences are nouns belonging to the two principal forms ʿinab and aʿnāb. No verb from this root occurs in the Quran.

1. The word in early Arabic

ʿInab: a particular fruit

ʿInab means grapes, ʿinabah is a single grape, and aʿnāb is a plural of ʿinab.

Unlike roots with broad senses such as moving, taking, or covering, its principal use names a particular fruit.

Arabic lexicons describe ʿinab as the fresh fruit of the vine, contrasting it with zabīb, dried grapes or raisins.

Freshly picked grapes are ʿinab; after drying, they are normally called zabīb.

A fruit and its processed form can therefore have separate names, despite coming from the same crop.

ʿInabāʾ is also recorded as another name for grapes.

Historical evidence and pre-Quranic meaning

Grapes and viticulture were known in various parts of the Near East before Islam. That agricultural history and evidence for the precise Arabic word ʿinab in a pre-Quranic text are separate matters.

Lexicons record ʿinab as grapes, and the Quran itself clearly attests the word in the Arabic of revelation.

Dating a particular verse of poetry or expression to the pre-Islamic period requires checking that witness's attribution and date. An example appearing in a dictionary is not automatically certain evidence of pre-Islamic speech or poetry.

Despite that historical limitation, the basic meaning is clear: grapes, and in horticultural contexts their crop and sometimes the vines themselves.

Vine, cluster, and grape

Several referents must be distinguished within this lexical field.

A vine is the plant that bears grapes. A cluster consists of berries attached together to a stem; each grape is one component of the cluster.

ʿInabah denotes one grape, while ʿinab is the collective name of the fruit.

ʿUnqūd means a cluster of grapes, but belongs to a separate lexical family and is not a derivative of ʿ N B.

Karm is an established agricultural name for the grapevine, also belonging to another root.

Translation must therefore attend to whether a verse concerns the fruit, plant, or vineyard.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important forms include:

عِنَب: grapes; the collective fruit name.

عِنَبَة: a single grape.

عِنَبَات: several individual grapes.

أَعْنَاب: plural of ʿinab; grapes, and in horticultural contexts grapevines.

عِنَبَاء: another lexical form for grapes.

عَانِب: someone who has grapes; the form alone does not necessarily imply abundance.

عَنَّاب: a grape seller.

عَنَّبَ الْكَرْمُ: the vine bore grapes.

Dictionaries also list ʿunnāb in this entry. It names jujube, a different fruit. Being listed under one entry does not by itself establish a shared historical origin.

The Quran uses only ʿinab and aʿnāb among these forms.

ʿInab appears in Al-Isrāʾ 17:91 and ʿAbasa 80:28; aʿnāb appears in nine other verses.

ʿInab is a collective noun whose unit is ʿinabah. Its lack of a plural ending does not make it a single grape in Al-Isrāʾ 17:91.

3. Quranic uses: fruit, vines, and gardens

A. Vineyards: using the fruit's name for its plant

Al-Isrāʾ 17:91

أَوْ تَكُونَ لَكَ جَنَّةٌ مِنْ نَخِيلٍ وَعِنَبٍ فَتُفَجِّرَ الْأَنْهَارَ خِلَالَهَا تَفْجِيرًا

aw takūna laka jannatun min nakhīlin wa-ʿinabin fa-tufajjira al-anhāra khilālahā tafjīrā

Al-Isrāʾ 17:91

“Or you have a garden of palms and grapes, and cause rivers to gush abundantly through it.”

Here ʿinab stands beside nakhīl in jannatun min nakhīlin wa-ʿinab.

Nakhīl denotes palms, and ʿinab concerns grape-bearing vines in this context.

Although the basic meaning is grapes, possessing a grape garden means possessing a garden planted with vines.

The word is ʿinabin rather than aʿnābin. A collective noun can therefore refer to a planting of grapevines.

Apparent singular or plural form must be understood alongside noun type and context.

Al-Kahf 18:32

وَاضْرِبْ لَهُمْ مَثَلًا رَجُلَيْنِ جَعَلْنَا لِأَحَدِهِمَا جَنَّتَيْنِ مِنْ أَعْنَابٍ وَحَفَفْنَاهُمَا بِنَخْلٍ وَجَعَلْنَا بَيْنَهُمَا زَرْعًا

wa-iḍrib lahum mathalan rajulayni jaʿalnā li-aḥadihimā jannatayni min aʿnābin wa-ḥafafnāhumā bi-nakhlin wa-jaʿalnā baynahumā zarʿā

Al-Kahf 18:32

“Set forth for them the example of two men: We gave one of them two gardens of grapes, surrounded them with palms, and placed cultivated land between them.”

Aʿnāb here describes the two gardens.

The surrounding palms and cultivated land between them depict an agricultural estate.

Jannatayni min aʿnāb means two grape gardens; “two vineyards” is also a natural rendering.

Later in the story, the owner boasts of his wealth and standing. That pride comes from his conduct and the narrative, rather than the meaning of ʿinab.

Pride, wealth, and arrogance should not be made meanings of the grape word itself.

B. Vineyards and the gift of water

Several verses place grapes alongside palms, crops, and water.

Al-Baqarah 2:266 depicts a garden of palms and grapes with rivers flowing beneath it and many kinds of fruit. Its destruction by a fiery whirlwind forms part of the parable.

Aʿnāb identifies a valuable garden crop, while the parable's message comes from the whole passage and its connection with spending, not from a new lexical meaning of grapes.

Al-Anʿām 6:99 names grapes beside palms, olives, and pomegranates, describing rainfall, plant growth, fruiting, and ripening.

The word thus stands within natural processes of growth and fruitfulness.

Al-Raʿd 13:4 mentions vineyards, crops, and palms in neighboring plots watered with the same water, yet differing in their produce and eating qualities.

Aʿnāb names the crop; differences in produce come from the passage and its reference to al-ukul. Taste or quality variation is not the meaning of grapes itself.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:19 describes gardens of palms and grapes brought forth through water, containing abundant fruit from which people eat.

Yā Sīn 36:34 likewise mentions gardens of palms and grapes alongside springs made to flow.

In these passages, aʿnāb concerns grapes and their vines. Water, growth, fruitfulness, and human benefit come from context.

The recurrence gives grapes a clear place among earthly gifts and crops, but does not justify replacing their lexical meaning with “blessing,” “abundance,” or “provision.”

C. Grapes among other fruits and plants

Al-Naḥl 16:11

يُنْبِتُ لَكُمْ بِهِ الزَّرْعَ وَالزَّيْتُونَ وَالنَّخِيلَ وَالْأَعْنَابَ وَمِنْ كُلِّ الثَّمَرَاتِ ۗ إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لِقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ

yunbitu lakum bihi al-zarʿa wa-al-zaytūna wa-al-nakhīla wa-al-aʿnāba wa-min kulli al-thamarāti inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-qawmin yatafakkarūn

Al-Naḥl 16:11

“With it He causes crops, olives, palms, grapes, and every kind of fruit to grow for you. In that is a sign for people who reflect.”

Aʿnāb appears among various plant products.

Yunbitu means causing growth, presenting grapes as a living plant's product.

The word cannot be restricted to harvested fruit, since the context concerns the growth of plants bearing these products.

Aʿnāb still names grapes; yunbitu supplies the action of causing growth.

ʿAbasa 80:28

وَعِنَبًا وَقَضْبًا

wa-ʿinaban wa-qaḍbā

ʿAbasa 80:28

“And grapes and cut herbage.”

ʿInaban continues the account of edible things growing.

ʿAbasa 80:24 calls people to consider their food. The following verses describe rain, the earth splitting open, and various crops growing.

Grapes provide a clear example of an edible crop.

Qaḍb belongs to another root and concerns vegetation that is cut or gathered.

Their juxtaposition does not make them identical: ʿinab names grapes, while qaḍb identifies another kind of vegetation.

D. Grapes among the gifts of the Hereafter

Al-Nabaʾ 78:32

حَدَائِقَ وَأَعْنَابًا

ḥadāʾiqa wa-aʿnābā

Al-Nabaʾ 78:32

“Gardens and grapes.”

Aʿnāban continues the description of the success of the God-conscious in Al-Nabaʾ 78:31.

The word again means grapes, but its setting differs from earthly cultivation passages.

Earthly passages associate grapes with water, growth, gardening, and food; Al-Nabaʾ names them among the rewards of the Hereafter.

The change of setting does not alter the lexical meaning of grapes.

Using that name in the Hereafter does not establish that every earthly biological feature, growing process, ripening time, or environmental requirement is identical there.

The verse identifies the gift with an intelligible name without describing every aspect of its nature.

4. Grapes and their products: fruit and what is made from it

Al-Naḥl 16:67 provides an important occurrence.

Al-Naḥl 16:67

وَمِنْ ثَمَرَاتِ النَّخِيلِ وَالْأَعْنَابِ تَتَّخِذُونَ مِنْهُ سَكَرًا وَرِزْقًا حَسَنًا ۗ إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لِقَوْمٍ يَعْقِلُونَ

wa-min thamarāti al-nakhīli wa-al-aʿnābi tattakhidhūna minhu sakaran wa-rizqan ḥasanan inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-qawmin yaʿqilūn

Al-Naḥl 16:67

“From the fruits of palms and grapevines you obtain intoxicating drink and good provision. In that is a sign for people who use reason.”

The verse distinguishes fruit from what people make from it.

Thamarāti al-nakhīli wa-al-aʿnāb concerns the fruits of palms and grapevines.

Tattakhidhūn expresses what people do with those crops, while sakaran wa-rizqan ḥasanan identifies resulting products.

Aʿnāb itself therefore does not mean wine or an intoxicating drink here.

This is an important lexical distinction.

Grapes can be eaten fresh, dried, pressed for juice, or processed in other ways. The original crop's name is not identical to every possible product's name.

Some lexical reports attest ʿinab meaning wine in particular Arabic dialects. That limited usage does not justify translating Quranic aʿnāb as wine.

In Al-Naḥl 16:67, the sentence itself distinguishes fruit from the products made from it.

The lexical meaning of ʿinab alone cannot determine the permissibility of every grape product. That question requires separate study of sakar, khamr, and the relevant verses.

For this root, the clear point is that grapes are a familiar raw material from which people make various products.

5. Distinctions from related words

ʿInab and karm

ʿInab names grapes, whereas agricultural karm names the grapevine.

The distinction resembles that between a fruit and the plant producing it.

Ordinary language can nevertheless use a fruit name for its plant or garden, as in “grape garden.”

The words may therefore refer to the same planting in some agricultural contexts while retaining distinct roots and forms.

Uses of K R M concerning nobility and honor belong to another semantic field. The moral meaning of karīm should not be transferred to grapes, or vice versa.

ʿInab and zabīb

Zabīb means dried grapes or raisins.

ʿInab names the vine's fruit; zabīb names the product obtained by drying it.

They share a source but express different conditions of the product.

Nothing in the Quranic occurrences calls for translating ʿinab as raisins by default.

ʿInab and nakhl

Nakhl concerns date palms, while ʿinab means grapes.

They occur together in several verses but identify different plants and products.

A date palm has a distinct trunk and produces dates. A grapevine is a woody trailing or climbing plant that can grow with support and produces grape clusters.

Their association illustrates agricultural variety rather than lexical synonymy.

ʿInab and thamar

Thamar concerns fruit, yield, or produce and has a broader range than grapes.

Grapes are a fruit, but not all fruit is grapes.

In Al-Naḥl 16:67, thamarāt is the broader term for products of palms and grapevines.

It expresses the product's relationship to the plant, while aʿnāb specifies the grape crop or vine.

The two words perform different semantic roles within one phrase.

ʿInab and khamr

ʿInab names fruit, while khamr in its intoxicating-drink use names a product that may be made from materials such as grapes.

The use of grapes in some drinks does not make these words lexically identical.

This distinction matters in Al-Naḥl 16:67, which names palm and grape fruits as raw materials and describes making products from them.

Nor should khamr be restricted to grape wine in every Quranic context; its scope requires study under its own root and passages.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

“Grapes” is the basic equivalent. In garden constructions, “grape garden” or “vineyard” may be more natural.

عِنَبٍ — Al-Isrāʾ 17:91

Brief root meaning: grapes, the fruit of the vine.

Contextual meaning: grapes and their vines in a garden.

Suggested phrase: “a garden of palms and grapes.”

عِنَبًا — ʿAbasa 80:28

Brief root meaning: grapes, the fruit of the vine.

Contextual meaning: grapes as one of the crops growing from the earth.

Suggested rendering: “and grapes.”

أَعْنَابٍ — Al-Baqarah 2:266, Al-Anʿām 6:99, Al-Raʿd 13:4, Al-Kahf 18:32, Al-Muʾminūn 23:19, and Yā Sīn 36:34

Brief root meaning: grapes, the fruit of the vine.

Contextual meaning: grapevines and their gardens.

Suggested rendering: “grape gardens,” “vineyards,” or “grapes,” according to the sentence.

الْأَعْنَابَ — Al-Naḥl 16:11

Brief root meaning: grapes, the fruit of the vine.

Contextual meaning: grapes and their vines among growing crops.

Suggested rendering: “and grapes.”

الْأَعْنَابِ — Al-Naḥl 16:67

Brief root meaning: grapes, the fruit of the vine.

Contextual meaning: grapevines whose fruit supplies raw material for products.

Suggested rendering: “and grapevines,” within “the fruits of palms and grapevines.”

أَعْنَابًا — Al-Nabaʾ 78:32

Brief root meaning: grapes, the fruit of the vine.

Contextual meaning: grapes among the gifts of the Hereafter.

Suggested rendering: “and grapes.”

“Grapes” should remain the basic equivalent, with “grape garden” or “vineyard” used where the cultivation context supports it.

7. Conclusion

ʿ N B concerns grapes in the Quran, the familiar vine fruit normally growing in clusters.

ʿInab is the collective fruit name, ʿinabah a single grape, and aʿnāb a plural of ʿinab.

The Quran uses only nominal forms, with no verbal occurrence.

The uses concern two closely related referents: edible fruit, and the vines or gardens producing it.

Context establishes the distinction. Jannatayni min aʿnāb concerns vineyards, while a list of foods refers directly to grapes.

Grapes must also be distinguished from products such as raisins or drinks made from them. The fruit's name should not be replaced by the name of a processed product.

Final definition of ʿ N B:

“Grapes, the fruit of the vine, with the plural aʿnāb. In cultivation and garden contexts the name can refer to grapevines and their plantings. The principal meaning names this familiar fruit; context determines whether fruit, vines, or vineyards are meant.”
