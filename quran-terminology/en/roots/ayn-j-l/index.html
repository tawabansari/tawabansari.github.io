1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ع ج ل appears in the Qur'an in two important branches:

  • hastening, wanting something quickly, and seeking the occurrence of something before its proper time;
  • عِجْل, meaning calf.

The first branch concerns human behavior toward time, result, and outcome. A human being may decide before all consequences become clear, want good or evil immediately, demand punishment in haste out of denial, hurry regarding revelation, or want what has been promised to arrive before its appointed time.

The Qur'an presents this tendency as one of the known weaknesses of the human being:

وَكَانَ الْإِنْسَانُ عَجُولًا

And the human being is very hasty.

Al-Isra 17:11

In the second branch, عِجْل is the name of a calf. In Musa's absence, Bani Isra'il turned a calf into an object of worship. The verbal similarity between haste and calf is worth noticing, but it should not be turned, without evidence, into the claim that the calf is originally a linguistic symbol of civilizational immaturity.

Within the story, one may reflect on a relationship between Bani Isra'il's haste to make a visible object of worship and their choice of a calf; but the direct meaning of عِجْل is calf.

The shortest statement of this root is therefore:

hastening and seeking a result before the proper time arrives; and in a separate nominal branch, calf.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of the verbal branch of ع ج ل may be stated as:

moving or seeking a result before the proper time, the necessary premises, or the consequences have fully arrived.

Haste is not merely speed. An action may be quick and still be precise, necessary, and timely.

What separates haste from speed is getting ahead of the proper time or ignoring premises and consequences.

A hasty human may want the result before the path has been completed, judge before sufficient knowledge, demand punishment in haste out of denial, move his tongue before revelation has been completed, or prefer the near pleasure over the farther outcome.

In the nominal branch عِجْل, the direct meaning is calf. The meaning of haste should not be automatically inserted into the definition of the animal.

5. The Human and Temporal Image of the Root

The clear image of haste is a human being who picks fruit before it ripens, answers before the question is complete, or wants the result before its premises have come together.

Haste may arise from fear of losing an opportunity, intense desire for an immediate result, inability to tolerate ambiguity, lack of trust, anger or excitement, or disregard for farther consequences.

In such a state, the issue is not necessarily physical speed. A person may be motionless while still being hasty in judgment, request, or decision.

The best image for this root is therefore:

wanting the harvest before the crop has ripened, or moving toward a result before the path has been completed.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, عَجِلَ في الأمر means that he hastened in the matter.

عَجِلَ بالشيء means that he did it quickly or moved toward it before the usual time.

اسْتَعْجَلَهُ means that he asked him to do something sooner.

العاجل is what is available now or sooner.

الآجل, in contrast, is what arrives later.

العِجْل is calf.

Without firm evidence, one should not say that this animal was named because of "quick but immature growth." Such an explanation may be a reflection, but it does not establish the certain meaning of the word.

7. History of Understanding, Misuse, or Semantic Restriction

In common understanding, haste has often been treated only as a blameworthy moral trait opposite to patience.

That sees part of the truth, but the Qur'anic field of ع ج ل is wider. Haste is not only moral impatience; it can be an error in judgment, request, timing, reception of revelation, and understanding of consequences.

At the same time, turning haste into an "engineering error" or a "violation of physical laws of growth" goes beyond the word. The Qur'an speaks about human behavior, desire, request, and choice, not about a mechanical system.

Another error is to treat every kind of speed as negative. In some matters the Qur'an calls people to compete and move quickly in good:

فَاسْتَبِقُوا الْخَيْرَاتِ

So compete with one another in good things.

Al-Baqarah 2:148

The problem is not speed in good. The problem is when a human moves without knowledge, before the proper time, or without considering consequences.

8. Methodological Note: Haste and Patience

Patience is not the simple and complete opposite of haste, though in many situations it can restrain haste.

Haste concerns getting ahead of the proper time and seeking a result before its premises are ready.

Patience concerns holding oneself on the path of truth, bearing pressure, remaining firm, not abandoning responsibility, and not collapsing under hardship.

Patience therefore has a much wider meaning than "not being hasty."

A person may not be hasty and still not be patient; for example, he may do nothing out of fear or indecision.

A person may also act quickly and still be patient, if his speed comes after the duty is clear and remains on the right path.

The precise relation is therefore:

Patience often restrains haste, but the whole meaning of patience is not limited to restraining haste.

9. A Short Look at Related Semitic Languages

Claims that ع ج ل in Semitic languages meant "unripe fruit," "apparently early growth," or "hasty twisting" require independent linguistic evidence.

Similarity among Semitic forms may show the antiquity of this family, but Qur'anic meaning must first be drawn from Arabic usage and the verses themselves.

The link between عَجَل, haste, and عِجْل, calf, is clear in form, but the historical development of meaning is not certain.

The theory "calf means an animal that appears grown but is immature" should therefore not be made the foundation of the article.

10. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

A. The human being is very hasty

وَيَدْعُ الْإِنْسَانُ بِالشَّرِّ دُعَاءَهُ بِالْخَيْرِ وَكَانَ الْإِنْسَانُ عَجُولًا

The human being calls for evil as he calls for good, and the human being is very hasty.

Al-Isra 17:11

This verse directly connects haste with failure to see consequences. In anger, despair, or ignorance, a person may want something that would harm him if it occurred immediately.

Haste is therefore not merely speed of action; it is inability to see the distance between an immediate desire and its real consequence.

B. "The human being was created from haste"

خُلِقَ الْإِنْسَانُ مِنْ عَجَلٍ سَأُرِيكُمْ آيَاتِي فَلَا تَسْتَعْجِلُونِ

The human being was created from haste; I will soon show you My signs, so do not ask Me to hasten.

Al-Anbiya 21:37

The expression من عجل is a powerful statement about the human tendency toward haste. It does not mean that the physical substance of human creation was "haste."

The verse highlights the rooted human inclination toward immediate result, then immediately forbids hastening the signs and punishment.

Suitable translation: The human being was created hasty. Or: Haste has been placed in the human disposition.

C. Musa's haste

قَالَ هُمْ أُولَاءِ عَلَىٰ أَثَرِي وَعَجِلْتُ إِلَيْكَ رَبِّ لِتَرْضَىٰ

He said: They are close behind me, and I hastened to You, my Lord, so that You may be pleased.

Ta-Ha 20:84

Musa's haste in this verse comes from longing for the pleasure of his Lord. But the response that follows shows that his early separation from the people coincided with a major event among them.

The verse does not necessarily condemn Musa as sinful, but it shows that a good intention does not always remove all consequences of haste.

This is an important example: haste may be joined to a good motive, while a distance between intention and outcome remains.

D. Haste in receiving revelation

لَا تُحَرِّكْ بِهِ لِسَانَكَ لِتَعْجَلَ بِهِ

Do not move your tongue with it in order to hasten with it.

Al-Qiyamah 75:16

The Prophet is commanded not to move his tongue in haste before the reception of revelation is complete, whether out of preservation or repetition.

The following verses say that its gathering and recitation are upon The God. This use shows that haste may arise from fear of losing something. Its remedy is trust and following the order of reception.

E. Not hastening with the Qur'an

وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِالْقُرْآنِ مِنْ قَبْلِ أَنْ يُقْضَىٰ إِلَيْكَ وَحْيُهُ

And do not hasten with the Qur'an before its revelation to you is completed.

Ta-Ha 20:114

The verse gives an important principle for understanding and conveying revelation: before reception is complete, do not draw the final conclusion or give the final expression.

This is not only about speed of pronunciation; it also highlights the order of receiving, understanding, and conveying.

F. Hastening punishment

وَيَسْتَعْجِلُونَكَ بِالْعَذَابِ

And they ask you to hasten the punishment.

Al-Ankabut 29:53

This expression appears in several verses. Those who deny ask, in mockery or denial, for punishment to occur immediately. They mistake the delay of punishment for the falseness of warning.

But delay may be respite, a chance to return, or part of wise timing. Hastening punishment shows that a human may demand something whose reality he has not understood.

G. If evil were hastened as good is hastened

وَلَوْ يُعَجِّلُ اللَّهُ لِلنَّاسِ الشَّرَّ اسْتِعْجَالَهُمْ بِالْخَيْرِ لَقُضِيَ إِلَيْهِمْ أَجَلُهُمْ

And if The God hastened evil for people as they hasten good, their term would surely be concluded for them.

Yunus 10:11

This is one of the clearest verses about the danger of haste. A human wants good immediately, yet in anger and ignorance he may also call evil upon himself or others. Divine mercy prevents every hasty request of the human being from being fulfilled immediately.

H. Hastening the Hour

يَسْتَعْجِلُ بِهَا الَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهَا

Those who do not have faith in it ask for it in haste.

Ash-Shuraa 42:18

Those who do not believe in the Hour ask for it to come sooner in mockery. Those with faith fear it and know that it is truth. Hastening the Hour therefore does not arise from awareness or readiness; it arises from denial of its consequence.

I. The day when the demanded haste arrives

هَٰذَا مَا كُنْتُمْ بِهِ تَسْتَعْجِلُونَ

This is what you used to ask to be hastened.

Adh-Dhariyat 51:14

This expression is said to the deniers when punishment or judgment arrives. What was once demanded in mockery is no longer desirable when it occurs.

These verses reveal the gap between a hasty request and real knowledge of the outcome.

J. Love of the immediate life

كَلَّا بَلْ تُحِبُّونَ الْعَاجِلَةَ
وَتَذَرُونَ الْآخِرَةَ

No; rather, you love the immediate life and leave the hereafter.

Al-Qiyamah 75:20-21

العاجلة is the near and quickly accessible life, the present world whose results are seen sooner.

The problem here is not merely material existence. The problem is preferring the immediate result over the farther and more lasting consequence.

K. The one who wants the immediate life

مَنْ كَانَ يُرِيدُ الْعَاجِلَةَ عَجَّلْنَا لَهُ فِيهَا مَا نَشَاءُ لِمَنْ نُرِيدُ

Whoever wants the immediate life, We hasten for him in it what We will, for whom We will.

Al-Isra 17:18

The verse does not say that everyone who wants the world receives whatever he wants. It states two explicit limits: what We will, and for whom We will.

Seeking the immediate life is therefore neither a guarantee of complete worldly success nor a sign of divine pleasure.

L. The calf of Bani Isra'il

وَاتَّخَذَ قَوْمُ مُوسَىٰ مِنْ بَعْدِهِ مِنْ حُلِيِّهِمْ عِجْلًا جَسَدًا لَهُ خُوَارٌ

The people of Musa took, after him, from their ornaments, a bodily calf that had a lowing sound.

Al-Araf 7:148

عِجْلًا in this verse means calf. جسدًا highlights its bodily form, and له خوار reports a sound like the sound of cattle.

The verse does not say that this calf was so named because of immaturity or premature growth.

At the level of reflection, however, the people quickly deviated from their covenant in Musa's short absence and replaced guidance with something visible. Their haste in seeking a tangible object of worship may be conceptually related to the story of the calf, but this must be presented as contextual reflection, not the lexical meaning of عِجْل.

M. The people's haste toward the calf

وَعَجِلْتُ إِلَيْكَ رَبِّ لِتَرْضَىٰ
قَالَ فَإِنَّا قَدْ فَتَنَّا قَوْمَكَ مِنْ بَعْدِكَ وَأَضَلَّهُمُ السَّامِرِيُّ

Musa hastened, and the people turned toward the calf in his absence.

Ta-Ha 20:84-85

This narrative adjacency is significant. Musa hastened toward his Lord out of longing, while his people could not remain firm in covenant and waiting during his absence.

But the two forms of haste are not morally equal: Musa's haste came from longing for The God's pleasure; the people's haste led to making an object of worship, breaking the covenant, and going astray.

The root alone does not determine the full moral judgment. Direction, object, and outcome of haste matter.

11. Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

عَجَلَ / عَجِلَ

Indicates haste in action or arrival. This haste may have a good or bad motive, but it always requires consideration of time and consequence.

اسْتَعْجَلَ

Asked for something to occur sooner. In the Qur'an it often concerns deniers asking for punishment, a sign, or the Hour. Form X highlights seeking haste or seeking earlier occurrence.

عَجُول

An intensive form expressing the strong or repeated human inclination toward haste.

الْعَاجِلَة

The near and present life. This word appears in contrast to the hereafter and emphasizes the immediate accessibility of worldly results.

عِجْل

Calf. It must be distinguished in pronunciation and meaning from عَجَل, haste.

12. Grammatical and Structural Notes

عَجِلَ إِلَىٰ

وَعَجِلْتُ إِلَيْكَ

And I hastened to You.

Ta-Ha 20:84

The preposition إلى marks the destination of haste: I hastened to You.

عَجِلَ بِـ

لِتَعْجَلَ بِهِ

In order to hasten with it.

Al-Qiyamah 75:16

The preposition بِـ introduces the object around which the haste occurs.

اسْتَعْجَلَ بِالْعَذَابِ

He sought the punishment in haste, or wanted it to come sooner.

خُلِقَ الْإِنْسَانُ مِنْ عَجَلٍ

"From haste" is a powerful rhetorical expression for the intensity of the human connection with haste. It should not be translated as the physical substance of creation.

وَكَانَ الْإِنْسَانُ عَجُولًا

عجولاً is an intensive predicate and expresses the strong human tendency.

الْعَاجِلَة

A feminine noun that conveys the near and immediate matter, and in Qur'anic context points to the present life.

13. AYN J L Compared with S R AYN

Speed indicates quick performance or rapid movement and is not negative in itself.

Haste emphasizes moving before the proper time or without completing assessment and premises.

Speed is quickness of doing; haste is getting ahead of proper time or ignoring consequences.

An action may be quick and right, as in competing in good. An action may be slow and still wrong. The measure is not time alone.

14. AYN J L Compared with A N Y

Anah means deliberateness, calmness, and avoiding premature action.

This concept is closer than patience to the direct opposite of haste, even though its forms do not have a broad Qur'anic role.

Haste is action before assessment and proper time; anah is waiting until clarity and the time of action arrive.

15. AYN J L Compared with S B R

Patience is not merely slowness or delay in action.

Patience is holding direction, bearing pressure, remaining firm, and not abandoning responsibility.

Haste may be one of the things restrained by patience, but patience is wider.

Haste means getting ahead of proper time; patience means remaining firm and preserving the right direction through pressure and time.

This comparison should remain a methodological note and should not replace the semantic core of ع ج ل.

16. AYN J L Compared with T Y SH

Taysh is connected with rashness, instability, and disturbance of judgment.

Haste concerns the timing of action and the seeking of result.

Haste may arise from taysh, but the two are not synonyms. Haste is premature action; taysh is lightness and instability in behavior or judgment.

17. Specialized Discussion: The Hasty Human, Consequences, and the Calf of Bani Isra'il

The Qur'an repeatedly places human haste in relation to the future and the consequences of human action.

The human being lives in the present and senses near results more quickly. The farther future, punishment, resurrection, and moral consequences are not yet present before the senses. This distance can cause the near matter to be preferred over the lasting result:

بَلْ تُحِبُّونَ الْعَاجِلَةَ
وَتَذَرُونَ الْآخِرَةَ

Rather, you love the immediate life and leave the hereafter.

Al-Qiyamah 75:20-21

This is not merely love of the world; it is preferring a present result over a truth that becomes clear later.

The hasty human may act on immediate pleasure and pay its cost later. He may make a prayer in anger and then regret it if it is answered immediately. He may mockingly demand punishment because he has not yet touched its reality.

Haste in the Qur'an is therefore connected with weakness in seeing the outcome.

Hastening punishment and the Hour are complete examples of this weakness. The denier asks why punishment does not come now and treats delay as proof of absence. He ignores the difference between "not yet occurred" and "will never occur."

In Musa's story, two relations with ع ج ل stand together: Musa hastens toward his Lord, and Bani Isra'il make a calf, an عِجْل.

Lexically, one should not say that the calf is "haste embodied." عِجْل is the name of the animal.

Yet at the level of the narrative, a significant reflection is possible: the people could not bear the period of Musa's absence and the waiting for his return. To have an immediate, visible object of worship, they turned to a sounding body.

The calf was a quick and tangible answer to their anxiety: something visible, touchable, and audible. In contrast, divine guidance required covenant loyalty, trust, and waiting.

The relation between calf and haste can therefore be stated carefully:

Lexically, عِجل is calf; but in the structure of the story of Bani Isra'il, its selection coincides with the people's inability to wait, their desire for an immediate object of worship, and their preference for the tangible over the unseen covenant.

This reflection should not be imposed on the root meaning, but it is worth studying for narrative coherence.

19. Conclusion

The root ع ج ل in the Qur'an is chiefly connected with hastening, seeking immediate result, and getting ahead of the proper time.

The human being is described as عجول because he may seek good and evil without weighing consequence, prefer the near result over the farther outcome, and confuse delay of occurrence with absence of truth.

خلق الإنسان من عجل states that the tendency toward haste is deeply rooted in the human being; it does not mean that haste is the physical substance of his creation.

Hastening punishment and the Hour are examples of haste in matters whose consequences the human being has not understood. What is mockingly demanded in haste is no longer desirable when it arrives.

Musa's haste shows that haste may arise from a good intention, but a good intention does not always remove every consequence of haste.

The Prophet's being forbidden to hasten in receiving the Qur'an also shows that reception, understanding, and expression must pass through their order, and anxiety must not pull the human being ahead of revelation.

العاجلة is the near and present life. The problem appears when a human prefers the immediate result of the world over the hereafter and the lasting consequence.
