The root Q R N has several branches in early Arabic. One concerns joining things, accompanying one another, and placing two or more things together. Another names horns and projections extending from a surface. A further branch concerns a community living during a particular historical period.

Historical and literary witnesses

A verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet ʿAntarah ibn Shaddād reads:

وَأَنَا امْرُؤٌ إِنْ يَأْخُذُونِي عَنْوَةً / أُقْرَنْ إِلَى شَرِّ الرِّكَابِ وَأُجْنَبِ

I am a man who, if they seize me by force, will be bound to the worst of the riding camels and led alongside.

Uqran in this verse describes a person being bound or joined to a riding animal.

The horn sense also appears in a verse attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays, transmitted with different openings. One recorded wording is:

إِذَا مَا لَمْ تَكُنْ إِبِلٌ فَمِعْزًى / كَأَنَّ قُرُونَ جِلَّتِهَا الْعِصِيُّ

If there are no camels, then goats, whose mature animals have horns like staffs.

Qurūn here is the plural of an animal’s horn. This witness concerns the physical horn sense; the generational sense should be examined through its own usage.

These witnesses illustrate the semantic field in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period. Their literary attribution does not independently establish an exact date of composition.

Forms examined: قَرَنَ (qarana), قَرْن (qarn), قَرَن (qaran), قِرْن (qirn), قُرُون (qurūn), أَقْرَان (aqrān), قَرِين (qarīn), قُرَنَاء (quranāʾ), قَرِينَة (qarīnah), قَارَنَ (qārana), مُقَارَنَة (muqāranah), قِرَان (qirān), اِقْتَرَنَ (iqtarana), اِقْتِرَان (iqtirān), قَرَّنَ (qarrana), مُقَرَّن (muqarran), أَقْرَنَ (aqrana), مُقْرِن (muqrin), and مُقْتَرِن (muqtarin).

Qur’anic forms: قَرْن (qarn), قُرُون (qurūn), قَرْنَيْن (qarnayn), قَرِين (qarīn), قُرَنَاء (quranāʾ), مُقَرَّنِين (muqarranīn), مُقْرِنِين (muqrinīn), and مُقْتَرِنِين (muqtarinīn). The root occurs 36 times in 34 verses: 23 instances of the qarn family, eight of qarīn and its plural, three of muqarranīn, one of muqrinīn, and one of muqtarinīn. It occurs twice in each of 6:6 and 4:38.

1. Core senses and branches

Joining and bringing together

When two camels were tied together with a rope, Arabic could describe the action with qarana. The rope used to join them was called qaran or qirān.

Qarīn names someone or something accompanying or joined to another.

Iqtirān denotes accompanying and entering a relationship; muqāranah denotes placing things together, accompanying them, or considering them in relation to one another.

The Persian loanword qarīneh belongs to this field too: something accompanying a statement or event and helping one understand it, such as a contextual clue.

Horns and projections

Qarn names an animal’s horn, and also certain projections, heights, and protruding parts. Qarnān is its dual and qurūn its plural.

Arabic also uses the word for locks of hair and certain elevations of the ground.

Peers, generations, and communities

Qirn denotes a peer, rival, or opponent comparable in strength. Its plural is aqrān.

Qarn can also denote a community living in a historical period, or the period of that community’s life.

The link between a generation and contemporaneity is understandable: members of a generation share part of their lifetime. Yet the historical relationship of every branch, particularly horns and joining, is not certain; they should not be forced into a single original image.

2. Qarn and qurūn: generations and human communities

In contemporary Persian and Arabic, qarn often means a century. Qur’anic usage is not confined to this fixed unit of time.

أَلَم يَرَوا كَم أَهلَكنا مِن قَبلِهِم مِن قَرنٍ مَكَّنّٰهُم فِى الأَرضِ ما لَم نُمَكِّن لَكُم وَأَرسَلنَا السَّماءَ عَلَيهِم مِدرارًا وَجَعَلنَا الأَنهٰرَ تَجرى مِن تَحتِهِم فَأَهلَكنٰهُم بِذُنوبِهِم وَأَنشَأنا مِن بَعدِهِم قَرنًا ءاخَرينَ

A-lam yaraw kam ahlaknā min qablihim min qarnin makkannāhum fī l-arḍi mā lam numakkin lakum wa-arsalnā l-samāʾa ʿalayhim midrāran wa-jaʿalnā l-anhāra tajrī min taḥtihim fa-ahlaknāhum bi-dhunūbihim wa-anshaʾnā min baʿdihim qarnan ākharīn.

Have they not seen how many generations We destroyed before them, whom We established in the land as We have not established you? We sent abundant rain upon them from the sky and made rivers flow beneath them. Then We destroyed them for their sins and brought forth another generation after them.

Al-An'am 6:6

Qarn occurs twice here. The first occurrence concerns a community granted material resources and subsequently destroyed for its sins; the second concerns another community brought forth after it.

The plural pronouns show that qarn, although grammatically singular, refers to a group of people.

The God does not attribute rivers, resources, and moral responsibility to a span of time itself, but to people living during a period of history.

The same sense occurs in Al-Muʾminun:

ثُمَّ أَنشَأنا مِن بَعدِهِم قَرنًا ءاخَرينَ

Thumma anshaʾnā min baʿdihim qarnan ākharīn.

Then, after them, We brought forth another generation.

Al-Mu'minun 23:31

And later:

ثُمَّ أَنشَأنا مِن بَعدِهِم قُرونًا ءاخَرينَ

Thumma anshaʾnā min baʿdihim qurūnan ākharīn.

Then, after them, We brought forth other generations.

Al-Mu'minun 23:42

The first verse describes another generation and the second multiple generations. Neither fixes their duration at a hundred years or any specified number of years.

Generations and the passage of time

وَلٰكِنّا أَنشَأنا قُرونًا فَتَطاوَلَ عَلَيهِمُ العُمُرُ وَما كُنتَ ثاوِيًا فى أَهلِ مَديَنَ تَتلوا عَلَيهِم ءايٰتِنا وَلٰكِنّا كُنّا مُرسِلينَ

Wa-lākinnā anshaʾnā qurūnan fa-taṭāwala ʿalayhimu l-ʿumur. Wa-mā kunta thāwiyan fī ahli Madyana tatlū ʿalayhim āyātinā wa-lākinnā kunnā mursilīn.

But We brought forth generations, and a long span of life passed over them. You were not dwelling among the people of Midian, reciting Our signs to them; rather, We were sending Messengers.

Al-Qasas 28:45

The verse distinguishes bringing forth generations from the lengthening passage of time over them.

Qurūn concerns the human communities; al-ʿumr concerns duration and the passage of time.

Translating qurūn as hundred-year centuries can therefore divert attention from the verse’s subject.

Generations and human historical experience

فَلَولا كانَ مِنَ القُرونِ مِن قَبلِكُم أُولوا بَقِيَّةٍ يَنهَونَ عَنِ الفَسادِ فِى الأَرضِ إِلّا قَليلًا مِمَّن أَنجَينا مِنهُم وَاتَّبَعَ الَّذينَ ظَلَموا ما أُترِفوا فيهِ وَكانوا مُجرِمينَ

Fa-lawlā kāna mina l-qurūni min qablikum ulū baqiyyatin yanhawna ʿani l-fasādi fī l-arḍi illā qalīlan mimman anjaynā minhum. Wa-ttabaʿa lladhīna ẓalamū mā utrifū fīhi wa-kānū mujrimīn.

Why, then, were there not among the generations before you people of remaining virtue who forbade corruption in the land, except a few of those whom We saved? Those who did wrong pursued the luxury they had been granted, and they were offenders.

Hud 11:116

Here qurūn denotes communities within which reform, resistance to corruption, and prevention of injustice were possible.

Elsewhere the Qur’an invites its audience to consider earlier generations. Yunus 10:13 and Al-Isra 17:17 describe their destruction; Maryam 19:74 describes their finer possessions and appearance, and Maryam 19:98 their disappearance.

Ta-Ha 20:128 and As-Sajdah 32:26 present the surviving dwellings of past generations as grounds for reflection.

Al-Qasas 28:78 and Qaf 50:36 recall that some of these people possessed greater strength and resources.

In these passages, qarn and qurūn primarily denote people who lived in history and left traces and consequences of their actions, rather than units for measuring time.

3. Qarīn: companionship and the influence of associates

In Arabic, qarīn means a companion or associate. The word by itself neither names Satan nor necessarily implies harmful companionship.

A verse attributed to ʿAdī ibn Zayd, and in some sources to Ṭarafah ibn al-ʿAbd, reads:

عَنِ الْمَرْءِ لَا تَسْأَلْ وَسَلْ عَنْ قَرِينِهِ / فَكُلُّ قَرِينٍ بِالْمُقَارِنِ يَقْتَدِي

Do not ask about a person; ask about his companion, for every companion follows the example of the one he associates with.

Despite variations in attribution and wording, the verse illustrates qarīn as an associate and the influence companions have on one another.

Satan as a companion

وَمَن يَعشُ عَن ذِكرِ الرَّحمٰنِ نُقَيِّض لَهُ شَيطٰنًا فَهُوَ لَهُ قَرينٌ

Wa-man yaʿshu ʿan dhikri l-Raḥmāni nuqayyiḍ lahu shayṭānan fa-huwa lahu qarīn.

Whoever turns away from the remembrance of the Entirely Merciful, We assign a satan to him, and he becomes his companion.

Az-Zukhruf 43:36

The verse first speaks of a person turning away from the remembrance of the Entirely Merciful, then of a satan becoming that person’s qarīn.

Shayṭānan identifies the companion; qarīn describes his relationship with the person.

If qarīn itself meant satan, this distinction between identity and relationship would disappear.

The next verse describes companions who bar people from the way while those people think themselves guided: Az-Zukhruf 43:37.

Eventually the person regrets such companionship:

حَتّىٰ إِذا جاءَنا قالَ يٰلَيتَ بَينى وَبَينَكَ بُعدَ المَشرِقَينِ فَبِئسَ القَرينُ

Ḥattā idhā jāʾanā qāla yā layta baynī wa-baynaka buʿda l-mashriqayni fa-biʾsa l-qarīn.

Until, when he comes to Us, he says, “If only the distance of the two easts lay between me and you!” What an evil companion!

Az-Zukhruf 43:38

The expression condemning this companion concerns the quality of the relationship. Companionship is a relationship; its harmful character is known from the companion’s conduct and influence.

An-Nisa 4:38 similarly condemns having Satan as one’s companion.

Companions who make conduct appear attractive

وَقَيَّضنا لَهُم قُرَناءَ فَزَيَّنوا لَهُم ما بَينَ أَيديهِم وَما خَلفَهُم وَحَقَّ عَلَيهِمُ القَولُ فى أُمَمٍ قَد خَلَت مِن قَبلِهِم مِنَ الجِنِّ وَالإِنسِ إِنَّهُم كانوا خٰسِرينَ

Wa-qayyaḍnā lahum quranāʾa fa-zayyanū lahum mā bayna aydīhim wa-mā khalfahum wa-ḥaqqa ʿalayhimu l-qawlu fī umamin qad khalat min qablihim mina l-jinni wa-l-insi innahum kānū khāsirīn.

We assigned companions to them, who made what was before them and what was behind them attractive to them. The word came due against them among communities of jinn and humans that had passed away before them. They were indeed losers.

Fussilat 41:25

Quranāʾ is the broken plural of qarīn. The verb fa-zayyanū describes how these companions exercise their influence.

Wrongdoing is not always imposed by force. Sometimes a person’s surroundings and companions make wrongful conduct so attractive that its ugliness is no longer recognized.

In a society where bribery has become normal, a corrupt person may be praised as clever and successful instead of being reproached. Companions can justify the conduct until the person also believes it right.

Such a situation shows how companionship can alter a person’s standards of recognition and evaluation.

The companion in As-Saffat

قالَ قائِلٌ مِنهُم إِنّى كانَ لى قَرينٌ

Qāla qāʾilun minhum innī kāna lī qarīn.

One of them said, “I once had a companion.”

As-Saffat 37:51

The following verses describe this companion as someone who questioned resurrection and recompense and challenged the other person’s belief.

As-Saffat 37:52 and As-Saffat 37:53 report his words. In As-Saffat 37:56, the saved person describes the danger this companionship had posed.

The Qur’an does not explicitly call this companion a satan here. What is clear is the misleading effect of his companionship.

A companion on the day of reckoning

وَقالَ قَرينُهُ هٰذا ما لَدَىَّ عَتيدٌ

Wa-qāla qarīnuhu hādhā mā ladayya ʿatīd.

His companion said, “This is what I have ready with me.”

Qaf 50:23

Earlier, Qaf 50:17 and Qaf 50:18 describe receiving and recording a person’s speech, while Qaf 50:21 describes each soul arriving with a driver and a witness.

This context supports understanding the companion here as one of those appointed to record or present the person and his deeds. Another interpretation identifies him as the person’s misleading companion.

A few verses later:

قالَ قَرينُهُ رَبَّنا ما أَطغَيتُهُ وَلٰكِن كانَ فى ضَلٰلٍ بَعيدٍ

Qāla qarīnuhu rabbanā mā aṭghaytuhu wa-lākin kāna fī ḍalālin baʿīd.

His companion said, “Our Lord, I did not drive him to rebellion; rather, he was far astray.”

Qaf 50:27

This companion’s words concern misleading another and human responsibility. Understanding him as a misleading companion therefore fits the context.

Nevertheless, the same word in both verses does not necessarily establish that the speaker is the same being in both.

Qarīn is not the name of a particular kind of being. It describes companionship, and the identity of each companion must be determined from context.

These verses also do not say that every person has, from birth, a particular satan, a supernatural double, or a being with matching physical and psychological traits. Such details do not follow from the lexical meaning of qarīn.

4. Muqarranīn and muqtarinīn: binding and accompanying

Muqarranīn: those bound together

وَتَرَى المُجرِمينَ يَومَئِذٍ مُقَرَّنينَ فِى الأَصفادِ

Wa-tarā l-mujrimīna yawmaʾidhin muqarranīna fī l-aṣfād.

You will see the offenders that day bound together in fetters.

Ibrahim 14:49

Muqarran is the passive participle of form II and describes someone or something joined or bound to another.

Al-aṣfād, from Ṣ F D, denotes fetters or chains used to restrict movement.

Muqarranīn describes the condition of those bound together, while aṣfād specifies the instruments of restraint.

The same form occurs in Al-Furqan:

وَإِذا أُلقوا مِنها مَكانًا ضَيِّقًا مُقَرَّنينَ دَعَوا هُنالِكَ ثُبورًا

Wa-idhā ulqū minhā makānan ḍayyiqan muqarranīna daʿaw hunālika thubūrā.

When they are cast into a narrow place in it, bound together, they will call there for destruction.

Al-Furqan 25:13

The narrowness of the place is conveyed by ḍayyiqan; being bound together is conveyed by muqarranīn.

This derivative is not restricted to scenes of the Hereafter. Describing the resources placed at Solomon’s disposal, the Qur’an says:

وَءاخَرينَ مُقَرَّنينَ فِى الأَصفادِ

Wa-ākharīna muqarranīna fī l-aṣfād.

And others bound together in fetters.

Sad 38:38

In all three occurrences, muqarranīn describes being bound and joined. The word alone does not specify how many are in each group, exactly how they are bound, or the material of the restraints.

Muqtarinīn: accompanying and joined

فَلَولا أُلقِىَ عَلَيهِ أَسوِرَةٌ مِن ذَهَبٍ أَو جاءَ مَعَهُ المَلٰئِكَةُ مُقتَرِنينَ

Fa-lawlā ulqiya ʿalayhi aswiratun min dhahabin aw jāʾa maʿahu l-malāʾikatu muqtarinīn.

Why, then, have bracelets of gold not been bestowed on him, or angels come with him in company?

Az-Zukhruf 43:53

Muqtarin is the active participle of form VIII, describing someone or something accompanying or joined to another.

Here muqtarinīn is grammatically a circumstantial qualifier of the angels, describing their coming.

The statement is Pharaoh’s, concerning Moses. Pharaoh presents gold adornments or accompanying angels as grounds for accepting Moses’ standing.

The verse does not endorse these as divine criteria for a Messenger’s commission; it reports Pharaoh’s argument against Moses.

Muqtarinīn describes accompanying or being joined; muqarranīn describes those who have been bound together.

In the Qur’anic uses of muqarranīn, restraint and captivity are understood from the context, including fetters or a narrow place.

5. Muqrinīn: being capable of confronting and controlling

لِتَستَوۥا عَلىٰ ظُهورِهِ ثُمَّ تَذكُروا نِعمَةَ رَبِّكُم إِذَا استَوَيتُم عَلَيهِ وَتَقولوا سُبحٰنَ الَّذى سَخَّرَ لَنا هٰذا وَما كُنّا لَهُ مُقرِنينَ

Li-tastawū ʿalā ẓuhūrihi thumma tadhkurū niʿmata rabbikum idhā stawaytum ʿalayhi wa-taqūlū subḥāna lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā wa-mā kunnā lahu muqrinīn.

So that you may settle upon their backs, then remember your Lord’s blessing when you have settled upon them, and say, “Glory be to the One who subjected this to us, for we were not capable of controlling it.”

Az-Zukhruf 43:13

Muqrin is the active participle of aqrana, a form IV verb.

The Arabic expression aqrana li-l-shayʾ denotes being capable of something, being equal to confronting it, or being able to manage it.

This sense is related to qirn: a peer or rival able to face another.

The preceding verse, Az-Zukhruf 43:12, mentions ships and livestock used for transportation.

Through natural resources, knowledge, and capacities made available to them, people gain command of things that their physical strength alone cannot control.

Someone riding a camel has not overcome the animal’s entire strength through muscle power. The animal’s characteristics and human ability to understand and train it make riding possible.

A comparable relationship can be observed in human use of wind, water, and other natural forces.

In this verse, taskhīr concerns making a thing available for use, while iqrān concerns the human capacity to handle and control it.

The distinction is morphological as well as semantic: مُقْرِنِين (muqrinīn), without a doubled rāʾ, is a form IV active participle meaning capable; مُقَرَّنِين (muqarranīn), with a doubled rāʾ, is a form II passive participle meaning bound together; مُقْتَرِنِين (muqtarinīn) is a form VIII active participle meaning accompanying. Similar spelling must not erase these differences.

6. Dhul-Qarnayn: a historical figure’s title

وَيَسـَٔلونَكَ عَن ذِى القَرنَينِ قُل سَأَتلوا عَلَيكُم مِنهُ ذِكرًا

Wa-yasʾalūnaka ʿan Dhī l-Qarnayn. Qul sa-atlū ʿalaykum minhu dhikrā.

They ask you about Dhul-Qarnayn. Say, “I will recite to you an account of him.”

Al-Kahf 18:83

Dhū means possessor or holder; al-qarnayn is the dual of qarn in the genitive case.

The title’s form varies with its grammatical role: dhī al-qarnayn in Al-Kahf 18:83, and yā dhā al-qarnayn in Al-Kahf 18:86 and Al-Kahf 18:94.

Different explanations have been proposed for the title: a crown or emblem bearing two horns, reaching the world’s two ends, or a connection with two periods or generations.

The lexical range of qarn allows some of these readings, but lexical possibility does not establish the historical reason for a title.

The Qur’an describes Dhul-Qarnayn’s resources, journeys, judgments among people, and assistance to a community threatened by corruption. It does not state why he bore this title or give his personal name.

Nor can his historical identity be established with certainty from the title alone.

To understand this figure, one must distinguish possible meanings of the title, historical evidence, and what the Qur’an says about his actions.

A translation should retain the title Dhul-Qarnayn.

7. Comparison with related roots

Q R N (qarīn) and Ṣ Ḥ B (ṣāḥib)

Both word families concern companionship.

Ṣāḥib can denote a companion, associate, or someone related to a person, place, or condition. Qarīn places greater emphasis on accompanying or being joined to another.

The Qur’an uses ṣāḥib across a broad field of human and social relationships, including association with a place or fate. Qarīn appears more particularly in descriptions of people’s companions and their relationship with them.

Q R N (iqtirān) and Z W J (zawj)

Zawj concerns pairing and a paired relationship; iqtirān concerns joining and accompanying.

Two things may be joined without constituting a natural or complementary pair.

Iqtirān is also not restricted to two individuals or objects: a group can be joined together.

Q R N (qarn) and A M M (ummah)

In its Qur’anic social usage, qarn denotes a generation or community living in a historical period.

Ummah can denote a community recognized through a shared way, belief, purpose, or characteristic.

People from different communities may live in the same period, while one community can continue across several generations.

Q R N (taqrīn) and Ṣ F D (ṣafd)

Taqrīn concerns joining and binding together; ṣafd concerns binding and restraining with fetters or chains.

In Ibrahim 14:49 and Sad 38:38, the two roots together describe the condition of those bound and the means of restraint.

Q R N (iqrān) and S KH R (taskhīr)

Taskhīr concerns subduing or making something available for use; iqrān in Az-Zukhruf 43:13 concerns the ability to control or manage it.

The two concepts occur together in that verse, clarifying the relationship between resources provided for human beings and their capacity to use them.

8. Translation guidance for Forqan

In historical contexts, render qarn as generation or community, and qurūn as generations or peoples of earlier periods.

Render qarīn as companion or associate, and quranāʾ as companions. Identify a satan or angel in explanatory discussion only where the context supports it.

Distinguish muqarranīn, those bound together; muqtarinīn, those accompanying; and muqrinīn, those capable of controlling something.

Retain Dhul-Qarnayn rather than replacing the title with a hypothesis about its origin.

Brief root meaning: Joining, accompanying, pairing, matching, and being equal to a task; in other branches, a generation or contemporary community, horns, and projections.

Concluding synthesis

Q R N includes several semantic branches: joining and companionship, matching, human generations, and horns.

The Qur’an uses qarn and qurūn for historical communities and generations, qarīn for companionship, muqarranīn for those bound together, muqtarinīn for accompanying, and muqrinīn, without a doubled consonant, for the capacity to control or confront.

Qarn is not restricted in the Qur’an to a fixed hundred-year period; qarīn is not an exclusive name for Satan or for a supernatural double.

A significant dimension of this root emerges in companionship. Other people can profoundly affect a person’s understanding, beliefs, and conduct, but this influence does not remove personal responsibility for choices and actions.
