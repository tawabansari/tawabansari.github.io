Qur’anic root ط و ع — Ṭ W AYN (Ṭ W ʿ)

Obedience, compliance, ability and voluntary action: from ṭawʿan wa-karhan to obedience, capacity and volunteering in the Qur’an

The three-letter Arabic root ط و ع (ṭāʾ wāw ʿayn) concerns complying, accepting and yielding to an action. Someone who obeys a command brings their conduct into accord with it. Someone acting voluntarily takes the initiative without that particular act being imposed as an obligation. اِسْتِطَاعَة (istiṭāʿah) belongs to the same family but concerns the ability and possibility of doing something: the conditions and means enabling a person to perform the act.

These meanings are related, but distinct. A person may be able to do something without wishing to do it. They may accept a command even though carrying it out is difficult. They may also willingly perform something ethically wrong. Willingness and obedience cannot therefore always be treated as virtues, nor should ability be identified with personal inclination or inward consent.

The Qur’an uses this root across a broad range: obeying The God and the Messenger, obeying other people, willing or unwilling compliance, voluntary good deeds, ability to undertake pilgrimage and other responsibilities, inability in the face of real obstacles, and a wrongful action being made easy within a person’s self. In Moses’ story, ability also concerns enduring events whose reasons are not yet clear to him.

Important Qur’anic forms include طَوْعًا (ṭawʿan), طَائِعِينَ (ṭāʾiʿīn), طَاعَة (ṭāʿah), أَطَاعَ (aṭāʿa), أَطِيعُوا (aṭīʿū), أَطَعْنَا (aṭaʿnā), يُطِيعُ (yuṭīʿu), يُطَاعُ (yuṭāʿu), مُطَاع (muṭāʿ), طَوَّعَتْ (ṭawwaʿat), تَطَوَّعَ (taṭawwaʿa), الْمُطَّوِّعِينَ (al-muṭṭawwiʿīn), اسْتَطَاعَ (istaṭāʿa), يَسْتَطِيعُ (yastaṭīʿu), اسْطَاعُوا (isṭāʿū) and تَسْطِعْ (tasṭiʿ). Differences in person, number, tense, attached pronouns or the omission of certain letters do not necessarily create a new derivative.

1. Early Arabic meanings: compliance, acceptance and readiness

Ṭawʿ contrasted with karh

Arabic places الطَّوْع (al-ṭawʿ) opposite الْكَرْه (al-karh). طَوْعًا (ṭawʿan) means with acceptance, compliance or willingness; كَرْهًا (karhan) concerns doing something unwillingly, under pressure or against one’s wishes.

The contrast is familiar in everyday life. A person may willingly respond to another’s request, or perform the same act under pressure and with reluctance. The outward action may be identical, while the doer’s relationship to it differs.

Arabic lexical sources record طَاعَ لَهُ (ṭāʿa lahu) for complying and obeying. أَطَاعَهُ (aṭāʿahu) describes acting in accordance with another’s command. طَاوَعَهُ (ṭāwaʿahu) can express agreeing and cooperating with someone.

These uses are very close. Lexical explanations nevertheless also distinguish acceptance and compliance from carrying out a command. Someone may agree with another without having acted yet, whereas obedience in its clear use involves responding to a command and behaving accordingly.

Ṭawʿ should therefore not be reduced to an inward feeling of consent, or obedience to mere bodily motion. Both concern a person’s relationship to a demand or command, though one can foreground acceptance and compliance, and the other performance.

Compliance in social life

A familiar Arabic expression is فَرَسٌ طَوْعُ الْعِنَانِ (farasun ṭawʿu al-ʿināni): a horse that readily responds to the rein and follows the rider’s hand.

طَوْعُ الْقِيَادِ (ṭawʿu al-qiyādi) and related expressions are also recorded for a camel that is readily led.

Here ṭawʿ means responsiveness and compliance in movement. The animal does not resist being guided, or is easy to control.

This helps explain the language, but does not reduce human obedience in the Qur’an to the relationship between an animal and the person holding its rein. Qur’anic descriptions of humans also involve faith, will, responsibility, choice and accountability. The underlying sense of compliance remains; the context identifies the relationship involved.

In another use, أَطَاعَ لَهُ الْمَرْعَى (aṭāʿa lahu al-marʿā) describes pasture becoming favourable and available for grazing. أَطَاعَ النَّخْلُ (aṭāʿa al-nakhlu) can describe date palms whose fruit has ripened and become ready for harvesting.

These expressions do not attribute moral obedience to plants or pasture. They concern conditions becoming favourable for use: something previously difficult or impossible is now possible.

This branch helps explain the relationship between ṭawʿ and ability. Sometimes compliance involves not resisting an action; sometimes conditions become ready enough to make an action possible. These lexical accounts explain usage but do not independently establish when every form was current before revelation.

Evidence from early Arabic poetry

A poem attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Aws ibn Ḥajar includes:

«كَأَنَّ جِيَادَنَا فِي رَعْنِ زُمٍّ
جَرَادٌ قَدْ أَطَاعَ لَهُ الْوَرَاقُ»

“Our swift horses at Raʿn Zumm seem like locusts for which green vegetation has become readily available.”

الْوَرَاق (al-warāq) here concerns the land’s green vegetation. أَطَاعَ لَهُ الْوَرَاقُ (aṭāʿa lahu al-warāqu) means that the vegetation has become available for the locusts to feed on.

Aṭāʿa describes plants that neither received a command from locusts nor made a moral decision. Its meaning here is becoming favourable and available for use.

The line has variant readings in lexical transmission. Some versions have جِيَادَنَا (jiyādanā), “our horses,” and others جِيَادَهُنَّ (jiyādahunna), “their horses”; related versions of the final word are also transmitted. These differences do not fundamentally alter the use of aṭāʿa for vegetation becoming available, but should be acknowledged when citing the evidence. Attribution also varies: some accounts name Aws ibn Ḥajar, others Aws ibn Zuhayr. The line should therefore not be assigned unconditionally to one poet and a certain date.

A poem by al-Mutanakhkhil al-Hudhalī also says:

«إِذَا سُدْتَهُ سُدْتَ مِطْوَاعَةً
وَمَهْمَا وَكَلْتَ إِلَيْهِ كَفَاهُ»

“If you exercise lordship over him, he is compliant and obedient; whatever you entrust to him, he sees it through.”

The line mourns a member of the poet’s family. مِطْوَاعَة (miṭwāʿah) describes the person’s compliance and obedience; كَفَاهُ (kafāhu), “he sufficed for it,” expresses his capacity to fulfil an entrusted task.

Two explanations of سُدْتَهُ (sudtahu) are transmitted: one concerns exercising leadership and command, the other confidential conversation. The first clearly fits the description of compliance, but the alternative should not be concealed.

The two examples show related areas: someone complying with another’s command, and something becoming ready or available for an action.

Their proximity does not mean that ability always results from obedience, or that an obedient person can do everything. Istiṭāʿah has an established Arabic meaning of being able, which requires examination independently of obedience in Qur’anic usage.

2. Ṭawʿan wa-karhan: willingness, pressure and creatures’ relation to divine command

Ṭawʿan wa-karhan in Āl ʿImrān

Āl ʿImrān 3:83 says:

«أَفَغَيْرَ دِينِ اللَّهِ يَبْغُونَ وَلَهُ أَسْلَمَ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ طَوْعًا وَكَرْهًا وَإِلَيْهِ يُرْجَعُونَ»

“Do they seek a religion other than The God’s, while whoever is in the heavens and the earth has submitted to Him, willingly and unwillingly, and to Him they will be returned?”

(Āl ʿImrān 3:83)

طَوْعًا وَكَرْهًا (ṭawʿan wa-karhan) expresses two different conditions: willing acceptance and compliance, and yielding unwillingly or against one’s wishes.

The principal verb أَسْلَمَ (aslama), “submitted,” belongs to س ل م (S L M), rather than ط و ع. Ṭawʿan does not itself mean Islam or submission; it describes its condition or manner.

The distinction matters. The Qur’an speaks of creatures submitting to The God, while describing that submission in two different conditions. The verse alone cannot establish that all creatures have freely chosen a particular religion in the conscious, human sense.

Conversely, karhan does not mean that all creatures stand unwillingly before The God at every moment. The verse encompasses both conditions.

Being within The God’s power and command should be distinguished from consciously accepting a religious path. By including ṭawʿan wa-karhan, the verse prevents reducing all submission to one kind of human experience.

Shadows and prostration willingly and unwillingly

Al-Raʿd 13:15 says:

«وَلِلَّهِ يَسْجُدُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ طَوْعًا وَكَرْهًا وَظِلَالُهُمْ بِالْغُدُوِّ وَالْآصَالِ»

“And to The God prostrates whoever is in the heavens and the earth, willingly and unwillingly, and their shadows in the mornings and evenings.”

(Al-Raʿd 13:15)

Ṭawʿan wa-karhan accompanies creatures’ prostration, after which their shadows are also mentioned.

Because the movement of a shadow relates to light and the object casting it, prostration and ṭawʿ here should not all be restricted to conscious, voluntary human behaviour.

Ṭawʿ itself does not mean prostration. Prostration comes from يَسْجُدُ (yasjudu), while ṭawʿan wa-karhan describes its different conditions.

The verse presents a broader sense of creatures standing before divine command and order. It does not give one uniform account of every creature’s awareness.

Ataynā ṭāʾiʿīn: the response of heaven and earth

Fuṣṣilat 41:11 says:

«ثُمَّ اسْتَوَىٰ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ وَهِيَ دُخَانٌ فَقَالَ لَهَا وَلِلْأَرْضِ ائْتِيَا طَوْعًا أَوْ كَرْهًا قَالَتَا أَتَيْنَا طَائِعِينَ»

“Then He turned towards the heaven while it was smoke, and said to it and to the earth: Come, willingly or unwillingly. They both said: We have come willingly, in obedience.”

(Fuṣṣilat 41:11)

This is among the clearest uses of ṭawʿ in relation to creation.

First comes ائْتِيَا (iʾtiyā), “come,” addressing heaven and earth. طَوْعًا أَوْ كَرْهًا (ṭawʿan aw karhan), “willingly or unwillingly,” then identifies two possible conditions, and the response is أَتَيْنَا طَائِعِينَ (ataynā ṭāʾiʿīna), “we have come willingly, in obedience.”

طَائِعِينَ (ṭāʾiʿīn) is a plural of the active participle طَائِع (ṭāʾiʿ). The verse attributes compliance to heaven and earth.

That construction alone cannot reconstruct every detail of their awareness, speech and will as if these were human speech and will. The Qur’an reports their answer, without explaining the nature of this exchange through ordinary human experience.

Conversely, the form and meaning should not be removed by translating as though no command or response existed in the text.

The linguistic meaning is clear: heaven and earth are described as ṭāʾiʿīn in responding to the command. Interpreting the nature of this compliance is separate from defining the root.

Ṭawʿ does not always mean goodness

An important counterexample occurs in Al-Māʾidah 5:30:

«فَطَوَّعَتْ لَهُ نَفْسُهُ قَتْلَ أَخِيهِ فَقَتَلَهُ فَأَصْبَحَ مِنَ الْخَاسِرِينَ»

“Then his self made killing his brother easy and acceptable to him; so he killed him and became one of the losers.”

(Al-Māʾidah 5:30)

طَوَّعَتْ (ṭawwaʿat) is Form II, the only occurrence of that form in the Qur’an.

Here the person’s self makes killing his brother easy or acceptable. The verb does not mean that his self gives him a divine or ethical command. Rather, the wrongful act becomes inwardly acceptable enough for him to perform it.

The lexical relationship to ṭawʿ is clear: something a person might have resisted doing becomes easy and acceptable to him.

This shows why the self’s compliance with an action does not establish its rightfulness. Someone can become inclined to perform a wrongful act.

Nor does Form II alone establish that the person previously had no intention or inclination to kill. The verse describes a process ending in murder; further psychological details do not follow from the text.

Thus ṭawʿ’s lexical core concerns acceptance, compliance and obedience. Its ethical value depends on what a person complies with or obeys.

3. Aṭāʿa and ṭāʿah: obedience and its limits in the Qur’an

Obeying The God and the Messenger

Al-Nisāʾ 4:59 says:

«يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ وَأُولِي الْأَمْرِ مِنْكُمْ فَإِنْ تَنَازَعْتُمْ فِي شَيْءٍ فَرُدُّوهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَالرَّسُولِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ ذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ وَأَحْسَنُ تَأْوِيلًا»

“You who have believed, obey The God, and obey the Messenger and those vested with authority among you. If you disagree about anything, refer it to The God and the Messenger, if you believe in The God and the Last Day. That is better and best in outcome.”

(Al-Nisāʾ 4:59)

أَطِيعُوا (aṭīʿū), “obey,” occurs explicitly twice: once concerning The God, and again concerning the Messenger. أُولِي الْأَمْرِ مِنْكُمْ (ulī al-amri minkum), “those vested with authority among you,” is then conjoined with the second construction.

Obedience here concerns acting in accordance with command. The verse does not end there, however: in disagreement it commands referring the matter to The God and the Messenger.

The lexical meaning of obedience alone therefore does not give unlimited, unconditional authority to every holder of power or social title.

The repeated verb in أَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ (aṭīʿū Allāha wa-aṭīʿū al-rasūla), “obey The God and obey the Messenger,” and the absence of a separate verb before ulī al-amr are syntactically noteworthy. That distinction should be retained, though repetition or omission of one verb alone cannot establish every detail of people’s authority.

What is clear is that the command to obey accompanies the rule of referring disagreement to The God and the Messenger.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:80 says:

«مَنْ يُطِعِ الرَّسُولَ فَقَدْ أَطَاعَ اللَّهَ وَمَنْ تَوَلَّىٰ فَمَا أَرْسَلْنَاكَ عَلَيْهِمْ حَفِيظًا»

“Whoever obeys the Messenger has indeed obeyed The God. As for those who turn away, We have not sent you as their guardian.”

(Al-Nisāʾ 4:80)

The verse explicitly states the relationship between obeying the Messenger and obeying The God.

Obedience does not here mean turning the Messenger into The God. The God and the Messenger are distinct designations; the verse establishes a relationship of obedience within the messenger’s mission.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:64 also says:

«وَمَا أَرْسَلْنَا مِنْ رَسُولٍ إِلَّا لِيُطَاعَ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ»

“And We sent no messenger except to be obeyed by The God’s permission.”

(Al-Nisāʾ 4:64, excerpt)

لِيُطَاعَ (li-yuṭāʿa), “to be obeyed,” is a passive verb of the basic triliteral form. Here obedience to the messenger accompanies بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (bi-idhni Allāhi), “by The God’s permission.”

This usage shows that the basic form can also express being obeyed. Obedience should not be assumed to occur only in Form IV.

Is obedience always right?

Al-Zukhruf 43:54 says of Pharaoh:

«فَاسْتَخَفَّ قَوْمَهُ فَأَطَاعُوهُ إِنَّهُمْ كَانُوا قَوْمًا فَاسِقِينَ»

“So he treated his people as of little account, and they obeyed him. Indeed, they were a defiantly disobedient people.”

(Al-Zukhruf 43:54)

This is important evidence for the root’s evaluative character.

The same verb aṭāʿa used elsewhere for obeying The God and the Messenger here describes the people obeying Pharaoh.

Lexically, obedience means acting in accordance with another’s wishes or command. Its rightfulness depends on who commands and what is commanded.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:67 also reports the words of a group facing punishment:

«وَقَالُوا رَبَّنَا إِنَّا أَطَعْنَا سَادَتَنَا وَكُبَرَاءَنَا فَأَضَلُّونَا السَّبِيلَا»

“And they said: Our Lord, indeed, we obeyed our masters and our great ones, and they led us astray from the way.”

(Al-Aḥzāb 33:67)

Here obedience to people of social standing has resulted in going astray.

These two uses show that compliance is not a virtue in itself. Someone may obey a person who leads them in the wrong direction.

Obeying a powerful person does not automatically remove the follower’s own responsibility. In these verses, those who followed wrongful commands themselves face their conduct.

Al-Kahf 18:28 gives an explicit command:

«وَلَا تُطِعْ مَنْ أَغْفَلْنَا قَلْبَهُ عَنْ ذِكْرِنَا وَاتَّبَعَ هَوَاهُ وَكَانَ أَمْرُهُ فُرُطًا»

“And do not obey one whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance, who follows his own desire and whose conduct has gone beyond bounds.”

(Al-Kahf 18:28, excerpt)

Here obedience to a person with specified characteristics is prohibited. Al-Anʿām 6:116 likewise warns that obeying most people on earth can lead one astray when they follow only conjecture.

Luqmān 31:15 and Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:8 also prohibit obeying parents when they demand shirk, associating others with The God, while good treatment of parents remains important.

Family relationships, large followings and social standing therefore do not independently guarantee that a command is right.

Obedience and responsibility in family relationships

Al-Nisāʾ 4:34 contains فَإِنْ أَطَعْنَكُمْ فَلَا تَبْغُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ سَبِيلًا (fa-in aṭaʿnakum fa-lā tabghū ʿalayhinna sabīlan), “then, if they obey you, seek no way against them.”

أَطَعْنَكُمْ (aṭaʿnakum) is the same verb of obedience, used in the marital relationship. The sentence concerns family discord and the measures stated in the verse, and then prohibits seeking a way against the women.

Here too, aṭāʿa alone cannot yield a theory of one party’s unlimited authority over the other. The subject of obedience, the conditions of discord and the concluding prohibition must be considered together.

The verse uses obedience in its established sense; the root Ṭ W AYN alone cannot determine the relationship’s legal and ethical limits.

Claimed obedience and actual obedience

Al-Nisāʾ 4:81 says:

«وَيَقُولُونَ طَاعَةٌ فَإِذَا بَرَزُوا مِنْ عِنْدِكَ بَيَّتَ طَائِفَةٌ مِنْهُمْ غَيْرَ الَّذِي تَقُولُ»

“And they say: Obedience! Yet when they leave your presence, a group of them secretly plans something other than what you say.”

(Al-Nisāʾ 4:81, excerpt)

طَاعَةٌ (ṭāʿatun), “obedience,” occurs in their words, but their behaviour is inconsistent with that claim.

Al-Nūr 24:47 likewise describes people who say they have believed and obeyed, but a group of them subsequently turns away.

By contrast, Al-Nūr 24:51 says of believers:

«إِنَّمَا كَانَ قَوْلَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِذَا دُعُوا إِلَى اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ لِيَحْكُمَ بَيْنَهُمْ أَنْ يَقُولُوا سَمِعْنَا وَأَطَعْنَا وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ»

“The believers’ response, when called to The God and His Messenger so that he may judge between them, is only to say: We have heard and obeyed. Those are the successful.”

(Al-Nūr 24:51)

These passages distinguish claiming compliance from behaving in accordance with a command.

Ṭāʿah should consequently not be identified merely with saying “we obey.” Words may express acceptance, but subsequent conduct reveals whether the claim is true.

Muḥammad 47:21 also contains طَاعَةٌ وَقَوْلٌ مَعْرُوفٌ (ṭāʿatun wa-qawlun maʿrūfun), “obedience and fitting speech.” In Al-Nūr 24:53, طَاعَةٌ مَعْرُوفَةٌ (ṭāʿatun maʿrūfatun), “recognised obedience,” appears in contrast to emphatic oaths. These uses distinguish speaking about obedience from performing it.

Muṭāʿin thamma amīn: obeyed there and trustworthy

Al-Takwīr 81:19, Al-Takwīr 81:20 and Al-Takwīr 81:21 say:

«إِنَّهُ لَقَوْلُ رَسُولٍ كَرِيمٍ ۝ ذِي قُوَّةٍ عِنْدَ ذِي الْعَرْشِ مَكِينٍ ۝ مُطَاعٍ ثَمَّ أَمِينٍ»

“Indeed, it is the word of a noble messenger, possessing strength, firmly established with the Possessor of the Throne, obeyed there and trustworthy.”

(Al-Takwīr 81:19; Al-Takwīr 81:20; Al-Takwīr 81:21)

مُطَاع (muṭāʿ) is a Form IV passive participle: someone who is obeyed.

It accompanies أَمِين (amīn), trustworthy, but the meanings are distinct. Muṭāʿ describes the standing of someone whom others obey; amīn concerns trustworthiness and reliability.

Muṭāʿ alone cannot identify the messenger. Identifying him requires the surah’s context and other verses. Others obeying him likewise does not by itself establish every detail of his authority and mission.

Ghāfir 40:18 also has وَلَا شَفِيعٍ يُطَاعُ (wa-lā shafīʿin yuṭāʿu), “nor an intercessor who is obeyed,” or whose plea is accepted. This belongs to a warning to wrongdoers and should not become a denial or affirmation of every form of intercession without examining the relevant verses fully.

4. Taṭawwaʿa: taking the initiative in good and its relationship to obligation

Wa-man taṭawwaʿa khayran: whoever voluntarily does good

Al-Baqarah 2:158 says:

«إِنَّ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةَ مِنْ شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ فَمَنْ حَجَّ الْبَيْتَ أَوِ اعْتَمَرَ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِ أَنْ يَطَّوَّفَ بِهِمَا وَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ شَاكِرٌ عَلِيمٌ»

“Indeed, al-Ṣafā and al-Marwah are among the sacred markers of The God. Whoever makes the pilgrimage to the House or performs ʿumrah incurs no sin by going around the two; and whoever willingly does good, indeed, The God is Appreciative, Knowing.”

(Al-Baqarah 2:158)

تَطَوَّعَ (taṭawwaʿa) here describes someone willingly doing good.

In its established Arabic use, taṭawwuʿ concerns doing something willingly without that particular act being obligatory. The general meaning of taking the initiative in good must nevertheless be distinguished from the technical category of a “recommended act” in later classifications.

Al-Ṣafā and al-Marwah have already been described as مِنْ شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ (min shaʿāʾiri Allāhi), “among The God’s sacred markers.” The verse then removes blame from going around them and ends by speaking of voluntary good.

Wa-man taṭawwaʿa khayran alone therefore does not make every preceding rite optional in every situation. Each action’s standing requires examination of its commands and context.

Conversely, taṭawwuʿ here does not mean any act one happens to choose. خَيْرًا (khayran), “good,” identifies its object.

Voluntary giving and care for the needy

Al-Baqarah 2:184 says:

«وَعَلَى الَّذِينَ يُطِيقُونَهُ فِدْيَةٌ طَعَامُ مِسْكِينٍ فَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَهُ وَأَنْ تَصُومُوا خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ»

“And upon those who are able to undertake it is a compensatory payment: feeding a needy person. Whoever voluntarily does additional good, it is better for him; and fasting is better for you, if you know.”

(Al-Baqarah 2:184, excerpt)

Here تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا (taṭawwaʿa khayran), “voluntarily does good,” occurs in the context of compensation and feeding. Voluntary goodness may concern increasing the food given or doing further good.

Taṭawwaʿa itself does not determine the additional quantity, the form of compensation or every condition of fasting.

Another distinction is between يُطِيقُونَهُ (yuṭīqūnahu) and تَطَوَّعَ (taṭawwaʿa). They belong to different roots: yuṭīqūnahu comes from ط و ق (Ṭ W Q) and concerns capacity and endurance, whereas taṭawwaʿa comes from ط و ع (Ṭ W AYN).

The proximity of capacity and ability does not make these roots or all their uses identical.

Al-muṭṭawwiʿīn: those who contribute voluntarily

Al-Tawbah 9:79 says:

«الَّذِينَ يَلْمِزُونَ الْمُطَّوِّعِينَ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ فِي الصَّدَقَاتِ وَالَّذِينَ لَا يَجِدُونَ إِلَّا جُهْدَهُمْ فَيَسْخَرُونَ مِنْهُمْ سَخِرَ اللَّهُ مِنْهُمْ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ»

“Those who find fault with the believers who give charity voluntarily, and with those who find nothing beyond their capacity and the fruits of their effort, and ridicule them: The God has ridiculed them, and theirs is a painful punishment.”

(Al-Tawbah 9:79)

الْمُطَّوِّعِينَ (al-muṭṭawwiʿīn) derives from الْمُتَطَوِّعِينَ (al-mutaṭawwiʿīn). The tāʾ of Form V stands next to ṭāʾ and is assimilated into it.

The word denotes those voluntarily contributing to good. The context here concerns charity and financial assistance.

The verse mentions voluntary charitable givers and those who have nothing beyond their capacity and the fruits of their effort, condemning fault-finding and ridicule directed at them. It does not require two entirely separate, non-overlapping groups, or imply that all voluntary givers are wealthy.

Taṭawwuʿ is therefore not restricted to wealthy people or large financial gifts. The verse also speaks of those with limited means.

In these Qur’anic uses, taṭawwuʿ concerns taking the initiative in good. The goodness comes from the action and context: an act is not right and worthy simply because it is voluntary.

5. Istaṭāʿa: being able and having the means to act

اِسْتِطَاعَة (istiṭāʿah) is one of the root’s broadest Qur’anic branches. It concerns more than inclination or consent: it concerns ability and the possibility of performing an act.

Someone may wish to act but lack the tools, strength, time or route needed. Conversely, they may be able to act but choose not to.

Wishing and being able must therefore be distinguished.

Ability to undertake pilgrimage

Āl ʿImrān 3:97 says:

«فِيهِ آيَاتٌ بَيِّنَاتٌ مَقَامُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَمَنْ دَخَلَهُ كَانَ آمِنًا وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا وَمَنْ كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَنِيٌّ عَنِ الْعَالَمِينَ»

“In it are clear signs, including Abraham’s standing place; whoever enters it is secure. Pilgrimage to the House is owed to The God by people who have the ability and a way to reach it. Whoever commits kufr, indeed, The God has no need of the worlds.”

(Āl ʿImrān 3:97)

Ability here accompanies إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا (ilayhi sabīlan), “a way to it.”

The subject is therefore more than bodily ability understood as muscular strength. A route to the destination and the possibility of performing pilgrimage also enter the sentence.

Ability may involve available resources, capacity to travel, access and other real conditions. The root alone does not establish a fixed list of travel expenses, transport arrangements or legal conditions for pilgrimage.

Nor should istaṭāʿa here be translated simply as “wished.” Someone may wish to perform pilgrimage without having the way and means to do it.

In this context, ability is a condition for being able to fulfil a responsibility; it names neither pilgrimage itself nor a mere inward desire.

Ability and the limits of responsibility

Al-Taghābun 64:16 says:

«فَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ وَاسْمَعُوا وَأَطِيعُوا وَأَنْفِقُوا خَيْرًا لِأَنْفُسِكُمْ وَمَنْ يُوقَ شُحَّ نَفْسِهِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ»

“So practise taqwa towards The God as far as you are able; listen, obey and spend: that is better for yourselves. Whoever is guarded against the avarice of their own self—those are the successful.”

(Al-Taghābun 64:16)

The verse is especially important for distinguishing obedience and ability, because both occur in one sentence.

مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ (mā istaṭaʿtum), “as far as you are able,” expresses the extent of capacity; أَطِيعُوا (aṭīʿū), “obey,” commands obedience. Treating the two as identical would erase their distinction in this construction.

The verse calls people to taqwa—mindful care to avoid wrongdoing—to listening, obedience and spending. Mā istaṭaʿtum also brings actual human ability into the matter.

Ability here neither replaces the command nor licenses ignoring it. The extent of fulfilling responsibility is related to a person’s capacity.

Al-Anfāl 8:60 also says:

«وَأَعِدُّوا لَهُمْ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ مِنْ قُوَّةٍ وَمِنْ رِبَاطِ الْخَيْلِ»

“And prepare against them whatever strength and horses held ready you are able to muster.”

(Al-Anfāl 8:60, excerpt)

Here ability concerns available means and the power to prepare. قُوَّة (quwwah), strength, identifies what is to be assembled; mā istaṭaʿtum expresses the extent of ability.

Ability can therefore be assessed against existing means and the act required. The root does not prescribe a single quantity of strength for every period.

Inability in the face of real obstacles

Al-Baqarah 2:273 says of a group of needy people:

«لِلْفُقَرَاءِ الَّذِينَ أُحْصِرُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ لَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ ضَرْبًا فِي الْأَرْضِ يَحْسَبُهُمُ الْجَاهِلُ أَغْنِيَاءَ مِنَ التَّعَفُّفِ تَعْرِفُهُمْ بِسِيمَاهُمْ لَا يَسْأَلُونَ النَّاسَ إِلْحَافًا وَمَا تُنْفِقُوا مِنْ خَيْرٍ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ بِهِ عَلِيمٌ»

“[Spending is] for the needy who are restricted in the way of The God and cannot travel through the land [to earn a livelihood]. Someone unaware of their condition supposes them wealthy because they refrain from asking. You recognise them by their signs; they do not ask people insistently. Whatever good you spend, indeed, The God knows it.”

(Al-Baqarah 2:273)

لَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ (lā yastaṭīʿūna), “they cannot,” concerns inability to perform a specified act: ضَرْبًا فِي الْأَرْضِ (ḍarban fī al-arḍi), travelling and moving through the land.

The context concerns poor people facing a restriction. The verse does not specify each individual’s precise cause of inability.

The verb alone therefore does not make all of them physically ill or unable to walk. The restriction may also relate to living conditions, responsibilities or external circumstances.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:98 also describes people who لَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ حِيلَةً وَلَا يَهْتَدُونَ سَبِيلًا (lā yastaṭīʿūna ḥīlatan wa-lā yahtadūna sabīlan): “have no means of escape and cannot find a way.”

This shows that istiṭāʿah is more than bodily ability. Someone may be physically healthy but have no way or means of escaping their situation.

In Al-Baqarah 2:282, ability also concerns dictating the terms of a financial obligation. Performing a task may consequently depend on knowledge, expression, access or other necessary means.

The disciples’ question: hal yastaṭīʿu rabbuka?

Al-Māʾidah 5:112 says:

«إِذْ قَالَ الْحَوَارِيُّونَ يَا عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ هَلْ يَسْتَطِيعُ رَبُّكَ أَنْ يُنَزِّلَ عَلَيْنَا مَائِدَةً مِنَ السَّمَاءِ قَالَ اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ مُؤْمِنِينَ»

“When the disciples said: Jesus, son of Mary, can your Lord send down to us a table spread from heaven? He said: Practise taqwa towards The God, if you are believers.”

(Al-Māʾidah 5:112)

In the recitation transmitted by Ḥafṣ, يَسْتَطِيعُ (yastaṭīʿu) is third-person and رَبُّكَ (rabbuka), “your Lord,” is its subject. In the sentence’s direct construction, the question concerns the Lord’s ability.

Conversationally, however, such a question can request an action rather than merely inquire about someone’s underlying capacity. Someone asking “Can you do something for me?” may be requesting the action rather than doubting the addressee’s physical ability.

Another recognised recitation, that of al-Kisāʾī, reads هَلْ تَسْتَطِيعُ رَبَّكَ (hal tastaṭīʿu rabbaka). This addresses Jesus and has been explained as “Can you ask your Lord?”

The recitations differ in subject and sentence construction, while the basic meaning of istiṭāʿah remains being able.

The following verses continue the disciples’ request and Jesus’ response. One verbal form in one recitation should therefore not be used alone to determine every disciple’s definitive belief about The God’s power.

Conversely, the actual difference between the two recitations should not be concealed by silently substituting one for the other.

Lan tastaṭīʿa maʿiya ṣabran: ability to endure patiently

In Al-Kahf 18:67, Moses is told:

«قَالَ إِنَّكَ لَنْ تَسْتَطِيعَ مَعِيَ صَبْرًا»

“He said: Indeed, you will not be able to endure patiently with me.”

(Al-Kahf 18:67)

Here ability concerns patient endurance, rather than movement or a physical task.

The following verse says:

«وَكَيْفَ تَصْبِرُ عَلَىٰ مَا لَمْ تُحِطْ بِهِ خُبْرًا»

“And how can you be patient with what you have not encompassed in knowledge?”

(Al-Kahf 18:68)

The connection shows that Moses’ difficulty in remaining patient relates to not knowing the reasons for certain events in the story.

Istiṭāʿah can therefore concern the capacity to endure and persevere in a situation that is difficult to understand and judge.

Istaṭāʿa itself does not mean being patient. صَبْرًا (ṣabran) separately names patience; istiṭāʿah expresses the ability to exercise it.

Human ability is thus not confined to bodily strength and external equipment. It can also concern understanding, circumstances and the capacity to sustain a particular behaviour.

Isṭāʿū and istaṭāʿū: different forms, rather than two certain meanings

Al-Kahf 18:97 says:

«فَمَا اسْطَاعُوا أَنْ يَظْهَرُوهُ وَمَا اسْتَطَاعُوا لَهُ نَقْبًا»

“So they could neither climb over it nor make a breach in it.”

(Al-Kahf 18:97)

The two forms اسْطَاعُوا (isṭāʿū) and اسْتَطَاعُوا (istaṭāʿū) occur together here.

In Arabic, اسْطَاعَ (isṭāʿa) is a shortened form of اسْتَطَاعَ (istaṭāʿa). The proximity of the sounds tāʾ and ṭāʾ provides the setting for omitting tāʾ in some uses.

A related difference occurs elsewhere in Al-Kahf. Al-Kahf 18:78 says:

«سَأُنَبِّئُكَ بِتَأْوِيلِ مَا لَمْ تَسْتَطِعْ عَلَيْهِ صَبْرًا»

“I will inform you of the interpretation of what you could not patiently endure.”

(Al-Kahf 18:78, excerpt)

Al-Kahf 18:82, however, says:

«ذَٰلِكَ تَأْوِيلُ مَا لَمْ تَسْطِعْ عَلَيْهِ صَبْرًا»

“That is the interpretation of what you could not patiently endure.”

(Al-Kahf 18:82, closing passage)

Both concern inability to remain patient. The forms تَسْتَطِعْ (tastaṭiʿ) and تَسْطِعْ (tasṭiʿ) clearly differ, but both verbs fundamentally mean being able.

Some rhetorical explanations relate the shorter form to understanding becoming easier after the interpretation has been given. In Al-Kahf 18:97, the shorter isṭāʿū for climbing over and the full istaṭāʿū for breaching the barrier have likewise been related to the different difficulty of the two acts.

These may be suggestions about the relationship between wording and meaning. They should not become an absolute rule that every omission of tāʾ marks an easier task and every retention a harder one.

Omitting tāʾ in this family is an established morphological phenomenon. A particular rhetorical effect in any verse requires separate evidence.

6. Ability, obedience and volunteering: semantic boundaries

Ṭ W AYN (ṭawʿ) and K R H (karh)

In uses concerning acceptance, طَوْع (ṭawʿ) contrasts with كَرْه (karh), from ك ر ه (kāf rāʾ hāʾ). One emphasises compliance and acceptance; the other pressure or reluctance.

Fuṣṣilat 41:11 places them together as طَوْعًا أَوْ كَرْهًا (ṭawʿan aw karhan), “willingly or unwillingly.” Āl ʿImrān 3:83 has طَوْعًا وَكَرْهًا (ṭawʿan wa-karhan), “willingly and unwillingly.”

This opposition does not make every obedience free from pressure or difficulty. Someone may accept a command while finding it hard to carry out.

Accepting a command, finding an action easy and being free from pressure are three related but distinct matters.

Ṭ W AYN (aṭāʿa) and T B AYN (tabiʿa)

تَبِعَ (tabiʿa), from ت ب ع (tāʾ bāʾ ʿayn), concerns following something or someone. أَطَاعَ (aṭāʿa) more particularly foregrounds responding to a command or wish.

Following and obeying can coincide within a Qur’anic relationship, but not every instance of following necessarily obeys an explicit command.

Someone may follow another’s path or practice without anyone issuing a command at that moment. Obedience, conversely, can respond to a particular instruction.

اِتَّبَعَ (ittabaʿa), “followed,” and aṭāʿa should therefore not receive one identical translation in every verse without justification.

Ṭ W AYN (istaṭāʿa) and Q D R (qadara)

قَدَرَ (qadara), from ق د ر (qāf dāl rāʾ), concerns ability and power in some uses. Istiṭāʿah likewise expresses being able.

Q D R, however, has a broader family: Qur’anic uses also concern measuring, determining and power.

Qur’anic istiṭāʿah often concerns the possibility of a particular act, such as undertaking pilgrimage, travelling, dictating or enduring patiently.

Each occurrence therefore requires identifying which action is in view. The word alone does not judge the extent of someone’s absolute power.

Ṭ W AYN (istaṭāʿa) and W S AYN (wasiʿa)

وَسِعَ (wasiʿa), from و س ع (wāw sīn ʿayn), concerns room and capacity. In some verses it approaches a person’s capacity to bear responsibility.

Al-Baqarah 2:286 says:

«لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا»

“The God does not charge any soul beyond its capacity.”

(Al-Baqarah 2:286, excerpt)

This is close in meaning to فَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ (fa-ttaqū Allāha mā istaṭaʿtum), “so practise taqwa towards The God as far as you are able,” in Al-Taghābun 64:16.

W S AYN and the root of istiṭāʿah are nevertheless different. Room and capacity can relate to the ability to perform an act without coinciding completely in every instance.

Ṭ W AYN (istaṭāʿa) and Ṭ W Q (aṭāqa)

طَاقَة (ṭāqah), capacity, and أَطَاقَ (aṭāqa), “was able to bear,” belong to ط و ق (ṭāʾ wāw qāf), rather than ط و ع.

This distinction matters in Al-Baqarah 2:184, where يُطِيقُونَهُ (yuṭīqūnahu) and تَطَوَّعَ (taṭawwaʿa) occur in one verse.

Aṭāqa concerns being able to bear or manage something. Taṭawwaʿa in that use concerns voluntarily doing good.

Some dictionaries explain istiṭāʿah and ṭāqah through one another, but semantic proximity does not make the two roots’ histories and morphological structures identical.

Nor does being able to endure a task’s hardship necessarily mean having the ability to undertake every preliminary step. Sometimes the chief obstacle is not bodily strength but the absence of a route, equipment or necessary conditions.

Suggested translations for Forqan

For طَوْعًا (ṭawʿan) contrasted with كَرْهًا (karhan), “willingly,” “with acceptance” or “voluntarily” is appropriate. In Āl ʿImrān 3:83 and Fuṣṣilat 41:11, preserve its opposition to “unwillingly” or “under compulsion.”

طَائِعِينَ (ṭāʾiʿīn) can be rendered “obedient” or “in willing compliance.” In Fuṣṣilat 41:11, the translation should not silently turn heaven and earth into humans possessing human will and bodily speech.

For أَطَاعَ (aṭāʿa) and طَاعَة (ṭāʿah), “obey” and “obedience” are clear equivalents. “Comply with a command” may fit some contexts. “Accept” alone can weaken the element of acting in accordance with the command.

For فَطَوَّعَتْ لَهُ نَفْسُهُ قَتْلَ أَخِيهِ (fa-ṭawwaʿat lahu nafsuhu qatla akhīhi), “his self made killing his brother easy and acceptable to him” is appropriate. “His self voluntarily killed his brother” changes the construction and wrongly identifies who actually commits the killing.

For تَطَوَّعَ (taṭawwaʿa), “did voluntarily” or “willingly took the initiative” is appropriate. In Al-Baqarah 2:158 and Al-Baqarah 2:184, خَيْرًا (khayran), “good,” must also be represented separately, because good action is the object of volunteering.

Translate الْمُطَّوِّعِينَ (al-muṭṭawwiʿīn) in Al-Tawbah 9:79 as “volunteers” or “those who contribute voluntarily.”

اسْتَطَاعَ (istaṭāʿa) generally means “was able,” while the sentence identifies what the ability concerns. مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا (mani istaṭāʿa ilayhi sabīlan) can be rendered “whoever has the ability and a way to reach it.” In لَنْ تَسْتَطِيعَ مَعِيَ صَبْرًا (lan tastaṭīʿa maʿiya ṣabran), “you will not be able to endure patiently with me” is precise.

In Al-Kahf 18:97, both isṭāʿū and istaṭāʿū can be rendered “were able.” Retain their difference in the morphological explanation, rather than inventing two degrees of ability in the basic translation without evidence.

7. Conclusion

Ṭ W AYN concerns acceptance, compliance and yielding to an act in Arabic. This semantic field includes obeying, acting voluntarily and becoming able to perform something.

Its Qur’anic range cannot be reduced to obedience. Ṭawʿan expresses acceptance in contrast to compulsion; aṭāʿa concerns responding to command, whose value depends on the commander and command. Taṭawwaʿa expresses taking the initiative in good. Istiṭāʿah concerns the ability and possibility of performing a particular act.

An important counterexample is fa-ṭawwaʿat lahu nafsuhu qatla akhīhi in Al-Māʾidah 5:30. The self’s acceptance of the act leads to murder. Ṭawʿ in all its forms therefore cannot inherently mean goodness, taqwa or conscious submission to The God.

Nor is obeying those in power always a virtue. While commanding obedience to The God and the Messenger, the Qur’an condemns Pharaoh’s people obeying him and others following masters who led them astray. Obedience’s value follows from the command’s rightfulness and contextual place, rather than compliance alone.

Ability is distinct from obedience. Someone may accept a command but lack the capacity, route or means to carry it out. Qur’anic istiṭāʿah concerns pilgrimage, movement, fulfilling responsibilities, dictating, patient endurance and overcoming obstacles. Being able is therefore more than bodily strength; it may depend on actual conditions of performance.

Ṭawʿan wa-karhan places acceptance alongside unwillingness. Without evidence, creatures’ compliance with The God should likewise not be restricted either to the human experience of choosing a religion or to purely mechanical motion. The verses concern creation’s broader relationship with divine command without explaining that relationship’s nature identically for all beings.

Ṭ W AYN’s semantic core can be expressed as becoming compliant and receptive to an action, or yielding to a command or wish, with a related branch foregrounding the capacity and possibility of performing an act.

Within the family, obedience particularly stresses acting in accordance with command; volunteering stresses taking the initiative without that particular act being obligatory; ability stresses the capacity and possibility of acting. These can be related in human life without being synonymous.

Each Qur’anic use therefore requires identifying who or what accepts the command, which command or action is involved, whether the subject is willingness and acceptance or ability, and whether the verse approves or condemns the behaviour. The answers come from construction and context, rather than a fixed moral value assigned to every derivative.
