Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root Q R R

Settling, becoming stable, finding rest in a proper place, and the calming of restlessness.

Scope of This Entry

The root ق ر ر (q r r) originally points to settling, becoming stable, resting in a place, and the subsiding of restlessness. This root is not merely “remaining”; it is a kind of remaining in which movement, anxiety, inner heat, or dispersal settles down, and something comes to rest in its proper place.

Important Forms from This Root

Important Qur’anic forms of this root include قَرَار (qarār), قُرَّة (qurrah), أَقَرَّ (aqarra), تَقَرَّ (taqarra), اسْتَقَرَّ (istaqarra), مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr), and قَوَارِير (qawārīr). قَرَار (qarār) refers to a fixed place, a place of rest, or a base of settlement. مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) is a place or state in which something settles or reaches the end of its settling. أَقَرَّ (aqarra) is close to confirming, acknowledging, accepting, and standing upon a covenant or truth. تَقَرَّ (taqarra) in the expression تَقَرَّ عَيْن (taqarra ʿayn) is connected with the eye becoming calm and grief and anxiety subsiding. قُرَّة عَيْن (qurrat ʿayn) is something by which the eye and heart become calm when seeing it. قَوَارِير (qawārīr) in the Qur’an refers to glass or crystal; it may be understood from the side of a vessel that holds liquid within itself and visibly displays a state of containment, but this layer must be kept carefully distinct from the primary meaning of settlement.

Central Meaning

The linguistic core of ق ر ر (q r r) is stability after movement and calm after anxiety. Something that has قَرَار (qarār) has come out of wandering and found a place. When the eye settles, it no longer trembles with worry and searching. When the earth is قَرَار (qarār), it becomes the bed of dwelling and life. When the womb is a قَرَار مَّكِين (qarār makīn), it is a secure and firm place for holding the drop.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root can be seen in water that becomes calm after boiling and waves; in a body that sits into its place after motion; in land that becomes a place of settlement beneath the human being’s feet; or in an eye that, after fear and tears, becomes calm by seeing what gives it security. From here the connection of ق ر ر (q r r) with coolness can also be understood. In Arabic, قُرَّة عَيْن (qurrat ʿayn) is not merely surface happiness; it is a cool calm that settles into the eye after the heat of grief and the inflammation of anxiety.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic usage, ق ر ر (q r r) is connected with settlement, stillness, stability, coolness, calm, and acknowledgment. Acknowledgment can also be understood from this same field: accepting something and becoming firm upon it; bringing a statement out of denial, hesitation, or evasion and placing it in the position of admission and establishedness. In the final version, if a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or old Arabic usage is found, it can be added.

The Earth as Qarār

One important Qur’anic use is the earth as قَرَار (qarār):

أَمَّن جَعَلَ الْأَرْضَ قَرَارًا وَجَعَلَ خِلَالَهَا أَنْهَارًا

(amman jaʿala al-arḍa qarāran wa-jaʿala khilālahā anhārā)

Is He not the One who made the earth a place of settlement and placed rivers through it? An-Naml 27:61

In this ayah, the earth is قَرَار (qarār): a bed upon which life can settle, movements can find order, and the human being can dwell and live. The earth is not only matter beneath the feet; it is the place of life’s settlement.

The same meaning appears in another ayah:

اللَّهُ الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ الْأَرْضَ قَرَارًا وَالسَّمَاءَ بِنَاءً

(Allāhu alladhī jaʿala lakumu al-arḍa qarāran wa-al-samāʾa bināʾā)

The God is the One who made the earth for you a place of settlement and the sky a structure. Ghafir 40:64

Here the earth’s قَرَار (qarār) stands opposite the sky’s building. The earth is the place of settlement, and the sky is like the structure above. So قَرَار (qarār) is the earth’s quality for dwelling and becoming calm, not merely another name for the earth itself.

The Secure Resting Place of the Womb

The second field is settlement in the womb:

ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَاهُ نُطْفَةً فِي قَرَارٍ مَّكِينٍ

(thumma jaʿalnāhu nuṭfatan fī qarārin makīn)

Then We made him a drop in a secure, firm resting place. Al-Mu’minun 23:13

And also:

فَجَعَلْنَاهُ فِي قَرَارٍ مَّكِينٍ

(fa-jaʿalnāhu fī qarārin makīn)

So We placed it in a secure, firm resting place. Al-Mursalat 77:21

In these ayat, قَرَار مَّكِين (qarār makīn) points to a secure, covered, stable, and capable place for holding and growth. The drop is fluid and vulnerable; the secure resting place is where this slippery fluid is preserved and given the possibility of growth. Being مَّكِين (makīn) means this resting place is not merely an empty container; it is a strong and fitting place for holding and nurturing.

The Human Mustaqarr on Earth

The third field is the human being’s مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) on earth:

وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

(wa-lakum fī al-arḍi mustaqarrun wa-matāʿun ilā ḥīn)

And for you there will be on the earth a place of settlement and provision for a time. Al-Baqarah 2:36

In this ayah, مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) comes together with مَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ (matāʿun ilā ḥīn), provision for a time. Earth is the human being’s place of settlement, but this settlement is not permanent. The human being settles on earth, lives, benefits, and is tested, but this settlement is limited until a time.

The same meaning is repeated in Al-A’raf:

وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

(wa-lakum fī al-arḍi mustaqarrun wa-matāʿun ilā ḥīn)

And for you there will be on the earth a place of settlement and provision for a time. Al-A’raf 7:24

This ayah shows that after the descent, the human being becomes settled on earth. Descent is the transfer into the earthly stage; مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) is the temporary place of life within that stage.

Mustaqarr and Mustawdaʿ

The fourth field is مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) in contrast with مُسْتَوْدَع (mustawdaʿ):

وَهُوَ الَّذِي أَنشَأَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ فَمُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمُسْتَوْدَعٌ

(wa-huwa alladhī anshaʾakum min nafsin wāḥidatin fa-mustaqarrun wa-mustawdaʿ)

And He is the One who brought you forth from a single self; so there is a place of settlement and a place of deposit. Al-An’am 6:98

مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) is connected with stability and settlement; مُسْتَوْدَع (mustawdaʿ) is connected with being deposited and temporarily held. The exact meaning of these two in the ayah requires separate study, but at the root level, مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) is a place of settlement and مُسْتَوْدَع (mustawdaʿ) is a place of deposit.

The Final Place of Settlement

The fifth field is the final place of settlement:

إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ يَوْمَئِذٍ الْمُسْتَقَرُّ

(ilā rabbika yawmaʾidhin al-mustaqarr)

On that Day, the place of settlement is toward your Lord. Al-Qiyamah 75:12

In this ayah, الْمُسْتَقَرّ (al-mustaqarr) is the end of fleeing and movement. After all going, running, denial, and restlessness, the human being reaches the final settlement toward his Lord.

The same field appears in another ayah:

وَإِنَّ الْآخِرَةَ هِيَ دَارُ الْقَرَارِ

(wa-inna al-ākhirata hiya dāru al-qarār)

And indeed, the Hereafter is the Abode of Settlement. Ghafir 40:39

The world is a place of movement, testing, change, and passing. The Hereafter is دَارُ الْقَرَار (dār al-qarār), the abode of final stability and lasting result. This ayah shows that قَرَار (qarār) in the Qur’an can also express final destiny, not only a material dwelling place.

The Eye Becoming Calm

The sixth field is the eye becoming calm:

فَكُلِي وَاشْرَبِي وَقَرِّي عَيْنًا

(fa-kulī wa-ishrabī wa-qarrī ʿaynan)

So eat and drink, and let your eye become calm. Maryam 19:26

In this ayah, قَرِّي عَيْنًا (qarrī ʿaynan) means that Maryam’s eye should become calm from anxiety, pressure, and fear. This expression is not merely “be happy”; it means the inner inflammation should subside and the gaze should be rescued from restlessness.

About the mother of Moses, it also says:

فَرَجَعْنَاكَ إِلَىٰ أُمِّكَ كَيْ تَقَرَّ عَيْنُهَا وَلَا تَحْزَنَ

(fa-rajaʿnāka ilā ummika kay taqarra ʿaynuhā wa-lā taḥzan)

So We returned you to your mother so that her eye might become calm and she would not grieve. Ta-Ha 20:40

And also:

فَرَدَدْنَاهُ إِلَىٰ أُمِّهِ كَيْ تَقَرَّ عَيْنُهَا وَلَا تَحْزَنَ

(fa-radadnāhu ilā ummihi kay taqarra ʿaynuhā wa-lā taḥzan)

So We returned him to his mother so that her eye might become calm and she would not grieve. Al-Qasas 28:13

In these two ayat, the settling of the eye is placed opposite grief. The mother’s eye is restless in worry and sorrow; with the child’s return, her eye becomes calm. قُرَّة عَيْن (qurrat ʿayn) here is calm after fear and waiting.

Qurrat Aʿyun

The seventh field is قُرَّةَ أَعْيُن (qurrata aʿyun):

رَبَّنَا هَبْ لَنَا مِنْ أَزْوَاجِنَا وَذُرِّيَّاتِنَا قُرَّةَ أَعْيُنٍ

(rabbanā hab lanā min azwājinā wa-dhurriyyātinā qurrata aʿyun)

Our Lord, grant us from our spouses and our descendants a calmness/brightness of eyes. Al-Furqan 25:74

In this supplication, قُرَّةَ أَعْيُن (qurrata aʿyun) points to a family through whom the eye and heart become calm. This is not merely family enjoyment; it is seeing righteousness, purity, growth, and calm in the closest human bonds.

In another ayah, Pharaoh’s wife says about Moses:

قُرَّتُ عَيْنٍ لِّي وَلَكَ

(qurratu ʿaynin lī wa-lak)

He is a calmness/brightness of eye for me and for you. Al-Qasas 28:9

Here قُرَّة عَيْن (qurrat ʿayn) is said about a child whose sight calms the heart and awakens affection, even though the future of the story shows that this same child carries a meaning beyond the desire of Pharaoh’s household.

Acknowledgment and Fixing the Covenant

The eighth field is acknowledgment and the fixing of a covenant:

ثُمَّ أَقْرَرْتُمْ وَأَنتُمْ تَشْهَدُونَ

(thumma aqrartum wa-antum tashhadūn)

Then you acknowledged, while you yourselves were witnessing. Al-Baqarah 2:84

And in the ayah of the covenant of the prophets:

قَالَ أَأَقْرَرْتُمْ وَأَخَذْتُمْ عَلَىٰ ذَٰلِكُمْ إِصْرِي قَالُوا أَقْرَرْنَا

(qāla a-aqrartum wa-akhadhtum ʿalā dhālikum iṣrī qālū aqrarnā)

He said: Have you acknowledged and taken My heavy covenant upon that? They said: We have acknowledged. Al-Imran 3:81

أَقَرَّ (aqarra) here means accepting, confirming, and standing upon it. Acknowledgment is speech that has come out of denial and instability and has been placed in the position of establishedness.

A Thing Settling in Place

The ninth field is something becoming settled in a place:

فَلَمَّا رَآهُ مُسْتَقِرًّا عِندَهُ قَالَ هَٰذَا مِن فَضْلِ رَبِّي

(fa-lammā raʾāhu mustaqirran ʿindahu qāla hādhā min faḍli rabbī)

Then when he saw it settled before him, he said: This is from the bounty of my Lord. An-Naml 27:40

In this ayah, مُسْتَقِرًّا (mustaqirran) means placed and settled before him. The object has moved from a state of transfer into a state of presence and settlement.

Qawārīr

The tenth field is قَوَارِير (qawārīr):

قَوَارِيرَ مِن فِضَّةٍ قَدَّرُوهَا تَقْدِيرًا

(qawārīra min fiḍḍatin qaddarūhā taqdīrā)

Crystal vessels of silver, which they have measured with precise measure. Al-Insan 76:16

قَوَارِير (qawārīr) in the Qur’an refers to transparent crystal cups or vessels. Its connection with ق ر ر (q r r) can be understood from the side of being a vessel: something that holds liquid within itself, brings it out of dispersal, and through its transparency reveals the state inside. Yet in translation, قَوَارِير (qawārīr) should remain “crystal,” “glass,” or “crystal vessels,” not have the meaning of settlement directly substituted for it.

Distinguishing the Uses

The uses of ق ر ر (q r r) must be distinguished. The earth as قَرَار (qarār) means the bed of life and dwelling. قَرَار مَّكِين (qarār makīn) means a secure, firm, and holding place. The earth as مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) means the human being’s temporary place of settlement in the world. دَارُ الْقَرَار (dār al-qarār) means the abode of final stability. قُرَّة عَيْن (qurrat ʿayn) means the calm of the eye and heart after anxiety or waiting. Acknowledgment means confirmation and acceptance. Settling means coming to rest after transfer. قَوَارِير (qawārīr) are transparent crystal vessels; at the root level they come near the field of holding and placing, but they have their own independent meaning.

Structural Note

Structurally, genitive constructions and compounds are important. قَرَار مَّكِين (qarār makīn) is a firm and capable resting place for holding. مُسْتَقَرّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ (mustaqarr wa-matāʿun ilā ḥīn) shows that worldly settlement is temporary. دَارُ الْقَرَار (dār al-qarār) is the abode of final stability. قُرَّة عَيْن (qurrat ʿayn) is a special expression that conveys the calm of the eye and heart together. أَقْرَرْتُمْ (aqrartum) in the context of covenant means that you accepted and became firm upon it.

Difference from Nearby Roots

ق ر ر (q r r) differs from several nearby roots. TH B T focuses on being firm and established; ق ر ر (q r r), in addition to stability, also carries calming and finding a place. S K N points to stillness, calm, and the subsiding of movement; ق ر ر (q r r) emphasizes more strongly settling into a place and the end of anxiety. Q AYN D (Q ʿ D) is sitting, but قَرَار (qarār) is not necessarily bodily sitting; it can be a place of settlement, a destiny, or the calm of the eye. A W Y is close to seeking refuge and taking place in a shelter, while ق ر ر (q r r) points more to stability and calm after taking place. M K N is related to capacity, enabling, and firm establishment; قَرَار مَّكِين (qarār makīn) combines these two fields: a fixed and capable place.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

For translation in Forqan, ق ر ر (q r r) must be translated according to context. قَرَار (qarār) can be “place of settlement,” “fixed place,” “calm bed,” or “place of settling.” مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) can be “place of settlement,” “place of settling,” “destination of settlement,” or “place of becoming calm.” دَارُ الْقَرَار (dār al-qarār) is better rendered as “the Abode of Settlement” or “the Abode of Final Stability.” قُرَّة عَيْن (qurrat ʿayn) should not be translated only as “happiness”; it is better rendered as “calmness of the eye,” “brightness of the eye,” or “that which lets the eye and heart become calm.” Acknowledgment is also “accepting and becoming firm upon it,” not merely saying a sentence. قَوَارِير (qawārīr) in translation should remain “crystal,” “glass,” or “crystal vessels.”

Linguistic Summary

Linguistically, the root ق ر ر (q r r) points to settling, becoming stable, becoming calm, the cooling of inner heat, and finding a place. Its material image is something that, after motion, boiling, or anxiety, sits into its place and becomes calm. In the Qur’an, this root extends from the earth as a place of settlement to the secure resting place of the womb; from the human being’s temporary مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) in the world to the دَارُ الْقَرَار (dār al-qarār) of the Hereafter; from acknowledgment in covenant to قُرَّة عَيْن (qurrat ʿayn) and the calming of the eye and heart; and from a thing settling in place to transparent crystal vessels. Its difference from stability is that stability mainly shows firmness, while ق ر ر (q r r) also carries calm, coolness, and finding a place after anxiety.
