1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ب ر أ is used in the Qur'an in several close but distinct branches:

  • bringing into being and originating;
  • healing and releasing from illness;
  • clearing and absolving from accusation;
  • being free of fault and unrelated to another person's act;
  • declaring separation from a belief, act, worship, or covenant;
  • and referring to created beings in the word بَرِيَّة.

In all these uses, one can see a kind of release from attribution, defect, illness, accusation, or a previous state. In the branch of creation, too, the being comes out of nonexistence or out of an unrealized state and appears as an existent created being.

Even so, not all branches should be reduced to "isolating a body" or "hardware separation." Bara'ah is sometimes from an accusation, sometimes from responsibility for another person's action, sometimes from false worship, and sometimes from a covenantal obligation. The kind of separation is specified by context.

The shortest expression for this root is:

Bringing into being; or becoming released, cleared, and separated from illness, accusation, responsibility, dependence, or an act attributed to a person.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

بَرَأَ / يَبْرَأُ

He brought into being, or he became free from illness, defect, and affliction, according to construction and context.

In the Qur'an, the simple verbal form appears in نَبْرَأَهَا for bringing into being.

بَرَّأَ / يُبَرِّئُ

He cleared and absolved; he removed a false accusation or attribution from someone.

أَبْرَأَ / يُبْرِئُ

He healed and released from illness.

تَبَرَّأَ / يَتَبَرَّأُ

He declared separation from someone or something; he withdrew from attribution, following, responsibility, or attachment to it.

بَرَاءَة

Bara'ah; release from obligation, responsibility, accusation, or a bond that had previously existed or was assumed to exist.

بَرَاء

Clear and separate; a strong or emphatic form of declaring bara'ah.

بَرِيء

Clear, free of fault, clear of attribution, or separate from another person's act and belief.

بُرَآء

A plural or collective form of bari'; those who declare themselves clear of another or of his act and worship.

بَارِئ

Originator, bringer into being.

This form is one of the descriptions of The God in the Qur'an.

بَرِيَّة

Created beings or creatures.

مُبَرَّأ

Someone who is considered clear of an accusation or false attribution.

The Qur'anic family of this root is not limited to bara'ah. Forms connected with bringing into being, clearing, healing, declaring separation, being free, and created beings are all present in the Qur'an.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

For the root B R A, one single English equivalent cannot cover all its uses without loss.

Three main branches must be preserved:

Bringing into being

In this branch, something comes into existence and is realized as an actual created being.

Release and clearing

In this branch, a person is freed from illness, defect, accusation, or false attribution.

Separation from attribution or responsibility

In this branch, a person declares that he is not attached to, responsible for, or participating in the other party's act, belief, worship, or obligation.

A semantic closeness can be seen among these branches: something comes out of a state of mixture, entanglement, or previous attribution, and its situation becomes clear and distinct.

But this closeness should not be turned into a definitive theory of "separation for independence." For example, healing from illness does not necessarily mean the independence of the being, and bara'ah from an accusation is not a physical separation.

A more cautious definition is:

Coming out of, or bringing out of, a state of defect, illness, accusation, responsibility, or attribution; and in the branch of creation, bringing a being into existence.

5. The Material Image of the Root

One of the clearest material images of this root is the body of a sick person who, after treatment, is released from illness. The illness had previously accompanied the body, but with recovery the person comes out of that state.

Another image is a human being to whom an accusation has been attributed, but the truth becomes clear and that attribution is removed from him. In this case, nothing is physically separated from his body; rather, a false relationship between the person and the accusation is cut off.

In bara'ah from another person's act, a person also clarifies the boundary of his responsibility:

This act is not mine, and I am not responsible for it or an approver of it.

The image of sharpening or shaving wood may be literarily attractive, but the root should not be built on it. The Qur'an does not use B R A to mean shaving wood or removing excess pieces.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, بَرِئَ مِنَ المرض refers to being released and recovering from illness.

بَرِئَ مِنَ التهمة means he was cleared of accusation and recognized as innocent.

بَرِئَ مِنَ الدَّيْن can refer to being released from a debt or its responsibility.

بَرِئَ مِنْهُ means he was clear and separate from him, from his act, or from an attribution connected to him.

بَرَّأَهُ means he cleared him from accusation.

أَبْرَأَهُ مِنَ المرض means he healed him from illness.

In the branch of creation, al-Bari' is used for the bringer into being.

These uses show that the field of the root is connected with release from a relation or state and also with bringing into being; but the claim that early Arabic treated it as a technical term for "geometric ordering of parts" is not defensible without clear evidence.

7. Semantic Restrictions and Misreadings

One restriction is translating all derivatives of B R A as "to create." This translation is suitable for:

الْبَارِئُ
نَبْرَأَهَا

but in:

أَنَا بَرِيءٌ مِمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ

it has no meaning of creation.

Another restriction is translating all cases as "being innocent." Bari' can mean clear of accusation, but in many verses it means being separate from and not responsible for another person's act or shirk.

Another error is turning bara'ah into cutting off all human relations. In the Qur'an, bara'ah is usually directed toward a specific matter:

  • bara'ah from shirk;
  • bara'ah from false objects of worship;
  • bara'ah from a criminal act;
  • bara'ah between followers and leaders on the Day of Judgment;
  • or the end of covenantal responsibility in a specific context.

Therefore, bari' minkum does not always mean a prohibition of every kind of conversation, justice, coexistence, or human relationship. One must ask exactly what bara'ah is being declared from.

8. Methodological Note: Bara'ah from the Person or from the Act?

The construction:

بَرِيءٌ مِنْ

may appear to express bara'ah from a person, but the context must clarify from which aspect of that person the separation is being declared:

  • his act;
  • his shirk;
  • his worship;
  • responsibility for his behavior;
  • following him;
  • or a covenant that has been violated.

For example:

أَنْتُمْ بَرِيئُونَ مِمَّا أَعْمَلُ وَأَنَا بَرِيءٌ مِمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ

Bara'ah is directly connected to the acts of the two sides.

In:

إِنَّنِي بَرَاءٌ مِمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ

Ibrahim declares bara'ah from what his people worship, not from their humanity or from the right to deal with them justly.

So Qur'anic bara'ah must be understood as subject-based, not as a permission for unlimited hostility toward persons.

9. A Brief Look at Related Semitic Languages

Determining the precise history of the different branches of B R A in related Semitic languages requires a separate study.

From Arabic itself and from Qur'anic usage, these fields can be recognized with greater confidence:

  • bringing into being;
  • release from illness;
  • being cleared of accusation;
  • release from responsibility or attribution;
  • and declaring separation.

One should not, merely from resemblance between words, present the theory of "carving a being out of raw matter" as a definitive historical meaning.

10. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

a. Al-Bari' Beside Al-Khaliq and Al-Musawwir

هُوَ اللَّهُ الْخَالِقُ الْبَارِئُ الْمُصَوِّرُ لَهُ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ

He is The God: the Creator, the Originator, the Form-Giver; to Him belong the most beautiful names.

Al-Hashr 59:24

Three descriptions come beside one another: al-Khaliq, al-Bari', and al-Musawwir.

This co-occurrence shows that the words are not completely indifferent to one another, but the Qur'an does not define in this verse a technical, step-by-step sequence for creation.

One may cautiously say: Khaliq emphasizes creation and measuring the created thing. Bari' indicates bringing the created being into existence and its realization as an existent. Musawwir emphasizes giving form and shape.

But expressions such as "Khaliq as engineer of matter, Bari' as separator of body, and Musawwir as designer of appearance" exceed what the verse itself can carry.

b. Bari'ikum in the Story of the Calf

فَتُوبُوا إِلَىٰ بَارِئِكُمْ

So turn back to your Originator.

Al-Baqarah 2:54

The people of Musa had taken something made by their own hands as an object of worship. Against that, the verse returns them to bari'ikum, their true Originator.

The contrast is important: they had made the calf, but they themselves were created by the Bari'.

The use of Bari' in this verse emphasizes the human being's fundamental dependence on his true Originator, not the "bodily independence" of the human being.

c. Before We Bring It into Being

مَا أَصَابَ مِنْ مُصِيبَةٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي أَنْفُسِكُمْ إِلَّا فِي كِتَابٍ مِنْ قَبْلِ أَنْ نَبْرَأَهَا

No calamity strikes in the earth or in yourselves except that it is in a book before We bring it into being.

Al-Hadid 57:22

Nabra'aha here is connected with bringing into being and actualizing.

The verse distinguishes between being recorded in the Book and the actual occurrence of the calamity. But it should not be translated in the language of "software," "computational system," or "hardware embodiment." The direct meaning is that the event, before external realization, is in divine knowledge and the Book.

d. Healing Illness by Permission of The God

وَأُبْرِئُ الْأَكْمَهَ وَالْأَبْرَصَ وَأُحْيِي الْمَوْتَىٰ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ

And I heal the one born blind and the leper, and I give life to the dead by permission of The God.

Ali 'Imran 3:49

The verb ubri'u points to releasing a person from illness and healing him.

The verse explicitly says that this happens by permission of The God. Therefore, the power of Isa is not presented as independent of Him.

This use is one of the clearest witnesses for the meaning of release from defect or illness in this root.

e. Clearing Musa from Accusation

فَبَرَّأَهُ اللَّهُ مِمَّا قَالُوا وَكَانَ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ وَجِيهًا

So The God cleared him from what they said, and he was honorable before The God.

Al-Ahzab 33:69

Barra'ahu means that He removed a false attribution or accusation from Musa and made his innocence clear.

No physical separation occurs here; what is cut off is the false relation between Musa and the accusation.

f. Not Declaring the Self Clear

وَمَا أُبَرِّئُ نَفْسِي إِنَّ النَّفْسَ لَأَمَّارَةٌ بِالسُّوءِ

And I do not declare my self clear; indeed, the self commands much evil.

Yusuf 12:53

Ubarri'u nafsi means: I do not declare myself pure, free of fault, and removed from the possibility of error.

This verse shows that clearing is a judgment about the attribution of error and responsibility.

g. Innocence Before Slander

وَمَنْ يَكْسِبْ خَطِيئَةً أَوْ إِثْمًا ثُمَّ يَرْمِ بِهِ بَرِيئًا فَقَدِ احْتَمَلَ بُهْتَانًا وَإِثْمًا مُبِينًا

And whoever earns a mistake or sin, then throws it upon an innocent person, has carried slander and clear sin.

An-Nisa 4:112

Bari'an here means free of fault and clear of the crime attributed to him.

This use is not the same as theological bara'ah or the end of a covenant; its legal and ethical context determines it.

h. Being Cleared of Improper Speech

أُولَٰئِكَ مُبَرَّءُونَ مِمَّا يَقُولُونَ

Those are cleared from what they say about them.

An-Nur 24:26

Mubarra'un expresses that they are clear of the attributions and improper statements made about them.

This is also a witness that the root can relate to removing accusation and making innocence clear.

i. Ibrahim's Bara'ah from the Objects of Worship of His People

وَإِذْ قَالَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ لِأَبِيهِ وَقَوْمِهِ إِنَّنِي بَرَاءٌ مِمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ
إِلَّا الَّذِي فَطَرَنِي فَإِنَّهُ سَيَهْدِينِ

And when Ibrahim said to his father and his people: I am clear of what you worship, except the One who originated me; He will guide me.

Az-Zukhruf 43:26-27

Ibrahim's bara'ah is related to the subject of worship.

He separates from the fabricated objects of worship and the worship-system of his people, and he makes the exception clear:

إِلَّا الَّذِي فَطَرَنِي

So his bara'ah is not an absolute negation of every human relationship; it is the rejection of participation in false worship.

j. Mutual Bara'ah from Acts

لِي عَمَلِي وَلَكُمْ عَمَلُكُمْ أَنْتُمْ بَرِيئُونَ مِمَّا أَعْمَلُ وَأَنَا بَرِيءٌ مِمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ

My action is for me and your action is for you. You are clear of what I do, and I am clear of what you do.

Yunus 10:41

The verse clarifies the boundary of responsibility for acts.

Each side is responsible for its own act and does not carry the act of the other.

Bara'ah here is not the cutting of all social relation; it is the separation of ethical and practical responsibility.

k. Leaders Disowning Followers

إِذْ تَبَرَّأَ الَّذِينَ اتُّبِعُوا مِنَ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوا وَرَأَوُا الْعَذَابَ

When those who were followed declared bara'ah from those who followed, and they saw the punishment.

Al-Baqarah 2:166

Misleading leaders, on the Day of Judgment, distance themselves from their followers and do not accept responsibility for them.

This tabarri shows that relations of power and following that seemed firm in the world collapse at the time of accountability.

l. Ibrahim's Tabarri After Enmity Became Clear

فَلَمَّا تَبَيَّنَ لَهُ أَنَّهُ عَدُوٌّ لِلَّهِ تَبَرَّأَ مِنْهُ

Then when it became clear to him that he was an enemy of The God, he declared bara'ah from him.

At-Tawbah 9:114

Tabarri occurs after tabayyun, after the truth of the other party's stance becomes clear.

The verse does not allow a person to declare bara'ah from another on the basis of guesswork, anger, or family relation. The matter must have become clear.

m. The Declaration of Bara'ah at the Beginning of Surah At-Tawbah

بَرَاءَةٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ إِلَى الَّذِينَ عَاهَدْتُمْ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ

A declaration of bara'ah from The God and His Messenger to those of the mushrikin with whom you made a covenant.

At-Tawbah 9:1

This verse comes in the context of covenants, violation of obligations, respite, and specific groups.

Bara'ah here is a declaration of the end or removal of covenantal obligation toward parties whom the context of the surah explains.

This verse must not be turned into a declaration of permanent hostility toward everyone called mushrik. The following verses distinguish between groups and require fulfillment of covenants toward those who did not break their covenant.

n. "The God and His Messenger Are Clear"

أَنَّ اللَّهَ بَرِيءٌ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ وَرَسُولُهُ

That The God and His Messenger are clear of the mushrikin.

At-Tawbah 9:3

This phrase is also understood in the same context of public declaration, covenants, and the behavior of specific groups.

Being clear means not approving shirk and ending covenantal responsibility in the specified context; it does not mean negating justice, conversation, or all human rights.

o. "We Are Clear of You and What You Worship"

إِنَّا بُرَآءُ مِنْكُمْ وَمِمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ

We are clear of you and of what you worship apart from The God.

Al-Mumtahanah 60:4

This verse expresses the theological bara'ah of Ibrahim and those with him from the shirk of their people.

But the surah later distinguishes between those who did not fight the believers because of religion and those who fought and expelled them. Therefore, this bara'ah must not be separated from the wider context of justice and good conduct.

12. The Divine Attribute: الْبَارِئ

Al-Bari' is one of the descriptions of The God:

هُوَ اللَّهُ الْخَالِقُ الْبَارِئُ الْمُصَوِّرُ

His being Bari' is connected with bringing created beings into being.

This description should not be translated as "engineer of separation." The Qur'an does not present beings merely as parts separated from a larger matter.

Also, being Bari' does not mean making the creature independent of The God. Created beings, after creation, remain dependent on Him in existence, continuance, and power.

A more fitting expression is:

Al-Bari': the Originator who brings created beings into being.

The co-occurrence of Khaliq, Bari', and Musawwir can show differences in the direction of attention of each description, but one should not build from it a definitive three-stage production line.

13. Grammatical and Structural Notes

بَرِيءٌ مِنْ

The letter min introduces the thing from whose attribution, responsibility, act, or attachment a person is clear:

بَرِيءٌ مِمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ
بَرِيءٌ مِمَّا تُشْرِكُونَ
بَرِيءٌ مِنْكُمْ

This construction does not mean the cutting of a "communication portal." It means declaring the absence of attribution, responsibility, or participation.

تَبَرَّأَ مِنْ

Form V can show a person's conscious entry into a state of bara'ah or his declaration of separation.

But complete cutting of all relations cannot be concluded from the verbal form itself.

بَرَّأَهُ مِمَّا قَالُوا

The first object is the person who is cleared, and the construction mimma qalu identifies the accusation or statement removed from him.

أُبَرِّئُ نَفْسِي

A person declares his self free of fault. Its negation:

وَمَا أُبَرِّئُ نَفْسِي

means: I do not regard myself as absolutely pure and free of fault.

أُبْرِئُ الْأَكْمَهَ وَالْأَبْرَصَ

In Form IV, the verb is transitive and releases the sick person from illness.

بَرَاءَةٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ

Bara'ah is the opening declaration or predicate of the sentence, and from The God identifies the source of this declaration.

14. B R A Compared with KH L W

Khuluww emphasizes being empty or being free from something in the sense of absence.

Bara'ah indicates the absence of responsibility, attribution, accusation, or participation.

A person may be clear of an accusation while his life is not empty of every defect.

So: khuluww: being empty of something; bara'ah: being clear or free of a specific attribution, responsibility, or accusation.

15. B R A Compared with KH L Q

KH L Q and B R A are close to one another in the field of creation, but they are not complete synonyms.

Khalq is related to creation, measuring, and bringing into being.

Bar', in the description al-Bari', emphasizes bringing the created being into existence as a realized being.

But the difference should not be made rigid in this way:

  • khalq is only calculating matter;
  • bar' is only separating the body.

The Qur'an does not present such a technical division.

The precise difference must be understood through examining all uses of both roots and their co-occurrence.

16. B R A Compared with F T R

Fatr is connected with opening, splitting, and beginning creation.

Bar', in its creative use, is connected with bringing the being into existence.

One may cautiously say: fatr: beginning and opening creation; bar': bringing the created being into existence.

But this difference must not be turned into a definitive sequence of the stages of creation. The Qur'an does not say that every being first undergoes fatr, then khalq, then bar'.

17. Specialized Note: The Limits of Qur'anic Bara'ah

Bara'ah in the Qur'an is a weighty ethical and legal word. Declaring bara'ah means that a person says:

  • I am not a partner in this act;
  • I am not responsible for it;
  • I do not approve it;
  • or the previous obligation toward the other party no longer stands.

But bara'ah from an act or belief, by itself, does not permit injustice toward the holder of that belief.

Several principles must be observed in understanding the limits of bara'ah.

Bara'ah Must Have a Specific Subject

In many verses, the Qur'an says:

مِمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ
مِمَّا تُشْرِكُونَ
مِمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ

So the subject of separation is clear.

Bara'ah from Responsibility Is Not Escaping Responsibility

A person cannot perform an act and then escape its consequences simply by saying, "I am clear." Being clear must correspond to the truth of the act.

Bara'ah from a Person Is Often Bara'ah from His Direction and Act

When leaders and followers disown one another, the subject is following, misguidance, and responsibility.

When Ibrahim declares bara'ah from his people and their objects of worship, the subject is shirk and worship.

Covenantal Bara'ah Depends on the Context of the Covenant

The beginning of Surah At-Tawbah cannot be separated from the verses that follow it. The Qur'an distinguishes between covenant-breakers and those who remained faithful to their covenant.

So covenantal bara'ah is not an unrestricted ruling about every human being outside the believing community.

Bara'ah Does Not Contradict Justice

A person may be clear of another person's belief or act while still being obligated to justice, good conduct, worthy conversation, and fulfillment of covenant.

Bara'ah clarifies the boundary of responsibility and participation; it does not create unlimited hatred.

18. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

بَرَأَ / نَبْرَأَهَا

Brought into being. Brought into existence. We bring it into being.

"Separated bodily" is not the translation of the word.

بَارِئ

Originator. Creator, in a suitable context.

It is better to preserve al-Bari' in the base translation and clarify the meaning in explanation.

"Engineer of separation" is not a suitable translation.

بَرَّأَهُ

He cleared him. He cleared him from accusation. He made his innocence clear.

أَبْرَأَ

He healed. He released from illness. He treated and healed.

بَرِيء

Clear. Free of fault. Clear of accusation. Separate and not responsible for that act, according to context.

بَرَاءَة

Bara'ah. Declaration of the end of responsibility or obligation. Release from attribution or covenant, according to context.

تَبَرَّأَ

He declared bara'ah. He separated from him or his act. He denied responsibility and following.

بَرِيَّة

Created beings. Creatures.

مُبَرَّأ

Cleared. Clear of accusation. Recognized as innocent.

19. Conclusion

The root B R A in the Qur'an has several connected branches: bringing into being; healing and releasing from illness; clearing from accusation; being free of fault; declaring separation from another person's act, shirk, worship, or responsibility; ending covenantal obligation in a specific context; and referring to created beings.

This root cannot be restricted only to "creation" or only to "separation." In the description al-Bari', the root is connected with bringing created beings into being. The God being Bari' does not mean making created beings independent of Him; created beings remain dependent on Him in existence and continuance.

In:

نَبْرَأَهَا

the meaning is bringing into being and actualizing.

In:

وَأُبْرِئُ الْأَكْمَهَ وَالْأَبْرَصَ

it points to healing and release from illness.

In:

فَبَرَّأَهُ اللَّهُ مِمَّا قَالُوا

the intended meaning is clearing from accusation.

In:

أَنَا بَرِيءٌ مِمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ

the boundary of responsibility for acts is clarified.

In:

بَرَاءَةٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ

bara'ah is raised in the context of covenants and the end of obligation toward specific groups, and it should not be turned into permanent hostility toward everyone else.

Qur'anic bara'ah usually has a specific subject: from which act, belief, worship, accusation, or obligation is bara'ah being declared?

This bara'ah does not permit injustice, breaking covenants, or denying human rights. A person may be clear of another's belief and act while still dealing with him justly.

The difference between B R A, KH L Q, and F T R must be stated cautiously. The Qur'an does not turn these three roots into a technical production line or rigid stages of creation.

The shortest expression for the core of this root is:

Bringing into being; or becoming released, cleared, and separated from illness, accusation, responsibility, dependence, or an act attributed to a person.
