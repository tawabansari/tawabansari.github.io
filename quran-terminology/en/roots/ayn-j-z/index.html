Principal meanings: inability to perform an act or attain an aim; in another branch, the rear or final part of something. The family also includes عَجُوز for an elderly person and مُعْجِز for someone who puts an objective beyond another's ability or escapes their reach.

1. Meaning in early Arabic

The three-consonant root ع ج ز has two principal Arabic branches: inability or failure to attain an aim, and the rear or final part of something.

عَجَزَ عَنِ الشَّيْءِ describes being unable to do something or attain an intended objective.

The inability may be bodily, as when someone cannot lift a heavy load, or concern skill, knowledge, resources, or circumstances, as when someone cannot solve a problem or reach a destination.

عَجُز names the rear or final part of a body or object: the hindquarters of a person or animal, the lower remaining trunk of a palm, or the end of a statement or undertaking.

Arabic dictionaries sometimes distinguish these two branches: weakness or inability, and the rear of something.

An illustrative connection is possible. Someone left behind by a caravan may be unable to catch up; someone unable to finish a task may similarly be seen as falling short of the intended outcome.

This image alone does not establish that inability historically developed from the rear-part sense.

Inability and the rear part are both established meanings; neither should be forced into the other to create an appearance of unity.

First witness: beyond reach in early poetry

A verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet al-Aʿshā of Qays says:

فَذَاكَ وَلَمْ يُعْجِزْ مِنَ الْمَوْتِ رَبَّهُ

fa-dhāka wa-lam yuʿjiz mina al-mawti rabbahu

وَلَكِنْ أَتَاهُ الْمَوْتُ لَا يَتَأَبَّقُ

wa-lākin atāhu al-mawtu lā yataʾabbaqu

Translation: Despite all that, it did not save its owner from death; death came to him, with no escape.

Dictionaries transmit this wording under al-Aʿshā's name. Versions of the poem beginning أَرِقْتُ وَمَا هَذَا السُّهَادُ الْمُؤَرِّقُ also contain different wording concerning the building and its owner's death; the lexical witness should not be treated as the only wording in every edition of the collected poems.

The image concerns a great, fortified building constructed with abundant resources and materials.

Despite its grandeur and strength, it could not protect its owner from death.

لَمْ يُعْجِزْ here concerns failing to place the owner beyond death's reach; it does not describe physical weakness in the building.

يَتَأَبَّقُ belongs to أ ب ق and concerns fleeing or absconding. The negative phrase denies escape; the verb itself does not mean “was unable to flee.”

The witness illustrates placing someone beyond reach or frustrating another's attainment of an objective in the semantic field of أَعْجَزَ.

That is connected with inability to attain an aim.

Related Quranic constructions include لَا يُعْجِزُونَ and بِمُعْجِزِينَ.

Second witness: the lower portions of palm trees

A poem attributed to ʿAntarah ibn Shaddād says:

وَطَرَحْتُهُمْ فَوْقَ الصَّعِيدِ كَأَنَّهُمْ

wa-ṭaraḥtuhum fawqa al-ṣaʿīdi ka-annahum

أَعْجَازُ نَخْلٍ فِي حَضِيضِ الْمَحْجَرِ

aʿjāzu nakhlin fī ḥaḍīḍi al-maḥjari

Translation: I cast them on the ground as though they were palm trunks in the low ground of a rocky enclosure.

أَعْجَازُ نَخْلٍ concerns palm trunks or their lower portions.

The poet uses fallen trunks to depict warriors lying on the battlefield.

The verse is transmitted in collections attributed to ʿAntarah. This article has not independently established the date or strength of attribution of this particular passage. It illustrates the expression, but the poet's name alone does not prove the verse's precise antiquity.

The Quran independently uses the same expression twice, establishing it in the language of revelation.

Here عجز names a part of the tree, not its inability.

A defensible historical account

Lexical and Quranic evidence establish two clear fields in early Arabic:

Insufficient ability or failure to attain an aim, and the rear, lower, or final part of something.

The first includes forms such as عاجز, إعجاز, and معجز.

The second includes عجز and أعجاز. Explanations of عجوز connect it with the later stage of life or the weakness that may accompany age; these are lexical explanations, not a demonstrated historical sequence.

Each derivative nevertheless requires its own analysis. عجوز cannot simply be rendered “a helpless woman,” nor أعجاز النخل “helpless trees.”

2. The lexical family and extensions of meaning

Forms include عَجَزَ, يَعْجِزُ, عَجْز, عَاجِز, عَجُز, أَعْجَاز, عَجُوز, عَجَائِز, أَعْجَزَ, إِعْجَاز, مُعْجِز, عَاجَزَ, مُعَاجِز, عَجَّزَ, تَعْجِيز, and مُعْجِزَة.

عَجَزَ: being unable to do something

عَجَزَ principally concerns inability to act or attain something.

عَجَزَ عَنِ الْعَمَلِ means someone was unable to do the work.

عَجَزَ عَنْ إِدْرَاكِهِ means someone could not reach or catch another.

The inability may result from insufficient strength, knowledge, resources, time, or suitable conditions.

Someone physically healthy may be unable to build a complex device because the necessary knowledge or tools are unavailable.

Another may know how to do the same work but lack the physical capacity to carry it out.

Both involve inability, but their causes differ.

عجز concerns ability falling short in relation to a particular task or aim, not necessarily permanent weakness in every aspect of a person.

عَجْز: inability or falling short

عَجْز is a familiar verbal noun of عَجَزَ and contrasts with ability.

Some Arabic uses also contrast it with حَزْم.

حزم concerns sound resolution, foresight, and effective management.

In that contrast, عجز may describe failure to achieve a result because of indecision, lack of planning, or inadequate action.

This does not make poor judgment or laziness appropriate translations everywhere.

Someone can make every effort while a task remains beyond their resources.

That inability differs from the negligence of a person who could act but refrains.

عَاجِز: someone unable

عَاجِز is the active participle of عَجَزَ.

It describes someone unable to perform an act or attain an aim.

Often the inability is assessed relative to a specific task.

A person may be unable to swim yet run exceptionally well.

عاجز therefore need not mean incapable in every field.

This matters ethically and socially: inability in one task should not become a judgment demeaning the whole person.

The form عاجز does not occur in the Quran, although the basic verb and derivatives of Forms IV and III do.

أَعْجَزَ: rendering unable or escaping reach

أَعْجَزَ is a Form IV verb.

It has several related uses in Arabic.

One is rendering someone unable to do something.

Another is outstripping or escaping someone so that they cannot catch up.

A person fleeing so quickly that a pursuer cannot reach them has rendered the pursuer unable to attain that particular objective.

The pursuer's bodily strength need not have disappeared; the failure concerns reaching the fugitive.

This use matters for Quranic مُعْجِزِين.

عَاجَزَ and مُعَاجِز: attempting to outstrip or frustrate another

عَاجَزَ is Form III.

Recorded uses concern outstripping someone, escaping their reach, or attempting to defeat or frustrate them.

مُعَاجِز is its active participle.

In confrontation, a person tries to prevent an opponent attaining their goal or acts as though the opponent cannot prevail over them.

Morphologically it differs from Form IV مُعْجِز.

Both forms occur in the Quran, so the distinction matters.

Form III does not by itself establish equality of power. It may describe confrontation or attempted outstripping without proving that the parties are equally capable.

عَجَّزَ and تَعْجِيز: disabling or attributing inability

عَجَّزَ is Form II.

Arabic uses it for making someone unable or declaring them incapable.

Some uses also concern discouraging or holding another back.

Someone might deter action through intimidation, obstruction, or discouragement.

In such a situation, تعجيز can concern creating obstacles to another's attainment of an aim.

Not every criticism, disagreement, or question is تعجيز. This use concerns an act of obstructing or rendering unable.

عَجُز: the rear or final part

In its physical branch, عَجُز names the rear of a body or object.

For people or animals it can concern the hindquarters or buttocks.

For palms it concerns the lower portion or trunk.

In speech and poetry it names an ending: Arabic prosody calls the second hemistich عَجُزُ الْبَيْتِ and the first صَدْرُ الْبَيْتِ.

The contrast between صدر and عجز clarifies beginning and ending.

أَعْجَازُ الْأُمُورِ similarly concerns the endings or later parts of affairs.

These uses indicate position at the rear or end, not necessarily weakness.

أَعْجَاز: rear parts and lower trunks

أَعْجَاز is the plural of عَجُز.

The Quran uses it twice in أَعْجَازُ نَخْلٍ.

The expression names palm trunks or their lower portions, particularly in images of fallen trees or trees whose upper portions are gone.

This relates to the rear or basal portion of an object.

“Inability” is therefore not an appropriate substitute in translation.

عَجُوز: an elderly person

عَجُوز denotes an elderly person, especially a woman.

عَجَائِز is its familiar plural.

Dictionaries also record it for an elderly man, though its application to women is more common.

Its connection with the family can be understood through advanced age or the weakness that may accompany it; neither explanation makes general incapacity part of every occurrence.

It does not follow that every elderly person is incapable of every task.

Old age is a stage of life; people vary in health, ability, experience, and bodily condition.

عجوز does not inherently mean infertile or childless. Those conditions, when relevant, are expressed by another word or the context.

All four Quranic occurrences concern elderly women.

3. Quranic occurrences

The root occurs 26 times in 25 verses.

In the Ḥafṣ reading used here, there are six morphological categories: one interrogative Form I أَعَجَزْتُ; four Form IV verbs; two أَعْجَاز; four عَجُوز; three مُعَاجِزِينَ; and twelve Form IV active participles مُعْجِز in different inflected forms.

The occurrences include inability, escaping reach, rear portions, and old age.

A. أَعَجَزْتُ: inability to perform an act

فَبَعَثَ اللَّهُ غُرَابًا يَبْحَثُ فِي الْأَرْضِ لِيُرِيَهُ كَيْفَ يُوَارِي سَوْءَةَ أَخِيهِ ۚ قَالَ يَا وَيْلَتَا أَعَجَزْتُ أَنْ أَكُونَ مِثْلَ هَٰذَا الْغُرَابِ فَأُوَارِيَ سَوْءَةَ أَخِي ۖ فَأَصْبَحَ مِنَ النَّادِمِينَ

fa-baʿatha allāhu ghurāban yabḥathu fī al-arḍi li-yuriyahu kayfa yuwārī sawʾata akhīhi qāla yā waylatā aʿajaztu an akūna mithla hādhā al-ghurābi fa-uwāriya sawʾata akhī fa-aṣbaḥa mina al-nādimīn

Al-Māʾidah 5:31

Then The God sent a raven scratching in the ground to show him how to cover his brother's body. He said, “Woe to me! Have I been unable to be like this raven and cover my brother's body?” Then he became one of the remorseful.

Al-Māʾidah 5:31

أَعَجَزْتُ comes from the basic Form I verb عَجَزَ.

Its initial hamzah is interrogative, not the hamzah of Form IV أَعْجَزَ.

The underlying عَجَزْتُ means “I was unable”; adding interrogative hamzah gives “Was I unable?”

What kind of inability is involved?

In the story, one of Adam's sons has killed his brother.

The God then sends a raven scratching in the ground to show him how to cover the body.

Observing the raven directs his attention to an act he had not performed.

أَعَجَزْتُ expresses self-reproach concerning this inability or failure.

The verb alone does not establish that he was physically incapable of digging.

Someone may possess bodily ability but not know or have considered how to act.

Here the inability concerns a particular unperformed act and failure to find how to carry it out, not general bodily weakness.

Inability and remorse

The verse ends with فَأَصْبَحَ مِنَ النَّادِمِينَ.

Remorse is expressed by a separate word from ن د م.

عَجَزْتُ does not mean “I regretted.”

The verse presents two states: recognizing inability or failure, and becoming one of the remorseful.

Someone can be unable to do something without regretting it; someone can also regret an act performed with ability and choice.

In this story, inability, new awareness, and remorse are related within an event, but remain lexically distinct.

Regarding the raven, the verse mentions scratching in the ground and showing how to cover the body. Unstated details about additional ravens or interactions between them are unnecessary for understanding ع ج ز.

B. أَعْجَزَ: escaping reach or frustrating attainment

Four Quranic occurrences are Form IV verbs.

They occur in Al-Anfāl 8:59, Fāṭir 35:44, and twice in Al-Jinn 72:12.

وَأَنَّا ظَنَنَّا أَنْ لَنْ نُعْجِزَ اللَّهَ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَنْ نُعْجِزَهُ هَرَبًا

wa-annā ẓanannā an lan nuʿjiza allāha fī al-arḍi wa-lan nuʿjizahu harabā

Al-Jinn 72:12

We realized that we could never frustrate The God on earth, nor escape His reach by fleeing.

Al-Jinn 72:12

نُعْجِزَ and نُعْجِزَهُ are both Form IV.

اللَّهَ is the object of the first.

The pronoun هُ in the second refers to The God.

هَرَبًا belongs to ه ر ب and concerns fleeing.

What does rendering The God unable mean here?

A mechanical rendering could suggest that people intend to abolish divine power.

In context, the issue is frustrating attainment by escaping.

Arabic can describe a fugitive who escapes a pursuer as having rendered that pursuer unable to reach them.

The verse denies that possibility in relation to The God.

No one can escape divine power and dominion by being on earth or by fleeing.

هَرَبًا supports this reading.

نُعْجِزَهُ here concerns supposed escape from reach, not destruction of divine power.

Human inability and divine power

Fāṭir 35:44 states that nothing in the heavens or earth can frustrate The God.

Al-Anfāl 8:59 rejects the disbelievers' supposition that they have escaped by getting ahead.

سَبَقُوا belongs to س ب ق, getting ahead; لَا يُعْجِزُونَ belongs to ع ج ز.

Their juxtaposition clarifies the distinction.

Getting ahead of someone does not necessarily make that person permanently unable to catch up.

Thus getting ahead or fleeing does not establish escape from divine power.

These verses concern unrestricted divine power. They should not be reduced to a bodily image of The God standing somewhere and traversing distances to pursue people.

C. مُعْجِز: one who frustrates attainment or escapes reach

مُعْجِز is the Form IV active participle.

It occurs twelve times: Al-Anʿām 6:134, Al-Tawbah 9:2, Al-Tawbah 9:3, Yūnus 10:53, Hūd 11:20, Hūd 11:33, Al-Naḥl 16:46, Al-Nūr 24:57, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:22, Al-Zumar 39:51, Al-Shūrā 42:31, and Al-Aḥqāf 46:32.

Most occurrences are مُعْجِزِينَ or بِمُعْجِزِينَ. The construct phrase in the two Al-Tawbah verses gives مُعْجِزِي اللَّهِ; Al-Aḥqāf 46:32 has singular بِمُعْجِزٍ.

Differences in inflection and number do not create new independent derivatives.

No one places themselves beyond divine reach

وَمَا أَنْتُمْ بِمُعْجِزِينَ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي السَّمَاءِ ۖ وَمَا لَكُمْ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ مِنْ وَلِيٍّ وَلَا نَصِيرٍ

wa-mā antum bi-muʿjizīna fī al-arḍi wa-lā fī al-samāʾi wa-mā lakum min dūni allāhi min waliyyin wa-lā naṣīr

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:22

You cannot escape His power on earth or in heaven; apart from The God, you have no protector or helper.

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:22

مُعْجِزِينَ concerns frustrating attainment or escaping reach; the construction وَمَا أَنْتُمْ بِمُعْجِزِينَ denies that possibility. The negation belongs to the sentence, not the participle alone.

فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي السَّمَاءِ states the scope of the denial.

The point is not merely inadequate earthly shelter: no position in heaven or earth places a person beyond divine power and dominion.

The following clause denies any protector or helper apart from The God.

Together, the clauses concern the impossibility of escaping divine power and accountability.

Does مُعْجِز mean victorious?

It is not simply a label for a battlefield victor in these passages.

Winning an earthly confrontation does not put someone beyond divine reach.

Temporarily escaping a government's penalty or another person's pursuit likewise does not frustrate The God.

Here the participle concerns frustrating attainment or escaping reach, a possibility the sentences deny; it does not affirm human superiority over The God.

In Hūd 11:33, Noah says that bringing the punishment his people demand depends on The God's will, and that they cannot escape His reach.

Al-Naḥl 16:46 concerns people being seized amid their movements and activity.

Al-Shūrā 42:31 pairs the denial of escape with the absence of a protector or helper apart from Him.

These contexts preserve a common point: no one can use power or flight to make divine attainment impossible.

D. مُعَاجِزِينَ: attempting to oppose and frustrate

مُعَاجِزِينَ is a plural active participle of Form III.

It occurs in Al-Ḥajj 22:51, Sabaʾ 34:5, and Sabaʾ 34:38.

وَالَّذِينَ سَعَوْا فِي آيَاتِنَا مُعَاجِزِينَ أُولَٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ الْجَحِيمِ

wa-alladhīna saʿaw fī āyātinā muʿājizīna ulāʾika aṣḥābu al-jaḥīm

Al-Ḥajj 22:51

Those who strove against Our signs, seeking to frustrate, are the companions of the blazing fire.

Al-Ḥajj 22:51

مُعَاجِزِينَ accompanies سَعَوْا.

سَعَوْا belongs to س ع ي and expresses effort or striving.

مُعَاجِزِينَ specifies the manner and direction of that effort.

The context concerns striving to oppose and frustrate the divine message, not merely asking questions about the signs or researching their meaning.

Attempted frustration and simple inability

With عَجَزَ, a person is unable to do something.

With أَعْجَزَ, the subject can render another unable or escape their reach.

عَاجَزَ concerns confrontation and an attempt to frustrate or outstrip an opponent.

مُعَاجِزِينَ therefore differs from عَاجِزِينَ.

عاجزين may mean people unable to act; معاجزين describes people engaged in attempted outstripping or frustration of another.

Sabaʾ 34:5 and Sabaʾ 34:38 also pair the word with effort against divine signs.

Interpretations include opposing or obstructing the message, and acting in the belief that one can outstrip divine accountability. These are related explanations of attempted frustration, not a single method specified by the word.

The word does not enumerate the means: speech, propaganda, obstruction, or other conduct may be involved.

The readings مُعَاجِزِينَ and مُعَجِّزِينَ

Recognized readings of these three verses include two forms.

The Ḥafṣ reading used here has مُعَاجِزِينَ, with alif after ʿayn and an undoubled jīm: the Form III active participle.

Another reading has مُعَجِّزِينَ, without that alif and with doubled jīm: the Form II active participle. Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr read this form in all three passages.

The first emphasizes attempted outstripping, opposition, or frustration.

The second can emphasize rendering unable, discouraging, or deterring others from accepting and following the signs.

This morphological difference should not be silently erased.

Both nevertheless occur in the context of effort against the signs.

The verses thus concern hostile effort associated with frustrating the divine message or deterring people from it.

Does every disagreement count as this opposition?

These verses do not make every question, doubt, or different interpretation of the Quran مُعاجزة.

Someone may sincerely seek to understand a verse while disagreeing with a familiar interpretation.

Even an incorrect interpretation may arise from an intention to find the truth.

Another person may recognize a truth yet deliberately work to conceal, distort, or neutralize it.

These situations differ.

The passages concern effort to oppose or frustrate; this should not be extended to every scholarly disagreement or sincere question.

4. أَعْجَاز: rear portions and tree trunks

This branch appears in two verses about the fate of ʿĀd.

تَنْزِعُ النَّاسَ كَأَنَّهُمْ أَعْجَازُ نَخْلٍ مُنْقَعِرٍ

tanziʿu al-nāsa ka-annahum aʿjāzu nakhlin munqaʿir

Al-Qamar 54:20

It tore people away, as though they were trunks of uprooted palm trees.

Al-Qamar 54:20

أَعْجَازُ is the plural of عَجُز.

نَخْلٍ names palm trees.

مُنْقَعِرٍ belongs to ق ع ر and concerns uprooting or removal from the base.

The phrase depicts trunks or lower portions of palms torn from their bases.

It describes people brought down by an overwhelming wind.

The comparison

كَأَنَّهُمْ introduces a comparison between the fallen people and palm trunks.

The verse does not say they literally turned into wood or trees.

The fallen bodies and uprooted trunks offer a visible resemblance. Their size may contribute to the image, but the comparison does not determine a precise bodily height.

أعجاز has a physical meaning here and should not be rendered “inability.”

The people's helplessness and death come from the event and context; أعجاز النخل itself names trunks or lower portions.

The second use: hollow or fallen trunks

سَخَّرَهَا عَلَيْهِمْ سَبْعَ لَيَالٍ وَثَمَانِيَةَ أَيَّامٍ حُسُومًا فَتَرَى الْقَوْمَ فِيهَا صَرْعَىٰ كَأَنَّهُمْ أَعْجَازُ نَخْلٍ خَاوِيَةٍ

sakhkharahā ʿalayhim sabʿa layālin wa-thamāniyata ayyāmin ḥusūman fa-tarā al-qawma fīhā ṣarʿā ka-annahum aʿjāzu nakhlin khāwiya

Al-Ḥāqqah 69:7

He subjected them to it for seven nights and eight successive days; you would see the people lying fallen there, as though they were hollow palm trunks.

Al-Ḥāqqah 69:7

This verse also uses أَعْجَازُ نَخْلٍ.

Its modifier is خَاوِيَةٍ.

خاوية belongs to خ و ي and concerns emptiness, being hollow, and in some uses falling or collapse.

The palm trunks thus receive a different description from those in Al-Qamar.

In Al-Qamar 54:20, مُنْقَعِرٍ emphasizes uprooting.

In Al-Ḥāqqah 69:7, خَاوِيَةٍ has been explained as hollow or fallen. These explanations should be distinguished, rather than making every use necessarily express both together.

Translation should preserve the difference between the two verses.

أعجاز itself names palm trunks or their lower portions in both.

Does أَعْجَاز mean powerless trees?

No. In these verses it names a particular portion of a tree.

As صدر can mean the front or beginning, عجز can mean the rear or ending.

“Helpless trees” is therefore unsuitable for أعجاز نخل.

أعجاز alone also does not mean dried, hollow, or uprooted trees.

Further features come from منقعر, خاوية, or the context.

5. عَجُوز: an elderly person

عَجُوز occurs four times in the Quran.

Two concern Abraham's wife: Hūd 11:72 and Al-Dhāriyāt 51:29.

Two occur in Lot's story: Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:171 and Al-Ṣāffāt 37:135.

In Abraham's story

قَالَتْ يَا وَيْلَتَىٰ أَأَلِدُ وَأَنَا عَجُوزٌ وَهَٰذَا بَعْلِي شَيْخًا ۖ إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَشَيْءٌ عَجِيبٌ

qālat yā waylatā a-alidu wa-anā ʿajūzun wa-hādhā baʿlī shaykhan inna hādhā la-shayʾun ʿajīb

Hūd 11:72

She said, “Woe to me! Shall I give birth when I am an old woman and this husband of mine is old? This is indeed an astonishing thing.”

Hūd 11:72

عَجُوزٌ describes Abraham's wife.

شَيْخًا describes her husband.

Both concern old age but belong to different roots.

عجوز belongs to ع ج ز; شيخ to ش ي خ.

Their old age relates here to surprise at the announcement of a child.

عجوز itself does not mean infertile.

Another verse makes the distinction clearer.

عَجُوز and عَقِيم: distinct descriptions

فَأَقْبَلَتِ امْرَأَتُهُ فِي صَرَّةٍ فَصَكَّتْ وَجْهَهَا وَقَالَتْ عَجُوزٌ عَقِيمٌ

fa-aqbalati imraʾatuhu fī ṣarratin fa-ṣakkat wajhahā wa-qālat ʿajūzun ʿaqīm

Al-Dhāriyāt 51:29

His wife came forward with a cry, struck her face, and said, “An old, barren woman!”

Al-Dhāriyāt 51:29

عَجُوزٌ and عَقِيمٌ stand together.

عجوز indicates old age.

عقيم belongs to ع ق م and concerns infertility or inability to reproduce.

Their meanings differ.

If عجوز always meant infertile, the distinct contribution of each description would be lost.

The sentence can state both conditions: she is elderly and also infertile.

“An infertile woman” alone therefore fails to translate عجوز accurately.

Nor should the inability branch be imported so that every elderly woman is described as incapable of every task.

In Lot's story

إِلَّا عَجُوزًا فِي الْغَابِرِينَ

illā ʿajūzan fī al-ghābirīn

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:171; Al-Ṣāffāt 37:135

Except an old woman among those who remained behind.

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:171

The same phrase occurs in Al-Ṣāffāt 37:135.

Both passages describe Lot and his household being saved, with an elderly woman excepted.

Other verses, including Al-Naml 27:57, identify her as Lot's wife.

عجوز itself means neither “a Prophet's wife” nor “a sinful person.”

It specifies the woman's old age.

Her place in the story comes from the exception to rescue and other passages concerning Lot's wife.

Is عَجُوز inherently derogatory?

Its lexical meaning names an elderly person.

It does not inherently mean worthless, ignorant, incapable of thought, or morally objectionable.

In Abraham's story it helps explain the woman's surprise at news of a child.

In Lot's story it identifies a woman among those remaining behind.

Moral worth cannot be derived from عجوز itself.

In both stories, context and conduct matter; old age is not in itself a virtue or fault.

6. إِعْجَاز and مُعْجِزَة: lexical and technical uses

إعجاز and the term for a miracle are established terms in later and contemporary Arabic and Persian religious discussion.

Morphologically, إِعْجَاز is the verbal noun of Form IV أَعْجَزَ: rendering unable.

In religious terminology, مُعْجِزَة can name a sign or event others cannot match; that is a brief account of the term, not a complete definition of every theological school's conditions for it.

Lexical meaning and later technical meaning should remain distinct.

Neither the noun مُعْجِزَة nor the verbal noun إِعْجَاز occurs in the Quran, whether as a technical term or otherwise.

What occurs includes verbs and participles such as نُعْجِزَ, مُعْجِزِينَ, and مُعَاجِزِينَ.

They concern inability, failure to escape divine reach, and attempts to oppose the signs.

For divine signs and clear proofs, the Quran uses words such as آيَة, بَيِّنَة, and بُرْهَان.

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:32, for example, mentions two proofs associated with Moses.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:88 speaks of humans and jinn being unable to produce the like of the Quran.

The idea relates to inability, but the word مُعْجِزَة does not occur there.

The later term may explain some Quranic topics, but should not be presented as the Quran's own word for all divine signs.

Nor does Quranic مُعْجِز mean “a performer of miracles.”

Many passages deny that the people described can escape divine reach.

A familiar modern technical meaning must not replace a derivative's actual use in its verse.

7. Comparison with nearby roots

ع ج ز and ض ع ف: inability and weakness

ض ع ف concerns weakness or diminished strength or ability.

Many uses of عجز also concern inability.

Weakness can describe a state of reduced capacity; عجز often relates to a task someone cannot perform or an aim they cannot attain.

A physically weak person may perform a suitably modest task well.

A strong person may be unable to perform a task requiring special knowledge or resources.

Weakness and inability can coexist without being complete synonyms.

Al-Māʾidah 5:31 concerns a particular unperformed act, not weakness in every physical ability.

ع ج ز and و ه ن: inability and diminished strength

وَهْن concerns feebleness or weakening.

In Maryam 19:4, Zechariah speaks of his bones weakening.

That describes a bodily condition.

عجز can arise from such weakness or from another cause.

Bodily frailty may prevent someone walking; in that case وهن and عجز are related.

Someone can also be unable to solve a problem without bodily weakness.

عجز concerns capacity falling short of a task; وهن can emphasize the weakened state itself.

ع ج ز and ق د ر: inability and ability

One branch of ق د ر concerns power or ability to act.

عجز contrasts with ability in this field.

Fāṭir 35:44 denies that anything in heaven or earth can frustrate The God and ends with قَدِيرًا.

The contrast is clear: nothing can render Him unable to act as He wills, for He is powerful.

Power and inability should not be reduced to bodily force.

Human ability can depend on knowledge, skill, resources, and circumstances.

Divine uses concern unrestricted power, not measurement against human bodily capacities.

ع ج ز and س ب ق: escaping reach and getting ahead

سَبَقَ means getting ahead or preceding.

In pursuit contexts, أَعْجَزَ can mean escaping another's reach.

Al-Anfāl 8:59 places these concepts together.

One contestant can get ahead while remaining within the other's ability to catch up.

When a fugitive gets beyond the pursuer's ability to reach them, the sense of أعجز becomes more prominent.

Getting ahead and frustrating pursuit are related but different situations.

In the Quranic context, getting ahead does not place people beyond divine reach.

ع ج ز and ف و ت: inability to attain and missing an opportunity

ف و ت concerns missing something, a passing opportunity, or something escaping reach.

فَاتَهُ الشَّيْءُ means something passed beyond a person's reach or opportunity.

أَعْجَزَهُ can also concern frustrating someone's attainment.

فوت emphasizes the missed opportunity or object; إعجاز emphasizes the other party's inability to attain it.

Someone might miss a journey because the departure time has passed.

In another situation, a traveler might flee a pursuer and prevent that pursuer from catching up.

The outcomes may resemble each other without the verbs meaning the same thing.

ع ج ز and د ب ر: the rear part

دُبُر concerns the back or rear of something.

عَجُز also has a rear or final-part sense.

The words overlap in some uses.

عجز additionally names lower palm trunks, bodily hindquarters, and the final hemistich of a verse.

دبر has further derivatives and uses, such as turning one's back and directing affairs.

Overlap in the rear-part sense does not make all derivatives interchangeable.

ع ج ز and ش ي خ: old age

شَيْخ denotes an elderly person and ordinarily describes a man.

عَجُوز likewise concerns an elderly person, especially a woman.

Both occur in Hūd 11:72.

The woman calls herself عجوز and her husband شيخ.

They overlap in old age while retaining distinct roots and usage.

Neither alone determines infertility, complete incapacity, or moral value.

ع ج ز and أ ي ي: rendering unable and a sign

إعجاز belongs to ع ج ز and concerns rendering unable.

آية means a sign or indication. This site's classification places it under أ ي ي; classical discussions differ over its derivational analysis, which does not alter its distinction from إعجاز.

Later technical language may call a divine sign a miracle.

Lexically, the two words remain distinct.

A sign can guide someone to a truth without آية itself meaning rendering unable.

إعجاز concerns the relation between ability and inability.

Root meanings should therefore be distinguished from later technical usage.

8. Morphological and semantic observations

Interrogative hamzah and Form IV hamzah

In Al-Māʾidah 5:31, أَعَجَزْتُ consists of Form I عَجَزْتُ preceded by interrogative hamzah.

أَعْجَزَ belongs to Form IV. Related نُعْجِزَ is its imperfect form: the initial hamzah is not retained in that form, but its morphological pattern identifies it as Form IV.

Similar spelling must not obscure this distinction.

أَعَجَزْتُ means “Was I unable?”

أَعْجَزَ can mean rendering unable or escaping reach.

Their subjects and objects have different roles.

مُعْجِز and مُعَاجِز

مُعْجِز is the Form IV active participle.

مُعَاجِز is the Form III active participle.

Quranic مُعْجِز generally occurs in constructions denying people's ability to escape divine power.

مُعَاجِزِينَ appears in the context of effort against divine signs.

Both belong to frustration and opposition, but differ in morphology and context.

عَجْز and عَجُز

عَجْز principally concerns inability.

عَجُز belongs to the rear or final-part branch.

Without vowel marks they may look identical.

Vowels and contextual meaning matter in lexical analysis.

أعجاز النخل therefore means palm trunks, not the trees' inability.

Inability and moral responsibility

عجز does not inherently carry a negative moral judgment.

Illness, inadequate resources, or lack of necessary knowledge may make someone unable to act.

This differs from the negligence of someone able to act who avoids responsibility.

Al-Baqarah 2:286 says that no soul is burdened beyond its capacity.

That principle matters in distinguishing genuine inability from voluntary neglect.

Not every inability should therefore be treated as sin or moral deficiency.

Conversely, a person may possess ability but not use it. عجز alone cannot fully explain such a situation.

Assessing responsibility requires attention to actual ability, knowledge, choice, and circumstances.

Old age and inability

عجوز shows that old age belongs to the family ع ج ز.

That does not place all elderly people in the same position regarding capacity or responsibility.

Abraham's wife describes her old age; in Al-Dhāriyāt 51:29, infertility is expressed separately by عقيم.

In Lot's story, عجوز identifies the woman's age; conduct and fate are known from other parts of the narrative.

Old age, infertility, bodily inability, and moral responsibility are four distinct matters.

9. Proposed renderings for Forqan

Equivalent wording should follow the semantic branch, morphology, and complement.

عَجَزَ: was unable; failed to perform an act or attain an aim.

عَجْز: inability; capacity falling short of a particular task.

عَاجِز: unable; someone lacking the capacity to act or attain an aim.

أَعْجَزَ: rendered unable; in pursuit, escaped reach or frustrated attainment.

مُعْجِز: one who renders unable or escapes reach. Divine-power passages deny that possibility through their sentence constructions; neither negation nor a claim about the person's belief is inherent in the participle alone.

عَاجَزَ: attempted to outstrip or frustrate an opponent.

مُعَاجِز: someone attempting to frustrate or outstrip another.

عَجَّزَ: rendered unable; in other uses, discouraged action or attributed inability.

عَجُز: the rear or final part.

أَعْجَاز: rear portions; in the Quran, palm trunks or their lower portions.

عَجُوز: an elderly woman; more broadly in Arabic, an elderly person.

إِعْجَاز: rendering unable; in later technical use, inability of others to produce a match for a sign or discourse.

مُعْجِزَة: a later religious term for a sign or event others cannot match; it does not occur in the Quran. This brief gloss does not cover all conditions in technical theological definitions.

Proposed Quranic renderings

Al-Māʾidah 5:31 — أَعَجَزْتُ

“Was I unable?”

Context: inability or failure to do what the raven has shown how to do.

Al-Anfāl 8:59 — يُعْجِزُونَ

Within لَا يُعْجِزُونَ: “They cannot escape reach.” Negation comes from لَا, not يُعْجِزُونَ alone.

Context: getting ahead does not allow them to escape divine power.

Al-Jinn 72:12 — نُعْجِزَ and نُعْجِزَهُ

“Frustrate” and “escape His reach,” within the verse's negative constructions.

Within وَمَا أَنْتُمْ بِمُعْجِزِينَ: “You cannot escape His power.” The negative meaning belongs to the full construction.

Context: people's inability to escape divine dominion on earth or in heaven.

“Seeking to frustrate and oppose.”

“An elderly woman.”

أَعَجَزْتُ: “Was I unable?” concerning a particular act.

Form IV verbs and participles: frustrating attainment or escaping reach; the divine-power passages deny that possibility.

10. Conclusion

ع ج ز has two principal Arabic branches: inability or failure to attain an aim, and the rear or final portion of something.

In the first, عَجَزَ describes inability to act or reach an objective. Its causes may include insufficient physical strength, knowledge, skill, resources, or suitable conditions.

أَعْجَزَ in pursuit contexts concerns frustrating an opponent or escaping their reach.

The verse attributed to al-Aʿshā illustrates this through death and the failure to escape it.

In the other branch, عَجُز names the rear of a body or object. The family also includes أَعْجَاز for palm trunks and عَجُوز for an elderly person; the latter's exact historical derivation should not be inferred solely from a semantic analogy.

The Quran uses the root 26 times.

In Al-Māʾidah 5:31, أَعَجَزْتُ expresses inability or failure in a particular act.

نُعْجِزَ and مُعْجِزِينَ occur in passages denying escape from divine power or the possibility of frustrating divine attainment.

The three مُعَاجِزِينَ occurrences concern effort against the signs and attempted frustration. This should not be extended to every question or interpretive disagreement about the Quran.

In Al-Qamar 54:20 and Al-Ḥāqqah 69:7, أَعْجَازُ نَخْلٍ names palm trunks or lower portions, not their inability.

All four عَجُوز occurrences concern elderly women. The word alone does not imply infertility, total incapacity, or positive or negative moral worth.

The derivatives should not all be reduced to weakness; nor should associations with the rear or end erase the independent inability branch.

The later term for a miracle must not replace actual Quranic uses. The Quran speaks of signs and proofs; its ع ج ز derivatives concern ability, frustration, escape, opposition to signs, palm trunks, and old age.

Final definition: ع ج ز has two principal branches. The first concerns inability to act or attain an aim, extending in causative and confrontational forms to frustrating another or escaping their reach. The second names the rear or final portion of a body or object; related forms include أَعْجَاز for palm trunks and عَجُوز for old age. Quranic meaning follows morphology, the verb's complement, and context; no branch should be imposed on another without evidence.
