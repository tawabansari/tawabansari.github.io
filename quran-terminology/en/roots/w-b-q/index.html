Wabaq: perishing, causing destruction, and becoming trapped in a situation from which escape is difficult or blocked

1. Meaning in early Arabic

The Arabic root و ب ق concerns perishing and becoming caught in a situation that can lead to destruction. وَبَقَ and وَبِقَ are recorded for perishing, suffering ruin, or becoming inextricably trapped.

أَوْبَقَ means causing someone or something to perish or be destroyed.

Someone may become trapped in a swamp and be unable to escape. If this results in death, the connection between entrapment and destruction is clear.

Someone may also become so entangled through misconduct that they lose their livelihood, property, or standing. Destruction here need not mean bodily death; it can mean a ruined situation or the loss of something valuable.

Arabic sources record وَبِقَتِ الْإِبِلُ فِي الطِّينِ for camels stuck in mud, and وَبِقَ فِي دَيْنِهِ for a person caught in debt without finding a way out.

The family therefore concerns more than the moment of death. Some forms also involve entrapment and inability to escape.

These examples do not establish that “getting stuck in mud” was historically the original meaning and later developed into destruction. That sequence requires independent historical evidence.

A poetic witness attributed to Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā

A poem attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā contains these lines:

وَمَنْ يَلْتَمِسْ حُسْنَ الثَّنَاءِ بِمَالِهِ

wa-man yaltamis ḥusna al-thanāʾi bi-mālihi

يَصُنْ عِرْضَهُ مِنْ كُلِّ شَنْعَاءَ مُوبِقِ

yaṣun ʿirḍahu min kulli shanʿāʾa mūbiqi

“Whoever seeks good praise through his wealth protects his honor from every ruinous disgrace.”

The poet connects generosity, a good reputation, and the preservation of honor.

مُوبِق here means something capable of causing ruin. The subject is not bodily death but disgrace and injury destroying a person's reputation and standing.

The family thus appears in early Arabic poetry for severe social harm as well as bodily destruction or human death.

The verse is recorded in poetry attributed to Zuhayr. Some transmissions have يَشْتَرِي instead of يَلْتَمِسْ and عَنْ instead of مِنْ. These variants do not alter the basic meaning of مُوبِق.

Zuhayr is a recognized pre-Islamic poet, but this verse's exact date is not independently known. Its attribution to that poetic tradition has support; the witness alone cannot date the root's earliest meaning.

Another early poetic witness

Poetry attributed to Khufāf ibn Nudbah al-Sulamī includes:

وَجَادَ شَرَوْرَى وَالسِّتَارَ فَلَمْ يَدَعْ

wa-jāda sharawrā wa-al-sitāra fa-lam yadaʿ

تِعَارًا لَهُ وَالْوَادِيَيْنِ بِمَوْبِقِ

tiʿāran lahu wa-al-wādiyayni bi-mawbiqi

“Rain poured on Sharawrā and al-Sitār, leaving neither Tiʿār nor the two valleys without their share.”

The verse names several places and elevations while describing widespread rainfall.

بِمَوْبِقِ is disputed here. Some lexicographers explain it through a meaning close to “appointment”: the rain did not leave those places waiting for their share. The rendering above follows this interpretation rather than presenting the phrase as unambiguous.

That sense is not established across all witnesses, and its connection with destruction is unclear.

Khufāf lived through both the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods. This passage's composition date is also unknown.

It illustrates the word's range and lexical disagreements, but cannot alone establish “appointment” as a general pre-Islamic meaning of مَوْبِق.

Historical conclusion: Perishing and causing destruction are established early Arabic meanings. Entrapment, ruin, and a place of destruction are also recorded, but no single earliest historical image should be imposed on all branches without sufficient evidence.

2. Forms, derivatives, and semantic core

Important recorded forms include:

  • وَبَقَ: perished or was ruined.
  • وَبِقَ: perished; in some uses, remained trapped.
  • يَبِقُ: perishes; one recorded imperfect form.
  • وَبْق: destruction or ruin.
  • وُبُوق: perishing.
  • وَبَق: in some constructions, a verbal noun for perishing.
  • وَابِق: perishing or caught in destruction.
  • أَوْبَقَ: caused destruction or made something perish.
  • إِيبَاق: causing destruction.
  • مُوبِق: destructive or ruinous.
  • مُوبَق: someone or something destroyed or trapped.
  • مَوْبِق: a place of destruction; in some uses, a barrier.
  • اِسْتَوْبَقَ: perished or exposed oneself to destruction.

These are Arabic forms; only مَوْبِقًا and يُوبِقْهُنَّ occur in the Quran.

The semantic core

In the destruction branch, و ب ق concerns a situation in which life, ability, property, or a previous condition undergoes severe ruin.

Someone may lose their life in an accident: this is bodily destruction.

Someone may instead ruin their property and standing through poor decisions. Ruin can then be described without their necessarily dying.

Wabaq should therefore not be restricted to physical death in every use.

Conversely, not every slight loss or ordinary difficulty is wabaq. Its main field concerns destruction, ruin, and severe entrapment.

Entrapment and destruction

Being stuck in mud and caught in debt share a feature: the person or animal is in a situation difficult to leave.

A camel stuck in mud may be unable to move; without rescue, that entrapment can cause death.

Someone trapped in debt may have no means to repay it and suffer damage to their economic life.

These examples make the connection between entrapment and ruin tangible.

Yet entrapment does not always mean destruction. The camel may be pulled out or the debtor helped to recover.

Each derivative must therefore be examined to determine whether it concerns entrapment itself or destruction as its outcome.

مَوْبِق and مُوبِق

Their vowels differ, and the two forms must not be confused.

مُوبِق is the active participle of أَوْبَقَ, naming someone or something that causes destruction.

Quranic مَوْبِق is a noun that can refer to a place of destruction or a destructive condition.

Its pattern, مَفْعِل, is compatible with a noun of place.

The pattern alone does not require a particular physical location. Lexical sources also record a verbal-noun interpretation and senses involving a barrier.

This matters in Al-Kahf 18:52, where مَوْبِقًا occurs in a sentence about something placed between two parties.

3. Quranic use: Al-Kahf 18:52

Al-Kahf 18:52

وَيَوْمَ يَقُولُ نَادُوا شُرَكَائِيَ الَّذِينَ زَعَمْتُمْ فَدَعَوْهُمْ فَلَمْ يَسْتَجِيبُوا لَهُمْ وَجَعَلْنَا بَيْنَهُمْ مَوْبِقًا

wa-yawma yaqūlu nādū shurakāʾiya alladhīna zaʿamtum fa-daʿawhum fa-lam yastajībū lahum wa-jaʿalnā baynahum mawbiqā

Al-Kahf 18:52

“And on the day He says: Call those you claimed as My partners. They will call them, but they will not respond to them; and We will place between them a place of destruction.”

مَوْبِقًا occurs in a scene of judgment.

People who imagined partners for The God are told to call those alleged partners.

They do so but receive no response.

The Quran then states that a مَوْبِق is placed between them.

The meaning of مَوْبِقًا

Arabic lexical sources record several meanings.

First, a place of destruction or perishing.

Second, a barrier standing between two parties.

Third, in some lexical accounts, an appointment associated here with destruction.

The place-of-destruction meaning connects clearly with the root's main use.

A barrier also fits وَجَعَلْنَا بَيْنَهُمْ, since something is placed between two parties.

Neither aspect necessarily excludes the other.

It can be understood as a place of destruction standing between and separating the parties.

The lexical meaning must still be distinguished from the sentence's image. Mawbiq alone does not specify every detail of distance, the barrier's shape, or the manner of separation.

بَيْنَهُمْ: between whom?

The pronoun هُمْ matters here.

The verse begins with people who imagined partners for The God.

They call those alleged partners but receive no response.

The most immediate reading therefore concerns the relationship between those making the claim and the people or things they regarded as divine partners.

On this reading, the verse describes the collapse of a bond they had trusted and hoped in during worldly life.

Someone may imagine that a person, power, or deity can protect them against The God. Turning to that support without receiving a response reveals their trust to have been groundless.

The verse pictures this separation through the مَوْبِق placed between them.

Other readings identify the parties differently, including misguided groups separated from one another or from believers.

The pronouns' continuity, however, more directly connects the callers with their alleged partners.

Is mawbiq the name of a particular valley in hell?

Some exegetical accounts identify it as a particular valley or place in hell.

Descriptions sometimes add great depth, a fiery river, and other details.

The verse itself does not state these details.

It says a مَوْبِق is placed between them; it does not describe a valley's name, dimensions, substance, or physical structure.

The lexical sense “place of destruction” should not be turned into a particular named location with specified features without Quranic evidence.

“Place of destruction” is a suitable direct rendering; reflection can explain its separating function.

Connections with other verses

Yūnus 10:28 describes separation between those associating partners with The God and their alleged partners.

In Maryam 19:82, the alleged deities oppose their worshippers; a bond expected to bring honor and support turns against them.

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:25 describes worldly ties based on fabricated objects of worship turning into mutual denial and cursing at the resurrection.

In Al-Baqarah 2:166, those who were followed disown their followers, and their ties are severed.

These passages help explain the context of Al-Kahf 18:52.

They do not make “disavowal,” “hostility,” or “severing ties” the lexical meaning of mawbiq.

Those ideas arise from the passages together and the relationship's outcome. Here mawbiq names a place of destruction or barrier between the parties.

Connection with Al-Kahf 18:53

The next verse describes offenders seeing the Fire and realizing they will fall into it.

They find no escape.

This continuation reinforces the preceding place-of-destruction sense.

In Al-Kahf 18:52, the alleged partners do not respond, and a place of destruction stands between the parties.

In Al-Kahf 18:53, the offenders face their deeds' outcome without finding a way away from it.

Together the verses depict false supports collapsing and people confronting their conduct's consequences.

Each still has its own subject: first, the alleged partners' failure and the place of destruction; then, seeing the Fire and finding no escape.

4. Quranic use: Al-Shūrā 42:34

Al-Shūrā 42:34

أَوْ يُوبِقْهُنَّ بِمَا كَسَبُوا وَيَعْفُ عَنْ كَثِيرٍ

aw yūbiqhunna bi-mā kasabū wa-yaʿfu ʿan kathīr

Al-Shūrā 42:34

“Or He may destroy them because of what they have earned, and pardon much.”

يُوبِقْهُنَّ is a Form IV imperfect verb from و ب ق.

أَوْبَقَ means causing destruction or making something perish.

يُوبِقْهُنَّ thus concerns destroying the things referred to by هُنَّ.

The referent of هُنَّ

The preceding verses identify the verb's object.

Al-Shūrā 42:32 mentions ships at sea resembling mountains.

Al-Shūrā 42:33 mentions the wind becoming still and ships remaining motionless on the sea's surface.

Al-Shūrā 42:34 then says أَوْ يُوبِقْهُنَّ.

The feminine plural pronoun refers to those ships.

Incorrectly assigning it directly to people changes the verb's object.

The verse concerns destroying ships. This can also endanger their occupants, but that consequence is distinct from the pronoun's direct referent.

يُوبِقْهُنَّ and رَوَاكِدَ

In Al-Shūrā 42:33, ships can remain motionless if the wind falls still.

Al-Shūrā 42:34 instead speaks of ībāq.

These are different conditions.

A ship may stop for lack of wind yet remain sound and afloat.

A destroyed ship may break apart, sink, or entirely lose its ability to continue the voyage.

Some lexical accounts also explain أَوْبَقَ here as confining the ships or preventing their movement.

Since the preceding verse already describes calm winds and stationary ships, “destroying” makes a clearer distinction between the situations.

Nor should يُوبِقْهُنَّ be restricted to “sink them”: sinking is one mode of destruction at sea, not the root's whole meaning.

بِمَا كَسَبُوا: what people have earned

The expression shifts from the feminine plural in يُوبِقْهُنَّ to the masculine plural in كَسَبُوا.

Translation and analysis must preserve this difference.

هُنَّ refers to the ships.

The plural subject of كَسَبُوا refers to people whose deeds and earning are under discussion.

Ships are the direct object of destruction; conduct and earning are attributed to people.

كَسَبُوا alone does not establish that every passenger committed a particular sin.

The sentence connects possible destruction of ships with human conduct without listing offenders, assigning each person's responsibility, or explaining precisely how the consequence occurs.

For example, a poor decision by owners or operators can endanger everyone aboard. Those making it and those harmed need not have equal responsibility.

This illustrates collective consequences of conduct; it does not justify calling every maritime accident divine punishment of its passengers.

وَيَعْفُ عَنْ كَثِيرٍ: pardoning much

The verse ends with pardon.

يَعْفُ comes from ع ف و and here concerns forgiving or pardoning.

Destruction is therefore not the verse's only theme; pardon is present in the same statement.

Al-Shūrā 42:30 also connects misfortune, human conduct, and divine pardon.

An accident's occurrence cannot by itself prove divine anger, nor can survival alone prove complete innocence.

The destruction of a ship or injury to people does not itself authorize judgments about each victim's intention, sin, or ultimate destiny.

Ībāq is presented as a possibility within divine power, alongside pardon. Assigning specific responsibility requires evidence beyond the event itself.

The significance of the two Quranic uses

In Al-Kahf 18:52, مَوْبِقًا concerns judgment and the fate of false supports.

In Al-Shūrā 42:34, يُوبِقْهُنَّ concerns ships and their possible destruction.

The family therefore does not concern only the hereafter.

It can describe destruction in the material world.

Conversely, that material usage does not make مَوْبِقًا in Al-Kahf necessarily a natural event, sea, or identifiable geographical place.

Each derivative must be understood in context.

5. Comparison with related roots

و ب ق and ه ل ك: perishing and destruction

هَلَكَ is used in the Quran for destruction, perishing, and passing away.

Its broad range includes people, groups, settlements, and other things.

The main branch of و ب ق also concerns destruction.

Dictionaries repeatedly explain وَبَقَ through هَلَكَ, showing clear overlap.

Some uses of و ب ق additionally foreground entrapment and inability to escape.

This can help explain mawbiq as a place of destruction or barrier. It does not mean every halāk lacks entrapment or every wabaq involves prolonged captivity.

و ب ق and غ ر ق: destruction and drowning

غَرَق concerns submersion and drowning.

Yūnus 10:90 describes Pharaoh drowning during pursuit of the Children of Israel.

و ب ق concerns destruction without always specifying its method.

Because Al-Shūrā 42:34 concerns ships, sinking is a conceivable form of their destruction.

Ships can nevertheless be destroyed in other ways.

“Destroy them” or “cause them to perish” is therefore preferable to restricting the root to sinking.

و ب ق and ح ب س: entrapment and confinement

حَبْس concerns confinement and preventing movement or departure.

Some lexical explanations also give mawbiq as a place of confinement or an obstructing barrier.

A camel stuck in mud illustrates the fields' relationship.

Confinement does not always lead to destruction.

Someone can be held for a time and then released.

The main branch of wabaq, by contrast, concerns perishing and ruin.

Confinement can be related to wabaq without being a complete equivalent for all its derivatives.

و ب ق and ف ر ق: a place of destruction and separation

فَرَقَ concerns separation or separating.

In Al-Kahf 18:52, مَوْبِقًا is placed between two parties and can separate them.

Mawbiq is not primarily the name of the act of separation.

A wall between two houses can divide them: it is a barrier but not intrinsically a place of destruction.

In Al-Kahf, mawbiq concerns destruction and a ruinous consequence.

The placement of a barrier in the sentence should therefore be distinguished from the root's central destruction sense.

و ب ق and ب و ر: destruction and ruin

Quranic ب و ر concerns ruin, stagnation, and the loss of results or prosperity.

In Ibrāhīm 14:28, دَارَ الْبَوَارِ concerns the outcome of people who exchanged The God's blessing for disbelief.

Bawār there involves destruction and ruin.

Mawbiq in Al-Kahf 18:52 involves a place of destruction and ruinous separation.

Both can concern harmful outcomes without being identical in form or range.

The stagnation found in some ب و ر uses must not be transferred to و ب ق without evidence.

و ب ق and و ق ب

The roots differ only in the order of their last two letters.

و ب ق concerns destruction and ruin.

و ق ب occurs in Al-Falaq 113:3 in غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ, concerning darkness arriving or entering.

Similar letters do not establish a semantic or historical relationship.

Their exact order must be preserved so meanings are not transferred between families.

6. Interpretation and translation proposals

Does wabaq always mean bodily death?

No. Arabic witnesses also concern ruined reputation, entrapment in debt, and destruction in various circumstances.

Its destructive sense can extend beyond bodily death.

Yet not every slight loss or failure is wabaq; the principal meaning concerns severe ruin.

Is mawbiq always a particular place?

Its structure is compatible with a noun of place.

“Place of destruction” suits both its form and the context of Al-Kahf 18:52.

The word does not itself identify a named location with specified physical features.

Accounts about valleys, rivers, or hell's architecture should not replace its lexical definition.

Does entrapment always lead to destruction?

No. The concepts are close in some uses but are not identical.

A person may become trapped and then be rescued.

Translation must determine whether a sentence concerns entrapment or actual destruction.

Translation proposals for Forqan

وَبَقَ: perished or was ruined.

وَبِقَ: perished; in some uses, remained trapped.

وَبْق and وُبُوق: destruction or ruin.

وَابِق: perishing or caught in destruction.

أَوْبَقَ: destroyed or caused to perish.

إِيبَاق: causing destruction.

مُوبِق: destructive or ruinous.

مُوبَق: destroyed or trapped.

مَوْبِق: a place of destruction; where context supports it, a destructive barrier.

اِسْتَوْبَقَ: perished or brought oneself into destruction.

Suitable renderings for the two Quranic occurrences are:

Al-Kahf 18:52 — مَوْبِقًا

“A place of destruction.”

Within the sentence:

“And We will place between them a place of destruction.”

This preserves the noun and its connection with destruction. Its separating role becomes clear through contextual explanation.

Al-Shūrā 42:34 — يُوبِقْهُنَّ

“He may destroy them” or “cause them to perish.”

The pronoun refers to the ships.

“Sink them” can express one mode of destruction but should not be presented as the verb's only lexical meaning.

7. Conclusion

In early Arabic, و ب ق concerns perishing and causing destruction. Some uses also involve being trapped where escape is difficult or impossible.

The witness attributed to Zuhayr illustrates severe harm to reputation and standing, showing that the family is not restricted to bodily death.

The root occurs twice in the Quran.

In Al-Kahf 18:52, مَوْبِقًا denotes a place of destruction or destructive barrier in a scene separating those who alleged divine partners from those partners.

In Al-Shūrā 42:34, يُوبِقْهُنَّ concerns possible destruction of ships, alongside human conduct and divine pardon.

Destruction and ruin are prominent in both, but one is a noun in a judgment scene and the other a verb describing ships' possible destruction.

Final definition for Forqan:

The main branch of و ب ق means perishing, being ruined, and causing destruction. Related uses can also include entrapment and inability to escape.

Quranic مَوْبِق denotes a place of destruction or destructive barrier, while أَوْبَقَ concerns causing something to perish.

The family connects entrapment, ruin, and destruction; each event's cause, form, and outcome must be understood from its own sentence and context.
