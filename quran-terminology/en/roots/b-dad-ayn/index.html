The Arabic root B Ḍ ʿ has several uses that initially appear different. It can refer to cutting and separating a piece of something; بِضَاعَة denotes goods or assets entering trade, while numerical بِضْع denotes a limited number of units.

These uses can often be connected through the idea of a piece separated or identified within a larger whole. This connection does not make every derivative synonymous. Numerical بِضْع has an established numerical meaning, and بِضَاعَة is a commercial term. Each Qur’anic form must be examined within its own construction and context.

The Qur’an uses two nouns from this root: بِضَاعَة, including forms with attached pronouns, and بِضْع. The first occurs in Joseph’s story; the second expresses a duration. The verb of cutting and other recognized derivatives do not occur in the Qur’an. These two nouns occur seven times in six verses across two surahs: five instances of بِضَاعَة and two of بِضْع.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: seven occurrences in two forms

1. The basic meaning and physical image

In early Arabic, بَضَعَ describes cutting something and separating a piece from it. For example, بَضَعَ اللَّحْمَ means cutting meat, and بَضْعَة denotes a piece of meat.

The image presents part of something as a distinct piece. It may be separated from a body, wood, or something else. Cutting is the action, while بَضْعَة names the resulting piece.

Other expressions belong to this family. A مِبْضَع is a cutting or incising instrument, especially for skin and flesh. In wound terminology, بَاضِعَة denotes an injury penetrating and cutting the flesh.

Cutting and dividing into pieces are therefore attested in this branch. Yet other derivatives should not all be translated as “cutting.” A commercial or numerical term has its own established meaning, even where a historical connection with a piece or portion can be explained.

2. Uses in early Arabic

Early poetry attests this family’s meanings of cutting and a piece of the body.

Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā describes the remains of a young gazelle eaten by a predator:

دَمًا عِنْدَ شِلْوٍ تَحْجُلُ الطَّيْرُ حَوْلَهُ
وَبَضْعَ لِحَامٍ فِي إِهَابٍ مُقَدَّدِ

daman ʿinda shilwin taḥjulu al-ṭayru ḥawlahu / wa-baḍʿa liḥāmin fī ihābin muqaddadi

Lisān al-ʿArab and Tāj al-ʿArūs: vocalization of the hopping verb

Translation: “Blood beside a carcass around which birds hop, and pieces of flesh in a hide torn into strips.”

Classical Arabic lexicons: qadd and muqaddad

بَضْعَ لِحَامٍ refers here to pieces of flesh. This illustrates the family’s concrete, bodily meaning.

Aws ibn Ḥajar also says, in a poem discussing weapons and bow making:

وَمَبْضُوعَةً مِنْ رَأْسِ فَرْعٍ شَظِيَّةٍ
بِطَوْدٍ تَرَاهُ بِالسَّحَابِ مُجَلَّلَا

wa-mabḍūʿatan min raʾsi farʿin shaẓiyyatin / bi-ṭawdin tarāhu bi-al-saḥābi mujallalā

Translation: “And [a bow of] wood cut from the tip of a branch, on a mountain you see wrapped in cloud.”

مَبْضُوعَة refers here to wood cut from a branch. The witness shows that cutting was not confined to meat but could also describe wood.

Tahdhīb al-Lughah: Aws’s witness describing a bow

Other forms are recorded in Arabic. بُضْع occurs in some bodily and sexual uses, and مُبَاضَعَة denotes sexual intercourse. This branch does not occur with these meanings in the Qur’an and must not be imposed on the two Qur’anic nouns.

Lexicons also record بَضَعَ مِنَ الْمَاءِ, meaning to drink and become satisfied or have one’s thirst quenched. Ibn Fāris treats this as a separate branch. Such uses remind us that a shared word structure does not prove that every meaning developed from a single image.

Ibn Fāris, Maqāyīs al-Lughah: branches of the root

The commercial family was also recognized in Arabic. أَبْضَعَ بِضَاعَةً concerns assigning assets to trade. In this use, a مُسْتَبْضِع makes something his merchandise; in the following proverb, the word also carries the sense of transporting goods.

The familiar proverb كَمُسْتَبْضِعِ التَّمْرِ إِلَى هَجَرَ compares someone to a person taking dates to Hajar, a place already famous for dates. It describes carrying goods somewhere those same goods abound. The proverb attests an established commercial meaning, although its recording in early sources alone does not date its precise origin.

Lisān al-ʿArab: commercial usage and the date-carrying proverb

3. From a portion of assets to merchandise

Lexicons define بِضَاعَة as assets or something assigned to trade. A familiar explanation is that a merchant sets aside part of his assets for commerce, and this portion is called بِضَاعَة.

This offers an intelligible connection with cutting and separating. It should not, however, become a permanent condition in the definition. Goods need not have been separated from a larger property at the moment of sale. They may already have been prepared for trade or may serve as payment in an exchange.

In commercial use, بِضَاعَة can denote goods offered for sale or assets brought to obtain other goods. Sometimes the assets themselves are for sale; sometimes they function as payment.

This difference is evident in Yūsuf. The Qur’an once calls a human being whom others have treated as merchandise بِضَاعَة; elsewhere the same word denotes what Joseph’s brothers brought in exchange for provisions.

Consequently, بِضَاعَة should not invariably be translated as “goods for sale,” “money,” or “capital.” Its central field is assets or something entering a commercial relationship. The sentence and transaction determine its precise role.

4. Biḍāʿah in Joseph’s story

Joseph treated as merchandise: Yūsuf, verse 19

Qur’anic text:

وَجَاءَتْ سَيَّارَةٌ فَأَرْسَلُوا وَارِدَهُمْ فَأَدْلَىٰ دَلْوَهُ ۖ قَالَ يَا بُشْرَىٰ هَٰذَا غُلَامٌ ۚ وَأَسَرُّوهُ بِضَاعَةً ۚ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ بِمَا يَعْمَلُونَ

wa-jāʾat sayyāratun fa-arsalū wāridahum fa-adlā dalwahu qāla yā bushrā hādhā ghulāmun wa-asarrūhu biḍāʿatan wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun bi-mā yaʿmalūna

Translation:

A caravan arrived and sent their water drawer. He lowered his bucket and said, “Good news! Here is a boy!” They concealed him as merchandise, and The God knows what they do.

بِضَاعَةً describes Joseph here: a human being whom a group has placed in the position of tradable merchandise.

The lexical point is that بِضَاعَة is not limited to a particular kind of property. Something entering buying and selling may receive this designation, even when that “something” is a human being.

This use primarily describes what people did. Employing a commercial term for Joseph does not morally endorse or legitimize human trafficking. The verse reports an event and, through وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ بِمَا يَعْمَلُونَ, emphasizes The God’s knowledge of their conduct.

Grammatically, بِضَاعَةً is accusative and clarifies the condition or designation imposed on Joseph. The translation should convey that the perpetrators placed him in the position of merchandise, rather than treating merchandise as his true identity or inherent quality.

Returning the assets brought for exchange: Yūsuf, verse 62

Qur’anic text:

وَقَالَ لِفِتْيَانِهِ اجْعَلُوا بِضَاعَتَهُمْ فِي رِحَالِهِمْ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَعْرِفُونَهَا إِذَا انْقَلَبُوا إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهِمْ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ

wa-qāla li-fityānihi ijʿalū biḍāʿatahum fī riḥālihim laʿallahum yaʿrifūnahā idhā inqalabū ilā ahlihim laʿallahum yarjiʿūna

Translation:

He told his attendants, “Put their trading goods in their packs, so that they may recognize them when they return to their family, and perhaps come back.”

The verse follows the brothers’ arrival in Egypt to obtain provisions. Joseph orders that بِضَاعَتَهُمْ be placed in their packs.

Their بِضَاعَة here is what they brought for the transaction, rather than the provisions received. It could have been money or goods serving as payment; the word itself does not specify which.

“Their money” is understandable as a familiar account of the transaction, but it is lexically narrower than “what they had brought for exchange.” The Arabic alone does not establish payment in coins.

The attached هُمْ marks the brothers’ ownership or connection with these assets. The هَا in يَعْرِفُونَهَا likewise refers to the trading goods. The stated aim is that they recognize their property on returning home and perhaps return again.

Biḍāʿah and matāʿ distinguished: Yūsuf, verse 65

Qur’anic text:

وَلَمَّا فَتَحُوا مَتَاعَهُمْ وَجَدُوا بِضَاعَتَهُمْ رُدَّتْ إِلَيْهِمْ ۖ قَالُوا يَا أَبَانَا مَا نَبْغِي ۖ هَٰذِهِ بِضَاعَتُنَا رُدَّتْ إِلَيْنَا ۖ وَنَمِيرُ أَهْلَنَا وَنَحْفَظُ أَخَانَا وَنَزْدَادُ كَيْلَ بَعِيرٍ ۖ ذَٰلِكَ كَيْلٌ يَسِيرٌ

wa-lammā fataḥū matāʿahum wajadū biḍāʿatahum ruddat ilayhim qālū yā abānā mā nabghī hādhihi biḍāʿatunā ruddat ilaynā wa-namīru ahlanā wa-naḥfaẓu akhānā wa-nazdādu kayla baʿīrin dhālika kaylun yasīrun

Translation:

When they opened their packs, they found their trading goods returned to them. They said, “Our father, what more could we seek? Here are our trading goods, returned to us! We will obtain provisions for our family, guard our brother, and gain an additional camel load. That is an easy measure.”

بِضَاعَة occurs twice here: first بِضَاعَتَهُمْ, then بِضَاعَتُنَا. Both refer to the same returned assets.

Distinguishing مَتَاعَهُمْ from بِضَاعَتَهُمْ matters. Here matāʿ denotes the packs and belongings they opened, while biḍāʿah denotes the assets involved in the transaction, which they found inside the packs.

The two words cannot therefore be translated identically as “goods” without losing a distinction. The sentence shows the brothers opening their packs and finding what they had paid inside them.

رُدَّتْ إِلَيْهِمْ also makes clear that these assets have come back to them. Here بِضَاعَة is what was returned as payment, rather than goods they had newly purchased.

This is clear evidence that the word’s commercial meaning must be understood through its role in exchange, rather than simply as goods ready for sale.

Merchandise of little value: Yūsuf, verse 88

Qur’anic text:

فَلَمَّا دَخَلُوا عَلَيْهِ قَالُوا يَا أَيُّهَا الْعَزِيزُ مَسَّنَا وَأَهْلَنَا الضُّرُّ وَجِئْنَا بِبِضَاعَةٍ مُزْجَاةٍ فَأَوْفِ لَنَا الْكَيْلَ وَتَصَدَّقْ عَلَيْنَا ۖ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَجْزِي الْمُتَصَدِّقِينَ

fa-lammā dakhalū ʿalayhi qālū yā ayyuhā al-ʿazīzu massanā wa-ahlanā al-ḍurru wa-jiʾnā bi-biḍāʿatin muzjātin fa-awfi lanā al-kayla wa-taṣaddaq ʿalaynā inna Allāha yajzī al-mutaṣaddiqīna

Translation:

When they entered his presence, they said, “O mighty one, hardship has touched us and our family, and we have brought goods of little value. Give us the full measure and be charitable to us. The God rewards those who give charity.”

بِبِضَاعَةٍ مُزْجَاةٍ contains two lexical elements: بِضَاعَة names the assets or goods brought for exchange; مُزْجَاة describes them.

Their low value, small quantity, or difficulty of acceptance comes from the adjective and context, rather than from بِضَاعَة itself. This distinction matters: the noun alone implies neither little nor abundant property.

مُزْجَاة belongs to Z J W, associated with driving, advancing, and setting in motion. Commercially, it can describe assets difficult to get accepted or exchanged. Accordingly, “of little value,” “hard to trade,” or “of negligible value in exchange” fits the context.

Study of Z J W

The brothers describe their own and their family’s hardship and request a full measure despite their inadequate trading goods. They then ask for charity. Their request shows that what they brought is insufficient or unsuitable to obtain what they seek, but the verse does not identify the precise goods.

Again, بِضَاعَة is not the name of a particular substance or commodity. Associated words and the circumstances of exchange clarify its value, quality, and function.

5. Numerical biḍʿ: a limited number of units

The other Qur’anic branch is بِضْع, used in counting for a limited number of units.

In familiar Arabic usage, بِضْع ordinarily covers three to nine. Lexicons and lexical reports differ over its exact limits, but its general range is a small number of units below ten.

Lisān al-ʿArab and Al-Miṣbāḥ al-Munīr: the numerical range

It is distinct from بَعْض, a portion of a whole. Numerical بِضْع concerns a number of units; بَعْض denotes part of a set without itself specifying a number.

A few years in prison: Yūsuf, verse 42

Qur’anic text:

وَقَالَ لِلَّذِي ظَنَّ أَنَّهُ نَاجٍ مِنْهُمَا اذْكُرْنِي عِنْدَ رَبِّكَ فَأَنْسَاهُ الشَّيْطَانُ ذِكْرَ رَبِّهِ فَلَبِثَ فِي السِّجْنِ بِضْعَ سِنِينَ

wa-qāla li-alladhī ẓanna annahu nājin minhumā udhkurnī ʿinda rabbika fa-ansāhu al-shayṭānu dhikra rabbihi fa-labitha fī al-sijni biḍʿa sinīna

Translation:

He told the one of the two whom he thought would be saved, “Mention me to your master.” But Satan caused him to forget to mention Joseph to his master, and Joseph remained in prison a few years.

بِضْعَ سِنِينَ expresses the duration of the imprisonment. It denotes a limited number of years, rather than one year or an indefinite number extending to any large quantity.

The verse does not specify five, seven, or nine years. Fixing one definite number requires independent evidence. “A few years” is therefore more suitable as a direct translation than choosing a particular number.

Grammatically, بِضْعَ forms a construct phrase with سِنِينَ and expresses the duration of the stay. Its accusative form follows the sentence structure; it does not create a new meaning distinct from the numerical use.

A few years until victory changes hands: Al-Rūm, verse 4

Qur’anic text:

فِي بِضْعِ سِنِينَ ۗ لِلَّهِ الْأَمْرُ مِنْ قَبْلُ وَمِنْ بَعْدُ ۚ وَيَوْمَئِذٍ يَفْرَحُ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ

fī biḍʿi sinīna li-Allāhi al-amru min qablu wa-min baʿdu wa-yawmaʾidhin yafraḥu al-muʾminūna

Translation:

Within a few years. Command belongs to The God, before and after; on that day the believers will rejoice.

This follows the report of the Romans’ defeat and future victory. Al-Rūm 30:3 says they will prevail after their defeat; Al-Rūm 30:4 gives the timing as فِي بِضْعِ سِنِينَ.

Again, بِضْع denotes a limited interval. It does not say that victory will occur precisely in the seventh or ninth year, but gives a span of a few years.

The preposition فِي sets the event’s temporal frame. بِضْعِ is genitive because it follows that preposition and is also the first term of a construct phrase with سِنِينَ. In Yūsuf 12:42 it was accusative. Different case marks reflect different grammatical roles without changing the basic numerical meaning.

These two Qur’anic uses show that بِضْع is an established limited number, rather than one exact number. “A few years” preserves the direct meaning; the customary numerical range is best explained in the lexical study.

6. Numerical biḍʿ and the meaning of cutting

Lexicons connect numerical بِضْع with a piece separated from a whole: a few units constitute a limited part of a larger numerical set.

The semantic connection is understandable. Just as بَضْعَة is a piece of something, numerical بِضْع identifies a limited group of units.

This explanation should not replace the established numerical sense. The general idea of a piece cannot by itself explain why the familiar range begins at three and ends before ten. Recognized linguistic usage determines these limits, rather than free inference from the image of cutting.

Connecting بِضْع with a piece or portion therefore explains a possible relationship among the family’s branches. Its practical Qur’anic meaning remains a limited number of units.

7. Related roots and neighboring words distinguished

B Ḍ ʿ and B ʿ Ḍ

Study of B ʿ Ḍ

These roots contain very similar letters, but in a different order, and must not be identified with one another.

بَعْض denotes part of a whole. In بَعْضُهُمْ, for example, some members of a set are meant; the word alone does not specify their number.

By contrast, Qur’anic numerical بِضْع denotes a few units within a recognized numerical range. بِضْعَ سِنِينَ means a few years, rather than simply an unspecified part of all years.

بِضَاعَة also belongs to commerce, a meaning not derived from using بَعْض.

The conceptual resemblance between a portion and a piece must not blur these two linguistic families.

B Ḍ ʿ and Q Ṭ ʿ

Study of Q Ṭ ʿ

Q Ṭ ʿ is a broad root for cutting, separating, and severing. In its physical branch, بَضَعَ denotes cutting and separating pieces, but its family also has established commercial and numerical uses.

The roots overlap in cutting, yet not every use of one can replace a use of the other.

For example, بِضَاعَة in Yūsuf does not mean “a cut-off thing.” Its commercial sense is established in the language.

Biḍāʿah, matāʿ, māl, and thaman

Study of M T ʿ; Study of M W L; Study of TH M N

مَال is a general designation for property, without necessarily referring to a particular transaction.

Depending on context, مَتَاع denotes belongings, goods, or things people use. In Yūsuf 12:65 it refers to the packs the brothers opened.

ثَمَن is the price given in exchange for something. Yūsuf 12:20 uses it in the account of Joseph’s sale to express the transaction’s price.

بِضَاعَة denotes assets or something entering exchange. It may be merchandise offered or what is brought to obtain other goods.

Payment can consequently be called بِضَاعَة in a particular context, but biḍāʿah and thaman are not always synonymous. One concerns assets circulating in trade, the other their role as a price given for something.

8. Morphological and grammatical points

بِضَاعَة is a noun appearing with different case marks and pronouns. بِضَاعَتَهُمْ and بِضَاعَتُنَا have the same basic meaning; the attached pronoun identifies whose assets they are.

In بِبِضَاعَةٍ, the first bā is a preposition; the second belongs to the word itself. This combination does not create a new, independent derivative.

بِضْع is likewise a numerical noun used with سِنِينَ in the Qur’an. Accusative بِضْعَ in Yūsuf and genitive بِضْعِ in Al-Rūm differ because of grammatical position, rather than meaning.

In Arabic counting, بِضْع relates to the counted noun’s grammatical gender, as certain numerals do. With masculine nouns, a form bearing the feminine ending occurs, as in بِضْعَةَ عَشَرَ رَجُلًا. This illustrates a recognized Arabic construction and must not be presented as a Qur’anic occurrence.

Thus بِضْع, بِضْعَة, and بِضَاعَة differ by more than a few surface vowel marks. Morphological form and sentence usage are essential to identifying the meaning.

9. Translation proposals for Forqan

For physical بَضَعَ, Persian equivalents meaning “cut,” “incise,” or “separate a piece” are suitable. The object and action determine the choice.

For بَضْعَة, “piece,” “portion,” or, in the particular bodily use, “a piece of meat” is appropriate.

Persian recognizes the loanword biḍāʿat, but “goods” or “trading assets” will often be clearer. In Yūsuf 12:19, “merchandise” fits. In Yūsuf 12:62 and Yūsuf 12:65, “what they had brought for exchange” is more precise than “goods for sale.” In Yūsuf 12:88, “goods of little value” or “low-value assets for exchange” fits the context.

Natural renderings of بِضْعَ سِنِينَ and فِي بِضْعِ سِنِينَ are “a few years” and “within a few years.” Explain the familiar three-to-nine range lexically instead of unnecessarily replacing the Qur’anic word with one of those numbers.

Each equivalent must fit the complete sentence. The aim is to preserve a word’s meaning in its particular position, rather than translate every family member identically.

10. Conclusion

The physical branch of Arabic B Ḍ ʿ concerns cutting and separating pieces. Early poetry attests meat and wood. Within the same family, بِضَاعَة denotes assets or goods entering trade.

The Qur’an presents two specific uses. بِضَاعَة in Joseph’s story denotes either merchandise offered or assets involved in exchange; بِضْع in two verses denotes a limited number of years.

A connection with a separated or identified portion explains a significant part of the family, but every recorded branch cannot confidently be reduced to that single image. Evidence and actual usage must establish each derivative’s meaning.

The principal translation conclusion is clear: biḍāʿah is a commercial word whose application depends on context; biḍʿ is numerical, denoting a limited number of units. They should not share one translation, and neither should be confused with the separate root B ʿ Ḍ.
