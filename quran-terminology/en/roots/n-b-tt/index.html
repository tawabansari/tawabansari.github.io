Drawing out what is concealed: from extracting water to obtaining information and understanding a matter

1. Introducing the root and its semantic core

The Arabic root N B Ṭ concerns drawing out and bringing to light something previously concealed or difficult to reach. One clear early use concerns reaching underground water while digging a well and drawing it out.

The same semantic field also includes bringing information to light, obtaining knowledge that is not readily available, and drawing a conclusion through examining a matter.

There is an important distinction between “coming out” and “bringing out.” In an expression such as نَبَطَ الماءُ, water emerges or becomes visible. In اِسْتَنْبَطَ الماءَ, a person reaches and extracts water through searching and effort.

A defensible semantic core is therefore the emergence or disclosure of something previously concealed or inaccessible. The verb istinbāṭ also brings the effort of obtaining it into focus.

This meaning is not confined to water: classical Arabic also uses this root for obtaining news, knowledge, and the result of an examination. Yet not every use necessarily means scientific research, legal reasoning, or the discovery of certain truth. These features depend on the verb’s construction and its subject matter.

Qur’anic forms and important derivatives

The root N B Ṭ occurs only once in the Qur’an, in Al-Nisāʾ 4:83, as يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ. This is a Form X verb.

Other forms belonging to this root are also known in Arabic:

  • نَبَطَ: emerged or became visible, especially of water.
  • نَبْط and نُبُوط: verbal nouns or forms concerning the emergence and appearance of water.
  • نَبَط and نَبِيط: names for water emerging from a well.
  • أَنْبَطَ: brought something out or reached it.
  • اِسْتَنْبَطَ: extracted something through searching, examination, or effort.
  • اِسْتِنْبَاط: the act of extracting and obtaining something from its source.

These are lexical forms within the root family, not multiple Qur’anic forms. The sole Qur’anic occurrence is the plural imperfect verb يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ.

2. Early Arabic usage and the root’s material image

Water concealed in the ground

A clear material image is water reached by digging a well. The water may already exist beneath the ground, yet remain invisible or inaccessible to someone standing on its surface.

When digging a well revealed water, Arabic used words such as nabṭ and nubūṭ. Verbs such as أَنْبَطَ and اِسْتَنْبَطَ also described someone reaching water through excavation.

Two aspects should be distinguished: water exists in the ground, but reaching it requires searching and effort. By digging and reaching the source, a person brings to light what was previously concealed.

This image helps explain istinbāṭ, since it is more than simply receiving something already prepared and apparent. Nevertheless, not every detail of digging a well, its tools, or the depth of the water should be transferred into the word’s figurative meaning.

A witness from early Arabic poetry

A verse attributed to Kaʿb ibn Saʿd al-Ghanawī uses نَبَط to describe someone possessing dignity and strength:

قَرِيبٌ ثَرَاهُ مَا يَنَالُ عَدُوُّهُ
لَهُ نَبَطًا آبِي الْهَوَانِ قَطُوبُ

qarībun tharāhu mā yanālu ʿaduwwuhu / lahu nabaṭan ābī al-hawāni qaṭūbu

Sense-based translation:

“His goodness is readily available, yet his enemy cannot reach his water; he refuses humiliation and frowns in the face of it.”

The image of nearby moist earth suggests readily available goodness and generosity. The inaccessible water conveys the praised person’s strength and resistance to an enemy. This can be understood figuratively as the enemy’s inability to reach his depths or overcome him; the first phrase does not merely describe an accessible outward appearance.

The wording and attribution vary. Some versions have mā and others lā; the ending is also transmitted as ʿinda al-hawān. Despite the familiar attribution to Kaʿb al-Ghanawī, another attribution is also recorded. The exact date of composition is uncertain.

The verse therefore illustrates a use preserved in Arabic literary and lexical tradition, but should not be presented without qualification as securely dated evidence from before the Qur’an.

Extending the usage from water to knowledge

Early Arabic also uses expressions for obtaining news, knowledge, and the understanding of a matter requiring thought.

For example, اِسْتَنْبَطْتُ مِنْ فُلَانٍ خَبَرًا means that I obtained a report from someone; اِسْتَنْبَطْتُ هَذَا الْأَمْرَ describes a matter that a person brings to light through thought.

What is extracted in these uses is no longer water or a material object, but information or a result obtained through inquiry, examination, or reflection.

Both material and intellectual uses are attested in Arabic. Their existence alone does not prove that the latter was first created in the Qur’an. Such a historical claim requires further independent, dated evidence.

Other uses that should not be overlooked

Arabic also uses نَبَط and أَنْبَاط to name groups of people, especially inhabitants of parts of Iraq and its surrounding regions.

One traditional explanation connects this name with their skill in extracting water. That explanation alone does not establish the historical origin of the ethnic name.

أَنْبَط also describes a horse with white coloring on its underside, while نُبْطَة names that white marking. This usage also appears in poetry of the Islamic period.

These branches show why every word indexed under N B Ṭ should not be reduced to a single mental act or historical story. None of these ethnic or descriptive forms occurs in the Qur’an. Qur’anic study should remain centered on the actual use of يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ.

3. Qur’anic usage: Al-Nisāʾ 4:83

وَإِذَا جَاءَهُمْ أَمْرٌ مِنَ الْأَمْنِ أَوِ الْخَوْفِ أَذَاعُوا بِهِ ۖ وَلَوْ رَدُّوهُ إِلَى الرَّسُولِ وَإِلَىٰ أُولِي الْأَمْرِ مِنْهُمْ لَعَلِمَهُ الَّذِينَ يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ مِنْهُمْ ۗ وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُ لَاتَّبَعْتُمُ الشَّيْطَانَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

wa-idhā jāʾahum amrun mina al-amni awi al-khawfi adhāʿū bihi wa-law raddūhu ilā al-rasūli wa-ilā ulī al-amri minhum la-ʿalimahu alladhīna yastanbiṭūnahu minhum wa-lawlā faḍlu allāhi ʿalaykum wa-raḥmatuhu la-ittabaʿtumu al-shayṭāna illā qalīlan

Translation:

“When a report concerning security or fear reaches them, they spread it. Had they referred it to the Messenger and those in authority among them, those among them who obtain it through examination would have known it. Were it not for the favor of The God upon you and His mercy, you would have followed Satan, except for a few.”

Al-Nisāʾ 4:83

This verse contains the root’s sole Qur’anic occurrence. Its meaning should be examined in relation to the problem addressed: spreading a report before its truth is established, and the importance of examination by people with the ability or position needed to obtain sound information.

Reports of security and fear: the verse’s main subject

The verse begins:

وَإِذَا جَاءَهُمْ أَمْرٌ مِنَ الْأَمْنِ أَوِ الْخَوْفِ أَذَاعُوا بِهِ

wa-idhā jāʾahum amrun mina al-amni awi al-khawfi adhāʿū bihi

“When a report concerning security or fear reaches them, they spread it.”

A group receives a report or matter concerning security or fear and spreads it.

أَذَاعُوا بِهِ concerns circulating and publicizing it. The verse does not say that all these reports were necessarily false. The problem is that a report with possible consequences for the community’s security and peace is spread before the matter is properly examined.

The surrounding verses discuss people whose words and actions do not agree and whose position on matters concerning the believing community is unstable. The following verse also mentions fighting and confronting an enemy. Reports of security and fear can therefore be understood against a background of danger, conflict, and collective affairs.

Nevertheless, مِنَ الْأَمْنِ أَوِ الْخَوْفِ does not name a particular battle or event. The translation should therefore not reduce it to “news of military victory or defeat.”

Referring a matter to those able to examine it

The next part says:

وَلَوْ رَدُّوهُ إِلَى الرَّسُولِ وَإِلَىٰ أُولِي الْأَمْرِ مِنْهُمْ

wa-law raddūhu ilā al-rasūli wa-ilā ulī al-amri minhum

“Had they referred it to the Messenger and those in authority among them.”

In response to hasty circulation, the verse speaks of referring the matter to the Messenger and those in authority among them.

رَدُّوهُ comes from R D D and means returning or referring something back. Here it concerns referring a matter for consideration, rather than physically returning a report to a place.

أُولِي الْأَمْرِ identifies a group by its authority or responsibility. Proposed referents range from those responsible for collective affairs to people of knowledge and sound judgment.

For a study of N B Ṭ, the central issue is not to settle every possible referent of ulī al-amr, but to examine their relationship to istinbāṭ. In contrast with immediate circulation, the verse discusses attending to a report and obtaining knowledge.

The meaning of يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ

The central expression is:

لَعَلِمَهُ الَّذِينَ يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ مِنْهُمْ

la-ʿalimahu alladhīna yastanbiṭūnahu minhum

“Those among them who obtain it through examination would have known it.”

يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ is a Form X imperfect verb in the third-person masculine plural. The wāw marks the plural subject, while هُ is a singular masculine object pronoun.

That pronoun refers to the matter or report mentioned in the verse.

In this construction, istinbāṭ means obtaining information about a matter whose truth is not yet clear to those receiving the report.

Several aspects can be distinguished:

First, there is a matter that is not yet sufficiently understood. Second, some people seek to obtain information or clarify it. Third, the expected knowledge results from this examination.

This does not mean that anyone claiming to perform istinbāṭ necessarily reaches the truth. The root expresses extraction and obtaining something; the correctness of the result must be assessed separately.

Here, لَعَلِمَهُ states the expected result. Attaining knowledge is therefore understood from the complete sentence, rather than from the verb istinbāṭ alone.

The distinction matters: istinbāṭ expresses extracting and obtaining something; inquiry and examination describe how it is obtained in this context. Knowledge is the result presented for proper examination. Istinbāṭ should therefore not be reduced to an attempt without a result, or equated with knowledge of the truth itself.

The role of مِنْهُمْ and differing syntactic readings

The expression ends with مِنْهُمْ. More than one reading of its pronoun’s referent and syntactic attachment is defensible.

One reading refers the pronoun to those in authority. It then means those among them who can investigate and extract the relevant information.

Another reading connects مِنْهُمْ with the act of istinbāṭ: those who pursue the matter through the Messenger or those in authority and obtain the relevant information from them.

It may also refer to a broader group of people involved in the situation.

These possibilities differ in the examiner’s position and the source of information. The central meaning remains obtaining something whose understanding requires examination and inquiry.

The pronoun alone cannot establish that istinbāṭ in every field belongs exclusively to one particular class. Such a general rule does not follow from the root’s meaning and this verse’s construction alone.

4. Istinbāṭ: examining a report or deriving a religious ruling?

A major obstacle to understanding this root is restricting istinbāṭ to its technical meaning in jurisprudence.

In legal usage, istinbāṭ generally means deriving a ruling from evidence. By examining texts, rules, and relationships between proofs, a jurist seeks a ruling not stated directly or fully in a single expression.

This use is linguistically related to the general concept of extraction. Yet the later technical meaning should not be projected directly back onto Al-Nisāʾ 4:83.

The verse’s immediate subject is not deriving a religious ruling from verses. It is a report concerning security or fear and how it should be handled.

Translating يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ here merely as “those who derive its religious ruling through legal reasoning” would therefore change the verse’s subject.

Conversely, this does not mean that Arabic never uses istinbāṭ for understanding a ruling or drawing a conclusion from a text. The same general meaning of extraction can operate in different fields.

The distinction is between these two claims:

The linguistic meaning of istinbāṭ: obtaining, through examination or effort, something that is not apparent or directly accessible.

Its specialized legal use: deriving a ruling from evidence according to methods accepted within a legal system.

The latter can be a technical application of the former, but does not encompass the root’s entire semantic field.

Does istinbāṭ mean guessing or conjecture?

Persian sometimes uses istinbāṭ for a personal interpretation, as when someone says, “This is what I inferred from his words.”

This is natural in present-day usage, but may obscure the distinction between a tentative impression and a supported conclusion.

Extraction and obtaining something are central to the root’s meaning. When the subject is news or knowledge, this can involve questioning, gathering evidence, reflection, and examination.

Istinbāṭ is therefore neither necessarily an unfounded guess nor, by itself, proof of truth.

An inference may be correct or incorrect. Its validity depends on evidence and the soundness of the method, rather than on someone calling the activity istinbāṭ.

In Al-Nisāʾ 4:83, istinbāṭ contrasts with a hasty response to a report and is linked to attaining knowledge. “Pursuing and examining a matter to clarify it” therefore fits better than an unsupported mental impression.

Connections with other verses about reports and knowledge

Although N B Ṭ occurs only once in the Qur’an, examining information is conceptually related to other verses.

Al-Ḥujurāt 49:6 commands examination when a report is brought by a transgressor:

فَتَبَيَّنُوا

fa-tabayyanū

“Then investigate and establish what is clear.”

Al-Isrāʾ 17:36 also prohibits following something of which a person has no knowledge.

These verses do not use N B Ṭ, but relate conceptually to Al-Nisāʾ 4:83: receiving a report should not be equated with correctly knowing its truth.

This connection does not make clarification, knowledge, and istinbāṭ complete synonyms. Each has its own meaning and approaches knowledge from a different angle.

Together these verses convey the importance of responsibility in handling information. How that responsibility is fulfilled depends on the report, the available evidence, and the circumstances.

5. Comparison with related roots

N B Ṭ (نَبَطَ) and N B ʿ (نَبَعَ): extracting and springing forth

The Arabic root N B ʿ concerns water springing forth from its source. يَنْبُوع and يَنَابِيع belong to this family.

The Qur’an uses يَنْبُوعًا in Al-Isrāʾ 17:90 and يَنَابِيعَ in Al-Zumar 39:21.

This root overlaps with N B Ṭ in the semantic field of water, though their uses show a difference of emphasis.

N B ʿ more often emphasizes water springing forth from its source. N B Ṭ, especially in anbaṭa and istanbaṭa, emphasizes revealing or reaching something previously concealed.

This distinction is not absolute, since نَبَطَ الماءُ can also describe water itself emerging.

The Qur’anic use of N B Ṭ is Form X and concerns understanding a report rather than water. Its relationship to N B ʿ illuminates the lexical image, but cannot replace analysis of the Qur’anic verb itself.

N B Ṭ (نَبَطَ) and KH R J (خَرَجَ): extracting and bringing out

KH R J has a broad semantic field concerning going out and bringing out.

اِسْتَخْرَجَ can also mean bringing something out of its location and can closely resemble اِسْتَنْبَطَ in many uses.

KH R J is not restricted to the emergence or extraction of something concealed. In familiar uses of N B Ṭ, however, accessing something previously concealed or inaccessible has a prominent role.

Extraction can therefore serve as a general Arabic equivalent of istinbāṭ, without expressing every nuance of its use in each context.

For Al-Nisāʾ 4:83, simply “bringing out a report” is not natural Persian. Since the subject is knowledge, “obtaining it through examination” or “clarifying it through inquiry” is more suitable.

Study of KH R J

N B Ṭ (نَبَطَ) and N B ʾ (نَبَأَ): a report and understanding its truth

N B ʾ concerns news, informing, and reporting. نَبَأ occurs frequently in the Qur’an.

N B ʾ and N B Ṭ resemble one another in sound and writing, but are separate roots.

Nabaʾ can refer to the report itself or to informing. Istinbāṭ in Al-Nisāʾ concerns the work undertaken to understand the subject of a report correctly.

Receiving news is therefore not necessarily performing istinbāṭ. Someone may hear or repeat a report without examining its accuracy, origin, or underlying reality.

This distinction helps explain Al-Nisāʾ 4:83. The verse distinguishes receiving and circulating a report from examining it to obtain knowledge.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

Translating N B Ṭ requires distinguishing its material use in Arabic from its Qur’anic use.

For نَبَطَ الماءُ, suitable Persian equivalents mean “the water emerged,” “the water became visible,” or “the water sprang from the ground.”

For أَنْبَطَ الماءَ and اِسْتَنْبَطَ الماءَ, one can use “he brought the water out” or “he reached water by digging.”

Water is not the subject in Al-Nisāʾ 4:83. Retaining the Arabic term istinbāṭ in Persian without explaining it may therefore restrict a reader to its legal meaning or its modern sense of a mental interpretation.

Several renderings of يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ merit consideration:

RenderingAssessment
They extract itClose to the lexical meaning, but somewhat cumbersome for reports in Persian
They infer itFamiliar, but may evoke specialized legal reasoning or a subjective interpretation
They examine itFluent, but does not fully convey obtaining a result
They obtain it through examinationClear and close to the verse’s use
They pursue the truth of itSuitable in context, but more explanatory than the direct wording

“They obtain it through examination” is a suitable rendering for Forqan.

It preserves the lexical meaning of extraction without restricting the reader to jurisprudence.

The complete clause لَعَلِمَهُ الَّذِينَ يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ مِنْهُمْ can be rendered:

“Those among them who obtain it through examination would have known it.”

This keeps knowledge distinct from the act of istinbāṭ. مِنْهُمْ is retained as “among them,” leaving the precise referent for contextual discussion.

7. Conclusion

In Arabic, N B Ṭ concerns the emergence or extraction of something previously concealed or inaccessible. Its clearest attested material image is reaching underground water by digging a well.

In broader usage, the same family also concerns obtaining information, knowledge, and the result of an examination. This breadth should not become a claim of certainty about the historical sequence in which the meanings developed.

The root occurs only once in the Qur’an, as يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ in Al-Nisāʾ 4:83. The verse discusses reports of security or fear and their circulation before examination. In response, it speaks of referring the matter for consideration and obtaining knowledge.

Here, istinbāṭ means pursuing and examining a matter to obtain information that clarifies it. The verse does not concern deriving a legal ruling from a religious text, although the technical legal use is linguistically related to the general meaning of extraction.

Istinbāṭ does not in itself guarantee that every conclusion is correct. In Al-Nisāʾ 4:83, attaining knowledge is the result understood from the complete construction and the context of proper examination.

Proposed final definition for Forqan:

N B Ṭ denotes the emergence or extraction of something previously concealed or inaccessible. In material uses, it is particularly associated with water becoming visible and being extracted from the ground. In intellectual uses, it extends to obtaining information or a result through inquiry and examination.

In the Qur’an, يَسْتَنْبِطُونَهُ means “they obtain it through examination.” This use emphasizes the effort to clarify a matter, since merely receiving a report is not yet correctly understanding it.
