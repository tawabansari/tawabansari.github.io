Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root S M W

Height, being above, elevation, superior position, and the upward direction

Important Forms from This Root

سَمَا / يَسْمُو (samā / yasmū)

samā / yasmū; rose, became high, gained elevation; rises or becomes elevated.

سُمُوّ / سَامٍ (sumuww / sāmin)

sumūw / sāmin; height, elevation, loftiness; high, lofty, elevated.

أَسْمَى (asmaā)

asmā; higher, loftier, more elevated.

سَمَاء (samāʾ)

samāʾ; sky, upward direction, the above, overhead, upper expanse.

سَمَاوَات (samāwāt)

samāwāt; heavens, heavenly levels, upper expanses.

سَمَاوِيّ / سَمِيّ (samāwiyy / samiyy)

samāwiyy / samiyy; heavenly; namesake or peer in name and rank, with lexical caution.

Central Meaning

The root S M W means height, being above, elevation, being placed higher, and the upward direction. From this field comes سَمَاء (samāʾ), meaning what is above the human being: the upward direction, the sky, or any high roof or upper expanse seen overhead.

This root is not limited to “the sky” as a physical object or location. S M W is first tied to being high, elevation, the above, and superior direction. The sky is one of the most important expressions of this height, but the root’s field is broader: anything that stands above the earth, the human being, or a lower level may approach this semantic field.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of S M W is being high and being placed in the upper direction. Height is central here, but not only numerical height. S M W may refer to spatial height, elevated rank, lofty status, or the higher relation of one thing to what is below it.

سَمَاء (samāʾ) is understood from this core. Samāʾ is what is above, or what stands over the human being. Arabic can understand anything that shades, surrounds, or rises over a person from above within the field of samāʾ: the sky, clouds, roof, or upward direction.

Thus samāʾ is originally directional. Human sight rises from the earth upward and meets an expanse that stands above. From this simple relation come broader meanings: the visible sky, the source of rain, the field of signs, and heavenly levels.

Material Image

The first material image is a human being standing on earth and looking upward. Above him are high space, cloud, roof, stars, sun, moon, and the unseen breadth above. This upward direction is tied to the field of S M W in Arabic.

The second image is growth and rising. When a plant emerges from soil and grows upward, it separates from the ground level and becomes visible. In Arabic, one may say the plant samā: it rose, grew tall, and became visible above the general level of the soil. This image shows that sumūw is not only “being above”; sometimes it is rising until something becomes visible.

The third image is a roof or overhead covering. In tent and desert life, anything placed above the head and holding what is below under it can linguistically approach the field of samāʾ. This shows that samāʾ is not always limited to the cosmic sky; sometimes it can be any overhead structure or above-relation in a given context.

Several elements gather in these images: height relative to earth, being above the human being, breadth or enclosure from above, emergence above a lower surface, and the possibility that something descends from that direction, such as rain, light, or command. Samāʾ is therefore not only a “thing”; it is a direction, relation, and higher plane.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, samāʾ refers to what is above. It may refer to the sky, cloud, rain, roof, or anything overhead. In old Arab life, the sky was not merely something to look at. It was the source of rain, the direction of stars, a guide for travel, a sign of time, and the vast roof of desert life.

The desert-dweller was constantly involved with the above: by day with the sun, by night with the moon and stars, with rain through clouds, and with navigation through heavenly bodies. For this reason, samāʾ has a wide field in early Arabic. It may mean the sky itself, rain-bearing cloud, upward direction, roof or overhead cover, or what comes from above.

Uses such as سَمَا النَّبَاتُ (samā al-nabātu), “the plant rose,” show that the root S M W is also connected with rising and becoming visible above a lower level. The use of samāʾ or samāwah for roof and overhead covering likewise shows that the main criterion is upwardness and overness, not necessarily the cosmic sky.

Qur’anic Usage

Samāʾ as Upward Direction

In some verses, samāʾ points to the upward direction or the above. For example: مَن كَانَ يَظُنُّ أَن لَّن يَنصُرَهُ اللَّهُ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ فَلْيَمْدُدْ بِسَبَبٍ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ (man kāna yaẓunnu an llan yanṣurahu allāh fī al-dunyā wālākhirah falyamdud bisababin ilā al-samāʾi); whoever thinks The God will not help him in this world and the Hereafter, let him extend a means toward the samāʾ 22:15.

In this verse, samāʾ should not be narrowed too quickly. Root-wise, samāʾ is upward direction, the above, or overhead. Some readings take it as the ceiling or overhead roof, because in Arabic samāʾ may refer to whatever is above the head. But the verse should not be forced into only one concrete image. For Forqan translation, it is better to keep the semantic field open: “a means toward the above / overhead,” then analyze the whole verse and context separately.

Samāʾ as the Visible Sky

The Qur’an often speaks of samāʾ as the visible sky or upper expanse: أَفَلَمْ يَنظُرُوا إِلَى السَّمَاءِ فَوْقَهُمْ (afalam yanẓurūā ilā al-samāʾi fawqahum); have they not looked at the sky above them? 50:6.

Here, samāʾ appears with فَوْقَهُمْ (fawqahum), “above them.” This use displays the root S M W clearly: the sky is what stands above the human being.

Samāʾ and Rain

In the Qur’an, samāʾ is often connected with water descending: وَأَنزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً (waanzala mina al-samāʾi māan); and He sent down water from the samāʾ. This expression appears in many verses. Here, samāʾ may refer to the upward direction, clouds, or the source of rain. From human and linguistic perception, water comes from above, so it descends from samāʾ.

Samāʾ should not always be equated with one fixed physical definition. In the context of rain, samāʾ may be the upper expanse or the rain-bearing cloud from which water descends.

Samāwāt and High Levels

The Qur’an speaks of the samāwāt, the heavens: سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا (sabʿa samāwātin ṭibāqanā); seven heavens in layers 67:3. It also says: اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ (allāh alladhī khalaqa sabʿa samāwātin); The God is the One who created seven heavens 65:12.

Samāwāt is the plural of samāʾ. In the Qur’an, it may point to high structures and heavenly levels. Its exact cosmological meaning should be approached carefully, because the Qur’an speaks in the language of guidance and sign, not in a simple modern scientific model. Root-wise, samāwāt means high, upper, and elevated expanses or levels in relation to the earth.

The Raising of the Sky

The Qur’an says: وَالسَّمَاءَ رَفَعَهَا وَوَضَعَ الْمِيزَانَ (wālssamāʾa rafaʿahā wawaḍaʿa al-mīzāna); and the sky He raised, and He set the measure 55:7.

Here, samāʾ appears with raising. Raising means lifting or setting high. The combination shows that in Qur’anic language, the sky is tied to height, order, and placement in an elevated level. It is not merely a physical roof; it is part of the order of creation and is connected with measure and balance.

Samāʾ and Cosmic Building

The Qur’an says: أَأَنتُمْ أَشَدُّ خَلْقًا أَمِ السَّمَاءُ بَنَاهَا (ʾaantum ashaddu khalqanā ami al-samāʾu banāhā); are you harder to create, or the sky which He built? 79:27. Then: رَفَعَ سَمْكَهَا فَسَوَّاهَا (rafaʿa samkahā fasawwāhā); He raised its height and proportioned it 79:28.

Here, samāʾ is connected with building, height, and proportioning. These verses show that samāʾ in the Qur’an can refer to a built, raised, and ordered system.

Samāʾ Opposite Arḍ

One of the Qur’an’s major pairs is the heavens and the earth. This contrast is not only physical above and below; it presents two great fields of creation: above and below, heavenly expanses and the earthly field of human dwelling. In this pair, samāʾ and arḍ form a structural couple: samāʾ is above, elevated, and the upper expanse; arḍ is below, earth, and the place of human settlement.

Samāʾ as a Field of Signs

The Qur’an repeatedly calls the human being to look at the sky: its structure, stars, rain, order, and absence of rupture. In this use, samāʾ is not only the roof overhead; it is a field of signs. By looking upward, the human being sees order, greatness, balance, and signs of The God’s power. Root-wise this is natural: samāʾ means the upward direction, and in the Qur’an that upward direction becomes a field of great signs in creation.

Samiyy in Name and Rank

Surat Maryam says: رَبُّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا فَاعْبُدْهُ وَاصْطَبِرْ لِعِبَادَتِهِ هَلْ تَعْلَمُ لَهُ سَمِيًّا (rabbu al-samāwāti wālarḍi wamā baynahumā fāʿbudhu wāṣṭabir liʿibādatihi hal taʿlamu lahu samiyyanā); Lord of the heavens and the earth and what is between them, so serve Him and be patient in His service. Do you know for Him a samiyy? 19:65.

سَمِيّ (samiyy) is usually understood as a namesake or one comparable in name. Some connect it with ism, and the root of ism is debated among lexicographers. If ism is connected with S M W, samiyy may also approach one who is equal in name, rank, or elevation. But in this entry the connection should not be treated as certain. In the verse’s context, samiyy denies any namesake, peer, or comparable one in the station of Lordship.

Samāʾ and Samāwāt

سَمَاء (samāʾ) is singular and may mean sky, upward direction, rain-bearing cloud, overhead roof, or upper expanse. سَمَاوَات (samāwāt) is plural and in the Qur’an often points to heavenly levels, structures, or upper expanses.

In translation, samāʾ and samāwāt should not always be flattened into one meaning. In the context of rain, samāʾ may mean sky, upward direction, or rain-bearing cloud. In contexts of looking and cosmic order, it is the visible sky or upper structure. In some contexts it may refer to a roof or overhead, but not every occurrence of samāʾ should be reduced to a house ceiling. Samāwāt in creation contexts means heavens, heavenly levels, or upper expanses.

Difference from Nearby Roots

AYN L W (ʿ L W) is being high, superiority, and rising. ʿUlūw emphasizes highness and superiority; S M W extends into upward direction and sky. R F AYN (R F ʿ) is the act of raising or lifting; samāʾ is the high field or high thing itself.

Ṣ AYN D (Ṣ ʿ D) is movement upward; S M W points to the state of height or upward direction. F W Q is above something, spatially or in rank; samāʾ is the upper expanse or upward field. A F Q is horizon, the edge of sight where sky and earth meet; samāʾ is the overhead and upper expanse.

AYN R SH (ʿ R SH) is throne, elevated structure of rule, or royal structure; samāʾ is upward direction and sky. S Q F is a roof, an earthly instance of something overhead; samāʾ is the wider natural, cosmic, or directional field above. S M K concerns height or structural elevation; samāʾ is the upper expanse or direction itself.

Method Note on Ism

The word ism is debated among lexicographers. Some connect it with S M W, meaning that a name raises a thing and makes it manifest. Others connect it with W S M, marking or sign-marking.

For this entry, the discussion of ism should not be placed at the center of S M W, except for a cautious note about samiyy. The focus here remains S M W as height, sky, and upward direction.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, samāʾ should not always be mechanically translated as “sky.” In contexts of looking upward, “sky” is appropriate. In rain contexts, depending on the verse, “sky,” “the above,” “the upward direction,” or “rain-bearing cloud / upper expanse” may be more precise. In contexts of creation and cosmic order, “sky” or “heavenly structure” may fit.

In al-Ḥajj 22:15, it is better to preserve “the above / overhead / upward direction” and not reduce the verse definitively to a house ceiling and suicide. In contexts of samāwāt, “heavens,” “heavenly levels,” or “upper expanses” should be considered by context. Samāʾ should not be forced into a modern scientific model. The Qur’an speaks in the language of guidance, sign, and human observation.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

سَمَا / يَسْمُو (samā / yasmū): rose, became high, gained elevation; rises, becomes elevated.

سُمُوّ (sumūw): height, elevation, loftiness.

سَمَاء (samāʾ): sky, heaven, upward direction, the above, overhead, upper expanse.

سَمَاوَات (samāwāt): heavens, heavenly levels, upper expanses.

سَامٍ / أَسْمَى (sāmin / asmā): high, lofty, elevated; higher, loftier, superior.

سَمِيّ (samiyy): namesake, peer, comparable one in name or rank; depending on context and with caution.

مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً (mina al-samāʾi māan): water from the sky; water from above; water from the upward direction, depending on context.

سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا (sabʿa samāwātin ṭibāqanā): seven heavens in layers; or seven layered heavenly levels.

بِسَبَبٍ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ (bisababin ilā al-samāʾi): a means toward the above; or a means toward the overhead.

هَلْ تَعْلَمُ لَهُ سَمِيًّا (hal taʿlamu lahu samiyyanā): do you know for Him a namesake? or do you find for Him any peer in name and rank?

Linguistic Summary

The root S M W denotes height, being above, elevation, rising, and the upward direction. Samāʾ emerges from this field: what is above the human being, the upper expanse, the sky, roof or overhead, or the upward direction. In the Qur’an, samāʾ may refer to the visible sky, the upward direction, the source of rain, a field of signs, an overhead or roof in some contexts, or cosmic structure. Samāwāt points to heavens, heavenly levels, or upper expanses.

Its difference from ʿulūw is that ʿulūw emphasizes highness and superiority, while S M W extends into sky and upward direction. Its difference from rafʿ is that rafʿ is the act of raising, while samāʾ is the high field or upper direction itself. Its difference from ṣuʿūd is that ṣuʿūd is movement upward, while samāʾ is the place or field above. The central image is this: something rises above a lower surface, or the human being looks upward from the earth; what stands above him is high, broad, and directionally elevated.
