Core meaning: revealing and presenting something, or extracting it from its place; in consultation, bringing out and receiving opinions through discussion toward a decision.

The Arabic root sh w r includes words concerned with showing and revealing, extracting something, and exchanging views.

أَشَارَ can mean pointing or indicating; شَاوَرَ means consulting someone and seeking an opinion; تَشَاوَرَ concerns mutual consultation.

Early Arabic also uses the root for tangible activities such as displaying livestock and extracting honey. These illuminate its semantic field: consultation likewise brings an unspoken opinion into the open for others to consider.

The root occurs four times in the Quran, in four forms: وَتَشَاوُرٍ in Al-Baqarah 2:233, وَشَاوِرْهُمْ in Al Imran 3:159, فَأَشَارَتْ in Maryam 19:29, and شُورَىٰ in Al-Shura 42:38.

Three occurrences concern consultation and participation in deliberation; one concerns indicating without speaking.

1. The root in early Arabic

Revealing and presenting

One established branch concerns revealing and displaying something.

Lexicons record شُرْتُ الدَّابَّةَ for presenting an animal for inspection.

Someone selling a horse, for example, may bring it out, have it move before the buyer, and demonstrate its qualities.

The animal and its qualities are thus made available for observation and assessment; it need not previously have been hidden.

مِشْوَار also names a place where animals are displayed and examined.

The point is not merely the animal's presence, but presenting it for observation and assessment.

Related words such as شَارَة and شَوْرَة describe appearance, clothing, and outward bearing.

One established sense of the family is therefore making something manifest to others' sight and understanding.

Extracting honey

Another important branch concerns extracting and harvesting something.

شَارَ الْعَسَلَ means extracting and collecting honey from its place.

اشْتَارَ الْعَسَلَ also occurs in this sense.

The person removes and harvests honey from where it is stored.

A poetic witness is attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Al-Aʿsha:

كَأَنَّ جَنِيًّا مِنَ الزَّنْجَبِيلِ
خَالَطَ فَاهَا وَأَرْيًا مَشُورًا

ka-anna janiyyan mina al-zanjabīli / khālaṭa fāhā wa-aryan mashūrā

“As though freshly gathered ginger and harvested honey had mingled in her mouth.”

The poet compares the pleasant sweetness of his beloved's mouth to ginger and honey.

أَرْي means honey; مَشُور describes honey extracted and harvested from its place.

The verse has variant readings, including بَاتَ بِفِيهَا instead of خَالَطَ فَاهَا. In either reading, مَشُور describes harvested honey.

Al-Aʿsha is a well-known pre-Islamic poet. His historical classification alone, however, does not independently establish this particular verse's date.

Together with the lexical record, this witness attests extracting honey as an established use of the root.

How these branches relate to consultation

Displaying a horse and extracting honey initially seem quite different activities.

In both, however, something becomes manifest or accessible.

Displaying a horse reveals its qualities for inspection.

Harvesting honey extracts something valuable from its place.

Consultation likewise brings out someone's opinion, knowledge, or experience for another to use.

A farmer deciding when to plant might seek advice from someone more experienced. That person shares knowledge the farmer can consider in deciding.

Discussion thus makes what was in one person's mind and experience available to another.

Some lexicons directly compare consultation to extracting honey, as though the person consulting draws useful advice from another.

This is an illuminating comparison, but should not become a claim about the certain historical origin of consultation vocabulary.

Its connection with revealing and presenting opinions is also intelligible. Lexical evidence supports both images without establishing either as the sole historical origin.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important forms in this Arabic family include:

شَارَ: extracted and harvested honey; in another use, presented something.

شَوْر: extracting and harvesting, or presenting something, according to context.

مَشُور: extracted or harvested, as with honey.

مِشْوَار: a place for displaying livestock.

أَشَارَ: pointed or indicated; with the appropriate construction, offered someone advice.

إِشَارَة: pointing, indicating, or communicating through a sign.

شَاوَرَ: consulted someone and sought an opinion about a matter.

مُشَاوَرَة: consultation and deliberation.

مُشَاوِر: a participant in consultation.

اسْتَشَارَ: sought someone's opinion or advice.

اسْتِشَارَة: seeking advice or guidance.

تَشَاوَرَ: consulted and exchanged views with one another.

تَشَاوُر: mutual consultation and exchange of views.

شُورَىٰ: consultation and deliberation, including in shared affairs.

مَشُورَة and مَشْوَرَة: consultation, seeking opinions, and proposed advice.

The Quran uses four of these forms: فَأَشَارَتْ, وَشَاوِرْهُمْ, وَتَشَاوُرٍ, and شُورَىٰ.

Their morphology matters: indicating, seeking advice, and exchanging views belong to one family but are distinct actions.

3. Quranic uses

Al-Baqarah 2:233: consultation and mutual consent

وَالْوَالِدَاتُ يُرْضِعْنَ أَوْلَادَهُنَّ حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ ۚ وَعَلَى الْمَوْلُودِ لَهُ رِزْقُهُنَّ وَكِسْوَتُهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۚ لَا تُكَلَّفُ نَفْسٌ إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا ۚ لَا تُضَارَّ وَالِدَةٌ بِوَلَدِهَا وَلَا مَوْلُودٌ لَهُ بِوَلَدِهِ ۚ وَعَلَى الْوَارِثِ مِثْلُ ذَٰلِكَ ۗ فَإِنْ أَرَادَا فِصَالًا عَنْ تَرَاضٍ مِنْهُمَا وَتَشَاوُرٍ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا ۗ وَإِنْ أَرَدْتُمْ أَنْ تَسْتَرْضِعُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا سَلَّمْتُمْ مَا آتَيْتُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۗ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ

wa-l-wālidātu yurḍiʿna awlādahunna ḥawlayni kāmilayni li-man arāda an yutimma al-raḍāʿata wa-ʿalā al-mawlūdi lahu rizquhunna wa-kiswatuhunna bi-l-maʿrūfi lā tukallafu nafsun illā wusʿahā lā tuḍārra wālidatun bi-waladihā wa-lā mawlūdun lahu bi-waladihi wa-ʿalā al-wārithi mithlu dhālika fa-in arādā fiṣālan ʿan tarāḍin minhumā wa-tashāwurin fa-lā junāḥa ʿalayhimā wa-in aradtum an tastarḍiʿū awlādakum fa-lā junāḥa ʿalaykum idhā sallamtum mā ātaytum bi-l-maʿrūfi wa-ttaqū allāha wa-ʿlamū anna allāha bi-mā taʿmalūna baṣīr

Al-Baqarah 2:233

“Mothers breastfeed their children for two complete years, for whoever wishes to complete nursing. The one for whom the child is born is responsible for their food and clothing in a fair manner. No soul is burdened beyond its capacity. No mother shall be harmed through her child, nor a father through his child; the heir bears a similar responsibility. If the two desire weaning by mutual consent and consultation, there is no blame upon them. If you wish to have your children nursed by another, there is no blame upon you when you hand over what you give in a fair manner. Be mindful of The God, and know that The God sees what you do.”

Al-Baqarah 2:233

تَشَاوُر here means mutual consultation.

It is a form VI verbal noun, describing an exchange of views between two parties in this context.

Within the discussion of completing two years of nursing, the verse addresses weaning by the parents' agreement, commonly understood to include weaning before that period is complete. It places two conditions together:

عَنْ تَرَاضٍ مِنْهُمَا وَتَشَاوُرٍ

ʿan tarāḍin minhumā wa-tashāwur

Al-Baqarah 2:233

That is, by the consent of both and through consultation.

تَرَاضٍ expresses mutual consent; تَشَاوُرٍ expresses consultation and an exchange of views.

These concepts are related but distinct.

Two people might consent without examining a matter properly, or discuss it without yet reaching agreement.

The verse brings consent and consultation together.

A father might wish nursing to end earlier because of living costs, while the mother sees matters differently because of the child's condition, her experience of caring for the child, or feeding needs.

Giving both parents an opportunity to express their views and consider the child's needs helps them reach an informed decision and mutual consent; it does not itself guarantee a correct outcome.

Consultation here goes beyond merely hearing another person: it is an exchange concerning a decision relevant to both parties.

تَشَاوُر alone does not include final consent, which makes the separate word تَرَاضٍ significant.

This verse addresses nursing specifically. It shows that Quranic consultation includes family decisions and is not restricted to government or public leadership.

Al Imran 3:159: consultation before a decision

فَبِمَا رَحْمَةٍ مِنَ اللَّهِ لِنْتَ لَهُمْ ۖ وَلَوْ كُنْتَ فَظًّا غَلِيظَ الْقَلْبِ لَانْفَضُّوا مِنْ حَوْلِكَ ۖ فَاعْفُ عَنْهُمْ وَاسْتَغْفِرْ لَهُمْ وَشَاوِرْهُمْ فِي الْأَمْرِ ۖ فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى اللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُتَوَكِّلِينَ

fa-bi-mā raḥmatin mina allāhi linta lahum wa-law kunta faẓẓan ghalīẓa al-qalbi la-nfaḍḍū min ḥawlika fa-ʿfu ʿanhum wa-staghfir lahum wa-shāwirhum fī al-amri fa-idhā ʿazamta fa-tawakkal ʿalā allāhi inna allāha yuḥibbu al-mutawakkilīn

Āl ʿImrān 3:159

“By mercy from The God, you were gentle with them. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have dispersed from around you. Pardon them, seek forgiveness for them, and consult them in the matter. Then, when you have resolved, trust in The God. Indeed, The God loves those who trust in Him.”

Al Imran 3:159

شَاوِرْهُمْ is a form III imperative.

شَاوِرْ means “consult”; هُمْ refers to those whom the Prophet is instructed to consult.

Unlike Al-Baqarah 2:233, this is not a joint parental decision: the command addresses the Prophet.

This matters because the Quran calls for consultation even when addressing the Prophet.

Consultation and gentle conduct

The verse begins by mentioning the Prophet's gentleness.

It then says that harshness and hardness of heart would have driven people away.

Three commands follow: pardon them, seek forgiveness for them, and consult them.

Consultation is thus situated within human relationships marked by forgiveness and gentleness.

Someone who humiliates others, harshly interrupts them, or denies them a chance to speak may formally convene a consultation, but that conduct conflicts with the relational setting described here.

Meaningful consultation allows others to express views and gives those views consideration.

Gentleness and forgiveness are nevertheless contextual features accompanying the command, rather than the lexical meaning of شاور itself.

فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ: reaching a decision

The verse continues:

فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى اللَّهِ

fa-idhā ʿazamta fa-tawakkal ʿalā allāh

Āl ʿImrān 3:159

“Then, when you have resolved, trust in The God.”

عَزَمْتَ comes from ʿ z m and concerns firm resolve.

The verse thus distinguishes consultation from deciding.

Consultation provides an opportunity to hear, examine, and weigh views; ultimately, the matter may require a decision and action.

عَزَمْتَ is singular and addresses the Prophet.

This identifies the person addressed about resolve, but does not alone establish or rule out a particular voting procedure or the binding force of consultation's outcome.

Nor does the singular عَزَمْتَ authorize making the preceding شَاوِرْهُمْ ineffective.

The verse expresses both consultation with others and subsequent resolve.

Final responsibility cannot be used to reduce consultation to an empty performance; equally, شاور alone does not establish that every decision must follow a majority vote.

Al-Shura 42:38: consultation as a feature of collective life

وَالَّذِينَ اسْتَجَابُوا لِرَبِّهِمْ وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَأَمْرُهُمْ شُورَىٰ بَيْنَهُمْ وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنْفِقُونَ

wa-lladhīna istajābū li-rabbihim wa-aqāmū al-ṣalāta wa-amruhum shūrā baynahum wa-mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn

Al-Shūrā 42:38

“And those who have responded to their Lord, established salat, whose affairs are conducted by consultation among themselves, and who spend from what We have provided them.”

Al-Shura 42:38

شُورَىٰ here means consultation and deliberation.

وَأَمْرُهُمْ شُورَىٰ بَيْنَهُمْ is a nominal sentence.

أَمْرُهُمْ names their affair or affairs; شُورَىٰ describes their consultative character; بَيْنَهُمْ emphasizes the relationship among them.

The difference from Al Imran 3:159 matters.

In Al Imran, the Prophet is commanded to consult.

In Al-Shura, consultation characterizes those who have responded to their Lord.

It appears alongside establishing salat and spending from divine provision.

The verse therefore describes how they conduct their affairs, rather than merely an incidental act or consultation on one isolated issue.

بَيْنَهُمْ: among themselves

بَيْنَهُمْ is especially significant here.

Consultation concerns relationships among people. Applied to shared decisions, this suggests that others' interests and responsibilities should not be displaced by one person's wishes alone.

If several families share irrigation water, decisions about its distribution directly affect all their lives.

One person diverting all the water to private fields without hearing others may benefit personally while disregarding their rights.

When those with rights and those affected discuss available water, field requirements, and distribution, the decision can draw on a fuller understanding of their shared situation.

This illustrates consultation's social use.

شورى alone does not specify every rule of water distribution, ownership, or voting; these involve rights, justice, and actual circumstances.

Does شُورَىٰ name a specific political system?

Today, shura can name an assembly, administrative institution, or representative council.

In this verse, however, شُورَىٰ directly describes consultation among people rather than a political institution with a specified structure.

The phrase does not prescribe membership numbers, selection methods, terms of office, voting thresholds, or institutional form.

A particular political structure therefore cannot be derived solely from its lexical meaning.

The absence of those details does not make consultation unimportant.

The verse presents it as a commendable feature of believers' collective life. It concerns their handling of shared affairs and goes beyond a ceremonial label.

Maryam 19:29: indicating without speaking

فَأَشَارَتْ إِلَيْهِ ۖ قَالُوا كَيْفَ نُكَلِّمُ مَنْ كَانَ فِي الْمَهْدِ صَبِيًّا

fa-ashārat ilayhi qālū kayfa nukallimu man kāna fī al-mahdi ṣabiyyā

Maryam 19:29

“Then she pointed to him. They said: How can we speak to one who is a child in the cradle?”

Maryam 19:29

فَأَشَارَتْ comes from the form IV verb أَشَارَ.

Mary is its subject, and إِلَيْهِ refers to the child with her.

The preceding verses describe her people's questions and reproaches. She then points to the child, prompting their question about speaking to a child in the cradle.

أَشَارَتْ here does not mean consulting or proposing an opinion.

Mary's gesture directs their attention to the child.

The verse does not specify a hand, head, or eye movement. What is clear is communication through a gesture.

This shows that sh w r includes showing and indicating, beyond spoken consultation.

أَشَارَ إِلَيْهِ and أَشَارَ عَلَيْهِ

An Arabic preposition can help identify a verb's contextual sense.

أَشَارَ إِلَيْهِ usually means pointing toward someone or something.

أَشَارَ عَلَيْهِ بِأَمْرٍ can mean recommending an action or offering advice.

Someone might point at a route to show the way, or advise a friend to follow that route to a destination.

The first action indicates the route.

The second offers a recommendation or guidance.

In Maryam 19:29, فَأَشَارَتْ إِلَيْهِ supports the sense of pointing toward the child.

أَشَارَ should therefore not always be translated as consulting or voting.

4. Consultation, consent, and decision

The Quran's three consultation passages present three different situations.

Al-Baqarah 2:233 concerns a joint parental decision about weaning, requiring consultation alongside mutual consent.

In Al Imran 3:159, the Prophet is commanded to consult others, followed by mention of resolve.

Al-Shura 42:38 presents consultation as characteristic of believers' shared affairs.

Consultation is thus a linguistic and social practice used in different situations, without necessarily imposing an identical decision procedure in every case.

Parents considering weaning, for example, are to reach mutual consent because the verse expressly includes that condition.

In Al Imran 3:159, the command to consult is followed by a singular verb of resolve addressed to the Prophet.

Al-Shura 42:38 does not separately describe the final decision procedure.

One should therefore not derive a universal rule from sh w r requiring unanimity, majority voting, or assignment of the decision to one individual in every setting.

What these uses share is the inclusion of views and deliberation in addressing affairs.

Consultation does not itself guarantee a correct outcome.

People may consult without sufficient information, or under the influence of prejudice and self-interest.

It is a means of drawing on others' knowledge and judgment. Results still require assessment against truth, justice, knowledge of the matter, and the responsibility of those deciding.

5. Comparison with related roots

sh w r and r ʾ y

رَأْي means an opinion or view, sometimes the outcome of reflection and judgment.

شُورَىٰ concerns the process of presenting and exchanging views.

Someone may hold a definite view without sharing it.

Having an opinion alone is not consultation.

Consultation involves presenting and examining views in relation to other people.

An opinion can thus be the subject of consultation, while consultation names the deliberative process.

sh w r and n ṣ ḥ

نُصْح concerns goodwill, sincerity, and seeking another's welfare.

شُورَىٰ concerns consultation and exchanging opinions.

Advice can be well intended, but goodwill is not inherent in every use of sh w r.

Someone may offer an opinion based on incorrect information or influenced by personal interest.

Consultation and goodwill may accompany one another without being identical.

Sound consultation needs honesty and goodwill, but شُورَىٰ should not be defined solely as benevolent counsel.

sh w r and ʾ m r

أَمْر can mean an affair, subject, or matter requiring action or decision.

فِي الْأَمْرِ in Al Imran 3:159 identifies the subject of consultation.

أَمْرُهُمْ in Al-Shura 42:38 similarly refers to their affairs.

أمر can therefore name what is discussed, while شورى describes deliberation about it.

Elsewhere أَمْر can also mean a command or instruction.

These senses should not be conflated: a matter discussed need not be an instruction issued by another person.

sh w r and r ḍ w

تَرَاضٍ belongs to r ḍ w and means mutual consent.

تَشَاوُر concerns a mutual exchange of opinions.

The words appear together in Al-Baqarah 2:233.

Their combination shows two distinct aspects of a shared decision.

People may consult without agreeing, or accept a proposal without adequate examination.

The weaning passage includes both.

تَشَاوُر is therefore not always synonymous with تَرَاضٍ.

sh w r and ʿ z m

عَزْم concerns firm and serious resolve.

مُشَاوَرَة concerns consultation and considering opinions.

In Al Imran 3:159, consultation precedes فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ.

The sequence distinguishes related acts of deliberation and resolve.

Consultation can clarify options; deciding selects a course and leads toward action.

This sequence does not imply that every human decision becomes valid only after a formal consultation meeting. The command's subject and scope must be understood in context.

sh w r and d l l

دَلَّ concerns guiding and indicating.

أَشَارَ can likewise show something to another person.

Pointing is a particular form of communication, potentially involving the hand, eyes, head, or another sign.

Guidance may instead use speech, signs, conduct, or detailed explanation.

In Maryam 19:29, Mary points to the child, and her people understand the gesture.

Pointing can convey meaning, but not every form of indication is a nonverbal gesture.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

وَتَشَاوُرٍ — Al-Baqarah 2:233

Brief root meaning: revealing, presenting, and extracting; in discussion, offering and receiving opinions.

Meaning in this verse: mutual consultation between parents about weaning.

Suggested rendering: “and consultation.”

The phrase may be translated:

“If the two desire weaning by mutual consent and consultation, there is no blame upon them.”

وَشَاوِرْهُمْ — Al Imran 3:159

Brief root meaning: presenting opinions, seeking views, and consulting.

Meaning in this verse: a command to the Prophet to consult them about the matter.

Suggested rendering: “and consult them.”

فِي الْأَمْرِ identifies the subject of consultation: “matter” comes from ʾ m r, not sh w r.

فَأَشَارَتْ — Maryam 19:29

Brief root meaning: showing and revealing; in another use, presenting an opinion.

Meaning in this verse: Mary directed others' attention to the child through a gesture.

Suggested rendering: “then she pointed.”

The phrase may be translated:

“Then she pointed to him.”

شُورَىٰ — Al-Shura 42:38

Brief root meaning: offering and receiving views; consultation and deliberation.

Meaning in this verse: the consultative character of believers' affairs among themselves.

Suggested rendering: “consultation.”

The phrase may be translated:

“And their affairs are conducted by consultation among themselves.”

This expresses the consultative character of their affairs naturally while preserving the nominal description.

7. Conclusion

The Arabic root sh w r relates to revealing, presenting, and extracting something.

Its physical uses include displaying livestock and extracting honey.

In communication, أَشَارَ can describe indicating by a gesture.

In social interaction, شَاوَرَ, تَشَاوَرَ, and شُورَىٰ concern presenting, receiving, and considering opinions.

The Quran uses both indication and consultation.

In Maryam 19:29, Mary's silent gesture draws attention to the child.

Al-Baqarah 2:233 places consultation alongside mutual consent in the parents' decision about weaning.

Al Imran 3:159 commands the Prophet to consult others and then speaks of resolve and trust in God.

Al-Shura 42:38 presents consultation as a feature of believers' shared affairs.

Quranic shura is therefore not merely the name of an assembly or political institution, nor does it mean voting on every matter. Its central sense is deliberation and participation in presenting and considering views; details of reaching a decision must be examined in each verse's construction and context.

Final definition of sh w r:

“Revealing and presenting something, or extracting it from its place. In communication, indicating and conveying meaning through a gesture; in social interaction, offering, receiving, and examining opinions through consultation. Shura names this consultative process without itself specifying a particular voting procedure or governmental structure.”
