Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root M SH Y

Walking, stepping, moving upon the earth; and more broadly, moving forward in a path, manner, behavior, or way of life

Important Forms from This Root

مَشَى / يَمْشِي (mashā / yamshī)

mashā / yamshī; walked, walks, stepped, or moved along a path.

مَشْي (mashy)

mashy; walking, step-by-step movement, or manner of walking.

مَاشٍ (māshin)

māshin; one who walks, one who is in motion.

مَشَّاء (mashshāʾ)

mashshāʾ; one who walks much, moves about constantly, or repeatedly carries something through social movement.

Method Note

The root M SH Y first appears simple: walking. But in the Qur’an, walking is not only the movement of feet. Sometimes it describes bodily movement upon the earth. Sometimes it describes the way a person moves in society. Sometimes it is walking with light. Sometimes it exposes the inability of false objects of worship to move or act.

Direction, bodily manner, and quality of movement matter in this root. A human being may walk with humility, walk with arrogance, stop in darkness, move with light, or stride in the earth with pride and corruption. M SH Y is therefore not mere relocation. It reveals a person’s relation to earth, people, and the path of life.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of M SH Y is gradual, step-by-step movement. Unlike flying, running, leaping, or falling, mashy is continuous, earth-bound, and staged. A person covers a path through repeated steps. Because of this gradual quality, the root can also speak of behavior, method, and way of life.

One who يَمْشِي (yamshī) is not only moving from one point to another. He is proceeding in a path. That path may be physical, like walking in the marketplace or on the earth; or it may be ethical and perceptive, like walking with light or walking in arrogance.

Material Image

The material image is a human being placing his feet on the earth and moving forward through repeated steps. The earth is the ground, the feet are the instrument, and direction gives meaning. Without direction, walking becomes scattered motion. With a clear path, walking becomes purposeful movement.

The quality of walking also carries meaning. Calm walking, arrogant walking, fearful walking, or walking with light each reveals something inward. In mashy, the body is not only a tool of movement. It becomes a language of character and condition.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, مَشَى (mashā) is used for the walking of human beings and animals, but it also expands into the progress of an affair, the circulation of a matter, or something taking effect. When an affair “walks,” it has begun to move through its course.

مَشَّاء (mashshāʾ) can describe someone who moves frequently among people, carries news, spreads speech, or continually moves in some activity. In M SH Y, bodily motion and social motion are not fully separate. A person who walks also enters fields of relation, effect, news, trade, invitation, or corruption.

Qur’anic Usage

Walking on the Earth with Humility

In وَعِبَادُ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الَّذِينَ يَمْشُونَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ هَوْنًا (waʿibādu al-raḥmāni alladhīna yamshūna ʿalā al-arḍi hawnan), the servants of the Most Merciful are those who walk upon the earth gently and humbly 25:63. This shows that walking is not merely physical movement. The quality of walking reveals the person’s inner condition.

هَوْنًا (hawnan) means their movement is gentle, light, free of arrogance, and free of harm. They walk on the earth, but they do not turn the earth into a stage for pride. Mashy here becomes social ethics: how do you appear on the earth, with the weight of arrogance or with calm humility?

The Prohibition of Arrogant Walking

In وَلَا تَمْشِ فِي الْأَرْضِ مَرَحًا (walā tamshi fī al-arḍi maraḥan), and also in Luqmān’s counsel, walking with مَرَح (maraḥ) is forbidden 17:37 and 31:18. Maraḥ here is proud, showy, uncontrolled movement.

These verses show that the body can carry arrogance. Pride is not only in thought. It appears in voice, face, gaze, and even walking. The Qur’an reads walking as a sign of ethics. One who struts in the earth with pride does not understand his place.

Walking with Light

In أَوَمَن كَانَ مَيْتًا فَأَحْيَيْنَاهُ وَجَعَلْنَا لَهُ نُورًا يَمْشِي بِهِ فِي النَّاسِ (awaman kāna maytan fa-aḥyaynāhu wajaʿalnā lahu nūran yamshī bihi fī al-nās), someone who had been in a state of guidance-death is given life, and a light is placed for him by which he walks among people 6:122. Here walking is not merely the movement of feet. It is moving in life with discernment.

The light allows him to move among people, choose, distinguish, and avoid being trapped in darkness. يَمْشِي بِهِ (yamshī bihi) means he travels the path of life and relationships by means of that light. The verse joins life, light, and walking: true revival must become illuminated movement among people.

Walking in the Parable of Light and Darkness

In كُلَّمَا أَضَاءَ لَهُم مَّشَوْا فِيهِ وَإِذَا أَظْلَمَ عَلَيْهِمْ قَامُوا (kullamā aḍāʾa lahum mashaw fīhi wa-idhā aẓlama ʿalayhim qāmū), whenever lightning gives them light, they walk in it; when darkness covers them, they stand still 2:20. This is one of the clearest uses of mashy in a scene of guidance and confusion.

Their walking depends on momentary light. They do not possess stable light; they move through unstable flashes. When the flash stops, their movement stops. Mashy here is the unstable movement of someone who has no inner path of light and advances only by occasional outside brightness.

The Walking of Living Creatures

In وَاللَّهُ خَلَقَ كُلَّ دَابَّةٍ مِّن مَّاءٍ فَمِنْهُم مَّن يَمْشِي عَلَىٰ بَطْنِهِ وَمِنْهُم مَّن يَمْشِي عَلَىٰ رِجْلَيْنِ وَمِنْهُم مَّن يَمْشِي عَلَىٰ أَرْبَعٍ (wallāhu khalaqa kulla dābbatin min māʾin faminhum man yamshī ʿalā baṭnihi waminhum man yamshī ʿalā rijlayni waminhum man yamshī ʿalā arbaʿ), the Qur’an describes the motion of living creatures: some move on their bellies, some on two legs, and some on four 24:45.

This verse shows that mashy is not only human. Every living creature has a structure for movement. Moving on the belly, on two legs, or on four all belong to the field of M SH Y. The root therefore covers bodily earth-bound movement across living creatures.

The Inability of False Objects of Worship to Walk

In أَلَهُمْ أَرْجُلٌ يَمْشُونَ بِهَا (alahum arjulun yamshūna bihā), the Qur’an asks about false objects of worship: do they have feet with which they walk? 7:195. The question exposes their inability at the most basic level of agency. If something cannot even move, how can it be lord, ally, or helper?

Here, walking becomes a minimum sign of life and effect. If something has feet but does not walk, or has no power of movement at all, it cannot possess independent power. This use connects M SH Y with the critique of fabricated objects of worship.

Mashshāʾ Binamīm: Movement for Spreading Corruption

In هَمَّازٍ مَّشَّاءٍ بِنَمِيمٍ (hammāzin mashshāʾin binamīm), the phrase مَشَّاءٍ بِنَمِيمٍ (mashshāʾin binamīm) describes one who moves much among people in order to spread slander and corrupt relations 68:11.

Here, mashy is physical movement, but its purpose is social corruption. Mashshāʾ shows repetition and intensity: someone repeatedly moves from one side to another, carrying speech in order to damage relationships. Human social movement can become a tool of repair or a tool of corruption.

The Messenger Walking and the Humanity of Messengers

In وَقَالُوا مَالِ هَٰذَا الرَّسُولِ يَأْكُلُ الطَّعَامَ وَيَمْشِي فِي الْأَسْوَاقِ (waqālū māli hādhā al-rasūli yaʾkulu al-ṭaʿāma wayamshī fī al-aswāq), the opponents object that this Messenger eats food and walks in the markets 25:7. For them, walking in the markets was a sign of ordinary human life.

The Qur’an presents this objection as weak reasoning. The Messenger is a human being who moves in real life, eats food, contacts people, and is present in society. Walking in the markets is a sign of his earth-bound human presence, not a defect in messengership.

Difference from Nearby Words

S Y R is movement along a route and is often broader than mashy. It can point to travel, historical movement, or movement through the earth. M SH Y more strongly suggests step-by-step bodily or behavioral movement.

M R R means passing by or passing through; it focuses more on crossing or going past something. Mashy itself shows the stepping movement. S ʿ Y is striving and movement with intention and effort. Every striving may include movement, but mashy does not always carry the weight of intense effort.

Harwalah is quick walking or light running. ʿAdw can mean running or hostility, depending on context. Mashy is calmer and more general. Dhahāb is going, with the direction of departure emphasized; mashy shows the quality of movement.

Translation Method

In translating M SH Y, “walking” is usually correct, but some verses require the quality and field of movement to be shown. يَمْشُونَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ هَوْنًا (yamshūna ʿalā al-arḍi hawnan) is not merely “they walk.” It means they move in the earth humbly and without harm. يَمْشِي بِهِ فِي النَّاسِ (yamshī bihi fī al-nās) is not merely walking while carrying light; it is moving through human life by means of light.

Similarly, مَشَّاءٍ بِنَمِيمٍ (mashshāʾin binamīm) should not be flattened into “one who walks much with slander.” The point is that he spreads slander through repeated social movement. In verses about false objects of worship, يَمْشُونَ بِهَا (yamshūna bihā) should show power of movement and agency.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

يَمْشُونَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ هَوْنًا (yamshūna ʿalā al-arḍi hawnan): they walk upon the earth gently and humbly; they move in the earth without arrogance or harm.

وَلَا تَمْشِ فِي الْأَرْضِ مَرَحًا (walā tamshi fī al-arḍi maraḥan): do not walk in the earth with prideful exuberance; do not stride in the earth with display and arrogance.

نُورًا يَمْشِي بِهِ فِي النَّاسِ (nūran yamshī bihi fī al-nās): a light by which he walks among people; a light by which he moves through human life.

كُلَّمَا أَضَاءَ لَهُم مَّشَوْا فِيهِ (kullamā aḍāʾa lahum mashaw fīhi): whenever it gave them light, they walked in it; whenever they found brightness, they advanced a few steps in it.

مَن يَمْشِي عَلَىٰ بَطْنِهِ (man yamshī ʿalā baṭnihi): one that moves on its belly; a creature that crawls or moves upon its belly.

أَلَهُمْ أَرْجُلٌ يَمْشُونَ بِهَا (alahum arjulun yamshūna bihā): do they have feet with which they walk? do they have any power of movement and effect?

مَشَّاءٍ بِنَمِيمٍ (mashshāʾin binamīm): one who moves about spreading slander; one whose repeated movement carries slander between people.

يَمْشِي فِي الْأَسْوَاقِ (yamshī fī al-aswāq): he walks in the markets; he moves among the people’s public life.

Summary

The root M SH Y in the Qur’an is not only bodily walking, though it begins from that image. Mashy is step-by-step movement upon the earth, but in the Qur’an it also becomes the quality of human presence in the world. A person may walk humbly, walk arrogantly, move among people with light, or use repeated movement to spread corruption.

In Forqan’s reading, mashy is visible movement along a path. That path may be physical and earthly, or social and ethical. The quality of a person’s steps reveals how he understands his relation to earth, people, light, and truth.
