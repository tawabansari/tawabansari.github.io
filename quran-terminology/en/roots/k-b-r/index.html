Foundational Meaning

The root ك ب ر fundamentally means greatness, being great, becoming great, regarding something as great, or seeing something as great. This greatness may be real, age-related, physical, ranked, ethical, spiritual, or value-based.

In another branch, however, the same root reaches self-magnification, superiority-seeking, and placing oneself above truth. Therefore, ك ب ر is not inherently negative. When it is used for The God, truth, the Hereafter, signs, or weighty matters, it indicates real greatness or majesty.

But when forms such as اسْتَكْبَرَ or takabbur appear in relation to Iblis, Pharaoh, rebellious peoples, or deniers of the signs, the meaning is false self-magnification, arrogance, and refusal to humble oneself before truth.

Important Forms

كَبُرَ in Form I means to become great, to be great, to become difficult or heavy, or to become great in size or significance. In some verses, كَبُرَ عَلَيْكَ means something became heavy or difficult for you.

كَبِير is an adjective meaning great, immense, important, or weighty. Depending on context, this greatness may be positive, negative, or merely descriptive, as in إِثْمٌ كَبِير, فَضْلٌ كَبِير, or when The God is called الْكَبِير.

أَكْبَر is the comparative or superlative form and means greater or greatest. In relation to The God, greatness must not be understood in a physical or numerical scale.

كَبِّرْ in Form II means to magnify and honor, as in وَرَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرْ and وَكَبِّرْهُ تَكْبِيرًا. تَكْبِير means magnifying The God and declaring His greatness.

اسْتَكْبَرَ is one of the most important Qur'anic forms of this root. It means seeking greatness, imagining oneself great, taking oneself as higher, and refusing to accept truth through superiority-seeking.

مُتَكَبِّر, when used for a human being, means one who falsely claims greatness for himself. But when used for The God, as in الْعَزِيزُ الْجَبَّارُ الْمُتَكَبِّرُ, it does not mean self-conceit. It means the One who possesses true الكبرياء, who is beyond every deficiency and beyond every rival or partner.

كِبْرِيَاء points to superior greatness, majesty, and rank. When this meaning belongs to The God, it is true and fitting. When a human being or taghut seeks it for himself, it becomes arrogance and transgression.

Linguistic Note

The semantic center of ك ب ر turns around greatness, but the Qur'an carefully distinguishes true greatness from claimed greatness. Not every greatness is blameworthy. The issue is where the greatness comes from, where it is placed, and what it stands against.

The greatness of The God is true. To magnify The God is to place Him above every power, name, standard, judgment, and created imagination. But the istikbar of a creature means that a human being or being places himself in a station that is not his right, makes himself the measure of truth, or refuses humility before a clear sign, command, or truth.

So in ك ب ر, the danger is not greatness itself. The danger is the displacement of measure. Takbir of The God returns majesty to its true owner. Istikbar of a creature attributes greatness to the self in a way that does not fit poverty, limitation, and createdness.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is something seen as larger than ordinary measure: a large body, advanced age, a heavy event, a difficult task, or an elevated rank.

In the negative branch, the material image is a human being who inflates himself, raises himself, stretches his neck, and refuses to lower himself before truth. In istikbar, the person is not merely great; he sees himself as greater than his real place.

It is like someone standing on an imaginary step and looking down from there upon truth or upon people. This image is clear in the story of Iblis: he took the material of creation as his standard, saw himself as better, and refused the command of The God.

Older and Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic, كبر is used for greatness in size, age, status, importance, and difficulty. كبير may mean older, greater, more important, or more majestic. كبر الأمر means the matter became great, heavy, or difficult. استكبر means he saw himself as great, sought greatness, or refused out of self-magnification.

In a later expanded version, a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or early Arabic usage may be added. Without a clear witness, no specific historical or poetic claim should be forced into the text.

Qur'anic Usage

The use of this root in the Qur'an is broad. Sometimes it refers to magnifying The God, sometimes to the real or relative greatness of a matter, sometimes to heaviness and difficulty, and sometimes to arrogance and false superiority.

For example, in the verse about wine and gambling:

قُلْ فِيهِمَا إِثْمٌ كَبِيرٌ

Say: In both of them is great sin. Al-Baqarah 2:219

Here كَبِير refers to moral greatness and the weight of consequence, not arrogance. Therefore, not every derivative of ك ب ر should be translated as pride.

The same verse says:

وَإِثْمُهُمَا أَكْبَرُ مِنْ نَفْعِهِمَا

And their sin is greater than their benefit. Al-Baqarah 2:219

أَكْبَر here is comparative: greater, weightier, and more significant in moral evaluation.

Sometimes كبر means that something became heavy or difficult:

وَإِنْ كَانَ كَبُرَ عَلَيْكَ إِعْرَاضُهُمْ

And if their turning away has become heavy upon you... Al-An'am 6:35

Here the root ك ب ر is not about self-magnification. It refers to the heaviness and difficulty of a situation for the Prophet.

Takbir of The God

One clear branch of this root is magnifying The God:

وَرَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرْ

And magnify your Lord. Al-Muddaththir 74:3

Here كَبِّرْ does not mean making The God great, because The God is great in Himself. Rather, it means declaring, recognizing, and manifesting His greatness. Through takbir, the human being places The God above every thing in understanding and orientation.

Another verse says:

وَكَبِّرْهُ تَكْبِيرًا

And magnify Him with complete magnification. Al-Isra 17:111

This takbir is the opposite of human istikbar. Takbir of The God means returning greatness to its true owner. Istikbar means that the human being wrongfully seizes greatness for himself or another.

Al-Kabir and Transcendent Theology

One of the most important derivatives of this root is الْكَبِير. When it is used for The God, it must be understood with transcendent precision. In ordinary usage, كبير may refer to age, size, rank, sin, reward, or importance. But when the Qur'an calls The God الْكَبِير, it is not speaking of physical, spatial, volumetric, numerical, or comparative greatness.

The God is not "larger than other large things" within a shared scale. Rather, He is the possessor of true, absolute, and unmatched greatness.

ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ الْحَقُّ وَأَنَّ مَا يَدْعُونَ مِنْ دُونِهِ هُوَ الْبَاطِلُ وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْكَبِيرُ

That is because The God is The Truth, and what they call upon besides Him is falsehood, and The God is The Most High, The Truly Great. Al-Hajj 22:62

In this verse, الْكَبِير comes with الْعَلِيّ. الْعَلِيّ shows absolute elevation and superiority, while الْكَبِير shows true greatness and majesty. This greatness is not of the same kind as the greatness of created things.

عَالِمُ الْغَيْبِ وَالشَّهَادَةِ الْكَبِيرُ الْمُتَعَالِ

Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, The Truly Great, The Exalted beyond every comparison. Ar-Ra'd 13:9

Here الْكَبِير is joined with الْمُتَعَال. الْمُتَعَال shows that the greatness of The God is above creaturely comparisons.

وَلَهُ الْكِبْرِيَاءُ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ

And to Him belongs الكبرياء in the heavens and the earth, and He is The Unassailable, The Wise. Al-Jathiyah 45:37

الْكِبْرِيَاء means superior greatness, majesty, and a rank that truly belongs to The God. When a created being claims such greatness for himself, that claim becomes istikbar.

هُوَ اللَّهُ الَّذِي لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ... الْعَزِيزُ الْجَبَّارُ الْمُتَكَبِّرُ

He is The God, the One besides whom there is no deity... The Unassailable, The Restorer/Compeller, The Possessor of Supreme Greatness. Al-Hashr 59:23

الْمُتَكَبِّر for The God does not mean self-conceit. Self-conceit only applies when a being claims more than its true measure. The God has no limited measure to exceed. Therefore, الْمُتَكَبِّر for The God means the Possessor of الكبرياء, above deficiency, and above every partner, equal, need, or limitation.

Istikbar Against the Signs of The God

Another major branch is istikbar against the signs of The God:

وَالَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا وَاسْتَكْبَرُوا عَنْهَا أُولَٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ

And those who denied Our signs and acted arrogantly toward them, those are the companions of the Fire. Al-A'raf 7:36

In this verse, istikbar is joined with denial of the signs. اسْتَكْبَرُوا عَنْهَا means they did not humble themselves before the signs, took themselves as above them, and refused to accept truth.

The story of Iblis is one of the clearest examples of istikbar:

إِلَّا إِبْلِيسَ أَبَىٰ وَاسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ الْكَافِرِينَ

Except Iblis; he refused, acted arrogantly, and was among the kafirun. Al-Baqarah 2:34

Three layers stand together in this verse: أَبَىٰ means he refused; اسْتَكْبَرَ means he refused from the position of self-magnification; and the result is being among the kafirun.

إِلَّا إِبْلِيسَ اسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ الْكَافِرِينَ

Except Iblis; he acted arrogantly and was among the kafirun. Sad 38:74

Then comes the divine question:

أَسْتَكْبَرْتَ أَمْ كُنْتَ مِنَ الْعَالِينَ

Did you act arrogantly, or were you among the exalted? Sad 38:75

This question exposes the root of the error: did you take yourself as great? Did you claim a rank that was not yours? Here ك ب ر shifts from greatness into false self-magnification.

In Surat al-Hijr, the logic of Iblis is made clearer:

قَالَ لَمْ أَكُنْ لِأَسْجُدَ لِبَشَرٍ خَلَقْتَهُ مِنْ صَلْصَالٍ مِنْ حَمَإٍ مَسْنُونٍ

He said: I was not one to prostrate for a human being whom You created from dry clay, from dark molded mud. Al-Hijr 15:33

Iblis did not take the command of The God as the measure of value. He took the material of creation as his measure. He looked at clay, but did not accept the command of The God and the honor given to Adam. This is the movement of istikbar: placing oneself in a higher seat of judgment and measuring the command of truth by one's own standard.

Social and Political Istikbar

Pharaoh is a social and political example of istikbar:

وَاسْتَكْبَرَ هُوَ وَجُنُودُهُ فِي الْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ

And he and his soldiers acted arrogantly in the earth without right. Al-Qasas 28:39

The phrase بِغَيْرِ الْحَقّ is crucial. Not every greatness is condemned; condemnation comes when the seeking of greatness is without right. Pharaoh had power, but he placed power where truth should be and turned it into superiority-seeking.

About Ad, the Qur'an says:

فَأَمَّا عَادٌ فَاسْتَكْبَرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ وَقَالُوا مَنْ أَشَدُّ مِنَّا قُوَّةً

As for Ad, they acted arrogantly in the earth without right and said: Who is stronger than us in power? Fussilat 41:15

Here istikbar is joined with physical and social power-measuring. They took their power as the measure of truth, but the Qur'an shows that created power is not the final standard before The God.

Istikbar also appears against worship of The God:

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِي سَيَدْخُلُونَ جَهَنَّمَ دَاخِرِينَ

Indeed, those who act arrogantly away from My worship will enter Hell humbled. Ghafir 40:60

Here istikbar is refusal of servitude. The human being sees himself as though he has no need for prayer, humility, and worship. The result of this istikbar is دَاخِرِينَ: the one who raised himself is finally brought low and humbled.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

كَبِير in إِثْمٌ كَبِير is not the same as اسْتَكْبَرَ in the story of Iblis. The first shows the greatness of a matter; the second shows false self-magnification.

أَكْبَر in comparisons such as إِثْمُهُمَا أَكْبَرُ مِنْ نَفْعِهِمَا belongs to comparative weight and size of meaning. But اسْتَكْبَرَ concerns the ethical state of an agent who takes himself as higher.

كَبِّرْ regarding The God means magnifying the One who truly possesses greatness. But اسْتَكْبَرَ regarding a creature means claiming greatness or superiority without right. Takbir of The God returns majesty to its proper place; istikbar of a creature displaces the self and truth.

الْكَبِير regarding The God is also not identical with ordinary uses of كَبِير. Regarding The God, greatness must be understood transcendentally: not physical, spatial, numerical, or comparative at the level of created things.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In اسْتَكْبَرُوا عَنْهَا, the preposition عَنْ matters. It shows that they turned away from the signs of The God and took themselves as above accepting them.

In اسْتَكْبَرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ, the phrase فِي الْأَرْض shows the social and earthly field of istikbar, while بِغَيْرِ الْحَقّ clarifies that this seeking of greatness is without right.

In يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِي, عَنْ again shows arrogant distancing, as though a person sees himself as above worship.

In وَرَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرْ, the object of magnification is The God. This is not creating greatness, but confessing and declaring true greatness.

In الْعَلِيُّ الْكَبِير and الْكَبِيرُ الْمُتَعَال, the combination of divine names shows that the greatness of The God must be understood with elevation and transcendence, beyond created comparisons.

Difference From Nearby Roots

ع ظ م points to majesty, weighty greatness, and grandeur. ك ب ر focuses more on greatness in size, rank, measure, or self-magnification. Azamah carries a stronger sense of grandeur and weight.

ع ل و relates to height and being above. Istikbar can be joined with uluww, but uluww itself means elevation; istikbar is the psychological and behavioral process of self-magnification.

ط غ ي relates to transgression and exceeding bounds. Istikbar can be the source of tyranny, but taghyan focuses more on crossing limits and practical rebellion.

ب غ ي relates to violation, wrongful seeking, and excess. Istikbar is more the state of self-magnification and superiority-seeking; baghy may be its practical result against others.

ف خ ر relates to boasting and glorying in possessions. Istikbar is deeper: it is not only boasting, but refusal of truth because one sees oneself as great.

ع ت و points to harsh rebellion and severe stiff-necked defiance. Istikbar can lead to utuww, but utuww more strongly indicates the severity and hardness of disobedience.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

كَبُرَ: became great, was great, became heavy, became difficult; depending on context.

كَبِير: great, weighty, important, immense; depending on context.

الْكَبِير: The Truly Great, The Absolutely Great, Possessor of true greatness; for The God, with a transcendent reading.

أَكْبَر: greater, greatest, weightier, more important.

كَبِّرِ اللَّهَ: magnify The God, declare the greatness of The God.

تَكْبِير: magnifying The God, declaring the greatness of The God.

اسْتَكْبَرَ: acted arrogantly, took himself as superior, refused through self-magnification.

اسْتَكْبَرَ عَنْ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ: acted arrogantly toward the signs of The God, took himself as above accepting the signs.

اسْتَكْبَرَ فِي الْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ: sought superiority in the earth without right.

مُتَكَبِّر: for a human being: self-magnifying, arrogant. For The God: Possessor of absolute greatness, beyond every deficiency.

كِبْرِيَاء: superior greatness and majesty; for The God, true majesty; for a creature, a dangerous claim if without right.

Linguistic Summary

The root ك ب ر indicates greatness, being great, magnifying, and imagining something as great. In the Qur'an, it is used both for true and rightful greatness, such as the greatness of The God, the Hereafter, signs, or weighty matters, and for false greatness-seeking, such as the istikbar of Iblis, Pharaoh, Ad, and the deniers of the signs.

At the highest level, الْكَبِير is one of the names of The God and must be understood transcendentally: The God is great, but not like the greatness of created things. He is the possessor of true and absolute greatness.

For this reason, takbir of The God is tawhid, while the istikbar of a creature is placing greatness where it has no right to be. The key to this root is identifying the direction and rightful status of greatness: takbir of The God places majesty in its proper place; istikbar of a creature raises the self against truth.
