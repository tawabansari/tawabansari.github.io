Linguistic Note

The semantic core of Ḥ Q Q is stability and firmness. Ḥaqq is what stands in its place and cannot be removed from reality by imagination, desire, political power, religious habit, or majority-making. From this core come several fields: reality, correctness, rightful claim, fixed share, and obligation.

Thus ḥaqq is not only an ethical or legal idea. Before that, it is what truly is, what is established, and what places a claim upon the human being. When something is ḥaqq, one is not free to turn it into bāṭil by preference.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of Ḥ Q Q is established and realized reality: something with foundation, something that does not collapse before falsehood, propaganda, assumption, or desire. Ḥaqq may be reality itself, a statement aligned with reality, a fixed right, a binding decree, or the criterion of judgment.

Opposite it stands bāṭil: hollow, baseless, unstable, or passing away. Ḥaqq carries the weight of reality. It is not merely said; it imposes itself upon the world, judgment, and conscience because it has foundation.

Material Image

The material image is something firmly seated in its place and not shaking: a foundation that holds a building, a hinge set correctly in place, or something that has moved from raw weakness into firmness.

Arabic lexical discussions speak of settling, fitting into place, reaching completion, and becoming established. In the Qur’an, ḥaqq has this same weight of reality: it has foundation, it stands, and it does not tolerate bāṭil.

Historical and Arabic Usage Note

In classical Arabic, Ḥ Q Q is tied to stability, obligation, realization, rightful claim, and correspondence with reality. When something becomes ḥaqq, it has become established and binding. Ḥaqq may also mean a fixed right or due share that belongs to someone and must not be taken away.

The Qur’an deepens this range: The God is الْحَقّ (al-ḥaqq); revelation is الْحَقّ (al-ḥaqq); divine promise is ḥaqq; correct judgment is ḥaqq; and the needy have ḥaqq in the wealth of others. The root therefore has ontological, ethical, and social force.

Allāh Huwa Al-Ḥaqq: The God as The Truth

One of the most important uses of this root is describing The God as الْحَقّ (al-ḥaqq). In Al-Hajj 22:62, the Qur’an says:

ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ الْحَقُّ وَأَنَّ مَا يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِهِ هُوَ الْبَاطِلُ وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْكَبِيرُ (dhālika bi-anna Allāha huwa al-ḥaqqu wa-anna mā yadʿūna min dūnihi huwa al-bāṭilu wa-anna Allāha huwa al-ʿaliyyu al-kabīr)

اللَّهَ هُوَ الْحَقُّ (Allāha huwa al-ḥaqq) means The God is the fixed, original, unfading reality. Against Him, fabricated objects of worship are bāṭil: without independent foundation, made from imagination and habit, collapsing before the reality of The God.

This use raises ḥaqq beyond a correct statement or valid rule. Ḥaqq is not only accurate speech; it is ultimate established reality.

Al-Ḥaqq and Transcendent Theology

When the Qur’an calls The God الْحَقّ (al-ḥaqq), He is not one truth beside other truths. He is The Truth: foundational, self-subsisting, unfading, and the source of every other truth.

In the created world, we encounter limited rights and truths: financial right, judicial right, the right of a person, the right of a covenant, or observable realities. These are real, but limited and dependent. The God is ḥaqq, but not like created rights or truths. His being The Truth is the foundation of the truth of everything else.

For Forqan translation and explanation, اللَّهُ هُوَ الْحَقُّ (Allāhu huwa al-ḥaqq) must be understood with transcendence: The God is the foundational and enduring reality, not a limited instance of truth among worldly truths.

Truth Against Falsehood

In Al-Anbiya 21:18, the Qur’an says:

بَلْ نَقْذِفُ بِالْحَقِّ عَلَى الْبَاطِلِ فَيَدْمَغُهُ فَإِذَا هُوَ زَاهِقٌ (bal naqdhifu bi-l-ḥaqqi ʿalā al-bāṭili fa-yadmaghuhu fa-idhā huwa zāhiq)

Ḥaqq is the established and weighty force of reality that crushes bāṭil. Falsehood may have an appearance for a time, but it has no foundation. When truth strikes it, its hollowness is exposed.

In Al-Isra 17:81, it says: وَقُلْ جَاءَ الْحَقُّ وَزَهَقَ الْبَاطِلُ ۚ إِنَّ الْبَاطِلَ كَانَ زَهُوقًا (wa-qul jā'a al-ḥaqqu wa-zahaqa al-bāṭilu inna al-bāṭila kāna zahūqā). Truth arrives, and falsehood passes away because bāṭil is inherently passing.

The Qur’an and Revelation as Truth

In Al-Isra 17:105, the Qur’an says:

وَبِالْحَقِّ أَنزَلْنَاهُ وَبِالْحَقِّ نَزَلَ (wa-bi-l-ḥaqqi anzalnāhu wa-bi-l-ḥaqqi nazal)

The origin of revelation is truth, and its descent is also with truth. The Qur’an comes from established divine reality and descends in the field of truth. This separates revelation from poetry, fantasy, legend, and fabrication.

In Fatir 35:31, it says: وَالَّذِي أَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْكَ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ هُوَ الْحَقُّ مُصَدِّقًا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ (wa-alladhī awḥaynā ilayka mina al-kitābi huwa al-ḥaqqu muṣaddiqan limā bayna yadayhi). Confirming what came before means confirming the core divine truth within earlier revelation, not every alteration or historical interpretation.

Truth in Promise and Realization

In Ghafir 40:6, the Qur’an says:

وَحَقَّتْ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَنَّهُمْ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ (wa-ḥaqqat kalimatu rabbika ʿalā alladhīna kafarū annahum aṣḥābu al-nār)

حَقَّتْ (ḥaqqat) means it became established, binding, and certain to occur. This is not an opinion or possibility; the decree has reached the stage of stability and necessity.

In Yunus 10:33, it says: كَذَٰلِكَ حَقَّتْ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ فَسَقُوا أَنَّهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ (kadhālika ḥaqqat kalimatu rabbika ʿalā alladhīna fasaqū annahum lā yu'minūn). In this branch, ḥaqq means moving from possibility and speech into realization and binding consequence.

Ḥaqq as Fixed Right and Social Responsibility

In Adh-Dhariyat 51:19, the Qur’an says:

وَفِي أَمْوَالِهِمْ حَقٌّ لِّلسَّائِلِ وَالْمَحْرُومِ (wa-fī amwālihim ḥaqqun li-l-sā'ili wa-l-maḥrūm)

حَقٌّ (ḥaqq) here is a real fixed share, not optional generosity or performative charity. The one who asks and the deprived have a right in the wealth of the people of taqwa.

In Al-Ma'arij 70:24 to 70:25, it says: وَالَّذِينَ فِي أَمْوَالِهِمْ حَقٌّ مَّعْلُومٌ لِّلسَّائِلِ وَالْمَحْرُومِ (wa-alladhīna fī amwālihim ḥaqqun maʿlūmun li-l-sā'ili wa-l-maḥrūm). حَقٌّ مَّعْلُومٌ (ḥaqqun maʿlūm) is a known fixed right.

In Al-An'am 6:141, it says: وَآتُوا حَقَّهُ يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ (wa-ātū ḥaqqahu yawma ḥaṣādih). The right of the produce must be given on the day of harvest. If it is not given, more than kindness has been withheld; a fixed reality has been ignored.

Truth in Judgment and Speech

In Ṣād 38:26, the Qur’an says:

فَاحْكُم بَيْنَ النَّاسِ بِالْحَقِّ وَلَا تَتَّبِعِ الْهَوَىٰ (fa-uḥkum bayna al-nāsi bi-l-ḥaqqi wa-lā tattabiʿi al-hawā)

Judging by ḥaqq means judging by reality, justice, and a stable criterion, not by desire, anger, kinship, power, or benefit. The contrast between بِالْحَقِّ (bi-l-ḥaqq) and الْهَوَىٰ (al-hawā) is important: truth is stable; desire pulls and shifts.

In Al-Baqarah 2:42, it says: وَلَا تَلْبِسُوا الْحَقَّ بِالْبَاطِلِ وَتَكْتُمُوا الْحَقَّ وَأَنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ (wa-lā talbisū al-ḥaqqa bi-l-bāṭili wa-taktumū al-ḥaqqa wa-antum taʿlamūn). Truth may be known and still be mixed with falsehood or concealed.

Truth and Kufr: Covering the Established Reality

The connection between Ḥ Q Q and K F R is important. Kufr is, at root, covering a clarified truth, while ḥaqq is the established reality that becomes clear. Therefore, Qur’anic kufr must not be reduced to disagreement with a sect, school, or historical reading.

In Al-Baqarah 2:42, concealment of truth is joined with knowledge: وَتَكْتُمُوا الْحَقَّ وَأَنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ (wa-taktumū al-ḥaqqa wa-antum taʿlamūn). This is where ḥaqq and kufr meet: truth is established and known, but the human being covers it.

For that reason, ḥaqq is a safeguard against sectarian takfir. A person cannot be declared kāfir merely for opposing a school, group, or historical interpretation. The Qur’anic criterion is confrontation with clarified truth, deliberate concealment, denial, and covering.

Ḥaqq and Ṣidq

Ḥaqq is close to ṣidq, but not identical. Ḥaqq points more to established reality itself; ṣidq points more to the agreement of speech, claim, or action with that reality.

If a reality stands in its place, it is ḥaqq. If human speech agrees with that reality, the speech is ṣādiq. When we say the promise of The God is ḥaqq, the promise itself is established and certain to be realized. When we say someone’s statement is ṣidq, the statement corresponds to reality.

Distinguishing Qur’anic Uses

In Al-Hajj 22:62, الْحَقّ (al-ḥaqq) describes The God and points to divine fixed and unfading reality. In Al-Anbiya 21:18 and Al-Isra 17:81, truth stands against falsehood and removes it. In Al-Isra 17:105 and Fatir 35:31, revelation and the Book are truth.

In Ghafir 40:6 and Yunus 10:33, حَقَّتْ (ḥaqqat) means a decree or promise has become established. In Adh-Dhariyat 51:19, Al-Ma'arij 70:24-25, and Al-An'am 6:141, ḥaqq is a fixed social and financial right. In Ṣād 38:26, ḥaqq is the criterion of judgment. In Al-Baqarah 2:42, ḥaqq must not be mixed with bāṭil or concealed.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In اللَّهَ هُوَ الْحَقُّ (Allāha huwa al-ḥaqq), the separating pronoun هُوَ (huwa) and the definiteness of الْحَقّ (al-ḥaqq) stress that The God is The Original Truth, not one truth among others.

In بِالْحَقِّ أَنزَلْنَاهُ وَبِالْحَقِّ نَزَلَ (bi-l-ḥaqqi anzalnāhu wa-bi-l-ḥaqqi nazal), the repetition of بِالْحَقِّ (bi-l-ḥaqq) shows that both the origin and the descent of revelation are in the field of truth. In حَقَّتْ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ (ḥaqqat kalimatu rabbik), the verb shows that a word or decree has reached stability and necessity.

In فَاحْكُم بَيْنَ النَّاسِ بِالْحَقِّ (fa-uḥkum bayna al-nāsi bi-l-ḥaqq), the bā' marks the criterion and means of judgment. In حَقٌّ مَّعْلُومٌ (ḥaqqun maʿlūm), the adjective معلوم (maʿlūm) shows that the right is not vague or dependent on passing emotion.

Difference from Nearby Roots

Ḥ Q Q is close to Ṣ D Q, but not identical. Ḥaqq is established reality; ṣidq is the agreement of speech or claim with reality. Ḥaqq points more to the thing itself; ṣidq points to the relation of speech or action to that thing.

Ḥ Q Q differs from ʿ D L. ʿAdl is placing things where they belong and balanced judgment. Ḥaqq is the fixed reality, right, or necessary matter. Just judgment must stand upon ḥaqq.

Ḥ Q Q stands opposite B Ṭ L. Bāṭil is baseless, hollow, or unable to remain before reality. Ḥ Q Q is also close to Th B T, W J B, and Q S Ṭ, but each has its field: thubūt stresses stability, wujūb obligation, and qisṭ precise distributive justice.

Forqan Translation Method

In Forqan translation, ḥaqq should not always be reduced to “true” or “right.” In many verses, “established reality,” “enduring reality,” “truth,” “fixed right,” “just judgment,” or “rightful and necessary matter” is more accurate.

When الْحَقّ (al-ḥaqq) describes The God, the dignity of the divine name or attribute must be preserved: “The God is The Truth” or “The God is the foundational and enduring reality.” This is not a limited, measurable truth within the created world; The God is the source and foundation of all truths.

When it concerns wealth, ḥaqq means a fixed right, not optional help. When it concerns judgment, it means ruling by reality and justice, not desire and power.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For الْحَقّ (al-ḥaqq): truth; The Truth; established reality; enduring reality; rightful reality, in some contexts.

For اللَّهَ هُوَ الْحَقُّ (Allāha huwa al-ḥaqq): The God is The Truth; The God is the foundational and enduring reality; The God is the fixed and unfading truth, in explanation.

For جَاءَ الْحَقُّ (jā'a al-ḥaqq): truth came; the truth became manifest; established reality appeared, in explanation.

For وَزَهَقَ الْبَاطِلُ (wa-zahaqa al-bāṭil): and falsehood vanished; and falsehood passed away; hollow illusion collapsed, in explanation.

For بِالْحَقِّ أَنزَلْنَاهُ (bi-l-ḥaqqi anzalnāh): We sent it down with truth; We sent it down upon the foundation of truth.

For حَقَّتْ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ (ḥaqqat kalimatu rabbik): the word of your Lord became established; the decree of your Lord was realized; the command of your Lord reached realization and necessity.

For حَقٌّ لِّلسَّائِلِ وَالْمَحْرُومِ (ḥaqqun li-l-sā'ili wa-l-maḥrūm): a right for the one who asks and the deprived; a fixed share for the needy requester and the deprived.

For حَقٌّ مَّعْلُومٌ (ḥaqqun maʿlūm): a known right; a fixed and known share.

For وَآتُوا حَقَّهُ يَوْمَ حَصَادِهِ (wa-ātū ḥaqqahu yawma ḥaṣādih): give its right on the day of its harvest; deliver its fixed share on harvest day.

For فَاحْكُم بَيْنَ النَّاسِ بِالْحَقِّ (fa-uḥkum bayna al-nāsi bi-l-ḥaqq): judge between people by truth; judge between people on the basis of reality and justice.

For لَا تَلْبِسُوا الْحَقَّ بِالْبَاطِلِ (lā talbisū al-ḥaqqa bi-l-bāṭil): do not mix truth with falsehood.

Summary

The root Ḥ Q Q in the Qur’an points to stability, realization, enduring reality, and obligation arising from reality. Ḥaqq is what has foundation, stands, and is not removed by assumption, desire, or falsehood. It can describe The God, revelation, the criterion of judgment, realized promise, or a fixed social right.

When applied to The God, الْحَقّ (al-ḥaqq) has a transcendent meaning. The God is not one limited truth beside other truths; He is The Foundational Truth upon whom every other truth depends. Ḥaqq stands against bāṭil, and it is close to ṣidq but not identical: ḥaqq is established reality itself; ṣidq is the agreement of speech or action with that reality.
