Foundational Meaning

The root ظ ل م fundamentally points to darkening, covering light, placing something outside its proper place, and diminishing a right. In the Qur'an, this root is used both in the material field of darknesses and in the ethical and legal field of wrongdoing, right-violation, shirk, denial, and disturbing the proper place of things.

Important Forms of the Root

Important Qur'anic forms of this root include ظُلْم, يَظْلِم, ظَالِم, ظَالِمُون, ظَالِمِين, ظُلُمَات, أَظْلَم, ظَلَّام, ظَلُوم, مَظْلُوم, and مُظْلِم.

The verb ظَلَمَ means to wrong, diminish a right, or move something out of its proper place. A zalim is one who performs this darkening or right-violation. A mazlum is one whose right has been violated. Zulumaat refers to darknesses, whether sensory darknesses or darknesses of guidance and misguidance.

أَظْلَم is an elative form and usually appears in the Qur'an in a rhetorical question: "Who is more wrongful than...?" Through it, the Qur'an marks some of the most severe kinds of zulm.

Linguistic Note

The linguistic center of ظ ل م should be understood through the connection between darkness and injustice. Zulm is not merely mistreating someone. It means that the clear right of a thing is covered, its proper place is displaced, or its due share is diminished.

Just as darkness prevents things from being seen correctly, zulm darkens truth, right, and proper relation.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is darkness that covers the path, boundary, face, and place of things. When there is light, things are seen in their places. When there is darkness, recognition becomes difficult and the path is lost.

Some lexical reports connect this root with the covering of a thing's shine or clarity. If this layer is strengthened with a reliable early witness, it can help clarify the root: zulm means that the light or rightful clarity of something is hidden under an unlawful covering.

Older and Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic usage, zulm is connected both with darkness and with wrongdoing. The well-known definition, "placing a thing outside its proper place," is important for this root, but it should not be separated from the image of darkness.

Zulm means that a right is taken out of its light and its proper place. In a final version, a reliable witness from early Arabic poetry or old usage may be added if one is found.

Wronging Oneself

One of the clearest Qur'anic uses is wronging oneself:

وَإِذْ قَالَ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِ يَا قَوْمِ إِنَّكُمْ ظَلَمْتُمْ أَنفُسَكُم بِاتِّخَاذِكُمُ الْعِجْلَ

And when Musa said to his people: O my people, you have wronged yourselves by taking the calf. Al-Baqarah 2:54

In this verse, wronging the self means that a person, through shirk and a corrupt choice, removes the self from the clear place of servanthood and guidance. This wrongdoing does not harm The God; its darkness returns to the human being.

The same meaning appears elsewhere:

وَمَا ظَلَمُونَا وَلَٰكِن كَانُوا أَنفُسَهُمْ يَظْلِمُونَ

They did not wrong Us; rather, they were wronging themselves. Al-Baqarah 2:57

This structure is repeated many times in the Qur'an. Through zulm, the human being does not diminish The God; he darkens his own place and path.

Wrongdoing in Financial and Legal Relations

A second field is zulm in financial and legal relations:

وَإِن تُبْتُمْ فَلَكُمْ رُءُوسُ أَمْوَالِكُمْ لَا تَظْلِمُونَ وَلَا تُظْلَمُونَ

And if you repent, then your principal sums are yours: you do not wrong, and you are not wronged. Al-Baqarah 2:279

In this verse, zulm means unlawful decrease or increase in a financial relation. لَا تَظْلِمُونَ means: do not take another's right. لَا تُظْلَمُونَ means: your right is not violated either. This verse shows that Qur'anic justice is not only the prohibition of committing wrong; protection from being wronged is also part of the balance of right.

A precise use of this same meaning appears in Surat al-Kahf:

كِلْتَا الْجَنَّتَيْنِ آتَتْ أُكُلَهَا وَلَمْ تَظْلِم مِّنْهُ شَيْئًا

Both gardens gave their produce and did not diminish anything from it. Al-Kahf 18:33

Here لَمْ تَظْلِم is said about the garden: it did not fall short in its fruit. This is an important witness, because it shows that zulm can mean diminishing, withholding, or failing to give the full due, even outside a direct human oppression context.

Negating Wrongdoing from The God

The Qur'an repeatedly negates zulm from The God:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَظْلِمُ النَّاسَ شَيْئًا وَلَٰكِنَّ النَّاسَ أَنفُسَهُمْ يَظْلِمُونَ

Indeed, The God does not wrong people in anything; rather, people wrong themselves. Yunus 10:44

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَظْلِمُ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ

Indeed, The God does not wrong by the weight of an atom. An-Nisa 4:40

Negating zulm from The God means that His judgment and lordship are not built on right-violation, darkness, unlawful displacement, or diminishing the right of any being. Even the smallest measure of right is not lost in the divine scale.

Judgment, Measure, and the Hereafter

In the Hereafter, zulm is placed opposite qist:

وَنَضَعُ الْمَوَازِينَ الْقِسْطَ لِيَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ فَلَا تُظْلَمُ نَفْسٌ شَيْئًا

And We set the scales of equity for the Day of Resurrection; so no soul is wronged in anything. Al-Anbiya 21:47

Qist is proper measure and placing each thing in its weight and position. Zulm would be an unlawful decrease or increase in that very measurement.

Darknesses in Contrast with Light

One of the major fields of this root is darknesses in contrast with light:

اللَّهُ وَلِيُّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا يُخْرِجُهُم مِّنَ الظُّلُمَاتِ إِلَى النُّورِ

The God is the protector of those who have faith; He brings them out from the darknesses into the light. Al-Baqarah 2:257

In this verse, zulumaat appears in the plural, while nur appears in the singular. This matters: the darknesses are the many paths of misguidance, shirk, fear, ignorance, tyranny, and lost direction; but the light of guidance has one direction. Darknesses are scattered and layered; light makes the path coherent.

The layered image of darknesses is clearer in Surat an-Nur:

أَوْ كَظُلُمَاتٍ فِي بَحْرٍ لُّجِّيٍّ يَغْشَاهُ مَوْجٌ مِّن فَوْقِهِ مَوْجٌ مِّن فَوْقِهِ سَحَابٌ ظُلُمَاتٌ بَعْضُهَا فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ

Or like darknesses in a deep sea, covered by a wave, above it another wave, and above it a cloud: darknesses, some above others. An-Nur 24:40

Here zulumaat is not merely the absence of light; it is the layering of barriers. This sensory image helps us understand intellectual and moral darknesses: sometimes one error does not stand alone. Ignorance, fear, desire, power, and justification may pile upon one another and block the sight of truth.

The Structure of Azlam

The Qur'an asks in important cases:

وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّن مَّنَعَ مَسَاجِدَ اللَّهِ أَن يُذْكَرَ فِيهَا اسْمُهُ

And who is more wrongful than one who prevents the name of The God from being remembered in His places of prostration? Al-Baqarah 2:114

فَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّنِ افْتَرَىٰ عَلَى اللَّهِ كَذِبًا

So who is more wrongful than one who fabricates a lie against The God? Al-An'am 6:21

أَظْلَم in this structure is not a simple comparison. Through this rhetorical question, the Qur'an identifies severe forms of darkening the truth: blocking remembrance of The God, fabricating lies against The God, concealing testimony, or preventing people from the signs. These are structural forms of zulm, because they do not harm only one individual; they darken the public space of guidance.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

Wronging oneself means removing oneself from the proper place of guidance. Wronging people means diminishing or violating their rights. Zulm in wealth means that the balance of right is disturbed. Zulm in accounting means, if it were to occur, the weight of deeds would be measured incorrectly; the Qur'an negates this from The God.

Zulumaat may be physical darkness or guidance-related darkness. Azlam marks intensity in cases where divine right and the public path of guidance are darkened. A mazlum is one upon whom wrongdoing has occurred, not merely someone weak or defeated.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

The object and grammatical construction matter in this root. ظَلَمَ نَفْسَهُ means he wronged himself. لَا تَظْلِمُونَ وَلَا تُظْلَمُونَ brings both sides of the relation: do not violate rights, and do not have your rights violated.

لَا يَظْلِمُ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ carries the negation of wrongdoing down to the smallest measure. ظُلُمَات in plural form shows layers and many paths of darkness, while نُور often indicates the clear and unified direction of guidance.

Difference from Nearby Roots

ج و ر points to deviation from justice and unlawful leaning in judgment or path, while zulm is wider and includes every diminishing of a right or unlawful displacement. ب غ ي indicates unlawful seeking, crossing the limit, and rebellion. Every baghy may be zulm, but not every zulm is necessarily baghy in the sense of seeking excess.

ع د و indicates crossing a limit and open transgression. ف س د points to corruption of order; zulm may cause fasad, but fasad is the broader result of order being ruined. ك ف ر focuses on covering, ingratitude, and denial. It approaches zulm through covering truth, but kufr centers on covering, while zulm centers on displacing right and darkening the proper place. ض ل ل is losing the way; zulm may lead to dalal, but zulm itself is diminishing or unlawful placement.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

Zulm should not always be translated only as "oppression." Depending on context, it may be rendered as "wrongdoing," "right-violation," "violating a right," "diminishing a right," "placing a right wrongly," or "darkening the proper place of a right."

Zalim may be "wrongdoer," "oppressor," or "right-violator." Mazlum is "one who has been wronged" or "one whose right has been violated." Zulumaat in natural contexts is "darknesses," and in guidance-related contexts may be "darknesses of misguidance" or "darknesses of falsehood." Azlam is usually "more wrongful," but in root explanation it may also be understood as "more darkening of the truth."

Linguistic Summary

The root ظ ل م points to darkness, covering light, placing something outside its proper place, and diminishing a right. Its material image is darkness: a place where light is covered and recognizing the proper place of things becomes difficult.

The Qur'an extends this root from sensory darknesses to moral wrongdoing, from wronging oneself to financial and social injustice, from negating zulm from The God to the complete justice of the Hereafter, and from the darknesses of misguidance to the light of guidance. Its difference from jawr, baghy, udwan, fasad, kufr, and dalal is that ظ ل م focuses above all on darkening right and removing things from their clear and proper place.
