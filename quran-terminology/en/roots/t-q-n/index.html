Core Meaning

The root ت ق ن comes through the word atqana. This root appears in the Qur'an in only one clear form:

صُنْعَ اللَّهِ الَّذِي أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ

The making of The God, the One who has precisely and firmly fashioned every thing.

Al-Naml 27:88

T Q N is a low-frequency Qur'anic root. Its axis is making something precise, firm, coordinated, and free of structural flaw.

The root T Q N indicates making firm, finishing with precision, coordinating the parts, and bringing a made thing into a state where weakness, disorder, and malfunction do not remain in its structure and function.

Atqana does not mean merely bringing something into existence. It means that the parts, measures, connections, and functions are placed with such measure that the whole structure works properly.

The semantic core may be summarized as finishing a made thing with precision, proportion, and firmness, so that its parts are coordinated and its structure and function do not fall into flaw.

Important Forms

أَتْقَنَ is a form IV verb: he made firm; finished with precision and measure; coordinated the parts of something; performed an act in a firm and flaw-free way; brought a made thing into a sound and stable state.

Its Qur'anic form is:

أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ

He has precisely and firmly fashioned every thing.

Al-Naml 27:88

Form IV here indicates bringing something into the state of itqan; that is, the made thing reaches a state of precision, firmness, and coordination.

إِتْقَان is the verbal noun of form IV: making firm, precise workmanship, measured finishing, coordinating parts, making something firm and free of flaw, and performing work skillfully. This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

مُتْقِن is the active participle: one who performs work precisely and firmly; a skilled craftsperson; one who performs a measured and flaw-free act. This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

مُتْقَن is the passive participle: firmly made, precisely finished, coordinated and measured, firm and free of flaw. This form also does not occur in the Qur'an.

تَقِنَ, as a form I verb, does not have a broad and clear field like form IV. The central and recognized forms of this material in Arabic are atqana and itqan. Therefore, without clear evidence, one should not build separate form I meanings and fields for it.

Linguistic Note

The core of T Q N is not only “being hard and solid.”

A wall may be very heavy and resistant, but its measurements and joints may be imprecise. Something may also look beautiful outwardly, while its construction lacks durability or proper function.

Itqan includes several layers together: firmness, precision, proportion, coordination of parts, soundness of connections, preservation of function, absence of weakness and disorder, and skillful finishing.

For this reason, itqan concerns the quality of making and finishing.

Atqana does not say merely that something came into existence; it says that its structure was measured through size, relation, connection, and function.

This root is also not equal to stillness. A made thing can be mutqan while moving, rotating, or changing, so long as the motion of its parts does not leave order and proportion.

This may be put simply as firmness within flow.

This expression does not mean absolute stillness. It points to a system that moves while preserving the measured connection and function of its parts.

Material Image

The material image of T Q N is the work of a master craftsperson who places the pieces of a made object together with proper measure and firm connection.

In a mutqan construction, the measurements are not accidental; each piece is in its proper place; the connections are not weak; pressure in one part does not needlessly collapse the whole structure; form and material agree with function; and the motion of the parts, if there is motion, does not leave order.

For example, making a wooden door is not merely cutting several pieces of wood. If the measurements are not precise, the door will not sit in its frame. If the connections are weak, it will not last. If the weight is not distributed properly, it will not open and close well.

Itqan is present when the material is suitable, the measurement is right, the connection is firm, and the whole made thing performs its task.

The same image applies to a moving system. Wheels, joints, or parts of a mechanism may move continually, but if that motion is precise and coordinated, it is a sign of itqan.

So itqan does not mean lack of motion. It means motion or stability without structural disorder.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, atqana al-amal meant to do a work firmly, precisely, and well.

The word was used for something made firmly in terms of measure, connection, quality, durability, proportion, and function.

A mutqin craftsperson was not someone who merely finished the work. He performed it in such a way that haste, weakness, and disharmony were not visible in it.

In this sense, itqan is connected with craft and skill. A thing is mutqan when traces of precision and finishing are visible in it.

Qur'anic Use

صُنْعَ اللَّهِ الَّذِي أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ

The making of The God, the One who has precisely and firmly fashioned every thing.

Al-Naml 27:88

This is the only direct Qur'anic use of the root T Q N.

Atqana is attached to kulla shay'. This structure does not confine itqan to one limited part of creation.

But kulla shay' is understood within the context of sunu, making. The verse speaks about the structure and system of creation, not about morally endorsing every human act, decision, or structure.

For example, oppression, corruption, and falsehood can also be called “things” in language, but one cannot say that this verse presents them as morally mutqan and pleasing to The God.

The axis of the verse is this: every thing that belongs to His system of making is measured and firm in its structure, relation, and place.

Why Sunu and Not Only Khalq?

The verse says:

صُنْعَ اللَّهِ

Then it says:

أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ

Sunu and itqan are close in meaning, but they do not merely repeat one another.

Sunu emphasizes making, finishing, and forming the parts. Itqan shows the quality of that making: sunu means making and finishing; itqan means the precision and firmness of that making.

Khalq has a broader field and can include bringing into existence, measuring, and giving form. Sunu more strongly highlights the angle of skillful making and composition of parts.

However, one should not build an absolute rule that khalq is only the initial material and sunu only the final stage. Qur'anic uses of these two roots are broader than such a mechanical division.

A careful distinction is this: khalq emphasizes origination, measuring, and forming. Sunu emphasizes the act of making and finishing a form or system. Itqan expresses the precise, firm, and coordinated quality of that same sunu.

From this angle, the connection between sunu and atqana is natural: the verse highlights not only existence itself, but the quality of finishing and organization in what is made.

Itqan and the Motion of Mountains

Before the expression atqana, the verse says:

وَتَرَى الْجِبَالَ تَحْسَبُهَا جَامِدَةً وَهِيَ تَمُرُّ مَرَّ السَّحَابِ

And you see the mountains, supposing them still, while they pass like the passing of clouds.

Al-Naml 27:88

Then comes:

صُنْعَ اللَّهِ الَّذِي أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ

The making of The God, the One who has precisely and firmly fashioned every thing.

This adjacency shows that itqan is not incompatible with motion.

Something may move, pass from one stage to another, flow within a larger system, and still preserve its structure and relations firmly.

Firmness does not always mean stillness. Sometimes the sign of itqan is that a very large motion occurs within a coordinated order.

But this verse should not be reduced, without careful study of context, to a specific scientific theory about the movement of the earth or mountains. There are different views about whether the verse describes a scene of the Final Hour or a motion in the present order.

What is clear for the root T Q N is that motion is not presented in the verse as the opposite of itqan.

Stable Dynamism

The adjacency between the passing of the mountains and itqan clarifies an important linguistic point: a firm system can be dynamic.

In a mutqan system, parts may move; outward forms may change; processes may continue; but the foundational relations and law of motion do not leave order.

This condition may be called “stable dynamism,” or more simply, “firmness within flow.”

But this expression is explanatory, not a direct equivalent of atqana.

Fixed Appearance and Moving Reality

The verse says:

تَحْسَبُهَا جَامِدَةً

You suppose them still.

The word tahsabuha shows a difference between the observer's assumption and the reality being described.

A human being sees something as fixed, while the verse speaks of its motion. Then this whole scene is attributed to mutqan sunu.

Therefore, itqan does not necessarily mean that the entire order is directly visible to human observation. A human being may see only one level of a system and fail to perceive larger motions and relations.

This point can be understood from the context, but it should not be turned into a scientific claim beyond the text.

Sunu of The God as Scene Summary

صُنْعَ اللَّهِ appears in the accusative. This structure can function like a compressed and emphatic sentence: this is the making of The God; look at the making of The God; such is the making of The God.

The verse first shows a scene: the mountains are seen; the viewer supposes them still; while they pass. Then, through sunu Allah, the whole scene is attributed to its maker and doer.

One may say that this expression functions like a summarizing seal for the image, but the phrase “final signature” is a literary explanation, not the grammatical meaning of sunu itself.

وَهِيَ تَمُرُّ مَرَّ السَّحَابِ صُنْعَ اللَّهِ

Sunu of The God is not limited only to one nearby word; it can include the whole scene of movement and system being described.

A translation should preserve this connection: while they pass like the passing of clouds; this is the making of The God, the One who has precisely and firmly fashioned every thing.

Itqan and Completion

Itqan should not be mechanically equated with perfection or completion.

Itqan means precision, firmness, and coordination of structure. Kamal means reaching wholeness or the intended complete state.

Something may have a mutqan structure at a stage of growth, while its process of growth is not yet finished.

For example, a child's body may possess a precise and proportionate biological system at that stage of growth, even though it has not yet reached maturity.

So mutqan does not necessarily mean “finished in every respect.”

Itqan and Apparent Defect

The expression atqana kulla shay' should not be turned into denial of illness, decay, death, or painful events.

Itqan speaks about the quality of structure and order, not about every event being pleasing to human beings.

A biological system may be precise and law-governed, while aging occurs within it, cells wear down, illness appears, and death is part of the cycle.

A natural system may also be law-governed, while earthquakes, storms, or erosion occur in it.

Therefore, one cannot conclude from itqan that every event is desirable for humans, that no real pain or harm exists, or that every human-made structure is also right and ordered.

Itqan concerns the system of making and its relations. The problem of suffering, justice, and responsibility must be studied through their own verses and roots.

Itqan of Making and Awareness of Human Action

The end of the verse says:

إِنَّهُ خَبِيرٌ بِمَا تَفْعَلُونَ

Indeed, He is deeply aware of what you do.

Al-Naml 27:88

The verse moves from mutqan making to human actions: the structure of creation is not outside knowledge and itqan; human action is also not hidden from the awareness of The God.

This connection shows that the human being does not act in a structureless and unaccounted world. His actions stand within a system whose maker is aware of both the structure and human conduct.

But one should not conclude from this sentence that the verse directly commands human beings to make all their work mutqan. Such a principle may be ethically valuable, but in this verse the verb atqana belongs to the sunu of The God, not to human action.

The direct connection of the verse is this: itqan in His making, and His awareness of what you do.

Therefore, turning divine itqan into a technical command about the quality of human work is a secondary inference, not the explicit meaning of the verse.

Inna gives emphasis. Khabir does not indicate only surface-level awareness; it is closer to penetrating knowledge of reality and details. Ma taf'alun includes human actions. This final part shows that after describing the making, the verse returns attention to the responsibility of human action.

Similar Uses

Itqan and making: Making indicates bringing a made thing into being. Itqan is the quality of that making: making means something came to be; itqan means it was made precise, firm, and coordinated.

Itqan and firmness: Firmness emphasizes resistance and lack of weakness in a structure. Itqan includes firmness, but also precision, proportion, and coordination of parts. Something may be hard but not mutqan.

Itqan and order: Order indicates the arrangement of parts. Itqan, beyond arrangement, highlights the quality of connection, durability, and correct function.

Itqan and beauty: Beauty concerns good appearance and pleasant form. Itqan concerns technical and structural quality. Something may be beautiful but weakly made, or mutqan without outward beauty being its main purpose.

Itqan and completion: Completion concerns wholeness and reaching the intended end. Itqan focuses on precision and firmness of structure.

Itqan and skill: Skill is more often a quality of the doer. Itqan can describe the act or the made thing: a skilled craftsperson, a mutqan work.

Itqan and ihsan: Ihsan indicates doing well and choosing the better act. Itqan emphasizes precise and technical quality in making and execution. The two may come together, but they are not identical.

Itqan and dynamism: Itqan does not mean stillness. A moving system can also be mutqan if its motion is measured and its parts are coordinated.

Grammar and Structure

أَتْقَنَ in أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ is a form IV past verb. Its subject is an implicit pronoun that returns to alladhi, and ultimately to The God. Kulla shay' is the object of the verb.

Kulla shay' expands the scope of the verb. But this universality stands within the context of sunu. It should not be separated from the context and used to morally endorse all human behaviors.

صُنْعَ اللَّهِ is an accusative verbal noun. One may understand an implied verb or structure: this is the making of The God; look at the making of The God; He made it as the making of The God.

The accusative form of sunu gives the sentence a compressed, emphatic, and summarizing quality.

الَّذِي أَتْقَنَ returns to The God: the One who made every thing mutqan. Atqana expresses the quality of the maker's act.

In connection with the previous sentence, sunu Allah is not limited to one nearby word; it can include the whole described scene of motion and system.

In إِنَّهُ خَبِيرٌ بِمَا تَفْعَلُونَ, inna gives emphasis. Khabir indicates penetrating awareness of reality and details. Ma taf'alun includes human actions.

Nearby Roots

T Q N and H K M (hakam): Ihkam indicates making firm, restraining, and preventing disorder. Itqan emphasizes precision, proportion, skill, and quality of finishing. Ihkam: the structure was bound so that it would not be weak or disordered. Itqan: the structure was finished with precision and coordination.

T Q N and Q D R (qadar): Qadar indicates measuring and determining proportions. Itqan may be the result of properly carrying out those measures: taqdir means measure and proportion were set; itqan means the made thing came into being precisely and firmly on the basis of those measures.

T Q N and S W Y (sawwa): Taswiyah indicates making parts even, coordinated, and proportionate. Itqan expresses the broader quality of the whole structure and function.

T Q N and S N AYN (sunu): Sunu indicates the act of making and finishing. Itqan is the precise and firm quality of sunu. Sunu: he made. Atqana: he made it without flaw and with measure.

T Q N and KH L Q (khalq): Khalq indicates bringing into existence, measuring, and giving form. Itqan emphasizes the precise and firm quality of the made thing. One should not reduce khalq to initial material and sunu to final stage only, though sunu in this verse more clearly highlights the angle of finishing and making.

T Q N and TH B T (thabat): Thabat indicates remaining firm and not slipping from place. Itqan is broader. A mutqan made thing may move, yet remain coordinated and stable in its motion.

T Q N and M T N (matn): Matanah indicates strength, toughness, and firmness. Itqan includes strength but also precision and coordination of parts.

T Q N and K M L (kamal): Kamal indicates wholeness and completion. Itqan focuses on quality of structure and precise execution.

T Q N and H S N (husn): Husn indicates goodness, beauty, and desirability. Itqan focuses on firmness and precision of construction.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For أَتْقَنَ: made firm; made precisely and with measure; finished firmly and in coordination; made without flaw; brought into being with precision and skill.

For إِتْقَان: making firm; precise workmanship; measured finishing; flaw-free making; firmness with precision; coordination of structure and function.

For مُتْقِن: precise craftsperson; skilled performer; one who performs the work firmly.

For مُتْقَن: firmly made; precisely finished; coordinated and measured; firm and without flaw.

For أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ: He has precisely and firmly fashioned every thing; He has made the structure of every thing measured and firm; He has made every thing with precision and coordination; He has brought every thing into being with precision and firmness.

For صُنْعَ اللَّهِ الَّذِي أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ: the making of The God, the One who has precisely and firmly fashioned every thing. Or: this is the sunu of The God, the One who has made the structure of every thing measured and firm. Or: this is the making of The God, who has finished every thing with precision and firmness.

Suggested translation of the verse segment:

وَتَرَى الْجِبَالَ تَحْسَبُهَا جَامِدَةً وَهِيَ تَمُرُّ مَرَّ السَّحَابِ صُنْعَ اللَّهِ الَّذِي أَتْقَنَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ

And you see the mountains, supposing them still, while they pass like the passing of clouds; this is the making of The God, the One who has precisely and firmly fashioned every thing.

For jamidah, “lifeless” may introduce too much of coldness or deadness into the text. “Still” or “motionless” is clearer in this context.

Summary

The root T Q N indicates making something precise, firm, coordinated, and free of flaw. Its material image is the work of a craftsperson who arranges material, measure, connection, and function so that the whole made thing works properly.

In the Qur'an, this root appears only in the expression atqana kulla shay'. The verse does not merely call the structure of creation existent or hard; it presents it as measured, coordinated, and firm.

The connection between sunu and itqan matters. Sunu indicates making and finishing, and itqan clarifies the quality of that making. At the same time, the difference between khalq and sunu should not be limited to a mechanical division between “initial material” and “final stage.”

The adjacency of itqan with the passing of the mountains shows that firmness is not incompatible with motion. A mutqan system can be dynamic; its parts may move, while its order, relations, and function do not collapse.

The end of the verse also moves from mutqan making to human action: The God is aware of what human beings do. But the verse does not explicitly say that human beings must make their work mutqan like divine making; that can only be a secondary ethical inference.

T Q N is close to H K M, Q D R, S W Y, S N AYN, KH L Q, TH B T, M T N, K M L, and H S N, but it is not identical with any of them. Ihkam emphasizes preventing weakness, taqdir measuring, taswiyah proportion, sunu making itself, and kamal completion.

The special core of T Q N is finishing a made thing with precision, proportion, firmness, and coordination, so that its parts are in their proper places and no flaw remains in its structure and function.
