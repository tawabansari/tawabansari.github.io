Qur'anic Root F W M

An earth-grown food whose exact meaning is debated between garlic and wheat.

1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ف و م in Qur'anic usage points to one of the earth-grown foods whose exact meaning is debated among exegetes and lexicographers.

Some have interpreted it as garlic, while others have understood it as wheat or a kind of edible grain. This disagreement shows that the word pointed to a known food substance in the environment of the first audience, but determining its exact referent became debated over time.

The semantic core of the root can be stated as follows:

ف و م denotes a kind of earth-grown food whose exact meaning is debated between garlic and wheat.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

فُوم

A food from the products of the earth whose meaning is debated; garlic or wheat are among the explanations given.

فُومِهَا

That food from the earth, attributed to a land or to its set of products.

3. Reference Cards for Qur'anic Derivatives

فُوم

A food from the products of the earth whose meaning is debated (garlic or wheat).

فُومِهَا

That food from the products of the earth, attributed to a land or earth.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The core of this root rests on several points:

pointing to one specific food from the products of the earth;

being placed among other known food materials;

the existence of disagreement in identifying its exact referent.

Unlike words such as بقل, which have a general meaning, this root points to one particular item, even though identifying that particular item is disputed.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is one of the edible products of the earth, consumed alongside other food materials.

Depending on the interpretation, this image may include:

a plant such as garlic, with a distinctive taste and smell;

or a grain such as wheat, which plays a basic role in nourishment.

In both cases, the shared element is being edible and growing from the earth.

6. Use of the Root in Arabic

In Arabic, فُوم is known as the name of a food, but its exact identification is disputed.

Some lexicographers relate it to garlic, while others interpret it as wheat or a kind of edible grain. This disagreement shows that the word originally pointed to a known food substance, but in the historical transmission of meaning, its precise identification became difficult.

7. Qur'anic Use of the Root

وَإِذْ قُلْتُمْ يَا مُوسَىٰ لَنْ نَصْبِرَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامٍ وَاحِدٍ فَادْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ يُخْرِجْ لَنَا مِمَّا تُنْبِتُ الْأَرْضُ مِنْ بَقْلِهَا وَقِثَّائِهَا وَفُومِهَا وَعَدَسِهَا وَبَصَلِهَا

And when you said: O Moses, we will not be patient with one kind of food; so call upon your Lord for us, that He may bring out for us from what the earth grows: its greens, its cucumbers, its garlic or wheat, its lentils, and its onions.

Al-Baqarah 2:61

In this verse, فُومِهَا appears beside other products of the earth. This companionship shows that فوم was one of the known foods among agricultural products.

Its placement beside بقل, قثاء, عدس, and بصل shows that we are dealing with an everyday food substance, consumable directly or indirectly.

8. Semantic Notes

فوم points to one specific food from the products of the earth;

there is disagreement in identifying it precisely as garlic or wheat;

in Qur'anic usage, it appears beside other food materials and shows part of the variety of foods;

the word points to one specific instance, not to a general category of plants.

9. Conclusion

The root ف و م in the Qur'an points to one of the earth-grown foods whose exact meaning is disputed and has been interpreted, among other things, as garlic or wheat.

The final definition of the root is:

ف و م denotes a kind of food from the products of the earth whose exact identification is debated between garlic and wheat.
