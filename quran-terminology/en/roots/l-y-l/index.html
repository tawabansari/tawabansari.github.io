1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ل ي ل in the Qur'an is tied to night: the part of the time-cycle known by the fading of daylight, the spread of darkness, the reduction of ordinary activity, and the preparation of rest.

Night in the Qur'an is not evil, nothingness, or the domain of demonic forces. It is part of the order of creation and, alongside day, organizes human life. The Qur'an speaks of night as covering, a time of stillness, an opportunity for sleep, a vessel for worship, a time for hidden or safe movement, and a unit for calculating time.

Yet layl must not be turned into a theory about the “dark body of existence,” “the base frequency of the world,” or a “cosmic cooling system.” Such explanations may discuss some natural effects of night, but the lexical and Qur'anic meaning of layl is simply night.

Night is also not the complete absence of all light. Moon, stars, fire, and other lights may be seen at night. Layl names a period of time and natural condition, not a scientific definition of the absence of every radiation.

The shortest core is: the darker part of the day-night cycle, which covers day and serves for stillness, sleep, worship, movement, and measurement of time.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives

لَيْل

Night; the period between the end of daylight and the return of dawn.

اللَّيْل

The night known in the context, or night as a general kind.

لَيْلًا

At night or during part of the night. This accusative form may function as a time adverb.

لَيْلَة

A specific night; a defined unit of night.

لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْر

The Night of Qadr.

لَيْلَةٍ مُبَارَكَةٍ

A blessed night.

لَيَالٍ / لَيَالِي

Nights; the plural of laylah. In the Qur'an ليالٍ appears in several temporal contexts.

لَيْلِيّ

Nightly or related to night. This is not a prominent Qur'anic derivative and should not be listed as a central Qur'anic form merely because it exists in Arabic.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of L Y L is night as a period of time known by the reduction of sunlight and the spread of darkness.

In Qur'anic usage, night covers day, provides the ground for stillness and rest, is part of the system for measuring time, can be a time of worship and recitation, can provide cover for hidden or safe movement, and together with day is among the signs of the order of creation.

None of these features alone is the whole meaning. Night may be a time of stillness, but not all beings sleep at night. It may cover, but it is not always absolute darkness. It may be a time of worship, but the word itself does not mean worship.

The base meaning must remain simple: layl means night, the darker and more covered part of the day-night cycle.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The clear image is the arrival of night and the gradual covering of the earth by darkness. Daylight decreases, forms become less visible, and many daytime activities subside. The human being needs another light to see, and the environment becomes more prepared for sleep or calm.

وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا يَغْشَىٰ

By the night when it covers.

Al-Layl 92:1

The word يغشى expresses covering. Layl itself names the period of night, and covering is one function of it in this verse.

Night may naturally involve lower temperature and biological changes, but these should not become the lexical core of the root.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, اللَّيل means night. Structures such as entering the night refer to people coming into nighttime. سَارَ لَيْلًا means he traveled at night. لَيْلَة is one night, and ليالٍ means several nights.

In names and literary expressions, layl may be connected with darkness, concealment, silence, or length. But literary uses do not change the dictionary meaning of the word.

Claims that some forms necessarily refer to camouflaging an object for military passage, or that “a sleepless night” is a technical expression for total environmental blackout, should not enter the root article without clear evidence.

7. History of Understanding, Misuse, or Semantic Restriction

One distortion is to tie night to evil, sin, or the certain presence of demonic forces. The Qur'an does not define layl this way. Night is among the signs of creation and is used for stillness, sleep, movement, worship, and measuring time.

The opposite error is to build from night a full scientific theory about thermodynamics, radiation, frequency, and cellular repair. Reduced light and temperature may affect sleep and human life, but the Qur'an does not call night a hardware-repair system for the body. Sleep also does not occur only at night, and not all functions of night are sleep.

Another restriction is to interpret night as the original and default state of the world. The verse of stripping day from night gives a powerful image of the movement between day and night, but it does not prove that darkness is the basic body of existence and day is merely an accidental shell.

8. Methodological Note: Stripping Day from Night

وَآيَةٌ لَهُمُ اللَّيْلُ نَسْلَخُ مِنْهُ النَّهَارَ فَإِذَا هُمْ مُظْلِمُونَ

And night is a sign for them; We strip the day from it, and suddenly they are in darkness.

Ya-Sin 36:37

The verb نسلخ comes from the image of skinning. The verse pictures the departure of day as the removal of a bright covering from night.

This image shows that day and night replace each other gradually, that with the removal of daylight darkness appears, and that this change is a sign of the order of creation.

But a rhetorical image must not be turned into a physical theory. The verse does not say that day is a real material skin or that night is the flesh beneath the skin of the world.

9. A Short Look at Related Semitic Languages

Forms close to layl are found in several Semitic languages for night. This similarity shows that the word belongs to an old layer of the Semitic language family.

But a direct link between L Y L and L W L, twisting or hinge, cannot be proven without sufficient phonetic and historical evidence. Similar letters are not enough to conclude root unity.

For Qur'anic understanding, the clear use of layl as night comes first.

10. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

A. Night when it covers

وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا يَغْشَىٰ

By the night when it covers.

Al-Layl 92:1

Night is described here through covering. The covering may point to the spread of darkness and the reduced visibility of things. The verse does not make night evil; it places it among the signs.

B. Night as clothing

وَجَعَلْنَا اللَّيْلَ لِبَاسًا

And We made the night a garment.

An-Naba 78:10

Clothing covers the body. Night covers the environment and human activity with its darkness. The comparison may indicate calm, privacy, reduced visibility, and the stopping of some activities.

C. Night for stillness

هُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ اللَّيْلَ لِتَسْكُنُوا فِيهِ وَالنَّهَارَ مُبْصِرًا

He is the One who made the night for you so that you may rest in it, and the day visible.

Yunus 10:67

Stillness is tied to night here. The meaning is rest and reduction of movement, not heat discharge or reconstruction of all bodily systems.

D. Night and sleep

وَهُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمُ اللَّيْلَ لِبَاسًا وَالنَّوْمَ سُبَاتًا وَجَعَلَ النَّهَارَ نُشُورًا

He is the One who made the night a covering for you, sleep a rest, and the day a rising.

Al-Furqan 25:47

The verse separates night, sleep, and day. Layl and sleep are not identical. Night prepares a setting for sleep, but the word layl does not mean sleep or body repair.

E. Stripping day from night

وَآيَةٌ لَهُمُ اللَّيْلُ نَسْلَخُ مِنْهُ النَّهَارَ فَإِذَا هُمْ مُظْلِمُونَ

And night is a sign for them; We strip the day from it, and suddenly they are in darkness.

Ya-Sin 36:37

The verse expresses the change from day to night through the image of stripping. مظلمون reports that people enter darkness, not that the dark body of the universe is revealed.

F. Wrapping night and day over one another

يُكَوِّرُ اللَّيْلَ عَلَى النَّهَارِ وَيُكَوِّرُ النَّهَارَ عَلَى اللَّيْلِ

He wraps the night over the day and wraps the day over the night.

Az-Zumar 39:5

The verse speaks of the continuous and ordered succession of night and day. Neither is presented as the absolute origin while the other is a meaningless accident.

G. The alternation of night and day

إِنَّ فِي اخْتِلَافِ اللَّيْلِ وَالنَّهَارِ وَمَا خَلَقَ اللَّهُ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ لَآيَاتٍ لِقَوْمٍ يَتَّقُونَ

Indeed, in the alternation of night and day and what The God created in the heavens and the earth are signs for a people who have taqwa.

Yunus 10:6

The alternation can point to coming and going, difference, and succession. Night and day shape time and human activity through their difference.

H. Night for hidden movement

فَأَسْرِ بِأَهْلِكَ بِقِطْعٍ مِنَ اللَّيْلِ

So travel with your family in a portion of the night.

Hud 11:81

Because ordinary visibility and public activity decrease, night can be a suitable time for safe departure. But layl itself does not mean military concealment; that function comes from the story-context.

I. The night journey

سُبْحَانَ الَّذِي أَسْرَىٰ بِعَبْدِهِ لَيْلًا

Glory be to the One who carried His servant by night.

Al-Isra 17:1

ليلًا is a time adverb and shows that this journey occurred at night.

J. Night worship

قُمِ اللَّيْلَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

Stand in the night, except a little.

Al-Muzzammil 73:2

Because of relative stillness and fewer daytime concerns, night can be a fitting time for recitation, prayer, and attention. Night is not only for sleep.

K. Glorification at night

وَمِنَ اللَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْهُ

And in part of the night, glorify Him.

Qaf 50:40

The verse shows that night is also a vessel for conscious action and worship. It is not a phase of complete shutdown.

L. The Night of Qadr

إِنَّا أَنْزَلْنَاهُ فِي لَيْلَةِ الْقَدْرِ

Indeed, We sent it down in the Night of Qadr.

Al-Qadr 97:1

ليلة names one particular night, and القدر gives that night its significance. The verse speaks of the descent of the Qur'an in that night.

M. A blessed night

إِنَّا أَنْزَلْنَاهُ فِي لَيْلَةٍ مُبَارَكَةٍ

Indeed, We sent it down in a blessed night.

Ad-Dukhan 44:3

The blessedness of the night is tied to its goodness and blessing, not to physical compression of time.

N. Ten nights

وَلَيَالٍ عَشْرٍ

And by ten nights.

Al-Fajr 89:2

ليالٍ is the plural of laylah. The phrase itself does not determine exactly which ten nights these are; that identification requires separate evidence.

O. Seven consecutive nights

سَخَّرَهَا عَلَيْهِمْ سَبْعَ لَيَالٍ وَثَمَانِيَةَ أَيَّامٍ حُسُومًا

He subjected it against them for seven nights and eight days, consecutively.

Al-Haqqah 69:7

Nights are units for counting the duration of punishment. Layali are natural units of night, not merely stages of systemic concealment.

11. Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

اللَّيْل

The general name or period of night. It may be linked to covering, stillness, sleep, worship, movement, and measuring time, but none alone is the full meaning.

لَيْلًا

A time adverb meaning at night.

لَيْلَة

A specific night. Layl is night as a period or kind; laylah is a defined unit of night.

لَيَالٍ

The plural of laylah, meaning nights.

12. Grammatical and Structural Notes

اللَّيْل

The definite article may refer to a known night, night as a kind, or the general period of night.

لَيْلًا

Often a time adverb:

أَسْرَىٰ بِعَبْدِهِ لَيْلًا

He carried His servant by night.

Al-Isra 17:1

Its indefinite form can point to part of the night or one night among nights.

لَيْلَةِ الْقَدْر

A genitive construction: laylah is night; qadr specifies that night. The meaning of qadr should be studied separately in the root Q D R.

سَبْعَ لَيَالٍ

The numeral comes with the plural of night and specifies a duration.

قُمِ اللَّيْلَ

The night is the time-vessel for standing and worship. The meaning is not that the night itself is made to stand, but that part of the night is devoted to standing.

Singular and plural

The Qur'an often uses layl in the singular, but the plural also appears:

وَلَيَالٍ عَشْرٍ

And by ten nights.

Al-Fajr 89:2

سَبْعَ لَيَالٍ

Seven nights.

Al-Haqqah 69:7

Grammatical forms follow sentence need and intended meaning, not a theory of the physical unity of darkness.

13. L Y L Compared with N H R

Layl is the period of night, and nahar is the bright period of day. Night is tied to reduced light and relative stillness; day is tied to greater visibility, movement, and seeking provision.

وَجَعَلْنَا اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ آيَتَيْنِ

And We made the night and the day two signs.

Al-Isra 17:12

Neither is merely the insignificant shell of the other. Both have roles in time and life.

14. L Y L Compared with Z L M

Layl is a time-period. Zulmah is the state of darkness or reduced light.

Night is usually accompanied by darkness, but they are not identical. Darkness can exist by day in a cave or closed room, and night may be lit by moon, stars, or lamps while remaining night.

15. L Y L Compared with S W D

Sawad concerns blackness or dark color. Layl is a period and environmental condition. An object may be black in daytime, and night may be lit by moon or other lights.

Therefore layl should not be understood as the black color of the world.

16. L Y L Compared with GH S Q

Ghasaq concerns the intensifying darkness of night or the beginning of its concentration. Layl covers the whole period of night.

إِلَىٰ غَسَقِ اللَّيْلِ

Until the darkening of the night.

Al-Isra 17:78

Ghasaq is attached to layl and specifies a stage of the night.

17. Specialized Discussion: Night, Stillness, and Natural Order

The Qur'an connects night with stillness:

لِتَسْكُنُوا فِيهِ

So that you may rest in it.

Yunus 10:67

But stillness does not mean total stoppage of all movement. At night many humans sleep, some animals become more active, some humans travel or work, believers may worship, and natural phenomena continue.

Reduced direct sunlight may help lower surface temperature and create conditions more suitable for sleep, but such effects are scientific explanations, not the lexical meaning of layl.

Night is not absolute absence of energy. At night there are still heat, moonlight, starlight, invisible radiation, and many natural movements. The Qur'an does not claim otherwise. The language of the verses is observational and guiding: night covers, people rest in it, day is stripped from it, and night and day follow each other.

Regarding Laylat al-Qadr, one must distinguish the vessel from the event. Laylah is one night. Its significance comes from the descent of the Qur'an, qadr, peace, and the descent of the angels and the Spirit in it. The word laylah itself only denotes a unit of night.

Night in the Qur'an is a real period of time and a sign of the order of creation. Some nights gain special rank because of what occurs in them, but the word laylah itself simply names one night.

18. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

اللَّيْل

night. In explanation: the darker period of the day-night cycle. “Hidden body of existence” is not a translation of the word.

لَيْلًا

by night; at night; in part of the night, according to context.

لَيْلَة

one night; a specific night.

لَيَالٍ

nights; several nights.

وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا يَغْشَىٰ

By the night when it covers.

جَعَلَ لَكُمُ اللَّيْلَ لِتَسْكُنُوا فِيهِ

He made the night for you so that you may rest in it.

نَسْلَخُ مِنْهُ النَّهَارَ

We strip the day from it. Or: We separate the day from it as skin is stripped.

لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْر

The Night of Qadr. The expression should usually be preserved, and qadr explained separately.

19. Conclusion

The root L Y L in the Qur'an concerns night: the darker period of the day-night cycle, marked by the fading of sunlight, covering of the environment, and relative stillness.

Night in the Qur'an is not evil, nothingness, or the domain of demons. It is one of the signs of creation and part of the order of life.

The Qur'an calls night a covering, presents it as a ground of rest, and uses it as a time for sleep, worship, night travel, and measuring duration.

Layl names the period of night. Laylah is one specific night, and layalin is its plural.

Night and darkness are not complete synonyms. Layl is a time-period; darkness is a state. Night can contain moonlight and starlight, and darkness can occur in daytime.

The image of stripping day from night is a rhetorical image for the removal of daylight and the appearance of darkness. It does not prove that night is the main body of the world and day a physical skin on it.

Laylat al-Qadr is a specific important night. Its importance comes from the descent of the Qur'an and the qualities stated in the verses, not from laylah meaning a compressed package of time and place.

The shortest core is: night, the darker and more covered period of the day-night cycle, which in the order of creation provides a setting for stillness, sleep, worship, movement, and counting time.
