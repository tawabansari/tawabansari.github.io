Foundational Meaning

The root S B Ḥ is first connected with swimming and smooth movement in water. In this image, a body does not sink or drown in its environment; rather, through continuous movement, it keeps itself on course.

From this material image, wider meanings are formed: free and smooth movement, going along a course, being far from heaviness and contamination, and then transcendence: declaring The God far from every deficiency, false attribution, partner, child, injustice, or limitation.

Important Forms

The simple form of the root is سَبَحَ (sabaḥa): to swim, to move smoothly, or to float along a course. In the Qur'an, the form يَسْبَحُونَ (yasbaḥūn) appears for the movement of heavenly bodies in orbit.

Its Form II is سَبَّحَ (sabbaḥa), to make tasbih. This form is very important in the Qur'an and is usually used concerning The God: the heavens and the earth make tasbih, beings make tasbih, believers are commanded to make tasbih, and messengers also make tasbih.

تَسْبِيح (tasbīḥ) is the verbal noun and names the act of tasbih itself. سُبْحَان (subḥān) is another very important form of this root. In Qur'anic use, subḥāna Allāh means that The God is pure, far, and above what is falsely attributed to Him.

سَبْح (sabḥ) also appears as a noun or verbal noun, as in سَبْحًا طَوِيلًا (sabḥan ṭawīlan) and وَالسَّابِحَاتِ سَبْحًا (wa-al-sābiḥāti sabḥan). This form preserves the image of movement and smooth motion. سَابِحَات (sābiḥāt) is a feminine plural active participle and points to things that move in a floating, smooth way. مُسَبِّحُون (musabbiḥūn) means those who make tasbih.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of S B Ḥ is smooth and free movement within an environment, in such a way that the thing does not sink into it and does not leave its course. At the material level, a swimmer moves in water and keeps themselves in the current. This motion is not static; if the swimmer loses motion, the danger of sinking and drowning appears.

So in the root S B Ḥ there is movement, steadiness in a course, and staying away from falling. At a broader level, the sun and the moon yasbaḥūn in their orbit: they are floating and moving in their course, not stationary, not without orbit, and not outside the order of motion.

When this same root moves to Form II and is used in relation to The God, the meaning moves from material motion to linguistic and existential transcendence. Tasbih means declaring The God far from false attributions and regarding Him as pure from deficiencies, partners, and limitations made by the human mind or tongue.

Material Image

The main image of the root is a swimmer in water. If the swimmer loses motion, they sink; but with coordinated movement, they remain on the surface and in their course. From here, S B Ḥ carries both motion and freedom from sinking.

This image matters for Qur'anic usage: beings are floating in their own order, each moves in its own orbit, and tasbih too is a kind of keeping understanding and language in the right orbit concerning The God.

In classical Arabic, sabaḥa was used for swimming in water. It then expanded to smooth and extended movement in space, a path, or a field of motion. Words from the same field are also mentioned for a fast horse with an extended stride: a being that moves broadly and distances itself from others. This layer helps the meaning of transcendence as well: tasbih is putting distance between the rank of The God and attributions that are not worthy of Him.

Older Arabic Usage

In older Arabic, sabaḥa was known first of all through swimming and movement in water. Arabic understood bodily motion in water through this root: a motion in which the body does not drown, but moves through effort and order on the surface or in depth. From this image came extended uses for smooth movement, stretched movement, and swift passing.

If a precise early Arabic witness is found for sabaḥa in the meaning of swimming, horse movement, or smooth motion in space, it can be added here. Without such a precise witness, it is better to avoid a specific claim about pre-Islamic poetry and remain with the general lexical field.

Movement of Heavenly Bodies in Orbit

The first Qur'anic field is the movement of heavenly bodies in orbit:

كُلٌّ فِي فَلَكٍ يَسْبَحُونَ (kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn)

Each is floating in an orbit.

Al-Anbiya' 21:33

وَكُلٌّ فِي فَلَكٍ يَسْبَحُونَ (wa-kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn)

And each is floating in an orbit.

Ya-Sin 36:40

In these two verses, yasbaḥūn is from the simple form of the root. The meaning is not uttering verbal remembrance; it is smooth movement in an orbit. The sun, the moon, the night, and the day move within their order. One foundation for understanding this root, then, is that sabaḥa is not only speech; it is also movement in a course.

Tasbih of All Beings

The second field is the tasbih of all beings:

تُسَبِّحُ لَهُ السَّمَاوَاتُ السَّبْعُ وَالْأَرْضُ وَمَن فِيهِنَّ (tusabbiḥu lahu al-samāwātu al-sab'u wa-al-arḍu wa-man fīhinna)

The seven heavens, the earth, and whoever is in them make tasbih for Him.

Al-Isra' 17:44

The same verse continues:

وَإِن مِّن شَيْءٍ إِلَّا يُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِهِ وَلَٰكِن لَّا تَفْقَهُونَ تَسْبِيحَهُمْ (wa-in min shay'in illā yusabbiḥu bi-ḥamdihi walākin lā tafqahūna tasbīḥahum)

And there is nothing except that it makes tasbih with His praise, but you do not understand their tasbih.

Al-Isra' 17:44

This verse is important for understanding tasbih. The tasbih of all beings does not necessarily mean human speech. The verse itself says that you do not understand their tasbih. So tasbih should be seen as wider than human language: every being, in its existence, motion, order, dependence, and station, declares The God far from false attributions and shows that He is not needy, partner-bearing, or limited.

Tasbih of Birds

The third field is the tasbih of birds and living beings:

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يُسَبِّحُ لَهُ مَن فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَالطَّيْرُ صَافَّاتٍ (alam tara anna allāha yusabbiḥu lahu man fī al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍi wa-al-ṭayru ṣāffātin)

Did you not see that whoever is in the heavens and the earth makes tasbih for The God, and the birds while spread-winged?

An-Nur 24:41

Then it says:

كُلٌّ قَدْ عَلِمَ صَلَاتَهُ وَتَسْبِيحَهُ (kullun qad 'alima ṣalātahu wa-tasbīḥahu)

Each has known its salat and its tasbih.

An-Nur 24:41

In this use, birds are mentioned with their bodily state and movement in the air. This is an important witness that tasbih in the Qur'an is not always limited to human speech. Every being has its suitable form: the human being with tongue and action, the bird with its order of movement and being, and heavenly bodies with their orbit.

Tasbih of Believers and Messengers

The fourth field is the tasbih of believers and messengers:

فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ (fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika)

So make tasbih with the praise of your Lord.

Al-Hijr 15:98

سَبِّحِ اسْمَ رَبِّكَ الْأَعْلَى (sabbiḥ isma rabbika al-a'lā)

Make tasbih of the name of your highest Lord.

Al-A'la 87:1

In this use, the human being is commanded to make tasbih of The God. This tasbih can be with the tongue, but it is not only words. If tasbih is understood from the core of the root, the human being must keep tongue, thought, and action away from attributing deficiency, partnerhood, or falsehood to The God.

Subhan

The fifth field is سُبْحَان (subḥān). The Qur'an uses this form often:

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ (subḥāna allāhi 'ammā yushrikūn)

Pure and far is The God from what they associate as partners.

Al-Hashr 59:23

سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَىٰ عَمَّا يَقُولُونَ (subḥānahu wa-ta'ālā 'ammā yaqūlūn)

Pure and exalted is He above what they say.

Al-Isra' 17:43

Subḥān is a word of transcendence. It means that The God is far from false speech, false attribution, partner, child, need, weakness, or any deficiency. It is better not to translate subḥān only as pure, because it also carries the meaning of distance and removal from an improper attribution. In many contexts, pure and far is, transcendent is, or far above and pure is will be closer to the context.

Subḥān often comes in places where a false attribution has been made to The God or where the station of The God must be purified from a limiting conception. Therefore subḥān is a linguistic answer to false attribution. When subḥāna Allāh is said, it means that The God is far from what you imagine or attribute.

Sabhan Tawilan

The sixth field is sabḥ in the meaning of extended movement and effort during the day:

إِنَّ لَكَ فِي النَّهَارِ سَبْحًا طَوِيلًا (inna laka fī al-nahāri sabḥan ṭawīlan)

Indeed, for you in the day there is extended movement.

Al-Muzzammil 73:7

In this verse, sabḥan ṭawīlan points to extended movement and occupation during the day. This use fits the same core of smooth and broad movement. Therefore sabḥ in the Qur'an does not only mean transcendence; in some uses it remains close to movement and going about.

As-Sabihat

The seventh field is سَابِحَات (sābiḥāt):

وَالسَّابِحَاتِ سَبْحًا (wa-al-sābiḥāti sabḥan)

And by those that swim or float, with a swimming motion.

An-Nazi'at 79:3

In this expression, the word itself preserves the image of floating and smooth movement. Identifying the exact referent of sābiḥāt requires a separate discussion, but for root analysis the main point is clear: smooth, floating, and extended movement in a field of motion.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

Yasbaḥūn in the orbit verses refers to the movement of heavenly bodies, not verbal tasbih.

Yusabbiḥu and tusabbiḥu concerning beings can point to a form of tasbih whose quality we do not understand, just as the verse says لَا تَفْقَهُونَ تَسْبِيحَهُمْ (lā tafqahūna tasbīḥahum).

Sabḥ in سَبْحًا طَوِيلًا (sabḥan ṭawīlan) is close to extended movement and occupation, not verbal transcendence.

Subḥān is almost always in the field of declaring The God far from false attributions.

Sabbiḥ bismi rabbika or fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika points to human tasbih, which can include words, attention, worship, and keeping attribution concerning The God pure.

Structural Notes

In كُلٌّ فِي فَلَكٍ يَسْبَحُونَ (kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn), the preposition fī is important. Movement happens inside an orbit. Falak is the container of motion, and yasbaḥūn shows smooth movement within that container.

In يُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِهِ (yusabbiḥu bi-ḥamdihi) or فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ (fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika), the bā' in bi-ḥamd shows that tasbih is accompanied by praise. Tasbih is declaring The God far from deficiency; hamd is praising Him for perfection, blessing, and Lordship. The two are close, but they are not the same.

In سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ (subḥāna allāhi 'ammā yushrikūn), 'ammā shows the direction of distance: The God is transcendent and far from what they associate as partners. Thus subḥān in this structure is not merely an independent remembrance; it is tied to negating a false attribution.

Nearby Roots

S B Ḥ is not the same as Ḥ M D. Hamd is praise: recognizing and expressing goodness, perfection, or blessing. Tasbih is transcendence: declaring distance from deficiency and false attribution. When the Qur'an says yusabbiḥu bi-ḥamdihi, it brings both layers together: transcendence together with praise.

S B Ḥ also differs from Q D S. Quds focuses on purity, sanctity, and spiritual cleanliness. Sabḥ focuses on declaring distance from deficiency and remaining in the proper orbit. Both are near purity, but their root images are not the same.

S B Ḥ is not the same as Z K W. Zakā is connected with pure growth, flourishing, and becoming clean. Sabḥ is connected with floating, distance from deficiency, and transcendence. Tazkiyah can be growth and purification; tasbih is transcendence and distancing from false attribution.

S B Ḥ also differs from J R Y. Jary is flowing or running in a course, like the flow of water or the movement of a ship. Sabḥ is floating and light movement in an environment. In sabḥ, the image of not drowning and moving smoothly is stronger.

S B Ḥ is not the same as Ṭ W F. Ṭawāf is movement around something. Sabḥ can be in an orbit, but its root image is floating and smooth movement, not necessarily circling around a center.

S B Ḥ differs from S Y R. Sayr is movement and going along a path, especially earthly or historical movement. Sabḥ is floating and smooth movement in an environment: water, space, orbit, or the semantic field of transcendence.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For يَسْبَحُونَ (yasbaḥūn) in the orbit verses, suitable renderings are they float, they move in a floating motion, or they move smoothly in their orbit. Translating it as they say tasbih is not suitable in those verses, because the context is the movement of heavenly bodies.

For سَبَّحَ (sabbaḥa) in Form II, depending on context, one may use made tasbih, declared transcendent, regarded as pure and far, or remembered in purity.

For تَسْبِيح (tasbīḥ), the main rendering is tasbih; in explanation, it can be described as declaring The God pure from deficiency and false attribution.

For سُبْحَان (subḥān), suggested renderings are pure and far is, transcendent is, or far above and pure is. In contexts where a false attribution is rejected, pure and far from... is stronger than pure alone.

For سَبِّحِ اسْمَ رَبِّكَ (sabbiḥ isma rabbika), one may say: remember the name of your Lord in purity, or declare the name of your Lord transcendent. The object is isma rabbika, so the command should not be treated as a verb without an object.

For سَبْحًا طَوِيلًا (sabḥan ṭawīlan), suitable renderings are extended movement and occupation, long going about, or broad movement in the day's work, not long tasbih unless the explanation preserves the context.

Summary

The root S B Ḥ indicates smooth movement, floating, not drowning, and remaining in a course. At the material level, a swimmer moves in water and stays in their path. In Qur'anic use, this image appears both for the movement of heavenly bodies in orbit and for the tasbih of beings and human beings.

Subḥān is the transcendence branch of this root: The God is pure and far from deficiency, partner, child, need, and every false attribution. Tasbih is not merely saying a word; it is keeping language, understanding, and attribution in the right orbit concerning The God. Its difference from hamd is that hamd praises perfection, while tasbih declares distance from deficiency and false attribution.
