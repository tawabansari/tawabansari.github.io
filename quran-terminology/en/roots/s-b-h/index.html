Foundational Meaning

Sabaḥa denotes swimming and smooth movement; sabbaḥa, in religious usage, denotes glorifying The God and declaring Him free from imperfection. These branches must be distinguished by verb form and context. Neither “not drowning” nor “remaining in orbit” is a fixed root definition, and the image of swimming does not establish the historical origin of the sense of glorification.

Important Forms

The simple form of the root is سَبَحَ (sabaḥa): to swim, to move smoothly, or to float along a course. In the Qur'an, the form يَسْبَحُونَ (yasbaḥūn) appears for the movement of heavenly bodies in orbit.

Its Form II is سَبَّحَ (sabbaḥa), to make tasbih. This form is very important in the Qur'an and is usually used concerning The God: the heavens and the earth make tasbih, beings make tasbih, believers are commanded to make tasbih, and Prophets also make tasbih.

تَسْبِيح (tasbīḥ) is the verbal noun and names the act of tasbih itself. سُبْحَان (subḥān) is another very important form of this root. In Qur'anic use, subḥāna Allāh means that The God is pure, far, and above what is falsely attributed to Him.

سَبْح (sabḥ) also appears as a noun or verbal noun, as in سَبْحًا طَوِيلًا (sabḥan ṭawīlan). This form preserves the image of movement and smooth motion. سَابِحَات (sābiḥāt) is a feminine plural active participle and points to things that move in a floating, smooth way. مُسَبِّحُون (musabbiḥūn) means those who make tasbih.

Linguistic Note

A shared root does not make all its uses identical. Yasbaḥūn belongs to the simple verb, while yusabbiḥūn belongs to Form II. The former denotes movement in the orbit passages; the latter denotes glorification in devotional contexts. Glorification cannot be explained merely as an intensified form of swimming.

Material Image

Swimming in water provides a concrete image for sabḥ and helps explain extended, flowing motion. It does not establish how created beings glorify The God. Expressions such as “keeping understanding in the right orbit” may serve as explanatory metaphors, but they are not lexical translations of tasbīḥ.

Older Arabic Usage

In the Muʿallaqah attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Imruʾ al-Qays, a horse is described as follows:

مِسَحٍّ إِذَا مَا السَّابِحَاتُ عَلَى الْوَنَى
أَثَرْنَ الْغُبَارَ بِالْكَدِيدِ الْمُرَكَّلِ

[A horse] pouring forth its speed, while the smoothly running horses, despite their weariness, raise dust on the hard, hoof-beaten ground.

Sābiḥāt here describes running horses. The witness illustrates the extension of the swimming image to a horse’s movement; it does not establish either the devotional meaning of glorification or the identity of the sābiḥāt in Sūrat al-Nāziʿāt.

Movement of Heavenly Bodies in Orbit

وَهُوَ الَّذى خَلَقَ الَّيلَ وَالنَّهارَ وَالشَّمسَ وَالقَمَرَ كُلٌّ فى فَلَكٍ يَسبَحونَ

And He is the One who created the night and the day, and the sun and the moon; each swims in an orbit.

Al-Anbiya 21:33

لَا الشَّمسُ يَنبَغى لَها أَن تُدرِكَ القَمَرَ وَلَا الَّيلُ سابِقُ النَّهارِ وَكُلٌّ فى فَلَكٍ يَسبَحونَ

The sun cannot overtake the moon, nor does the night outstrip the day; each swims in an orbit.

Ya-Sin 36:40

In both verses, yasbaḥūn expresses movement in an orbit. It is morphologically distinct from the verb for glorification. The orbit belongs to the context of these passages, not to the definition of every derivative of the root.

Tasbih of All Beings

تُسَبِّحُ لَهُ السَّمٰوٰتُ السَّبعُ وَالأَرضُ وَمَن فيهِنَّ وَإِن مِن شَىءٍ إِلّا يُسَبِّحُ بِحَمدِهِ وَلٰكِن لا تَفقَهونَ تَسبيحَهُم إِنَّهُ كانَ حَليمًا غَفورًا

The seven heavens, the earth, and whoever is in them glorify Him. There is nothing that does not glorify Him with His praise, but you do not understand their glorification. Indeed, He is Forbearing, Ever-Forgiving.

Al-Isra 17:44

The verse associates glorification with praise and explicitly states that human beings do not understand it. We should neither restrict it to human speech nor assert that it is nothing but motion or natural order. Seeing signs of divine perfection in creation is an interpretive reflection; this sentence alone does not disclose the manner of every being’s glorification.

Tasbih of Birds

أَلَم تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يُسَبِّحُ لَهُ مَن فِى السَّمٰوٰتِ وَالأَرضِ وَالطَّيرُ صٰفّٰتٍ كُلٌّ قَد عَلِمَ صَلاتَهُ وَتَسبيحَهُ وَاللَّهُ عَليمٌ بِما يَفعَلونَ

Have you not seen that whoever is in the heavens and the earth glorifies The God, as do the birds with wings outspread? Each has known its salat and glorification. The God knows what they do.

An-Nur 24:41

The verse mentions birds with outspread wings without stating that their glorification is identical with wing movement. The translation here takes each creature as the subject of ʿalima; another syntactic reading assigns knowledge of each creature’s salat and glorification to The God. This grammatical question should remain distinct from the basic meaning of glorification.

Tasbih of Believers and Messengers

فَسَبِّح بِحَمدِ رَبِّكَ وَكُن مِنَ السّٰجِدينَ

So glorify your Lord with praise, and be among those who prostrate.

Al-Hijr 15:98

سَبِّحِ اسمَ رَبِّكَ الأَعلَى

Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High.

Al-A'la 87:1

In Al-Ḥijr, the command to glorify accompanies praise and prostration; this connection places glorification within devotional practice. In Al-Aʿlā, the Lord’s name is the object of the command. Glorification is devotional in these contexts. Its implications for thought and conduct may be discussed without inserting that commentary into the direct translation.

Subhan

هُوَ اللَّهُ الَّذى لا إِلٰهَ إِلّا هُوَ المَلِكُ القُدّوسُ السَّلٰمُ المُؤمِنُ المُهَيمِنُ العَزيزُ الجَبّارُ المُتَكَبِّرُ سُبحٰنَ اللَّهِ عَمّا يُشرِكونَ

He is The God; there is no deity except Him: the Sovereign, the All-Holy, the Source of Peace, the Giver of Security, the Guardian, the Almighty, the Compeller, the Possessor of Greatness. Exalted is The God above what they associate with Him.

Al-Hashr 59:23

سُبحٰنَهُ وَتَعٰلىٰ عَمّا يَقولونَ عُلُوًّا كَبيرًا

Glory be to Him! He is far exalted above what they say.

Al-Isra 17:43

In the formula of transcendence, subḥān is analyzed as an accusative verbal noun or noun of verbal meaning expressing glorification. In Al-Ḥashr, the divine names and attributes are followed by rejection of association with The God. In Al-Isrāʾ, glorification accompanies His great exaltation above what is said. Its rendering should fit the whole sentence.

Sabhan Tawilan

إِنَّ لَكَ فِى النَّهارِ سَبحًا طَويلًا

Indeed, during the day you have ample scope for activity.

Al-Muzzammil 73:7

Set against the nighttime practice, sabḥan ṭawīlan indicates ample daytime scope for activity and attending to affairs. Lexical explanations include both activity and available time. “Prolonged glorification” overlooks the distinction between the simple form and Form II and the contrast between night and day.

As-Sabihat

وَالسّٰبِحٰتِ سَبحًا

And by those that swim along.

An-Nazi'at 79:3

Al-sābiḥāt is a feminine plural active participle of the simple verb, and sabḥan emphasizes the action. The form alone does not identify its referents; angels, stars, or other beings should not be inserted into the translation without identifying that as interpretation.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

Yasbaḥūn in the orbit passages, sabḥ in the daily schedule, and sābiḥāt in the oath in Al-Nāziʿāt belong to the movement branch. Sabbaḥa, tasbīḥ, and subḥān in devotional contexts concern glorification and transcendence. Shared consonants must not obscure differences of form and context.

Structural Notes

In the orbit verses, fī locates the movement within an orbit. In glorification with praise, bi- expresses the accompanying praise. The construction ʿammā relates transcendence to an attribution or association being rejected. These syntactic relations clarify the sentence without defining every use of the root.

Nearby Roots

Praise emphasizes commendation, while glorification emphasizes freedom from imperfection; their combination does not make them identical. Q D S concerns holiness and purity, and Z K W growth and purification. In the motion branch, running, traveling, and circumambulating are not interchangeable with swimming: swimming need not be circular.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For yasbaḥūn in the orbit passages, “swim” or “move along” fits. For Form II in devotional settings, “glorify” or “declare free from imperfection” should be selected according to the object and construction. Subḥān may be rendered “glory be to” or “exalted is.” In Al-Muzzammil, “ample scope for activity” preserves the context better than “prolonged glorification.”

Summary

This family has two prominent uses: swimming and extended movement, and devotional glorification. Understanding them requires attention to morphology, syntax, and context. The verses about created beings’ glorification establish its breadth and our limited understanding of its manner; they should not be reduced to a definitive mechanical explanation.
