The Qur’anic Root GH R Q

Sinking into water, being overwhelmed so that escape is closed, and causing drowning through the complete surrounding of water

1. Short Introduction

The root GH R Q moves in the field of sinking into water, being fully submerged, being surrounded by liquid, and losing the ability to breathe or escape.

The main image is a being overpowered by water, going below the surface and unable to pull itself out. Drowning is therefore not simply being wet or being in water; it is a state in which the water-environment overcomes the person or object.

In the Qur’an, this root is mostly used in stories of peoples who faced drowning because of denial, injustice, and continued transgression, including the household of Pharaoh and the people of Noah.

The shortest core is: complete sinking in water so that the environment overpowers the person or object and the way of rescue is lost.

2. Important Forms

غَرِقَ / يَغْرَقُ

He drowned; he sank in water and could not save himself.

أَغْرَقَ / يُغْرِقُ

He drowned another or caused another to sink in water.

أَغْرَقْنَا

We drowned. This form appears in the Qur’an about Pharaoh’s people and other deniers.

أُغْرِقُوا

They were drowned; the passive form highlights drowning happening to them.

مُغْرَقُون

Those who are drowned, or those whose drowning has become certain.

الْغَرَق

The drowning itself, or the state in which water overcomes a person.

غَرْقًا

An accusative verbal noun or emphatic form; in Sūrat an-Nāzi‘āt its precise meaning depends on the interpretation of an-nāzi‘āt.

4. Semantic Core

The semantic core is sinking into water and coming under its surrounding power so that the ability to breathe, move, or exit disappears.

Drowning is a physical event in itself. In the Qur’an, its moral meaning comes from context. Drowning may be a consequence of injustice and denial, but every drowning in language is not automatically divine punishment.

GH R Q is not the same as general destruction. Destruction is broader; drowning is a particular form connected with water.

5. Material Image

The direct image is a person or vessel losing the surface of water and sinking below it. Water blocks breathing and the body can no longer remain in its normal environment.

The lexical meaning does not need medical detail. It concerns sinking in water and inability to escape.

Arabic can also use drowning metaphorically for one who is overwhelmed by debt, work, sin, or sorrow, but the main Qur’anic cases are real drowning.

6. Early Arabic Usage

Ghariqa fi al-ma’ means he drowned in water. Aghraqahu al-ma’ means the water drowned him. Al-ghariq is a drowned person, whether rescued or dead; context clarifies.

Metaphorical usage describes being overwhelmed by something. Reports about drawing a bow to its limit should not be treated as the core Qur’anic meaning.

7. Misuse and Restriction

One restriction is to turn Qur’anic drowning into only a psychological symbol, such as drowning in sin. Such metaphor exists in language, but the stories of Pharaoh and Noah speak of real drowning.

The opposite mistake is to turn every Qur’anic drowning into a full theory of the destruction of a civilization or state apparatus. The verses speak of people and groups drowning, but do not always mention every political or military object.

Drowning in the Qur’an is related to the decision and command of The God, without denying natural means such as water, wave, storm, and the closing of escape.

8. Methodological Note

Distinguish physical drowning, causing another to drown, the moral and historical consequence, metaphorical drowning, and the disputed use of gharqan in Sūrat an-Nāzi‘āt.

The drowning of Pharaoh is presented in the story as divine judgment, but this does not permit every natural event today to be declared a certain divine punishment without clear textual proof.

9. Related Semitic Languages

Related forms in some Semitic languages connect with sinking, submersion, or disappearing in water. This may show the antiquity of the semantic field, but Qur’anic meaning must first be read through Arabic usage and Qur’anic context.

10. Qur’anic Uses

A. Drowning the household of Pharaoh

وَإِذْ فَرَقْنَا بِكُمُ الْبَحْرَ فَأَنْجَيْنَاكُمْ وَأَغْرَقْنَا آلَ فِرْعَوْنَ وَأَنْتُمْ تَنْظُرُونَ

And when We split the sea for you, then We saved you and drowned the household of Pharaoh while you were looking on.

Al-Baqarah 2:50

The same sea becomes passage for one group and drowning-place for another.

B. Drowning after denial

فَكَذَّبُوهُ فَأَنْجَيْنَاهُ وَمَنْ مَعَهُ فِي الْفُلْكِ وَأَغْرَقْنَا الَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا

So they denied him; then We saved him and those with him in the ship, and We drowned those who denied Our signs.

Al-A‘raf 7:64

C. The drowning of Noah’s people

مِمَّا خَطِيئَاتِهِمْ أُغْرِقُوا فَأُدْخِلُوا نَارًا

Because of their errors they were drowned, then they were entered into fire.

Nuh 71:25

D. Do not address Me about the wrongdoers

وَلَا تُخَاطِبْنِي فِي الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا إِنَّهُمْ مُغْرَقُونَ

Do not address Me about those who did wrong; they are surely to be drowned.

Hud 11:37

E. The army to be drowned

فَأَسْرِ بِعِبَادِي لَيْلًا إِنَّكُمْ مُتَّبَعُونَ
وَاتْرُكِ الْبَحْرَ رَهْوًا إِنَّهُمْ جُنْدٌ مُغْرَقُونَ

Set out with My servants by night, for you will be pursued; and leave the sea calm and open, for they are an army to be drowned.

Ad-Dukhan 44:23-24

F. Drowning reaches Pharaoh

حَتَّىٰ إِذَا أَدْرَكَهُ الْغَرَقُ قَالَ آمَنْتُ أَنَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا الَّذِي آمَنَتْ بِهِ بَنُو إِسْرَائِيلَ

Until, when drowning reached him, he said: I believe that there is no god except the One in whom the Children of Israel have believed.

Yunus 10:90

G. Preserving Pharaoh’s body

فَالْيَوْمَ نُنَجِّيكَ بِبَدَنِكَ لِتَكُونَ لِمَنْ خَلْفَكَ آيَةً

Today We preserve your body so that you may be a sign for those after you.

Yunus 10:92

H. Drowning among other forms of taking

فَكُلًّا أَخَذْنَا بِذَنْبِهِ فَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ أَرْسَلْنَا عَلَيْهِ حَاصِبًا وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ أَخَذَتْهُ الصَّيْحَةُ وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ خَسَفْنَا بِهِ الْأَرْضَ وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ أَغْرَقْنَا

Each We seized for his sin: among them were those upon whom We sent a stone-bearing wind, those taken by the cry, those We caused the earth to swallow, and those We drowned.

Al-‘Ankabut 29:40

11. Important Derivatives

Aghraqna is transitive: We drowned them. Ughriqu is passive: they were drowned. Mughraqun points to those whose drowning has become certain. Al-gharaq names the state of drowning. Gharqan in Sūrat an-Nāzi‘āt should be treated with interpretive caution.

وَالنَّازِعَاتِ غَرْقًا

An-Nazi‘at 79:1

12. Grammatical Notes

Aghraqna takes a direct object. Ughriqu removes the agent from the wording and focuses attention on the drowned. Adrakahu al-gharaq pictures drowning as an event that reaches and overtakes Pharaoh.

13. Compared with GH M R

GH M R means covering or overwhelming and may be used for water or other states. GH R Q is more specific: sinking in water until escape or breathing is lost.

14. Compared with H L K

Halak is general destruction. Drowning is one specific mode of destruction. Not every drowning must end in death in ordinary language, but most Qur’anic cases here are tied to destruction.

15. Compared with KH N Q

KH N Q concerns constricting the throat and closing breath. In drowning, breathing is also blocked, but the cause is sinking in water.

16. Specialized Discussion

In the story of Moses, three roots form a clear network: the sea is split, the Children of Israel are saved, and Pharaoh’s household is drowned.

فَرَقْنَا بِكُمُ الْبَحْرَ
فَأَنْجَيْنَاكُمْ
وَأَغْرَقْنَا آلَ فِرْعَوْنَ

Al-Baqarah 2:50

This should not become a simplistic law that nature always saves believers and destroys opponents. In this story it is a particular judgment at the end of a historical confrontation.

آلْآنَ وَقَدْ عَصَيْتَ قَبْلُ وَكُنْتَ مِنَ الْمُفْسِدِينَ

Yunus 10:91

The answer to Pharaoh shows that faith announced only after all paths are closed cannot transform a past of corruption into righteous action.

18. Conclusion

GH R Q denotes sinking into water and being overtaken by it so that normal breathing, movement, and escape disappear.

In the Qur’an it is mainly used for the people of Noah and the household of Pharaoh. These are real drownings in water, yet their moral and historical meaning is tied to denial, injustice, and transgression.

The shortest core is: complete sinking in water and being overwhelmed by it so that breath and rescue are lost.
