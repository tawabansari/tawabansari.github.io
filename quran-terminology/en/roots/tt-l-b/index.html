Seeking, pursuing, and requesting: from night pursuing day to searching for water and the weakness of false objects of worship

The Arabic root ṭ l b means seeking something, pursuing it, or trying to obtain it. What is sought becomes the object of desire or search, potentially prompting action to find, obtain, or reach it.

Arabic طلب is not confined to verbal requests. Searching for water in the desert, pursuing prey, claiming an entitlement, and seeking knowledge or an objective can all use this family.

طالب and مطلوب have a clear relationship: the former seeks or pursues; the latter is the person or thing toward which seeking, desire, or pursuit is directed.

Seeking does not itself mean obtaining what is sought. A search may succeed or fail; fulfillment requires another verb or contextual indication.

The root occurs four times in three verses: يَطْلُبُهُ in Al-Aʿrāf 7:54, طَلَبًا in Al-Kahf 18:41, and الطَّالِبُ and الْمَطْلُوبُ in Al-Ḥajj 22:73.

The first describes the succession of night and day. The second concerns water sinking into the ground beyond human reach. The third uses seeker and sought in a parable about powerless objects of worship.

Seeking and pursuing form the semantic core, while context determines the search, its aim, and whether attainment is possible.

1. Historical meaning and early Arabic usage

Seeking and trying to obtain

طَلَبَ الشَّيْءَ يَطْلُبُهُ طَلَبًا describes seeking something and trying to find or acquire it.

Older lexical explanations define it as مُحَاوَلَةُ وِجْدَانِ الشَّيْءِ وَأَخْذِهِ: trying to find and obtain something.

This definition has two important features.

First, seeking is directed toward an object of search or desire.

Second, it does not necessarily mean successful attainment. Effort may occur without obtaining the object.

طَلَبَ الْمَاءَ means searching for water, not necessarily finding it.

طَلَبَ الرَّجُلَ can mean looking for or pursuing a person.

طَلَبَ حَقَّهُ means claiming one's due or acting to obtain it.

The objects differ, but the relationship remains: a seeker pursues what is sought.

Pursuit and hunting

A clear Arabic usage concerns pursuing animals in hunting.

The seeker may be a hunter or pursuing animal; the sought is the fleeing prey.

Dhū al-Rummah says:

فَانْصَاعَ جَانِبُهُ الْوَحْشِيُّ وَانْكَدَرَتْ
يَلْحَبْنَ لَا يَأْتَلِي الْمَطْلُوبُ وَالطَّلَبُ

fa-nṣāʿa jānibuhu al-waḥshiyyu wa-nkadarat / yalḥabna lā yaʾtalī al-maṭlūbu wa-al-ṭalabu

“The wild bull veered aside, and the hunting dogs rushed after him; neither pursued nor pursuers spared their efforts.”

The poet describes an animal fleeing with dogs running behind it.

الْمَطْلُوب is the pursued animal, while الطَّلَب refers to the pursuers.

Besides being a verbal noun, طَلَب is recorded as a collective designation for seekers.

Here it denotes the group pursuing the prey.

This extends beyond verbal requesting: the dogs do not ask the animal to approach them; they pursue it.

Seeking can therefore involve following and trying to reach something without speech.

Dhū al-Rummah was an Umayyad poet. His verse witnesses early Islamic Arabic, not a period certainly before Quranic revelation.

No independently verified pre-Quranic poem securely dated and illustrating this exact usage was established in this review. Dictionaries record seeking and following, and the Quran directly uses them in the language of revelation.

Claiming an entitlement

Another branch concerns requesting something from another person.

طَالَبَهُ بِحَقٍّ means claiming a right believed to be owed by someone else.

مُطَالَبَة is its verbal noun and can express requesting and pursuing that claim.

The seeker need not be looking for a lost object: they may ask another person to fulfill a commitment or deliver what is owed.

A creditor, for example, may demand payment from a debtor.

مطالبة does not itself establish the validity of the claim.

Someone may demand a real entitlement or something not owed to them.

The root alone therefore establishes neither legitimacy nor wrongfulness.

Nor does claiming establish receipt: a person may demand what is due without yet obtaining it.

تَطَلَّبَ: repeated or sustained searching

تَطَلَّبَ الشَّيْءَ also means seeking something.

Some lexical accounts associate it with repeated searching, looking in different places, or more attentive effort.

Form V alone does not establish that every occurrence involves many repetitions, great difficulty, or lengthy effort.

Those features require the particular usage and context.

طَلَّبَ and اطَّلَبَ are also recorded in Arabic but do not occur in the Quran.

The semantic core

The lexical usages support this definition:

Seeking or pursuing something with a view to finding, reaching, receiving, or obtaining it.

This encompasses physical searching, desire, and requesting.

It does not itself specify the request's rightness, effort, duration, complete understanding, or success.

Even nonhuman descriptions, such as night and day pursuing one another, can use this relationship without attributing human-like will.

2. يَطْلُبُهُ حَثِيثًا: night and day in the order of creation

Al-Aʿrāf 7:54 states:

إِنَّ رَبَّكُمُ اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ اسْتَوَىٰ عَلَى الْعَرْشِ يُغْشِي اللَّيْلَ النَّهَارَ يَطْلُبُهُ حَثِيثًا وَالشَّمْسَ وَالْقَمَرَ وَالنُّجُومَ مُسَخَّرَاتٍ بِأَمْرِهِ أَلَا لَهُ الْخَلْقُ وَالْأَمْرُ تَبَارَكَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ

inna rabbakumu allāhu alladhī khalaqa al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍa fī sittati ayyāmin thumma istawā ʿalā al-ʿarshi yughshī al-layla al-nahāra yaṭlubuhu ḥathīthan wa-al-shamsa wa-al-qamara wa-al-nujūma musakhkharātin bi-amrihi alā lahu al-khalqu wa-al-amru tabāraka allāhu rabbu al-ʿālamīn

Al-Aʿrāf 7:54

“Your Lord is indeed The God, who created the heavens and earth in six days, then established Himself upon the Throne. He covers the day with night, which pursues it swiftly; and the sun, moon, and stars are subjected to His command. Surely creation and command belong to Him. Blessed is The God, Lord of all worlds.”

Al-Aʿrāf 7:54

يَطْلُبُهُ consists of the imperfect يَطْلُبُ and attached pronoun هُ.

It expresses following or pursuing something.

Night and day are described in succession: night covers and follows day.

The usage resembles pursuit, one thing moving after another.

The subject is the natural succession of night and day, not a human search for a lost object.

طلب must therefore be understood in this context.

حَثِيثًا: swiftness in pursuit

حَثِيثًا follows يَطْلُبُهُ.

It belongs to ح ث ث and here conveys haste or rapid pursuit.

Swiftness follows from حثيثا rather than طلب itself.

This distinction matters.

Seeking may be slow, sustained, or swift. Here حثيثا specifies speed; merely having an additional description, however, is not a universal test of whether a meaning is already present in a verb.

The two words together picture the succession of night and day.

Does night desire something as a human does?

The verse attributes طلب to night.

This does not itself establish a human-like intention, decision, or conscious desire in night.

Arabic and Persian can describe night following day or light following darkness without assigning human personhood to them.

The verb images pursuit and succession.

Nor should this image become a complete astronomical theory.

Day and night as experienced on earth relate to sunlight and the earth's motion, but طلب specifies no numerical speed, orbital direction, or physical structure.

The passage presents ordered, connected succession within creation.

Here طلب means following and pursuing, rather than requesting or searching for a lost thing.

3. فَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعَ لَهُ طَلَبًا: searching for inaccessible water

Al-Kahf 18:41 states:

أَوْ يُصْبِحَ مَاؤُهَا غَوْرًا فَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعَ لَهُ طَلَبًا

aw yuṣbiḥa māʾuhā ghawran fa-lan tastaṭīʿa lahu ṭalabā

Al-Kahf 18:41

“Or its water may sink away, and you will not be able to seek it.”

Al-Kahf 18:41

طَلَبًا is the verbal noun of ط ل ب.

The subject is garden water that may sink into the ground beyond its owner's reach.

غَوْرًا belongs to غ و ر and concerns sinking into depth and becoming inaccessible.

The verse continues:

فَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعَ لَهُ طَلَبًا

fa-lan tastaṭīʿa lahu ṭalabā

Al-Kahf 18:41

“Then you will not be able to seek it.”

تَسْتَطِيعَ expresses ability; طَلَبًا concerns seeking and pursuing the water.

The water may become so inaccessible that the owner cannot effectively seek and reach it.

Seeking water and attaining it

The distinction between seeking and obtaining matters here.

A person may search for water without finding it, or know its general location without being able to reach it.

طلب concerns the effort to find or obtain water.

فَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعَ adds inability.

Inability is therefore conveyed by the negation of ability, not the root طلب.

طَلَبًا itself does not mean digging a well, drilling, or constructing a water channel. These may be practical methods, but the word specifies none.

Water and the garden's flourishing

The account of two men and the gardens belonging to one of them supplies the context.

The owner boasts of wealth and strength in numbers and supposes his possessions will not perish.

Al-Kahf 18:35 states:

وَدَخَلَ جَنَّتَهُ وَهُوَ ظَالِمٌ لِنَفْسِهِ قَالَ مَا أَظُنُّ أَنْ تَبِيدَ هَٰذِهِ أَبَدًا

wa-dakhala jannatahu wa-huwa ẓālimun li-nafsihi qāla mā aẓunnu an tabīda hādhihi abadā

Al-Kahf 18:35

“He entered his garden while wronging himself and said: I do not think this will ever perish.”

Al-Kahf 18:35

His companion subsequently warns that the garden may be destroyed or its water sink away.

Water is a real foundation of the garden's flourishing.

Large acreage, many trees, and substantial wealth cannot preserve much of its productivity and life if its necessary water becomes inaccessible.

Seeking water therefore relates to maintaining possessions and cultivation in this passage.

Agriculture, wealth, and preserving capital are contextual themes, not definitions of طلب.

A warning against relying on possessions

Al-Kahf 18:41 describes the possibility of water sinking away.

It begins with أَوْ يُصْبِحَ as part of the companion's warning.

It should not be treated as a report that this precise event had already occurred at that moment.

Al-Kahf 18:42 describes the destruction of produce and the owner's regret; how each warning's details are realized must be assessed from the narrative.

Present prosperity should not be taken as proof of permanent immunity from changing conditions.

That contextual lesson is not the lexical meaning of طلب either.

Here طلب is searching for water; inability illustrates human limits when circumstances change.

4. الطَّالِبُ وَالْمَطْلُوبُ: seeker and sought

Al-Ḥajj 22:73 states:

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ ضُرِبَ مَثَلٌ فَاسْتَمِعُوا لَهُ إِنَّ الَّذِينَ تَدْعُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ لَنْ يَخْلُقُوا ذُبَابًا وَلَوِ اجْتَمَعُوا لَهُ وَإِنْ يَسْلُبْهُمُ الذُّبَابُ شَيْئًا لَا يَسْتَنْقِذُوهُ مِنْهُ ضَعُفَ الطَّالِبُ وَالْمَطْلُوبُ

yā ayyuhā al-nāsu ḍuriba mathalun fa-stamiʿū lahu inna alladhīna tadʿūna min dūni allāhi lan yakhluqū dhubāban wa-law ijtamaʿū lahu wa-in yaslubhumu al-dhubābu shayʾan lā yastanqidhūhu minhu ḍaʿufa al-ṭālibu wa-al-maṭlūb

Al-Ḥajj 22:73

“People, a parable is presented, so listen to it. Those you invoke besides The God will never create a fly, even if they gather for it. If the fly takes something from them, they cannot recover it from it. Weak are both the seeker and the sought.”

Al-Ḥajj 22:73

Two derivatives occur together here.

الطَّالِبُ is the active participle of طَلَبَ: the one seeking or pursuing.

الْمَطْلُوبُ is its passive participle: the person or thing sought.

Their structures and semantic roles differ.

The seeker initiates seeking or requesting; the sought is its object.

The verse places this relationship within a parable about powerless objects of worship.

The fly and the weakness of false objects of worship

The opening concerns beings or things invoked instead of The God.

They cannot create even a fly, despite gathering to do so.

A second image follows: they cannot recover anything a fly takes from them.

The subject of the example is clear.

Those invoked as deities can neither create the fly nor recover what it takes.

How then can they occupy the position of a true deity?

Their weakness is demonstrated through inability to create and recover something taken.

Who are the seeker and the sought?

Two recognized interpretations among those proposed explain the expression.

In the first, the seekers are the false objects of worship, hypothetically seeking to recover what a fly takes; the sought is the fly.

This connects directly with the preceding words:

لَا يَسْتَنْقِذُوهُ مِنْهُ

lā yastanqidhūhu minhu

Al-Ḥajj 22:73

“They cannot recover it from it.”

This reading emphasizes their powerlessness before a small fly.

Calling them seekers does not establish actual will or ability to search. The verse shows that even the hypothetical task of recovering something is beyond them.

In a second reading, the seeker is the human petitioner and the sought the false deity approached.

This connects with the opening reference to invoking others besides The God.

It emphasizes the weakness of the petitioner and the deity unable to fulfill the request.

The first reading connects more directly with the immediate account of the fly and failed recovery. The second relates to the broader theme of invoking false deities.

Neither changes the root's lexical meaning.

طالب is seeker or petitioner; مطلوب is the person or thing sought.

The difference concerns identifying the referents, not defining the words.

Is the seeker always weaker than the sought?

Both parties are described as weak in the verse.

This does not establish a general rule that either role is invariably weaker than the other.

They are simply two roles in a seeking relationship.

A powerful seeker may succeed, or the object may remain inaccessible. What is sought may be valuable or lack the value expected.

Weakness in Al-Ḥajj 22:73 follows from the parable and context, not from ط ل ب.

The verse does not imply that the fly lacks all natural capabilities, value, or a role in creation.

It is a living creature with its own structure and abilities, used here to demonstrate the weakness of false deities.

The central contrast is between their claimed divine status and actual inability.

Asking someone is not identical to worshipping them

The verse opens with تَدْعُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ.

دعا belongs to another root and concerns calling, summoning, or requesting.

طلب can overlap that field in contexts of requesting.

This does not make every interpersonal request an act of worship.

People may seek treatment from a physician, knowledge from a teacher, help from a neighbor, or fulfillment of an obligation from an institution.

Such requests do not inherently place those people in the role of a deity.

Al-Ḥajj 22:73 concerns those invoked instead of The God and assigned a status incompatible with their actual inability.

False worship and associating partners with The God arise from the context, not from طلب alone.

5. Distinguishing related roots

ط ل ب and ب غ ي: seeking and pursuing

ب غ ي has diverse usages: seeking or wanting in some constructions, aggression or injustice in others.

ابتغاء frequently concerns seeking divine approval, provision, or a means of approach.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:57 states:

أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ يَبْتَغُونَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِمُ الْوَسِيلَةَ أَيُّهُمْ أَقْرَبُ وَيَرْجُونَ رَحْمَتَهُ وَيَخَافُونَ عَذَابَهُ

ulāʾika alladhīna yadʿūna yabtaghūna ilā rabbihimu al-wasīlata ayyuhum aqrabu wa-yarjūna raḥmatahu wa-yakhāfūna ʿadhābahu

Al-Isrāʾ 17:57

“Those whom they invoke themselves seek a means of approach to their Lord—which of them is nearer—and hope for His mercy and fear His punishment.”

Al-Isrāʾ 17:57

يَبْتَغُونَ concerns seeking a means of nearness to the Lord.

طلب likewise describes seeking, so the families overlap in some usages.

Not every form of ب غ ي means seeking: aggression and wrongdoing are distinct contextual usages.

Nor does طلب itself imply aggression or oppression.

ط ل ب and س ع ي: seeking and striving

س ع ي concerns movement, effort, and striving toward an activity.

Al-Najm 53:39 states:

وَأَنْ لَيْسَ لِلْإِنْسَانِ إِلَّا مَا سَعَىٰ

wa-an laysa li-al-insāni illā mā saʿā

Al-Najm 53:39

“And that a human being has only what they have striven for.”

سعى here concerns human effort.

طلب may involve effort, but foregrounds the relationship between seeker and object.

Searching for desert water is طلب; walking, digging, or other actions may exemplify سعي as effort.

This is not an absolute rule that طلب is merely wanting while سعي is merely physical motion.

Both have broad fields and overlap in some usages.

ط ل ب and د ع و: requesting and searching

د ع و concerns calling, summoning, or requesting.

It appears at the beginning of Al-Ḥajj 22:73 before the seeker-and-sought expression.

Calling on someone and asking for something can involve both دعا and طلب.

دعا may simply summon someone, whereas طلب can describe searching without speech or a human addressee.

Night follows day in Al-Aʿrāf 7:54 without making a verbal request.

طلب should therefore not be restricted to praying or calling out.

ط ل ب and ب ح ث: seeking and digging

ب ح ث concerns investigating and searching, including digging or turning over earth.

Al-Māʾidah 5:31 states:

فَبَعَثَ اللَّهُ غُرَابًا يَبْحَثُ فِي الْأَرْضِ لِيُرِيَهُ كَيْفَ يُوَارِي سَوْأَةَ أَخِيهِ

fa-baʿatha allāhu ghurāban yabḥathu fī al-arḍi li-yuriyahu kayfa yuwārī sawʾata akhīhi

Al-Māʾidah 5:31

“Then The God sent a crow scratching in the earth to show him how to cover his brother's body.”

Al-Māʾidah 5:31

يَبْحَثُ here describes working the earth.

طلب in Al-Kahf 18:41 describes seeking water without specifying digging or drilling.

In these contexts, بحث names a particular action, while طلب expresses seeking and aiming to reach something.

Not every search should be reduced to one physical method.

ط ل ب and ر و د: wanting and seeking attainment

أَرَادَ and إِرَادَة belong to ر و د, not أ ر د, and concern wanting or willing in this usage.

Someone may want something without yet acting to find or obtain it.

طلب expresses seeking or orientation toward attainment and can involve practical action.

Wanting water and going in search of it are not exactly identical.

Yet طلب can also mean requesting or wanting, rather than invariably physical searching.

The fields overlap without being interchangeable everywhere.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

The principal Persian equivalents are seeking and being in pursuit of something.

Requesting, pursuing, following, and wanting may also fit particular contexts.

طَلَبَ: sought, pursued, wanted, or requested.

يَطْلُبُ: seeks, pursues, or follows.

طَلَب: seeking, desire, or pursuit.

طَالِب: seeker, petitioner, or pursuer.

مَطْلُوب: what is wanted or sought; the object of pursuit.

طَالَبَ in a claim context: demanded or claimed an entitlement.

تَطَلَّبَ: searched, sought out, or persistently requested.

مُطَالَبَة: requesting and pursuing a right or obligation.

مَطْلَب: something sought; also a place or object of seeking in other usages.

The Quran uses طالب, مطلوب, طلب, and يطلب, but not every derivative in the family.

The following renderings are proposed.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:54

يُغْشِي اللَّيْلَ النَّهَارَ يَطْلُبُهُ حَثِيثًا

yughshī al-layla al-nahāra yaṭlubuhu ḥathīthā

Al-Aʿrāf 7:54

“He covers the day with night, which swiftly follows it.”

Following naturally expresses طلب in this relation between night and day.

Al-Kahf 18:41

فَلَنْ تَسْتَطِيعَ لَهُ طَلَبًا

fa-lan tastaṭīʿa lahu ṭalabā

Al-Kahf 18:41

“Then you will not be able to seek it.”

The object is the water that has sunk away. Inability to access it can be explained in the reflection.

Al-Ḥajj 22:73

ضَعُفَ الطَّالِبُ وَالْمَطْلُوبُ

ḍaʿufa al-ṭālibu wa-al-maṭlūb

Al-Ḥajj 22:73

“Weak are both the seeker and the sought.”

This retains active and passive roles without substituting one interpretive identification for the words.

Explanation can discuss the immediate context of failing to recover something from a fly and the broader criticism of invoking powerless deities.

7. Summary

ط ل ب concerns seeking, pursuing, and trying to find or attain something.

Historical usages include searching for water, pursuing prey, requesting, and claiming entitlement. Dhū al-Rummah's Umayyad-era verse clearly distinguishes pursuers from pursued: الطلب names the pursuers and المطلوب the fleeing animal.

The root occurs four times in three Quranic verses.

يَطْلُبُهُ in Al-Aʿrāf 7:54 describes the succession of night and day, showing pursuit without a human conscious desire or verbal request.

طَلَبًا in Al-Kahf 18:41 concerns water that may sink beyond the owner's reach. Seeking is lexical; inability to seek and attain it follows from the full construction.

الطَّالِبُ and الْمَطْلُوبُ in Al-Ḥajj 22:73 name seeker and sought in a parable about powerless objects of worship. Their precise referents require the fly episode and broader context, while their lexical roles remain clear.

طلب can involve physical searching, pursuit, requesting, or orientation toward something.

It does not alone establish rightness, effort, duration, or success.

Being a seeker is neither inherently virtuous nor blameworthy. One may seek knowledge, truth, provision, or fulfillment of rights, or pursue something harmful. Evaluation depends on the object, intention, method, and consequences.

The root means seeking or pursuing something with a view to finding, reaching, receiving, or obtaining it, through practical pursuit or through requesting and claiming.

طالب is the seeker or petitioner; مطلوب is the person or thing toward which the search or desire is directed.

The key distinctions are that seeking is not finding, wanting is not being able, and being sought does not prove something's value or legitimacy.

People can strive without success, seek something unworthy, or ask someone incapable of granting the request.

The three Quranic contexts illustrate the relationship through creation's ordered succession, human limits in accessing water, and the weakness of false deities.

Understanding it distinguishes desire, ability, the reality of what is sought, and the possibility of attaining it.
