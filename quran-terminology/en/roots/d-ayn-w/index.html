Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root D AYN W (D ʿ W)

To call, summon, invite, invoke for help, ask, or name someone by a given relation and identity

Important Forms from This Root

دَعَا / يَدْعُو (daʿā / yadʿū)

he called, summoned, invited, invoked, or asked.

اُدْعُ / اُدْعُوا (udʿu / udʿū)

call; summon; invite.

دُعَاء (duʿāʾ)

calling, invocation, request, or turning toward someone with need.

دَاعِي / دَعْوَة (dāʿī / daʿwah)

caller, inviter, invitation, call, or summons.

اِدَّعَى / يَدَّعُونَ (iddaʿā / yaddaʿūn)

to claim, demand, or seek something for oneself.

أَدْعِيَاء (adʿiyāʾ)

adopted sons or those called by a lineage that is not their true lineage.

Method Note

The root D AYN W (D ʿ W) should not be reduced to “praying” in the narrow devotional sense. Its core field is calling or turning toward someone. A human being may call upon The God. A messenger may invite people to the way of The God. Those who associate others with The God may call upon powerless beings apart from Him. Someone may summon witnesses or supporters. A person may also be called by a name, father, or social relation.

For this root, translation must ask: who is calling whom, for what purpose, and in what situation? In the Qur’anic challenge, وَادْعُوا (wadʿū) does not mean devotional prayer. It means: summon your witnesses or supporters. In other contexts, the same root may mean worshipful invocation, invitation, request, public call, naming, attribution, or claim.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of D AYN W is directing a voice, request, or address toward a reference point: seeking presence, help, response, judgment, guidance, or attribution. The meaning changes according to the relationship between the caller and the one called.

When a servant calls upon The God, duʿāʾ carries need, humility, and servanthood. When the Prophet invites people to the way of his Lord, the root means calling people toward guidance. When a mushrik calls upon what is apart from The God, his hope and dependence turn toward something powerless. When someone summons witnesses during an argument, the root means calling them to be present and support the claim. When someone is called by his father or lineage, the root enters the field of identity and attribution.

In all these layers, duʿāʾ is not a sound released into empty space. It creates a relationship between caller and called, and it shows that the caller expects presence, response, effect, help, or recognition from the other side.

Material Image

The material image of this root is a person calling someone by voice. He turns toward a person or group, speaks a name or title, and by that call wants the other side to come, answer, help, or enter the scene. The root is not random sound. It is directed address.

The same image remains in deeper uses. In worship, the human being directs need toward The God. In invitation, a messenger or caller directs people toward a specific path. In argument, a person summons witnesses. In naming and lineage, a person is called by a specific relation, and social identity is shaped by that calling.

Qur’anic Usage

Summoning Witnesses in the Qur’anic Challenge

Surat al-Baqarah says: فَأْتُوا بِسُورَةٍ مِّن مِّثْلِهِ وَادْعُوا شُهَدَاءَكُم مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ إِن كُنتُمْ صَادِقِينَ (faʾtū bisūratin min mithlih, wadʿū shuhadāʾakum min dūni allāh in kuntum ṣādiqīn) 2:23.

Here wadʿū means “summon” or “call in” your witnesses. It is not devotional prayer. It is the calling of those who can appear, support the claim, or bear witness. The Qur’an opens the field of challenge: if you are truthful, bring a surah like it and summon your witnesses too.

Calling Upon The God with Need and Expectation of Response

The Qur’an says: ادْعُونِي أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ (udʿūnī astajib lakum) 40:60.

Here the root means calling upon The God: turning toward the Lord with need and request. This usage places D AYN W inside the direct relation between the human being and The God. To call upon The God is to direct need toward Him and expect answer from Him.

Inviting to the Way of the Lord

Surat al-Naḥl says: ادْعُ إِلَىٰ سَبِيلِ رَبِّكَ بِالْحِكْمَةِ وَالْمَوْعِظَةِ الْحَسَنَةِ (udʿu ilā sabīli rabbika bil-ḥikmati wal-mawʿiẓati al-ḥasanah) 16:125.

Here the root means to invite or call people toward the Lord’s way. This calling must be with wisdom and good counsel. D AYN W here is not a private request or prayer; it is directing human beings toward a clear and right path.

Calling Upon What Is Apart from The God

The Qur’an speaks of those who call upon what is apart from The God: وَيَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُمْ وَلَا يَنفَعُهُمْ (wayadʿūna min dūni allāhi mā lā yaḍurruhum walā yanfaʿuhum) 10:18.

Here the root means turning toward powerless references. The problem is not merely pronouncing a name. Hope, fear, need, and dependence are directed away from The God toward something that can neither harm nor benefit. This usage shows why duʿāʾ can become a marker of tawḥīd or shirk.

Calling People by Their True Lineage and Identity

Surat al-Aḥzāb says: ادْعُوهُمْ لِآبَائِهِمْ هُوَ أَقْسَطُ عِندَ اللَّهِ (udʿūhum li-ābāʾihim huwa aqsaṭu ʿinda allāh) 33:5.

Call them by their fathers; that is more just with The God. This verse shows that D AYN W is not only invocation or request. It also functions in naming and attribution. Calling a person by true lineage belongs to social justice and the preservation of identity. From the same root comes أَدْعِيَاء (adʿiyāʾ), those called as sons though their real lineage is not that.

Claiming or Seeking Something for Oneself

About the people of the Garden, Surat Yā Sīn says: وَلَهُمْ مَا يَدَّعُونَ (walahum mā yaddaʿūn) 36:57.

Here the form yaddaʿūn carries the meaning of claiming, asking for, or seeking something for oneself. They will have whatever they desire or request. This layer shows how D AYN W can move from “calling another” to “calling something toward oneself,” that is, claiming or seeking a share for oneself.

Difference from Nearby Words

Nādā focuses more on calling out openly or loudly. D AYN W is broader; it may include invitation, request, invocation, summons, and attribution. Saʾala is closer to asking or requesting. Duʿāʾ can be a request, but it is not always a direct question; sometimes it is a summons to presence or a call to action.

Istaghātha means seeking rescue or help in distress. Duʿāʾ may become istighāthah, but it does not always carry that level of emergency. Sentence structure is also important: دَعَا إِلَىٰ (daʿā ilā) usually means to invite toward a path, goal, or direction; دَعَا فُلَانًا (daʿā fulānan) may mean to summon a person; and دَعَا لِفُلَانٍ (daʿā li-fulān) may mean to pray or ask for someone’s good.

Translation Method

D AYN W should not always be translated as “pray.” In many verses, “call,” “summon,” or “invite” is more accurate. In al-Baqarah 2:23, وَادْعُوا شُهَدَاءَكُم (wadʿū shuhadāʾakum) does not mean “pray to your witnesses.” It means “summon your witnesses.” In al-Naḥl 16:125, the root means invite. In al-Aḥzāb 33:5, it means call people by the correct relation. In Yā Sīn 36:57, it means desire, claim, or request for oneself.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

وَادْعُوا شُهَدَاءَكُم (wadʿū shuhadāʾakum): summon your witnesses.

ادْعُونِي أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ (udʿūnī astajib lakum): call upon Me, and I will answer you.

ادْعُ إِلَىٰ سَبِيلِ رَبِّكَ (udʿu ilā sabīli rabbik): invite to the way of your Lord.

يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ (yadʿūna min dūni allāh): they call upon what is apart from The God.

ادْعُوهُمْ لِآبَائِهِمْ (udʿūhum li-ābāʾihim): call them by their fathers.

وَلَهُمْ مَا يَدَّعُونَ (walahum mā yaddaʿūn): they will have whatever they desire; they will have whatever they request.

Summary

The root D AYN W (D ʿ W) in the Qur’an is the root of directed calling. This calling may be invocation, invitation, summons, request, attribution, or claim. Its exact meaning must be read from context: sometimes the human being calls upon The God, sometimes he calls upon what is apart from The God, sometimes a messenger invites people to The God’s way, sometimes witnesses are summoned, and sometimes a person is called by a particular name or lineage.

Forqan translation should free D AYN W from the narrow automatic meaning of “prayer” and return it to its wider Qur’anic field: turning, by voice, request, or address, toward a reference from whom presence, response, help, recognition, or effect is expected.
