Central meanings: making firm and secure; writing and recording; and, in material uses, substantial compact pieces.

1. Introducing the root and its semantic field

Arabic Z B R ranges from constructing and strengthening material objects to writing, records, books, and restraining someone from an action.

Zabara can mean strengthening, as when a well’s walls are reinforced with stone. It also means writing or recording. Zabūr means a written text or book and in some Qur’anic passages denotes the scripture given to David.

Zubrah, by contrast, denotes a substantial piece, especially of iron. The root also occurs for forbidding, restraining, and speaking sharply.

An early lexical analysis distinguishes two principles:

أَحَدُهُمَا يَدُلُّ عَلَى إِحْكَامِ الشَّيْءِ وَتَوْثِيقِهِ، وَالْآخَرُ يَدُلُّ عَلَى قِرَاءَةٍ وَكِتَابَةٍ وَمَا أَشْبَهَ ذَلِكَ

aḥaduhumā yadullu ʿalā iḥkāmi al-shayʾi wa-tawthīqihi wa-al-ākharu yadullu ʿalā qirāʾatin wa-kitābatin wa-mā ashbaha dhālika

Meaning: “One indicates making something firm and secure; the other indicates reading, writing, and similar activities.”

This division prevents reducing every use to one restricted meaning such as writing or a piece of iron.

The Qur’an uses three principal forms: zabūr, zubur, and zubar. Their eleven occurrences concern writings, books, and iron pieces, with one zubur occurrence describing human division into groups.

2. Principal forms and derivatives

زَبَرَ يَزْبُرُ — zabara yazburu

Several meanings are recorded for this verb.

In construction, it means making something firm and secure.

زَبَرَ الْبِئْرَ

zabara al-biʾra

“He lined and strengthened the well’s wall with stone.”

In writing:

زَبَرَ الْكِتَابَ

zabara al-kitāba

“He wrote the book.”

Some lexical accounts associate the expression with careful, firm writing or engraving on a hard surface.

Reading a written text is also recorded, although writing is more prominent in the zabūr family.

زَبْر — zabr

A verbal noun that may concern construction and strengthening, writing, or restraining, depending on context.

Some Arabic expressions associate zabr with reason, firmness of judgment, and the ability to restrain oneself from improper conduct.

Mā lahu zabrun describes someone lacking sound judgment or self-restraint.

This usage connects firmness in material structures with human conduct and judgment.

زُبْرَة — zubrah

A substantial, compact piece of a material such as iron.

Its familiar plural is zubar.

Zubrat al-ḥadīd means a piece of iron.

The word also describes the prominent upper back and shoulders of an animal, especially the mass of hair forming a lion’s mane.

Size, prominence, and gathered material matter in these uses, but not every derivative necessarily denotes something bulky or heavy.

زَبُور — zabūr

Zabūr means a written text or book.

Morphologically, it is explained as faʿūl with a passive sense: something written, as certain other words of that pattern also have passive meanings.

In Qur’anic passages explicitly connected with David, zabūr denotes the book given to him.

Arabic also uses zabūr generally for a written text or book; it is not exclusively a proper name for sacred scripture.

زُبُر — zubur

As the plural of zabūr, it means books or writings. Al-Muʾminūn 23:53 also has a separate explanation in terms of pieces or divisions.

The Qur’an uses the form for earlier writings, texts associated with messengers, and records of deeds.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:53 uses it in a context of divided groups and requires separate examination.

مِزْبَر — mizbar

A writing instrument or pen.

This Arabic word does not occur in the Qur’an.

زَبَرَهُ عَنِ الْأَمْرِ — zabarahu ʿani al-amri

He restrained, forbade, or sharply prevented someone from doing something.

Zabr accordingly has a sense of rebuke and restraint.

Its connection with strengthening and control is intelligible, but should not be imposed on every written-text derivative.

3. Early Arabic usage

Strengthening a well’s walls

Wells were important sources of water. Walls dug in loose soil could collapse and need reinforcement.

Zabara al-biʾra refers to lining a well with stone and strengthening it.

Firmness and cohesion are clear: stones are placed together to form a resistant wall.

An early lexical analysis uses this example to explain the root’s first branch.

Zubrah for a substantial iron piece is intelligible within the same field.

Writing and recording

Zabartu al-kitāba is recorded for writing a book or text.

Early lexical accounts record both writing and reading, including writing associated with engraving on stone.

The root is therefore not confined to writing on paper. A text may be recorded on a hard surface, skin, or another material.

This does not mean every zabūr was necessarily inscribed on stone or had carved lettering.

A poetic witness from Labīd ibn Rabīʿah

Labīd says in his Muʿallaqah:

وَجَلَا السُّيُولُ عَنِ الطُّلُولِ كَأَنَّهَا
زُبُرٌ تُجِدُّ مُتُونَهَا أَقْلَامُهَا

wa-jalā al-suyūlu ʿani al-ṭulūli ka-annahā / zuburun tujiddu mutūnahā aqlāmuhā

Its sense is:

“The floods uncovered the remains of the dwellings, as though they were writings whose pens renew their lines.”

Zubur here means writings or books.

The poet compares old dwelling traces to writing obscured by dust and wear, its lines becoming visible again.

The witness shows the use of zubur for writing independently of interpretation of a Qur’anic verse.

It also shows that the plural could refer to ordinary writing and was not intrinsically restricted to heavenly books.

Labīd lived both before and after Islam. Without establishing this line’s date, it should not be classified as certainly pre-Islamic.

4. Qur’anic writings and books

Zabūr: the book given to David

Al-Nisāʾ 4:163 and Al-Isrāʾ 17:55 explicitly state that David was given zabūr.

In both verses, zabūran is singular and indefinite.

The sentence identifies something given by The God to David, making it a book or writing associated with revelation in this context.

Zabūr has the general Arabic meaning of writing or book, but these two occurrences identify David’s book through their explicit connection with him.

The general lexical meaning and this specific application should remain distinct.

Mentioning David’s Zabūr does not by itself settle the contents of every surviving Psalms text or its correspondence with the book given to David. That requires independent textual and historical study.

Zabūr in Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:105

وَلَقَدْ كَتَبْنَا فِي الزَّبُورِ مِن بَعْدِ الذِّكْرِ أَنَّ الْأَرْضَ يَرِثُهَا عِبَادِيَ الصَّالِحُونَ

wa-laqad katabnā fī al-zabūri min baʿdi al-dhikri anna al-arḍa yarithuhā ʿibādiya al-ṣāliḥūn

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:105

Translation:

“We indeed wrote in the Zabūr, after the Reminder, that My righteous servants shall inherit the earth.”

Al-zabūr is definite here.

Since two other verses connect zabūr with David’s book, that book is a possible referent here.

The general Arabic sense of writing or book is also established, and some interpretations understand the word more broadly as revealed writings.

Al-dhikr likewise has different proposed referents: an earlier writing, the Torah, or another record or reminder.

The verse does not repeat David’s or Moses’ name. A lexical study should not make any particular identification an indispensable meaning of zabūr.

The clear structural point is a written text containing the declaration that righteous servants will inherit the earth.

Katabnā, “We wrote,” reinforces the writing sense.

Zubur: earlier writings

Several verses use the plural zubur.

Āl ʿImrān 3:184 and Fāṭir 35:25 mention messengers arriving with clear signs, zubur, and the illuminating Book.

Al-Naḥl 16:44 also places zubur alongside clear signs.

“Writings” or “books” fits these usages.

Juxtaposing zubur with al-kitāb al-munīr does not necessarily establish two completely separate classes of scripture.

The expressions may concern different writings, different qualities of revealed messages, or ways of expressing truth. Coordinating related words does not by itself establish a fixed universal boundary.

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:196

وَإِنَّهُ لَفِي زُبُرِ الْأَوَّلِينَ

wa-innahu la-fī zuburi al-awwalīn

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:196

Translation:

“It is indeed in the writings of the earlier peoples.”

Zubur al-awwalīn means the writings of earlier peoples.

The preceding context concerns the Qur’an’s revelation, its source, and its clear Arabic language.

The verse concerns a message or report associated with revelation in earlier writings.

It should not be read as saying every Qur’anic expression appeared in precisely the same Arabic wording in earlier books.

The phrase affirms a presence in earlier writings without specifying its exact form, degree of verbal resemblance, or extent of shared content.

Preserving that distinction matters for accurate understanding.

Al-Qamar 54:43: writings and claimed immunity

أَكُفَّارُكُمْ خَيْرٌ مِّنْ أُولَٰئِكُمْ أَمْ لَكُم بَرَاءَةٌ فِي الزُّبُرِ

a-kuffārukum khayrun min ulāʾikum am lakum barāʾatun fī al-zubur

Al-Qamar 54:43

Translation:

“Are your disbelievers better than those, or do you have an exemption in the writings?”

Al-zubur here refers to writings that might be cited in support of a claim.

The question asks whether the addressees possess written assurance of exemption or immunity.

This fits the meaning of books or writings.

Zubur itself does not mean exemption, immunity, or a decree of salvation. Those ideas come from barāʾah and the interrogative construction.

Al-Qamar 54:52: recorded deeds

وَكُلُّ شَيْءٍ فَعَلُوهُ فِي الزُّبُرِ

wa-kullu shayʾin faʿalūhu fī al-zubur

Al-Qamar 54:52

Translation:

“Everything they did is in the records.”

Zubur here means writings or records in which deeds are preserved.

This differs significantly from usages concerning revealed books.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:163 concerns a book given to David; Al-Qamar 54:52 concerns the recording of actions.

Both involve writing, but their subject matter and function differ.

Zubur therefore cannot be translated as “heavenly books” in every verse.

Its Qur’anic range extends beyond names of prophetic scriptures.

Al-Qamar 54:53 continues with the recording of small and great matters. Together the verses emphasize deeds being recorded and preserved.

5. Zubar: pieces of iron

Al-Kahf 18:96

آتُونِي زُبَرَ الْحَدِيدِ حَتَّىٰ إِذَا سَاوَىٰ بَيْنَ الصَّدَفَيْنِ قَالَ انفُخُوا حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَعَلَهُ نَارًا قَالَ آتُونِي أُفْرِغْ عَلَيْهِ قِطْرًا

ātūnī zubara al-ḥadīdi ḥattā idhā sāwā bayna al-ṣadafayni qāla unfukhū ḥattā idhā jaʿalahu nāran qāla ātūnī ufrigh ʿalayhi qiṭran

Al-Kahf 18:96

Translation:

“Bring me pieces of iron.” When he had leveled the space between the two sides, he said, “Blow!” When he had made it fiery, he said, “Bring it to me, that I may pour molten metal over it.”

Zubar is the plural of zubrah here.

It means substantial pieces of iron.

This differs from books or writing and belongs to the material branch.

In Dhū al-Qarnayn’s account, the iron pieces are used to construct a strong barrier.

Al-Kahf 18:95 mentions making a radm, a barrier closing the passage.

Al-Kahf 18:97 describes the result: they could neither climb it nor make a breach in it.

The iron pieces are thus materials for an actual barrier.

Its complete engineering specifications cannot be extracted from zubar alone.

Zubar al-ḥadīd names the iron pieces. Their placement, heating, and the pouring of molten material are conveyed by the rest of the sentence.

Zubar itself does not mean a wall, dam, or metal structure.

6. Zuburan in Al-Muʾminūn: writings or separate portions?

Al-Muʾminūn 23:53

فَتَقَطَّعُوا أَمْرَهُم بَيْنَهُمْ زُبُرًا كُلُّ حِزْبٍ بِمَا لَدَيْهِمْ فَرِحُونَ

fa-taqaṭṭaʿū amrahum baynahum zuburan kullu ḥizbin bi-mā ladayhim fariḥūn

Al-Muʾminūn 23:53

Translation:

“They divided their affair among themselves into separate portions, each faction rejoicing in what it possessed.”

This is particularly important because the root’s two fields converge in interpretations of the verse.

The Ḥafṣ reading used here has zuburan, with ḍammah on both zāy and bāʾ.

One analysis takes it as the plural of zabūr, meaning books. The same vocalization also has an explanation as pieces or divisions; that meaning is not confined to the alternative vowel reading.

On the writing interpretation, people divided their shared affair among different writings or books, each group rejoicing in its own possession.

Another transmitted reading has zubaran, with fatḥah on the bāʾ, explained as the plural of zubrah, “piece.”

This supports fragmentation of the shared affair and division into separate groups, although the interpretation is also reported for zuburan.

Both directions are discussed in lexical and interpretive accounts.

The role of context

Al-Muʾminūn 23:52 states that the community is one community and The God is its Lord.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:53 then describes people dividing their affair.

“Each faction rejoicing in what it possessed” shows the resulting groups taking satisfaction in their own holdings.

Context therefore clearly supports division and fragmentation.

It does not entirely exclude a connection with differing books and writings.

Groups may rely on texts, beliefs, or interpretations and make them grounds for separation.

The word alone does not establish that every group wrote a new book or altered revelation. Such a claim requires independent evidence.

“Separate portions” conveys the broad contextual sense in a basic translation, while the explanation should retain the interpretation involving writings.

Disagreement and division

The criticism concerns a shared affair divided among groups, each rejoicing in its own possession.

It should not be taken as forbidding every disagreement, independent inquiry, or difference in understanding verses.

The passage concerns division accompanied by factional attachment and satisfaction with each group’s own position.

Al-Rūm 30:32 uses closely related language about those who divided their religion into groups, each faction rejoicing in what it had.

The connection reaches beyond the existence of different writings to differences and affiliations becoming boundaries that break a shared affair apart.

This conclusion comes from the passages together, not from zubur inherently meaning prejudice or sectarianism.

7. Related words

Z B R (zabr) and K T B (katb)

K T B has a broad Qur’anic range including writing, recording, and prescribing.

The written-text branch of Z B R likewise concerns recording and writing.

Its uses for iron pieces and strengthening material structures show that the roots are not complete synonyms.

Nor does Z B R always mean writing on stone while K T B means writing on paper. That boundary is incompatible with Arabic usage.

Z B R (zabūr) and Ṣ Ḥ F (ṣuḥuf)

Ṣuḥuf denotes sheets or writings carrying a text.

Zabūr likewise denotes writing or a book.

They can overlap in referring to revealed texts without having identical linguistic structures or ranges.

The Qur’an mentions the scrolls of Abraham and Moses, whereas zabūr is explicitly associated with David.

These different Qur’anic referents do not confine the words throughout Arabic to two wholly separate kinds of writing.

Z B R (zubar) and Q Ṭ ʿ (qaṭʿ)

Qaṭʿ concerns cutting and separating.

Material zubar denotes substantial, distinct pieces.

Al-Kahf 18:96 concerns iron pieces, but the noun does not necessarily specify how they were cut or separated.

In Al-Muʾminūn 23:53, fa-taqaṭṭaʿū expresses division and fragmentation.

The meaning of cutting and dividing should not be sought entirely in zubur; the verb and sentence construction also convey it.

Z B R (zabara) and Z J R (zajara)

Both roots have Arabic uses concerning restraint and prohibition.

Zajara means driving away, preventing, or restraining, sometimes with a sharp voice or command.

Zabarahu ʿani al-amri also means restraining or sharply forbidding someone.

Sharing one branch does not make the roots identical. Z B R also concerns writing, texts, and material pieces.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

Equivalents must follow each form and context. These proposals do not replace the existing verse translations or authored word-box explanations.

Zabūran: Zabūr, the book given to David in these passages.

Al-zabūr: Zabūr or a written book, according to its contextual referent.

Zubur: writings, books, or records.

Zubar al-ḥadīd: pieces of iron.

Zuburan in Al-Muʾminūn 23:53: separate portions, retaining the possible writings interpretation in the explanation.

For non-Qur’anic derivatives:

Zabara al-biʾra: he lined and strengthened the well’s wall.

Zabara al-kitāba: he wrote or recorded the text.

Mizbar: a pen or writing instrument.

Zabarahu ʿani al-amri: he restrained or sharply forbade someone from that action.

Zabūr should not always be restricted to David’s book, just as zubar does not always refer to material pieces.

Form, prepositions, genitive complements, and context should guide the equivalent chosen for each verse.

9. Conclusion

Two important branches of Z B R are making firm and secure, and writing and recording.

The material branch concerns substantial pieces, strong structures, and reinforcement. The writing branch concerns texts, books, and writing instruments.

The Qur’an draws on both fields.

Zabūr associated with David denotes his given book. Elsewhere zubur concerns earlier writings and records of deeds. Zubar in Al-Kahf 18:96 means iron pieces; zuburan in Al-Muʾminūn 23:53 allows discussion of writings and separate portions in a context of divided groups.

Zabūr is not exclusively a book’s proper name in Arabic, although some Qur’anic passages identify David’s book. Likewise, zubur does not inherently mean sect, prejudice, or religious disagreement; those ideas depend on the relevant construction and context.

An accurate account must preserve both principal branches without confining every derivative to one material image or one written-text meaning.
