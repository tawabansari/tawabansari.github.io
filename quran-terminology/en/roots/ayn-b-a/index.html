Principal meanings: a load and weight; preparing and arranging something; and, concerning people or affairs, paying attention, attaching importance, or assigning weight and value.

1. The root in early Arabic

The triliteral root ع ب أ has several related branches in early Arabic. ʿIbʾ denotes a load or burden; ʿabaʾa can mean preparing and arranging; and ʿabaʾa bihi means paying attention or attaching importance to something.

In the first sense, ʿibʾ is a load carried by a person or animal. It may be material, such as goods on a camel's back, or figurative, such as a heavy responsibility assumed by someone.

In the second sense, ʿabaʾa means preparing and arranging something: organizing goods, readying equipment, or arranging warriors for a confrontation.

In the third sense, the verb used with bi- concerning a person or matter expresses regard or importance. Mā ʿabaʾtu bihi means that the person or matter held no importance for the speaker.

These uses can be connected semantically. Something with weight may count in a person's calculations and decisions; something unimportant seems to carry no weight. Preparing equipment also involves collecting and arranging things for transport or use.

Such similarities alone, however, do not prove that every meaning historically developed from one material image. Early evidence establishes different usages; claims about their historical relationship must remain proportionate to the evidence.

First witness: ʿibʾ as a heavy burden

A verse attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā reads:

الْحَامِلُ الْعِبْءَ الثَّقِيلَ عَنِ الْجَانِي بِغَيْرِ يَدٍ وَلَا شُكْرِ

al-ḥāmilu al-ʿibʾa al-thaqīla ʿani al-jānī bi-ghayri yadin wa-lā shukri

Translation: One who carries the wrongdoer's heavy burden, without a prior favor or thanks being involved.

Al-ʿibʾ denotes a heavy burden here, but not goods carried on a person or animal. It concerns a weighty responsibility or consequence assumed on another's behalf.

The image shows that early usage was not restricted to physical objects. Responsibility, debt, compensation, and obligation could also be described as burdens carried by a person.

Literary and lexical works attribute this verse to Zuhayr. The attribution helps establish the antiquity of ʿibʾ, although the particular verse's composition date is not independently known.

Second witness: ʿabaʾa as preparing

Another verse attributed to Zuhayr says:

عَبَأْتُ لَهُ حِلْمًا وَأَكْرَمْتُ غَيْرَهُ

ʿabaʾtu lahu ḥilman wa-akramtu ghayrahu

وَأَعْرَضْتُ عَنْهُ وَهُوَ بَادٍ مَقَاتِلُهُ

wa-aʿraḍtu ʿanhu wa-huwa bādin maqātiluhu

Translation: I prepared forbearance for him, honored another, and turned away from him, though his vulnerable points were exposed.

ʿAbaʾtu here means preparing or making ready.

The poet describes someone whose speech is mistaken and intemperate. Rather than respond with anger or confrontation, he prepares forbearance and refrains from engaging him.

The verb concerns something immaterial: ḥilm, or forbearance.

Forbearance is not a bodily load. The poet speaks of readying it to deal with a difficult person.

The witness illustrates preparing and making ready as an established early use of this family. Some transmissions vocalize the verb with gemination, ʿabbaʾtu; that variation should be recognized when identifying its precise morphological form.

The two Zuhayr witnesses illustrate burden and preparation. Regard must be examined separately: those meanings alone do not establish when or how the sense of regard developed.

Third witness: preparing and mixing materials

A verse attributed to Abū Zubayd al-Ṭāʾī reads:

كَأَنَّ بِنَحْرِهِ وَبِمَنْكِبَيْهِ

ka-anna bi-naḥrihi wa-bi-mankibayhi

عَبِيرًا بَاتَ يَعْبَؤُهُ عَرُوسُ

ʿabīran bāta yaʿbaʾuhu ʿarūsu

Translation: As though on his neck and shoulders there were fragrant perfume that a newlywed had spent the night preparing and mixing.

Yaʿbaʾuhu concerns preparing and mixing the aromatic substance.

The image evokes carefully prepared perfume placed on the body. The verb concerns the preparation and combination of materials.

Abū Zubayd lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods. Without dating this verse, it cannot be treated as certainly pre-Islamic. Its transmission in early lexical works nevertheless matters for classical Arabic usage.

These witnesses connect the family with burdens and responsibilities, and with preparation and arrangement.

2. Lexical forms and the word family

Recorded forms include ʿabaʾa, yaʿbaʾu, ʿabʾ, ʿibʾ, aʿbāʾ, ʿabbaʾa, taʿbiʾa, muʿabbaʾ, and iʿtabaʾa.

They belong to several branches of usage.

ʿIbʾ: a load or burden

ʿIbʾ, with kasra on ʿayn and an unvocalized bāʾ, denotes a load or burden.

Materially, it may be goods carried by a person or animal.

Figuratively, it can be responsibility, debt, compensation, or an obligation assumed by someone.

Its plural is aʿbāʾ.

ʿIbʾ is also recorded for the balanced loads on either side of a pack animal. Each load can be called an ʿibʾ; the dual is ʿibʾān.

The word is also used for a counterpart or equivalent. This can be understood through the image of two loads balanced in weight or quantity.

The branch clearly concerns loads and weight, but the load itself, its weight, and a figurative responsibility remain distinguishable.

ʿAbaʾa: preparing and arranging

One use of ʿabaʾa concerns making something ready and arranging it.

In ʿabaʾa al-matāʿa, someone prepares and arranges equipment or goods.

In ʿabaʾa al-jaysha, troops are readied for confrontation and arranged in appropriate positions.

In ʿabaʾa al-ṭība, aromatic materials are prepared or mixed.

The shared feature is preparing and organizing something needed for use or action.

The object determines the precise activity. Preparing perfume and arranging troops are different acts, even though both can be expressed with ʿabaʾa.

ʿAbbaʾa: arranging and making ready

ʿAbbaʾa is a Form II verb used for preparing, arranging, and organizing.

ʿAbbaʾa al-jaysha means readying and deploying an army.

Taʿbiʾa is its verbal noun, denoting preparation and organization.

The unhamzated form taʿbiya is also recorded. Early lexical works recognize both forms.

In modern Arabic, taʿbiʾa can concern packing goods, preparing resources, or mobilizing personnel and capabilities.

These uses extend the field of preparation and arrangement. Modern technical meanings should not all be projected onto the language of revelation or the Quranic yaʿbaʾu.

ʿAbaʾa bihi: paying regard

With bi-, the verb expresses another sense: paying attention, attaching importance, or assigning weight and value.

Mā ʿabaʾtu bi-fulānin means “I paid no regard to that person” or “I did not consider him important.”

Mā aʿbaʾu bi-hādhā al-amri means “this matter is of no importance to me” or “I pay it no attention.”

The preposition bi- identifies the person or matter to which regard is given or withheld.

This cannot be directly translated as loading something onto someone's shoulders.

Its relation to burden is intelligible through weight and importance: what matters seems to have weight and standing; what receives no regard carries no weight in one's attention or decisions.

That explains a semantic relationship without independently proving a definite historical sequence.

Iʿtabaʾa: taking possession and gathering

Some lexical works record iʿtabaʾa for taking possession, gathering, or appropriating something.

This can be connected semantically with gathering and arranging, but the form itself does not occur in the Quran.

It should therefore not determine the interpretation of yaʿbaʾu in Al-Furqān.

3. Quranic usage

The root occurs once in the Quran, as yaʿbaʾu in Al-Furqān.

قُلْ مَا يَعْبَؤُا۟ بِكُمْ رَبِّي لَوْلَا دُعَاؤُكُمْ ۖ فَقَدْ كَذَّبْتُمْ فَسَوْفَ يَكُونُ لِزَامًا

qul mā yaʿbaʾu bikum rabbī lawlā duʿāʾukum fa-qad kadhdhabtum fa-sawfa yakūnu lizāmā

Al-Furqān 25:77

Say: If it were not for your supplication, what regard would my Lord have for you? Yet you have denied, and it will soon be inescapable.

Al-Furqān 25:77

Yaʿbaʾu is an imperfect verb from ع ب أ. In yaʿbaʾu bikum, it expresses paying regard or attaching importance to those addressed.

This differs from preparing equipment or arranging troops. There is no material object being prepared; the subject is the Lord's regard for human beings.

Mā yaʿbaʾu bikum rabbī: what regard would my Lord have for you?

Mā can be understood as negation or as a rhetorical question with a negative force.

Both yield a similar central point: without what follows in lawlā duʿāʾukum, what regard would there be for the addressees in this relationship?

Yaʿbaʾu is the imperfect verb; rabbī is its subject.

Bikum refers to the people addressed, whose standing or reception of regard is under discussion.

“What regard would my Lord have for you?” conveys this linguistic sense.

The verb does not mean that God needs human beings. Attaching importance differs from being dependent.

Someone may value another without depending on that person for existence. Yaʿbaʾu consequently does not imply that God needs people to complete divine power, sovereignty, or existence.

The root alone also does not specify the conduct or outcome in which this regard appears. That requires examining the verse and related passages about the human relationship with God.

Lawlā duʿāʾukum: the relevant condition

Lawlā hypothetically considers something's absence and indicates that its presence matters to the sentence's outcome.

Duʿāʾukum belongs to د ع و and concerns calling, summoning, or supplication.

A grammatical question arises: the pronoun -kum can stand in different relationships to the verbal noun duʿāʾ.

First reading: your supplication to God

On this reading, the addressees themselves perform the calling: they call upon God and turn to Him.

The sense is: without your calling upon God, what regard would my Lord have for you?

This reading can be related to the nearby context, although that connection does not by itself settle the interpretation.

Al-Furqān 25:63 introduces the servants of the Entirely Merciful. In Al-Furqān 25:65, they call upon their Lord to turn the punishment of Hell away from them.

In Al-Furqān 25:74, they ask their Lord for joy in their spouses and offspring, and to make them an example for the mindful.

These supplications accompany other conduct: avoiding association of partners with God, wrongful killing, sexual relations outside valid marriage, false witness, and uncomprehending responses to the signs.

Supplication in this context can thus express turning to the Lord and accepting servanthood, rather than merely uttering a request without regard to conduct and responsibility.

Ghāfir 40:60 likewise connects calling upon God with divine response and condemns arrogant refusal of worship.

Supplication and worship can therefore be closely connected in Quranic usage, without being identical in root or lexical meaning.

Second reading: God's calling you

The construction also permits the addressees to be the object of the calling: God has called them.

The sense would then be: without God's calling you, what regard would be given to you?

An Arabic verbal noun can be annexed to its subject or its object, making this reading grammatically possible.

It also fits the topic of being called to truth and bearing responsibility for the divine message. Yūnus 10:25 describes God as calling to the abode of peace.

The continuation, fa-qad kadhdhabtum, speaks of the addressees' denial, which can be a response to a call or message received.

The agent of calling must be inferred from context in this reading, since duʿāʾukum does not name that agent separately.

In the first reading, -kum identifies the supplicants, connecting the phrase to the prayers mentioned nearby.

Accordingly, “your supplication” is a defensible choice for the basic translation. The alternative “God's calling you” is also grammatically and exegetically established; the first choice should not be presented as the only possible meaning.

The disagreement concerns duʿāʾukum, not the meaning of ع ب أ. In both readings, yaʿbaʾu retains the sense of regard or importance.

Fa-qad kadhdhabtum: denial and its consequence

The verse continues with fa-qad kadhdhabtum.

This speaks of the addressees' denial.

Kadhdhabtum is a Form II verb from ك ذ ب, concerning denial and treating the message as false here.

It is followed by fa-sawfa yakūnu lizāman.

Lizāman belongs to ل ز م and concerns something that adheres or becomes inescapable.

What will become inescapable is not stated as a separate explicit noun. Identifying the consequence and its character requires context.

The ending indicates that denial will not be without consequence.

The verse therefore does more than value supplication: it ends by warning those who have denied the truth.

Yaʿbaʾu should be understood in relation to human responses to divine calling and guidance, while its lexical meaning remains regard and importance.

Does the verse deny inherent human worth?

Al-Furqān 25:77 links divine regard with duʿāʾukum, but this should not become a denial of all human worth or dignity without considering other verses.

Al-Isrāʾ 17:70 speaks of honoring the children of Adam. The verse attributes this honor to God and mentions benefits granted to them.

The Al-Furqān passage concerns human responses to truth, supplication, and denial.

A distinction can therefore be drawn between dignity granted by God and standing resulting from a person's response to guidance and responsibility.

This is an interpretive distinction drawn by reading the passages together, not a lexical meaning of yaʿbaʾu.

The verb in this construction conveys regard and assigning importance. The verse's topic establishes the setting of that regard.

4. Morphology and construction

The Quranic yaʿbaʾu

Yaʿbaʾu is a third-person masculine singular imperfect of the basic triliteral verb ʿabaʾa.

Its subject in Al-Furqān 25:77 is rabbī, “my Lord.”

The construction begins with mā and contains the hypothetical condition lawlā duʿāʾukum.

This is the family's only Quranic derivative.

ʿIbʾ, aʿbāʾ, ʿabbaʾa, and taʿbiʾa are established Arabic forms but do not occur in the Quran.

The preposition bi-

In yaʿbaʾu bikum, bi- is attached to the pronoun for those addressed.

This is an established construction expressing regard.

Mā aʿbaʾu bi-hādhā al-amri means “I attach no importance to this matter.”

In ʿabaʾtu al-matāʿa, by contrast, the direct object is the equipment being prepared or arranged.

Meaning cannot be extracted from root letters alone: syntax, prepositions, and the kind of complement also help select the sense.

Since the verse uses bikum and concerns the Lord's regard for people, “attaching importance” fits rather than “preparing” or “carrying.”

Burden and value

ʿIbʾ primarily denotes a load or burden.

Mā ʿabaʾtu bihi does not discuss a physical load.

Something not considered important is figuratively without weight or standing in the speaker's estimation.

“Assigning weight and value” can therefore help explain yaʿbaʾu.

Weight must not become a material measurement in the basic translation. The verse concerns regard, not weighing human bodies.

Evaluative force

ʿAbaʾa has no inherently positive or negative moral meaning.

Something can be prepared for beneficial or harmful purposes.

Bearing a burden may be a worthy duty or a consequence of wrongdoing.

Paying regard to a person or matter does not necessarily imply moral approval.

Al-Furqān 25:77 supplies a context of God and humanity. Yaʿbaʾu expresses divine regard; judgments about denial, supplication, and consequences come from other words in the verse.

5. Related roots and distinctions

ع ب أ and ث ق ل: load and heaviness

ث ق ل concerns heaviness and weight.

Thiql can refer to physical weight, a heavy load, or importance.

ʿIbʾ also concerns burden, but commonly names what is carried or a responsibility assumed.

A load can be heavy: ʿibʾ identifies the load, while thaqīl describes its heaviness.

The same distinction appears in Zuhayr's al-ʿibʾa al-thaqīla.

In that phrase, the noun naming the load and the adjective describing it have distinct roles. Their combination alone does not establish every semantic difference between the two root families.

In yaʿbaʾu bikum, weight is figurative, not a measurement of physical heaviness.

ع ب أ and ق د ر: regard and value

ق د ر concerns measure, amount, ability, and standing in its various uses.

Al-Zumar 39:67 describes people who have not esteemed God as He deserves.

Qadr can denote something's measure or standing.

ʿAbaʾa bihi concerns someone's attitude toward something: whether it receives regard and is considered important.

Something may truly have value while someone disregards it.

Having value and assigning value are different relationships.

Yaʿbaʾu in Al-Furqān 25:77 expresses regard, not a numerical measurement of human worth.

ع ب أ and ه م م: regard and concern

ه م م concerns intention, resolve, attention, and mental involvement.

Ihtamma bi-l-amri means attaching importance to or becoming occupied with a matter.

This is close to ʿabaʾa bihi.

Ihtimām may emphasize mental attention, concern, or an intention to act; ʿabaʾa bihi more plainly expresses assigning importance and not being indifferent.

The distinction is not absolute: both expressions can convey regard in classical Arabic.

“Paying regard” is a natural Quranic equivalent for yaʿbaʾu, without adding anxiety or worry.

ع ب أ and د ع و: regard and calling

The two roots occur together in Al-Furqān 25:77.

Daʿā concerns calling, summoning, requesting, and inviting.

ʿAbaʾa bihi concerns paying regard and attaching importance.

Their lexical meanings differ.

The sentence and lawlā connect them: supplication or calling matters to the regard under discussion.

Yaʿbaʾu should therefore not be translated as “He hears your prayer.” Hearing, answering, and paying regard may be related, but are not identical concepts.

Al-Baqarah 2:186 speaks of God's answering the supplicant's call. It illuminates supplication and response without changing yaʿbaʾu into “answering prayer.”

ع ب أ and ع ب ث: separate families

ع ب ث concerns futility, purposeless play, and vain activity.

Al-Muʾminūn 23:115 asks whether people supposed they were created in vain.

ع ب أ concerns burdens, preparation, and regard.

Sharing the first two letters does not make the roots identical.

Mā yaʿbaʾu bikum must not become “He created you in vain” or a similar expression.

The subjects might be connected in a philosophical discussion of human creation, but their lexical meanings remain distinct.

6. Translation recommendations for Forqan

Translation must follow the form and its complement.

ʿIbʾ: a load, burden, or weighty responsibility or obligation.

Aʿbāʾ: loads, burdens, or heavy responsibilities and obligations.

ʿAbaʾa al-matāʿa: he prepared and arranged the equipment or goods.

ʿAbbaʾa al-jaysha: he readied and organized the army.

Taʿbiʾa: preparation, arrangement, or organization, according to the object.

ʿAbaʾa bihi: he paid regard to it or him; attached importance; assigned weight and value.

Mā ʿabaʾa bihi: he paid him no regard; attached no importance to him.

Al-Furqān 25:77

For mā yaʿbaʾu bikum rabbī, the primary recommendation is:

“What regard would my Lord have for you?”

Together with the following phrase:

“If it were not for your supplication, what regard would my Lord have for you?”

This preserves the established verbal sense without turning it into physically carrying a burden or preparing equipment.

“What value would He assign you?” conveys part of the sense, but “what regard would He have for you?” more closely follows the verb with bi-.

The study can explain the relationship with weight and importance.

7. Summary

ع ب أ has several established semantic branches in Arabic.

ʿIbʾ denotes a load or burden, whether material or figuratively a weighty responsibility or obligation.

ʿAbaʾa and ʿabbaʾa express preparing and organizing equipment, goods, materials, or troops.

With bi-, the sense of regard and importance becomes prominent. Its semantic connection with weight and value can be understood through the image of what matters having weight and standing.

Poetry attributed to Zuhayr illustrates burden and preparation. A poem attributed to Abū Zubayd illustrates preparing perfume. These semantic similarities alone do not establish the precise historical relationship among every branch.

The root occurs once in the Quran: yaʿbaʾu in Al-Furqān 25:77.

There, yaʿbaʾu bikum expresses paying regard and attaching importance.

Lawlā duʿāʾukum relates that regard to supplication or calling. Human supplication to God fits nearby prayers of the servants of the Entirely Merciful, while God's calling the addressees is also an established reading. Contextual affinity alone does not conclusively settle the choice.

The disagreement does not change yaʿbaʾu itself.

The ending, fa-qad kadhdhabtum fa-sawfa yakūnu lizāman, concerns denial and its consequence. The regard in question belongs to a context of human response to truth and responsibility.

Final definition: ع ب أ has three principal usages: burden and weight; preparation and arrangement; and paying regard or assigning importance to someone or something. Quranic yaʿbaʾu bikum expresses regard and importance. Its connection with burden can be understood through weight and standing, but the direct translation should preserve the meaning of regard.
