Qur’anic root ك س ل

Sluggishness, reluctance, and heaviness in undertaking work

1. Meaning in early Arabic

The Arabic root ك س ل concerns sluggishness, reluctance, a feeling of heaviness in acting, and failure to begin or continue an activity.

Ibn Faris locates its basic meaning in finding an action burdensome and refraining from performing or completing it.

Lexicons explain كَسِلَ عَنِ الْعَمَلِ as being sluggish about work or lacking the inclination and energy to undertake it.

A verse attributed to Labid ibn Rabiʿa, a poet who lived through both the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods, states:

وَإِذَا رُمْتَ رَحِيلًا فَارْتَحِلْ وَاعْصِ مَا يَأْمُرُ تَوْصِيمُ الْكَسَلْ

When you intend to travel, set out; disregard what the languor of sluggishness commands you.

تَوْصِيم here denotes languor, bodily weariness, and heaviness. The poet describes someone intending to travel but potentially held back by lethargy.

The line appears in an elegy for Labid’s brother Arbad. It is therefore evidence from a poetic tradition close to the period of revelation, rather than securely dated evidence from before Islam.

Classical Arabic also uses مِكْسَال and كَسُول of a woman who seldom rises from her seat. In some early literary contexts this description is complimentary, suggesting a life of ease.

The word alone thus does not always determine whether such inactivity is praised or criticized; circumstances, the activity, and expectations of the person matter.

The evidence considered here does not securely establish the family’s pre-Islamic chronology. Qur’anic usage and early Arabic evidence nevertheless confirm that sluggishness and heaviness in acting were familiar concepts at revelation.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important Arabic forms include:

  • كَسِلَ ـ يَكْسَلُ: was sluggish; found acting burdensome.
  • كَسَل: sluggishness, languor, and reluctance to act.
  • كَسْلَان: a sluggish or reluctant person.
  • كَسْلَى: feminine of كَسْلَان; also recorded as a plural.
  • كُسَالَى and كَسَالَى: plurals of كَسْلَان.
  • كَسُول: markedly sluggish or inactive.
  • مِكْسَال: inactive; inclined to remain seated.
  • كَسَّلَ: made another sluggish.
  • أَكْسَلَ: became sluggish; in some constructions caused another’s sluggishness.
  • تَكَاسَلَ: acted sluggishly or neglected effort.
  • تَكَاسُل: sluggishness in undertaking work.
  • اِسْتَكْسَلَ: showed sluggishness or judged someone sluggish.

Form examined in the Qur’an:

كُسَالَىٰ

The Qur’an employs only the plural كُسَالَىٰ from this family. The verb كَسِلَ, verbal noun كَسَل, and singular كَسْلَان do not occur.

3. Core meaning

The core meaning is sluggishness and heaviness in approaching or continuing an activity.

This can accompany reluctance, bodily languor, reduced motivation, or a desire for ease.

Consider someone capable of performing a necessary task, yet repeatedly postponing it through reluctance or making minimal effort. Such conduct fits كَسَل.

It should not, however, be equated with physical incapacity. Someone may be eager to act but prevented by illness, injury, or severe fatigue.

Nor is sluggishness always a conscious choice. Bodily languor and heaviness can affect inclination and activity.

The word alone does not establish the cause of sluggishness, a person’s capacity, or moral responsibility. These require attention to circumstances and context.

In both Qur’anic occurrences, sluggishness in salat appears alongside other conduct revealing the people’s inward orientation.

4. Qur’anic usages

كُسَالَىٰ: rising for salat while showing off

The people described have not abandoned salat altogether. They rise for it, but the manner and orientation of their action are criticized.

قَامُوا كُسَالَىٰ describes their state as they rise: they approach an outwardly religious activity with sluggishness and reluctance.

Two further features follow: showing off before people and remembering The God only a little.

يُرَاءُونَ, from ر أ ي, concerns acting to be seen by others. The issue is therefore more than bodily sluggishness: outward conduct and inward purpose diverge.

Someone acting from conviction and interest does not make the activity’s value depend on being watched. Someone seeking attention may have little inclination to perform it when no one is present.

The verse describes salat accompanied by display and little remembrance, rather than sincerity and attention to The God.

The importance of remembrance in salat also appears in the address to Moses:

Al-Nisa 4:142 describes people who rise for salat yet remember The God little in life and conduct, while showing off.

This contrast reveals the distance between an action’s outward form and its purpose.

كُسَالَىٰ: sluggishness in salat and unwillingness to give

Here sluggishness in salat is placed alongside reluctance to spend.

كُسَالَىٰ belongs to ك س ل, while كَارِهُونَ belongs to ك ر ه.

كَسَل describes heaviness and sluggishness in approaching salat; كَرَاهَة describes unwillingness when giving wealth.

Together these behaviors reveal a lack of willingness to fulfill religious and social responsibilities.

Yet their spending is not rejected merely because salat or giving is difficult. The verse expressly mentions their rejection of The God and His Messenger.

Its judgment cannot therefore be extended to everyone who finds rising for salat or giving under difficult circumstances hard.

Someone may rise for salat despite fatigue after exhausting work. Another may devote some possessions to helping the needy despite poverty.

The difficulty of acting in such circumstances is not identical with the conduct described in al-Tawba 9:54.

The verse describes a set of characteristics in a particular group; it does not make every feeling of heaviness or reluctance conclusive evidence of hypocrisy and rejection.

Sluggishness in worship and human limitations

The Qur’an acknowledges the limits of human capacity.

Al-Muzzammil 73:20 likewise mentions illness, travel in pursuit of The God’s bounty, and struggle in His way as circumstances affecting people’s capacity for worship.

These verses help establish the limits of conclusions drawn from al-Nisa 4:142 and al-Tawba 9:54.

Someone whose capacity is reduced by illness or fatigue is not in the same situation as someone using worship for display and prestige.

Apparent sluggishness alone therefore does not justify judging a person’s faith, sincerity, or hypocrisy.

5. Morphology and syntax

كُسَالَىٰ is a broken plural of كَسْلَان, following the pattern فُعَالَى.

In al-Nisa 4:142 it functions as an accusative circumstantial expression:

قَامُوا كُسَالَىٰ

They rose in a state of sluggishness.

Repeating قَامُوا in the condition and its response draws attention to how they rise.

Al-Tawba 9:54 has a different construction:

وَهُمْ كُسَالَىٰ

هُمْ is the subject and كُسَالَىٰ its predicate; the whole nominal clause functions circumstantially.

Thus كُسَالَىٰ itself is circumstantial in the first verse, while هُمْ كُسَالَىٰ describes their state as they approach salat in the second.

The final case vowel is not pronounced because the word ends in alif maqsura.

The negation and exception in al-Tawba 9:54 also matter. The construction describes sluggishness as their characteristic condition when approaching salat, rather than an isolated exception.

Lexically, however, كُسَالَىٰ does not name a religious group. Their hypocrisy in al-Nisa 4:142 is established by the opening and combined descriptions, not this word alone.

6. Comparison with related roots

ك س ل compared with ف ت ر

فُتُور concerns subsiding intensity, diminished strength, and reduced continuity of activity.

It can describe diminished heat, bodily strength, activity, and other phenomena.

كَسَل more particularly concerns sluggishness and heaviness in approaching or continuing an activity.

Someone may begin eagerly and later lose strength and intensity: this fits فُتُور. كَسَل, by contrast, may be present from the outset.

This is a semantic tendency; the families overlap.

ك س ل compared with ك ر ه

كَرَاهَة denotes dislike or lack of willingness and satisfaction.

كَسَل describes sluggishness and heaviness in acting.

Al-Tawba 9:54 uses both: كَسَل with salat and كَرَاهَة with spending.

Someone can dislike a task yet perform it diligently, or like a task yet approach it sluggishly through bodily languor.

Dislike and sluggishness are therefore related but distinct states.

ك س ل compared with ث ق ل

ث ق ل concerns heaviness and the difficulty it brings.

Here clinging heavily to the earth depicts reluctance to set out.

This is close to كَسَل, but ث ق ل has a broader range, covering weight, heavy loads, and serious responsibilities.

كَسَل more particularly describes someone’s response to an activity they should or wish to undertake.

ك س ل compared with ض ع ف

ضَعْف concerns diminished capacity, strength, or power.

A weak person may be eager to perform a task yet unable to do so.

كَسَل can also occur in someone capable of acting but approaching the task sluggishly.

Physical weakness may itself produce languor. Assessing conduct therefore requires attention to its causes and circumstances.

ك س ل compared with ن ص ب

One branch of نَصَب concerns distress and fatigue resulting from work or hardship.

Someone may be exhausted after difficult work despite having undertaken it eagerly and diligently.

كَسَل need not follow prior exertion; it can precede the activity.

Fatigue from strenuous work should therefore not itself be treated as evidence of laziness or reluctance.

ك س ل compared with ر أ ي

رِئَاء concerns acting to be seen by others.

كَسَل describes sluggishness in action; رِئَاء concerns its orientation and the motive of being seen.

Al-Nisa 4:142 places these features together.

Someone can act energetically for show, while another acts sincerely despite fatigue and sluggishness.

Outward diligence alone does not prove sincerity, and outward sluggishness alone does not prove ostentation.

7. Suggested renderings for Forqan

Arabic word or expressionSuggested rendering
كَسِلَwas sluggish
كَسَلsluggishness; languor; reluctance
كَسْلَانsluggish; reluctant
كُسَالَىٰsluggish; in a state of sluggishness
تَكَاسُلacting sluggishly
قَامُوا كُسَالَىٰthey rose sluggishly
وَهُمْ كُسَالَىٰwhile they are sluggish and reluctant

“Sluggishly” or “in a state of sluggishness” suits both Qur’anic occurrences.

“Laziness” is possible in some lexical contexts, but may imply a lasting, blameworthy character trait. The Qur’anic form describes people’s condition while undertaking a particular activity.

“Fatigue” alone is also insufficient, since it does not fully convey reluctance and heaviness in approaching action.

Conclusion

ك س ل concerns sluggishness, languor, reluctance, and heaviness when beginning or continuing an activity.

Al-Nisa 4:142 and al-Tawba 9:54 use the family to describe people approaching salat sluggishly.

The first combines this with display and little remembrance of The God; the second combines it with rejection of The God and His Messenger and unwillingness to give.

كَسَل does not inherently mean hypocrisy, rejection, illness, or incapacity. Its moral evaluation depends on circumstances, motives, and the person’s conduct as a whole.

The Qur’anic criticism concerns the distance between outward religious conduct and inward willingness, conviction, and responsibility.
