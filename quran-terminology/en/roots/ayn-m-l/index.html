Linguistic Note

The core of ع م ل (ʿ M L) is tied to conscious work with direction. ʿAmal is not simply the occurrence of a movement. It is work issuing from a doer and connected with intention, choice, habit, skill, responsibility, or moral direction.

For this reason, ʿamal in the Qur’an is very close to human responsibility. A human being does not only believe; he works. He does not only speak; he leaves effect. He does not only hold inward intention; something from him appears in the world. The Qur’an repeatedly joins faith with righteous work because faith that leaves no effect in the world remains incomplete.

So ʿ M L should not always be flattened into “doing.” Fiʿl also means doing, but ʿamal is usually weightier, more directed, more responsible, and more open to evaluation. Work is something by which a person is formed and by which he is measured.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of ʿ M L is conscious, purposeful, attributable work: work that issues from a person, has effect, is recorded, and returns to him.

Work may be righteous or unrighteous, good or evil, lasting or rootless, grateful or corrupt, individual or social, devotional or industrial. The Qur’an does not look only at the occurrence of work; it also weighs direction, quality, righteousness, and outcome.

Material Image

The material image is a person moving something forward with hand, thought, decision, and energy: cultivating land, making something, carrying out a transaction, giving help, committing injustice, turning speech into action, or repeating a habit in life.

In this image, ʿamal is not a passing instant. It has effect and leaves something in the world. It may be good or evil, open or hidden. But in Qur’anic logic, work is not lost.

Classical Language Note

In classical Arabic, ʿ M L is close to working, doing, occupation, craft, assignment, and purposeful activity. A ʿāmil may be someone who does a task or is responsible over it. ʿAmal al-yad points to handwork and what a person makes.

In some older uses, ʿamal is not mere simple movement. It is connected with bearing work, continuing in it, and carrying out a duty. In this sense, ʿamal is weightier than a passing or directionless act: it gathers energy, time, intention, and effect.

Righteous Work: Faith in the Form of Action

One of the most repeated Qur’anic phrases is آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ (āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt). It shows that Qur’anic faith is not separate from work. In 2:25, the Qur’an says: وَبَشِّرِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ (wa-bashshiri alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt).

Righteous work is not merely socially approved good behavior. It is work aligned with ṣalāḥ, repair, truth, growth, and right direction. In 18:30, righteous work is connected with أَحْسَنَ عَمَلًا (aḥsana ʿamalā). So the issue is not only doing; the quality of work matters.

Work and the Test of Quality

The Qur’an presents the world as a test of work, not merely a test of claims. In 67:2, it says: لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا (li-yabluwakum ayyukum aḥsanu ʿamalā). The verse does not say “more in work,” but “better in work.”

The test is not only quantity. It concerns what is more right, more sincere, more firm, and more aligned with truth. 18:7 uses the same expression. The Qur’an does not measure work only by number and volume; quality, direction, and truthfulness also enter the scale.

Work and Individual Responsibility

The Qur’an repeatedly stresses that a person’s work returns to him. In 41:46, it says: مَنْ عَمِلَ صَالِحًا فَلِنَفْسِهِ وَمَنْ أَسَاءَ فَعَلَيْهَا (man ʿamila ṣāliḥan fa-li-nafsih wa-man asāʾa fa-ʿalayhā). Work does not remain separate from the person; its effect returns to him.

In 16:97, the Qur’an says: مَنْ عَمِلَ صَالِحًا مِّن ذَكَرٍ أَوْ أُنثَىٰ وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ (man ʿamila ṣāliḥan min dhakarin aw unthā wa-huwa muʾmin). The value of work is not tied to gender; it is tied to faith and the righteousness of the work.

Work Seen by The God

The Qur’an treats work as something seen and not hidden. In 9:105, it says: وَقُلِ اعْمَلُوا فَسَيَرَى اللَّهُ عَمَلَكُمْ وَرَسُولُهُ وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ (wa-quli iʿmalū fa-sa-yarā Allāhu ʿamalakum wa-rasūluhu wa-al-muʾminūn).

A person may hide, justify, or rename his work, but work is seen by The God and its truth will be disclosed. The verse continues that human beings will be returned to the Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, and He will inform them of what they used to do.

Small and Large Work

The Qur’an does not treat even the smallest work as without effect. In 99:7 and 99:8, it says: فَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ (fa-man yaʿmal mithqāla dharratin khayran yarah) and وَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ شَرًّا يَرَهُ (wa-man yaʿmal mithqāla dharratin sharran yarah).

Here, work is tied to seeing its result. The smallest work of good or evil is not lost in the divine order; work is accountable and its return will be seen.

Aʿmāl: A Person’s Collected Works

The Qur’an speaks of أَعْمَال (aʿmāl) as a person’s collected works. In 6:132, it says: وَلِكُلٍّ دَرَجَاتٌ مِّمَّا عَمِلُوا (wa-li-kullin darajātun mimmā ʿamilū). Degrees are shaped by what people did; human rank is not fixed by claim alone.

Sometimes a person’s works are presented as losing all lasting worth when they are severed from truth. In 25:23, the Qur’an says: فَجَعَلْنَاهُ هَبَاءً مَّنثُورًا (fa-jaʿalnāhu habāʾan manthūrā). The work exists outwardly, but because it lacks truth, sincerity, and right direction, it does not endure in divine judgment.

Righteous Work and Reform

Ṣāliḥ belongs to the field of ṣalāḥ and iṣlāḥ. Therefore, righteous work is work that is right, constructive, aligned with truth, and away from corruption. In 19:96, the Qur’an says: إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ سَيَجْعَلُ لَهُمُ الرَّحْمَٰنُ وُدًّا (inna alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāti sa-yajʿalu lahumu al-raḥmānu wuddā).

Righteous work is not only private devotion. Any work in the path of truth, repair, justice, mercy, and growth enters the field of righteous work. The Qur’an repeats this phrase to show that faith must become ṣalāḥ in the world.

Bad Work and Responsibility

Work may be righteous, and it may be bad, corrupt, or harmful. About the son of Nūḥ, 11:46 says: إِنَّهُ عَمَلٌ غَيْرُ صَالِحٍ (innahu ʿamalun ghayru ṣāliḥ).

The expression is compressed and weighty. The Qur’an does not merely say, “he did unrighteous work”; it says, “he is unrighteous work.” In this context, work moves beyond a separate behavior and becomes a sign of a person’s real direction and relation. True relation is not determined only by blood and family; a person’s practical direction also reveals the truth of relation.

Work and Speech

The Qur’an does not separate claim from work. A person may say something while his work contradicts it. In 61:2, the Qur’an says: لِمَ تَقُولُونَ مَا لَا تَفْعَلُونَ (lima taqūlūna mā lā tafʿalūn).

This verse uses the root F ʿ L, but its meaning is close to the field of ʿamal. Faith-speech without realization in work and behavior loses weight. In Qur’anic logic, a person’s truthfulness appears through the relation between speech and work.

Work and Practical Gratitude

The Qur’an commands the family of Dāwūd to show gratitude in the form of work. In 34:13, it says: اعْمَلُوا آلَ دَاوُودَ شُكْرًا (iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukrā).

This verse is important. Gratitude is not only speech; it requires work. Divine blessing should appear in building, repair, service, justice, and right action. In this sense, work can become the language of gratitude.

Industrial Work and Human Making

The root ʿ M L is not only ethical and otherworldly; it also appears for making and production. In 34:13, it says: يَعْمَلُونَ لَهُ مَا يَشَاءُ مِن مَّحَارِيبَ وَتَمَاثِيلَ (yaʿmalūna lahu mā yashāʾu min maḥārība wa-tamāthīl). The same verse connects this capacity to practical gratitude.

In the critique of idol-making, 37:96 says: وَاللَّهُ خَلَقَكُمْ وَمَا تَعْمَلُونَ (wa-Allāhu khalaqakum wa-mā taʿmalūn). Here mā taʿmalūn points to what they make and produce in the context of carving idols. What a human being makes by hand is not worthy of worship.

ʿĀmil and Executive Responsibility

The form الْعَامِلِينَ (al-ʿāmilīn) appears for those who work over ṣadaqāt. In 9:60, the Qur’an says: وَالْعَامِلِينَ عَلَيْهَا (wa-al-ʿāmilīna ʿalayhā).

Al-ʿāmilīn ʿalayhā means those who work over it: administrators and responsible executors of it. This use shows that ʿ M L appears in social and executive fields too, not only in private ethics.

Distinguishing Qur’anic Uses

In 2:25, righteous work stands beside faith. In 67:2 and 18:7, work is the measure of life’s qualitative test. In 41:46, work returns for or against the human being.

In 9:105, work is seen and disclosed. In 99:7 and 99:8, the smallest good and evil work is seen. In 25:23, work without root in truth becomes scattered dust. In 34:13, work concerns both making and practical gratitude. In 9:60, ʿāmilīn are responsible workers and administrators.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

In آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ (āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt), the verb ʿamila takes الصَّالِحَاتِ (al-ṣāliḥāt) as its object: a set of right and reforming works. In أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا (aḥsanu ʿamalā), the accusative ʿamalā clarifies the quality being tested.

In فَسَيَرَى اللَّهُ عَمَلَكُمْ (fa-sa-yarā Allāhu ʿamalakum), work appears as a noun, something seen and assessed. In بِمَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ (bimā kuntum taʿmalūn), the structure suggests continuity: what you used to do repeatedly, not only an isolated movement.

In إِنَّهُ عَمَلٌ غَيْرُ صَالِحٍ (innahu ʿamalun ghayru ṣāliḥ), ʿamal is the predicate and identifies the person through his practical direction. In الْعَامِلِينَ عَلَيْهَا (al-ʿāmilīna ʿalayhā), ʿalā shows that they work over the matter of ṣadaqāt and carry responsibility for it.

Difference From Nearby Words

ʿ M L is close to F ʿ L, but they are not identical. Fiʿl is broader and points to the basic doing or occurrence of an act; it may be used for human action, divine action, events, or natural movements. ʿAmal usually names work joined to intention, awareness, direction, skill, responsibility, or moral evaluation.

ʿ M L differs from ṣunʿ. Ṣunʿ is close to skilled making, craft, and precise production. ʿAmal is wider and may be ethical, devotional, social, intellectual, or industrial. ʿ M L is also not identical with kasb. Kasb concerns acquiring and gaining an outcome, while ʿamal is the work and process issuing from the doer.

ʿ M L also differs from saʿy, qawl, niyyah, and iḥsān. Saʿy is striving toward a goal. Qawl is speech. Niyyah is inward direction. Iḥsān is doing beautifully and at a higher quality. ʿAmal is the realized work and conduct issuing from a person; it may be righteous, bad, incomplete, or aḥsan.

Forqan Translation Method

Forqan should not always translate ʿ M L only as “work.” Sometimes “act,” “do,” “conduct,” “works,” “make,” or “administrator” may fit better.

The distinction between ʿamal and fiʿl should also remain visible. When the Qur’an speaks of ʿamal, responsibility, direction, reckoning, reward, punishment, righteousness, or corruption is usually nearby. Therefore, ʿamal ṣāliḥ is not merely a simple “good deed”; it is work aligned with ṣalāḥ and truth.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

  • āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt: they had faith and did righteous works; or they had faith and engaged in right, reforming works.
  • aḥsanu ʿamalā: better in work; or more excellent and measured in conduct.
  • fa-sa-yarā Allāhu ʿamalakum: then The God will see your work.
  • bimā kuntum taʿmalūn: about what you used to do; or about the works you used to carry out continually.
  • man ʿamila ṣāliḥan: whoever does righteous work.
  • mithqāla dharratin khayrā: an atom’s weight of good.
  • habāʾan manthūrā: scattered dust; or weightless, dispersed dust.
  • innahu ʿamalun ghayru ṣāliḥ: indeed, he is unrighteous work; or, in explanation: he embodies a conduct opposed to righteousness.
  • wa-Allāhu khalaqakum wa-mā taʿmalūn: and The God created you and what you make; or The God created you and your made works and products.
  • iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukrā: work in gratitude, family of Dāwūd; or show gratitude in action, family of Dāwūd.
  • al-ʿāmilīna ʿalayhā: those working over it; or its administrators and responsible workers.

Summary

The Qur’anic root ʿ M L points to conscious, purposeful, attributable work. Work issues from a human being, has effect, is seen, is recorded, and returns to him. This is why the Qur’an repeatedly joins faith with righteous work.

Work is not merely movement or occurrence. It may be righteous or unrighteous, good or evil, lasting or scattered dust, grateful or corrupt, individual or social, devotional or industrial. The Qur’an does not measure work only by quantity; its quality, direction, and righteousness matter.

In short: ʿ M L in the Qur’an points to doing conscious, purposeful, effective work by which a human being is known, tested, and made to see its outcome.
