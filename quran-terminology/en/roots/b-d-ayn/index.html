The Qur’anic Root B D ʿ

From being without precedent to originating without a prior model

The root ب د ع occurs only four times in the Qur’an, in three forms: بَدِيعُ twice concerning the heavens and the earth, بِدْعًا once concerning the Prophet among the messengers, and the verb ابْتَدَعُوهَا once concerning monasticism.

These four occurrences reveal the central orientation of the Qur’anic uses. The issue is not simply being new. Something beginning now, or new to us, is not necessarily bidʿ. This family highlights the absence of a precedent or comparable earlier model: something has no precedent in its kind or setting, or someone brings something about without reproducing a prior model.

The evidence examined does not establish a particular physical image as the historical origin of all the root’s meanings. We therefore examine unprecedentedness through actual usage, rather than through a hypothetical physical image.

Our opening definition concerns the uses found in the Qur’an:

The Qur’anic uses of B D ʿ: being first or without precedent in a particular setting, or initiating and bringing something about without a comparable prior model serving as its basis. This meaning alone says nothing about initial material, speed of emergence, legitimacy, or moral value.

Qur’anic forms

بِدْعًا
Noun; 1 occurrence

بَدِيعُ
Descriptor on the faʿīl pattern; 2 occurrences

ابْتَدَعُوهَا
Form VIII perfect verb; 1 occurrence

Pre-Qur’anic Arabic and Arabic close to the period of revelation

The poem beginning لمن الديار عفون بالجزع, attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Bashāmah ibn al-Ghadīr, includes:

أَبْلِغْ بَنِي سَهْمٍ لَدَيْكَ فَهَلْ
فِيكُمْ مِنَ الْحَدَثَانِ مِنْ بِدْعِ

“Tell the Banū Sahm there with you: is there, among what time brings upon you, anything strange and unprecedented?”

Here bidʿ describes events brought by time and can be understood as something remarkable and unfamiliar. The subject is not the making of an object. Nor does this line alone establish that the event has never had any parallel in all history.

In the poem beginning بانت سعاد وأمسى حبلها انقطعا, al-Aʿshā Maymūn ibn Qays, a poet close to the period of revelation, praises Hawdhah ibn ʿAlī:

يُرْعِي إِلَى قَوْلِ سَادَاتِ الرِّجَالِ إِذَا
أَبْدَوْا لَهُ الْحَزْمَ أَوْ مَا شَاءَهُ ابْتَدَعَا

“He listens to the words of leading men when they present sound counsel to him, or initiates whatever he himself wishes.”

Here ابتدعا contrasts with accepting the counsel of leading men and concerns undertaking something on one’s own initiative. The line provides evidence for human use of this family, but its precise date of composition cannot be established from the line itself.

These two examples show unfamiliarity and initiative in bringing something about. Each takes its precise meaning from its sentence and setting; neither alone supplies a comprehensive definition of every use of the root.

Mā kuntu bidʿan mina al-rusul: the clearest internal Qur’anic example

The verse in Al-Ahqaf offers perhaps the simplest way to see the meaning:

قُلْ مَا كُنْتُ بِدْعًا مِنَ الرُّسُلِ وَمَا أَدْرِي مَا يُفْعَلُ بِي وَلَا بِكُمْ إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰ إِلَيَّ وَمَا أَنَا إِلَّا نَذِيرٌ مُبِينٌ

“Say: I am not without precedent among the messengers, and I do not know what will be done with me or with you. I follow only what is revealed to me, and I am only a clear warner.”

Al-Ahqaf 46:9

مَا كُنْتُ بِدْعًا مِنَ الرُّسُلِ means that the Prophet is not an unprecedented phenomenon in being a messenger: messengers came before him. Bidʿ here concerns being first and without precedent, not beauty or a negative judgment about messengership.

This also shows that bidʿ need not concern making an object. A person, event, practice, or position can be bidʿ within its kind if it has no comparable precedent.

Precedence and prior examples therefore matter, but this does not mean that everything new is bidʿ. Something may begin for someone today even though thousands of comparable examples already exist.

Badīʿ al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ: originating without a prior model

بَدِيعُ occurs twice in the Qur’an, both times in the identical construction بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ.

First, in Al-Baqarah:

وَقَالُوا اتَّخَذَ اللَّهُ وَلَدًا ۗ سُبْحَانَهُ ۖ بَلْ لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ كُلٌّ لَهُ قَانِتُونَ ۝ بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ وَإِذَا قَضَىٰ أَمْرًا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُ كُنْ فَيَكُونُ

“They said, ‘The God has taken a child.’ Glory be to Him! Rather, whatever is in the heavens and the earth belongs to Him; all are obedient to Him. The Originator of the heavens and the earth without a prior model. When He determines a matter, He only says to it, ‘Be,’ and it is.”

Al-Baqarah 2:116–117

And again, in Al-An‘am:

وَجَعَلُوا لِلَّهِ شُرَكَاءَ الْجِنَّ وَخَلَقَهُمْ وَخَرَقُوا لَهُ بَنِينَ وَبَنَاتٍ بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ ۚ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَىٰ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ ۝ بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ أَنَّىٰ يَكُونُ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَلَمْ تَكُنْ لَهُ صَاحِبَةٌ ۖ وَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ ۖ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

“They assigned the jinn as partners to The God, though He created them, and without knowledge fabricated sons and daughters for Him. Glory be to Him, exalted above what they describe. The Originator of the heavens and the earth without a prior model. How could He have a child when He has had no consort? He created everything, and He knows everything.”

Al-An‘am 6:100–101

The repetition matters: both occurrences of بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ are in a context discussing a child. Yet the root’s meaning must be distinguished from the argument of the surrounding passage.

Badīʿ does not itself mean ‘childless,’ ‘without a consort,’ or ‘creating without procreation.’ Those points come from the surrounding sentences. Its contribution is to attribute the heavens and the earth to an originator whose act is not presented as following a prior model.

The context thus sets the language of procreation and family relationship alongside the origination of the heavens and the earth. This does not require a biological theory, or the claim that the lexical meaning of badaʿa itself negates procreation. That conclusion belongs to the whole passage, not to one word alone.

Does badīʿ mean creation from nothing?

This use highlights the absence of a prior model: origination is not described as reproducing or copying an earlier example.

That is not identical to the philosophical expression ‘creation from nothing.’

B D ʿ concerns precedent and example. The root itself cannot determine whether material existed before the act. Human use makes this boundary clear: a person can also undertake ابتداع, although the verb does not mean that they created the material and resources for the activity out of absolute nothingness.

Thus no prior model is not the same as no prior material.

This neither establishes nor rejects creation from nothing. It only marks the boundary of the root’s meaning: that philosophical question cannot be decided from B D ʿ alone.

In Al-An‘am 6:101, بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ is also followed separately by:

وَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ

In this context, badīʿ highlights origination without a model, while خلق كل شيء expresses the creation of everything. Their co-occurrence alone establishes neither complete synonymy nor complete separation.

Badīʿ describes the agent

بَدِيع follows the pattern فَعِيل. In بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ, it has an active sense: the originator of the heavens and the earth. Its morphological pattern is faʿīl, even though translation expresses the role of an originator.

Both Qur’anic occurrences of badīʿ are in the genitive construction بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ. The independent form الْبَدِيع does not occur in these verses. Analysis should begin with the construction actually present.

Kun fa-yakūn is not part of the meaning of badaʿa

In Al-Baqarah 2:117, بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ is followed by:

وَإِذَا قَضَىٰ أَمْرًا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُ كُنْ فَيَكُونُ

This juxtaposition should not turn badaʿa into a word meaning ‘instantaneous creation.’

B D ʿ addresses a different question: does the phenomenon have a comparable earlier model or precedent within its kind? Speed, duration, and stages of realization do not follow from this root alone.

Something may therefore be badīʿ in relation to precedent without the word saying how long it took to come about.

Monasticism which they originated: origination and evaluation

The Qur’an’s only verb from this root occurs in Al-Hadid:

ثُمَّ قَفَّيْنَا عَلَىٰ آثَارِهِمْ بِرُسُلِنَا وَقَفَّيْنَا بِعِيسَى ابْنِ مَرْيَمَ وَآتَيْنَاهُ الْإِنْجِيلَ وَجَعَلْنَا فِي قُلُوبِ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوهُ رَأْفَةً وَرَحْمَةً وَرَهْبَانِيَّةً ابْتَدَعُوهَا مَا كَتَبْنَاهَا عَلَيْهِمْ إِلَّا ابْتِغَاءَ رِضْوَانِ اللَّهِ فَمَا رَعَوْهَا حَقَّ رِعَايَتِهَا ۖ فَآتَيْنَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنْهُمْ أَجْرَهُمْ وَكَثِيرٌ مِنْهُمْ فَاسِقُونَ

“Then We sent Our messengers in their footsteps, followed them with Jesus son of Mary, and gave him the Gospel. We placed tenderness and mercy in the hearts of those who followed him; and monasticism which they themselves originated—We had not prescribed it for them—[they originated it] only to seek the pleasure of The God. Yet they did not observe it as it deserved to be observed. We gave those among them who believed their reward, but many of them are defiantly disobedient.”

Al-Hadid 57:27

The components of meaning in this verse need to be distinguished.

ابْتَدَعُوهَا says that they themselves originated and instituted monasticism. That it was not prescribed for them comes from the separate clause مَا كَتَبْنَاهَا عَلَيْهِمْ, not from ibtadaʿū alone.

The translation above connects إِلَّا ابْتِغَاءَ رِضْوَانِ اللَّهِ with the motive for instituting monasticism; the supplied verb making that reading explicit is placed in brackets. In the connection between clauses, the phrase can also be related to ما كتبناها عليهم: what was required of them was seeking the pleasure of The God. These are different readings of the sentence’s structure, not different meanings of ibtadaʿū.

This syntactic ambiguity should not be resolved by adding a value judgment to the root’s meaning.

What the word itself expresses is bringing about and instituting something new. Whether it was prescribed, right or wrong, and how it should be evaluated comes from the rest of the sentence and its context.

This distinction prevents treating ibtidāʿ as a lexical synonym for ‘misguidance’ or ‘forbidden.’ The root concerns new origination and precedent; moral or religious evaluation requires further grounds.

Qur’anic forms and evaluation

The noun بِدْعَة in that form does not occur in the Qur’an.

The Qur’an presents a broader range: The God is بديع السماوات والأرض, the Prophet says ما كنت بدعا من الرسل, and human beings can also originate something, ابتدعوا.

At the level of the root, B D ʿ therefore means neither sin nor religious deviation nor commendable innovation. First identify what is unprecedented or newly instituted; its value must then be assessed from context and other criteria.

B D ʿ alongside related roots

B D ʿ and B D ʾ: beginning and the question of precedent

B D ʾ concerns beginning. Yunus 10:4, for example, says يَبْدَأُ الْخَلْقَ ثُمَّ يُعِيدُهُ: He begins creation, then brings it back.

Beginning does not by itself mean that no comparable example existed before. A person may start something today that others have done many times.

B D ʿ highlights a different aspect: being first or unprecedented within a kind, or originating without a comparable prior model.

Al-Ahqaf 46:9 makes this distinction clear:

مَا كُنْتُ بِدْعًا مِنَ الرُّسُلِ

The Prophet is not saying, ‘I have not begun messengership.’ He is saying that he is not without precedent among messengers, because others came before him.

The distinction between B D ʾ and B D ʿ should not become a rigid rule of ‘time versus form.’ Both can concern beginning, but B D ʿ particularly brings in the question of an absent precedent or comparable model.

B D ʿ and F Ṭ R: related perspectives, not separate domains

The Qur’an uses both بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ and, in Al-An‘am 6:14, فَاطِرِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ.

This proximity permits comparison. Within F Ṭ R, Al-Infitar 82:1 speaks of the sky splitting apart with إِذَا السَّمَاءُ انْفَطَرَتْ; fāṭir in Al-An‘am 6:14 describes the originator of the heavens and the earth. In the evidence examined here, B D ʿ highlights the absence of precedent and model. These several uses do not, however, establish the historical order in which their meanings arose.

This difference does not make faṭara exclusive to material creation or badaʿa exclusive to design, geometry, or form. Such a division adds more meaning than the evidence supports.

The two families overlap in origination. The present comparison identifies a difference of emphasis, not a division of all creation into separate domains.

Translation proposals for Forqan

For مَا كُنْتُ بِدْعًا مِنَ الرُّسُلِ, a rendering such as:

“I am not without precedent among the messengers”

conveys the expression more naturally than ‘I am not an innovation among the messengers.’

For بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ, one can say:

“The Originator of the heavens and the earth without a prior model”

Or, more explicitly:

“The One who originated the heavens and the earth without an earlier model serving as their basis”

‘Innovator’ is shorter, but may suggest continuing innovation or artistic creativity to an English reader without adequately expressing the absence of a prior model.

For رَهْبَانِيَّةً ابْتَدَعُوهَا, a natural rendering is:

“Monasticism which they themselves originated”

Or:

“Monasticism which they themselves instituted”

‘They themselves originated’ conveys the meaning without issuing a value judgment through the verb. The absence of prescription and the failure to observe it properly must also be translated from their respective clauses.

Conclusion

B D ʿ occurs only four times in the Qur’an, but its uses have a clear semantic orientation.

Al-Ahqaf 46:9 provides the simplest example. The Prophet is not بدع من الرسل, because messengers came before him. Bidʿ here means being first and unprecedented within one’s kind.

In بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ, that orientation appears in a description of the agent rather than of a being or event: the originator of the heavens and the earth without a comparable earlier model being presented as His basis. Both occurrences of badīʿ discuss a child in their context, but the denial of offspring and procreation belongs to the whole passage, not to the lexical definition of badaʿa alone.

Badaʿa is not equivalent to ‘creation from nothing.’ The root concerns the absence of precedent or model, not the existence or nonexistence of prior material. Human ابتدعوا is therefore consistent with its meaning.

Nor does كُنْ فَيَكُونُ place speed or duration inside the meaning of badaʿa. Whether something is unprecedented is a different question from the time and stages of its realization.

Al-Hadid 57:27 distinguishes the verb’s meaning from the surrounding evaluation. ابتدعوا says that they themselves originated monasticism; ما كتبناها عليهم concerns its not having been prescribed; فما رعوها حق رعايتها concerns their failure to observe it properly. These components should not be compressed into the definition of a single verb.

The resulting definition of the Qur’anic uses is:

B D ʿ: being first or without precedent in a particular setting, or initiating and bringing something about without a comparable prior model serving as its basis. The root alone says nothing about initial material, speed of emergence, moral value, legitimacy, or illegitimacy.
