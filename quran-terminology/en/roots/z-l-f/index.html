Core meaning: drawing near, advancing, and bringing near; more broadly, proximity in place, time, or standing, and enjoying closeness or a position with another.

1. The root and its meaning in early Arabic

Z L F concerns approaching, advancing, and reducing distance. This may be spatial, as when one person approaches another; temporal, as with parts of night close to day; or a matter of standing, as when someone gains respect and recognition with another.

زَلَفَ إِلَيْهِ means to advance toward and approach someone. أَزْلَفَهُ means to bring someone or something near. زُلْفَى and زُلْفَة can denote closeness, rank, and standing with another.

An early lexical explanation defines the root as follows:

الزَّاءُ وَاللَّامُ وَالْفَاءُ يَدُلُّ عَلَى انْدِفَاعٍ وَتَقَدُّمٍ فِي قُرْبٍ إِلَى شَيْءٍ

al-zāʾu wa-al-lāmu wa-al-fāʾu yadullu ʿalā indifāʿin wa-taqaddumin fī qurbin ilā shayʾ

Meaning: “The root indicates advancing and moving closer to something.”

This explanation combines advancing and approaching.

Physical movement is not necessary in every derivative. Nearness may concern attaining a standing, or something becoming close in occurrence or accessibility.

A pre-Islamic poetic witness: zulfah as degree of proximity

The pre-Islamic poet Amr ibn Kulthum is credited with this verse criticizing al-Numan ibn al-Mundhir; its second hemistich has variant transmissions:

لَحَا اللَّهُ أَدْنَانَا إِلَى اللُّؤْمِ زُلْفَةً
وَأَلْأَمَنَا خَالًا وَأَعْجَزَنَا أَبَا

laḥā Allāhu adnā-nā ilā al-luʾmi zulfatan / wa-alʾama-nā khālan wa-aʿjaza-nā abā

Translation:

“May The God condemn whichever of us is closest to baseness, basest in maternal kin, and weakest in father.”

Here زُلْفَةً expresses a degree of proximity to a quality. A variant exchanges “basest” and “weakest” in the second hemistich without changing this use of zulfah.

The poet is not describing physical movement toward a place or person, but closeness to a quality he condemns.

أَدْنَانَا إِلَى اللُّؤْمِ زُلْفَةً shows that zulfah can express degree of proximity to a quality or position.

The witness matters because this sense occurs in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period; it is not inferred solely from Quranic passages about divine nearness.

It also shows that zulfah is not inherently morally positive. A word used for favorable standing can also express proximity to baseness.

The value of nearness follows from its object and context.

What the historical evidence supports

The attributed poetic witness and recorded early Arabic uses support the following account, while individual derivatives require separate dating:

Zalf concerns drawing near and advancing toward something, whether in place or in degree and standing. Zulfah can express the degree of someone’s proximity to a particular position or quality.

The verse attributed to Amr ibn Kulthum directly supports the nonspatial sense of proximity to a quality.

Spatial approach is also recorded in early lexical works, but this one verse cannot date the earliest use of every verbal form.

The use of زُلَف for parts of night is clear in Arabic and the Quran. Its precise pre-Quranic range should not be assumed without independent evidence.

Nearness and approach unite the attested uses; temporal and positional extensions require their own evidence.

2. Important forms and derivatives

زَلَفَ يَزْلُفُ

This Form I verb means to approach, advance, or move toward something.

زَلَفَ إِلَيْهِ

zalafa ilayhi

Meaning: “He advanced toward him and drew near.”

The preposition إلى specifies the direction of approach.

Some uses also express bringing near or presenting something.

زَلْف and زَلَف

These verbal nouns and related nouns concern proximity, nearness, and standing.

Some uses denote a degree or position enjoyed with another.

This need not be an official rank: it can concern relationship, respect, or recognition.

زُلْفَة

It denotes nearness or a degree of proximity.

لَهُ عِنْدَهُ زُلْفَةٌ

lahu ʿindahu zulfatun

Meaning: “He enjoys closeness and standing with him.”

The poem likewise illustrates nonspatial proximity, although there it concerns an undesirable quality.

In temporal use, zulfah can denote a portion of night, especially its early hours.

Its plural in this use is زُلَف.

زُلْفَى

It denotes nearness, proximity, and standing.

Frequently it describes a close position or standing with another person.

In the Quran, zulfā denotes closeness and standing with The God: sometimes a position affirmed for worthy servants, sometimes a goal people claim to attain by worshipping intermediaries.

The word itself does not validate a path or establish divine acceptance. The rest of the verse determines the legitimacy of the approach.

زُلَف

This is the plural of زُلْفَة.

Temporally it denotes portions or hours of night described as adjoining or associated with the night’s beginning and boundaries.

This form occurs in Hud 11:114.

أَزْلَفَ يُزْلِفُ

This Form IV verb means to bring near, bring forward, or make accessible.

أَزْلَفَ الشَّيْءَ

azlafa al-shayʾa

Meaning: “He brought the thing near.”

It can also express gathering people, since bringing people close can gather them in one place.

Gathering is not necessary in every use.

The Quran uses this form for bringing a group of people near and, in the passive, for the Garden being brought near.

زَلَّفَ يُزَلِّفُ

This Form II verb chiefly denotes bringing near or presenting something.

Another recorded use, زَلَّفَ فِي حَدِيثِهِ, means adding to one’s account.

That sense should not be imported into Quranic nearness without evidence.

اِزْدَلَفَ يَزْدَلِفُ

This Form VIII verb means to approach or advance.

اِزْدَلَفَ إِلَيْهِ

izdalafa ilayhi

Meaning: “He drew near to him.”

The form’s tāʾ changes to dāl after zāy.

The root remains Z L F; dāl is not a root consonant.

تَزَلَّفَ

It means to draw near or seek someone’s attention and favor.

Social uses sometimes concern approaching a powerful person for benefit or approval.

Thus some later uses connect tazalluf with flattery.

Approaching and seeking closeness do not inherently mean flattery or wrongdoing.

When accompanied by deceit, display, or opportunism, the negative value arises from that conduct and purpose.

مُزْدَلِف

The active participle of izdalafa denotes someone approaching or advancing.

مُزْدَلِفَة

Muzdalifah is a place on the pilgrimage route between Arafat and Mina.

Explanations of its name mention pilgrims drawing closer to Mina or gathering there.

These fit the semantic field, but each historical naming account needs independent support before being treated as certain.

The name Muzdalifah itself does not occur in the Quran, although the Sacred Monument is mentioned in Al-Baqarah 2:198.

مِزْلَف

It is recorded for a step or means of ascending to another level.

Its plural is مَزَالِف.

Here movement between levels or stages is involved.

Related forms also have recorded senses such as a full dish, basin, mirror, or meadow; their vocalization must be distinguished, including زَلَفَة for a full dish. The historical connection of every such sense with proximity is not established, and they should not be imposed on the Quranic definition.

The root occurs ten times in the Quran, in ten verses, as زُلَفًا, زُلْفَةً, زُلْفَى, أَزْلَفْنَا, and أُزْلِفَتْ.

3. Spatial and temporal nearness in the Quran

Hud 11:114: portions of the night

وَأَقِمِ الصَّلَاةَ طَرَفَيِ النَّهَارِ وَزُلَفًا مِنَ اللَّيْلِ إِنَّ الْحَسَنَاتِ يُذْهِبْنَ السَّيِّئَاتِ ذَٰلِكَ ذِكْرَىٰ لِلذَّاكِرِينَ

wa-aqimi al-ṣalāta ṭarafayi al-nahāri wa-zulafan mina al-layli inna al-ḥasanāti yudhhibna al-sayyiʾāti dhālika dhikrā li-al-dhākirīn

Hūd 11:114

Translation:

“Establish ṣalāh at the two ends of the day and in portions of the night. Good deeds indeed remove bad deeds. This is a reminder for those who remember.”

زُلَفًا is the plural of زُلْفَة and denotes portions of night.

Syntactically, it functions as an adverbial expression of time.

طَرَفَيِ النَّهَارِ denotes the day’s two ends; زُلَفًا مِنَ اللَّيْلِ adds portions of night.

Two related explanations are recorded for the night’s zulaf.

One concerns early night hours close to the day’s end.

The other concerns neighboring or consecutive portions of night.

Some lexical accounts also mention hours close to day and portions adjacent to the boundary between night and day.

Temporal proximity is therefore significant.

The word alone does not establish a fixed number of hours or precise boundaries for each portion.

The night’s portions and ṣalāh

Hud 11:114 commands establishing ṣalāh at the two ends of day and in portions of night.

Related passages include Al-Isra 17:78, Ta-Ha 20:130, and Al-Nur 24:58, which connect times of day and night with ṣalāh, glorification, or daily life.

Zulafan is not lexically the name of a particular prayer.

It denotes periods of time; its connection with ṣalāh follows from أَقِمِ الصَّلَاةَ and the complete sentence.

Substituting the names of particular prayers therefore supplies an interpreted application rather than the lexical meaning.

A root study should retain the temporal sense and leave the detailed relation to prayer times to a dedicated study.

Al-Shuara 26:64: bringing a group of people near

وَأَزْلَفْنَا ثَمَّ الْآخَرِينَ

wa-azlafnā thamma al-ākharīn

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:64

Translation:

“And We brought the others near there.”

أَزْلَفْنَا is a past-tense Form IV verb.

Its first-person plural subject refers here to The God.

الْآخَرِينَ is its object.

ثَمَّ, with fatḥah on thāʾ, means “there”; it must be distinguished from ثُمَّ, “then.”

The preceding verses recount Moses, Pharaoh, and their arrival at the sea.

Al-Shuara 26:63 describes the sea splitting.

Al-Shuara 26:64 describes the other group being brought near.

Al-Shuara 26:65 describes Moses and his companions being saved.

Al-Shuara 26:66 describes the other group drowning.

The context identifies “the others” as Pharaoh and his accompanying forces.

Azlafnā expresses bringing them near in that setting.

Some lexical explanations render it “gathered,” connecting gathering in one place with proximity among people.

“Brought near” more directly preserves the root’s sense in translation.

Their drowning is stated two verses later, in Al-Shuara 26:66; azlafnā itself does not mean drowning or destroying.

Al-Mulk 67:27: the threatened promise seen close at hand

فَلَمَّا رَأَوْهُ زُلْفَةً سِيئَتْ وُجُوهُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا وَقِيلَ هَٰذَا الَّذِي كُنْتُمْ بِهِ تَدَّعُونَ

fa-lammā raʾawhu zulfatan sīʾat wujūhu alladhīna kafarū wa-qīla hādhā alladhī kuntum bihi taddaʿūn

Al-Mulk 67:27

Translation:

“When they see it close at hand, the faces of those who disbelieved become distressed, and it is said: This is what you were calling for.”

زُلْفَةً describes the nearness of what they see.

The antecedent of رَأَوْهُ must be understood from the preceding verses.

Al-Mulk 67:25 records opponents asking when the promise will occur.

Al-Mulk 67:26 answers that knowledge of it rests with The God and that the messenger is a clear warner.

Al-Mulk 67:27 then describes seeing that promise close at hand.

Zulfah concerns the nearness of something whose fulfillment they had considered distant or whose timing they questioned.

Here proximity accompanies terror and altered faces.

The word does not itself mean punishment or fear. These come from the context and the response to the fulfilled promise.

This also shows that zulfah need not denote welcome proximity or favorable standing.

4. Zulfā: closeness and standing with The God

Sad 38:25: David’s nearness and good return

فَغَفَرْنَا لَهُ ذَٰلِكَ وَإِنَّ لَهُ عِنْدَنَا لَزُلْفَىٰ وَحُسْنَ مَآبٍ

fa-ghafarnā lahu dhālika wa-inna lahu ʿindanā la-zulfā wa-ḥusna maʾāb

Ṣād 38:25

Translation:

“We forgave him that; indeed, he has closeness with Us and a good return.”

The verse occurs in David’s story.

After his trial and return to The God, it speaks of forgiveness and his position.

لَزُلْفَى denotes David’s closeness and standing with The God.

Here nearness is not a measurable distance between two bodies.

عِنْدَنَا together with zulfā expresses standing.

حُسْنَ مَآبٍ separately expresses a good return.

Zulfā should not be defined as forgiveness or a good return, although these appear together.

Sad 38:40: Solomon’s nearness and good return

وَإِنَّ لَهُ عِنْدَنَا لَزُلْفَىٰ وَحُسْنَ مَآبٍ

wa-inna lahu ʿindanā la-zulfā wa-ḥusna maʾāb

Ṣād 38:40

Translation:

“Indeed, he has closeness with Us and a good return.”

The same expression is used of Solomon.

The preceding verses describe his blessings, abilities, and trials.

His position with The God is then expressed through zulfā and a good return.

This parallels the verse about David.

Both use zulfā for favorable closeness and standing.

Neither restricts zulfā to prophets: other verses connect it with righteous action and human standing.

Saba 34:37: wealth and children are not the measure of closeness

وَمَا أَمْوَالُكُمْ وَلَا أَوْلَادُكُمْ بِالَّتِي تُقَرِّبُكُمْ عِنْدَنَا زُلْفَىٰ إِلَّا مَنْ آمَنَ وَعَمِلَ صَالِحًا فَأُولَٰئِكَ لَهُمْ جَزَاءُ الضِّعْفِ بِمَا عَمِلُوا وَهُمْ فِي الْغُرُفَاتِ آمِنُونَ

wa-mā amwālukum wa-lā awlādukum bi-allatī tuqarribukum ʿindanā zulfā illā man āmana wa-ʿamila ṣāliḥan fa-ulāʾika lahum jazāʾu al-ḍiʿfi bi-mā ʿamilū wa-hum fī al-ghurufāti āminūn

Sabaʾ 34:37

Translation:

“Neither your wealth nor your children bring you close to Us in standing, except one who believes and acts righteously: such people receive a multiplied reward for what they have done and will be secure in the chambers.”

Here زُلْفَى accompanies تُقَرِّبُكُمْ.

The expression uses the verb “bring near” in speaking of attaining zulfā.

The two words nevertheless have distinct roles.

تُقَرِّبُكُمْ expresses the act of bringing near.

زُلْفَى expresses the closeness and standing being discussed.

The verse explicitly denies that wealth and children themselves establish nearness to The God.

It places belief and righteous action against reliance on material advantages alone.

Closeness here concerns belief and conduct, rather than mere wealth, number of children, or social position.

This does not condemn possessing wealth or having children.

The verse does not declare these blessings forbidden or objectionable; it rejects treating them as guarantees of divine nearness.

Al-Zumar 39:3: claiming proximity through worship of intermediaries

أَلَا لِلَّهِ الدِّينُ الْخَالِصُ وَالَّذِينَ اتَّخَذُوا مِنْ دُونِهِ أَوْلِيَاءَ مَا نَعْبُدُهُمْ إِلَّا لِيُقَرِّبُونَا إِلَى اللَّهِ زُلْفَىٰ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَهُمْ فِي مَا هُمْ فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَهْدِي مَنْ هُوَ كَاذِبٌ كَفَّارٌ

alā li-Allāhi al-dīnu al-khāliṣu wa-alladhīna ittakhadhū min dūnihi awliyāʾa mā naʿbuduhum illā li-yuqarribūnā ilā Allāhi zulfā inna Allāha yaḥkumu baynahum fī mā hum fīhi yakhtalifūna inna Allāha lā yahdī man huwa kādhibun kaffār

Al-Zumar 39:3

Translation:

“Indeed, pure religion belongs to The God. Those who take protectors apart from Him say: We worship them only so that they may bring us near to The God. The God will judge between them concerning their disagreements. The God does not guide one who is a liar and persistently ungrateful.”

Zulfā is the goal claimed to justify worshipping other protectors.

The stated justification does not require claiming that these protectors independently created the heavens and earth. It is that worshipping them brings the worshippers near to The God.

The Quran presents this claim while emphasizing pure religion.

Their conduct is not condemned by the word zulfā itself.

Zulfā means closeness; seeking nearness to The God is elsewhere affirmed.

The issue is the chosen path: worshipping others apart from The God.

Saba 34:37 connects divine nearness with belief and righteous action.

Al-Zumar 39:3 challenges worship of other protectors as a means to that nearness.

The distinction shows that intending to draw near to The God does not by itself validate every means or action.

The path must also be measured against divine command and guidance.

Related passages concerning means and intercession include Al-Maidah 5:35, Al-Isra 17:56, Al-Isra 17:57, and Yunus 10:18.

Zulfā should not be lexically redefined as intercession, worship of intermediaries, or belief and righteous action.

These concern paths to closeness, rather than the word’s direct meaning.

5. The Garden brought near

Three Quranic verses use أُزْلِفَتْ of the Garden.

Al-Shuara 26:90

وَأُزْلِفَتِ الْجَنَّةُ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ

wa-uzlifati al-jannatu li-al-muttaqīn

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:90

Translation:

“The Garden was brought near for the mindful.”

أُزْلِفَتْ is the passive past tense of Form IV.

الْجَنَّةُ is its passive subject.

لِلْمُتَّقِينَ identifies those for whom the Garden is brought near.

This forms part of the description of their reward and provision.

The verb itself means bringing near, rather than reward.

Qaf 50:31

وَأُزْلِفَتِ الْجَنَّةُ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ غَيْرَ بَعِيدٍ

wa-uzlifati al-jannatu li-al-muttaqīna ghayra baʿīd

Qāf 50:31

Translation:

“The Garden was brought near for the mindful, not far away.”

غَيْرَ بَعِيدٍ makes nearness more explicit.

أُزْلِفَتْ expresses bringing near; غَيْرَ بَعِيدٍ adds that it is not distant.

The root here concerns proximity and accessibility.

The expression does not establish a particular geographical distance between people and the Garden.

The verse reports the Garden being brought near without fully explaining how this occurs in the hereafter.

Al-Takwir 81:13

وَإِذَا الْجَنَّةُ أُزْلِفَتْ

wa-idhā al-jannatu uzlifat

Al-Takwīr 81:13

Translation:

“And when the Garden is brought near.”

This is one of a series of resurrection events.

Preceding verses describe great transformations and hidden matters becoming manifest.

Al-Takwir 81:12 describes the blazing Fire being kindled.

Al-Takwir 81:13 describes the Garden being brought near.

Al-Takwir 81:14 concludes the sequence with each soul knowing what it has brought forward.

Here أُزْلِفَتْ describes an event associated with the Day of Reckoning.

Is bringing the Garden near merely metaphorical?

All three verses describe the Garden being brought near.

This can be understood in relation to readiness for entry and accessibility.

Unknown features of the hereafter do not justify reducing the account to a psychological feeling or a metaphor without reality.

Nor should its distance, route, or physical mechanism be settled by the geography and physics of our familiar world.

The linguistic meaning is clear: the Garden is brought near.

These verses alone do not fully specify how it happens.

6. Related roots

Z L F (زَلْف) and Q R B (قُرْب)

Both concern nearness.

Qurb covers spatial, temporal, relational, and positional proximity.

Zalf also concerns proximity, frequently emphasizing approach, bringing near, or attaining closeness.

Saba 34:37 and Al-Zumar 39:3 use both roots in one construction.

This supports their semantic affinity, but does not prove that zulfā always denotes a higher degree than qurb.

Zulfā can itself mean nearness and standing, with degree supplied by context.

Z L F (زَلْف) and D N W (دُنُوّ)

Dunūw denotes approaching and being near.

Its Quranic uses are both spatial and nonspatial.

Zalf also concerns approaching, while zulfā often emphasizes standing with another.

Dunūw, however, can likewise be nonspatial.

No absolute boundary makes dunūw exclusively bodily and zalf exclusively spiritual.

Z L F (زُلْفَى) and W S L (وَسِيلَة)

Wasīlah concerns seeking a means of approach and reaching a destination.

Al-Maidah 5:35 commands believers to seek a means of approach to The God.

Quranic zulfā denotes closeness and standing themselves.

The concepts are related but not identical.

Wasīlah concerns a means or path; zulfā concerns the closeness or position sought.

Zulfā in Al-Zumar 39:3 should therefore not be translated directly as “intermediary.”

The worshipped intermediaries are called أَوْلِيَاءَ; zulfā is the claimed goal of closeness to The God.

Z L F (زَلْف) and S B Q (سَبْق)

Sabq denotes preceding or getting ahead.

Some uses of zalf likewise involve advancing and approaching.

Getting ahead does not necessarily mean approaching a destination or another person.

Someone may surpass another without gaining any particular closeness or standing.

Conversely, zulfā can express position without competition or overtaking anyone.

Z L F (زَلْف) and Z L Q (زَلَق)

Their consonants resemble one another, but the roots differ in meaning.

Zalaq concerns slipping, slipperiness, and sliding out of place.

Zalf concerns approaching and advancing.

Sound similarity does not establish a common origin or identical meaning.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

The following equivalents suit the principal forms. These research proposals do not replace installed verse translations or existing authored word-box explanations.

زَلَفَ: approached, advanced, or moved toward something.

زَلْف and زَلَف: closeness, proximity, or standing.

زُلْفَة: nearness or degree of proximity; temporally, a portion of night.

زُلَف: portions or hours of night, particularly its early portions.

زُلْفَى: closeness or standing with another.

أَزْلَفَ: brought near or brought forward.

أُزْلِفَتْ: was brought near.

زَلَّفَ: brought near or presented.

اِزْدَلَفَ: approached and advanced.

تَزَلَّفَ: sought closeness; sometimes sought attention or benefit socially.

مُزْدَلِف: one approaching or advancing.

مُزْدَلِفَة: a place between Arafat and Mina.

مِزْلَف: a step or means of ascending and approaching.

Proposed Quranic equivalents

In Hud 11:114, زُلَفًا مِنَ اللَّيْلِ can be “portions of the night,” with their proximity to its beginning or to one another explained separately.

In Al-Shuara 26:64, أَزْلَفْنَا means “We brought near.”

In Al-Mulk 67:27, زُلْفَةً describes the nearness of what is seen.

In Sad 38:25 and Sad 38:40, زُلْفَى denotes closeness and standing with The God.

In Saba 34:37, it similarly denotes standing with The God that wealth and children alone cannot guarantee.

In Al-Zumar 39:3, it is the claimed goal of drawing near to The God, not a name for intermediaries or approval of their worship.

In Al-Shuara 26:90, Qaf 50:31, and Al-Takwir 81:13, passive أُزْلِفَتْ means the Garden being brought near.

A brief root explanation could read:

“Drawing near and bringing near; proximity in place, time, or standing.”

Verse-specific meanings should remain distinct: temporal proximity in Hud 11:114, standing in Sad 38:25, and the Garden being brought near in Qaf 50:31 are different applications of the same family.

8. Conclusion

Z L F denotes approaching, advancing, and bringing near.

Nearness can concern place, time, or standing.

The verse attributed to Amr ibn Kulthum supports nonspatial proximity in pre-Islamic poetry; it does not, by itself, date every derivative and meaning.

The Quran uses the root in several settings.

In Hud 11:114, zulafan denotes portions of night.

In Al-Shuara 26:64, azlafnā describes bringing a group of people near.

In Al-Mulk 67:27, zulfah describes the nearness of a promise whose fulfillment terrifies the deniers.

In Sad 38:25, Sad 38:40, and Saba 34:37, zulfā concerns closeness and standing with The God.

In Al-Zumar 39:3, it is the goal claimed to justify worshipping other protectors.

Al-Shuara 26:90, Qaf 50:31, and Al-Takwir 81:13 describe the Garden being brought near.

These uses do not make zalf inherently mean favorable standing, intercession, worship, or punishment. It expresses proximity and approach; value, direction, and outcome follow from context.

The root concerns drawing near or bringing near in place, time, or standing; zulfā particularly emphasizes closeness and a position enjoyed with another.

This account accommodates the recorded early Arabic evidence and Quranic uses without restricting the family to one application.
