1. Introduction and Qur’anic forms

The triliteral root ʿ SH R has two prominent Qur’anic fields: the number ten and its related forms, and association, companionship, and bonds between people. The family also includes ʿishār, pregnant she-camels, a name connected with the number of months of pregnancy.

In Arabic, ʿasharah is ten; ʿushr is one of ten parts; ʿishrah concerns association and living with others; ʿashīr is a companion or associate; ʿashīrah is a body connected through kinship or social bonds; and maʿshar is a group sharing a characteristic.

These words belong to one root but are not interchangeable. In particular, ʿashīrah and maʿshar do not simply mean ten people, nor should ʿāshirūhunna be explained through the number ten.

The root occurs 27 times in 26 verses across 19 surahs. Its Qur’anic forms include:

  • عَشْر / عَشَر: ten, independently or within compound numbers. Qur’anic: yes.
  • عَشَرَة: ten, in numerical constructions. Qur’anic: yes.
  • عِشْرُونَ: twenty. Qur’anic: yes, in Al-Anfāl 8:65.
  • مِعْشَار: one tenth, or a portion amounting to one tenth. Qur’anic: yes, in Sabaʾ 34:45.
  • عِشَار: pregnant she-camels approaching delivery. Qur’anic: yes, in Al-Takwīr 81:4.
  • عَاشَرَ / يُعَاشِرُ: to associate, live with, or maintain close relations. Qur’anic: yes, only as the imperative wa-ʿāshirūhunna in Al-Nisāʾ 4:19.
  • عَشِير: a companion or person in a close, continuing relationship. Qur’anic: yes, in Al-Ḥajj 22:13.
  • عَشِيرَة: kindred, clan, or a group connected by social and family bonds. Qur’anic: yes, in Al-Tawbah 9:24, Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:214, and Al-Mujādilah 58:22.
  • مَعْشَر: a group or assembly sharing a characteristic or bond. Qur’anic: yes, in Al-Anʿām 6:128, Al-Anʿām 6:130, and Al-Raḥmān 55:33.
  • عِشْرَة: association and living with another. Qur’anic: no, in this nominal form.
  • مُعَاشَرَة: companionship, living together, and interaction within a relationship. Qur’anic: no, in this verbal-noun form.
  • تَعَاشَرَ: to associate with one another. Qur’anic: no.
  • عُشْر: one tenth of an amount or property. Qur’anic: no, as this independent form; miʿshār does occur.
  • عَشَّرَ / تَعْشِير: to make ten, take a tenth, or, in other uses, repeat an action a specified number of times. Qur’anic: no.
  • عَشَّار: someone taking a tenth or a levy. Qur’anic: no.
  • عَاشُورَاء: the familiar name of the tenth day of Muḥarram in historical and post-Qur’anic usage. Qur’anic: no.

Numerical forms make up the most frequent branch, while derivatives concerning association and social bonds retain their own place.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: twenty-seven occurrences of ʿ SH R

2. Two semantic fields in early Arabic

Ten and counting

ʿAsharah names the number ten: a definite, countable quantity that also contributes to larger and compound numbers such as eleven, twelve, and twenty.

This field yields words for a tenth, the tenth person, bringing a group’s membership to ten, groups of ten, and taking a tenth of property.

For example, one use of ʿashartu al-qawma describes joining nine people as their tenth member.

Another use concerns taking a tenth of property.

Ten connects these senses, but becoming a group’s tenth member is not the same act as taking a tenth of its assets.

ʿUshr, “one tenth,” differs in vowels and meaning from ʿashr, “ten.” This matters in reading Arabic: a vowel change can turn a whole number into the name of one of its fractions.

Association and companionship

The second field is ʿishrah and muʿāsharah.

It concerns living with, accompanying, relating to, and interacting with another, rather than counting people.

ʿĀsharahu means associating or living with someone. The relationship can involve spouses, relatives, or others in continuing contact.

ʿAshīr likewise denotes someone in close association with another.

The relationship can be good or bad. Association does not lexically guarantee a righteous, loyal, or upright companion.

The Qur’an uses al-ʿashīr in a reproachful phrase, whereas ʿāshirūhunna with bi-al-maʿrūf gives a positive command about conduct in a relationship.

Moral force therefore comes from context, not the root alone.

Did association develop from the number ten?

One might imagine that life in a small, familiar group, perhaps ten people, helped name certain social relations.

Sharing a root does not prove that historical origin.

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris identifies two distinct origins: a definite number, and mixing or association.

On that account, ʿashīrah is explained through its members’ association, not a necessary membership of ten.

We should not invent a unified, appealing definition claiming that every meaning arose from ten people gathering in a house or tribe.

Without independent historical evidence, that image remains a hypothesis.

Linguistic conclusion: ʿ SH R has at least two established fields: counting through ten, and association between people. Qur’anic analysis should keep them distinct.

3. Early Arabic witnesses: counting, association, and social life

Association in Zuhayr’s poetry

A poem attributed to Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā states:

لَعَمْرُكَ وَالْخُطُوبُ مُغَيِّرَاتٌ
وَفِي طُولِ الْمُعَاشَرَةِ التَّقَالِي

la-ʿamruka wa-al-khuṭūbu mughayyirātun / wa-fī ṭūli al-muʿāsharati al-taqālī

English translation

By your life, events bring change, and prolonged association can bring estrangement and aversion.

Al-muʿāsharah concerns continuing contact and companionship.

The poet does not discuss ten people or ten days. The relationship can change over time.

Association itself does not mean aversion; al-taqālī supplies that meaning.

The witness belongs to the poetic tradition attributed to a pre-Islamic poet and illustrates the family’s social use.

Al-Zajjājī’s Al-Lāmāt: the Zuhayr witness

Maʿshar in Imruʾ al-Qays’ poetry

Another verse attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays states:

تَجَاوَزْتُ أَحْرَاسًا إِلَيْهَا وَمَعْشَرًا
عَلَيَّ حِرَاصًا لَوْ يُسِرُّونَ مَقْتَلِي

tajāwaztu aḥrāsan ilayhā wa-maʿsharan / ʿalayya ḥirāṣan law yusirrūna maqtalī

English translation

To reach her, I passed guards and a company eager to kill me who, if able, would have killed me secretly.

Maʿshar denotes a company of people.

Nothing in the verse establishes that they numbered exactly ten.

This early collective meaning accords with the Qur’anic maʿshar al-jinn wa-al-ins: companies of jinn and humans, not groups of ten.

Kamāl’s Sharḥ al-Shāfiyah: maʿshar and the explanation of secret killing

Aʿshār in Imruʾ al-Qays’ poetry

The Muʿallaqah attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays states:

وَمَا ذَرَفَتْ عَيْنَاكِ إِلَّا لِتَضْرِبِي
بِسَهْمَيْكِ فِي أَعْشَارِ قَلْبٍ مُقَتَّلِ

wa-mā dharafat ʿaynāki illā li-taḍribī / bi-sahmayki fī aʿshāri qalbin muqattali

English translation

Your eyes shed tears only so that, with your two arrows, you might strike the portions of a wounded heart.

Aʿshār concerns pieces or portions of the heart.

Two classical readings matter: broken pieces, like a shattered vessel, and ten divided shares. In the maysir reading, the two lots al-muʿallā and al-raqīb carry seven and three shares, depicting possession of all ten. On this reading, “two arrows” does not merely describe two shots in battle.

Both readings illustrate figurative use.

Neither proves that Arabic literally divided the human heart into ten anatomical parts or that the poet was discussing its anatomy.

This is a poetic image.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ and Lisān al-ʿArab: aʿshār and the two maysir lots

ʿIshār and pastoral life

Arabic ʿusharāʾ denotes a she-camel about ten months into pregnancy and approaching delivery. Its plural is ʿishār.

The connection with ten is clear.

In a society where camels supplied important property, transport, and livelihood, a she-camel approaching delivery could have particular economic value.

That historical background is not the lexical meaning itself; the word first identifies an animal at a particular stage of pregnancy.

Lexicons also record continued use after delivery. It cannot therefore be restricted, without examination, to one exact day or unvarying stage in every Arabic occurrence.

The Qur’an uses the word in Al-Takwīr.

4. Ten in the Qur’an: a definite count, not an automatic symbol of perfection

Al-Baqarah 2:196: three days and seven days

Verse text

وَأَتِمُّوا الْحَجَّ وَالْعُمْرَةَ لِلَّهِ فَإِنْ أُحْصِرْتُمْ فَمَا اسْتَيْسَرَ مِنَ الْهَدْيِ وَلَا تَحْلِقُوا رُءُوسَكُمْ حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ الْهَدْيُ مَحِلَّهُ فَمَنْ كَانَ مِنْكُمْ مَرِيضًا أَوْ بِهِ أَذًى مِنْ رَأْسِهِ فَفِدْيَةٌ مِنْ صِيَامٍ أَوْ صَدَقَةٍ أَوْ نُسُكٍ فَإِذَا أَمِنْتُمْ فَمَنْ تَمَتَّعَ بِالْعُمْرَةِ إِلَى الْحَجِّ فَمَا اسْتَيْسَرَ مِنَ الْهَدْيِ فَمَنْ لَمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ فِي الْحَجِّ وَسَبْعَةٍ إِذَا رَجَعْتُمْ تِلْكَ عَشَرَةٌ كَامِلَةٌ ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ أَهْلُهُ حَاضِرِي الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ شَدِيدُ الْعِقَابِ

wa-atimmū al-ḥajja wa-al-ʿumrata li-Allāhi fa-in uḥṣirtum fa-mā istaysara mina al-hadyi wa-lā taḥliqū ruʾūsakum ḥattā yablugha al-hadyu maḥillahu fa-man kāna minkum marīḍan aw bihi adhan min raʾsihi fa-fidyatun min ṣiyāmin aw ṣadaqatin aw nusukin fa-idhā amintum fa-man tamattaʿa bi-al-ʿumrati ilā al-ḥajji fa-mā istaysara mina al-hadyi fa-man lam yajid fa-ṣiyāmu thalāthati ayyāmin fī al-ḥajji wa-sabʿatin idhā rajaʿtum tilka ʿasharatun kāmilatun dhālika li-man lam yakun ahluhu ḥāḍirī al-masjidi al-ḥarāmi wa-ittaqū Allāha wa-iʿlamū anna Allāha shadīdu al-ʿiqābi

English translation

Complete the Ḥajj and ʿUmrah for The God. If you are prevented, then whatever offering is feasible; and do not shave your heads until the offering reaches its place. Whoever among you is ill or has discomfort affecting his head, then a compensation of fasting, charity, or sacrifice. When you are secure, whoever benefits from ʿUmrah until the Ḥajj, then whatever offering is feasible; whoever cannot find one shall fast three days during the Ḥajj and seven when you return: ten complete days. This is for one whose family is not among those present at the Sacred Mosque. Be mindful of The God, and know that The God is severe in requital.

Usage analysis

Tilka ʿasharatun kāmilah explicitly states the number ten.

Three and seven days precede it, so ten is the sum of two definite quantities.

ʿAsharah alone should not mean moral perfection or the completion of a spiritual journey.

Kāmilah belongs to the separate root K M L and expresses the completeness of the count.

Ten days specifies an obligation in the pilgrimage context, so the number should retain its numerical meaning.

Its presence does not establish that every group of ten in the Qur’an has a fixed, special degree of sanctity.

Al-Baqarah 2:234: four months and ten days

Verse text

وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنْكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنْفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا فَإِذَا بَلَغْنَ أَجَلَهُنَّ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِيمَا فَعَلْنَ فِي أَنْفُسِهِنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَاللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ خَبِيرٌ

wa-alladhīna yutawaffawna minkum wa-yadharūna azwājan yatarabbaṣna bi-anfusihinna arbaʿata ashhurin wa-ʿashran fa-idhā balaghna ajalahunna fa-lā junāḥa ʿalaykum fī-mā faʿalna fī anfusihinna bi-al-maʿrūfi wa-Allāhu bi-mā taʿmalūna khabīrun

English translation

Those among you who die and leave wives: those women shall wait concerning themselves for four months and ten days. When they reach the end of their term, there is no blame upon you for what they do concerning themselves in a recognized good manner. The God is aware of what you do.

Usage analysis

ʿAshran accompanies four months.

The counted unit for ten is not explicit in the Arabic phrase. Arabic conventions for counting periods relate it to ten days and nights.

“Four months and ten days” conveys the sense naturally in Persian.

Ten can thus specify a definite legal period.

The number’s meaning remains distinct from the ruling’s subject: ten itself does not mean waiting, mourning, or restriction on marriage. The complete construction supplies those concepts.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:142: completing Moses’ appointed period

Verse text

وَوَاعَدْنَا مُوسَىٰ ثَلَاثِينَ لَيْلَةً وَأَتْمَمْنَاهَا بِعَشْرٍ فَتَمَّ مِيقَاتُ رَبِّهِ أَرْبَعِينَ لَيْلَةً وَقَالَ مُوسَىٰ لِأَخِيهِ هَارُونَ اخْلُفْنِي فِي قَوْمِي وَأَصْلِحْ وَلَا تَتَّبِعْ سَبِيلَ الْمُفْسِدِينَ

wa-wāʿadnā Mūsā thalāthīna laylatan wa-atmamnāhā bi-ʿashrin fa-tamma mīqātu rabbihi arbaʿīna laylatan wa-qāla Mūsā li-akhīhi Hārūna ukhlufnī fī qawmī wa-aṣliḥ wa-lā tattabiʿ sabīla al-mufsidīna

English translation

We appointed thirty nights for Moses and completed them with ten, so his Lord’s appointed period became forty nights. Moses told his brother Aaron, “Take my place among my people, act to put things right, and do not follow the way of those who spread corruption.”

Usage analysis

Bi-ʿashrin means ten further nights. The unit is not repeated but is understood from thalāthīna laylah, “thirty nights.”

Ten is again a definite quantity, added to thirty.

The result is forty nights.

Ten itself cannot establish a hidden stage in a symbolic or mystical theory of Moses’ journey.

The complete verse concerns an appointment, a promise, and a completed period; ʿashr itself simply gives the quantity ten.

Al-Māʾidah 5:89: feeding ten people in need

Verse text

لَا يُؤَاخِذُكُمُ اللَّهُ بِاللَّغْوِ فِي أَيْمَانِكُمْ وَلَٰكِنْ يُؤَاخِذُكُمْ بِمَا عَقَّدْتُمُ الْأَيْمَانَ فَكَفَّارَتُهُ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ أَوْ كِسْوَتُهُمْ أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ فَمَنْ لَمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ ذَٰلِكَ كَفَّارَةُ أَيْمَانِكُمْ إِذَا حَلَفْتُمْ وَاحْفَظُوا أَيْمَانَكُمْ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ آيَاتِهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

lā yuʾākhidhukumu Allāhu bi-al-laghwi fī aymānikum wa-lākin yuʾākhidhukum bi-mā ʿaqqadtumu al-aymāna fa-kaffāratuhu iṭʿāmu ʿasharati masākīna min awsaṭi mā tuṭʿimūna ahlīkum aw kiswatuhum aw taḥrīru raqabatin fa-man lam yajid fa-ṣiyāmu thalāthati ayyāmin dhālika kaffāratu aymānikum idhā ḥalaftum wa-iḥfaẓū aymānakum kadhālika yubayyinu Allāhu lakum āyātihi laʿallakum tashkurūna

English translation

The God does not hold you accountable for idle words in your oaths, but holds you accountable for oaths you have bound firmly. Their expiation is feeding ten people in need from the average food you give your families, or clothing them, or freeing a neck [a person from bondage]. Whoever cannot find the means shall fast three days. That is the expiation of your oaths when you swear; guard your oaths. Thus The God makes His signs clear to you, so that you may be thankful.

Usage analysis

ʿAsharati masākīn specifies the number of people in need.

Ten itself does not mean food, charity, or expiation. Other phrases specify the obligation and its nature.

Ten people in need must be distinguished from ten meals. Masākīn is the counted noun: ten people in need.

Without an independent argument, feeding one person ten times cannot be declared the exact literal meaning of feeding ten people.

Numbers in Qur’anic rulings must be read with their counted nouns and syntax.

5. Other numerical uses: eleven, twelve, and twenty

The family also forms compound numbers.

In Al-Baqarah 2:60 and Al-Aʿrāf 7:160, ithnatā ʿashrata concerns twelve springs. Al-Aʿrāf 7:160 also uses ithnatay ʿashrata for twelve groups of the Children of Israel.

Al-Māʾidah 5:12 uses ithnay ʿashara for twelve representatives.

These forms reflect grammatical gender and syntactic role, not different meanings of twelve.

Aḥada ʿashara in Yūsuf 12:4 gives eleven for the stars in the dream.

Ithnā ʿashara shahran in Al-Tawbah 9:36 gives the year’s twelve months.

Al-Muddaththir 74:30 states:

عَلَيْهَا تِسْعَةَ عَشَرَ

ʿalayhā tisʿata ʿashara

English translation

Over it are nineteen.

Tisʿata ʿashara means nineteen. Al-Muddaththir 74:30 does not explicitly name the counted beings; Al-Muddaththir 74:31 discusses angels appointed over the Fire.

Nineteen alone should not become an overarching theory of the Qur’an’s entire numerical structure without independent evidence.

Al-Anfāl 8:65 gives twenty:

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ حَرِّضِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ عَلَى الْقِتَالِ إِنْ يَكُنْ مِنْكُمْ عِشْرُونَ صَابِرُونَ يَغْلِبُوا مِائَتَيْنِ وَإِنْ يَكُنْ مِنْكُمْ مِائَةٌ يَغْلِبُوا أَلْفًا مِنَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا بِأَنَّهُمْ قَوْمٌ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ

yā ayyuhā al-nabiyyu ḥarriḍi al-muʾminīna ʿalā al-qitāli in yakun minkum ʿishrūna ṣābirūna yaghlibū miʾatayni wa-in yakun minkum miʾatun yaghlibū alfan mina alladhīna kafarū bi-annahum qawmun lā yafqahūna

English translation

Prophet, urge the believers to battle. If twenty among you are steadfast, they will overcome two hundred; if a hundred, they will overcome a thousand of those who disbelieve, because those are a people who do not understand.

ʿIshrūn names twenty.

The ratio of twenty to two hundred belongs to the construction. Twenty itself does not mean steadfastness, victory, or military power.

Al-Anfāl 8:66 then considers the addressees’ strength and weakness in expressing ratios. These numbers should not be detached from conditions and context to create an absolute rule for every historical battle.

Ten surahs, ten nights, and ten units of time

Bi-ʿashri suwar in Hūd 11:13 means ten surahs.

ʿAshran in Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:27 concerns completing a period of service or work understood from the preceding clause.

In Ṭā Hā 20:103, ʿashran occurs in the speech of people who consider their stay very short. Their count must not be made into a certain external measurement without considering who is speaking.

Al-Fajr 89:2 also states:

وَلَيَالٍ عَشْرٍ

wa-layālin ʿashrin

English translation

By ten nights.

ʿAshr denotes ten nights.

The verse does not name them. Identifying the first or last ten nights of a particular month requires independent evidence, not merely the number’s lexical meaning.

Together these uses show that ten is a real, countable quantity before any symbolic interpretation. Al-Anʿām 6:160 also uses fa-lahu ʿashru amthālihā for a tenfold reward for a good deed. That ratio comes from the number combined with amthāl, not an independent meaning of ten itself.

6. Miʿshār: one tenth of an amount

Sabaʾ 34:45

Verse text

وَكَذَّبَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِهِمْ وَمَا بَلَغُوا مِعْشَارَ مَا آتَيْنَاهُمْ فَكَذَّبُوا رُسُلِي فَكَيْفَ كَانَ نَكِيرِ

wa-kadhdhaba alladhīna min qablihim wa-mā balaghū miʿshāra mā ātaynāhum fa-kadhdhabū rusulī fa-kayfa kāna nakīri

English translation

Those before them also denied; these people have not attained a tenth of what We gave those earlier peoples. They denied My messengers: how, then, was My rejection and requital!

Usage analysis

Miʿshār belongs to the root’s numerical derivatives.

In its familiar lexical meaning and Al-Ṭabarī’s interpretation here, it denotes one tenth. Some lexical accounts record smaller proportions, but one tenth is the common reading and the basis of this translation.

Miʿshāra mā ātaynāhum expresses a comparative ratio.

The Qur’an compares the present recipients of warning with earlier peoples, saying they have not attained a tenth of what those predecessors received.

The context concerns denying divine messengers and its consequences.

Miʿshār marks the smallness of one amount against a greater one.

It does not establish that the verse concerns a tenth as tax, a compulsory economic share, or government revenue.

It names a ratio, rather than commanding that it be taken from people’s property.

A comparative tenth can emphasize a sharp difference, but its numerical meaning should not become a wholly indefinite proportion without reason.

“One tenth” is therefore appropriate; the expression’s comparative function belongs in a separate explanation.

Al-Ṭabarī on Sabaʾ 34:45: one tenth

7. ʿĀshirūhunna: association and life with recognized good conduct

Al-Nisāʾ 4:19

Verse text

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا يَحِلُّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَرِثُوا النِّسَاءَ كَرْهًا وَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ لِتَذْهَبُوا بِبَعْضِ مَا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ إِلَّا أَنْ يَأْتِينَ بِفَاحِشَةٍ مُبَيِّنَةٍ وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ فَإِنْ كَرِهْتُمُوهُنَّ فَعَسَىٰ أَنْ تَكْرَهُوا شَيْئًا وَيَجْعَلَ اللَّهُ فِيهِ خَيْرًا كَثِيرًا

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā yaḥillu lakum an tarithū al-nisāʾa karhan wa-lā taʿḍulūhunna li-tadhhabū bi-baʿḍi mā ātaytumūhunna illā an yaʾtīna bi-fāḥishatin mubayyinatin wa-ʿāshirūhunna bi-al-maʿrūfi fa-in karihtumūhunna fa-ʿasā an takrahū shayʾan wa-yajʿala Allāhu fīhi khayran kathīran

English translation

You who believe, it is not lawful for you to inherit women against their will. Do not constrain them to take back part of what you have given them, unless they commit manifest indecency. Associate with them in a recognized good manner. If you dislike them, you may dislike something in which The God places much good.

The verb’s construction

ʿĀshirūhunna is an imperative of Form III, mufāʿalah.

Its subject is the plural addressees; hunna, “them,” is the object pronoun.

The verb concerns associating, maintaining a relationship, and living with another.

Here the relationship is marital and familial.

The lexical meaning of ʿāshara is not limited to sexual intercourse.

Association includes life in relation to another and conduct within that relationship. Physical intimacy may form part of marriage, but does not exhaust association’s meaning.

Translating ʿāshirūhunna only as sexual relations would remove much of the word’s range.

Conversely, limiting it to inward affection or pleasant feeling would neglect actual conduct and shared life.

Association is a practical relationship expressed in behavior, encounters, and daily living.

The role of bi-al-maʿrūf

The command is not merely ʿāshirūhunna, but wa-ʿāshirūhunna bi-al-maʿrūf.

Maʿrūf belongs to ʿ R F and specifies the quality of association.

The verb’s meaning and the qualification bi-al-maʿrūf should therefore be examined separately.

Association may be good or bad; this verse commands association according to recognized good.

That qualification prevents every widespread social practice from automatically counting as good simply because it is common.

For example, the prevalence of humiliation, financial pressure, deprivation of a woman’s rights, or forcing her to remain in a harmful relationship does not itself establish maʿrūf.

The preceding prohibitions in the verse support this conclusion.

Association, coercion, and pressure

The opening prohibits inheriting women against their will.

It then forbids constraining them to reclaim part of what they have received.

The command for association according to maʿrūf follows those prohibitions.

The sequence connects good family conduct with women’s rights and agency.

One cannot wrong a woman through force, pressure, or financial deprivation while considering continued residence in one house sufficient proof of good association.

Association’s lexical meaning does not, by itself, determine every marital right.

Dower, divorce, reconciliation, family arbitration, and mutual rights require study of the relevant verses.

Forqan’s separate study, “Marriage in the Qur’an,” also examines these matters.

Marriage in the Qur’an

Fa-in karihtumūhunna: conduct amid dislike

Immediately after commanding good association, the verse raises the possibility of disliking a spouse.

Good association is therefore not confined to times when a person feels satisfied with the relationship.

Disagreement, hurt, or dissatisfaction does not itself authorize unjust conduct.

Fa-ʿasā an takrahū shayʾan wa-yajʿala Allāhu fīhi khayran kathīran does not mean every harmful relationship must continue under all circumstances.

Other verses also provide avenues for repair, arbitration, and separation.

This phrase should not be used to force someone to endure oppression or humiliation.

Disliking a person does not grant a right to mistreat them.

Does Form III prove reciprocal conduct?

Arabic Form III can express a relationship or participation between parties.

The pattern alone does not prove that both parties perform exactly the same act in every occurrence.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:19 addresses a command to the addressees, with women as the verb’s pronominal object.

The construction explicitly states the addressees’ responsibility in their conduct toward women.

Association presupposes a relationship between people, but the bounds of mutual rights and duties require the full set of family verses, not merely the fāʿala pattern.

Lexical conclusion: ʿĀshirūhunna means associate and live with them. Bi-al-maʿrūf specifies how. The command should not be reduced to sexual intercourse, inward affection, or one-sided obedience.

8. ʿAshīr: a companion who can be bad

Al-Ḥajj 22:13

Verse text

يَدْعُو لَمَنْ ضَرُّهُ أَقْرَبُ مِنْ نَفْعِهِ لَبِئْسَ الْمَوْلَىٰ وَلَبِئْسَ الْعَشِيرُ

yadʿū la-man ḍarruhu aqrabu min nafʿihi la-biʾsa al-mawlā wa-la-biʾsa al-ʿashīru

English translation

He calls upon one whose harm is nearer than his benefit. What an evil patron, and what an evil companion!

Usage analysis

Al-ʿashīr means a companion or associate.

It belongs to association, not the numerical field of ten.

Arabic can use ʿashīr for someone in a close or continuing relationship; some uses also designate a spouse.

Here someone invokes other than God and turns toward something whose harm is nearer than its benefit.

“Husband” is therefore inappropriate in this verse.

The word accompanies al-mawlā and describes harmful companionship or reliance.

ʿAshīr is not inherently praise.

A relationship that can support help and good shared life can also become harmful companionship.

“Companion” or “associate” is appropriate; la-biʾsa supplies the condemnation.

9. ʿAshīrah: kinship and social bonds

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:214: warning the nearest kindred

Verse text

وَأَنْذِرْ عَشِيرَتَكَ الْأَقْرَبِينَ

wa-andhir ʿashīrataka al-aqrabīna

English translation

Warn your nearest kindred.

Usage analysis

ʿAshīrataka denotes the Prophet’s close kinship and social group.

Al-aqrabīn emphasizes their closeness.

The clan is not a company of ten. It is the addressee’s nearest circle through family and social bonds.

The word does not establish that every member shares the same faith, character, or responsibility.

Nor does warning one’s kindred confer authority for religious coercion upon a family or clan leader.

The issue is conveying a warning, not owning relatives’ beliefs.

Al-Tawbah 9:24: family affection and the place of loyalty

Verse text

قُلْ إِنْ كَانَ آبَاؤُكُمْ وَأَبْنَاؤُكُمْ وَإِخْوَانُكُمْ وَأَزْوَاجُكُمْ وَعَشِيرَتُكُمْ وَأَمْوَالٌ اقْتَرَفْتُمُوهَا وَتِجَارَةٌ تَخْشَوْنَ كَسَادَهَا وَمَسَاكِنُ تَرْضَوْنَهَا أَحَبَّ إِلَيْكُمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ وَجِهَادٍ فِي سَبِيلِهِ فَتَرَبَّصُوا حَتَّىٰ يَأْتِيَ اللَّهُ بِأَمْرِهِ وَاللَّهُ لَا يَهْدِي الْقَوْمَ الْفَاسِقِينَ

qul in kāna ābāʾukum wa-abnāʾukum wa-ikhwānukum wa-azwājukum wa-ʿashīratukum wa-amwālun iqtaraftumūhā wa-tijāratun takhshawna kasādahā wa-masākinu tarḍawnahā aḥabba ilaykum mina llāhi wa-rasūlihi wa-jihādin fī sabīlihi fa-tarabbaṣū ḥattā yaʾtiya llāhu bi-amrihi wa-llāhu lā yahdī al-qawma al-fāsiqīna

English translation

Say, “If your fathers, children, brothers, spouses, kindred, property you have acquired, trade whose decline you fear, and dwellings you like are dearer to you than The God, His Messenger, and striving in His way, then wait until The God brings His command. The God does not guide the defiantly disobedient people.”

Usage analysis

ʿAshīratukum appears with fathers, children, brothers, and spouses.

This pairing shows a wider circle than an individual close relationship: kindred or the social clan.

The list does not establish that loving family is inherently wrong.

The issue is the place of affection and loyalty relative to God’s will.

Someone can love family while remaining loyal to truth and justice. A different issue arises when family or group demands so dominate decisions that the person knowingly violates truth.

Context supplies this distinction; ʿashīrah itself does not mean false allegiance or associating partners with God.

Al-Mujādilah 58:22: kinship and deliberate opposition

ʿAshīratahum again accompanies fathers, children, and brothers in Al-Mujādilah 58:22.

The issue there is believers’ relationship with those who oppose God and His Messenger.

Mere difference of thought or family membership is not the issue; ḥādda Allāha wa-rasūlahu requires its own examination.

ʿAshīrah alone cannot command severing every family relationship with people of differing beliefs.

Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:8 also discusses good and just treatment of those who have not fought or expelled others.

Family connection and judgment of conduct remain distinct matters.

Lexical conclusion: ʿAshīrah concerns kindred and social bonds. The bond itself guarantees neither virtue nor deviation; actions and the place of loyalty establish their value.

10. Maʿshar: an assembly sharing a characteristic

Maʿshar occurs three times, each in an address to jinn or to jinn and humans.

Al-Anʿām 6:130

Verse text

يَا مَعْشَرَ الْجِنِّ وَالْإِنْسِ أَلَمْ يَأْتِكُمْ رُسُلٌ مِنْكُمْ يَقُصُّونَ عَلَيْكُمْ آيَاتِي وَيُنْذِرُونَكُمْ لِقَاءَ يَوْمِكُمْ هَٰذَا قَالُوا شَهِدْنَا عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِنَا وَغَرَّتْهُمُ الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا وَشَهِدُوا عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِهِمْ أَنَّهُمْ كَانُوا كَافِرِينَ

yā maʿshara al-jinni wa-al-insi a-lam yaʾtikum rusulun minkum yaquṣṣūna ʿalaykum āyātī wa-yundhirūnakum liqāʾa yawmikum hādhā qālū shahidnā ʿalā anfusinā wa-gharrathumu al-ḥayātu al-dunyā wa-shahidū ʿalā anfusihim annahum kānū kāfirīna

English translation

Assembly of jinn and humans, did messengers from among you not come to you, recounting My signs and warning you of the meeting of this day? They say, “We testify against ourselves.” Worldly life had deceived them, and they testify against themselves that they were disbelievers.

Usage analysis

Maʿshara is a vocative here and means an assembly.

It neither gives the number ten nor restricts the group to a blood family.

Jinn and humans are addressed as groups.

Maʿshar can designate people brought together in an address because of a shared kind or characteristic.

This alone does not make jinn and humans identical in nature, abilities, or obligations; those questions require their relevant verses.

Al-Anʿām 6:128 and Al-Raḥmān 55:33

Yā maʿshara al-jinn in Al-Anʿām 6:128 addresses the jinn; the verse continues with relations between jinn and humans.

Al-Raḥmān 55:33 also addresses yā maʿshara al-jinn wa-al-ins and discusses the ability to pass beyond the regions of the heavens and earth.

All three occurrences name a group being addressed.

The word does not establish exactly ten members or universal kinship among them.

Maʿshar and ʿashīrah

Qur’anic ʿashīrah chiefly concerns kinship and the social clan.

Maʿshar has a broader range and can include a group sharing a characteristic without a common family or descent.

The Qur’an uses ʿashīrah for relatives and close kin, and maʿshar in addresses to jinn and humans.

This is not an absolute rule for every Arabic use. Early maʿshar can designate diverse human groups, including those with family bonds.

11. ʿIshār: pregnant she-camels in an image of great upheaval

Al-Takwīr 81:4

Verse text

وَإِذَا الْعِشَارُ عُطِّلَتْ

wa-idhā al-ʿishāru ʿuṭṭilat

English translation

When she-camels approaching delivery are left untended.

Usage analysis

Al-ʿishār is the plural of ʿusharāʾ.

Arabic applies this name to she-camels about ten months into pregnancy.

The numerical connection is genuine and documented.

ʿIshār does not mean ten camels. It denotes camels at a particular stage of pregnancy without specifying their number.

ʿUṭṭilat belongs to ʿ Ṭ L and concerns leaving something unattended, unused, or neglected.

The verse thus combines two distinct concepts: she-camels approaching delivery, and their abandonment.

Al-Takwīr describes major events overturning the familiar order of the world and human life.

Abandoning valuable camels can portray such upheaval that even important property no longer receives its owners’ usual attention.

That conclusion depends on context and camels’ economic place in people’s lives.

ʿIshār itself does not mean wealth, trade, or property becoming worthless.

Nor should it become “clouds” or something wholly different without evidence. The familiar, strongly supported Arabic meaning is she-camels approaching delivery.

Lisān al-ʿArab and Al-Ṣiḥāḥ: ʿusharāʾ, ʿishār, and continued use after delivery

12. Morphology and related roots

ʿAshr and ʿasharah

The form of ten relates to the counted noun’s grammatical gender.

For a masculine noun, for example, Arabic uses ʿasharatu rijāl; for a feminine noun, ʿashru niswah.

Compound numbers such as eleven and twelve have a different construction, with the second component following its own gender agreement.

These differences do not create distinct numerical meanings. Each number retains its intended quantity.

Twenty, ʿishrūn, also has its own structure and case marking; it is not simply an ordinary plural of ʿasharah.

ʿAsharah and ʿishrah

The vowels distinguish the forms.

ʿAsharah means ten; ʿishrah concerns association and companionship.

Similar unvocalized spelling must not merge the meanings.

Association in Al-Nisāʾ 4:19 should therefore not be explained by counting tens.

ʿUshr and miʿshār

ʿUshr is one tenth; Qur’anic miʿshār also expresses that ratio.

These nouns do not automatically establish a financial ruling.

Taking a tenth of property is known in historical Arabic usage, but its legal basis, addressees, and legitimacy require independent textual evidence.

Miʿshār in Sabaʾ 34:45 is a comparative ratio, not a tax directive.

ʿ SH R compared with ʾ L F (ulfah)

Study of ʾ L F

ʾ L F concerns familiarity, attachment, and becoming accustomed to another.

Association may involve affection, but not every association is filled with love and intimacy.

People may associate amid disagreement, distrust, or hostility.

Association concerns interaction and relationship; ulfah concerns familiarity and closeness of hearts.

ʿ SH R compared with Ṣ Ḥ B (ṣuḥbah)

Study of Ṣ Ḥ B

Qur’anic ṣuḥbah and ṣāḥib concern accompanying or relating to another.

ʿAshīr can likewise mean companion, with a notable connection to continuing association.

These fields are close but not completely synonymous.

ʿAshīr does not necessarily identify an intimate friend or spouse.

ʿ SH R compared with Q R B (qarābah)

Study of Q R B

Qurb means nearness, which can be physical, social, or figurative.

ʿAshīrah concerns kindred and social bonds.

Its members can live far apart geographically while retaining kinship.

Nor is everyone nearby a member of the kindred.

ʿ SH R compared with R H Ṭ, Q W M, and ʾ M M

Study of R H Ṭ; Study of Q W M; Study of ʾ M M

Rahṭ, qawm, and ummah each have their own Qur’anic fields of grouping and social connection.

Maʿshar also names a group but does not alone specify its bond, membership count, or political structure.

These terms should not replace one another indiscriminately across verses.

ʿ SH R compared with ʿ SH W and ʿ SH Y

Study of ʿ SH W

ʿ SH W and ʿ SH Y differ from ʿ SH R.

ʿIshāʾ, ʿashiyy, and related words for evening or dim-light vision belong to those other families.

Sharing the first two consonants does not place ʿishāʾ, evening or night time, under ʿ SH R.

Nor does ʿishār in Al-Takwīr 81:4 share the root of ʿishāʾ, “evening.”

13. Translation proposals for Forqan

Numerical uses should retain their direct equivalents:

ʿAshr and ʿasharah mean ten as appropriate; ʿishrūn is twenty; miʿshār is one tenth; aḥada ʿashara, ithnā ʿashara, and tisʿata ʿashara mean eleven, twelve, and nineteen respectively.

Tilka ʿasharatun kāmilah in Al-Baqarah 2:196 is close to “these are ten complete days.”

Arbaʿata ashhurin wa-ʿashran in Al-Baqarah 2:234 can be “four months and ten days.”

Miʿshār in Sabaʾ 34:45 is one tenth, not a tax or a legally prescribed share of assets.

The main proposal for wa-ʿāshirūhunna bi-al-maʿrūf is “associate with them in a recognized good manner.” “Live with them kindly” is also defensible, although “live” alone can understate conduct and interaction.

“Companion” or “associate” suits al-ʿashīr in Al-Ḥajj 22:13.

“Kindred,” “clan,” or “kinship group” suits ʿashīrah according to context.

“Assembly” or “group” better suits maʿshar; it should not be translated as “ten people.”

“She-camels approaching delivery” conveys al-ʿishār in Al-Takwīr 81:4 naturally. A reflection can explain the connection with about ten months of pregnancy if needed.

No translation should combine every meaning of the root. Ten, a tenth, association, kindred, an assembly, and pregnant camels each have their own meaning within their construction and context.

14. Final synthesis

ʿ SH R has two principal Qur’anic fields: counting through ten and association and bonds between people.

The first counts days, nights, people, groups, surahs, and numerical ratios through ten and its compounds. Miʿshār is a tenth; ʿishār names she-camels approaching delivery.

Ten is a definite quantity before any symbolic reading. It does not inherently mean moral perfection, nor does its presence in one religious ruling make it a universal law for every subject.

In the second field, ʿāshirūhunna concerns association and life with women in a family context. ʿAshīr is a companion, ʿashīrah kindred, and maʿshar a group sharing a characteristic or bond.

Despite their shared root, these words differ in scope: association concerns conduct and relationship, kindred concerns family and social bonds, and an assembly can extend beyond kinship.

The root is not inherently morally positive or negative. Association can be good or bad. Kinship can support help and loyalty to truth or lead astray when it displaces justice. Companionship can benefit or harm.

Bi-al-maʿrūf therefore matters in Al-Nisāʾ 4:19. The Qur’an does not merely require a relationship’s existence; it connects its manner with recognized good.

Final definition: One branch of ʿ SH R concerns ten and its derived ratios and forms; another concerns association, companionship, and belonging to a group. Without independent historical evidence, their connection should not be reduced to one image.

Each Qur’anic form’s meaning follows its morphology, subject, and context.

Thus ʿasharah is ten, miʿshār a tenth, ʿishār she-camels approaching delivery, ʿāshirūhunna a command to associate, ʿashīr a companion, ʿashīrah kindred, and maʿshar an assembly.

Preserving these distinctions guards against unsupported etymology and prevents numerical rulings, family rights, and social bonds from collapsing into one artificial meaning.
