Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root M T AYN (M T ʿ)

Time-bound benefit, usable provision, temporary enjoyment, and tools for the road; matāʿ is equipment for the journey, not the place of final settlement.

Important Forms from This Root

Root
Used in the Qur’an: Yes

Time-bound benefit, making use of something on the way, and enjoyment that comes to an end.

Scope of This Entry

The root م ت ع (m t ʿ) originally points to benefiting, time-bound use, enjoying something within a defined limit, and having a means by which a need is met on the road. مَتَاع (matāʿ) is something a human being uses, but it is not meant to be his final destination. From here, its important Qur’anic meaning becomes clear: مَتَاع (matāʿ) is equipment for the road, not the place of final settlement.

Important Forms from This Root

Important forms of this root in the Qur’an include مَتَاع (matāʿ), مَتَّعَ (mattaʿa), تَمَتُّع (tamattuʿ), and اسْتَمْتَعَ (istamtaʿa). مَتَاع (matāʿ) is used both for the thing itself, such as equipment, load, and tools, and for the benefit a person takes from life or from a blessing. مَتَّعَ (mattaʿa) in Form II points to letting someone benefit and making someone enjoy or possess a benefit. تَمَتُّع (tamattuʿ) means benefiting and making use within a time-bound limit. اسْتَمْتَعَ (istamtaʿa) points to taking benefit from a share, relation, opportunity, or something placed within reach.

Central Meaning

The linguistic core of م ت ع (m t ʿ) is extended but finite enjoyment or benefit. Something is stretched within a person’s reach for a time, gives room for use, meets a need, but both the thing itself and the time of benefiting from it come to an end. From this angle, مَتَاع (matāʿ) is not compatible with eternity or absolute ownership. مَتَاع (matāʿ) is something whose value lies in using it rightly, not in attaching the heart to it as a final end.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root can be seen in provision for a journey, a load, travel equipment, a rope stretched and used for work, or the light of a day that extends but eventually ends. A traveler uses his provision and tools in order to walk the road. If he treats the provision as the destination, he has lost the road. The world is repeatedly called مَتَاع (matāʿ) in the Qur’an in this same way: usable, beautiful, real, but time-bound.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic usage, م ت ع (m t ʿ) is connected with benefit, enjoyment, extension, provision, means, and the possibility of use. مَتَاع (matāʿ) can be equipment carried along, and it can also be a benefit taken from life or from a blessing. In the final version, if a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or old Arabic usage is found, it can be added.

The Provision of Earth after Descent

One of the first important uses of this root appears in the story of Adam’s descent:

وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

(wa-lakum fī al-arḍi mustaqarrun wa-matāʿun ilā ḥīn)

And for you there will be on the earth a place of settlement and provision until a time. Al-Baqarah 2:36

In this ayah, مَتَاع (matāʿ) comes together with مُسْتَقَرّ (mustaqarr) and إِلَىٰ حِينٍ (ilā ḥīn), “until a time.” Earth is the human being’s temporary place of settlement, and its provision is also until an appointed time. This combination is very important: earth is both the place of life and the place of benefiting, but neither is endless.

The same structure is repeated in Al-A’raf:

وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

(wa-lakum fī al-arḍi mustaqarrun wa-matāʿun ilā ḥīn)

And for you there will be on the earth a place of settlement and provision until a time. Al-A’raf 7:24

This repetition shows that the earth’s مَتَاع (matāʿ) is part of the human being’s earthly stage. So مَتَاع (matāʿ) is not bad; The God placed it for human life. The problem begins when a human being seats مَتَاع (matāʿ) in the place of the Hereafter, the place of truth, or the place of the final destination.

The Provision of Worldly Life before the Hereafter

The second field is the provision of worldly life in contrast with the Hereafter:

زُيِّنَ لِلنَّاسِ حُبُّ الشَّهَوَاتِ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ وَالْبَنِينَ وَالْقَنَاطِيرِ الْمُقَنطَرَةِ مِنَ الذَّهَبِ وَالْفِضَّةِ وَالْخَيْلِ الْمُسَوَّمَةِ وَالْأَنْعَامِ وَالْحَرْثِ ۗ ذَٰلِكَ مَتَاعُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا ۖ وَاللَّهُ عِندَهُ حُسْنُ الْمَآبِ

(zuyyina li-al-nāsi ḥubbu al-shahawāti mina al-nisāʾi wa-al-banīna wa-al-qanāṭīri al-muqanṭarati mina al-dhahabi wa-al-fiḍḍati wa-al-khayli al-musawwamati wa-al-anʿāmi wa-al-ḥarth; dhālika matāʿu al-ḥayāti al-dunyā wa-Allāhu ʿindahu ḥusnu al-maʾāb)

Love of desires has been adorned for people: women, children, heaped-up treasures of gold and silver, marked horses, cattle, and fields. That is the provision of worldly life; and with The God is the best return. Al-Imran 3:14

In this ayah, the provision of worldly life includes real, attractive, and natural things of life. The Qur’an does not negate them, but clarifies their place: they are مَتَاع (matāʿ), not حُسْنُ الْمَآب (ḥusn al-maʾāb), the good return. They are for benefiting from, not for the human heart to end in them.

Another ayah states this boundary more sharply:

وَمَا الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا إِلَّا مَتَاعُ الْغُرُورِ

(wa-mā al-ḥayātu al-dunyā illā matāʿu al-ghurūr)

And worldly life is nothing but deceptive provision. Al-Imran 3:185

مَتَاعُ الْغُرُور (matāʿ al-ghurūr) means a provision that causes the human being to miscalculate. The deception is not that the world has no value; the deception is that something finite is imagined to be permanent and absolute. The human being thinks the extension of مَتَاع (matāʿ) means its permanence, while its extension is only a period of use.

The same expression also appears in Surah Al-Hadid:

وَمَا الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا إِلَّا مَتَاعُ الْغُرُورِ

(wa-mā al-ḥayātu al-dunyā illā matāʿu al-ghurūr)

And worldly life is nothing but deceptive provision. Al-Hadid 57:20

In this context, worldly life is described through play, diversion, adornment, mutual boasting, and rivalry in increase. When the provision of the world is not understood under the light of truth, it keeps the human being inside the shell of consumption and competition.

The Smallness of Worldly Provision

The third field is the smallness of worldly provision:

قُلْ مَتَاعُ الدُّنْيَا قَلِيلٌ وَالْآخِرَةُ خَيْرٌ لِّمَنِ اتَّقَىٰ

(qul matāʿu al-dunyā qalīlun wa-al-ākhiratu khayrun li-mani ittaqā)

Say: the provision of the world is little, and the Hereafter is better for whoever practices taqwā. An-Nisa 4:77

Its smallness here is not only a matter of quantity; it is a matter of relation. Even if the world’s provision looks abundant, before the Hereafter it is short, limited, and unstable.

In At-Tawbah, it also says:

فَمَا مَتَاعُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا فِي الْآخِرَةِ إِلَّا قَلِيلٌ

(fa-mā matāʿu al-ḥayāti al-dunyā fī al-ākhirati illā qalīl)

So the provision of worldly life, compared with the Hereafter, is nothing but little. At-Tawbah 9:38

So م ت ع (m t ʿ) teaches the human being to measure the value of every benefit by the destination. If the Hereafter is the measure, the world becomes a tool; if the world becomes the measure, provision turns into deception.

Good Provision

The fourth field is good provision:

وَأَنِ اسْتَغْفِرُوا رَبَّكُمْ ثُمَّ تُوبُوا إِلَيْهِ يُمَتِّعْكُم مَّتَاعًا حَسَنًا إِلَىٰ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى

(wa-ani istaghfirū rabbakum thumma tūbū ilayhi yumattiʿkum matāʿan ḥasanan ilā ajalin musamman)

And that you seek forgiveness from your Lord, then turn back to Him; He will grant you good provision until a named term. Hud 11:3

This ayah shows that مَتَاع (matāʿ) is not always negative. Provision can be good: a healthy, pure, balanced benefit in the right path. But even good provision is إِلَىٰ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى (ilā ajalin musamman), until a named term. The goodness of provision does not remove its time-bound nature.

Granting Enjoyment and Giving Respite

The fifth field is granting enjoyment and giving respite:

فَأُمَتِّعُهُ قَلِيلًا ثُمَّ أَضْطَرُّهُ إِلَىٰ عَذَابِ النَّارِ

(fa-umattiʿuhu qalīlan thumma aḍṭarruhu ilā ʿadhābi al-nār)

So I will grant him a little enjoyment, then I will drive him toward the punishment of the Fire. Al-Baqarah 2:126

In this ayah, worldly enjoyment is possible even for the one who rejects truth, but this enjoyment is not proof of The God’s final approval. مَتَاع (matāʿ) may be respite, not lasting honor.

In Surah Ash-Shu’ara, it also says:

أَفَرَأَيْتَ إِن مَّتَّعْنَاهُمْ سِنِينَ ثُمَّ جَاءَهُم مَّا كَانُوا يُوعَدُونَ مَا أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُم مَّا كَانُوا يُمَتَّعُونَ

(a-fa-raʾayta in mattaʿnāhum sinīn thumma jāʾahum mā kānū yūʿadūn mā aghnā ʿanhum mā kānū yumattaʿūn)

Have you seen: if We grant them enjoyment for years, then what they were promised comes to them, what they used to enjoy will not avail them. Ash-Shu’ara 26:205-207

Here, even long years are still مَتَاع (matāʿ). At the moment of truth, the length of enjoyment cannot replace truth and salvation.

Provision in Divorce and Financial Support

The sixth field is مَتَاع (matāʿ) in divorce and financial support:

وَمَتِّعُوهُنَّ عَلَى الْمُوسِعِ قَدَرُهُ وَعَلَى الْمُقْتِرِ قَدَرُهُ مَتَاعًا بِالْمَعْرُوفِ

(wa-mattiʿūhunna ʿalā al-mūsiʿi qadaruhu wa-ʿalā al-muqtiri qadaruhu matāʿan bi-al-maʿrūf)

And provide them with a benefit: upon the one of means according to his capacity, and upon the one of limited means according to his capacity; a provision according to what is recognized as right. Al-Baqarah 2:236

In this ayah, مَتَاع (matāʿ) is close to a benefit or supportive provision after the breaking of marriage. This provision is not the dowry, nor is it humiliating charity; it is a worthy supply for moving from one situation into another. بِالْمَعْرُوف (bi-al-maʿrūf) means it must accord with fairness, healthy custom, financial capacity, and the woman’s dignity.

In another ayah, it says:

وَلِلْمُطَلَّقَاتِ مَتَاعٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ حَقًّا عَلَى الْمُتَّقِينَ

(wa-li-al-muṭallaqāti matāʿun bi-al-maʿrūfi ḥaqqan ʿalā al-muttaqīn)

And for divorced women there is a provision according to what is recognized as right: a right upon those who practice taqwā. Al-Baqarah 2:241

Here مَتَاع (matāʿ) is connected with حَقّ (ḥaqq), right. So this provision is not only an optional kindness; it is an ethical and social responsibility. At the moment of family rupture, the Qur’an does not leave the woman without provision or support.

Matāʿ as Tools and Equipment

The seventh field is مَتَاع (matāʿ) as tools and equipment:

وَدَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لَوْ تَغْفُلُونَ عَنْ أَسْلِحَتِكُمْ وَأَمْتِعَتِكُمْ

(wadda alladhīna kafarū law taghfulūna ʿan asliḥatikum wa-amtiʿatikum)

Those who rejected truth wish that you would become heedless of your weapons and your equipment. An-Nisa 4:102

Here أَمْتِعَة (amtiʿah) refers to equipment, supplies, loads, and tools carried along. This use is close to the material image of the root: things a human being carries for movement, defense, travel, or life.

In the story of Yusuf, it also says:

وَلَمَّا فَتَحُوا مَتَاعَهُمْ وَجَدُوا بِضَاعَتَهُمْ رُدَّتْ إِلَيْهِمْ

(wa-lammā fataḥū matāʿahum wajadū biḍāʿatahum ruddat ilayhim)

And when they opened their loads, they found their goods returned to them. Yusuf 12:65

مَتَاع (matāʿ) here is travel and trade load. This use shows that مَتَاع (matāʿ) does not always carry an ethical or Hereafter-facing meaning; sometimes it is simply a load, tool, container, goods, and usable equipment.

Istimtāʿ

The eighth field is اسْتِمْتَاع (istimtāʿ):

رَبَّنَا اسْتَمْتَعَ بَعْضُنَا بِبَعْضٍ وَبَلَغْنَا أَجَلَنَا الَّذِي أَجَّلْتَ لَنَا

(rabbanā istamtaʿa baʿḍunā bi-baʿḍin wa-balaghna ajalanā alladhī ajjalta lanā)

Our Lord, some of us benefited from others, and we reached the term that You appointed for us. Al-An’am 6:128

اسْتِمْتَاع (istimtāʿ) in this ayah is mutual benefit, but in a warning context connected with the relation between human beings and jinn. That means benefiting, if it is not under the criterion of truth, can become a polluted, deceptive, or harmful relation.

In Surah At-Tawbah, it also says:

فَاسْتَمْتَعُوا بِخَلَاقِهِمْ فَاسْتَمْتَعْتُم بِخَلَاقِكُمْ

(fa-istamtaʿū bi-khalāqihim fa-istamtaʿtum bi-khalāqikum)

They benefited from their share, so you too benefited from your share. At-Tawbah 9:69

خَلَاق (khalāq) means a share or portion. اسْتِمْتَاع (istimtāʿ) here is use of a worldly share, but it appears in a warning context about repeating the path of earlier peoples. The human being consumes his share, but he must see where this consumption is taking him.

Istimtāʿ in the Marital Relation

The ninth field is اسْتِمْتَاع (istimtāʿ) in the marital relation:

فَمَا اسْتَمْتَعْتُم بِهِ مِنْهُنَّ فَآتُوهُنَّ أُجُورَهُنَّ فَرِيضَةً

(fa-mā istamtaʿtum bihi minhunna fa-ātūhunna ujūrahunna farīḍah)

So whatever benefit you have taken through them, give them their financial due as an obligation. An-Nisa 4:24

This ayah has become the site of many historical legal debates, but in this article we must remain root-centered. Linguistically, اسْتِمْتَاع (istimtāʿ) points to real benefiting within the relation, and the ayah connects it with paying the woman’s right. So م ت ع (m t ʿ) here is not benefit without responsibility; it is benefit that makes financial right and commitment necessary.

Tamattuʿ in Hajj

The tenth field is تَمَتُّع (tamattuʿ) in Hajj:

فَمَن تَمَتَّعَ بِالْعُمْرَةِ إِلَى الْحَجِّ فَمَا اسْتَيْسَرَ مِنَ الْهَدْيِ

(fa-man tamattaʿa bi-al-ʿumrati ilā al-ḥajji fa-mā istaysara mina al-hady)

So whoever benefits from the ʿumrah until the Hajj, then whatever offering is easy. Al-Baqarah 2:196

In this ayah, تَمَتُّع (tamattuʿ) points to benefiting from a practical and temporal opening between ʿumrah and Hajj. This benefit is also limited and ritual; the human being uses an opportunity within the frame of worship.

Distinguishing the Uses

The uses of م ت ع (m t ʿ) must be distinguished. The provision of the world means the passing and time-bound benefit of life. Good provision means healthy and good benefit until a specified term. Deceptive provision means a benefit that deceives the human being if it is not measured correctly. Divorce provision means worthy support or supply for transition. أَمْتِعَة (amtiʿah) means tools and equipment for use. اسْتِمْتَاع (istimtāʿ) means benefiting from something or someone in a relation, which depending on context may be legitimate, warning-laden, or responsibility-bearing. تَمَتُّع (tamattuʿ) in Hajj is benefiting from the opening between ʿumrah and Hajj.

Structural Note

Structurally, time markers in this root are very important: إِلَىٰ حِينٍ (ilā ḥīn), قَلِيلًا (qalīlan), إِلَىٰ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى (ilā ajalin musamman), سِنِينَ (sinīn), and حَتَّىٰ حِينٍ (ḥattā ḥīn). These markers show that مَتَاع (matāʿ) in the Qur’an is usually linked to limited time. Even when the provision is good, it is still not absolute or eternal.

Difference from Nearby Roots

م ت ع (m t ʿ) differs from several nearby roots. R Z Q points to provision being delivered and the share of life; مَتَاع (matāʿ) points to benefiting from something placed within one’s reach. N AYN M (N ʿ M) is a pleasant gift or blessing; مَتَاع (matāʿ) is the use and enjoyment of that gift within limited time. Z Y N relates to adornment and outward beauty; مَتَاع (matāʿ) may have adornment, but its core is benefit and use. GH N M relates to something gained through war or a specific acquisition; مَتَاع (matāʿ) is broader and includes every tool or time-bound benefit. M W L is wealth or property; مَتَاع (matāʿ) is the usable quality of that property on the path of life. N F AYN (N F ʿ) points to benefit or profit; مَتَاع (matāʿ) may bring benefit, but its temporariness and consumption are more prominent.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

For translation in Forqan, مَتَاع (matāʿ) should not always be translated as “pleasure.” Depending on context, one may use “benefit,” “enjoyment,” “provision,” “supplies,” “equipment,” “tools,” “goods,” “temporary benefit,” or “supportive provision.” مَتَاعُ الدُّنْيَا (matāʿ al-dunyā) is better rendered as “the provision of worldly life” or “the passing benefit of the world.” مَتَاعُ الْغُرُور (matāʿ al-ghurūr) can be rendered as “deceptive provision.” مَتَاعًا حَسَنًا (matāʿan ḥasanan) means “good provision” or “good enjoyment.” مَتَاعًا بِالْمَعْرُوف (matāʿan bi-al-maʿrūf) in the divorce ayat is better rendered as “a worthy provision,” “a provision according to what is recognized as right,” or “worthy support.” أَمْتِعَة (amtiʿah) in the context of war and travel is “equipment” or “loads and tools.”

Linguistic Summary

Linguistically, the root م ت ع (m t ʿ) points to benefiting, use, time-bound enjoyment, and having a tool or possibility of consumption for a period. Its material image is the provision and equipment of a traveler: something used on the road, but not the final destination. In the Qur’an, this root extends from the provision of earth after descent to the provision of worldly life; from good provision to deceptive provision; from financial support in divorce to equipment for travel and war; and from اسْتِمْتَاع (istimtāʿ) to تَمَتُّع (tamattuʿ) in Hajj. Its difference from رِزْق (rizq) is that رِزْق (rizq) is the provision itself and its being delivered, while مَتَاع (matāʿ) is time-bound benefiting from what has been placed within reach.
