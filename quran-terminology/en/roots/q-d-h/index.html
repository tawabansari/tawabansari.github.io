The Qur’anic Root Q D Ḥ

Striking sparks, producing fire through contact, and leaving an effect

Qur’anic forms

قَدْحًا

qadḥan

Striking sparks through impact

In early Arabic, Q D Ḥ describes striking or rubbing fire-making implements to produce fire. Poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period also preserves this usage, although attribution to an early poet does not by itself establish the precise date of a line or the priority of a particular wording.

In a transmitted version of his celebrated Muʿallaqah, ʿAntarah ibn Shaddād describes a fly moving in a meadow:

هَزِجًا يَحُكُّ ذِرَاعَهُ بِذِرَاعِهِ
قَدْحَ الْمُكِبِّ عَلَى الزِّنَادِ الْأَجْذَمِ

Hazijan yaḥukku dhirāʿahu bi-dhirāʿihi / qadḥa l-mukibbi ʿalā l-zinādi l-ajdhami.

Buzzing continuously, it rubs one foreleg against the other, like the fire-striking of a man with a severed hand, bending over his fire-making implements.

The poet compares the fly’s forelegs to the movement of someone rubbing two implements together to produce fire.

The line has variant wordings: some transmissions have fiʿla, “the action of,” instead of qadḥa, “the fire-striking of.” The wording with qadḥ is recorded in classical literary commentary. Al-ajdham is commonly explained as describing the man whose hand is severed; another explanation applies it to a shortened fire-making implement. The translation here follows the first explanation without presenting it as the only one.

Here, qadḥ does not name the fire or flame itself. It denotes the action undertaken to produce fire.

Forms examined: qadaḥa, qadḥ, iqtadaḥa, inqadaḥa, qādiḥ, miqdāḥ, qaddāḥah, qidḥ, and qadaḥ.

Qur’anic form: qadḥan, occurring once, in Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:2.

1. Core meaning and semantic branches

Before matches and modern fire-making tools, people rubbed suitable pieces of wood together or struck suitable stone and metal to obtain the heat or spark needed to kindle fire.

Arabic expressed this action as qadaḥa bi-l-zand. Implements used for it were also called miqdāḥ and qaddāḥah.

In this usage, qadḥ emphasizes the physical act of striking or rubbing to produce fire. Someone may strike a fire-making implement several times without successfully lighting a fire. Performing the action does not necessarily imply successful ignition.

The root has other Arabic usages. Qādiḥ can designate a pest or destructive agency affecting wood or teeth and causing decay. Qadaḥa fī ʿirḍihi means to disparage someone and damage that person’s honor.

A further branch concerns removing the contents of a vessel, especially with a ladle.

The following three forms should also be distinguished:

  • Qadḥ: striking sparks, affecting something, or disparaging.
  • Qidḥ: an arrow shaft, including one used in drawing lots and maysir.
  • Qadaḥ: a cup or drinking vessel.

Their similar written forms do not make their meanings identical. Nor is the historical relationship among all branches of this family established with certainty.

2. Qadḥ in the Qur’an: producing fire through contact

فَالْمُورِيَاتِ قَدْحًا

Fa-l-mūriyāti qadḥā.

And by those that strike sparks through impact.

Al-'Adiyat 100:2

This verse belongs to the opening sequence of oaths in Al-ʿĀdiyāt:

وَالْعَادِيَاتِ ضَبْحًا

Wa-l-ʿādiyāti ḍabḥā.

By the panting runners,

Al-'Adiyat 100:1

فَالْمُورِيَاتِ قَدْحًا

Fa-l-mūriyāti qadḥā.

and those that strike sparks through impact,

Al-'Adiyat 100:2

فَالْمُغِيرَاتِ صُبْحًا

Fa-l-mughīrāti ṣubḥā.

and those raiding at dawn,

Al-'Adiyat 100:3

فَأَثَرْنَ بِهِ نَقْعًا

Fa-atharna bihi naqʿā.

then stirring up dust thereby,

Al-'Adiyat 100:4

فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا

Fa-wasaṭna bihi jamʿā.

then penetrating into a gathering thereby.

Al-'Adiyat 100:5

The familiar, predominant interpretation pictures swift horses running and panting: their hooves strike stones and raise sparks; they raid at dawn, stir up dust, and enter a gathering.

Neither horses nor hooves are expressly named in the verses. The combined descriptions and actions support this interpretation, but it remains a contextual explanation rather than an addition to the Arabic wording.

Two different roots occur together in the second verse:

Al-mūriyāt, from W R Y, refers to those producing fire; qadḥan describes how it is produced.

Qadḥ names the action of striking and sparking, whereas īrāʾ concerns bringing fire into view.

On this interpretation, the image is not of establishing a lasting blaze. Repeated impacts during movement produce sparks that contribute to the depiction of motion and intensity.

The oaths introduce a statement about the human being:

إِنَّ الْإِنْسَانَ لِرَبِّهِ لَكَنُودٌ

Inna l-insāna li-rabbihi la-kanūd.

Indeed, the human being is deeply ungrateful to his Lord.

Al-'Adiyat 100:6

This ingratitude is the subject of the statement affirmed by the oath; it should not be incorporated into the lexical meaning of qadḥ.

The syntax of qadḥan

Qadḥan is an accusative verbal noun from qadaḥa.

One grammatical analysis takes it as a cognate accusative governed by an omitted yaqdaḥna: those producing fire while striking.

Another treats the verbal noun as a circumstantial expression equivalent to qādiḥāt, “striking.” It can also be understood as standing in for a verbal noun cognate with al-mūriyāt because of the semantic proximity of qadḥ and īrāʾ. These are grammatical analyses, not variant recitations.

Across these analyses, qadḥ clarifies how the fire is produced.

3. Non-Qur’anic usages

In Arabic, qadaḥa fī nasabihi means to impugn someone’s lineage, while qadaḥa fī ʿirḍihi means to disparage a person and damage their reputation or honor.

Persian also uses the expression madḥ wa qadḥ for praise and disparagement.

This usage can be understood through the image of making an impression on a body and damaging it. Just as something eats into wood or teeth, a person’s words can injure another’s standing. This is a semantic comparison, not proof of a single historical derivation.

In another usage, iqtadaḥa l-amra means to consider or deliberate over a matter. The Persian image of “a spark arising in the mind” offers a comparable illustration.

Disparagement, damage to reputation, and deliberation are not the meaning of the Qur’anic occurrence. The root’s sole Qur’anic use belongs to the fire-producing branch.

4. Comparison with related roots

Q D Ḥ (qadḥ) and W R Y (īrāʾ)

In its fire-related branch, W R Y concerns fire emerging or being produced.

أَفَرَأَيْتُمُ النَّارَ الَّتِي تُورُونَ

A-fa-raʾaytumu l-nāra llatī tūrūn.

Have you considered the fire that you produce?

Al-Waqi'ah 56:71

Qadḥ foregrounds striking or rubbing to produce fire, whereas īrāʾ concerns the fire appearing through that action.

Their combination in Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:2 helps clarify the distinction.

Q D Ḥ (qadḥ) and W Q D (īqād)

W Q D concerns kindling, blazing, and combustible material.

الَّذِي جَعَلَ لَكُمْ مِنَ الشَّجَرِ الْأَخْضَرِ نَارًا فَإِذَا أَنْتُمْ مِنْهُ تُوقِدُونَ

Alladhī jaʿala lakum mina l-shajari l-akhḍari nāran fa-idhā antum minhu tūqidūn.

The One who made fire for you from the green tree, and then from it you kindle.

Ya-Sin 36:80

A person may produce a spark by qadḥ and use it to kindle a fire. Qadḥ describes the first action; īqād describes the second.

Kindling is not confined to striking sparks. A person can also bring an existing flame to fuel and set it alight.

Q D Ḥ (qadḥ) and Q B S (qabas)

Qadḥ concerns producing sparks or fire by striking and rubbing; qabas denotes a firebrand taken from an already burning source.

Someone without fire may strike to produce it; someone with access to a burning fire can take a firebrand from it.

Q D Ḥ (qadḥ) and SH R R (sharar)

Sharar names sparks and fragments of fire that leap out from a fire.

إِنَّهَا تَرْمِي بِشَرَرٍ كَالْقَصْرِ

Innahā tarmī bi-shararin ka-l-qaṣr.

Indeed, it throws out sparks like a palace.

Al-Mursalat 77:32

Qadḥ names the act of striking sparks; sharar names the sparks and leaping fragments themselves.

5. Translation proposals for Forqan

In Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:2, qadḥan alongside al-mūriyāt can be conveyed as “striking sparks through impact.”

The complete verse can be translated:

“And by those that strike sparks through impact.”

Brief root meaning: Striking or rubbing to produce sparks and fire; affecting or injuring something or someone’s reputation; and, in another branch, removing the contents of a vessel.

Conclusion

In its Qur’anic use, Q D Ḥ describes an action that produces sparks and fire through impact or rubbing.

In Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:2, qadḥ explains how fire is produced and combines with al-mūriyāt to depict movement and spark-producing contact.

Qadḥ differs from īrāʾ, īqād, and iqtibās: qadḥ is striking sparks, īrāʾ bringing forth fire, īqād kindling it, and iqtibās taking fire from an already burning source.
