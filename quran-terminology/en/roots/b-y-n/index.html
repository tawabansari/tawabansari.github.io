This article studies the root ب ي ن (b y n), a root connected in the Qur'an with the separation of boundaries, the clarity of proof, the clarification of revelation, the Clear Book, the Clear Conveyance, and the Manifest Truth.

Foundational Meaning

The root ب ي ن (b y n) is one of the very important roots of the Qur'an. Its first meaning goes back to separating, becoming distant, being between, becoming manifest through separation, and the boundary between things becoming clear. In this root, clarity is not merely light shining upon something; clarity means that something comes out of mixture and ambiguity, its boundary with another thing becomes known, and it is no longer confused with its opposite.

So ب ي ن (b y n) has two closely related layers: one is “between” and the distance between two things; the other is بيان (bayān), clarification through separating boundaries. From here words such as بين (bayn), بيان (bayān), بيّنة (bayyinah), تبيين (tabyīn), تبيّن (tabayyun), تبيان (tibyān), and مبين (mubīn) are formed.

Important Qur'anic Forms

The important forms of this root in the Qur'an include بين (bayn), بينكم (baynakum), بيان (bayān), بيّنة (bayyinah), بينات (bayyināt), يبيّن (yubayyin), تبيين (tabyīn), تبيّن (tabayyun), تبيان (tibyān), مبين (mubīn), and المبين (al-mubīn). بين (bayn) points to distance, between-ness, relation, or the boundary between things. بيان (bayān) is clarification of meaning. بيّنة (bayyinah) is clear proof, a clear sign, or a clear argument. تبيين (tabyīn) is clarifying and opening the boundary of meaning. تبيّن (tabayyun) is becoming clear or investigating until the truth becomes manifest. تبيان (tibyān) is a stronger form of clarification and boundary-giving. مبين (mubīn) can mean “clear,” “manifest,” “clarifying,” or “making manifest,” depending on the context.

Linguistic Core

The linguistic core of ب ي ن (b y n) is separation together with manifestation. When something is ambiguous, its boundaries are mixed together. When بيان (bayān) occurs, things become separated from each other: truth from falsehood, رشد (rushd) from غي (ghayy), guidance from misguidance, revelation from conjecture, and the clear message from confused speech.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is two things becoming distant from each other so that their boundary becomes known. As long as two colors, two paths, two sounds, or two meanings are mixed together, recognition is difficult. But when distance and boundary appear, each thing shows its own place. بيان (bayān) does the same thing in language and understanding: it separates meaning from ambiguity.

Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic usage, ب ي ن (b y n) is connected to distance, separation, being between, becoming manifest, and making clear. From this same root, both the meaning of “between” and the meaning of “clarification” are formed. This connection is very important: بيان (bayān) means making clear by separating boundaries. In the final version, if a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or ancient Arabic usage is found, it can be added.

The Separation of Rushd from Ghayy

One foundational use of this root in the Qur'an is the separation of رشد (rushd) from غي (ghayy):

لَا إِكْرَاهَ فِي الدِّينِ ۖ قَد تَّبَيَّنَ الرُّشْدُ مِنَ الْغَيِّ

There is no compulsion in religion; the boundary of right direction has become clear from misguidance.

Al-Baqarah 2:256

In this verse, تبيّن (tabayyana) means becoming clear after separation. رشد (rushd) and غي (ghayy) do not remain mixed together; their boundary has become manifest. For this reason, compulsion in religion is negated: when the path has become clear, the human response must arise from understanding and choice, not from force.

This verse is also directly connected to طاغوت (ṭāghūt). In the continuation of the same verse it says:

فَمَن يَكْفُرْ بِالطَّاغُوتِ وَيُؤْمِن بِاللَّهِ فَقَدِ اسْتَمْسَكَ بِالْعُرْوَةِ الْوُثْقَىٰ

So whoever rejects the ṭāghūt and believes in The God has grasped the firmest handhold.

Al-Baqarah 2:256

طاغوت (ṭāghūt) works by confusing boundaries: the boundary between God and other-than-God, truth and power, revelation and tradition, justice and domination, servitude to The God and obedience to a false authority. بيّنة (bayyinah) and تبيّن (tabayyun) stand against this ambiguity. Once رشد (rushd) has become clear from غي (ghayy), طاغوت (ṭāghūt) can no longer seat itself in the place of truth, except for one who turns away from clarity.

Bayyinah

The second field is بيّنة (bayyinah):

قَدْ جَاءَتْكُم بَيِّنَةٌ مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ

A clear proof from your Lord has certainly come to you.

Al-A'raf 7:73

بيّنة (bayyinah) is a clear sign or clear proof; something that brings truth out of ambiguity and makes the argument manifest. بيّنة (bayyinah) is not merely information; it is the clarity of proof. For this reason, after a بيّنة (bayyinah) has come, human responsibility becomes heavier.

In another verse it says:

لِيَهْلِكَ مَنْ هَلَكَ عَن بَيِّنَةٍ وَيَحْيَىٰ مَنْ حَيَّ عَن بَيِّنَةٍ

So that whoever perishes may perish upon clear proof, and whoever lives may live upon clear proof.

Al-Anfal 8:42

Here, بيّنة (bayyinah) is the measure of responsibility. The human being should not be judged in darkness and ambiguity; the proof must become clear. بيّنة (bayyinah) brings the space out of a foggy state so that human choice becomes meaningful.

Bayan as Divine Clarification

The third field is بيان (bayān) as divine clarification:

هَٰذَا بَيَانٌ لِّلنَّاسِ وَهُدًى وَمَوْعِظَةٌ لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ

This is a clarification for the people, and guidance and admonition for the people of taqwā.

Al-Imran 3:138

In this verse, the Qur'an is بيان (bayān): speech that clarifies reality, shows boundaries, and brings the human being out of ambiguity. The clarification is for all people, but its effect as guidance and admonition becomes more complete in the people of تقوى (taqwā).

The Messenger's Clarification and the Book's Tibyan

The fourth field is تبيين (tabyīn) as the duty of the Messenger and the work of revelation:

وَأَنزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ الذِّكْرَ لِتُبَيِّنَ لِلنَّاسِ مَا نُزِّلَ إِلَيْهِمْ

And We sent down the Reminder to you so that you may clarify for the people what has been sent down to them.

An-Nahl 16:44

This verse is very important. The Messenger’s تبيين (tabyīn) is connected to ما نزل إليهم (mā nuzzila ilayhim); that is, clarifying the very thing that was sent down. تبيين (tabyīn) here is not creating a parallel religion, an independent law, or a rival source beside revelation; it is the clarification of the message sent down for the people.

In the same surah it says:

وَنَزَّلْنَا عَلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ تِبْيَانًا لِّكُلِّ شَيْءٍ

And We sent down the Book upon you as a clarification for every thing.

An-Nahl 16:89

Here, the Book itself is تبيان (tibyān). This point must be preserved when understanding the Messenger’s تبيين (tabyīn): the Messenger clarifies, but his clarification is not separate from the Book and not against the Book. The revealed Book itself is تبيان (tibyān), and the Messenger’s mission is to deliver, express, and clarify that same message.

Al-Balagh al-Mubin

The fifth field is البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn):

فَهَلْ عَلَى الرُّسُلِ إِلَّا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ

So is anything upon the messengers except delivering the Mubin?

An-Nahl 16:35

And also:

وَمَا عَلَى الرَّسُولِ إِلَّا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ

And nothing is upon the Messenger except delivering the Mubin.

An-Nur 24:54

In common translation, البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn) is usually rendered “clear delivery” or “manifest conveyance.” This translation is understandable from the simple grammatical angle, because المبين (al-mubīn) can be an adjective of البلاغ (al-balāgh). But in the Forqan method, considering that the Qur'an repeatedly introduces itself as كتاب مبين (kitāb mubīn) and قرآن مبين (qur'ān mubīn), البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn) can be read more closely and more deeply as: “delivering that Mubin.”

In this reading, مبين (mubīn) points to the revealed message itself: the Clear Book, the Clear Qur'an, the Criterion, and the Reminder. Therefore the duty of the Messenger is to deliver that Mubin, not to produce an independent authority outside revelation. The Messenger, in the station of messengership, carries and delivers the message of The God; obedience to the Messenger in this station is obedience to the clear message he delivered.

This point is very important for understanding the verses أطيعوا الله وأطيعوا الرسول (aṭīʿū Allāh wa-aṭīʿū al-rasūl). If the Messenger is commanded to deliver that Mubin, then obedience to the Messenger in the path of messengership returns to obedience to revelation, the Qur'an, the Criterion, and the clear divine message. Using these verses to seat later, scattered, and conjectural reports beside the Qur'an or above the Qur'an is not compatible with the meaning of البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn). The Qur'an and the Book as Mubin

The sixth field is the Qur'an and the Book as مبين (mubīn):

تِلْكَ آيَاتُ الْكِتَابِ وَقُرْآنٍ مُّبِينٍ

These are the signs of the Book and a clear and clarifying Qur'an.

Al-Hijr 15:1

And also:

تِلْكَ آيَاتُ الْكِتَابِ الْمُبِينِ

These are the signs of the Clear Book.

Yusuf 12:1

In these verses, the Qur'an and the Book carry the description مبين (mubīn). Here, مبين (mubīn) can mean both “clear” and “clarifying.” The Qur'an is clear because it does not hide itself in sealed ambiguity; and it is clarifying because it separates truth from falsehood.

There are many similar verses: An-Naml 27:1, Ash-Shuʿara 26:2, Al-Qasas 28:2, Ya-Sin 36:69, Ad-Dukhan 44:2, and Az-Zukhruf 43:2. This repetition shows that مبين (mubīn) is one of the central descriptions of the Qur'an and the Book.

An Arabic Tongue That Is Mubin

The seventh field is an Arabic tongue that is مبين (mubīn):

بِلِسَانٍ عَرَبِيٍّ مُّبِينٍ

In an Arabic tongue that is clear and clarifying.

Ash-Shu'ara 26:195

The language of revelation is مبين (mubīn); that is, a language that has the capacity to clarify and to carry the message in a form that is understandable and manifest. This verse should not be turned into ethnic superiority for Arabs; the point is the capacity of the language carrying revelation in that station, not a racial privilege.

Imam Mubin

The eighth field is إمام مبين (imām mubīn):

وَكُلَّ شَيْءٍ أَحْصَيْنَاهُ فِي إِمَامٍ مُّبِينٍ

And every thing We have enumerated in a clear and clarifying imām.

Ya-Sin 36:12

In this verse, إمام مبين (imām mubīn) points to a manifest reference of recording, enumeration, or clear leading. It should not be reduced without evidence to a particular historical person unless there is sufficient Qur'anic proof. At the level of the root, مبين (mubīn) means something clear, manifest, and indicating.

Mubin for Enmity and Misguidance

The ninth field is that مبين (mubīn) also comes for misguidance, enmity, and error:

إِنَّ الشَّيْطَانَ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ مُّبِينٌ

Indeed, Satan is for you a manifest enemy.

Al-Baqarah 2:168

And:

إِنْ أَنتُمْ إِلَّا فِي ضَلَالٍ مُّبِينٍ

You are only in manifest misguidance.

Ya-Sin 36:47

Here, مبين (mubīn) does not have the positive meaning of clarifying truth; rather, it means manifest, clear, unveiled. So مبين (mubīn) is not always a sacred adjective. Depending on what it qualifies, it can be the clarification of truth or the manifestness of enmity and misguidance.

Al-Haqq al-Mubin

Now one must speak carefully about المبين (al-mubīn). In some famous lists of the Beautiful Names, المبين (al-mubīn) is brought as one of the names of The God. But from a Qur'anic perspective, precision is necessary. The Qur'an repeatedly describes the Book, the Qur'an, the conveyance, the tongue, the warner, the enemy, misguidance, and other matters with the description مبين (mubīn). Regarding The God, the main expression is:

وَيَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ الْحَقُّ الْمُبِينُ

And they will know that The God is the Manifest Truth.

An-Nur 24:25

In this verse, المبين (al-mubīn) comes as a description of الحق (al-ḥaqq): الله هو الحق المبين (Allāh huwa al-ḥaqq al-mubīn). That is, The God is the manifest and manifesting Truth. This verse clearly states الحق المبين (al-ḥaqq al-mubīn), but extracting المبين (al-mubīn) separately and independently as a divine name requires caution. The Qur'an does not independently say الله هو المبين (Allāh huwa al-mubīn). The Qur'anic expression is that The God is الحق المبين (al-ḥaqq al-mubīn).

From a perspective of divine transcendence, this difference is important. The God is absolute Truth, and His being the Truth is manifest and manifesting. But المبين (al-mubīn) in the Qur'an appears more prominently and repeatedly as a description of the Qur'an, the Book, and the conveyance of messengership. If المبين (al-mubīn) is taken without qualification and cut off from context as an independent name of The God, the boundary between Qur'anic description, divine name, and the function of revelation becomes ambiguous. It is better in the work of Forqan to say: الحق المبين (al-ḥaqq al-mubīn) is the Qur'anic description of The God, while كتاب مبين (kitāb mubīn), قرآن مبين (qur'ān mubīn), and البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn) are central descriptions and names of revelation and messengership.

Distinguishing the Uses

One must distinguish between the uses of ب ي ن (b y n). بين (bayn) can point to distance, between-ness, and relation. بيان (bayān) is clarification of meaning. بيّنة (bayyinah) is a clear proof or clear sign. تبيين (tabyīn) is the act of clarifying and separating the boundary of meaning. تبيّن (tabayyun) is the act of investigation and truth becoming clear. مبين (mubīn) is sometimes clear, sometimes clarifying, and sometimes making manifest. كتاب مبين (kitāb mubīn) and قرآن مبين (qur'ān mubīn) are both clear and clarifying. البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn) in the Forqan method is “delivering that Mubin.” عدو مبين (ʿaduww mubīn) is a manifest enemy. ضلال مبين (ḍalāl mubīn) is manifest misguidance. الحق المبين (al-ḥaqq al-mubīn) is the manifest and manifesting Truth.

Structural Notes

Structurally, مبين (mubīn) must always be brought back to the noun it qualifies and to its context. When we say كتاب مبين (kitāb mubīn), مبين (mubīn) is a description of the Book. When we say قرآن مبين (qur'ān mubīn), مبين (mubīn) is a description of the Qur'an. When we say عدو مبين (ʿaduww mubīn), مبين (mubīn) is a description of manifest enmity. When we say الحق المبين (al-ḥaqq al-mubīn), مبين (mubīn) is a description of the Truth. And when we say البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn), in the Forqan method it can be understood as delivering that same Mubin: delivering the clear and clarifying Book/Qur'an/Criterion.

Difference from Nearby Roots

ب ي ن (b y n) differs from several nearby roots. نور (nūr) illuminates the space so that things may be seen; but ب ي ن (b y n) clarifies the boundary between things so that they may be distinguished from each other. ظهر (ẓahara) is close to becoming apparent and appearing; but ب ي ن (b y n) emphasizes becoming clear through separation and distinction. وضح (waḍaḥa) is close to clarity and being clear; بيان (bayān) is more active and opens the boundary of meaning. فصل (faṣl) points to separating and cutting between matters; ب ي ن (b y n) also contains separation, but it is more closely connected to making meaning manifest. فرقان (furqān) is also connected to separating truth from falsehood; بيان (bayān) is clarification, and فرقان (furqān) is the criterion of separation. شرح (sharḥ) is opening and expanding; بيان (bayān) is clarifying and setting the boundary of meaning. كشف (kashf) is removing a covering; بيان (bayān) is making meaning and proof manifest.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For translation in Forqan, ب ي ن (b y n) must be translated according to context. بين (bayn) can be “between,” “among,” “in between,” “the boundary between,” or “the relation between.” بيان (bayān) is “clarification,” “making clear,” or “clarifying speech.” بيّنة (bayyinah) is better translated as “clear proof,” “clear sign,” or “clear argument.” تبيين (tabyīn) is “clarifying,” “making manifest,” or “making the boundary of meaning clear.” تبيّن (tabayyun) is “becoming clear,” “investigating until the truth becomes clear,” or “verification.” مبين (mubīn), depending on context, is “clear,” “manifest,” “clarifying,” or “making manifest.” كتاب مبين (kitāb mubīn) is “the Clear Book” or “the clear and clarifying Book.” قرآن مبين (qur'ān mubīn) is “the Clear Qur'an” or “the clear and clarifying Qur'an.” البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn) in the Forqan method is better translated as “delivering that Mubin.” الحق المبين (al-ḥaqq al-mubīn) regarding The God is better translated as “the Manifest Truth” or “the manifest and manifesting Truth,” rather than carelessly extracting from it an independent name.

Linguistic Summary

Linguistic summary: the root ب ي ن (b y n) points to distance, separation, becoming manifest, making clear, and separating the boundary of meaning. Its material image is two things separating and the distance between them becoming manifest. In the Qur'an, this root extends from بين (bayn) and بيان (bayān) to بيّنة (bayyinah) and تبيين (tabyīn), from كتاب مبين (kitāb mubīn) and قرآن مبين (qur'ān mubīn) to البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn), and from عدو مبين (ʿaduww mubīn) to الحق المبين (al-ḥaqq al-mubīn). مبين (mubīn) in the Qur'an is one of the central descriptions of the Qur'an, the Book, and the delivered message. Regarding The God, the Qur'anic expression is الحق المبين (al-ḥaqq al-mubīn); that is, The God is the Manifest Truth. Therefore, in the Forqan method, مبين (mubīn) must be understood from its context; and in the verses of messengership, البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn) must return us to delivering that Mubin, meaning the clear message of revelation, not to an endless authority of reports outside the Qur'an.
