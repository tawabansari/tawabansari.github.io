Nafḥ in early Arabic and the Qur’an: wind, fragrance, giving, striking and a portion of punishment

The Arabic root ن ف ح concerns blowing, dispersing, bursting out and something reaching another. Established uses include wind, fragrance, an animal’s sudden kick, blood spurting and a gift reaching someone.

In many uses something emerges, moves or extends its effect elsewhere. This may be pleasant, like cool air or fragrance, or harmful, like scorching wind or an animal’s blow.

The root occurs only once in the Qur’an: nafḥah in Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46. Nafḥatun min ʿadhābi rabbika concerns a portion of punishment reaching people. Those disregarding the warning would acknowledge their wrongdoing if it reached them.

Two distinctions matter: nafḥ does not inherently mean mercy or punishment, and nafḥah need not lexically mean a weak, ineffective blow. Quantity, severity and outcome depend on construction and context.

1. Forms and Semantic Core

Important Arabic forms include:

  • نَفَحَ (nafaḥa): blew, dispersed, conveyed something or struck; the subject and construction determine the sense.
  • نَفْح (nafḥ): blowing, diffusion, bursting out or striking.
  • نُفُوح (nufūḥ): blowing or spreading, particularly of scent and wind.
  • نَفْحَة (nafḥah): one gust, an effect reaching someone, a blow or a gift.
  • نَفَحَات (nafaḥāt): plural occurrences, gusts or gifts.
  • نَافِح (nāfiḥ): something blowing or conveying an effect.
  • نَفُوح (nafūḥ): describing strong wind, a kicking animal or a she-camel releasing milk.
  • نَفِيحَة (nafīḥah): in some lexical uses, a bow.
  • نَافَحَ و مُنَافَحَة (nāfaḥa, munāfaḥah): defending, confronting or contending; مُنَافِح (munāfiḥ): a defender.

Infaḥah and minfaḥah are also recorded: terms for part of a suckling lamb’s or kid’s stomach and material used to coagulate milk for cheese. Listing them within the same family does not by itself establish derivation from blowing or giving.

Only nafḥah occurs in the Qur’an.

Semantic Core

One lexical analysis relates N F Ḥ to something emerging, moving or rising.

This fits many recorded uses: fragrance spreads, wind blows, blood spurts, an animal kicks, or property passes from a giver to a recipient.

Such resemblance does not establish a historical account deriving every meaning from wind.

The uses suggest a shared tendency toward emerging, moving, propelling or conveying an effect. The thing involved and how its effect reaches another must be identified in each use.

This should not become so broad that every movement, gift or impact is called nafḥ. Arabic has particular established uses; sharing a general feature does not equate it with every movement or transfer root.

2. Early Arabic Uses

Wind and Its Effect

One familiar use concerns wind:

نَفَحَتِ الرِّيحُ

nafaḥati al-rīḥu

Translation: “The wind blew.”

Nafḥat al-rīḥ denotes a gust or an occurrence of wind reaching something.

It may be pleasant or unpleasant. Nafḥatun mina al-ṣabā describes an easterly breeze and its pleasant effect; nafḥatun min samūmin can describe a wave of hot, uncomfortable wind.

Nafḥah itself determines neither coolness, heat, comfort nor pain. These follow from the wind and its accompanying description.

One lexical distinction uses nafḥ mainly for cool wind and lafḥ for hot wind.

Some uses support that distinction, but not as an exceptionless rule: other lexical accounts record nafḥah for pleasant and scorching wind alike.

“Blowing” and “the effect of wind reaching something” are therefore more precise general definitions than limiting the root to cold air.

Fragrance Spreading

Another important use concerns perfume and pleasant scent.

Qays ibn al-Khaṭīm, a pre-Islamic poet of Aws, describes a woman named ʿAmrah:

وَعَمْرَةُ مِنْ سَرَوَاتِ النِّسَاءِ
تَنْفَحُ بِالْمِسْكِ أَرْدَانُهَا

wa-ʿamratu min sarawāti al-nisāʾi / tanfaḥu bi-al-miski ardānuhā

Translation: “ʿAmrah is among the noble women; her sleeves diffuse musk.”

Tanfaḥu concerns the spreading of musk’s scent.

Fragrance travels from the clothing or sleeves into the surroundings, where others perceive it. Nafḥ here concerns scent, rather than money given or a blow delivered.

The witness provides a usage attributed to a pre-Islamic poet, independent of the Qur’anic setting.

It does not establish that nafḥ always conveys something pleasant and delicate. Pleasantness follows from musk and the description, not every use of the root.

Giving Property

N F Ḥ also expresses giving.

For example:

نَفَحَهُ بِالْمَالِ

nafaḥahu bi-al-māli

Translation: “He gave him property.”

Nafaḥātun mina al-maʿrūf refers to gifts and acts of kindness reaching others.

Property passes from giver to recipient, fitting the image of sending or conveying something.

Giving and kindness are not universal parts of the root’s meaning: it also describes blows, unpleasant wind and punishment reaching people.

The Umayyad poet Rammāḥ ibn Abrad, known as Ibn Mayyādah, says:

لَمَّا أَتَيْتُكَ أَرْجُو فَضْلَ نَائِلِكُمْ
نَفَحْتَنِي نَفْحَةً طَابَتْ لَهَا الْعَرَبُ

lammā ataytuka arjū faḍla nāʾilikum / nafaḥtanī nafḥatan ṭābat lahā al-ʿarabu

Translation: “When I came hoping for your generous bounty, you gave me a gift that pleased the soul.”

Nafḥah denotes the gift received by the poet.

The word does not specify its exact amount. A gift may be small or large; its value and size depend on the circumstances and description.

The witness belongs to the Umayyad period, rather than pre-Islamic times, and illustrates continued classical usage.

An Animal’s Blow

Animal-related Arabic also uses nafaḥa for a kick with the hind foot:

نَفَحَتِ الدَّابَّةُ بِرِجْلِهَا

nafaḥati al-dābbatu bi-rijlihā

Translation: “The animal kicked with its foot.”

Some accounts distinguish nafḥ, a one-footed kick, from ramḥ, a kick with both feet. Not all sources present this as a fixed rule.

Here a movement originates from an animal and strikes something.

The family also describes sword blows, sometimes emphasizing an oblique strike or one delivered from a distance.

Nafḥ is therefore not necessarily gentle, pleasant or harmless; it can involve impact and injury.

Blood and Milk Emerging

Lexical accounts also describe blood spurting from a wound through N F Ḥ.

Ṭaʿnatun naffāḥatun or ṭaʿnatun nafūḥun describes a wound emitting blood forcefully or quickly.

No wind need be involved. Emerging, spurting liquid is the descriptive focus.

Nāqatun nafūḥun can also describe a she-camel whose milk flows without milking.

Both show something issuing or flowing from its place within this family.

They do not establish blood or milk as the historically first sense. Establishing the order of recorded meanings requires independent historical evidence.

Defending and Confronting

Nāfaḥa is also recorded for defending someone or confronting another.

For example:

نَافَحَ عَنْهُ

nāfaḥa ʿanhu

Translation: “He defended him.”

The issue is defense against attack or opposition, rather than wind or scent.

Some accounts explain it through the proximity of opponents and their blows or effects reaching each other. This may clarify a semantic connection without establishing a certain historical origin.

Form III alone also does not guarantee equally reciprocal participation. Defense and confrontation follow from the verb’s established use and its accompanying preposition.

3. Qur’anic Use: Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46

وَلَئِن مَّسَّتْهُمْ نَفْحَةٌ مِّنْ عَذَابِ رَبِّكَ لَيَقُولُنَّ يَا وَيْلَنَا إِنَّا كُنَّا ظَالِمِينَ

wa-laʾin massathum nafḥatun min ʿadhābi rabbika la-yaqūlunna yā waylanā innā kunnā ẓālimīna

Translation: “If even a little of your Lord’s punishment touches them, they will surely say: Woe to us! We were wrongdoers.”

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46

The warning concerns people who disregard it despite long enjoyment of life’s resources.

The preceding verse says:

قُلْ إِنَّمَا أُنذِرُكُم بِالْوَحْيِ وَلَا يَسْمَعُ الصُّمُّ الدُّعَاءَ إِذَا مَا يُنذَرُونَ

qul innamā undhirukum bi-al-waḥyi wa-lā yasmaʿu al-ṣummu al-duʿāʾa idhā mā yundharūna

Translation: “Say: I warn you only through revelation. The deaf do not hear the call when they are warned.”

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:45

Deafness here concerns rejecting warning. Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46 then describes the addressees’ response if punishment reaches them.

The contrast is central: those ignoring warning would acknowledge wrongdoing if even a small portion of punishment reached them.

The Meaning of Nafḥah

Nafḥah follows the faʿlah pattern.

This can name one occurrence of an action, such as one blow or one gust.

Nafḥah can thus express a single occurrence of nafḥ.

The pattern alone does not establish weakness, brevity or ineffectiveness. One blow can be severe, and one gust gentle or powerful.

Nafḥatun min ʿadhābi rabbika concerns an impact or a portion of punishment reaching someone.

Smallness is reinforced through several features together: one occurrence, min ʿadhāb and the accompanying massathum, “touched them.”

“A little punishment” is therefore defensible here without becoming the fixed definition of every nafḥah.

Massathum: Punishment Reaching Them

Massathum belongs to M S S and concerns contact, impact or an effect reaching someone.

The verse does not say the entire punishment has descended or that they are in its full extent. It describes a nafḥah of punishment reaching them.

This distinguishes mass from nafḥah.

Nafḥah describes an occurrence of an impact or arrival; massathum specifies its contact with the addressees.

Together they depict punishment reaching people without specifying its physical mechanism.

Mass itself does not establish harmlessness or an extremely mild effect. Slight contact with something severe can have major consequences.

Even a limited portion of punishment would produce an evident response here.

Min ʿAdhābi Rabbika

Min fits a partitive reading: an occurrence from the punishment, rather than all of it.

This fits the conditional construction, which presents a portion reaching them to demonstrate their response.

N F Ḥ itself does not mean “part of punishment”; that meaning arises from nafḥah combined with min ʿadhābi rabbika.

Nor is the punishment’s type specified: scorching wind, fire, illness, military defeat and natural disaster are not identified.

Wind-related Arabic uses do not justify restricting this punishment to one physical phenomenon.

The verse concerns its effect, not its material mechanism.

Why Is Their Response So Strong?

The verse continues:

لَيَقُولُنَّ يَا وَيْلَنَا إِنَّا كُنَّا ظَالِمِينَ

la-yaqūlunna yā waylanā innā kunnā ẓālimīna

Translation: “They will surely say: Woe to us! We were wrongdoers.”

The response is emphatically stated.

Those previously ignoring the warning would acknowledge wrongdoing when confronted with its consequence.

A strong response is contrasted with a limited amount of punishment.

Nafḥah is not inherently the severest punishment. Rather, the portion reaching them would suffice to change their position.

The construction is conditional: “if … touches them, they will say ….” It should not become a report of punishment necessarily already inflicted on these addressees.

Acknowledging injustice also does not alone establish accepted repentance or practical reform. The verse reports their response without separately explaining its final outcome.

Justice and Reckoning

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:47 says:

وَنَضَعُ الْمَوَازِينَ الْقِسْطَ لِيَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ فَلَا تُظْلَمُ نَفْسٌ شَيْئًا

wa-naḍaʿu al-mawāzīna al-qisṭa li-yawmi al-qiyāmati fa-lā tuẓlamu nafsun shayʾan

Translation: “We set up the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection, so no self is wronged at all.”

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:47

This connection matters: acknowledgment of wrongdoing is followed by just reckoning.

The warning is not detached from justice; the following verse denies injustice in divine judgment.

Justice, reckoning and acknowledgment are nevertheless distinct meanings supplied by the surrounding verses, rather than the lexical definition of N F Ḥ.

4. Related Roots

N F Ḥ and L F Ḥ

These overlap particularly when wind or heat reaches something.

The Qur’an uses L F Ḥ for fire:

تَلْفَحُ وُجُوهَهُمُ النَّارُ وَهُمْ فِيهَا كَالِحُونَ

talfaḥu wujūhahumu al-nāru wa-hum fīhā kāliḥūna

Translation: “Fire scorches their faces, and in it their faces are drawn back, their teeth exposed.”

Al-Muʾminūn 23:104

Lafḥ concerns fire’s effect scorching faces.

Some lexical explanations distinguish cold nafḥ from hot lafḥ. Another treats them as close and describes nafḥ as stronger in effect; still others emphasize greater harm in lafḥ.

This variation prevents a universal rule that nafḥ is always gentle and lafḥ always severe.

Lafḥ is more closely associated with heat, particularly fire and scorching wind. Nafḥ has a wider range of blowing, diffusion, impact and conveying effects.

Their resemblance should not restrict nafḥah in Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46 to burning by fire.

N F Ḥ and N F KH

Nafkh concerns blowing and expelling air or something similar through blowing.

Qur’anic examples concern the Trumpet and certain creation settings.

Nafḥ can involve moving air but is not limited to blowing.

Diffusing musk, kicking or giving property need not involve expelled breath.

Similar consonants and overlapping uses do not make nafḥ and nafkh one root or meaning.

“Blowing punishment” or “a nafkhah of punishment” is therefore imprecise for Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46, importing N F KH into N F Ḥ.

N F Ḥ and N F TH

Nafth ordinarily concerns expelling air or blowing with a little oral moisture.

Al-Falaq 113:4 uses this family in al-naffāthāti fī al-ʿuqad.

Qur’anic nafḥ is not restricted to that action. Al-Anbiyāʾ concerns punishment reaching people without mentioning a mouth, blowing or moisture.

The meanings should not be transferred, although both families can involve something emerging or an effect reaching another.

Study of N F TH

N F Ḥ and M S S

Mass concerns contact or an effect reaching someone. Nafḥ describes more particular kinds of blowing, diffusion, impact or arrival in its established uses.

Both appear in Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46.

Massathum establishes contact with the addressees; nafḥatun min ʿadhāb identifies what reaches them as an occurrence or portion of punishment.

This shows why root meaning should be distinguished from accompanying words’ contributions.

5. Translation Proposals for Forqan

The Persian equivalent of نَفَحَ (nafaḥa) depends on the sentence’s subject matter.

For wind, “blew” fits; for fragrance, “spread” or “its scent reached the surroundings.”

For property, “gave”; for an animal’s blow, “kicked.”

One equivalent should not be imposed on every derivative and use.

In general language نَفْحَة (nafḥah) can mean a gust, an effect reaching someone, one occurrence or a gift, depending on context.

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46 concerns punishment. Translation should convey a portion reaching people and its limited extent contrasted with their strong response.

The following rendering fits:

وَلَئِن مَّسَّتْهُمْ نَفْحَةٌ مِّنْ عَذَابِ رَبِّكَ لَيَقُولُنَّ يَا وَيْلَنَا إِنَّا كُنَّا ظَالِمِينَ

wa-laʾin massathum nafḥatun min ʿadhābi rabbika la-yaqūlunna yā waylanā innā kunnā ẓālimīna

“If even a little of your Lord’s punishment touches them, they will surely say: Woe to us! We were wrongdoers.”

This naturally conveys the small amount and its reaching them.

“A little punishment” does not convey the entire image of nafḥah, including one occurrence of something reaching people. That feature belongs in explanation.

“A breeze of punishment” can provide literary imagery, but risks making readers imagine literal wind, which the verse does not specify.

“Mild punishment” is also imprecise. Limited quantity does not necessarily mean negligible severity.

For Forqan’s word box:

Brief root meaning: blowing, dispersing, bursting out or conveying an effect; also striking and giving in some uses.

Meaning in Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46: an occurrence or portion of punishment reaching them; even a little punishment in this context.

Punishment and smallness belong to this combination and setting, rather than every root use.

6. Conclusion

N F Ḥ concerns movement and something’s effect reaching another, as with wind, fragrance, spurting blood, an animal’s blow and a gift.

These are not identical acts. Shared root membership does not make nafḥ always pleasant, gentle, generous or harmful.

The sole Qur’anic form is nafḥah in Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:46, a portion of punishment reaching those disregarding warning.

Nafḥah presents one occurrence. Massathum and min ʿadhābi rabbika reinforce a limited quantity reaching them.

One occurrence or a small amount is not necessarily weak in effect. The verse emphasizes that even a portion would suffice to change their stance and elicit acknowledgment of wrongdoing.

Nafḥ does not establish that this punishment is wind, fire or a particular natural phenomenon; its nature is unspecified.

Final definition: N F Ḥ concerns blowing, diffusion, bursting out or conveying an effect, with striking and giving in other branches. Nafḥah can name one occurrence. Qur’anically it concerns a portion of punishment whose small extent is emphasized by construction and context, without determining its exact type or severity.
