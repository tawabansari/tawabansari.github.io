Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root B H R

A broad expanse of water, openness and breadth, and an environment that is both a source of life and movement and a place of danger and human limitation

1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root B H R moves in the field of openness, breadth, spaciousness, and a broad expanse of water.

Its most important Qur’anic derivative is بَحْر: a large body of water that may be the place of ships, fishing, extracting adornment, travel, trade, and also waves, darkness, storm, and drowning.

In the Qur’an, bahr is not merely geography. It is part of human life: provision, travel, movement, and danger that exposes human limitation.

The base meaning remains a broad expanse of water. Modern theories about maritime civilization, chemical identity, or absolute human mastery should not replace the contextual meaning of each verse.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives

بَحْر

A large expanse of water; sea or broad water.

الْبَحْر

The known bahr in context, or the general category of sea/water-expanse.

بَحْرَيْن

Two bahrs or two large water-expanses, sometimes described as sweet and salty.

أَبْحُر

Plural of bahr; seas or water-expanses, used in Luqman 31:27 for the vast image of the words of The God.

بِحَار

Another plural of bahr, used in verses about the transformation of the world.

بُحُور

A plural of bahr in Arabic; Qur’anic forms must still be checked separately.

بَحِيرَة

A she-camel given a special prohibited status in pre-Qur’anic custom by splitting or marking the ear.

4. Semantic Core

The core is openness and breadth in a large expanse of water. Depth may accompany it, but unlimited depth is not required for every bahr.

Bahr may be calm or turbulent, sweet or salty, a place of rescue or drowning. Its value is determined by context.

5. Material Image

The material image is a broad water horizon whose end is not easily seen. To cross it, the human being needs ship, route, wind, and suitable conditions.

Thus bahr carries both openness and the possibility of travel, and the danger of losing support.

6. Early Arabic Usage

In early Arabic, bahr was used for a large expanse of water and was not limited to the modern scientific idea of ocean.

A very large river or basin could be called bahr because of breadth and abundance. Salinity is not the root meaning, since the Qur’an itself speaks of a sweet bahr.

7. Misuse and Restriction

One mistake is turning bahr into a purely mystical symbol and detaching it from the natural reality of the verses.

The opposite mistake is making every mention of bahr into a theory of naval power, economy, or civilization.

ظَهَرَ الْفَسَادُ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ

Corruption on land and sea should not be limited to only one modern example such as pollution or fishing.

8. Methodological Note

Distinguish lexical meaning, natural function, rhetorical function, geographic referent, and contemporary scientific explanation. Scientific explanation should not replace the first levels.

9. Related Semitic Languages

Related forms in Semitic languages point to sea, water-expanse, or connected meanings. This shows an old relation to water and sea, but Qur’anic meaning must first come from Arabic usage and the Qur’anic context.

10. Qur’anic Uses

A. Splitting the bahr for the Children of Israel

وَإِذْ فَرَقْنَا بِكُمُ الْبَحْرَ فَأَنْجَيْنَاكُمْ وَأَغْرَقْنَا آلَ فِرْعَوْنَ وَأَنْتُمْ تَنْظُرُونَ

And when We split the sea for you, then We saved you and drowned the household of Pharaoh while you were looking on.

Al-Baqarah 2:50

Bahr is the obstacle, then the place of rescue and drowning.

B. Ships and movement in bahr

وَسَخَّرَ لَكُمُ الْفُلْكَ لِتَجْرِيَ فِي الْبَحْرِ بِأَمْرِهِ

And He made the ship serviceable for you, so that it may run in the sea by His command.

Ibrahim 14:32

C. Seeking bounty in bahr

رَبُّكُمُ الَّذِي يُزْجِي لَكُمُ الْفُلْكَ فِي الْبَحْرِ لِتَبْتَغُوا مِنْ فَضْلِهِ

Your Lord is the One who drives the ship for you in the sea so that you may seek from His bounty.

Al-Isra 17:66

D. Fresh flesh and adornment

وَهُوَ الَّذِي سَخَّرَ الْبَحْرَ لِتَأْكُلُوا مِنْهُ لَحْمًا طَرِيًّا وَتَسْتَخْرِجُوا مِنْهُ حِلْيَةً تَلْبَسُونَهَا

And He is the One who made the sea serviceable, so that you may eat from it fresh flesh and extract from it adornment that you wear.

An-Nahl 16:14

E. Wave, harm, and compelled tawhid

وَإِذَا مَسَّكُمُ الضُّرُّ فِي الْبَحْرِ ضَلَّ مَنْ تَدْعُونَ إِلَّا إِيَّاهُ

And when harm touches you in the sea, those whom you call upon vanish from your awareness, except Him.

Al-Isra 17:67

F. Waves like canopies

وَإِذَا غَشِيَهُمْ مَوْجٌ كَالظُّلَلِ دَعَوُا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ

And when a wave like canopies covers them, they call upon The God, making the deen sincere for Him.

Luqman 31:32

G. Darknesses of a deep sea

أَوْ كَظُلُمَاتٍ فِي بَحْرٍ لُجِّيٍّ يَغْشَاهُ مَوْجٌ مِنْ فَوْقِهِ مَوْجٌ مِنْ فَوْقِهِ سَحَابٌ

Or like darknesses in a deep sea, covered by a wave, above it another wave, and above it a cloud.

An-Nur 24:40

H. Corruption on land and sea

ظَهَرَ الْفَسَادُ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِي النَّاسِ

Corruption has appeared on land and sea because of what the hands of people have earned.

Ar-Rum 30:41

I. Two bahrs with different qualities

وَمَا يَسْتَوِي الْبَحْرَانِ هَٰذَا عَذْبٌ فُرَاتٌ سَائِغٌ شَرَابُهُ وَهَٰذَا مِلْحٌ أُجَاجٌ

The two bahrs are not equal: this one is sweet, pleasant, and easy to drink; and that one is salty and bitter.

Fatir 35:12

J. Maraja al-bahrayn

مَرَجَ الْبَحْرَيْنِ يَلْتَقِيَانِ
بَيْنَهُمَا بَرْزَخٌ لَا يَبْغِيَانِ

He released the two bahrs, meeting one another; between them is a barrier that they do not transgress.

Ar-Rahman 55:19-20

K. Sweet and salty water

وَهُوَ الَّذِي مَرَجَ الْبَحْرَيْنِ هَٰذَا عَذْبٌ فُرَاتٌ وَهَٰذَا مِلْحٌ أُجَاجٌ وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَهُمَا بَرْزَخًا وَحِجْرًا مَحْجُورًا

And He is the One who released the two bahrs: this one sweet and pleasant, and that one salty and bitter; and He placed between them a barzakh and a restraining barrier.

Al-Furqan 25:53

L. Seven bahrs

وَلَوْ أَنَّمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ مِنْ شَجَرَةٍ أَقْلَامٌ وَالْبَحْرُ يَمُدُّهُ مِنْ بَعْدِهِ سَبْعَةُ أَبْحُرٍ مَا نَفِدَتْ كَلِمَاتُ اللَّهِ

And if every tree on earth were pens, and the sea were extended after it by seven more seas, the words of The God would not be exhausted.

Luqman 31:27

M. Transformation of the seas

وَإِذَا الْبِحَارُ فُجِّرَتْ

And when the seas are burst open and brought into upheaval.

Al-Infitar 82:3

and:

وَإِذَا الْبِحَارُ سُجِّرَتْ

And when the seas are set aflame or filled to overflowing.

At-Takwir 81:6

N. Bahirah and pre-Qur’anic prohibition

مَا جَعَلَ اللَّهُ مِنْ بَحِيرَةٍ وَلَا سَائِبَةٍ وَلَا وَصِيلَةٍ وَلَا حَامٍ

The God has not appointed any bahirah, sa’ibah, wasilah, or ham.

Al-Ma’idah 5:103

11. Important Derivatives

Bahr may be travel, provision, danger, or sign. Bahrayn is the dual. Abhur and bihar are plurals. Bahirah is a separate pre-Qur’anic custom in Qur’anic usage.

12. Grammatical Notes

The al- in al-bahr may be contextual or generic. Fi al-bahr shows movement or presence in bahr. Min al-bahr marks source. Bahrayn expresses two; the qualities are supplied by context.

13. Compared with B R R

Barr is land; bahr is water-expanse. Land gives stable surface; bahr requires ship, swimming, and conditions.

14. Compared with Y M M

Yamm also points to a large body of water, especially in the Moses narrative. It should not be rigidly opposed to bahr as chaos versus order.

15. Compared with N H R

Nahr emphasizes watercourse and flow. Bahr emphasizes expanse and breadth.

16. Specialized Discussion

Modern geographic categories should not be imposed on every Qur’anic bahr. The word may refer to open sea, gulf, connected water, basin, or even a very large river in usage.

Al-bahrayn should not be reduced to one mechanism such as halocline. The verses speak of meeting, qualities, barzakh, and order. Meeting does not deny every natural mixing.

Subjugation means nature is placed in an order from which humans benefit, not that bahr becomes fully owned or risk-free.

17. Translation Suggestions

بَحْر / الْبَحْر

sea; large expanse of water; water-expanse where geography is unclear.

الْبَحْرَيْن

the two seas; two expanses of water.

أَبْحُر

seas; several seas or water-expanses, not endless expanses.

بَحِيرَة

bahirah, with explanation of the pre-Qur’anic marked she-camel.

18. Conclusion

B H R in the Qur’an is connected above all with a broad expanse of water: travel, food, adornment, trade, wave, darkness, storm, and drowning.

Its core is breadth and openness. Depth may be a feature, but it is not necessary in every use.

Bahr is neither an independent chaotic force outside the command of The God nor a harmless substance fully owned by human beings. It is opened within creation for benefit while still exposing human limitation and need.
