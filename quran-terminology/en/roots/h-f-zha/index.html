1. The root and its semantic field

The triliteral root Ḥ F Ẓ concerns keeping something, caring for it, and preventing its loss, being forgotten, or suffering harm. Its different uses include guarding a person or property, remaining faithful to a covenant, observing limits, retaining speech in memory, and maintaining care over an activity.

Their shared core is care for something that should remain or whose due should be respected. Sometimes this prevents an external danger, as with a child or property. Sometimes it concerns the mind, as with retaining words in memory. Sometimes it concerns a responsibility that must not be abandoned or violated, such as a covenant, oath, or divine limit.

The Qur’an uses the root of The God and angels, as well as prophets, ordinary people, and their ethical and devotional duties. It also describes a record, the heaven, and what is under protection.

Qur’anic usage cannot therefore always be translated simply as physical protection or memorization. Its meaning depends on the object kept, the keeper’s responsibility, and the sentence’s construction.

2. Semantic core and principal forms

The fundamental meaning: attending to and keeping

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris identifies one semantic foundation:

الْحَاءُ وَالْفَاءُ وَالظَّاءُ أَصْلٌ وَاحِدٌ يَدُلُّ عَلَى مُرَاعَاةِ الشَّيْءِ

al-ḥāʾu wa-al-fāʾu wa-al-ẓāʾu aṣlun wāḥidun yadullu ʿalā murāʿāti al-shayʾi

“The consonants ḥāʾ, fāʾ, and ẓāʾ have one foundation: attending to and caring for something.”

Murāʿāh matters here: keeping is not confined to the final result of something remaining. Care, attention, and observance also belong to its semantic field.

Lisān al-ʿArab likewise explains the root through keeping, care, remembering, and avoiding neglect. In memory it stands opposite forgetting; with people and property it concerns guarding and care.

Maqāyīs al-Lughah: Ḥ F Ẓ and attentive care

One meaning thus operates in different settings: preserving wealth from loss, speech from forgetting, a covenant from violation, and a person from danger.

These are different applications, rather than meanings that must all be simultaneously present whenever ḥafiẓa occurs.

Principal Arabic and Qur’anic forms

Ḥafiẓa yaḥfaẓu is the form I verb: kept, cared for, guarded, or remembered. It occurs in the Qur’an.

Ḥifẓ is the verbal noun concerning keeping and care. Qur’anic examples include ḥifẓuhumā and wa-ḥifẓan.

Ḥāfiẓ is the form I active participle, a keeper, guardian, or one who attends to something. Its masculine and feminine plurals also occur in the Qur’an.

Ḥafīẓ follows the faʿīl pattern and describes someone or something associated with keeping and care. It may convey firmness or breadth of that quality, but intensity cannot be inferred in every use from the pattern alone. The Qur’an applies it to The God, a human being, and a record.

Ḥafaẓah is a plural for keepers or guardians. It occurs in Al-Anʿām 6:61.

Maḥfūẓ is the passive participle: something kept or protected. The Qur’an uses it of the heaven and a tablet.

Ḥāfaẓa yuḥāfiẓu is form III. Its Qur’anic use concerns continued care and observance of ṣalāh.

Istaḥfaẓa is form X. The Qur’anic passive ustuḥfiẓū describes people entrusted with keeping something.

Arabic also has forms such as ḥaffaẓa, taḥaffaẓa, iḥtafaẓa, ḥafīẓah, and ḥifāẓ, which do not occur in the Qur’an.

The morphological concordance records 44 occurrences in 42 verses, falling into eight morphological groups.

Qur’anic morphology: 44 occurrences in 42 verses

The material image and relationships among meanings

Keeping property or entrusting a child to a carer provides a useful material image.

Someone entrusted with property does more than possess it: they should prevent its disappearance, destruction, or wrongful use. Likewise, having a child in one’s charge does not itself discharge the duty of care.

Keeping therefore involves attention, care, and preventing loss.

It need not always oppose an enemy or immediate danger. Without any visible threat, someone may keep a covenant, remain committed to a responsibility, or preserve learning from forgetfulness.

The word is therefore broader than protection against attack. Its object may be a living being, object, statement, right, covenant, or practice.

In memory, keeping concerns knowledge remaining available in the mind and capable of recall. Socially, it concerns respecting a right or responsibility rather than neglecting it. These uses are related, but each requires its own context.

3. Early Arabic and the relationship with social responsibility

Keeping property, speech, and covenants

Classical Arabic uses ḥafiẓa al-māl for keeping property and ḥafiẓa al-sirr for keeping a secret.

The first prevents property from being lost; the second withholds disclosure of something meant to remain hidden.

Ḥafiẓa al-ʿahd means observing a covenant and remaining faithful to it. Remembering its terms is insufficient: avoiding its violation also matters.

Care and trustworthiness are thus related, although the word alone identifies neither the owner nor the kind of agreement involved.

Ḥafīẓah: anger when an inviolable claim is challenged

Ḥafīẓah is a significant branch in early Arabic.

It describes protective zeal, sensitivity, or anger aroused when someone’s dignity, family, or covenant is challenged. Its relationship to keeping lies in responding to an affront against something considered worthy of protection.

A poem attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Zuhayr ibn Abī Sulmā says:

أَبْلِغْ بَنِي نَوْفَلٍ عَنِّي فَقَدْ بَلَغَتْ
مِنِّي الْحَفِيظَةُ لَمَّا جَاءَنِي الْخَبَرُ

abligh banī Nawfalin ʿannī fa-qad balaghat / minnī al-ḥafīẓatu lammā jāʾanī al-khabaru

“Convey my message to Banū Nawfal: when the news reached me, my anger and protective zeal were stirred.”

Ḥafīẓah here is not the act of keeping itself, but the feeling aroused over something the poet considers a matter of his dignity or right. The poem is transmitted in Zuhayr’s collected verse. The opening is not uniform across transmissions: fa-qad balaghū also occurs. This article retains the supplied balaghat reading.

Zuhayr’s collected poems: ḥafīẓah and variants in the opening

This must be distinguished from Qur’anic ḥafīẓ. Here ḥafīẓah concerns aroused anger or sensitivity; ḥafīẓ concerns a keeper, guardian, or a quality connected with keeping.

Protective anger is not necessarily morally justified. Someone may defend a right or support wrongdoing out of familial or group prejudice. The reaction’s moral value requires assessment independently of the root’s meaning.

Maintaining care and observance

Recorded Arabic uses of muḥāfaẓah involve continued observance, care, defending inviolable claims, and faithfulness to commitments.

Ḥāfaẓa ʿalā al-shayʾ can therefore express sustained care or commitment toward something.

The preposition ʿalā connects the object of care with the verb, as in attending to a covenant or maintaining a practice.

Form III does not by itself require two parties mutually protecting each other. In ḥāfiẓū ʿalā al-ṣalawāt, people are commanded to maintain ṣalāh and avoid neglecting it. Reciprocity should not be imposed solely from the verbal pattern.

4. Qur’anic uses

A. Divine keeping: preservation, encompassing knowledge, and care

Divine keeping is one of the root’s broadest Qur’anic applications.

The well-known verse in Al-Baqarah says:

اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ ۚ لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۗ مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِنْدَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ ۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِنْ عِلْمِهِ إِلَّا بِمَا شَاءَ ۚ وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ ۖ وَلَا يَئُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْعَظِيمُ

Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa al-ḥayyu al-qayyūmu lā taʾkhudhuhu sinatun wa-lā nawmun lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍi man dhā alladhī yashfaʿu ʿindahu illā bi-idhnihi yaʿlamu mā bayna aydīhim wa-mā khalfahum wa-lā yuḥīṭūna bi-shayʾin min ʿilmihi illā bi-mā shāʾa wasiʿa kursiyyuhu al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍa wa-lā yaʾūduhu ḥifẓuhumā wa-huwa al-ʿaliyyu al-ʿaẓīmu

“The God: no deity exists except Him, the Living, the Sustainer. Neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes Him. Whatever is in the heavens and earth belongs to Him. Who can intercede with Him except by His permission? He knows what is before them and behind them, and they encompass nothing of His knowledge except what He wills. His kursī encompasses the heavens and earth, and keeping the two does not burden Him. He is the Exalted, the Great.”

Al-Baqarah 2:255

Ḥifẓuhumā concerns keeping the heavens and earth. The dual pronoun humā refers back to those two.

Yaʾūduhu concerns something burdensome or difficult. The verse states that keeping the heavens and earth does not burden The God.

Keeping here relates to divine power and encompassing knowledge. The God is Living and Sustainer; neither sleep nor drowsiness overtakes Him, and His knowledge encompasses what lies before and behind created beings.

This should not be imagined as a human guard standing somewhere, keeping watch, and struggling against fatigue to protect an object.

Nor can ḥifẓuhumā be reduced to remembering the heavens and earth. Its context concerns keeping them through divine power and sustaining authority, rather than merely retaining them in memory.

Hūd 11:57 says inna rabbī ʿalā kulli shayʾin ḥafīẓ. Sabaʾ 34:21 expresses the same breadth through wa-rabbuka ʿalā kulli shayʾin ḥafīẓ.

Divine keeping is thus not confined to one person or possession. That does not mean no created being suffers harm or any human being escapes suffering and death. Death, harm, trials, and the consequences of conduct are also Qur’anic realities.

Divine keeping relates to sovereignty, knowledge, and governance; the word is not a guarantee against every unwelcome event.

B. Ḥafīẓ and ḥāfiẓ concerning The God

The Qur’an uses both ḥafīẓ and ḥāfiẓ concerning The God.

Al-Shūrā says:

وَالَّذِينَ اتَّخَذُوا مِنْ دُونِهِ أَوْلِيَاءَ اللَّهُ حَفِيظٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَمَا أَنْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ بِوَكِيلٍ

wa-alladhīna ittakhadhū min dūnihi awliyāʾa Allāhu ḥafīẓun ʿalayhim wa-mā anta ʿalayhim bi-wakīlin

“Those who have taken other patrons besides Him: The God watches over them, and you are not their entrusted overseer.”

Al-Shūrā 42:6

Ḥafīẓun ʿalayhim here concerns those who take patrons besides The God.

It therefore goes beyond affectionate protection from danger. Divine keeping can include oversight, encompassing awareness, and preserving people’s conduct for reckoning.

ʿAlā here relates to oversight and encompassing authority. Ḥafīẓ is not itself a word for punishment or condemnation: judgments on conduct and consequences arise from the context and other verses.

Yūsuf uses another form:

قَالَ هَلْ آمَنُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ إِلَّا كَمَا أَمِنْتُكُمْ عَلَىٰ أَخِيهِ مِنْ قَبْلُ ۖ فَاللَّهُ خَيْرٌ حَافِظًا ۖ وَهُوَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ

qāla hal āmanukum ʿalayhi illā ka-mā amintukum ʿalā akhīhi min qablu fa-Allāhu khayrun ḥāfiẓan wa-huwa arḥamu al-rāḥimīna

“He said: Shall I entrust him to you except as I entrusted his brother to you before? The God is the best keeper, and He is the most merciful of those who show mercy.”

Yūsuf 12:64

Jacob is discussing entrusting another son to his brothers.

Ḥāfiẓan describes The God in a setting of keeping and caring for a person potentially exposed to danger.

The divine description can thus concern protecting people, while Al-Shūrā 42:6 also presents encompassing oversight.

These are not wholly unrelated meanings. Keeping and care remain common, while their object and sentence position establish the direction of the sense.

The Qur’anic form matters: the text uses the indefinite ḥafīẓ describing The God. Adding al- to form al-Ḥafīẓ is familiar in the naming tradition, but a report of Qur’anic wording should not silently replace the indefinite adjective with a definite name.

Ḥafīẓ is a genuine divine description. Its scope must be understood from the verses together, rather than solely from the faʿīl pattern or later lists of divine names.

C. Human keeping: trust and the responsibility of care

The story of Joseph offers particularly clear human uses.

In Yūsuf 12:12, Joseph’s brothers assure their father that they will guard him. Yūsuf 12:63 repeats the promise about his other brother, and Yūsuf 12:65 speaks of keeping their brother safe.

The object is someone accompanying them. Keeping involves responsibility for care during travel and safeguarding what has been entrusted.

A promise of care does not establish its fulfillment. Earlier in the story, the brothers promise to guard Joseph but subsequently act otherwise.

Calling someone ḥāfiẓ therefore does not by itself prove honesty or successful performance.

In Yūsuf 12:81, the brothers say that they testified only to what they knew and were not keepers of the unseen. This has a different significance.

Keeping here concerns matters beyond their knowledge. A person cannot possess complete awareness and care over every hidden or unknown matter.

Human keeping is limited by capacity, knowledge, and authority. Divine keeping does not share those limitations in the same way.

Joseph’s description: ḥafīẓun ʿalīm

Yūsuf says:

قَالَ اجْعَلْنِي عَلَىٰ خَزَائِنِ الْأَرْضِ ۖ إِنِّي حَفِيظٌ عَلِيمٌ

qāla ijʿalnī ʿalā khazāʾini al-arḍi innī ḥafīẓun ʿalīmun

“He said: Appoint me over the storehouses of the land; I am a knowledgeable keeper.”

Yūsuf 12:55

Joseph offers himself for responsibility over the land’s storehouses, describing himself as both ḥafīẓ and ʿalīm.

Ḥafīẓ concerns keeping, care, and preventing the loss of what is entrusted. ʿAlīm concerns knowledge and awareness.

Their combination matters. Managing the stores requires more than knowing how to administer them: trustworthiness and preventing loss also matter. Conversely, trustworthiness without the knowledge and competence needed for the task is insufficient.

The pattern ḥafīẓ does not establish unlimited knowledge of the world’s entire economy. Joseph is requesting a particular responsibility from the ruler.

The verse also shows that ḥafīẓ is not exclusively divine. A person may bear the description within the limits of their capacity and responsibility.

Divine and human keeping differ in the scope of power, knowledge, and independence, rather than having wholly unrelated meanings.

D. Ustuḥfiẓū: entrusted with keeping the Book

Al-Māʾidah says:

إِنَّا أَنْزَلْنَا التَّوْرَاةَ فِيهَا هُدًى وَنُورٌ ۚ يَحْكُمُ بِهَا النَّبِيُّونَ الَّذِينَ أَسْلَمُوا لِلَّذِينَ هَادُوا وَالرَّبَّانِيُّونَ وَالْأَحْبَارُ بِمَا اسْتُحْفِظُوا مِنْ كِتَابِ اللَّهِ وَكَانُوا عَلَيْهِ شُهَدَاءَ ۚ فَلَا تَخْشَوُا النَّاسَ وَاخْشَوْنِ وَلَا تَشْتَرُوا بِآيَاتِي ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا ۚ وَمَنْ لَمْ يَحْكُمْ بِمَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْكَافِرُونَ

innā anzalnā al-tawrāta fīhā hudan wa-nūrun yaḥkumu bihā al-nabiyyūna alladhīna aslamū li-alladhīna hādū wa-al-rabbāniyyūna wa-al-aḥbāru bi-mā ustuḥfiẓū min kitābi Allāhi wa-kānū ʿalayhi shuhadāʾa fa-lā takhshawū al-nāsa wa-ikhshawnī wa-lā tashtarū bi-āyātī thamanan qalīlan wa-man lam yaḥkum bi-mā anzala Allāhu fa-ulāʾika humu al-kāfirūna

“We sent down the Torah, containing guidance and light. The prophets who submitted judged by it for those who were Jews, as did the learned religious leaders and scholars, through what they had been entrusted to keep of the Book of The God, and they were witnesses over it. Do not fear people, but fear Me, and do not exchange My signs for a small price. Whoever does not judge by what The God has sent down: they are the disbelievers.”

Al-Māʾidah 5:44

Ustuḥfiẓū is the form X passive perfect.

It expresses responsibility for keeping entrusted to people who speak about the Book of The God and judge by it.

This differs from merely possessing a copy. Keeping the Book in this setting concerns responsibility toward what The God has revealed.

The continuation matters: not fearing people and not selling divine signs for a small price belong to the same context.

Keeping the Book is therefore linked with intellectual and ethical responsibility. Those entrusted with it should remain faithful to its truth.

The verb does not establish that everyone entrusted with this responsibility fulfilled it correctly. The passive expresses entrustment; complete and correct fulfillment is another matter.

The verse concerns the Torah. This verb alone does not establish a general ruling about the historical preservation or alteration of every scripture. Such study requires independent examination of the relevant passages on scripture, distortion, concealment, and revelation’s preservation.

E. Keeping the Reminder and the Book: divine and human care

Al-Ḥijr says:

إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا الذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ

innā naḥnu nazzalnā al-dhikra wa-innā lahu la-ḥāfiẓūna

“We Ourselves sent down the Reminder, and We are indeed its keepers.”

Al-Ḥijr 15:9

Both sending down and keeping the Reminder are attributed to The God.

The independent naḥnu after innā and the emphatic construction innā lahu la-ḥāfiẓūn emphasize the attribution of keeping to The God.

Lahu refers to the Reminder, identifying the object of keeping.

The verse can be compared with Al-Māʾidah 5:44, but their constructions differ.

Al-Māʾidah describes humans entrusted with keeping something of the Book of The God. Al-Ḥijr attributes keeping the Reminder to The God Himself.

This matters, within the limits of what the wording establishes. The root alone does not explain every historical stage or mechanism of divine keeping, nor the details of manuscripts, readings, and transmission.

The verse explicitly affirms divine keeping of the Reminder. The scope of al-dhikr, its relationship with the Qur’an, and how that keeping occurs require contextual and wider Qur’anic examination.

A preserving record and a guarded tablet

Qāf says:

قَدْ عَلِمْنَا مَا تَنْقُصُ الْأَرْضُ مِنْهُمْ ۖ وَعِنْدَنَا كِتَابٌ حَفِيظٌ

qad ʿalimnā mā tanquṣu al-arḍu minhum wa-ʿindanā kitābun ḥafīẓun

“We know what the earth diminishes of them, and with Us is a preserving record.”

Qāf 50:4

Ḥafīẓ here qualifies the record, rather than The God or a human being.

The surrounding verses concern human return after death. After knowledge of what the earth diminishes of people, a ḥafīẓ record is mentioned.

Keeping can thus concern recording and retaining information. The phrase may describe a record whose contents do not disappear, or a record that keeps account of matters.

Al-Ṭabarī: a record preserving its contents without alteration

Al-Burūj also says:

فِي لَوْحٍ مَحْفُوظٍ

fī lawḥin maḥfūẓin

“In a guarded tablet.”

Al-Burūj 85:22

This continues Al-Burūj 85:21, which speaks of a glorious Qur’an.

The forms must remain distinct. Ḥafīẓ follows faʿīl and qualifies a record in Qāf 50:4, whereas maḥfūẓ is a passive participle denoting something under preservation.

Their resulting senses may approach one another, but their morphology differs.

Nor should “a guarded tablet” immediately become a material object of a specified size, substance, and location. The verse does not explain those details.

These uses concern recorded content remaining and not being lost, without necessarily describing protection against physical attack.

F. Keeping the heaven and protecting against intrusion

Al-Anbiyāʾ says:

وَجَعَلْنَا السَّمَاءَ سَقْفًا مَحْفُوظًا ۖ وَهُمْ عَنْ آيَاتِهَا مُعْرِضُونَ

wa-jaʿalnā al-samāʾa saqfan maḥfūẓan wa-hum ʿan āyātihā muʿriḍūna

“We made the heaven a guarded roof, yet they turn away from its signs.”

Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:32

Maḥfūẓan qualifies the heaven.

It presents the heaven as kept or protected, but does not by itself establish a particular physical mechanism such as a specific atmospheric layer, magnetic field, or biological protection.

Scientific knowledge of the heavens and their role in human life may prompt reflection, but that application does not follow directly from maḥfūẓ’s lexical meaning.

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:7 and Fuṣṣilat 41:12 also use ḥifẓan concerning the heaven.

Al-Ṣāffāt concerns keeping rebellious devils away. Fuṣṣilat places adorning the nearest heaven with lamps alongside preservation.

In Al-Ḥijr 15:17, The God also speaks of keeping the heaven against an expelled devil.

The same subject can thus be described through different forms: an adjective, verb, or verbal noun.

Their association does not make every detail of one particular account of devils, fiery streaks, or heavenly mechanisms part of the word maḥfūẓ whenever it qualifies the heaven.

G. Keeping by angels: care and recording deeds

Al-Anʿām says:

وَهُوَ الْقَاهِرُ فَوْقَ عِبَادِهِ وَيُرْسِلُ عَلَيْكُمْ حَفَظَةً ۖ حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ تَوَفَّتْهُ رُسُلُنَا وَهُمْ لَا يُفَرِّطُونَ

wa-huwa al-qāhiru fawqa ʿibādihi wa-yursilu ʿalaykum ḥafaẓatan ḥattā idhā jāʾa aḥadakumu al-mawtu tawaffathu rusulunā wa-hum lā yufarriṭūna

“He is the Subduer above His servants and sends guardians over you. When death comes to one of you, Our messengers take him in death, without neglecting their task.”

Al-Anʿām 6:61

Ḥafaẓah denotes keepers or guardians, sent by The God in this context.

The verse concerns keeping and care, without alone explaining every detail of their duties.

Al-Infiṭār uses another form:

وَإِنَّ عَلَيْكُمْ لَحَافِظِينَ

wa-inna ʿalaykum la-ḥāfiẓīna

“Over you are indeed keepers.”

Al-Infiṭār 82:10

Al-Infiṭār 82:11 describes these keepers as kirāman kātibīn, honorable recorders.

Keeping here therefore also concerns recording and preserving deeds.

Qāf 50:17 and Qāf 50:18 likewise concern receiving and recording human words and conduct, although neither verse itself contains Ḥ F Ẓ.

Al-Ṭāriq says:

إِنْ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ لَمَّا عَلَيْهَا حَافِظٌ

in kullu nafsin lammā ʿalayhā ḥāfiẓun

“There is no soul without a keeper over it.”

Al-Ṭāriq 86:4

Ḥāfiẓ is assigned over every soul. The verse itself does not identify that keeper or specify every duty.

Other verses can inform the investigation, but one noun alone cannot establish a complete account of the unseen world, numbers of guardians, or recording mechanisms.

Al-Raʿd 13:11 describes muʿaqqibāt connected with a person from before and behind, using yaḥfaẓūnahu. Min amri Allāh requires careful contextual examination of the preposition’s relationship with keeping and the divine command.

Keeping and care remain fundamental throughout, but looking after a child, protecting the heaven, and recording deeds are not identical activities.

H. Ḥafīẓ and the limits of a prophet’s responsibility

Several verses deny that the prophet has the position of ḥafīẓ over people.

Al-Nisāʾ 4:80 discusses obedience and turning away, stating that the messenger was not sent as a keeper over those who turn away.

In Al-Anʿām 6:104, after insights from the Lord are mentioned, the messenger says wa-mā anā ʿalaykum bi-ḥafīẓ.

Al-Anʿām 6:107 likewise states that The God has not appointed him their keeper.

In Hūd 11:86, Shuʿayb tells his people that he is not their keeper.

Al-Shūrā 42:48 tells the messenger that if people turn away, he was not sent as their keeper.

Delivering the divine message is therefore distinct from having complete control over people’s choices, belief, and inward conduct.

Ḥafīẓ here concerns comprehensive oversight and responsibility, rather than giving prophets ownership of their addressees’ wills.

This does not deny every responsibility to teach, convey, warn, or judge in settings where the Qur’an separately assigns such duties.

Denying one kind of authority or responsibility does not deny them all.

Al-Muṭaffifīn 83:33 offers another significant use. After describing wrongdoers mocking believers, it says they had not been sent as keepers over the believers.

The issue is again lacking an appointed position of oversight over others.

Ḥāfiẓ therefore extends beyond affectionate protection: it can denote an appointed overseer, without necessarily someone defending a person against an enemy.

I. Maintaining ṣalāh: observance and continuity

A principal Qur’anic application of form III concerns ṣalāh.

حَافِظُوا عَلَى الصَّلَوَاتِ وَالصَّلَاةِ الْوُسْطَىٰ وَقُومُوا لِلَّهِ قَانِتِينَ

ḥāfiẓū ʿalā al-ṣalawāti wa-al-ṣalāti al-wusṭā wa-qūmū li-Allāhi qānitīna

“Maintain the prayers and the middle prayer, and stand before The God in devoted obedience.”

Al-Baqarah 2:238

Ḥāfiẓū is a form III imperative followed by ʿalā.

It calls for care and observance so that ṣalāh is not neglected or abandoned.

Al-Anʿām 6:92 describes believers in the hereafter as maintaining their ṣalāh. Al-Muʾminūn 23:9 concerns maintaining the prayers; Al-Maʿārij 70:34 repeats this quality.

The repeated construction presents maintaining ṣalāh as a significant, sustained quality.

Ḥāfiẓū nevertheless does not simply mean perform the prayer. It emphasizes keeping, observing, and avoiding neglect of it.

Ṣalāh is a separate subject whose timing, structure, and purpose require the relevant verses together. Ḥ F Ẓ does not contain all those details.

Nor does form III establish mutual protection by two parties here. Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:45 separately describes ṣalāh’s restraining effect, but that effect is not part of ḥāfiẓū’s morphological meaning.

Keeping here concerns sustained, responsible observance of an activity that should not be forgotten or neglected.

J. Keeping limits, oaths, and ethical responsibilities

Al-Tawbah says:

التَّائِبُونَ الْعَابِدُونَ الْحَامِدُونَ السَّائِحُونَ الرَّاكِعُونَ السَّاجِدُونَ الْآمِرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَالنَّاهُونَ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ وَالْحَافِظُونَ لِحُدُودِ اللَّهِ ۗ وَبَشِّرِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ

al-tāʾibūna al-ʿābidūna al-ḥāmidūna al-sāʾiḥūna al-rākiʿūna al-sājidūna al-āmirūna bi-al-maʿrūfi wa-al-nāhūna ʿani al-munkari wa-al-ḥāfiẓūna li-ḥudūdi Allāhi wa-bashshiri al-muʾminīna

“Those who repent, worship, praise, travel, bow, prostrate, command what is right, forbid what is wrong, and keep the limits of The God—and give good news to the believers.”

Al-Tawbah 9:112

Al-ḥāfiẓūna li-ḥudūdi Allāh are those who observe and safeguard divine limits.

Ḥudūd belongs to another root and concerns established boundaries or limits. Keeping here means observing them rather than allowing their violation.

This differs from keeping physical property. The object is limits to be respected in conduct.

Keeping limits should not be reduced to imposing punishments or implementing a particular legal system. Qur’anic divine limits have a wider range, including boundaries in relationships, rulings, and responsibilities.

Al-Māʾidah 5:89 says:

لَا يُؤَاخِذُكُمُ اللَّهُ بِاللَّغْوِ فِي أَيْمَانِكُمْ وَلَٰكِنْ يُؤَاخِذُكُمْ بِمَا عَقَّدْتُمُ الْأَيْمَانَ ۖ فَكَفَّارَتُهُ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ أَوْ كِسْوَتُهُمْ أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ ۖ فَمَنْ لَمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ كَفَّارَةُ أَيْمَانِكُمْ إِذَا حَلَفْتُمْ ۚ وَاحْفَظُوا أَيْمَانَكُمْ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ آيَاتِهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

lā yuʾākhidhukumu Allāhu bi-al-laghwi fī aymānikum wa-lākin yuʾākhidhukum bi-mā ʿaqqadtumu al-aymāna fa-kaffāratuhu iṭʿāmu ʿasharati masākīna min awsaṭi mā tuṭʿimūna ahlīkum aw kiswatuhum aw taḥrīru raqabatin fa-man lam yajid fa-ṣiyāmu thalāthati ayyāmin dhālika kaffāratu aymānikum idhā ḥalaftum wa-iḥfaẓū aymānakum kadhālika yubayyinu Allāhu lakum āyātihi laʿallakum tashkurūna

“The God does not hold you accountable for inadvertent oaths, but for the oaths you have firmly bound. Their expiation is feeding ten needy people from the average food you give your families, or clothing them, or freeing a person from bondage. Whoever cannot find the means must fast three days. That is the expiation for your oaths when you swear. Keep your oaths. The God thus makes His signs clear to you, so that you may give thanks.”

Al-Māʾidah 5:89

Wa-iḥfaẓū aymānakum concerns human oaths and the responsibility they create.

Keeping an oath is more than retaining it in memory: it involves respecting its responsibility and avoiding treating it lightly or neglecting it.

The precise ruling, conditions of a binding oath, and expiation come from the full verse and related passages. The root alone cannot specify all oath rulings.

Keeping limits and oaths thus demonstrate legal and ethical uses, beyond physical or material protection.

K. Guarding the private parts: safeguarding sexual boundaries

Al-Muʾminūn says:

وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ لِفُرُوجِهِمْ حَافِظُونَ

wa-alladhīna hum li-furūjihim ḥāfiẓūna

“Those who guard their private parts.”

Al-Muʾminūn 23:5

The object is furūj, and the following context concerns sexual relationships and their boundaries.

Keeping here therefore involves safeguarding sexual boundaries and avoiding transgression of the relevant limits.

The same construction occurs in Al-Maʿārij 70:29.

Al-Nūr 24:30 and Al-Nūr 24:31 command believing men and women to lower their gaze and guard their private parts.

Guarding and lowering the gaze occur together but are different actions.

Ghaḍḍ concerns lowering or reducing, while ḥifẓ concerns keeping and guarding. Together they concern conduct and respect for boundaries.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:35 likewise includes men and women who guard their private parts among the commended groups.

Chastity and sexual care arise from these contexts. The root itself should not be restricted to sexual chastity, since it also concerns records, wealth, ṣalāh, and other subjects.

Ḥāfiẓātun li-al-ghayb in Al-Nisāʾ 4:34

Al-Nisāʾ 4:34 describes upright women as ḥāfiẓātun li-al-ghaybi bi-mā ḥafiẓa Allāh.

Several elements need separate examination.

Ḥāfiẓāt is a feminine plural active participle, concerning keeping and observance.

Li-al-ghayb identifies a domain of absence, hiddenness, or what lies beyond another person’s view.

Bi-mā ḥafiẓa Allāh relates this keeping to what The God has kept. The precise reference of mā and function of bi- require examination of the context and grammatical possibilities.

Keeping should not be restricted without argument to a husband’s property, sexual chastity, or obedience to another person, although these may arise in contextual interpretation.

Conversely, the family setting should not be ignored by turning the phrase into a purely abstract ethical principle.

Ḥāfiẓātun li-al-ghayb concerns keeping something in absence or hiddenness. Identifying what is to be kept requires examination beyond the root’s basic meaning.

L. A human ḥafīẓ: remembrance and commitment

Qāf says:

هَٰذَا مَا تُوعَدُونَ لِكُلِّ أَوَّابٍ حَفِيظٍ

hādhā mā tūʿadūna li-kulli awwābin ḥafīẓin

“This is what you are promised, for every returning, observant person.”

Qāf 50:32

Ḥafīẓ describes a human being alongside awwāb.

Awwāb belongs to ʾ W B and denotes returning. Ḥafīẓ here describes someone who keeps and observes what is entrusted.

Qāf 50:33 concerns reverence toward the Entirely Merciful in the unseen and coming with a heart turned toward Him.

Keeping here thus relates to human responsibility and orientation toward The God.

Ḥafīẓ alone does not mean repentant, reverent, or possessing a returning heart. The verses express those qualities through separate words.

This is not physical protection of an object, but observing what a person should keep before The God.

5. Differences among Qur’anic forms and syntactic points

Ḥāfiẓ and ḥafīẓ

Ḥāfiẓ is an active participle describing someone who keeps or possesses the quality of keeping.

Ḥafīẓ follows faʿīl and may express firm keeping, broad oversight, or an established quality.

This does not make ḥāfiẓ always weak or ḥafīẓ always complete and incapable of error.

Joseph describes himself as ḥafīẓun ʿalīm, while Jacob describes The God as khayrun ḥāfiẓan.

If ḥafīẓ necessarily meant unlimited, perfect keeping and ḥāfiẓ only limited keeping, those uses would not fit the distinction.

The difference lies in form and usage, without establishing two universal ranks of keeping.

Ḥafaẓah and ḥāfiẓīn

Ḥafaẓah in Al-Anʿām 6:61 refers to a group of keepers.

Ḥāfiẓīn is the sound masculine plural of ḥāfiẓ, appearing in several contexts, including keepers of deeds.

The plural forms do not by themselves establish bodily protectors as distinct from recorders of actions.

Their responsibility must be identified from the verse and its indications.

Maḥfūẓ and ḥafīẓ

Maḥfūẓ is a passive participle, something kept or preserved.

Ḥafīẓ may describe a keeper or something in which keeping is realized. Qāf 50:4 describes a ḥafīẓ record; Al-Burūj 85:22 describes a maḥfūẓ tablet.

The formal difference should remain visible.

Rendering kitāb ḥafīẓ as a preserved record may convey the resulting sense while weakening an active reading of the description. Lexicographers nevertheless also recognize preserved and not subject to loss as a meaning here. The passive rendering cannot be rejected solely because the word follows faʿīl.

“A preserving record,” or retaining ḥafīẓ with explanation, can preserve the active interpretation. This is one of two lexical explanations and does not exclude the recorded passive sense.

Primary lexicons: ḥifẓ, ḥafīẓ, and maḥfūẓ

Ḥāfaẓa ʿalā and the preposition

Qur’anic form III occurs with ʿalā.

The construction recurs in commands to maintain ṣalāh and descriptions of people maintaining their prayers.

Form I can instead take a direct object, as in keeping a brother, guarding private parts, or protecting the heaven.

This does not create an absolute rule that form I is always bodily protection and form III always worship.

Form I also has ethical Qur’anic uses; form III has Arabic uses beyond ṣalāh.

Within the Qur’anic occurrences, however, form III’s application to maintaining ṣalāh is distinctive and should be reflected in translation.

Ustuḥfiẓū and entrusted responsibility

Ustuḥfiẓū is the form X passive.

Al-Māʾidah 5:44 uses it for being entrusted to keep the Book.

Compared with ḥafiẓū, they kept, it emphasizes being placed in the position of keeping something.

The pattern alone, without context, does not specify the agreement, kind of trust, or successful performance.

6. Distinctions from related roots

Ḥ F Ẓ and W Q Y: keeping and preventing harm

W Q Y concerns protection, shielding, and placing a barrier between oneself and danger.

In taqwā, this image relates to mindfulness and care against harmful ethical and spiritual consequences.

Ḥifẓ is broader. Its object may face no immediate danger, as with keeping a covenant or retaining words in memory.

Wiqāyah tends to emphasize preventing and distancing harm; ḥifẓ emphasizes retaining, observing, and preventing neglect or loss.

Their uses nevertheless overlap in many situations.

Study of W Q Y

Ḥ F Ẓ and R ʿ Y: keeping and tending

R ʿ Y concerns tending a flock or caring for something. Observing rights and attending to duties extend from that field.

It is close to keeping: Ibn Fāris himself uses murāʿāt al-shayʾ to explain Ḥ F Ẓ.

Raʿy may emphasize tending to the needs of something under one’s care; ḥifẓ emphasizes retaining it and preventing loss.

This is a semantic tendency, rather than a rigid boundary.

Study of R ʿ Y

Ḥ F Ẓ and Ḥ R S: keeping and guarding

Ḥ R S concerns keeping watch against danger.

Al-Jinn 72:8 uses ḥarasan for strong guarding of the heaven.

Ḥifẓ can include that field but is not confined to it. Keeping an oath or maintaining ṣalāh requires no physical guard.

Ḥaras helps explain one kind of keeping, without reducing all Ḥ F Ẓ uses to watching against intrusion or attack.

Study of Ḥ R S

Ḥ F Ẓ and W K L: care and entrusted authority

Wakīl concerns entrusting affairs, undertaking responsibility, and relying on someone.

In passages such as Al-Anʿām 6:107, denying the messenger’s position as ḥafīẓ accompanies denying his position as wakīl.

Their association indicates proximity without complete identity.

Ḥafīẓ can denote someone keeping or watching; wakīl someone entrusted with or responsible for an affair.

One person may perform both roles, but the words do not have one lexical meaning.

Study of W K L

Ḥ F Ẓ and DH K R: retaining and remembering

Keeping words or knowledge in memory is a familiar Arabic use.

It relates to dhikr because recalling knowledge depends on its retention.

Keeping and remembering nevertheless differ.

Someone may have memorized something without attending to it at a particular moment. Dhikr can concern recalling, mentioning, or bringing a truth to mind.

Qur’anic dhikr is also broader than recalling information, including revelation and divine reminders.

Keeping the Reminder should therefore not automatically become memorizing words, nor should memorization be excluded from keeping’s lexical field.

Study of DH K R

Ḥ F Ẓ and ʿ H D: keeping a responsibility and the covenant itself

ʿAhd concerns a covenant, instruction, or responsibility between parties or before The God.

Keeping can concern a covenant but is not itself the covenant.

ʿAhd identifies the commitment; ḥifẓ concerns the way it is treated.

Someone may establish a covenant and keep it, or neglect and violate it.

Keeping a covenant therefore means observing and remaining faithful to it, without restricting the root to covenants alone.

Study of ʿ H D

7. Challenges of interpretation and translation

Does keeping always mean preventing harm?

No. Preventing harm is a principal use but does not exhaust the field.

With property or a person, preventing harm or loss may be prominent.

With oaths and limits, observing responsibility matters.

With speech, retention in memory is at issue.

With a ḥafīẓ record, recorded information is kept from being lost.

“Protection” therefore fits many uses but not all.

Does keeping always mean memorization?

No. Retaining content in memory is one recognized use.

In contemporary usage, memorizing a text ordinarily means being able to reproduce it from memory.

Qur’anic keeping of the heaven, maintaining ṣalāh, guarding private parts, and observing divine limits demonstrate a much broader range.

This does not prohibit ordinary talk of memorizing the Qur’an or speech. That particular sense should simply not be imposed on every verse.

Does being ḥafīẓ mean having authority over others?

Not necessarily.

Some uses involve care and oversight, but the word itself does not establish ownership or absolute authority over another person’s will.

Passages concerning prophets distinguish delivering the message from being a keeper over people’s choices.

Conversely, human keeping, as with Joseph’s storehouse responsibility, may involve real administrative authority.

Its extent follows from the responsibility and context, rather than the root alone.

Does keeping mean remaining unchanged in every circumstance?

Keeping can prevent alteration, loss, or destruction, but its precise meaning depends on its object.

A record may involve preventing loss of its text or recorded contents.

A person may require care for life, safety, or responsibility.

Ṣalāh requires maintained observance, rather than one physical state remaining eternally unchanged.

Absence of change is thus one possible result, rather than the root’s universal definition.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

“Kept” is a principal equivalent for form I ḥafiẓa. Depending on context, “cared for,” “guarded,” “protected,” or “remembered” may fit.

Ḥifẓ can be rendered keeping, preservation, care, or safeguarding.

Ḥifẓuhumā in Al-Baqarah 2:255 is best rendered keeping the two, concerning the heavens and earth, rather than remembering them.

Ḥāfiẓū ʿalā al-ṣalawāt in Al-Baqarah 2:238 can be rendered maintain the prayers or carefully observe the prayers. Maintain naturally emphasizes sustained observance.

For ustuḥfiẓū in Al-Māʾidah 5:44, were entrusted with keeping conveys both the passive and the entrusted responsibility.

Ḥafīẓun ʿalīm in Yūsuf 12:55 can be a knowledgeable keeper or a knowledgeable, trustworthy custodian. Trustworthiness fits the storehouses but does not cover every use of ḥafīẓ elsewhere.

Allāhu ḥafīẓun ʿalayhim in Al-Shūrā 42:6 can be The God watches over them. Oversight is closer here than protection against danger.

Wa-innā lahu la-ḥāfiẓūn in Al-Ḥijr 15:9 naturally becomes We are indeed its keepers.

Kitāb ḥafīẓ in Qāf 50:4 can be a preserving record, with commentary explaining that recorded contents do not disappear. Lexical sources also recognize a preserved record not subject to loss.

Lawḥin maḥfūẓin in Al-Burūj 85:22 is a guarded tablet, preserving the passive construction.

Al-ḥāfiẓūna li-ḥudūdi Allāh in Al-Tawbah 9:112 can be keepers of the limits of The God or those who observe the limits of The God. The latter more clearly expresses practical responsibility.

Li-furūjihim ḥāfiẓūn in Al-Muʾminūn 23:5 is naturally those who guard their private parts, with sexual boundaries and chastity explained contextually.

ʿAlayhā ḥāfiẓ in Al-Ṭāriq 86:4 can be a keeper over it. The keeper’s identity and methods should not be inserted into the basic translation without evidence.

9. Conclusion

Ḥ F Ẓ concerns keeping and caring for something. It may prevent property’s loss, a person’s injury, speech being forgotten, or a covenant or responsibility being violated.

Qur’anic contexts apply this core in different fields: The God keeps the heavens and earth and speaks of keeping the Reminder; keepers are assigned over people; people must keep property, covenants, limits, and personal boundaries; believers are called to maintain ṣalāh.

Ḥāfiẓ, ḥafīẓ, ḥifẓ, maḥfūẓ, and ustuḥfiẓū belong to one family but differ morphologically and semantically. Ḥāfiẓ and ḥafīẓ may describe a keeper; maḥfūẓ describes what is kept; ustuḥfiẓū concerns entrusted responsibility.

Divine and human keeping must also be distinguished in power and knowledge. People may promise care and fail, or accept a task beyond their capacity. Divine keeping relates to knowledge, power, and encompassing authority without being reduced to human limitations.

Ethically, keeping is more than possession. Keeping covenants, limits, oaths, and responsibilities involves respecting something for which one is answerable and preventing its neglect.

The brief, fundamental meaning is keeping and caring for something so that it is not lost, forgotten, abandoned, or ruined. Each sentence and context identifies what is to be kept and the responsibility involved.
