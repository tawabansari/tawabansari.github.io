Qur’anic root ك ش ط

Stripping away, removing a covering, and exposing what was concealed

Qur’anic forms

1. Meaning in early Arabic

ك ش ط concerns stripping, removing, or separating something that lies over and covers something else.

Classical Arabic particularly uses it for skinning a camel, removing a horse’s covering, and drawing aside a curtain or covering.

Recorded expressions include:

  • كَشَطَ الْبَعِيرَ: he skinned the camel.
  • كَشَطَ الْجِلْدَ عَنِ الذَّبِيحَةِ: he removed the slaughtered animal’s skin.
  • كَشَطَ الْجُلَّ عَنْ ظَهْرِ الْفَرَسِ: he removed the covering from the horse’s back.
  • كَشَطَ الْغِطَاءَ عَنِ الشَّيْءِ: he drew aside its covering.
  • اِنْكَشَطَ رَوْعُهُ: his fear subsided.

Kitab al-ʿAyn defines كَشْط as removing from something what had covered it.

Ibn Faris similarly identifies removal and exposure as its basic meaning.

A line by Ruʾba ibn al-ʿAjjaj, an Umayyad-era poet, contains:

بَرَّاقَةُ الْبَرْقِ ذِي الْكِشَاطِ

Shining like lightning that becomes visible.

كِشَاط here concerns visibility and the removal of concealment. Since Ruʾba lived after the Qur’an’s revelation, the line documents classical Arabic usage rather than pre-Islamic usage.

The evidence examined here does not independently establish a datable pre-Islamic example. The Qur’an and early lexicons nevertheless attest stripping and removing a covering as recognized meanings of this family.

2. Forms and derivatives

Important Arabic forms include:

  • كَشَطَ ـ يَكْشِطُ: stripped away; removed a covering.
  • كَشْط: stripping away or separating a covering.
  • كُشِطَ: was stripped away.
  • كَاشِط: one who strips or removes a covering.
  • مَكْشُوط: stripped or uncovered.
  • كِشَاط: a camel’s removed skin; also visibility in some usages.
  • كَشَّاط: someone who skins animals.
  • اِنْكَشَطَ: drew away; disappeared; became exposed.
  • اِنْكِشَاط: removal or disappearance of a covering.

Arabic lexicons record قَشْط as another linguistic form of كَشْط, with kaf and qaf reflecting dialectal variation.

Form examined in the Qur’an:

كُشِطَتْ

The Qur’an uses this family only in al-Takwir 81:11, in this verbal form.

3. Core meaning and material imagery

The core meaning is separating and removing something lying over or covering something else.

Removing an animal’s skin exposes what lay beneath. Removing a horse’s covering similarly reveals the previously covered surface.

In both examples the covering leaves its former position.

كَشْط is not restricted to skinning or detaching an adhered layer. It can also describe removing a curtain, cloth, or covering simply resting on something.

Nor does it necessarily mean tearing, crushing, or destroying the covering. A covering can be removed without being destroyed.

Figurative usages can describe the disappearance of a condition previously dominating a person, such as fear.

The common idea is removal or separation from a former covering position or state.

4. Qur’anic usage

كُشِطَتْ: the sky stripped away

The opening of al-Takwir describes great transformations in creation, including the sun being folded up, the stars falling, mountains being moved, and the sky being stripped away.

كُشِطَتْ presents a different image from the sky splitting or darkening.

People see the sky as an encompassing expanse above them. This verse describes that expanse being removed and stripped away.

The lexical image conveys the magnitude of the transformation: as a covering is removed, the sky will be stripped from its familiar state.

The verse speaks of the sky itself being stripped away, not a separate covering being taken off the sky.

“Removing the sky’s covering” is therefore not equivalent to “the sky being stripped away”: the former adds a separate covering not expressed in the verse.

Relation to other Qur’anic descriptions

Al-Anbiya states:

Here the sky is folded up. The scroll image helps convey the gathering of something previously spread out.

Al-Infitar likewise states:

Here the focus is on the sky splitting open.

These three expressions are not lexically identical:

  • كُشِطَتْ: is stripped away.
  • انْفَطَرَتْ: splits open.
  • نَطْوِي: We fold up.

All concern a transformation of the familiar sky, but each describes a different aspect.

These verses do not specify an exact chronology or physical mechanism. كَشْط therefore does not establish that the sky is a solid covering or necessarily means the atmosphere, ozone layer, or another particular physical structure.

The verse in al-Takwir’s context

The opening descriptions culminate in:

The transformations of sky, sun, stars, and other phenomena are connected here with the day people encounter what they have done.

The sky being stripped away thus forms part of the Qur’anic depiction of resurrection and the end of the familiar worldly order.

5. Morphology and syntax

كُشِطَتْ is the third-person feminine singular passive perfect of the basic triliteral verb كَشَطَ.

The active form expresses stripping or separation; the passive focuses on what undergoes the action.

In al-Takwir 81:11, السَّمَاءُ is nominative and names what is stripped away.

One grammatical analysis treats it as the passive subject of an implied verb explained by the following verb; another analyzes the sentence in terms of subject and predicate.

Both preserve the central meaning: the sky itself is stripped away.

The verb has a feminine ending because السَّمَاء is feminine here.

إِذَا places the event in the future. Although كُشِطَتْ is morphologically perfect, translating it as a future event accords with the context.

6. Comparison with related roots

ك ش ط compared with س ل خ

The concrete sense of سَلْخ concerns skinning and separating a covering.

Ya-Sin states:

The separation of daylight from night is expressed through imagery resembling stripping off a covering.

كَشْط and سَلْخ are very close: lexicons even use كَشْط to define سَلْخ.

كَشْط also describes removing various coverings, including cloth and a horse’s covering. Concrete سَلْخ is particularly associated with skinning, extending elsewhere to separation from a bond or the passing of a period.

Ya-Sin 36:37 describes day being stripped from night; al-Takwir 81:11 describes the sky itself being stripped away.

ك ش ط compared with ك ش ف

كَشْف concerns removing a covering or obstacle and revealing something.

Here removal of a covering reveals something of which the person was previously heedless.

كَشْف has a broad range, including relieving distress, revealing truth, and removing a physical covering.

Concrete كَشْط gives a more specific image of stripping and drawing away a covering.

The families overlap, however, and cannot be separated in every Arabic usage.

ك ش ط compared with ن ز ع

نَزْع concerns pulling out, separating, and taking something from its former position.

كَشْط is a kind of separation and removal associated with coverings in its established usages.

كَشْط may be a kind of نَزْع, but not every نَزْع concerns a covering.

ك ش ط compared with ف ط ر

One branch of فَطْر concerns splitting and opening.

Al-Infitar 82:1 speaks of the sky splitting; al-Takwir 81:11 speaks of it being stripped away.

Something may split while remaining in place. A covering may also be removed without necessarily tearing or splitting.

ك ش ط compared with ط و ي

طَيّ concerns folding, rolling, or gathering something previously spread out.

كَشْط emphasizes separating and removing a covering; طَيّ describes folding or rolling something up.

A covering could be removed and then folded, but this example merely illustrates the lexical distinction; it does not establish the order of resurrection events.

7. Suggested renderings for Forqan

كُشِطَتْwas stripped away
وَإِذَا السَّمَاءُ كُشِطَتْand when the sky is stripped away

“Is stripped away” suits al-Takwir 81:11, preserving separation and removal without adding a separate covering over the sky.

“Splits open” obscures the distinction from ف ط ر, while “is destroyed” introduces a broader meaning than the word directly conveys.

Conclusion

ك ش ط concerns stripping away, removing a covering, and exposing what had been concealed.

Its familiar material imagery includes skinning an animal, removing a horse’s covering, and drawing aside a curtain.

Al-Takwir 81:11 uses the family for the sky being stripped away on the day of resurrection.

It is close to سَلْخ, كَشْف, and نَزْع, but not identical with splitting, folding, or destroying.

The central Qur’anic meaning is the sky being stripped away from its former state.
