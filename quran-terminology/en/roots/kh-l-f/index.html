Foundational Meaning

The root KH L F is fundamentally connected with being behind and coming after something. From this simple image several semantic branches emerge: succeeding, the next generation coming, following after someone, leaving something behind, lagging behind, opposing, and becoming different. The common point in all of these meanings is relation to behindness, afterwardness, continuation, and replacement.

For that reason, the root should not be started from the meaning of kingship, empire, or a religious office. Its first image is that someone or something stands after another, behind another, or in continuation of another's place.

The Spatial Image of Khalf and Amam

In classical Arabic, خَلْف (khalf) stands opposite أَمَام (amām): behind, in the rear, or after something. Khalf is the point that lies behind a person's direct view, yet it still belongs to the same line or direction.

From this spatial image, the temporal and generational meaning is built: one person or people goes, and another comes after. When someone comes after another and takes a later place, words such as خَلِيفَة (khalīfah) or خَلَف (khalaf) are formed from this root.

Khalifah in the Earth

One of the most important derivatives of this root in the Qur'an is خَلِيفَة (khalīfah):

إِنِّي جَاعِلٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً (innī jā'ilun fī al-arḍi khalīfatan)

I am placing a khalifah in the earth.

Al-Baqarah 2:30

In this use, khalīfah is understood from succession and coming after. The word itself does not mean king, sultan, or religious ruler. The important point is that the Qur'anic phrase is فِي الْأَرْضِ (fī al-arḍ), in the earth: not khalifah over people's faith and not khalifah over human conscience.

The relation of khilafah in the Qur'an is with the earth: with the responsibility of the human being in the arena in which the human being has been placed. The earth is the field of testing, trust, reform, corruption, judgment, cultivation, and human conduct.

Dawud as Khalifah in the Earth

The same expression is used about Dawud:

يَا دَاوُودُ إِنَّا جَعَلْنَاكَ خَلِيفَةً فِي الْأَرْضِ (yā dāwūdu innā ja'alnāka khalīfatan fī al-arḍ)

O Dawud, We have made you a khalifah in the earth.

Sad 38:26

The Qur'an does not say that Dawud is the khalifah of the believers, the khalifah of a religious class, or the owner of people's faith. Again, the expression is فِي الْأَرْضِ (fī al-arḍ). Even in the case of Dawud, khilafah is related to the earth.

This relation clarifies the field of responsibility: maintaining truth, preserving measure, preventing corruption, judging rightly, caring for life, and bearing trust in the earth where the human being has been placed. Managing people can connect with this root when it belongs to justice, security, preservation of right, and prevention of corruption. But taking control of people's religion, faith, and belief is another meaning, and it is not obtained from the expression خَلِيفَةً فِي الْأَرْضِ (khalīfatan fī al-arḍ) itself.

The Boundary With Control Over Faith

This boundary is also made clear by other Qur'anic verses. The messenger is charged with delivering, warning, and giving glad news; he is not a trustee over people's faith:

وَمَا أَنتَ عَلَيْهِم بِوَكِيلٍ (wa-mā anta 'alayhim bi-wakīl)

And you are not a trustee over them.

Al-An'am 6:107

فَإِنَّمَا عَلَيْكَ الْبَلَاغُ (fa-innamā 'alayka al-balāgh)

So upon you is only the delivering of the message.

Ali 'Imran 3:20

If even the messenger is not a trustee over people's faith, the word khalīfah cannot be turned into an office by which human beings become owners of other people's religion and faith. Qur'anic khilafah, when read beside these verses, is responsibility in the earth, not succession to The God in controlling the conscience of servants.

Khala'if and Khulafa'

The plural form خَلَائِف (khalā'if) also appears in the Qur'an:

هُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَكُمْ خَلَائِفَ فِي الْأَرْضِ (huwa alladhī ja'alakum khalā'ifa fī al-arḍ)

He is the One who made you successors in the earth.

Fatir 35:39

ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ خَلَائِفَ فِي الْأَرْضِ مِن بَعْدِهِمْ (thumma ja'alnākum khalā'ifa fī al-arḍi min ba'dihim)

Then We made you successors in the earth after them.

Yunus 10:14

In these places, khalā'if does not mean kings or rulers. The speech is about generations and groups that come after other groups and receive the earth after them. This use clearly shows that khilafah in the Qur'an is connected with the passing of time, the replacement of generations, and responsibility after those who came before.

The form خُلَفَاء (khulafā') carries the same meaning:

وَاذْكُرُوا إِذْ جَعَلَكُمْ خُلَفَاءَ مِن بَعْدِ قَوْمِ نُوحٍ (wa-udhkurū idh ja'alakum khulafā'a min ba'di qawmi nūḥ)

And remember when He made you successors after the people of Nuh.

Al-A'raf 7:69

وَيَجْعَلُكُمْ خُلَفَاءَ الْأَرْضِ (wa-yaj'alukum khulafā'a al-arḍ)

And He makes you successors of the earth.

An-Naml 27:62

In these structures, the main axis is after and succession. One people goes, another comes; one generation leaves the earth, and another receives responsibility. This meaning is different from hereditary monarchy or religious kingship.

Istikhlaf

From the same root comes the form اِسْتِخْلَاف (istikhlāf). This form commonly gives the meaning of causing someone to succeed or placing someone after others:

وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنكُمْ وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ لَيَسْتَخْلِفَنَّهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ (wa'ada allāhu alladhīna āmanū minkum wa-'amilū al-ṣāliḥāti layastakhlifannahum fī al-arḍ)

The God has promised those among you who have faith and do righteous works that He will surely cause them to succeed in the earth.

An-Nur 24:55

Here too, istikhlāf does not mean giving a crown and throne or granting absolute domination. The phrase itself is joined with faith, righteous work, worship of The God, and not associating partners. At the level of the root, istikhlāf means being placed in a later and responsible station in the earth, not becoming a sacred ruling class.

Khalf as a Later Generation

Another branch of the root is خَلْف (khalf), meaning those behind or after someone:

فَخَلَفَ مِن بَعْدِهِمْ خَلْفٌ (fa-khalafa min ba'dihim khalfun)

Then after them came a later generation.

Maryam 19:59

Here, khalf refers to a generation or group that comes after those before. In lexical usage, a distinction is sometimes made between khalaf and khalf, with the first more often used for a praiseworthy successor and the second for a blameworthy successor. In Qur'anic analysis, however, this distinction should be weighed by context rather than imposed as a rigid rule. In this verse, the context shows that the later generation did not continue the right path of those before.

Mukhallafun as Those Left Behind

Another branch is تَخَلُّف (takhalluf), meaning lagging behind or being left behind from accompanying others:

فَرِحَ الْمُخَلَّفُونَ بِمَقْعَدِهِمْ (fariḥa al-mukhallafūna bi-maq'adihim)

Those left behind rejoiced in their sitting.

At-Tawbah 9:81

Here again, the image of staying behind is present. Someone who should have moved with the movement remained behind and stood at the rear.

Opposition and Khilaf

From KH L F comes the meaning of opposition. In forms such as يُخَالِف (yukhālif) or خِلَاف (khilāf), the basic image is that someone stands in another direction, separates from a path or command, and turns away from the way they should have continued.

Opposition, then, is not cut off from the spatial and directional meaning of the root. It is often not merely having a different opinion; it can be standing against the course and separating from the proper continuation.

Ikhtilaf

The form اخْتِلَاف (ikhtilāf) also belongs to this root. Ikhtilāf means non-sameness, separation in direction, state, color, speech, or path. The Qur'an speaks of the ikhtilāf of night and day, the ikhtilāf of tongues and colors, and the ikhtilāf of human beings.

Here the root expands from coming after into the meaning of being not identical and not standing on one line. In contexts such as the coming and going of night and day, ikhtilāf can also be understood as successive alternation: one comes after the other.

Breaking a Promise

The form أَخْلَفَ (akhlafa) appears in the sense of bringing something contrary to a promise or failing to fulfill what was expected. In this branch, the root connects with deviation from the expected continuation.

When a promise is given and the outcome comes contrary to it, ikhlāf occurs. This too is understood from the same principle: separating from the expected line.

Qur'anic Khilafah and Later Political Caliphate

A distinction must be made between Qur'anic khilafah and the later political caliphate. The Qur'an does not use the word khalīfah to mean king, emperor, absolute ruler, or religious head of Muslims. Those meanings were later carried by the word in Muslim political history, but they should not be taken as the original Qur'anic meaning.

In the Qur'an, khalīfah and its nearby derivatives mostly point to succession, coming after others, responsibility in the earth, and being tested after those who came before. If khalīfah is automatically equated with ruler, sultan, king, or political leader of believers, a later historical meaning has been imposed on the text.

Nearby Roots

KH L F is not the same as M L K. M L K is connected with ownership, power, rule, and having something under one's possession or command. KH L F is connected with afterwardness, succession, and coming after another. A person may be both khalīfah and malik, but the two words are not the same. Being khalīfah by itself does not mean owning or ruling.

KH L F also differs from W R TH. Waratha focuses on the transfer of inheritance, property, book, knowledge, or station from the past to the future. Khalafa focuses on coming after another and standing in continuation after them. Every inheritor comes after another in one sense, but not every khalaf necessarily inherits wealth, kingdom, or gifts from those before. Sometimes the person or people simply receives later responsibility and testing.

KH L F also differs from B D L. Badal means that one thing stands in place of another. Khalaf, in addition to replacement, carries a temporal and spatial direction: someone or something comes after another, stands behind them, or is situated in their continuation. In badal, the first element may simply be replaced; in khalaf, the emphasis is on coming after and standing in continuation.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For خَلِيفَةً فِي الْأَرْضِ (khalīfatan fī al-arḍ): a khalifah in the earth; or a responsible successor in the earth.

For خَلَائِفَ فِي الْأَرْضِ (khalā'ifa fī al-arḍ): successors in the earth; or later generations in the earth.

For خُلَفَاءَ مِن بَعْدِ قَوْمِ نُوحٍ (khulafā'a min ba'di qawmi nūḥ): successors after the people of Nuh.

For لَيَسْتَخْلِفَنَّهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ (layastakhlifannahum fī al-arḍ): He will surely cause them to succeed in the earth.

For فَخَلَفَ مِن بَعْدِهِمْ خَلْفٌ (fa-khalafa min ba'dihim khalfun): then after them came a later generation.

For الْمُخَلَّفُونَ (al-mukhallafūn): those left behind; or those who lagged behind from accompanying.

For اخْتِلَاف (ikhtilāf): non-sameness, difference, or successive alternation, according to context.

For أَخْلَفَ (akhlafa): brought contrary; or broke a promise.

Summary

KH L F is the root of afterwardness: coming behind, standing after another, taking the place of a person or people, or lagging and separating from an expected course. Khalīfah in the Qur'an comes from this field, and its basic meaning is successor or one placed in a later station, not king or absolute ruler.

In the main Qur'anic uses, khilafah appears with فِي الْأَرْضِ (fī al-arḍ). Its relation is with the earth, responsibility, and bearing trust in the arena of life, not domination over people's faith. Any political or religious use of khilafah should be measured against this principle: the Qur'an does not speak of khilafah as sacred domination, but of the responsibility of the human being and generations in the earth.
