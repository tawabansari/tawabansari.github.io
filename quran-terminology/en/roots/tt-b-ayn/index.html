Qur'anic Root

Qur'anic Root Ṭ B ʿ

Sealing, imprinting, and fixing a lasting mark upon the channels of reception after a settled human direction.

Core Meaning

The root ط ب ع indicates pressing a seal or mold upon receptive matter so that its form and mark become fixed upon that matter.

In Qur'anic usage, ṭabʿ upon the heart means that its closed and unreceptive condition has become so settled that hearing, seeing, understanding, and responding to truth are impaired.

The semantic core is this: placing a fixing effect upon something, so that its previous flexibility decreases and a new form remains upon it.

Important Forms

طَبَعَ is the past verb of form I: he sealed, molded, placed a fixed mark upon something, or fixed a certain condition upon it.

وَطَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

And The God sealed their hearts, so they do not know.

At-Tawbah 9:93

يَطْبَعُ is the imperfect verb: He seals, fixes a condition, or places the way of reception in a closed condition.

كَذَٰلِكَ يَطْبَعُ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِ الْكَافِرِينَ

Thus The God seals the hearts of the kafirin.

Al-A'raf 7:101

نَطْبَعُ is the first-person plural imperfect:

وَنَطْبَعُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَسْمَعُونَ

And We seal their hearts, so they do not hear.

Al-A'raf 7:100

طُبِعَ is the passive past verb: it was sealed; the closed condition was fixed upon it; the mark of the seal was placed upon it.

وَطُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ

And their hearts were sealed, so they do not understand deeply.

At-Tawbah 9:87

فَطُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ

So their hearts were sealed, and therefore they do not understand deeply.

Al-Munafiqun 63:3

طَبْع is the verbal noun: sealing, imprinting, fixing a mark, or placing something in a settled form. This noun form does not occur in the Qur'an.

مَطْبُوع is the passive participle: sealed, molded, or carrying a fixed form. This form does not occur in the Qur'an.

طَبِيعَة in later Arabic came to mean nature, inner constitution, or the natural order. This word does not occur in the Qur'an. Therefore, translating Qur'anic ṭabʿ as “innate nature,” “pre-made instinct,” or “unchangeable essence” is incorrect. Such translation imposes later philosophical language upon the Qur'anic structure.

Linguistic Note

The core of ط ب ع is not merely “being closed.” In this root, something first has the capacity to receive and take form; then the effect of a seal or mold becomes fixed upon it.

The material image of the root has several stages: the matter is initially receptive; the seal or mold is placed upon it; pressure is applied; the mark is transferred; and the new form remains upon the matter.

When used for the heart, this image shows that the heart is originally a channel of reception, judgment, and response. But repeated human directions can fix a condition upon it so that it no longer easily receives a new imprint.

From this angle, ṭabʿ may be understood as the gradual loss of the heart's flexibility. This does not mean that the heart is literally wax-like matter; rather, in the linguistic image, initial receptivity exists, the pressure of choices is repeated, a behavioral direction becomes settled, and the heart is fixed in that form.

Still, one should not say that every mistake immediately and directly leads to ṭabʿ. The verses speak of settled directions such as kufr, arrogance, transgression, and following desires.

Material Image

The material image of ط ب ع is a seal or mold pressed upon clay, wax, soft metal, or another receptive material.

Before ṭabʿ, the material can accept different forms. During ṭabʿ, the mold is placed on the surface, pressure is applied from above, the mark of the mold settles into or upon the material, and after the mold is lifted the effect remains.

In coin striking, metal is pressed into a die and a specific mark is fixed upon it. After this stage, the metal is no longer only formless matter; it carries the form of the die.

In Qur'anic use, the heart also acquires a settled condition after repeated choices and directions. Ṭabʿ is not merely covering a surface; it is the fixing of a perceptive and behavioral form.

But the expression “complete transformation of the heart's identity” is too severe. The verse speaks of being sealed and of impaired reception, not of the total destruction of human nature.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, ṭabʿ was used for sealing, molding, and fixing a mark upon matter. This field appears in several uses: sealing clay or wax, striking and molding coins, marking metal, fixing form in material, and in extended usage, a trait or condition that has become settled in a person.

From this extension, ṭabʿ and ṭabiʿah later came to mean nature or inner constitution.

But the Qur'anic use should not be equated with later philosophical terms. When the Qur'an says طَبَعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ, it does not mean that they were created with an “evil nature.” The contexts show that ṭabʿ comes after specific actions and choices.

Ṭabʿ Because of Kufr

بَلْ طَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهَا بِكُفْرِهِمْ فَلَا يُؤْمِنُونَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

Rather, The God sealed them because of their kufr, so they do not have faith except a little.

An-Nisa 4:155

The phrase بكفرهم makes the cause clear: their kufr, then ṭabʿ, and as a result the restriction of faith.

The ba in بكفرهم is causal: because of their kufr. So ṭabʿ in this verse is not the human being's first and causeless condition. It is the result of a prior direction.

Ṭabʿ and Not Hearing

وَنَطْبَعُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَسْمَعُونَ

And We seal their hearts, so they do not hear.

Al-A'raf 7:100

Not hearing here is not bodily deafness. Sound may reach the ear, yet the message is not received, weighed, and answered in the heart.

The order of the verse, sealing the heart and then not hearing, shows that guided hearing is not only the work of the ear. The heart too must be receptive to the meaning that is heard.

Ṭabʿ After Repeated Denial

كَذَٰلِكَ يَطْبَعُ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِ الْكَافِرِينَ

Thus The God seals the hearts of the kafirin.

Al-A'raf 7:101

كذلك connects ṭabʿ to the prior behavior and story. Signs have come, but denial has continued. The direction of denial, through repetition, becomes a settled condition.

So ṭabʿ is not a sudden event without preparation; it is the end of a process of fixation.

Ṭabʿ and Not Understanding Deeply

وَطُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ

And their hearts were sealed, so they do not understand deeply.

At-Tawbah 9:87

Fiqh here is not merely knowing words or information. It is understanding that connects speech, meaning, and responsibility.

The sealed heart may hear words, yet not allow them to become responsible understanding.

Contentment With Staying Behind, Then Ṭabʿ

Before the verse, it says:

رَضُوا بِأَنْ يَكُونُوا مَعَ الْخَوَالِفِ

They were content to be with those who stayed behind.

At-Tawbah 9:87

Then it says:

وَطُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ

And their hearts were sealed.

At-Tawbah 9:87

Inner contentment with staying behind is mentioned before ṭabʿ. This order shows that a person may first make a decision, then become content with and attached to it, and finally that same direction becomes fixed in his heart.

Ṭabʿ and Not Knowing

وَطَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

And The God sealed their hearts, so they do not know.

At-Tawbah 9:93

لا يعلمون does not necessarily mean a lack of information. A person may know data, yet not recognize the truth of the situation, the responsibility, or the consequence of his action.

In different verses, the results of ṭabʿ appear with different phrases: لا يؤمنون, لا يسمعون, لا يفقهون, and لا يعلمون. This variety shows that ṭabʿ does not impair only one single channel; it affects the whole chain of reception and response.

Ṭabʿ Upon the Hearts of Transgressors

كَذَٰلِكَ نَطْبَعُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِ الْمُعْتَدِينَ

Thus We seal the hearts of the transgressors.

Yunus 10:74

Iʿtida means crossing the limit. When crossing the limit becomes a settled behavior, the ability to recognize the limit itself is weakened. The person not only crosses the boundary; he also loses the ability to see and accept that boundary.

So ṭabʿ here is tied to the fixation of transgression.

Ṭabʿ Upon Heart, Hearing, and Sight

أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ طَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَسَمْعِهِمْ وَأَبْصَارِهِمْ وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْغَافِلُونَ

Those are the ones upon whose hearts, hearing, and sights The God has sealed; and those are the heedless.

An-Nahl 16:108

In this verse three channels of perception stand together: heart, hearing, and sight.

This verse does not condemn people who are blind or deaf. The issue is the closing of the guidance-function of the tools of perception, not the bodily absence of organs.

A person may have eyes and not see the sign; have ears and not hear the message; and have a heart but not accept the meaning.

Ṭabʿ Upon the Hearts of Those Who Do Not Know

كَذَٰلِكَ يَطْبَعُ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِ الَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

Thus The God seals the hearts of those who do not know.

Ar-Rum 30:59

This verse should not be used against illiterate people or those deprived of education. لا يعلمون comes in the context of denying clear signs. The issue is not merely lacking data; it is remaining in a kind of not-knowing whose path to knowledge the person refuses to accept.

Ṭabʿ Upon Every Arrogant, Tyrannical Heart

كَذَٰلِكَ يَطْبَعُ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ قَلْبِ مُتَكَبِّرٍ جَبَّارٍ

Thus The God seals every arrogant, tyrannical heart.

Ghafir 40:35

Arrogance prevents accepting a truth that comes from another. Jabbariyyah is tied to imposing one's will and refusing limits.

The arrogant heart does not examine the message in order to understand. It accepts or rejects the message according to the rank of the speaker and whether the message fits its own superiority. As a result, self-magnification becomes a mold into which every new message is pressed and distorted.

Ṭabʿ and Following Desires

أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ طَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَاتَّبَعُوا أَهْوَاءَهُمْ

Those are the ones whose hearts The God has sealed, and they followed their desires.

Muhammad 47:16

Want and desire are not themselves ṭabʿ. The problem appears when hawa and desire become the final standard of judgment. The person accepts truth only when it confirms his desire.

With the continuation of this behavior, the heart no longer weighs truth; it pours everything into the mold of prior desire.

Faith, Then Kufr, Then Ṭabʿ

ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ آمَنُوا ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا فَطُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ

That is because they had faith, then they committed kufr; so their hearts were sealed, and therefore they do not understand deeply.

Al-Munafiqun 63:3

This verse clarifies the sequence of the process: they had faith; then they committed kufr; then their hearts were sealed; as a result, they do not understand.

ثم shows a change of direction, and the fa in فطبع makes ṭabʿ the result of the previous stage.

This structure is incompatible with the idea of “eternal innate kufr.” They were in another condition before ṭabʿ. Then, through choice and change of direction, the seal became fixed upon their hearts.

Similar Uses

Material ṭabʿ and ṭabʿ upon the heart: In material ṭabʿ, a seal is pressed upon receptive matter. In Qur'anic ṭabʿ, the image is transferred to the channel of perception. This is a figurative or imaginal use and should not be turned into a physical seal on the bodily organ of the heart.

Ṭabʿ as result and then cause: In the first stage, ṭabʿ is the result of human action: kufr, transgression, arrogance, following desire, and turning back after faith. After it is fixed, ṭabʿ itself becomes a barrier to understanding and return.

So the relation is not a circle but a chain: wrong choice, fixation of direction, closing of receptivity, and the return becoming more difficult.

Ṭabʿ and the heart's flexibility: The image of sealing shows that the matter was receptive before ṭabʿ. But one should not conclude from the image that after ṭabʿ the heart has absolutely and forever no possibility of change, unless a verse states that explicitly. Ṭabʿ shows the severity and fixation of closedness, not necessarily a philosophical judgment that every return is impossible.

Ṭabʿ and original nature: Qur'anic ṭabʿ is a later condition, not the first fiṭrah. Translations such as “their nature was like this,” “they were innately unreceptive to truth,” “they were created with the instinct of kufr,” or “their nature was closed from the beginning” are incorrect. Such expressions erase the explicit causes given in the verses.

Bodily disability and guidance-closure: Ṭabʿ upon hearing and sight does not mean contempt for people with bodily disabilities. Real deafness and blindness are bodily conditions. Qur'anic ṭabʿ in this context concerns refusing, not attending, and not using the channels of perception.

Grammar and Structure

Ṭabʿ with ʿala: The dominant Qur'anic structure is:

طَبَعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ

He sealed their hearts.

على preserves the image of a seal being placed upon a surface and its effect settling upon it. In sealing, the seal is placed on the matter, pressure is applied, and the mark settles upon it.

For this reason, the construction طبع على is harmonious with the material image of the root.

But one should not infer from على alone that the seal always comes physically from above or covers all layers like a shade. What is clear is the settling and dominance of the effect over the place of reception.

Causal ba:

طَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهَا بِكُفْرِهِمْ

The ba in بكفرهم states the cause: because of their kufr. This preposition is one of the clearest evidences against a deterministic reading.

Fa of result:

آمَنُوا ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا فَطُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ

The fa makes ṭabʿ the result of having faith and then committing kufr. If this sequence disappears in translation, the logic of the verse disappears as well.

Active and passive verb: The active form, such as طَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ, makes the agent explicit. The passive form, such as طُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ, focuses on the resulting condition. The passive does not erase the human background; the context explains it.

Singular samʿ and plural abṣar:

عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَسَمْعِهِمْ وَأَبْصَارِهِمْ

سمع can function as a verbal noun or generic noun for the faculty of hearing in the group. أبصار is the plural of baṣar. This morphological difference does not mean that the group has only one bodily hearing.

Fa in perceptive results: After ṭabʿ, several verses use fa of result:

فَهُمْ لَا يَسْمَعُونَ

So they do not hear.

فَهُمْ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ

So they do not understand deeply.

فَهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

So they do not know.

The fa shows that these perceptive incapacities are the result of the sealed condition.

Nearby Roots

Ṭ B ʿ and KH T M: KH T M indicates ending, sealing, and closing an opening or boundary. Ṭ B ʿ emphasizes pressing a mold and fixing a mark upon matter. Khatm: an ending and entrance are sealed. Ṭabʿ: the effect and form of the seal become fixed upon the place of reception.

The distinction should not be made too absolute; both can indicate the result of closed perception in the Qur'an. But their material images differ. Khatm is closer to closing and final seal. Ṭabʿ is closer to molding and fixed imprint.

The claim that ṭabʿ necessarily “transforms the identity of the heart” is not established by the word itself. The more precise expression is that it fixes and limits the condition and quality of reception.

Ṭ B ʿ and R Y N: R Y N indicates a layer that settles over the heart because of deeds:

كَلَّا بَلْ رَانَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ مَا كَانُوا يَكْسِبُونَ

No indeed; rather, what they used to earn has rusted over their hearts.

Al-Mutaffifin 83:14

Rayn is an accumulating cover or layer; ṭabʿ is a seal and fixed condition. Rayn highlights the process of settling and accumulation; ṭabʿ highlights the settling of form.

Ṭ B ʿ and Q F L: Qafl closes the way of opening:

أَمْ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبٍ أَقْفَالُهَا

Or are there locks upon hearts?

Muhammad 47:24

Qafl is a barrier to opening; ṭabʿ is the fixing of a mark and a closed condition. A lock can be removed; ṭabʿ emphasizes the lasting effect more strongly, although the root itself does not prove that return is impossible.

Ṭ B ʿ and GH L F: A covering places something inside an enclosing sheath. Ṭabʿ places the seal upon the locus. Ghalf: being covered and within a covering. Ṭabʿ: the effect of mold or seal becomes fixed upon it.

Ṭ B ʿ and W S M: Wasm indicates marking for recognition. Ṭabʿ also creates a mark, but through pressure of a mold and fixation of form. Wasm is mark and sign; ṭabʿ is a molded and lasting imprint.

Ṭ B ʿ and N Q SH: Naqsh indicates drawing or carving a design on a surface. Ṭabʿ transfers a prepared design from a mold to material.

Ṭ B ʿ and J B L: Jabal, in the field of jibilah, can indicate original constitution or innate make. Qur'anic ṭabʿ describes a condition after behavior. Jibilah is the first make; ṭabʿ upon the heart is the fixation of a direction after choice and repetition.

Ṭ B ʿ and F Ṭ R: Fiṭr indicates the beginning of creation and the opening of the first structure. Ṭabʿ focuses on a later effect and fixed form. Therefore, ṭabʿ upon the heart should not be equated with fiṭrah.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For طَبَعَ: sealed; placed a seal; fixed a closed mark; established a closed condition, in explanation.

For يَطْبَعُ: seals; places a seal; fixes a closed condition.

For طُبِعَ: was sealed; became sealed; a closed condition was fixed upon it.

For طَبَعَ عَلَىٰ قَلْبِهِ: sealed his heart; placed a seal upon his heart; fixed a closed condition upon his heart.

For بَلْ طَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهَا بِكُفْرِهِمْ: rather, The God sealed them because of their kufr; rather, The God placed a seal upon their hearts as a result of their kufr.

For وَنَطْبَعُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَسْمَعُونَ: and We seal their hearts, so they do not hear. Or, explanatorily: and We place a seal upon their hearts, so they do not hear the message receptively.

For وَطُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ: and their hearts were sealed, so they do not understand deeply. Or: their hearts became sealed, so they do not grasp the meaning.

For أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ طَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَسَمْعِهِمْ وَأَبْصَارِهِمْ: those are the ones upon whose hearts, hearing, and sights The God has sealed. Or: those are the ones upon whose heart and faculties of hearing and seeing The God has placed a seal.

For آمَنُوا ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا فَطُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ: they had faith, then committed kufr; so their hearts were sealed. Or: they had faith and then committed kufr; as a result their hearts became sealed.

Suggested standard rendering: That is because they had faith, then committed kufr; so their hearts were sealed, and therefore they do not understand deeply.

Adding “fixation” in brackets is not necessary in the standard translation; it is clearer in the lexical note.

Summary

The root ط ب ع indicates sealing, molding, and fixing a mark or form upon receptive matter.

Its material image is a seal or mold pressed upon clay, wax, or soft metal. The material is initially shapeable; then, through pressure, it receives the mark of the mold, and that mark remains upon it.

In Qur'anic usage, this image is transferred to the heart and channels of perception. Ṭabʿ upon the heart means that a closed and unreceptive direction has become so fixed upon it that hearing, seeing, understanding, and having faith are impaired.

But ṭabʿ in the Qur'an is not the human being's eternal innate nature. The verses bring it after kufr, transgression, arrogance, following desires, contentment with staying behind, or having faith and then committing kufr. The verse in Al-Munafiqun shows the process clearly: they had faith; then they committed kufr; then their hearts were sealed.

Therefore, translating ṭabʿ as “innate nature,” “pre-made instinct,” or “unchangeable essence” does not fit the Qur'anic structure.

Ṭabʿ is both the result of a prior direction and, after becoming fixed, a condition that makes later reception more difficult: repeated choices fix the form of the heart; the sealed heart does not easily receive a new imprint.

Ṭ B ʿ is close to KH T M, R Y N, Q F L, GH L F, W S M, and N Q SH, but it is identical with none of them. Khatm closes an ending or entrance; rayn lays a layer upon the heart; qafl blocks opening; ghalf places something in a covering; and wasm places a sign upon it.

The distinctive core of Ṭ B ʿ is pressing a seal or mold upon something and fixing a lasting effect upon it; in Qur'anic usage, it is the fixation of a closed condition upon the heart and channels of reception after a settled human direction.
