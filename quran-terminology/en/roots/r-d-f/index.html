Root R D F (radf)

Qur’anic and lexical forms

مُرْدِفِينَ
(murdifīna)
Form IV plural active participle; Al-Anfāl 8:9

رَدِفَ
(radifa)
Followed or approached; masculine singular perfect; Al-Naml 27:72

الرَّادِفَة
(al-rādifah)
The following one; feminine active participle; Al-Nāziʿāt 79:7

مُرْدَفِينَ
(murdafīna)
Passive participle; another recognized reading of Al-Anfāl 8:9

رِدْف
(ridf)
A companion behind; non-Qur’anic lexical form

رَدِيف
(radīf)
Second rider or follower; non-Qur’anic form

أَرْدَفَ
(ardafa)
Brought after or followed; non-Qur’anic verb

تَرَادُف
(tarāduf)
Succession; later, synonymy; not Qur’anic

Following after, coming in succession, and joining one thing to another

Arabic R D F denotes following after, being behind, and one thing succeeding another. This can be spatial, as with someone riding behind another, or temporal, as with an event following an earlier event.

Ridf can name the person behind the foremost rider; radīf a companion following or sitting behind someone; and tarāduf, in its basic usage, successive following.

The root concerns a relationship between things or events: one precedes, another follows. This need not involve physical movement. It may concern a sequence of events, the approach of something following someone, or one group joining another.

R D F occurs three times in three Qur’anic forms: murdifīna in Al-Anfāl, radifa in Al-Naml, and al-rādifatu in Al-Nāziʿāt.

They share a semantic field while differing in syntax and setting.

Qur’anic forms: murdifīna (Al-Anfāl 8:9), radifa (Al-Naml 27:72), and al-rādifatu (Al-Nāziʿāt 79:7).

Arabic also records ridf, radf, radīf, rudafāʾ, rudāfā, ardāf, ardafa, rādafa, tarādafa, irtadafa, istardafa, ridāf, and ridāfah. These do not occur as such in the Qur’an, but help establish the family’s range.

1. Core meaning and early Arabic usage

Following after and being behind

Radifa al-shayʾa means following or being behind something.

The root’s semantic core is following something and coming after it.

Ridf names what follows another thing; radīf can name someone following another person.

Radiftu fulānan can mean that I came behind someone or rode behind them on a mount.

Ardaftuhu can also mean bringing someone after oneself or seating them behind oneself.

Lexical accounts differ over the precise distinction between radifa and ardafa. Some distinguish following someone from seating them behind oneself, while others record overlapping uses.

Morphology alone therefore does not establish an absolute rule that Form I always expresses the subject’s own movement and Form IV always moves another.

The Qur’anic form, syntax, and context must be examined together.

Ridf and radīf: a companion riding behind

Riding provides a clear physical use of the root.

Two people sometimes shared one mount in early Arabic life, one in front and another behind.

The rear rider was called ridf or radīf.

Ridāf can also denote the second rider’s seat behind the first.

Istardafahu can express asking to ride behind someone.

This illustrates the basic relationship: two people travel along one route, one behind the other.

It should not become the definitive image for every derivative.

Qur’anic radifa concerns an event that may have approached people, without two riders or a mount.

Al-rādifah in Al-Nāziʿāt concerns an event following another. A rider’s rear position is thus only one use of this field.

Spatial and temporal succession

Tarādafati al-ashyāʾu expresses things coming one after another.

Jāʾa al-qawmu rudāfā means that people came in succession.

This need not concern one person behind a particular other; it can describe several individuals or groups arriving in sequence.

Radf is also recorded for a subsequent development or event following another.

If one event occurs and another follows, members of this root can express that sequence.

The preceding-following relationship can thus be temporal as well as spatial.

Following does not necessarily make the second event a causal consequence of the first.

Two events may occur consecutively without the first causing the second.

Nor does succession necessarily exclude an interval. Its duration requires context or other evidence.

Ridf and the rear of a body or load

Radf and ridf are also recorded for the back part of the body, particularly the buttocks or seat.

The connection is understandable: the body’s rear lies behind its forward parts.

Ridf can also name a bag or load behind a traveler.

Different family members thus express a rear position and one thing accompanying another.

Without historical evidence, however, the bodily term cannot be established as the first meaning from which following developed.

Temporal or abstract uses should not all be reduced to the physical rear of a body.

The imagery is understandable, but historical derivation requires independent evidence.

2. Early poetry: succession in movement and position

Stars appearing in succession

Lexical traditions attribute this verse to Khuzaymah ibn Mālik ibn Nahd:

إِذَا الْجَوْزَاءُ أَرْدَفَتِ الثُّرَيَّا
ظَنَنْتُ بِآلِ فَاطِمَةَ الظُّنُونَا

idhā al-jawzāʾu ardafati al-thurayyā / ẓanantu bi-āli fāṭimata al-ẓunūnā

“When al-Jawzāʾ follows al-Thurayyā, I entertain many thoughts about Fāṭimah’s people.”

Ardafat concerns al-Jawzāʾ following al-Thurayyā in their order of appearance.

Al-Jawzāʾ and al-Thurayyā are established early Arabic names for stars or stellar groupings.

A literary explanation connects the verse with intense heat and people dispersing to find water, separating the poet from his beloved and causing anxiety.

The root’s main point is ardafat: something appears after another in the relevant sequence.

Irdāf need not concern two people or mounts; it can describe the observed order of celestial phenomena.

Lexical transmissions attribute the verse to that poet, but attribution alone does not establish its precise date or every detail of the poet’s name. Caution is needed before treating it as certainly dated pre-Islamic evidence.

Rudāfā in Labīd’s poetry

Labīd ibn Rabīʿah’s collected poetry includes:

عُذَافِرَةٌ تَقَمَّصُ بِالرُّدَافَى
تَخَوَّنَهَا نُزُولِي وَارْتِحَالِي

ʿudhāfiratun taqammaṣu bi-al-rudāfā / takhawwanahā nuzūlī wa-irtiḥālī

“A large, powerful she-camel, springing to the alternating drivers’ song; my repeated stopping and departure have worn her strength away.”

ʿUdhāfirah describes a large, powerful she-camel.

One lexical account applies al-rudāfā to camel drivers taking turns in singing and driving.

Another understands it as the plural of radīf, those following one another, including companions riding one behind another on a single mount.

Al-rudāfā thus concerns taking turns and following in sequence.

The witness is recorded in Labīd’s poetry. He lived across the pre-Islamic and Islamic periods, so without dating this particular poem it should not necessarily be called pre-Islamic.

The witnesses and lexical uses establish an early field of sequence and following in movement, position, and human activity.

Ridāfah and royal deputies

Arabic historical accounts record ridāfah for a position alongside the kings of al-Ḥīrah.

Ridf could name someone close to the king who, in certain circumstances, handled governmental affairs during his absence.

Accounts vary over the office’s details, while its title clearly relates to following or taking another’s place.

The root’s field can thus extend from physical following to social position and deputizing.

This does not make minister, political deputy, or governmental official the original meaning of ridf.

Those are specialized social uses and should not be transferred into Qur’anic verses.

3. Murdifīna: angelic aid and successive arrival

The first Qur’anic occurrence is in Al-Anfāl 8:9:

إِذْ تَسْتَغِيثُونَ رَبَّكُمْ فَاسْتَجَابَ لَكُمْ أَنِّي مُمِدُّكُمْ بِأَلْفٍ مِنَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ مُرْدِفِينَ

idh tastaghīthūna rabbakum fa-istajāba lakum annī mumiddukum bi-alfin mina al-malāʾikati murdifīna

“When you sought your Lord’s help, He answered: I will reinforce you with a thousand angels coming in succession.”

The context concerns believers confronting an enemy and asking The God for help.

Several related but different words appear together.

Tastaghīthūna concerns asking for help in difficulty.

Fa-istajāba expresses the answering of that request.

Mumiddukum comes from M D D and concerns providing aid and supporting strength.

Murdifīna comes from R D F and concerns following or bringing others after.

The aid comes from mumiddukum, while murdifīna describes how the angels accompany or follow.

The morphology of murdifīna

In the Ḥafṣ reading, murdifīna is a sound masculine plural active participle of Form IV, ardafa.

It can describe the angels, expressing successive arrival or bringing others after.

Two principal lexical and grammatical possibilities are proposed.

In one, angels arrive successively, an individual or group after another.

In another, they bring others after themselves or join preceding forces.

The distinction concerns Form IV and the relation between agent and object.

Both preserve following and joining.

Murdifīna and murdafīna

Recognized readings include two familiar vocalizations.

Murdifīna, with kasrah on dāl, is an active participle.

Murdafīna, with fatḥah, is passive.

Ḥafṣ has the first form.

Nāfiʿ’s reading records the second.

The difference matters for syntax and the direction of the relationship.

The active form concerns those following or bringing others after.

One passive analysis has angels brought after other angels. Another places the angels after the believers. Identifying both sides of the relationship therefore requires syntax, rather than the passive form alone.

Both preserve R D F’s basic relationship of following and joining in sequence.

The variant alone does not establish the number of angelic groups or their precise manner of presence.

Does murdifīna mean several thousand angels?

Alf, a thousand, is explicitly stated.

Murdifīna expresses following or bringing others after.

The description alone adds no further number.

Successive arrival does not itself prove a total several times greater than a thousand.

Part of Āl ʿImrān 3:124 also mentions angelic aid:

أَلَنْ يَكْفِيَكُمْ أَنْ يُمِدَّكُمْ رَبُّكُمْ بِثَلَاثَةِ آلَافٍ مِنَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ مُنْزَلِينَ

a-lan yakfiyakum an yumiddakum rabbukum bi-thalāthati ālāfin mina al-malāʾikati munzalīna

“Is it not enough that your Lord reinforce you with three thousand angels sent down?”

The next verse mentions five thousand conditionally (Āl ʿImrān 3:125).

These passages require their own conditions and settings. Mechanically adding numbers and murdifīna cannot extract a certain angel count from this root.

Nor does the word establish exact arrival times, intervals, or a particular military formation.

The aid’s purpose in context

Part of Al-Anfāl 8:10 continues:

وَمَا جَعَلَهُ اللَّهُ إِلَّا بُشْرَىٰ وَلِتَطْمَئِنَّ بِهِ قُلُوبُكُمْ وَمَا النَّصْرُ إِلَّا مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ

wa-mā jaʿalahu allāhu illā bushrā wa-li-taṭmaʾinna bihi qulūbukum wa-mā al-naṣru illā min ʿindi allāhi

“The God made it only good news, and that your hearts might find reassurance through it. Victory is only from The God.”

The aid is connected with good news and reassurance for the believers.

Reassurance, good news, and victory come from this verse’s own words, rather than murdifīna’s independent meaning.

Murdifīna concerns following or joining in sequence; its spiritual and practical significance emerges from the wider context.

It should not be defined lexically as victory-producing angels, bringers of reassurance, or forces guaranteeing victory.

4. Radifa lakum: an approaching event

The second occurrence is in Al-Naml 27:72:

قُلْ عَسَىٰ أَنْ يَكُونَ رَدِفَ لَكُمْ بَعْضُ الَّذِي تَسْتَعْجِلُونَ

qul ʿasā an yakūna radifa lakum baʿḍu alladhī tastaʿjilūna

“Say: Perhaps some of what you seek to hasten has drawn near to you.”

It answers people questioning the divine promise.

The preceding verse, Al-Naml 27:71, states:

وَيَقُولُونَ مَتَىٰ هَٰذَا الْوَعْدُ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ صَادِقِينَ

wa-yaqūlūna matā hādhā al-waʿdu in kuntum ṣādiqīna

“They say: When is this promise, if you speak truth?”

The answer says that some of what they seek to hasten may have approached.

Radifa: following and approaching

Radifa is a Form I perfect verb.

Its subject is baʿḍu alladhī tastaʿjilūna, some of what they seek to hasten.

Lexically, radifa concerns following and arriving after.

An event following a person or another event can acquire the sense of approaching.

Early explanations use danā and iqtaraba, drew near.

This fits the question about when the promise will arrive and the answer drawing attention to its possible approach.

R D F does not therefore always mean temporal proximity.

Following remains the core in uses for riders or events. Temporal proximity is highlighted here by the promise and the demand for haste.

The role of lakum

Radifahu amrun is an established construction for an event following someone.

Al-Naml instead has radifa lakum, with lām.

Lexical and grammatical accounts explain the lām differently.

One treats it as introduced into the verb’s construction, close to radifakum: something has followed you.

Another relates it to danā lakum: something has drawn near to you.

One exegetical preference favors the latter because radifa here includes the meaning of approaching.

The difference cautions against a mechanically fixed function for lām.

Both concern an event related to the audience and its possible approach.

“Has drawn near to you” therefore suits radifa lakum here.

ʿAsā: warning without a fixed date

In Al-Naml 27:72, this part begins with ʿasā:

عَسَىٰ أَنْ يَكُونَ رَدِفَ لَكُمْ

ʿasā an yakūna radifa lakum

“Perhaps it has drawn near” preserves the warning’s form of expression. Yet ʿasā does not function identically in every context; it alone does not establish uncertainty or lack of knowledge on the speaker’s part.

It should not become a statement fixing the punishment’s arrival date.

The verse specifies no particular day, year, or period.

Nor does perfect radifa within ʿasā an yakūna independently establish a calendar date.

“Perhaps it has drawn near” preserves that restraint.

Baʿḍu alladhī tastaʿjilūna: some of what they seek to hasten

The verse includes baʿḍ, some.

It concerns some, rather than all, of what the audience sought to hasten.

Baʿḍ should not be omitted or made to mean the entire promise.

Tastaʿjilūna comes from ʿ J L and concerns asking for haste.

It describes the audience’s desire that the promise be fulfilled sooner.

That desire is not part of R D F’s meaning.

Radifa concerns the event’s arrival or approach; tastaʿjilūna expresses the audience’s demand to hasten it.

Does radifa lakum refer only to earthly punishment?

Al-Naml’s context concerns denial of resurrection, rejection of the promise, and questions about its fulfillment.

Al-Naml 27:67 and Al-Naml 27:68 express the unbelievers’ doubt and denial about being raised after death.

The passage then concerns the wrongdoers’ end and the divine promise.

Baʿḍu alladhī tastaʿjilūna thus concerns some of the promised consequences whose fulfillment they demand.

Some explanations apply it to punishment in earthly life, including events associated with Badr.

The verse itself names no Badr, location, or date.

Those identifications require historical or exegetical examination, rather than becoming radifa’s lexical meaning.

The passage warns against inferring from apparent delay that the promise is absent or impossible.

That message emerges from the connected verses; the root independently concerns following arrival and approach.

5. Al-rādifah: an event following another

The third occurrence is in Al-Nāziʿāt 79:7, read with Al-Nāziʿāt 79:6 and Al-Nāziʿāt 79:8:

يَوْمَ تَرْجُفُ الرَّاجِفَةُ ۝ تَتْبَعُهَا الرَّادِفَةُ ۝ قُلُوبٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ وَاجِفَةٌ

yawma tarjufu al-rājifatu / tatbaʿuhā al-rādifatu / qulūbun yawmaʾidhin wājifatun

“On the day the trembling one trembles; the following event follows it; hearts that day are fearful and trembling.”

Al-rājifah and al-rādifah are related but distinct descriptions.

Al-rājifah comes from R J F, concerning trembling and disturbance.

Al-rādifah comes from R D F, naming what follows another.

The distinction should be preserved.

The structure of al-rādifah

Al-rādifah is a feminine active participle from radifa.

Its lexical meaning is the following one, or what comes after another.

It has the definite article and points to something or an event identified in context.

Its feminine form alone does not establish a particular earthquake, blast, or physical event.

An Arabic feminine adjective can refer to an unstated feminine noun.

Identifying al-rādifah therefore requires the related passages.

Tatbaʿuhā al-rādifatu

Tatbaʿuhā comes from T B ʿ.

The verb concerns following.

Al-rādifah expresses the same subsequent relationship through a description from R D F.

Their combination emphasizes the order of events.

Grammatically, al-rādifah is the subject of tatbaʿu; the object pronoun -hā refers to al-rājifah in the preceding verse.

The structure is clear: what is called rādifah follows rājifah.

This order alone specifies neither the interval nor either event’s physical character.

Nor does rādifah make the second event necessarily stronger, smaller, longer, or more destructive than the first.

Those properties are absent from the root itself.

Is al-rādifah the second blast?

One familiar interpretation identifies al-rājifah and al-rādifah with two resurrection blasts.

Al-Zumar 39:68 states:

وَنُفِخَ فِي الصُّورِ فَصَعِقَ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَنْ فِي الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا مَنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ ثُمَّ نُفِخَ فِيهِ أُخْرَىٰ فَإِذَا هُمْ قِيَامٌ يَنْظُرُونَ

wa-nufikha fī al-ṣūri fa-ṣaʿiqa man fī al-samāwāti wa-man fī al-arḍi illā man shāʾa allāhu thumma nufikha fīhi ukhrā fa-idhā hum qiyāmun yanẓurūna

“The trumpet is blown, and those in the heavens and earth fall stricken, except whom The God wills. Then it is blown again, and suddenly they stand, looking.”

This explicitly describes two blowings.

Connecting the two Al-Nāziʿāt events with them is therefore an interpretive possibility supported by another Qur’anic passage.

Tatbaʿuhā al-rādifatu itself mentions neither nafkhah nor ṣūr, blast or trumpet.

R D F’s lexical meaning alone cannot establish al-rādifah as necessarily the proper name of the second blast.

Another reading concerns two tremors or great events associated with resurrection.

This possibility too requires comparison with related verses.

What the phrase clearly establishes is an order: the second event follows the first.

Does rādifah mean return?

Later verses concern denial of resurrection and return, for example Al-Nāziʿāt 79:10:

يَقُولُونَ أَئِنَّا لَمَرْدُودُونَ فِي الْحَافِرَةِ

yaqūlūna a-innā la-mardūdūna fī al-ḥāfirati

“They say: Will we really be returned to our former state?”

Then Al-Nāziʿāt 79:13 and Al-Nāziʿāt 79:14 state:

فَإِنَّمَا هِيَ زَجْرَةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ ۝ فَإِذَا هُمْ بِالسَّاهِرَةِ

fa-innamā hiya zajratun wāḥidatun / fa-idhā hum bi-al-sāhirati

“It is only a single shout; suddenly they are on the open expanse.”

The context thus concerns resurrection and its events.

Al-rādifah does not lexically mean bringing the dead back.

Mardūdūna comes from R D D and concerns being returned.

Al-rādifah comes from R D F and describes one event following another.

Their shared resurrection setting should not merge the roots.

Study of R D D

6. Comparison with related roots

R D F (radf) and T B ʿ (tabaʿ)

Both approach the field of following.

Tabiʿa can mean following a person, route, command, or something else.

Qur’anic ittibāʿ frequently concerns deliberately following guidance, a messenger, a path, or desires.

R D F more often highlights being behind something or arriving after another thing in its clear uses.

For example, Al-Nāziʿāt 79:7 states:

تَتْبَعُهَا الرَّادِفَةُ

tatbaʿuhā al-rādifatu

Both fields appear together: the second event follows the first and is described as the following one.

The association shows proximity of meaning without making every ittibāʿ a radf.

Someone can follow guidance without a spatial position behind it.

An event can follow another without will, choice, or obedience being involved.

Thus Qur’anic R D F particularly emphasizes sequence and subsequent arrival, while T B ʿ has a broader field of following and adherence.

This is a tendency, rather than an absolute boundary across Arabic usage.

Study of T B ʿ

R D F (radf) and Q R B (qurb)

Qurb denotes proximity.

It may be spatial, temporal, or relational.

Radifa lakum in Al-Naml 27:72 concerns an event drawing near, overlapping with qaruba and danā.

The roots are not therefore completely identical.

Radifa retains a basic following relationship; qaruba expresses proximity without necessarily implying a preceding-following order.

Two things can be near without one having followed the other.

“Has drawn near” suits Al-Naml 27:72, but proximity should not define every R D F derivative.

Study of Q R B

R D F (radf) and ʿ Q B (ʿaqib)

ʿAqib and its derivatives also concern the rear, coming after, and consequences.

ʿĀqibah can denote a task’s outcome or end.

R D F can also describe an event following another.

Following and being a consequence nevertheless differ.

An event can follow another without directly resulting from it.

ʿĀqibah, by contrast, often emphasizes outcome or end.

Not every radf should therefore mean a causal consequence, punishment, or inevitable result.

Al-Naml 27:72’s approaching warned consequence comes from context; R D F does not always mean punishment.

Study of ʿ Q B

R D F (radf) and M D D (madad)

The roots appear together in Al-Anfāl 8:9:

إِنِّي مُمِدُّكُمْ بِأَلْفٍ مِنَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ مُرْدِفِينَ

innī mumiddukum bi-alfin mina al-malāʾikati murdifīna

“I will reinforce you with a thousand angels coming in succession.”

Mumiddukum concerns providing aid and additional strength.

Murdifīna describes following and joining.

They should remain distinct.

A force may join others and thereby increase their capacity, but added capacity and following are different aspects.

Here both contribute to the expression of divine aid.

Study of M D D

R D F (radf) and R J F (rajf)

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:6 and Al-Nāziʿāt 79:7 place al-rājifah and al-rādifah consecutively.

Despite their resemblance, they belong to different roots.

R J F concerns trembling and disturbance.

R D F concerns following and succession.

Al-rājifah should not mean the following one, nor al-rādifah the trembling one.

This matters particularly here: one event is described through trembling, the other through following.

Study of R J F

7. Later terminology: synonymy

Later linguistic and literary disciplines use tarāduf for a relationship between words with identical or very close meanings.

Mutarādif can describe words substitutable for one another in some settings.

The relationship with following is understandable: several expressions accompany one another in conveying a shared or related meaning.

Technical synonymy should not replace Qur’anic R D F’s meaning.

The Qur’an uses murdifīna, radifa, and al-rādifah, rather than the technical term for word synonymy.

Nor does the term mutarādif make every pair of related words identical in every respect.

T B ʿ and R D F can overlap in some sentences without identical semantic properties or ranges.

Technical synonymy is therefore a separate subject from this root’s basic Qur’anic definition.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

The preceding-following relationship should be retained while expressed according to context.

Radifa: followed, arrived behind, or, in some contexts, drew near.

Ridf: someone or something behind another; a following companion.

Radīf: a companion behind, a second rider, one who follows.

Ardafa: brought after, placed behind, followed, or seated someone behind oneself, according to construction.

Irdāf: bringing after, following, joining one thing to another.

Tarādafa: came in succession or stood one behind another.

Tarāduf: succession or following; later, the technical sense of synonymy.

Irtadafa: came behind; in riding, sat behind someone.

Istardafa: asked to ride behind another.

Rudāfā: people coming successively or taking turns.

Ridāfah: the position of a companion or deputy following a principal; historically, a title for some royal deputies.

Murdifīna: those following or bringing others after. “In succession” and “one after another” suit Al-Anfāl 8:9.

Murdafīna: those placed after others, or followed by others brought after them; the precise direction depends on the sentence’s analysis. This passive participle occurs in another recognized reading of Al-Anfāl 8:9.

Al-rādifah: the following one; an event subsequent to another.

The following renderings suit the three Qur’anic occurrences.

Al-Anfāl 8:9

إِنِّي مُمِدُّكُمْ بِأَلْفٍ مِنَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ مُرْدِفِينَ

innī mumiddukum bi-alfin mina al-malāʾikati murdifīna

“I will reinforce you with a thousand angels coming in succession.”

This preserves the explicit number and conveys sequence and following.

Al-Naml 27:72

قُلْ عَسَىٰ أَنْ يَكُونَ رَدِفَ لَكُمْ بَعْضُ الَّذِي تَسْتَعْجِلُونَ

qul ʿasā an yakūna radifa lakum baʿḍu alladhī tastaʿjilūna

“Say: Perhaps some of what you seek to hasten has drawn near to you.”

This fits radifa lakum in context while preserving ʿasā and baʿḍ.

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:7

تَتْبَعُهَا الرَّادِفَةُ

tatbaʿuhā al-rādifatu

“The subsequent event follows it.”

This preserves following without prematurely identifying al-rādifah solely as the second blast or tremor.

A smoother rendering could be:

“After it, another event follows.”

The word explanation should clarify that al-rādifah itself means the following one.

9. Conclusion

R D F concerns following, being behind, and one thing succeeding another.

Arabic uses range from two riders on one mount to successive people, groups, stars, and events.

Ridf and radīf can physically name a companion behind someone; temporal uses concern an event arriving after another.

The Qur’an has three principal occurrences.

Al-Anfāl 8:9 uses murdifīna in angelic aid, concerning following or bringing after. The active/passive reading changes the analyzed direction of the relation without removing the semantic core.

Al-Naml 27:72 uses radifa lakum for some of the promise the audience seeks to hasten. Following arrival approaches temporal proximity here, while ʿasā and baʿḍ should not become a fixed date or fulfillment of the entire promise.

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:7 names al-rādifah as following al-rājifah. The order is clear, while the second event’s physical character and precise identity do not come from the root alone.

The root should not always be restricted to riding behind, approaching, providing aid, or a second blast.

Later technical tarāduf, although related to the same field, should not be imposed on the Qur’anic uses.

Final definition: R D F denotes something being or coming after another. The relationship can be spatial or temporal and, in some settings, an approaching subsequent event. The Qur’an uses it for angels joining and following, part of a promise drawing near, and an event following another. Intervals, causes, outcomes, intensity, and precise identities require syntax and context rather than the root alone.
