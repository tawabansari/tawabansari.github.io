Core Meaning

The root ت ل و originally points to coming one after another, moving after something, following, and one thing standing in the extension of another. From this core, the meaning of tilawah emerges, because words, sentences, and verses in tilawah come after one another, and the listener or reader follows their path.

Tilawah is not originally only producing sound, pronouncing words, or looking at writing. This root carries movement, continuity, extension, and following. When its object is the Book or the verses, tilawah means presenting the verses continuously, preserving their relationship, and following their path of meaning.

For this reason, tilawah is not exactly identical with qira'ah, even though many translations render both as “reading.” Qira'ah emphasizes reading and gathering the parts of a text, while tilawah, in addition to reading, preserves the meaning of following after, pursuing, extension, and accompaniment.

A full study of the difference between tilawah, qira'ah, and tartil should be done in an independent article. In this entry, the principle to preserve is that tilawah cannot be reduced in every context to qira'ah or simple reading without losing part of the root's meaning.

Important Forms

تلا is the past verb and points to following, pursuing, or something coming in the extension of another. Its clearest use is about the moon, where no meaning of reading is involved and the verse itself reveals the root's foundational meaning:

وَالْقَمَرِ إِذَا تَلَاهَا

And by the moon, when it follows it.

Ash-Shams 91:2

In this verse, the moon follows the sun or is seen in the extension of its appearance. This use shows that reading is not the first and only meaning of the root; rather, it arises from the image of following and continuity.

يتلو is a present-tense form and shows continuity of action. When its object is the verses, its meaning is not only verbal reading. Continuous presentation, bringing one after another, and placing the audience before the flow of verses are also present in it:

يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِهِ

He recites His verses upon them in sequence.

Al-Jumu'ah 62:2

The present verb in this structure shows that reciting the verses is a continuous part of the Messenger's mission. The Messenger does not present the verses as isolated and unrelated pieces; he places the people before a flow of divine signs.

اتل is the imperative form and calls the addressee to recite what has been revealed. This form can indicate reading, continuous presentation, and following the content of revelation:

اتْلُ مَا أُوحِيَ إِلَيْكَ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ

Recite what has been revealed to you from the Book.

Al-'Ankabut 29:45

In this structure, ما أوحي إليك is the object of tilawah, and من الكتاب shows that the recited portion belongs to a larger whole. Therefore, reciting one part should not cut its relationship with the structure of the Book.

تتلو is a present-tense second-person form and can point to following, reading, and being engaged with a Book:

وَمَا كُنتَ تَتْلُو مِن قَبْلِهِ مِن كِتَابٍ وَلَا تَخُطُّهُ بِيَمِينِكَ

And before it, you did not recite any Book, nor did you write it with your right hand.

Al-'Ankabut 29:48

In this verse, تتلو is not limited to looking at writing. Reciting a written Book means following its lines, words, and meanings in order and being acquainted with its content. The verse negates that the Messenger, before the revelation of the Qur'an, had followed a Book in this way or written it with his own hand, so that the claim of borrowing from earlier writings would be raised.

تلاوة is the verbal noun of this root and points to the act of tilawah itself: reading and following something continuously in the extension of its structure and direction:

الَّذِينَ آتَيْنَاهُمُ الْكِتَابَ يَتْلُونَهُ حَقَّ تِلَاوَتِهِ

Those to whom We gave the Book follow it as is due in its tilawah.

Al-Baqarah 2:121

The phrase حق تلاوته shows that tilawah is not merely the occurrence of a sound-act. Tilawah can fulfill its due, or fall away from its due. The root meaning allows this due to include continuity in reading, correctly following the path of the Book, preserving the relationship among the verses, and accompanying the direction of guidance.

نتلو is a first-person plural present form. When its subject is the Lord, it points to the ordered, continuous, truth-oriented presentation of verses:

تِلْكَ آيَاتُ اللَّهِ نَتْلُوهَا عَلَيْكَ بِالْحَقِّ

These are the verses of The God; We recite them to you in truth.

Al-Baqarah 2:252

Divine tilawah does not mean movement of a tongue or human reading. The point is that the verses are presented to the Messenger from the Lord in order, connection, and the direction of truth. This tilawah may occur in the gradual process of revelation, but its meaning is not only the chronological order of descent; the guidance-oriented arrangement, semantic connection, and truth-centered presentation of the verses are also present.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of ت ل و is following after and extending. Something comes after something else and preserves its relation to it. This continuity may be material, as with the moon after the sun; linguistic, as with words and verses coming after one another; or guidance-related, as with following the direction of the Book in understanding and action.

For this reason, tilawah should not be reduced to the movement of the tongue and production of sound. When verses are recited, words come one after another; but the human being must also follow their path and not lose the relationship among their parts.

This point becomes especially important in حق تلاوته. If tilawah is only correct pronunciation of letters, then the expression حق تلاوته becomes limited to quality of pronunciation. But if the foundational meaning of the root is following and extension, the due of tilawah can include continuous reading, understanding the relationship among verses, recognizing the path of the Book, and accompanying its direction.

At the same time, one should not extract more from a lexical root than it can bear. ت ل و alone does not determine every method of interpretation. But it clearly provides a ground in which verses should not be understood as severed, island-like, and unrelated to each other.

Tilawah is compatible with understanding the Qur'an through the Qur'an itself, because this method places related verses after one another, follows the uses of a word, and allows parts of the Book to clarify one another. This is a natural methodological extension of the root, not the direct dictionary meaning of every instance of tilawah.

Material Image

The material image of this root is the movement of something behind or in the extension of something else. A caravan moves along a path, and someone behind it follows the same way. The footprints of those before clarify the direction of the next movement.

The clearest Qur'anic image is the moon following the sun:

وَالْقَمَرِ إِذَا تَلَاهَا

And by the moon, when it follows it.

Ash-Shams 91:2

In this image, the moon is seen in a relationship and extension. The same image is then transferred to language: a word comes after another word, a verse stands in the extension of another verse, and meaning is formed from the relationship of these parts.

But continuity does not mean the linear and historical order of every event. A narrative can place parts of a story together according to the aim of guidance without recounting every detail in chronological order. Tilawah in such a context concerns the continuity of the text and direction of meaning, not necessarily the time-record of every event.

So when the Qur'an says نتلو عليك من نبإ موسى وفرعون, the point is not that every event must be presented as a complete minute-by-minute historical report. The point is that selected parts of the account are presented to the Messenger in a guiding order and in the extension of a meaningful purpose.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, تلا points to coming after and following. One says تلاه, meaning he came after him, followed him, or stood after him. Then this same meaning moved into reading, because the reader follows letters, words, and sentences in order and brings them forth one after another on the tongue.

This semantic transfer is natural. From the view of this root, reading is following an ordered sequence. If order is broken or parts are separated without attention to their relation, part of the reality of tilawah is lost.

In later religious usage, tilawah has sometimes been limited to sound, melody, and correct pronunciation of letters. These matters may be qualities of tilawah, but they do not contain the whole meaning of the root. Tilawah still preserves within itself extension, following, accompaniment, and pursuit.

As for similar roots in other Semitic languages, no firm claim should enter the article unless a reliable and direct linguistic witness has been examined. The primary data of this entry should come from Arabic and from the Qur'an's own usages.

The Moon Following the Sun

The Qur'an says:

وَالشَّمْسِ وَضُحَاهَا

By the sun and its brightness.

Ash-Shams 91:1

وَالْقَمَرِ إِذَا تَلَاهَا

And by the moon, when it follows it.

Ash-Shams 91:2

This verse is the clearest witness for the root meaning. The moon does not read anything; it follows the sun. Therefore, the meaning of ت ل و must begin from following, not from reading.

Reading in this root is a meaning that emerged from following letters, words, and meanings. If this foundational meaning is forgotten, tilawah is reduced to vocal qira'ah, and the element of continuity and following disappears from translation.

Reciting Verses to the People

In the prayer of Ibrahim and Isma'il, it says:

رَبَّنَا وَابْعَثْ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًا مِّنْهُمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِكَ

Our Lord, raise among them a Messenger from themselves who recites Your verses upon them.

Al-Baqarah 2:129

And in Surah Al-Jumu'ah:

هُوَ الَّذِي بَعَثَ فِي الْأُمِّيِّينَ رَسُولًا مِّنْهُمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِهِ

He is the One who raised among the ummiyyin a Messenger from themselves, reciting His verses upon them.

Al-Jumu'ah 62:2

يتلو عليهم آياته does not only mean vocal reading. The Messenger delivers the verses to the people in sequence, presents them in an ordered flow, and places society before a chain of divine signs.

The preposition على also shows that the verses are presented upon them. They are placed before the flow of revelation and must clarify their relation to it.

Reciting What Has Been Revealed

The Qur'an says:

اتْلُ مَا أُوحِيَ إِلَيْكَ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ

Recite what has been revealed to you from the Book.

Al-'Ankabut 29:45

اتل in this verse relates to presenting and following what has been revealed from the Book. من الكتاب shows that the revealed portion comes from a larger whole and must be followed in relation to that same whole.

This structure is not compatible with separating verses from the Book. What is recited is part of the Book, and its place becomes clearer through its connections.

Reciting a Book Before the Qur'an

The Qur'an addresses the Messenger:

وَمَا كُنتَ تَتْلُو مِن قَبْلِهِ مِن كِتَابٍ وَلَا تَخُطُّهُ بِيَمِينِكَ

And before it, you did not recite any Book, nor did you write it with your right hand.

Al-'Ankabut 29:48

تتلو here does not point merely to looking at a written page. Reciting a written Book means following its lines and meanings, reading its contents in extension, and being acquainted with it.

The verse negates that the Messenger before the Qur'an followed a Book in this way or wrote it with his own hand. Therefore, no ground remains for attributing the Qur'an to direct borrowing from the study of previous books.

The Right of Tilawah

The Qur'an says:

الَّذِينَ آتَيْنَاهُمُ الْكِتَابَ يَتْلُونَهُ حَقَّ تِلَاوَتِهِ أُولَٰئِكَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهِ

Those to whom We gave the Book follow it as is due in its tilawah; they are the ones who have faith in it.

Al-Baqarah 2:121

This verse shows that tilawah can fulfill its due or fail to do so. If tilawah were only pronunciation of words, its connection with faith in the Book would become severely limited.

حق تلاوته can indicate that the Book must be followed in its continuity, that artificial separation should not be created among its parts, and that the reader must take a responsible stance before the path of its guidance.

The translation “they follow it as is due” preserves the root's core better than “they read it correctly.” Still, verbal reading is also part of tilawah and should not be removed from the meaning. The point is that reading is not all of tilawah.

Tilawah and Continuity of Verses

When the Qur'an speaks of reciting verses, the root itself points to coming one after another. A verse comes after another verse, and meaning forms in the extension of this chain.

This continuity does not necessarily mean that every surah or story states every event in complete chronological order. The Qur'an is not a linear history book. Pieces of a story may be presented in different places according to the aim of guidance, warning, comparison, or argument.

The continuity of tilawah is at the level of text and guidance. The presented parts must be followed in relation to one another, and their shared direction must be recognized. Therefore, narrative order may be guidance-oriented, not the complete chronological order of events.

This point matters for understanding the Qur'an. Separating one verse from what comes before and after it, or separating a term from its other uses, can cut the path of tilawah. Continuous tilawah asks the reader to follow onward, return, compare, and see the connections among the verses.

For this reason, understanding the Qur'an through the Qur'an itself is compatible with the core of tilawah. Verses confirm, detail, and clarify one another. This method does not allow a single verse to be turned alone into a tool for constructing a ruling or belief that conflicts with the overall structure of the Book.

Story-Tilawah and the Difference Between Continuity and Historical Order

The Qur'an says:

نَتْلُو عَلَيْكَ مِن نَّبَإِ مُوسَىٰ وَفِرْعَوْنَ بِالْحَقِّ

We recite to you part of the account of Musa and Pharaoh in truth.

Al-Qasas 28:3

نتلو عليك here points to the ordered and purposeful presentation of parts of a story. من نبإ موسى وفرعون shows that a portion of a broader account has been selected.

This selection does not have to include every event according to the complete chronological order of history. The continuity of tilawah here concerns the semantic coherence of the selected parts. Each piece serves the guiding aim of the surah and is presented in the extension of its argument.

بالحق also shows that the criterion of story-tilawah is truth and the direction of guidance, not entertainment, fantasy, or recording every historical detail.

Divine Tilawah

In several verses, the subject of tilawah is the Lord Himself:

تِلْكَ آيَاتُ اللَّهِ نَتْلُوهَا عَلَيْكَ بِالْحَقِّ

These are the verses of The God; We recite them to you in truth.

Al-Baqarah 2:252

Divine tilawah does not mean human reading or vocal movement. This expression points to presenting, delivering, and making the verses appear one after another in the order of truth.

The gradual descent of the verses may be one manifestation of this tilawah, but the meaning of the verse should not be limited only to the chronological order of revelation. بالحق shows that the verses are presented to the Messenger in correct relation, correct direction, and a guidance-oriented structure.

Thus, the difference between divine and human tilawah lies in the subject and the mode of the act. The human being reads the words and follows the meaning; the Lord presents the verses to the Messenger in the form of revelation, order, and truth. The shared core in both is continuity and extension, but the form of realization is different.

Tilawah and the Deniers' Stance Becoming Visible

The Qur'an says:

وَإِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتُنَا بَيِّنَاتٍ تَعْرِفُ فِي وُجُوهِ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا الْمُنكَرَ

And when Our clear verses are recited upon them, you recognize the munkar in the faces of those who cover truth.

Al-Hajj 22:72

تتلى is passive, and it shifts focus to the verses themselves and the effect of their being presented. They face clear and continuous verses, and the reaction of denial becomes visible on their faces.

This verse brings the roots ت ل و, ع ر ف, and ن ك ر into one structure. The verses are recited, the munkar is recognized in the faces, and the human inward stance before the flow of truth becomes visible.

Tilawah at Night and the State of Khushu'

The Qur'an says about a group among the People of the Book:

لَيْسُوا سَوَاءً ۗ مِّنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ أُمَّةٌ قَائِمَةٌ يَتْلُونَ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ آنَاءَ اللَّيْلِ وَهُمْ يَسْجُدُونَ

They are not all alike; among the People of the Book is an upright community who recite the verses of The God during the hours of the night while they prostrate.

Al 'Imran 3:113

In this verse, tilawah is accompanied by standing, night, and prostration. This association shows that tilawah is not only an activity of the tongue; it is also tied to the person's existential state, humility, and orientation.

The verses pass through the tongue, but at the same time the body and direction of the human being also stand before the Lord.

Tilawah of the Book, Salat, and Spending

The Qur'an says:

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَتْلُونَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَأَنفَقُوا مِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ سِرًّا وَعَلَانِيَةً

Those who recite the Book of The God, establish salat, and spend from what We have provided them, secretly and openly.

Fatir 35:29

Tilawah of the Book in this verse is accompanied by salat and spending. This association shows that complete tilawah is not detached from practical life.

The human being follows the Book, and its effects appear in salat, spending, and behavior. If tilawah is only vocal reading and finds no relation to action, part of the root's semantic extension is cut.

Similar Uses

ت ل و sometimes points to material following, as with the moon following the sun.

Sometimes it points to presenting the verses to the people one after another, as in يتلو عليهم آياته.

Sometimes it points to following and reading what has been revealed from the Book, as in اتل ما أوحي إليك من الكتاب.

Sometimes it points to worthy and responsible tilawah of the Book, as in يتلونه حق تلاوته.

Sometimes it points to the ordered and guidance-oriented presentation of part of a story, as in نتلو عليك من نبإ موسى وفرعون.

Sometimes its subject is the Lord, and it points to presenting verses in the order of truth, as in نتلوها عليك بالحق.

Sometimes it points to reading verses in an atmosphere of worship and humility, as in يتلون آيات الله آناء الليل.

Sometimes it is accompanied by social and devotional action, as in يتلون كتاب الله وأقاموا الصلاة وأنفقوا.

In all these uses, the core of continuity, following, and standing in the extension of something is preserved.

Grammar and Structure

In والقمر إذا تلاها, the pronoun ها returns to the sun. The verb تلا points to the moon following it and shows the root's foundational meaning without any connection to reading.

In يتلو عليهم آياته, the present verb conveys continuity. The preposition على also shows that the verses are presented upon the audience and that they are placed before the flow of revelation.

In اتل ما أوحي إليك من الكتاب, ما is the object of tilawah, and من الكتاب preserves the relation of the revealed portion to the whole Book.

In يتلونه حق تلاوته, the expression حق تلاوته measures the quality and worthiness of the act. Tilawah is not only that reading happens; its right must also be fulfilled.

In نتلو عليك من نبإ موسى وفرعون, the preposition من shows that part of the account is presented, not necessarily every historical detail. بالحق also clarifies the direction and criterion of this selection.

In نتلوها عليك بالحق, the subject of tilawah is the Lord, and بالحق shows that the presentation of the verses comes with truth, proportion, and right direction.

In تتلى عليهم آياتنا بينات, the passive form moves focus from the reciter to the verses themselves and to the effect of their presentation.

In يتلون آيات الله آناء الليل, the time expression indicates repetition and continuity during the hours of the night, and وهم يسجدون states their accompanying condition.

Nearby Roots

ق ر أ relates to reading and gathering the parts of a text. Qira'ah emphasizes reading and the gathering of words in tongue or mind, but tilawah also preserves the meaning of following, extension, and pursuit. They are not complete synonyms.

ر ت ل points to order, clear arrangement, spacing, and composed presentation. Tartil shows the ordered and clear quality of presenting words; tilawah shows the continuous movement in their extension.

ت ب ع points broadly to following and pursuit. Tilawah is close to taba'a, but in the use of verses and the Book, the meaning of reading and ordered presentation has also formed within it.

ق ص ص points to following traces and narrating events through pursuing their signs. Qasas highlights the structure of tracking events; tilawah highlights their continuous and guidance-oriented presentation.

ذ ك ر points to remembrance and making meaning present. Tilawah can be a means of dhikr, but it is not itself dhikr.

د ر س points to study, examination, and sustained engagement with a text. Tilawah can be part of dars, but dars emphasizes study and examination more strongly.

ب ي ن and ت ب ي ي ن point to becoming clear and making clear. Tilawah of verses can prepare the ground for tabyin, but tilawah itself does not mean detailed explanation.

These three roots show three different directions of encountering a text: qira'ah relates to reading and gathering the parts; tartil to order and clarity of presentation; and tilawah to continuity, extension, and following the path of the text.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

تلا: followed, came after, moved in its extension; depending on context.

والقمر إذا تلاها: and the moon when it follows it.

يتلو عليهم آياته: he presents His verses upon them in sequence; he recites His verses to them in the extension of one another.

اتل ما أوحي إليك: recite what has been revealed to you; present it continuously and follow it.

وما كنت تتلو من قبله من كتاب: before it, you did not recite and follow any Book.

يتلونه حق تلاوته: they follow it as is due; they fulfill the right of its reading, continuity, and following.

نتلو عليك: We recite to you; We present to you continuously and in the order of guidance.

تتلى عليهم آياتنا: Our verses are recited and presented upon them in sequence.

يتلون كتاب الله: they recite the Book of The God; they continuously read and follow it.

تلاوة: tilawah, continuous reading, following, and pursuing in the extension of meaning and order.

Related Note

For a detailed study of the difference between tilawah, qira'ah, and tartil, and the consequences of that difference for understanding the Qur'an, an independent article is needed. In this entry, it is enough to make clear that these words are not complete synonyms.

Always turning tilawah into “qira'ah” or “reading” can hide the meanings of continuity, following, and accompanying the path of the Book. But historically studying this reduction and attributing a specific motive to it requires an independent and documented study.

Summary

The root ت ل و indicates coming one after another, following, moving after something, and one thing standing in the extension of another. Its clearest witness is والقمر إذا تلاها: the moon follows the sun, and no meaning of reading is present in that use.

From this core, tilawah of verses and the Book takes shape. Words and verses come after one another, but the root's meaning does not remain limited to pronunciation of words. Tilawah also preserves following the path of the Book, maintaining the relationship among verses, and standing in the extension of their meaning.

The continuity of tilawah does not necessarily mean the chronological order of every event. In the Qur'an's narratives, parts of history may be selected and placed together according to the purpose of guidance. What tilawah preserves is the coherence of the text, continuity of meaning, and direction of truth.

Divine tilawah also does not mean human reading. The Lord presents the verses to the Messenger in the form of revelation, order, and truth. Gradual revelation may be part of this process, but divine tilawah is not limited to the chronological order of descent.

The difference between tilawah and qira'ah is that qira'ah emphasizes reading and gathering the parts of a text, while tilawah also preserves following, continuity, and extension. Its difference from tartil is that tartil shows the quality of order and clarity in expression, while tilawah shows movement in the extension of the text.

In Forqan translation, تلا and يتلو should not always be translated only as “read” and “reads.” Depending on context, “followed,” “followed through,” “presented in sequence,” “recited in the extension of one another,” or “continuously followed the Book” may be more precise.

This root also provides a strong ground for understanding the Qur'an through the Qur'an. The verses should be followed together, in the extension of one another, and in light of the overall structure of the Book. Such a method is more consistent with the reality of tilawah than a fragmented approach that separates verses from the Qur'an's internal network.
