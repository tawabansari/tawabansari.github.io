Foundational Meaning

The root ك ت م fundamentally means to cover, conceal, hold something firmly inside, and prevent something present with a person from becoming visible.

This root is most often used for something a person knows, possesses, has seen, has understood, or bears witness to, but keeps inside and does not bring out.

كتم is not merely silence. Silence may come from ignorance, lack of need, fear, wisdom, or having nothing to say. But كتم usually has meaning when something is inside or present with a person, and its path into speech, testimony, announcement, or visibility is blocked.

Important Forms

In the Qur'an, this root appears only in verbal Form I. كَتَمَ means he concealed, covered, or held something inside. يَكْتُمُ and يَكْتُمُونَ mean he conceals or they conceal. تَكْتُمُونَ means you conceal. نَكْتُمُ means we conceal.

The Qur'anic forms of this root usually concern concealing truth, testimony, what The God has made clear, or a reality that a person keeps in the heart or with himself.

In the Qur'an, كتم is often an inward-linguistic act: knowing and not saying, possessing and not showing, witnessing and not testifying, or holding back a truth that should move from the inside into the field of disclosure.

Linguistic Note

The semantic center of ك ت م turns around keeping something in concealment and preventing it from moving into speech, testimony, or the awareness of others. This root does not simply mean "being hidden." Usually, it means "concealing."

That means there is an agent who has something and holds it back from appearance, expression, or testimony. In كتم, something remains stored inside or with a person: knowledge, testimony, truth, faith, or a report that could be disclosed.

For this reason, كتم often carries responsibility in Qur'anic contexts. Concealing something that should be spoken can block understanding, judgment, guidance, or justice.

At the same time, the root itself is not always negative in lexical meaning. Its ethical value depends on context. Concealing truth and testimony is blameworthy, but concealing faith in a dangerous environment, as in the case of the believer from Pharaoh's household, can have a protective function.

Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is something held inside a sealed and tight container, like water held in a waterskin or firm vessel with no leakage.

The same image can be seen at the level of speech and testimony: a word remaining behind closed lips, testimony trapped in the chest, or knowledge that does not find a way out into public awareness.

Arabic lexical sources use images such as tight stitching, a leakless vessel, or a being that does not reveal its inner sign to explain this field of meaning. In a later expanded version, a precise and reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or early Arabic usage may be added. But the core image is clear: كتم means to confine something inside so that its sign or flow does not reach the outside.

In this image, chest, heart, tongue, and testimony come close together. The truth is inside; the tongue can be its path outward; but the agent closes that path. This is why the Qur'an says about concealing testimony: آثِمٌ قَلْبُهُ. Concealment is not only the tongue failing to move; it is an inward decision to hold truth back.

Older and Classical Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic, كتم is used for concealing a report, secret, testimony, or inward matter. كَتَمَ السِّرَّ means he concealed the secret. كَتَمَ الشَّهَادَةَ means he did not give testimony or concealed it.

In this root, concealment is usually intentional and connected to holding something inside. The root fits the idea of compressing meaning, report, or truth within the human being.

Therefore, when the Qur'an speaks of concealing truth, concealing testimony, or concealing what The God has sent down, the issue is not merely having nothing to say. The issue is holding back something that should become visible for guidance, judgment, or clarification of truth.

Qur'anic Usage

One of the earliest Qur'anic uses of this root appears opposite making something visible:

وَأَعْلَمُ مَا تُبْدُونَ وَمَا كُنْتُمْ تَكْتُمُونَ

And I know what you make visible and what you used to conceal. Al-Baqarah 2:33

Here كتم stands opposite ibda', making visible. تُبْدُونَ means you make visible, while تَكْتُمُونَ means you hold inside and do not disclose. This contrast shows that كتم is not merely something being unseen; it is the agent's concealment of something held with him.

One major use of the root is concealing truth after knowing it:

وَلَا تَلْبِسُوا الْحَقَّ بِالْبَاطِلِ وَتَكْتُمُوا الْحَقَّ وَأَنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ

And do not clothe truth with falsehood, nor conceal the truth while you know. Al-Baqarah 2:42

In this verse, كتم is joined to knowledge: وَأَنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ. The concealment is not from ignorance. The truth is known, but it is kept back from expression and appearance.

The same meaning appears in concealing what The God has made clear from the Book:

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَكْتُمُونَ مَا أَنْزَلْنَا مِنَ الْبَيِّنَاتِ وَالْهُدَىٰ

Indeed, those who conceal what We sent down of clear signs and guidance... Al-Baqarah 2:159

Here كتم concerns concealing clear signs and guidance: something sent for people's guidance is held by a group and kept away from public understanding.

The same theme continues:

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَكْتُمُونَ مَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ

Indeed, those who conceal what The God has sent down of the Book... Al-Baqarah 2:174

Here again, كتم relates to concealing revealed content or a part of the truth of the Book. The axis of meaning is holding back something that should be visible and accessible.

Concealing Testimony

كتم is also used for testimony:

وَلَا تَكْتُمُوا الشَّهَادَةَ

And do not conceal the testimony. Al-Baqarah 2:283

If testimony is concealed, it loses its function. Testimony exists to make truth visible in the place of judgment. Therefore, كتم الشهادة means holding testimony inside when truth or judgment needs it to become visible.

The same verse makes the force of this meaning clearer:

وَمَنْ يَكْتُمْهَا فَإِنَّهُ آثِمٌ قَلْبُهُ

And whoever conceals it, then his heart is sinful. Al-Baqarah 2:283

Here concealing testimony is connected to the heart, because كتم is not merely outward silence. It is the intentional holding of truth inside. The tongue is the path by which testimony exits, but the main decision is closed within the person.

Concealment on the Day of Judgment

In Surat an-Nisa, كتم is used for concealing speech or report on the Day of Judgment:

يَوْمَئِذٍ يَوَدُّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا وَعَصَوُا الرَّسُولَ لَوْ تُسَوَّىٰ بِهِمُ الْأَرْضُ وَلَا يَكْتُمُونَ اللَّهَ حَدِيثًا

On that day, those who disbelieved and disobeyed the Messenger will wish that the earth were leveled over them, and they will not conceal any report from The God. An-Nisa 4:42

Here كتم حديث means concealing speech or report. The verse presents a setting where concealment before The God becomes meaningless. What could be trapped in a person's chest, tongue, or narrative in this world will not remain hidden before the knowledge and judgment of The God.

Hidden Faith

كتم is also used for hidden faith:

وَقَالَ رَجُلٌ مُؤْمِنٌ مِنْ آلِ فِرْعَوْنَ يَكْتُمُ إِيمَانَهُ

And a believing man from Pharaoh's household, who concealed his faith, said... Ghafir 40:28

Here كتم is not necessarily negative, because the believing man concealed his faith in a dangerous environment under Pharaoh's domination. This verse shows that the root itself is not always condemnatory. Its value depends on context. Concealing truth from people for gain and control is one thing; concealing faith in a dangerous situation is another.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

Concealing truth in Al-Baqarah 2:42 is not the same as concealing faith in Ghafir 40:28. In the first, known truth is concealed from people and this concealment blocks guidance and justice. In the second, a believer conceals his faith inside a dangerous Pharaonic structure.

Concealing testimony also differs from concealing a report. Testimony is witness that should be spoken in the place of truth and judgment. A report is speech or narration that may be concealed or disclosed.

Concealing what The God has sent down is also not the same as concealing a private secret. In the verses of Al-Baqarah, the subject is clear signs, guidance, and the Book: knowledge with public, guiding, and responsibility-bearing force. In this context, كتم concerns depriving others of access to truth.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

The contrast between كتم and ibda' in Al-Baqarah 2:33 is important. مَا تُبْدُونَ and مَا كُنْتُمْ تَكْتُمُونَ show two opposite directions: making visible and concealing.

In Al-Baqarah 2:42, the phrase وَأَنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ shows that كتم can be joined to knowledge. Sometimes the issue is not ignorance; it is refusing to say what has been known.

In Al-Baqarah 2:283, the pronoun in يَكْتُمْهَا returns to testimony. Then the verse says آثِمٌ قَلْبُهُ. This structure shows that كتم, although outwardly connected to language and speech, is rooted in an inward decision of the heart.

In Ghafir 40:28, يَكْتُمُ إِيمَانَهُ shows that the object of كتم can be faith: something inward that a person does not disclose. This use must be read carefully in its ethical context.

Difference From Nearby Roots

خ ف ي relates to being hidden or concealed. Something may be hidden without anyone intentionally hiding it. But كتم usually means the deliberate concealment of something present with the agent.

س ت ر relates to covering and placing a veil. S T R may cover a fault, body, or outward thing. كتم is more connected to holding knowledge, testimony, faith, a secret, or speech inside.

غ ط ي relates to covering the surface of something, as with an external layer. كتم is more inward and linguistic.

أ خ ف ي relates to hiding or keeping concealed, but كتم often has the stronger sense of keeping something with oneself and preventing its expression.

ك ذ ب relates to lying. كتم is not always lying; a person may say nothing without saying something false. However, concealing truth can create the ground for distortion, misguidance, or unjust judgment.

ل ب س relates to mixing and making something ambiguous. In Al-Baqarah 2:42, the Qur'an mentions both clothing truth with falsehood and concealing the truth. L B S means mixing truth with falsehood; كتم means concealing the truth itself.

ب د و and ibda' relate to making visible and bringing into appearance, and they stand opposite كتم. Ibda' means bringing something into the field of visibility; كتم means holding it inside and not allowing it to appear.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

كتم: concealed, covered, held inside, did not disclose.

تَكْتُمُونَ: you conceal, you hold inside, you do not make visible.

يَكْتُمُونَ الْحَقَّ: they conceal the truth, they do not disclose the truth, they hold the truth back.

يَكْتُمُونَ مَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ: they conceal what The God has sent down, they prevent it from becoming visible.

لَا تَكْتُمُوا الشَّهَادَةَ: do not conceal the testimony, do not hold testimony inside, do not block testimony from becoming visible.

يَكْتُمْهَا: he conceals it, he holds it back and does not disclose it.

يَكْتُمُ إِيمَانَهُ: he concealed his faith, he did not disclose his faith, he held his faith inside.

وَلَا يَكْتُمُونَ اللَّهَ حَدِيثًا: they do not conceal any speech from The God, they do not imprison any report before The God.

Linguistic Summary

The root ك ت م indicates concealing, holding inside, and preventing something from moving into the field of visibility. In its material image, truth, testimony, faith, or speech is held in the chest and with the person, while its path into expression or disclosure remains closed.

In the Qur'an, this root most often appears in relation to concealing truth, the Book, clear signs, testimony, speech, or faith. Its ethical value depends on context: concealing truth and testimony is blameworthy, while concealing faith in a dangerous environment can serve a protective function.

The difference between كتم and Kh F Y, S T R, Gh T Y, Akhfa, K Dh B, L B S, and ibda' should be preserved. كتم is not merely being hidden, not every kind of covering, and not lying. It is holding back and not disclosing something present with a person.
