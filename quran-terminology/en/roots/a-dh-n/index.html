Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root A DH N

Ear, hearing, receiving sound; and more broadly permission, announcement, acceptance, and the opening of a path for an act to occur

Important Forms from This Root

أُذُن / آذَان (udhun / ādhān)

udhun / ādhān; ear / ears, the bodily opening for receiving sound.

أَذِنَ (adhina)

adhina; heard, listened, accepted, obeyed, or permitted, depending on construction.

إِذْن (idhn)

idhn; permission, opening of access, enabling of occurrence.

أَذَّنَ / أَذَان (adhdhana / adhān)

adhdhana / adhān; announced, called publicly; announcement, making a message heard.

إِيذَان / مُؤَذِّن / مَأْذُون (īdhān / muʾadhdhin / maʾdhūn)

īdhān; notification. Muʾadhdhin; announcer. Maʾdhūn; permitted or authorized.

Method Note

The root A DH N loses much of its Qur’anic logic if it is reduced to the ordinary English word “permission.” It begins with a bodily image: the ear, the opening through which sound is received. In the Qur’an, that material image expands into wider fields: public announcement, social permission, legal permission, and even the enabling of events within the created order.

So إِذْن (idhn) is not always verbal or administrative permission. In many verses, idhn means that the path of effect and occurrence has been opened within The God’s order. This does not mean moral approval of every event. It means no event acts independently outside The God’s law, power, knowledge, and pattern.

Central Meaning

The core meaning is openness for reception or occurrence. In the body, the ear is the passage through which sound enters. In society, permission is the passage by which an action may enter a lawful space. In creation, idhn is the passage by which an event enters actuality.

This is why “ear” and “permission” are not accidentally related. Both involve an opened path of relation. If the ear is closed, sound does not enter. If permission is absent, an action does not enter the relevant order of possibility or right. This root therefore helps explain the relation between human beings, command, world, and The God.

Material Image

The material image is the human ear: an opening at the side of the head through which sound enters. Unlike the eye, the ear does not need direct face-to-face orientation. A person may turn away, but sound can still come from the surrounding space and reach the ear. This makes the ear a strong image of receptivity.

From this image, the meaning of permission becomes clearer. Something needs an opening through which it can be heard, accepted, or made effective. Bodily, that opening is the ear. Socially, it is the permission of the one who has the right. Cosmically, it is the law and pattern The God has placed for an event to occur.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, أُذُن (udhun) first relates to the ear and hearing. From that field it expands to knowing, listening, announcing, and permitting. أَذَان (adhān) is announcement and making known. The one who gives adhān is not merely producing sound; he carries a message to people’s hearing and makes them aware of a time, call, or situation.

أَذِنَ لَهُ (adhina lahu) means he permitted him: the barrier is removed and the way into an action is opened. This matters for Qur’anic reading because idhn is not only an inner idea. Sometimes it protects a social boundary; sometimes it makes a message public; and sometimes it names the created condition by which an event becomes possible.

Qur’anic Usage

Ear as the Organ of Receiving Sound

In يَجْعَلُونَ أَصَابِعَهُمْ فِي آذَانِهِمْ (yajʿalūna aṣābiʿahum fī ādhānihim), the word آذَان (ādhān) means ears 2:19. The image is bodily and physical: they put their fingers into their ears. The ear is the passage of hearing, but that passage is blocked by the finger.

At the first level, then, udhun is the path of receiving sound. Closing the ear means closing one of the paths of perception.

One Who Listens and Receives Speech

Surat al-Tawbah says: وَمِنْهُمُ الَّذِينَ يُؤْذُونَ النَّبِيَّ وَيَقُولُونَ هُوَ أُذُنٌ (waminhum alladhīna yuʾdhūna al-nabiyya wayaqūlūna huwa udhun); among them are those who harm the Prophet and say, “He is an ear” 9:61.

They meant it as mockery: he listens too much, he is too receptive, as if he accepts anything. But the Qur’anic answer is precise: قُلْ أُذُنُ خَيْرٍ لَكُمْ (qul udhunu khayrin lakum); say, he is an ear of good for you. The Prophet receives good; he is not a receiver of every lie and corruption. Here the word moves from the body part to the quality of listening and receptive mercy.

Adhān as Public Announcement

In وَأَذَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ إِلَى النَّاسِ (wa-adhānun mina allāhi warasūlihi ilā al-nāsi), أَذَان (adhān) means public announcement 9:3. The message must leave concealment and reach people’s hearing. Adhān is not merely sound; it is a message brought into the field of hearing and awareness.

The same meaning appears in وَأَذِّنْ فِي النَّاسِ بِالْحَجِّ (wa-adhdhin fī al-nāsi bil-ḥajji); announce among the people for the pilgrimage 22:27. The form أَذَّنَ (adhdhana) brings out the public and emphatic nature of the announcement.

Social Permission and Entering a Private Boundary

Surat al-Nūr says: فَإِنْ لَمْ تَجِدُوا فِيهَا أَحَدًا فَلَا تَدْخُلُوهَا حَتَّىٰ يُؤْذَنَ لَكُمْ (fa-in lam tajidū fīhā aḥadan falā tadkhulūhā ḥattā yuʾdhana lakum); if you find no one in them, do not enter until permission is given to you 24:28.

A home has a boundary. Entering without permission crosses the right of the one who owns or occupies that space. Here idhn means the opening of access by the one who has the right. Permission is therefore not mere politeness; it is the structure of respect for human boundaries.

Cosmic Idhn and the Possibility of Occurrence

The Qur’an says: وَمَا كَانَ لِنَفْسٍ أَنْ تَمُوتَ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ كِتَابًا مُؤَجَّلًا (wamā kāna linafsin an tamūta illā bi-idhni allāhi kitāban muʾajjalan); no soul dies except by The God’s idhn, as an appointed writing 3:145.

Here idhn is not social permission. Death is a cosmic event within appointed term, causes, time, and divine law. In this context, بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (bi-idhni allāh) means within The God’s enabling order, decree, and governing pattern.

The same logic matters in وَمَا هُمْ بِضَارِّينَ بِهِ مِنْ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (wamā hum biḍārrīna bihi min aḥadin illā bi-idhni allāh); they cannot harm anyone through it except by The God’s idhn 2:102. This does not mean The God morally approves the harm. It means even harm has no independent existence or effect outside The God’s law, power, and created order.

Idhn in Healing and Miraculous Effect

In وَأُحْيِي الْمَوْتَىٰ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (wa-uḥyī al-mawtā bi-idhni allāh), Jesus speaks of bringing the dead to life by The God’s idhn 3:49. The wording clarifies the relation of effect. The effect appears through the messenger, but the final possibility and power of realization belong to The God.

For this reason, بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (bi-idhni allāh) protects the meaning from shirk. A miracle may appear through a prophet, but independent power is not given to the prophet. Idhn marks the boundary between being an instrument and The God’s ownership over the possibility of effect.

Idhn in Growth and Yielding Fruit

In وَالْبَلَدُ الطَّيِّبُ يَخْرُجُ نَبَاتُهُ بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهِ (wal-baladu al-ṭayyibu yakhruju nabātuhu bi-idhni rabbihi), idhn is used for a natural process 7:58. The good land brings forth its vegetation by the idhn of its Lord.

Soil, water, light, seed, season, and capacity all work together, but their working is not independent of The God. Idhn here is the channel of possibility for growth: the created pattern through which the Lord enables the land to bring forth.

Idhn in Revelation

In نَزَلَ بِهِ عَلَىٰ قَلْبِكَ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (nazala bihi ʿalā qalbika bi-idhni allāh), the descent of revelation also occurs within divine idhn 2:97. The message, the carrier, the receiving heart, and the time of descent all belong to the order opened by The God.

Listening, Acceptance, and Structural Submission

At the beginning of Surat al-Inshiqāq, the Qur’an says: إِذَا السَّمَاءُ انشَقَّتْ وَأَذِنَتْ لِرَبِّهَا وَحُقَّتْ (idhā al-samāʾu inshaqqat wa-adhinat lirabbihā waḥuqqat); when the sky is split, and it listens/responds to its Lord, and it is rightfully bound to do so 84:1-2.

This usage is important because أَذِنَتْ (adhinat) is said of the sky. This is not a human physical ear, nor social permission. It is receptivity and structural submission before The God. The sky offers no resistance to the command of its Lord; what must occur in it occurs within the divine order.

Collocation Notes

When Arabic says أَذِنَ لَهُ (adhina lahu), the meaning is often permission: the way to act is opened for someone. This appears in حَتَّىٰ يُؤْذَنَ لَكُمْ (ḥattā yuʾdhana lakum) in 24:28. But in some contexts, listening, acceptance, or submission becomes prominent, as in وَأَذِنَتْ لِرَبِّهَا (wa-adhinat lirabbihā) in 84:2.

The phrase بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (bi-idhni allāh) must be translated according to context. Sometimes “by The God’s permission” is enough. In many cosmic contexts, however, the meaning of enabling, command, power, law, and divine pattern should also be felt.

Difference from Nearby Words

S M ʿ is hearing itself, while A DH N is the ear or the channel of reception. More broadly, idhn is the path by which a matter enters occurrence. Later Arabic ijāzah often has a formal or legal sense, but Qur’anic idhn can be bodily, social, legal, or cosmic.

AMR is command, while idhn is the opening of the way for an action or occurrence. A divine command may create idhn, but idhn is not always a spoken command. Q D R is measuring and setting limits; idhn is the possibility of occurrence within those measured limits.

Mashīʾah relates to divine will and direction of willing, while idhn is the stage in which an event is allowed into realization. Sunnah is The God’s stable way or pattern in the world; idhn is the possibility of an event acting within that pattern.

Translation Method

بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (bi-idhni allāh) should not always be translated mechanically as “by The God’s permission.” In some verses that is accurate and enough. But in many cosmic contexts, a deeper sense is needed: by The God’s idhn, within The God’s command, through The God’s enabling, within The God’s law and pattern, through the channel The God has opened for it, or by the power and permission that belong to The God.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

فِي آذَانِهِمْ (fī ādhānihim): in their ears.

هُوَ أُذُنٌ (huwa udhun): he is an ear; or he is very listening and receptive.

قُلْ أُذُنُ خَيْرٍ لَكُمْ (qul udhunu khayrin lakum): say, he is receptive to good for you.

وَأَذَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ (wa-adhānun mina allāhi warasūlihi): an announcement from The God and His Messenger.

وَأَذِّنْ فِي النَّاسِ بِالْحَجِّ (wa-adhdhin fī al-nāsi bil-ḥajji): announce among the people for the pilgrimage.

حَتَّىٰ يُؤْذَنَ لَكُمْ (ḥattā yuʾdhana lakum): until permission is given to you; until the way of entry is opened for you.

إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (illā bi-idhni allāh): except by The God’s idhn; in cosmic context, except within The God’s enabling order and law.

بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهِ (bi-idhni rabbihi): by the idhn of its Lord; in the context of growth, through the channel of possibility and pattern opened by its Lord.

وَأَذِنَتْ لِرَبِّهَا (wa-adhinat lirabbihā): and it listened/responded to its Lord; or it became receptive and submitted to its Lord.

Summary

The root A DH N begins with the ear, the channel through which sound is received. From that image come announcement, listening, permission, acceptance, and enabling.

In the Qur’an, idhn is not merely verbal permission. Sometimes it is social, as in permission to enter a home. Sometimes it is legal or moral, opening or closing the way to an action. Sometimes it is cosmic, meaning that an event finds a path to occur within The God’s order.

No phenomenon acts independently of The God: not death, harm, healing, growth, or revelation. In Forqan’s reading, إِذْن (idhn) is the opening of a channel for receiving, accepting, or occurring within a system whose owner, hearer, knower, and lawgiver is The God.
