Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root A DH N

Ear, listening, making known, and giving permission

A DH N covers the ear and listening, as well as announcement and permission. The verbal form and its accompanying construction help distinguish these related senses in each passage.

Forms discussed

أُذُن / آذَان
Ear and ears

أَذِنَ / إِذْن
Listening or permission, according to construction

أَذَّنَ / أَذَان / مُؤَذِّن
Announcement, announcing, announcer

آذَنَ / تَأَذَّنَ
Making known and declaring

اسْتَأْذَنَ
Asking permission

Method Note

This family includes udhun, ear, as well as verbs of listening, becoming informed, announcing, and permitting. Each form and construction must be read separately: adhina lahu can mean giving permission, while adhinat li-rabbihā in al-Inshiqāq means listening or responding obediently.

In bi-idhni Allāh, permission is attributed to The God. Depending on context, this concerns authorization, will, and the dependence of an effect on Him. “Law of nature” is not a lexical definition of idhn and should not silently replace it in translation.

Central Meaning

The ear and auditory reception are clear sensory uses in this family. Hearing, becoming informed, and announcing are intelligibly connected; permission may likewise involve making an authorization known. This semantic connection does not establish the historical order in which every form developed.

Social permission and physical possibility are distinct: someone can enter a house without permission, although they should not. In divine contexts, an event’s dependence on permission emerges from the passage. The two levels should not be collapsed into a mechanical definition of “opening a channel.”

Material Image

Udhun names the ear; its plural is ādhān. Describing someone as “an ear” transfers the body part to the behaviour of listening. In 9:61 this usage is explicit.

No theory about the biological differences between eyes and ears is needed to explain that transfer. Nor does the body-part noun establish that permission in every use is a physical channel or law.

Arabic before and around the Qur’an

Abū ʿUbayd’s Gharīb al-ḥadīth quotes this verse of ʿAdī ibn Zayd to illustrate listening:

أَيُّهَا الْقَلْبُ تَعَلَّلْ بِدَدَنْ
إِنَّ هَمِّي فِي سَمَاعٍ وَأَذَنْ

O heart, console yourself with diversion; my concern is hearing and listening.

Adhan here is paired with samāʿ and concerns listening. ʿAdī was a pre-Islamic poet; the literary citation supports the auditory branch of the root, but does not establish a single known historical development for every meaning of idhn.

Qur’anic Usage

Ear as the Organ of Receiving Sound

In يَجْعَلُونَ أَصَابِعَهُمْ فِي آذَانِهِمْ (yajʿalūna aṣābiʿahum fī ādhānihim), the word آذَان (ādhān) means ears 2:19. The image is bodily and physical: they put their fingers into their ears. The ear is the passage of hearing, but that passage is blocked by the finger.

At the first level, then, udhun is the path of receiving sound. Closing the ear means closing one of the paths of perception.

One Who Listens and Receives Speech

Surat al-Tawbah says: وَمِنْهُمُ الَّذِينَ يُؤْذُونَ النَّبِيَّ وَيَقُولُونَ هُوَ أُذُنٌ (waminhum alladhīna yuʾdhūna al-nabiyya wayaqūlūna huwa udhun); among them are those who harm the Prophet and say, “He is an ear” 9:61.

The speakers mock his readiness to listen. “قُلْ أُذُنُ خَيْرٍ لَكُمْ” answers: say, a listener for your good. Khayrin lakum describes the benefit of his listening to them; it need not mean only hearing good statements.

Adhān as Public Announcement

In وَأَذَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ إِلَى النَّاسِ (wa-adhānun mina allāhi warasūlihi ilā al-nāsi), أَذَان (adhān) means public announcement 9:3. The message must leave concealment and reach people’s hearing. Adhān is not merely sound; it is a message brought into the field of hearing and awareness.

In “وَأَذِّنْ فِي النَّاسِ بِالْحَجِّ” (22:27), the command is to announce the pilgrimage among the people. Its public character is also supplied by fī al-nās and the setting; Form II alone does not invariably guarantee intensity or publicity.

Social Permission and Entering a Private Boundary

Surat al-Nūr says: فَإِنْ لَمْ تَجِدُوا فِيهَا أَحَدًا فَلَا تَدْخُلُوهَا حَتَّىٰ يُؤْذَنَ لَكُمْ (fa-in lam tajidū fīhā aḥadan falā tadkhulūhā ḥattā yuʾdhana lakum); if you find no one in them, do not enter until permission is given to you 24:28.

A home has a boundary. Entering without permission crosses the right of the one who owns or occupies that space. Here idhn means the opening of access by the one who has the right. Permission is therefore not mere politeness; it is the structure of respect for human boundaries.

Cosmic Idhn and the Possibility of Occurrence

The Qur’an says: وَمَا كَانَ لِنَفْسٍ أَنْ تَمُوتَ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ كِتَابًا مُؤَجَّلًا (wamā kāna linafsin an tamūta illā bi-idhni allāhi kitāban muʾajjalan); no soul dies except by The God’s idhn, as an appointed writing 3:145.

Death here is an event dependent on divine permission and an appointed term. Natural causes may be discussed in explanation, but replacing bi-idhni Allāh with “the law of nature” goes beyond the lexical meaning.

The same logic matters in وَمَا هُمْ بِضَارِّينَ بِهِ مِنْ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (wamā hum biḍārrīna bihi min aḥadin illā bi-idhni allāh); they cannot harm anyone through it except by The God’s idhn 2:102. This does not mean The God morally approves the harm. It means even harm has no independent existence or effect outside The God’s law, power, and created order.

Idhn in Healing and Miraculous Effect

In وَأُحْيِي الْمَوْتَىٰ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (wa-uḥyī al-mawtā bi-idhni allāh), Jesus speaks of bringing the dead to life by The God’s idhn 3:49. The wording clarifies the relation of effect. The effect appears through the messenger, but the final possibility and power of realization belong to The God.

For this reason, بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (bi-idhni allāh) protects the meaning from shirk. A miracle may appear through a prophet, but independent power is not given to the prophet. Idhn marks the boundary between being an instrument and The God’s ownership over the possibility of effect.

Idhn in Growth and Yielding Fruit

In وَالْبَلَدُ الطَّيِّبُ يَخْرُجُ نَبَاتُهُ بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهِ (wal-baladu al-ṭayyibu yakhruju nabātuhu bi-idhni rabbihi), idhn is used for a natural process 7:58. The good land brings forth its vegetation by the idhn of its Lord.

Soil, water, and growth provide the natural setting. Bi-idhni rabbihi attributes the growth to the Lord’s permission; idhn itself is not the name of a botanical mechanism.

Idhn in Revelation

In نَزَلَ بِهِ عَلَىٰ قَلْبِكَ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ (nazala bihi ʿalā qalbika bi-idhni allāh), the descent of revelation also occurs within divine idhn 2:97. The message, the carrier, the receiving heart, and the time of descent all belong to the order opened by The God.

Listening, Acceptance, and Structural Submission

At the beginning of Surat al-Inshiqāq, the Qur’an says: إِذَا السَّمَاءُ انشَقَّتْ وَأَذِنَتْ لِرَبِّهَا وَحُقَّتْ (idhā al-samāʾu inshaqqat wa-adhinat lirabbihā waḥuqqat); when the sky is split, and it listens/responds to its Lord, and it is rightfully bound to do so 84:1-2.

This usage is important because أَذِنَتْ (adhinat) is said of the sky. This is not a human physical ear, nor social permission. It is receptivity and structural submission before The God. The sky offers no resistance to the command of its Lord; what must occur in it occurs within the divine order.

Collocation Notes

Adhina lahu in the setting of entering a house expresses permission, while wa-adhinat li-rabbihā concerns listening or obedient response. The accompanying preposition matters, but the complete sentence is needed.

Bi-idhni Allāh means by the permission of The God. With events such as growth and death, explanation can discuss dependence on Him; “law” or “established pattern” should not replace idhn without evidence.

Difference from Nearby Words

Samʿ directly concerns hearing, while udhun names the organ of hearing; nevertheless, the verb adhina can itself mean listening. The families should not be divided so rigidly that actual attestations are excluded.

Amr is command and idhn permission: someone may permit an action without commanding it. Will, measure or decree, and established practice are expressed by other words. Their relation to divine permission may be discussed, but the vocabulary alone does not establish a fixed sequence of stages through which events occur.

Translation Method

A basic rendering of bi-idhni Allāh is “by the permission of The God.” An explanation may clarify that a particular passage concerns an event or effect not being independent of Him, rather than social authorization.

“Law of nature,” “the mechanism of the world,” and “channel of realization” are interpretive descriptions, not permanent lexical equivalents. In 2:102, harm occurring by permission does not amount to moral approval of the harm.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

Fī ādhānihim: “in their ears”; huwa udhun: “he is all ears”; qul udhunu khayrin lakum: “say: a listener for your good.”

Adhān: “announcement”; adhdhin fī al-nās: “announce among the people”; ḥattā yuʾdhana lakum: “until you are given permission”; bi-idhni rabbihi: “by the permission of its Lord”; wa-adhinat li-rabbihā: “and it listened to its Lord.”

Forms of ādhana, taʾadhdhana, and istaʾdhana also occur: making known, declaring, and asking permission. Īdhān and maʾdhūn are Arabic words, but these two forms themselves do not occur in the Qur’an.

Summary

A DH N is the family of the ear, listening, announcement, and permission. The construction determines which sense is active. Social permission, permission concerning events attributed to The God, and listening to His command cannot all receive one mechanical rendering. Dependence on permission is distinct from moral approval, and “law of nature” is not the word’s definition.
