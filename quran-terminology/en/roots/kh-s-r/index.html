Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root KH S R

Losing, decreasing, suffering loss, losing capital, and ending with a result below the original investment after measure

Important Forms from This Root

خ س ر (kh s r)

The root field; loss, decrease, deficiency, and loss of capital.

خَسِرَ / يَخْسَرُ (khasira / yakhsaru)

He lost; he suffered loss; he fell short; he lost capital.

خُسْر / خُسْرَان (khusr / khusrān)

Loss; deficiency; loss of capital or result.

خَاسِر (khāsir)

Loser; one whose capital or result has been lost.

خَاسِرُون / خَاسِرِين (khāsirūn / khāsirīn)

Losers; those whose result falls below their capital.

أَخْسَر / تَخْسِير (akhsar / takhsīr)

More losing; reducing, leading into loss, or corrupting measure and right.

Central Meaning

The root KH S R refers to loss, deficiency, decrease, losing something, and coming out of a transaction or path with a result lower than the original capital. It stands opposite R B Ḥ. If ribḥ means beneficial increase after calculation, khusr means decrease and loss after calculation.

In this root, the issue is not merely “not having.” Loss becomes meaningful when a person had something, placed it into a transaction, path, lifetime, effort, or choice, and in the end returned with less than he had or lost the original capital itself.

Linguistic Note

The root KH S R is tied to measuring the outcome. Loss becomes meaningful when the beginning and the end are compared. A person must ask what he had, what he spent, and what remained for him in the end.

So khusr is not merely poverty or lack. A person may outwardly gain something, but because he lost something greater in exchange for it, he is in reality a loser.

For this reason, khusrān has the sense of a balance sheet. Capital, cost, path, and result must all be measured together.

Main Image of the Root

The main image of this root is a trader who places capital into a transaction, but the transaction does not profit. He does not gain increase; rather, he loses part or all of the capital.

In this image, khusrān means the end of the matter is lower than its beginning. Capital has entered the field, but its return is deficient.

From this economic image, the root KH S R enters the wider field of life. The human being can lose not only wealth, but also lifetime, strength, natural disposition, opportunity, faith, ethics, and direction of life.

Loss and the Wasting Path

In some linguistic images, khusrān can be understood like a person who has lost the way and spends his energy on the wrong path. He moves, but his movement does not reach the destination. The more he strives, the more his capital is worn away.

This image matters for understanding Qur’anic loss. The loser is not always lazy or inactive. Sometimes he is highly active: he strives, runs, builds, plans. But because the direction is wrong, his effort does not preserve the capital; it burns it away.

So loss is not only stopping. Sometimes it is wasting movement on the wrong path.

Loss and Profit

Ribḥ means beneficial increase after transaction or effort. Khusr means deficiency and loss after transaction or effort.

In ribḥ, the capital returns and something is added to it. In khusr, the capital decreases, disappears, or the result remains lower than the cost.

So these two roots are two sides of one measurement: ribḥ means the end is better than the beginning; khusr means the end is lower than the beginning.

Loss and Trade

The root KH S R is directly connected with trade. Every trade ends either in profit or loss. When the Qur’an says فَمَا رَبِحَتْ تِجَارَتُهُمْ (famā rabiḥat tijāratuhum), it means a transaction took place, but its outcome was not profit. This expression appears in 2:16.

A losing trade does not always look like failure on the surface. Sometimes a person receives something and thinks he has won, but he has given away the true capital. This danger is very clear in the verses about hypocrisy: guidance was the capital, but misguidance was taken. Such a transaction does not profit; it is loss.

Loss and Measure

Khusrān is also connected with measure and balance. The Qur’an says: وَلَا تُخْسِرُوا الْمِيزَانَ (walā tukhsirūā al-mīzāna). This wording appears in 55:9: do not make the measure deficient; do not drag the balance of measurement into loss and short-giving.

Here loss is not only personal loss. By short-changing weight, measure, right, justice, or standard, a person causes deficiency for others. But before that, his own ethical scale has already been corrupted.

So takhsīr al-mīzān means disrupting the balance of measurement. When the scale is corrupted, not only one transaction is damaged; trust and justice are damaged too.

Loss and the Human Being

One of the deepest uses of this root appears in Surat al-ʿAsr: إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ (inna al-insāna lafī khusrin). This wording appears in 103:2. The human being is in loss, except those who believe, do sound deeds, urge one another to truth, and urge one another to patience.

Here loss is not financial only. The human being’s capital is his lifetime. Every moment of life is spent and does not return. If this capital is not transformed into faith, sound action, truth, and patience, the human being is decreasing, even if outwardly he gains wealth, power, or entertainment.

So in Surat al-ʿAsr, khusrān means that the human being spends the capital of time and life but gains nothing lasting and true.

The Most Losing in Deeds

The Qur’an speaks of people who are أَخْسَرِينَ أَعْمَالًا (akhsarīna ʾaʿmālanā): the most losing in deeds. It then explains that their effort in worldly life has gone astray while they think they are doing good work. This appears in 18:103 and 18:104.

This layer is very important. Sometimes a person loses and knows he has lost. That can still be the beginning of awakening. More dangerous is that a person is in loss while he imagines it to be profit.

In this state, not only has the capital been lost; the sensors of measurement have also been damaged. A person works, strives, and may even be proud of his work, but the direction of that work has separated from truth. This loss is deeper because, as long as the person sees himself as successful, he does not think to repair the path.

Manifest Loss

The Qur’an also speaks of خُسْرَان مُبِين (khusrān mubīn): manifest loss. This expression describes a state where the loss is not hidden or merely debatable; its result becomes clear and undeniable.

Sometimes a person does not see his loss in worldly life because surface measures deceive him: more wealth, more power, more followers, or more pleasure. But when the true account becomes clear, it becomes known that the original capital was lost.

Manifest loss is a loss that can no longer be denied in the end.

Loss and the Hereafter

In the Qur’an, khusrān is often tied to the Hereafter, because the Hereafter is the field where the final outcome of life’s transaction becomes manifest. In worldly life, a person’s outward balance sheet may look positive, but the Hereafter shows whether true profit was gained or the capital was lost.

So Qur’anic loss is not merely worldly failure. A person may appear successful before people in this world, yet be a loser in the final measure. The main criterion is what the human being has done with the capital of his existence.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

خَسِرَ / يَخْسَرُ (khasira / yakhsaru): lost; suffered loss; lost capital; fell short.

خُسْر (khusr): loss; deficiency; decrease of existential or financial capital.

خُسْرَان (khusrān): heavy loss; final loss; loss of capital.

خَاسِر (khāsir): loser; one whose result is lower than his capital.

خَاسِرُون / خَاسِرِين (khāsirūn / khāsirīn): losers.

أَخْسَرِينَ أَعْمَالًا (akhsarīna ʾaʿmālanā): the most losing in deeds; those whose record of action carries the greatest wasting and loss.

خُسْرَان مُبِين (khusrān mubīn): manifest loss; clear and undeniable loss.

إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ (inna al-insāna lafī khusrin): truly, the human being is in loss; the human being is losing his own capital.

وَلَا تُخْسِرُوا الْمِيزَانَ (walā tukhsirūā al-mīzāna): do not make the measure deficient; do not drag the balance of measurement into loss and short-giving.

فَمَا رَبِحَتْ تِجَارَتُهُمْ (famā rabiḥat tijāratuhum): so their trade did not profit; their exchange did not reach ribḥ; their transaction ended in loss.

Summary

The root KH S R refers to loss, deficiency, decrease, losing, and losing capital. It stands opposite R B Ḥ. If ribḥ means increase after calculation, khusr means decrease and loss after calculation.

Khusrān is not merely poverty or lack. It means that a person had capital, spent it, and in the end less than what should have remained, or nothing true at all, remained for him.

In Qur’anic reasoning, the greatest capital of the human being is his lifetime, natural disposition, faith, intellect, opportunity, and direction of life. If these capitals are not transformed into truth, patience, sound action, and growth, the human being is in loss, even if he outwardly gains things.

So khusr in the Qur’an’s economic and ethical balance is the name of the end of a transaction that did not profit: a transaction in which the human being received something, but lost the original capital.
