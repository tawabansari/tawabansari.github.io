Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root K Y L

Measuring by container, measuring with a measure, delivering the full amount, and volumetric measurement in exchange

Important Forms from This Root

ك ي ل (k y l)

The root field; measuring amount through a measure or measuring vessel.

كَالَ / يَكِيلُ (kāla / yakīlu)

Measured by a measure; measured with a container.

كَيْل (kayl)

Measuring by measure; measured amount.

مِكْيَال (mikyāl)

A measure; an instrument for measuring amount.

مَكِيل (makīl)

Measured by measure; something measured with a vessel.

اكْتَالَ / يَكْتَالُ (aktāla / yaktālu)

Took measure for oneself; received a measured amount from another.

Central Meaning

The root K Y L refers to measuring by a measure, measuring with a container, and determining the amount of something through a measuring vessel. This root is especially tied to goods measured by volume or container, such as grain, dates, liquids, or market goods given and received by measure.

Its central meaning is measuring amount by a measure. If W Z N is more tied to scale, heaviness, and balance, K Y L is more tied to measure, vessel, volume, and delivering a defined share in full.

Linguistic Note

The root K Y L is connected with a defined and deliverable amount. In kayl, the central issue is that the agreed amount must be given fully and correctly.

For this reason, kayl is not only a technical act. Measuring by vessel is also an ethical act because it creates trust between two sides. The one who measures is effectively saying: this amount is what is rightfully yours.

If the measure is correct, right is transferred correctly. If the measure is short, crooked, or manipulated, people’s right is reduced, even if the transaction appears ordinary.

Main Image of the Root

The main image of this root is a vessel used to measure amount. Goods are poured into it so their quantity becomes known. If the vessel is correct, stable, and known, the transaction becomes clear. If the vessel is smaller, crooked, or deceptive, right is displaced.

In real measuring, the appearance of fullness is not enough. Hidden emptiness, deception, and short-giving must be removed so the true amount of the right is delivered. A container that looks full from outside but contains air, hidden empty space, or deception does not transfer the right in full.

So kayl is tied to clarity of amount and faithfulness to the true capacity of the measure.

Kayl and Weight

The root K Y L is very close to W Z N, but they are not the same. Weight measures amount through heaviness and scale. Kayl measures amount through a measuring vessel and volume.

The Qur’an places the two beside each other because both are instruments of economic justice. In 7:85, it says: فَأَوْفُوا الْكَيْلَ وَالْمِيزَانَ (faawfūā al-kayla wal-mīzāna).

Kayl is justice in measured volume; weight is justice in weighing and balance. If either is corrupted, people’s right is reduced. Short-giving in measure or weight is not merely a market violation; it is the breaking of social trust.

Kayl and Fulfillment

One of the important Qur’anic expressions for this root is أَوْفُوا الْكَيْلَ (awfūā al-kayla): fulfill the measure. The word fulfillment here is very important. The measure must not only be taken; it must be given in full.

Justice in kayl is not only that a number is stated. The rightful amount must reach its owner completely and without deficiency.

This connection between kayl and fulfillment shows that sound transaction is built by delivering people’s rights fully.

Iktiyāl and Kayl

In Surat al-Muṭaffifīn, the Qur’an distinguishes between two behaviors: إِذَا اكْتَالُوا عَلَى النَّاسِ يَسْتَوْفُونَ (idhā aktālū ʿalā al-nāsi yastawfūna) and وَإِذَا كَالُوهُمْ أَوْ وَزَنُوهُمْ يُخْسِرُونَ (waidhā kālūhum aw wazanūhum yukhsirūna). These appear in 83:2 and 83:3.

In اكْتَالُوا (aktālū), they take measure for themselves from people and demand full measure. But when it is time to measure or weigh for others, they give less.

This contrast exposes the ethical disease of short-selling: when a person receives his own right, he wants it whole and complete; but when he must give another person’s right, he creates deficiency.

Taking Fully and Causing Loss

The word يَسْتَوْفُونَ (yastawfūna) means they take fully; they take their own right in full. Opposite it, يُخْسِرُونَ (yukhsirūna) means they cause deficiency; they reduce another person’s share and drag him into loss.

This contrast is very important. The Qur’an is not merely saying they have a technical error. It says the structure of their behavior is unjust. Their criterion for themselves and for others is not one.

A society in which people demand full taking for themselves and practice reduction toward others gradually loses trust and justice.

Kayl of a Camel

In the story of Joseph, the expression كَيْلَ بَعِيرٍ (kayla baʿīrin) appears: a measure or load of a camel. This wording appears in 12:65, and a closely related wording appears in 12:72. This shows that kayl is not always a small market vessel. Sometimes it points to a unit of food distribution and livelihood.

During famine, kayl was not merely an instrument of trade. It was the measure by which people accessed food and survival. Justice in kayl, in such a condition, becomes justice in life and livelihood.

This use shows that the measure can operate at the individual, family, and social level. When the measure is just, people’s share of provision becomes clear. When it is unjust, people’s life is harmed.

Kayl and Short-Measuring

Corruption in kayl usually begins by reducing the amount. Short-selling may be very small: a little less than the right, a little pressure on the measure, a little empty space in the vessel, or the use of an incorrect criterion. But these small reductions wear away trust.

In the Qur’an, reducing measure and weight appears beside social corruption and injustice. A society that betrays in small amounts becomes distant from justice in larger structures too.

Kayl and Mikyāl

مِكْيَال (mikyāl) is the instrument of measuring. This word shows that justice does not depend only on human intention; tools and criteria must also be sound.

If the instrument of measurement is corrupted, even someone who appears to be trading may transfer the right deficiently. In economy, the soundness of measuring tools is part of justice.

In today’s world, mikyāl is not only a vessel of wheat or dates. Criteria of value, currency units, rates, indexes, contracts, packaging, and accounting tools also play the role of mikyāl. If these tools do not show the reality of amount and value correctly, people’s right is quietly reduced.

Kayl and Today’s Economy

In today’s economy, measures have become more complex. People trade less with simple measuring vessels, but they still live with criteria: liters, kilograms, work hours, wage rates, currency value, inflation, package size, pricing, and economic indexes.

If packaging becomes smaller while the price stays the same, if currency value falls while the wage number stays the same, if rates are constructed in a way that hides the real decline of people’s lives, these may semantically approach corruption of kayl and mīzān.

Of course, this must be said carefully. Not every change in price or size is betrayal. But wherever the real amount is hidden, the criterion is manipulated for one side, or people’s right is quietly reduced, the warning of the root K Y L becomes alive.

Kayl and Social Justice

A correct measure is not only for one transaction. It is for the trust of society. If people feel that the criteria are not correct, trust leaves the market, governance, and social relations.

For this reason, the Qur’an emphasizes completing measure and weight. Large justice begins from small measurements. A society that betrays in the measure becomes untrustworthy in larger balances too.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

كَالَ / يَكِيلُ (kāla / yakīlu): measured by a measure; measured with a vessel.

كَيْل (kayl): measure; measuring by measure; measured amount.

مِكْيَال (mikyāl): a measure; an instrument for measuring amount.

مَكِيل (makīl): measured by measure; an amount measured with a vessel.

اكْتَالَ (iktāla): took measure for oneself; received a measured amount from another.

أَوْفُوا الْكَيْلَ (awfūā al-kayla): fulfill the measure; give the measured amount without deficiency.

أَوْفُوا الْكَيْلَ وَالْمِيزَانَ (awfūā al-kayla wal-mīzāna): fulfill the measure and the mīzān; complete amount and measurement with justice.

كَيْلَ بَعِيرٍ (kayla baʿīrin): the measure or load of a camel; a share equal to a camel-load.

ذَٰلِكَ كَيْلٌ يَسِيرٌ (dhaālika kaylun yasīrun): that is an easy and small measure; that share is easy for him.

إِذَا اكْتَالُوا عَلَى النَّاسِ يَسْتَوْفُونَ (idhā aktālū ʿalā al-nāsi yastawfūna): when they take measure for themselves from people, they take in full.

وَإِذَا كَالُوهُمْ أَوْ وَزَنُوهُمْ يُخْسِرُونَ (waidhā kālūhum aw wazanūhum yukhsirūna): when they measure or weigh for them, they give less and drag them into loss.

Summary

The root K Y L refers to measuring by measure, measuring with a vessel, and determining the amount of goods measured by volume. It is tied to amount, deliverable right, measuring tools, and trustworthiness in transaction.

If W Z N is the language of balance and scale, K Y L is the language of measure and amount. Both are material instruments of justice in the Qur’an.

A correct measure means that people’s right is given fully and without deficiency. A false measure means people’s right is reduced, even if the transaction appears normal.

In the Qur’an’s balance sheet, kayl reminds us that justice is not only in large words. It must also be present in small amounts, vessels, criteria, packages, rates, and everyday tools of measurement.
