Core Meaning

The root ت ب ب indicates loss, failure, fruitlessness of effort, loss of expected benefit, and a path reaching a ruined outcome.

Tabb is not only bodily destruction or death. A person may remain alive, wealthy, and powerful, yet his tools, capital, plans, and effort fail to produce the result for which they were assembled. In that condition, his path has reached tabab.

The semantic core may be summarized as the collapse of the benefit and result expected from an effort, tool, plan, or support.

Important Forms

تَبَّ is the past verb of form I: he suffered loss, failed, was deprived of good, or reached a ruined outcome.

تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ

The two hands of Abu Lahab failed, and he himself failed.

Al-Masad 111:1

The first verb returns to the two hands; the second returns to Abu Lahab himself. The verse speaks both of the failure of his instruments and efforts, and of the final outcome of the person himself.

تَبَّتْ is the feminine past form. Morphologically it appears as feminine singular because the verb comes before a dual subject, and يد is feminine. This verb is not from the root Q T ʿ, and it should not be translated as “cut off” in the sense of bodily cutting of the hands.

تَبَاب is a verbal noun or noun indicating continuing loss, failure, destruction, and collapse of result:

وَمَا كَيْدُ فِرْعَوْنَ إِلَّا فِي تَبَابٍ

The plot of Pharaoh was only in tabab, in a field of failure and collapse.

Ghafir 40:37

Tabab here is not only a momentary defeat. Pharaoh's plan is in a path whose result is fruitlessness and loss, even if for a time it appears large and effective.

تَتْبِيب is the verbal noun of form II: causing ruin, adding to loss, causing failure, or intensifying tabab.

وَمَا زَادُوهُمْ غَيْرَ تَتْبِيبٍ

They increased them in nothing except ruin and failure.

Hud 11:101

Form II can carry a causative or intensive sense here: those supports not only failed to save them, but added to the process of loss and collapse.

Tabban in lexical Arabic may be used as an accusative verbal noun to announce loss, curse failure, or express destruction. This exact independent form does not occur in the Qur'an. Forms such as tabb as an active adjective and matbub are morphologically possible, but they do not occur in the Qur'an and are not central for understanding the Qur'anic use.

Linguistic Note

The core of ت ب ب is not simply “being destroyed.” It is the loss of good, benefit, and the possibility of success. Usually this root's field contains several elements: a person has a goal; he uses tools; he gathers wealth, power, a plan, or a support; he expects that means to bring benefit, victory, or rescue; but the final result is fruitlessness and loss.

For this reason, T B B in the Qur'an is linked with hands, wealth, gain, plot, and claimed objects of worship. Each of these is supposed to produce benefit or protection for its owner, but in the field of tabab it loses the ability to bring him to his intended result.

Some lexical reports mention cutting for tabb. That branch should not make تبت يدا mean bodily cutting of hands. Even if “cutting” is accepted in some lexical uses, the Qur'anic axis of the verse is not the cutting of limbs. It is the cutting off of good, benefit, and success from the action and its owner. In other words, what is severed is the line of benefit and possibility of success, not necessarily the bodily organ itself.

Material Image

The material image of this root can be seen in hands that have worked intensely, yet the result of their work never reaches its aim. The hand is the tool of building, gathering, defending, gaining, and executing a decision. When the Qur'an says تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ, it does not mean that his hands were separated from his body. The image is that the practical power, actions, and achievements he formed by his own hands failed to produce the expected benefit.

مَا أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُ مَالُهُ وَمَا كَسَبَ

His wealth and what he gained did not avail him.

Al-Masad 111:2

Three layers stand together: hand, as the instrument of action; wealth, as material support; and gain, as the result of effort. Tabab becomes visible when tool, support, and achievement all exist, but cannot bring their owner to real rescue or benefit.

Another image is a large, costly plan that is built, yet from the beginning it moves in a direction that will not reach its aim:

وَمَا كَيْدُ فِرْعَوْنَ إِلَّا فِي تَبَابٍ

The plot of Pharaoh was only in failure.

Ghafir 40:37

The size of the tools and the breadth of the project do not guarantee success. A plan may appear great outwardly, yet in aim and result move in a dead end.

Classical Usage

In classical Arabic, tabb and tabab were used for loss, destruction, failure, being deprived of good, and continuing in loss. The expression tabban li-fulan was used to announce loss or call for the destruction and failure of a person. This usage did not only consider bodily death; it could also point to the loss of good, failure of effort, and collapse of hoped-for success.

Some sources report a meaning of cutting for tabb. Yet this use should not be imposed on the Qur'an without context. In Surat Al-Masad, the clues of wealth, gain, and the final outcome of the person show that the axis is the cutting off of benefit and the failure of effort, not the bodily cutting of hands.

Failure of the Hands: The Fruitlessness of the Instruments of Action

تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ

The two hands of Abu Lahab failed.

Al-Masad 111:1

The two hands can represent the entire field of action, power, effort, and acquisition. The verse speaks of hands that acted and gathered something, but that action ultimately has no power to rescue or bring success. Explanatory translations such as “the efforts of Abu Lahab failed” clarify part of the meaning, but direct translation should preserve the image of the two hands.

Failure of the Person Who Acts

تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ

The two hands of Abu Lahab failed, and he himself failed.

Al-Masad 111:1

There is a deep relation between the two parts of the verse, but it should not be reduced to a simple timeline, as if first the hands failed and then the person was ruined. The hands are instruments and extensions of the person's will. The person's direction appears in his tools, gain, and results.

The failure of the hands is not separate from the path and choice of their owner; nor is the failure of the person formed apart from his acts and efforts. The verse shows a two-sided relation: the person's wrong direction drives his tools and efforts toward tabab, and the ruined result of those efforts returns upon him.

Wealth and Gain That Do Not Avail

مَا أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُ مَالُهُ وَمَا كَسَبَ

His wealth and what he gained did not avail him.

Al-Masad 111:2

The tabab of Abu Lahab did not arise from a lack of means. He had wealth and gain, but these possessions failed to perform the function expected from them. Ighna means to meet a need, suffice, and make someone free of need. The negation of ighna shows that the wealth existed, but did not suffice.

Thus tabab cannot be reduced to becoming poor or losing wealth. Sometimes wealth remains, but its power to save and benefit collapses.

The Name Abu Lahab and the Scene of Blazing Fire

Abu Lahab is the known name or title of the person addressed in the surah, and the surah immediately speaks of a fire with flame:

سَيَصْلَىٰ نَارًا ذَاتَ لَهَبٍ

He will enter a fire of flame.

Al-Masad 111:3

There is an obvious verbal harmony between “Abu Lahab” and نارًا ذات لهب. Yet one should not conclude from this harmony that the meaning of T B B comes from fire and ashes. The surah uses the known name Abu Lahab to build a verbal and imaginal connection with his outcome, but the core of T B B remains loss, failure, and fruitlessness, not burning or becoming ash.

Pharaoh's Plot Inside Tabab

وَمَا كَيْدُ فِرْعَوْنَ إِلَّا فِي تَبَابٍ

The plot of Pharaoh was only in tabab, in a field of failure and collapse.

Ghafir 40:37

The preposition في is important. The verse does not merely say that Pharaoh's plan eventually failed; it places his plot inside tabab. His project may move outwardly, buildings may be raised, power may be mobilized, and people may be affected, yet in direction and result it is already within the space of failure.

يَا قَوْمِ اتَّبِعُونِ أَهْدِكُمْ سَبِيلَ الرَّشَادِ

O my people, follow me; I will guide you to the path of right reaching.

Ghafir 40:38

The placement of rushd and tabab in this part of the surah reveals two opposing directions: rushd is finding the right direction and reaching the proper aim; tabab is movement in a path whose result is failure and fruitlessness. These two words are not presented as direct lexical opposites in one sentence; the contrast emerges from the structure of the dialogue and the outcomes of the two paths.

A Grand Project With a Ruined Aim

يَا هَامَانُ ابْنِ لِي صَرْحًا لَعَلِّي أَبْلُغُ الْأَسْبَابَ

O Haman, build for me a lofty structure, so perhaps I may reach the means.

Ghafir 40:36

وَإِنِّي لَأَظُنُّهُ كَاذِبًا

And I certainly suppose him to be a liar.

Ghafir 40:37

This project is not merely a building. It is a display of power, the construction of a political narrative, and the transformation of a predetermined claim into a public project. Tabab here shows that outward greatness, complexity of tools, and executive power do not guarantee the rightness of the aim. A project may be materially large, yet because it is built on self-deception, denial, and wrong direction, it does not reach the true aim.

This verse should not be used as a general argument against technology, construction, or large projects. The issue is not the tool; it is the aim, claim, and direction in which the tool is used. The Qur'an places Pharaoh's plot in tabab, not the ability to build itself.

Claimed Objects of Worship That Do Not Avail

فَمَا أَغْنَتْ عَنْهُمْ آلِهَتُهُمُ الَّتِي يَدْعُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ مِنْ شَيْءٍ

Their objects of worship, which they called upon apart from The God, did not avail them in anything.

Hud 11:101

A human being expects support, rescue, and sufficiency from what he takes as an object of worship and reliance. But these claimed objects of worship produced no ighna when the result arrived. What was supposed to be a refuge revealed its inability.

From Uselessness to Added Loss

وَمَا زَادُوهُمْ غَيْرَ تَتْبِيبٍ

They increased them in nothing except ruin and failure.

Hud 11:101

The verse does not merely say that their objects of worship were useless. Dependence on them increased ruin. This increase can be understood from several directions: trusting a powerless refuge, moving away from the real reference point, building false security, continuing the wrong path in hope of rescue, and increasing the weight of consequences based on that dependence.

Tatbib is therefore a stage beyond ineffectiveness. The means not only fails to benefit; it plays a role in increasing the loss.

The Relation Between Ighna and Tabab

In Al-Masad and Hud, the negation of ighna appears beside the field of tabab:

مَا أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُ مَالُهُ وَمَا كَسَبَ

His wealth and gain did not suffice for him.

Al-Masad 111:2

فَمَا أَغْنَتْ عَنْهُمْ آلِهَتُهُمْ

Their objects of worship did not suffice for them.

Hud 11:101

In one case, the support is wealth and gain; in the other, claimed objects of worship. Both enter the field of tabab when a means chosen for sufficiency and rescue becomes ineffective or loss-increasing at the decisive moment.

This contrast clarifies the difference between outward increase and real benefit. Accumulation of wealth, tools, or followers may appear as growth, but if it does not reach its proper aim, then in terms of result it is moving inside tabab.

Similar Uses

Ruin of the tools and ruin of the person: تبت يدا directs attention to the person's tools, efforts, and achievements. وتب states the outcome of the owner himself. These two should not be completely separated. The tools are extensions of human will, and the result of actions returns to the person.

Gradual tabab and sudden destruction: Tabab may be a gradual process. Pharaoh's plan continues for a time and may even show outward successes, yet it is in a path of fruitlessness. Tabab is not necessarily instant destruction; it may be present in the structure of a path before final collapse.

Bodily cutting and cutting off good: Some lexical uses connect tabb with cutting. Yet تبت يدا in Surat Al-Masad does not speak of cutting off a limb. If the idea of cutting is preserved here, what is cut off is access to good, the line of benefit, the possibility of success, and the connection between tool and expected result.

Ineffectiveness and loss-increasing dependence: In Hud 11:101, the claimed objects of worship first do not benefit, then they add to tatbib. Ineffectiveness means the means has no positive result. Tatbib means dependence on the means increases the loss.

Outward success and final success: A person or government may possess wealth, power, buildings, followers, and executive capacity. These are outward successes. Tabab judges the final outcome and real benefit. If the tools do not reach the proper aim, their breadth does not prevent tabab.

Curse and declaration of result: تبت may carry the tone of a curse, but Surat Al-Masad is not merely an emotional reaction against a person. The following verses explain the result of his failure. Translation should preserve both the declarative force and the report-like dimension of the phrase.

Grammar and Structure

Why is tabbat feminine singular? In تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ, يدا is the dual of يد. Because the verb comes before the subject, it appears in singular form. Since يد is feminine, the verb is feminine. This structure does not mean that only one hand is intended.

Attributing action to hands: In Arabic and Persian alike, the hand can represent a person's action and gain: he built it with his own hand; his achievement; his hand was in this matter. Still, يدا should not be erased completely and translated only as “his deeds,” because the image of the hand is part of the verse.

The difference between tabbat and tabb: In تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ, the subject of تبت is the two hands of Abu Lahab, and the subject of تب is Abu Lahab himself. Repeating the root expands failure from the tools and efforts to the person, without necessarily establishing a timeline between them.

The structure fi tabab: In وَمَا كَيْدُ فِرْعَوْنَ إِلَّا فِي تَبَابٍ, the preposition في places the plot inside tabab. This structure is broader than “it eventually failed”; the plan is in the space of failure in its whole direction and structure.

The structure ghayra tatbib: In وَمَا زَادُوهُمْ غَيْرَ تَتْبِيبٍ, غير تتبيب states the result of the increase: nothing was added except more ruin. Tatbib may be translated as increase of ruin, loss-increasing, intensification of failure, or being driven deeper into loss.

Nearby Roots

T B B and KH S R: Khasr indicates losing capital, decrease of profit, or emerging from a transaction with loss. Tabb emphasizes the failure of a path and the fruitlessness of the result. Khasr: capital or profit decreased. Tabb: the effort, tool, or person did not reach success.

T B B and H L K: Halak indicates perishing, destruction, or the end of existence. Tabb may lead to halak, but its field is broader than bodily death. Hands, a plan, and effort can also reach tabab.

T B B and B Ṭ L: Batil indicates becoming invalid or ineffective. Tabb emphasizes loss and failure returning from that ineffectiveness to the one who acts. Batil: validity or effect disappeared. Tabab: the path and its owner reached loss and failure.

T B B and Ḥ B Ṭ: Habat indicates the loss of the effect and value of an action. Tabab can include action, tool, plan, and owner. Habat: the action lost its effect and reward. Tabab: the whole path reached fruitlessness and failure.

T B B and D M R: Damar indicates destroying and eliminating structure. Tabab focuses on the harmful end and fruitlessness of the result. Damar: the structure was destroyed. Tabab: the hope and result of success collapsed.

T B B and T B R: Tabara indicates severe smashing and destruction:

وَكُلًّا تَبَّرْنَا تَتْبِيرًا

And each We shattered with complete shattering.

Al-Furqan 25:39

The sound similarity between T B B and T B R should not make them identical. T B R is shattering and destroying; T B B is suffering loss and becoming fruitless.

T B B and T ʿ S: Taʿs is close to slipping, falling, and wretchedness. Taʿs highlights the fall of the person; tabab focuses on the collapse of benefit and result.

T B B and Ḍ L L: Dalal indicates losing the way and moving away from the destination. Dalal can cause tabab. Dalal: the wrong direction was chosen. Tabab: the result of that direction ended in failure.

T B B and F SH L: Fashal indicates weakness, loss of strength, and inability to continue action. Fashal may occur in the middle of movement; tabab concerns more the result and failed outcome of the path.

T B B and R SH D: Rushd and rashad are close to finding the right direction, maturity of judgment, and movement toward the correct result. Tabab shows a movement or plan that fails to reach benefit and destination. In the context of Surat Ghafir, sabil ar-rashad is a path that leads to the correct destination, while a plot in tabab is a plan moving inside failure and dead end. The two roots are not perfect lexical opposites, but in that context they show two different directions of outcome.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For تَبَّ: failed, suffered loss, was ruined, reached fruitlessness, was deprived of good and success, the result of his work collapsed.

For تَبَّتْ يَدَاهُ: his two hands failed; the power of his hands reached a dead end; his efforts became fruitless; what his two hands had prepared ended in loss.

For تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ: the two hands of Abu Lahab failed, and he himself failed; the power of Abu Lahab's hands reached a dead end, and he himself fell into ruin; Abu Lahab's efforts became fruitless, and he himself reached a failed end. For direct translation, “the two hands of Abu Lahab failed” preserves the image of the verse better.

For تَبَاب: failure, ruin, loss, fruitlessness, collapse of result, dead end of benefit and success.

For فِي تَبَاب: in a field of failure, in a path of ruin, inside fruitlessness and collapse, in a condition condemned to failed result.

For وَمَا كَيْدُ فِرْعَوْنَ إِلَّا فِي تَبَابٍ: the plot of Pharaoh was only in a field of failure and collapse; Pharaoh's plan was only in a path moving toward fruitlessness; Pharaoh's plot was in nothing but ruin and dead end.

For تَتْبِيب: increase of ruin, intensification of failure, driving into loss, pushing deeper into ruin, loss-increasing.

For وَمَا زَادُوهُمْ غَيْرَ تَتْبِيبٍ: they increased them in nothing except ruin; they only intensified their failure; they drove them only deeper into loss; they added nothing except loss and collapse.

Summary

The root ت ب ب indicates loss, failure, the cutting off of good, and the fruitlessness of an effort, tool, or plan. Its material image appears in hands that acted, but no longer have the power to bring their owner to benefit and rescue.

In the Qur'an, this root is used for the hands and person of Abu Lahab, the plot of Pharaoh, and claimed objects of worship that, instead of providing sufficiency and rescue, increase the ruin of those who rely on them. In all these uses, what was imagined as a means of success loses the power to fulfill its aim.

Tabab may be present in the structure of a path before outward destruction. Wealth may increase, a building may rise, and a plan may operate for a time; but if its aim and result collapse, the path is in tabab.

T B B is close to khasr, halak, batil, habat, damar, tabr, fashal, and dalal, but it is identical with none of them. Its distinctive core is the failure of effort and the collapse of the benefit and result expected from tools, power, wealth, or a support.
