Core meaning: restraining and preserving something against harm, separation, or loss; related uses include grasping a support, seeking refuge, and maintaining a bond that keeps a relationship from breaking.

1. The root in early Arabic

The triliteral root ع ص م concerns restraint, preservation, protection, preventing harm, and maintaining connections. One person may protect another from danger; someone may grasp something for support; or a bond may keep two parties from separating.

In early Arabic, عَصَمَهُ means that someone preserved another, restrained them from something, or protected them against harm. عِصْمَة denotes protection or preservation preventing harm or the loss of a bond.

عِصَام names a strap or rope used to hold or carry something. A waterskin's carrying strap is one example.

The strap does more than connect: it keeps the waterskin from falling or separating from its carrying support.

اعْتَصَمَ describes someone grasping something or seeking refuge in it to remain safe from falling, danger, or harm.

Imagine someone climbing a rock face and gripping a rope. The rope provides support, while the person's grip uses that support to prevent a fall.

These actions are related but distinct: preserving or protecting, and grasping a means of protection.

Socially, عِصْمَة can also designate the marriage bond. This is a recognized relationship holding two people together, rather than a physical cord.

The basic meaning can therefore be formulated as follows:

ع ص م concerns preserving and preventing separation, falling, harm, or loss, whether through physical support, protection, or maintaining a bond between people.

The entire family should not be confined to ropes or physical binding. A rope is one clear material image, not a definition of every derivative.

2. Early poetic evidence and attribution

An important example is attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Aws ibn Hajar:

فَأَشْرَطَ فِيهَا نَفْسَهُ وَهُوَ مُعْصِمٌ
وَأَلْقَى بِأَسْبَابٍ لَهُ وَتَوَكَّلَا

fa-ashraṭa fīhā nafsahu wa-huwa muʿṣimun / wa-alqā bi-asbābin lahu wa-tawakkalā

“He risked his life to reach it, holding to a rope; he deployed his ropes and committed himself to the undertaking.”

The poet describes someone exposing himself to danger to obtain wood high on a mountain.

مُعْصِم here denotes someone relying on a rope or another means of support.

The person remains exposed to danger. A rope may support him, but gripping it alone does not guarantee that no fall or accident can occur.

Here the family concerns support and protection, rather than the absolute impossibility of danger.

Early sources attribute this line to Aws ibn Hajar. Accepting the attribution makes it pre-Islamic evidence; the precise date of this line has not been independently established in this study.

Another example is attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Urwah ibn al-Ward:

إِذًا لَمَلَكْتُ عِصْمَةَ أُمِّ وَهْبٍ
عَلَى مَا كَانَ مِنْ حَسَكِ الصُّدُورِ

idhan la-malaktu ʿiṣmata Ummi Wahbin / ʿalā mā kāna min ḥasaki al-ṣudūri

“Then I would have retained the marriage bond with Umm Wahb, despite the bitterness within our hearts.”

عِصْمَة here refers to the marital bond.

This matters because the family's meanings of preservation and connection extend beyond physical objects.

As a cord holds things together, marriage establishes a relationship between two people with recognized consequences until that bond is dissolved.

The analogy does not make marriage lexically equivalent to ownership of a person's body or absolute authority over a spouse. In its historical social setting, the poem concerns retaining a relationship, not defining its entire nature.

Arabic sources also transmit a line about a waterskin's عِصَام:

وَقِرْبَةِ أَقْوَامٍ جَعَلْتُ عِصَامَهَا
عَلَى كَاهِلٍ مِنِّي ذَلُولٍ مُرَحَّلِ

wa-qirbati aqwāmin jaʿaltu ʿiṣāmahā / ʿalā kāhilin minnī dhalūlin muraḥḥali

“Many a people's waterskin whose carrying strap I set upon my shoulder, accustomed to bearing loads.”

The line is attributed in some transmissions to Imru al-Qays and in others to Ta'abbata Sharran. Disputed attribution does not prevent cautious use of the word's clear meaning, but it does prevent assigning a certain author and date without further examination.

عِصَام is a means of carrying and supporting the waterskin.

Together these examples show early uses involving physical support and connecting tools as well as the preservation of human bonds.

3. Lexical and Quranic forms

Important forms include عَصَمَ, يَعْصِمُ, عِصْمَة, عِصَام, عَاصِم, مَعْصُوم, اعْتَصَمَ, اعْتِصَام, اسْتَعْصَمَ, أَعْصَمَ, and مِعْصَم.

Despite semantic connections, their roles and uses differ.

Transitive عَصَمَ concerns preserving, preventing, and holding a person or thing safe.

عِصْمَة names protection or a bond enabling preservation.

عِصَام can name a supporting strap or rope.

عَاصِم is an active participle: someone or something protecting or preventing harm.

مَعْصُوم is a passive participle: someone or something protected.

اعْتَصَمَ concerns grasping, taking refuge, or holding fast to a protective means or source.

Quranic اسْتَعْصَمَ expresses restraint and resistance to a demand or pressure the person refuses to yield to.

مِعْصَم names the part of the arm where a bracelet rests. In another branch, أَعْصَم describes certain animals with distinctive coloration on their limbs.

These latter uses should not be reduced to protection or harm prevention merely because they share consonants.

Quranic forms

The root appears thirteen times in twelve verses, in five morphological categories:

  • The simple verb يَعْصِمُ with attached pronouns, in Al-Ma'idah 5:67, Hud 11:43, and Al-Ahzab 33:17.
  • Form VIII اعْتَصَمَ, in Al Imran 3:101, Al Imran 3:103, Al-Nisa 4:146, Al-Nisa 4:175, and Al-Hajj 22:78.
  • Form X اسْتَعْصَمَ, in Yusuf 12:32.
  • The broken plural عِصَم, in Al-Mumtahanah 60:10.
  • The active participle عَاصِم, in Yunus 10:27, Hud 11:43, and Ghafir 40:33.

The singular عِصْمَة and the form مَعْصُوم do not occur in the Quran. Later technical uses of these words should not be presented as forms present in the Quranic text.

4. عَصَمَ: protection against danger or harm

The simple verb عَصَمَ provides one important Quranic branch.

In this construction, someone or something generally provides protection, while another person or thing is preserved from a danger.

Noah's story offers a clear example.

The mountain Noah's son expected to save him

قَالَ سَآوِي إِلَىٰ جَبَلٍ يَعْصِمُنِي مِنَ الْمَاءِ ۚ قَالَ لَا عَاصِمَ الْيَوْمَ مِنْ أَمْرِ اللَّهِ إِلَّا مَنْ رَحِمَ ۚ وَحَالَ بَيْنَهُمَا الْمَوْجُ فَكَانَ مِنَ الْمُغْرَقِينَ

qāla sa-āwī ilā jabalin yaʿṣimunī mina al-māʾi qāla lā ʿāṣima al-yawma min amri Allāhi illā man raḥima wa-ḥāla baynahumā al-mawju fa-kāna mina al-mughraqīn

Hūd 11:43

Hud 11:43

“He said: I will take refuge on a mountain that will protect me from the water. He said: Today there is no protector against The God's command except the One who shows mercy. The waves came between them, and he was among those drowned.”

Two derivatives occur together: يَعْصِمُنِي and عَاصِمَ.

Noah's son says he will seek refuge on a mountain that will protect him from the water.

The image is linguistically natural: when water rises, someone may seek higher ground to escape its reach.

يَعْصِمُنِي in his speech therefore means protection from the danger of water.

Noah replies that no protection against The God's command is available that day apart from His mercy.

This does not negate a mountain's ordinary protective capacity as a general natural principle. It concerns a specific situation governed by the divine command, in which Noah's son considers himself safe by relying on the mountain.

The following words show that his confidence was not fulfilled: the waves separated them.

The root means protection from danger, but an apparently protective means does not guarantee that protection will actually occur.

The meaning of لَا عَاصِمَ

عَاصِم is an active participle whose basic sense is a protector or preserver.

لَا negates protection in لَا عَاصِمَ الْيَوْمَ: in that situation there is no protector against The God's command.

Two established grammatical and semantic explanations concern إِلَّا مَنْ رَحِمَ.

One relates the exception to the recipient of divine mercy: “but whoever The God has mercy upon is protected.” It can be explained as a discontinuous exception, or through understanding عَاصِم in a passive sense.

The other relates the exception to the source of mercy: the protector is the One who shows mercy, namely The God. This is the reading adopted in the translation above.

The difference should remain visible. Both readings nevertheless deny that reliance on a means independent of divine will guarantees salvation against His command.

5. Divine protection and the scope of عِصْمَة

Al-Ma'idah applies the simple verb directly to The God's protection of His Messenger:

يَا أَيُّهَا الرَّسُولُ بَلِّغْ مَا أُنْزِلَ إِلَيْكَ مِنْ رَبِّكَ ۖ وَإِنْ لَمْ تَفْعَلْ فَمَا بَلَّغْتَ رِسَالَتَهُ ۚ وَاللَّهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاسِ ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَهْدِي الْقَوْمَ الْكَافِرِينَ

yā ayyuhā al-rasūlu balligh mā unzila ilayka min rabbika wa-in lam tafʿal fa-mā ballaghta risālatahu wa-Allāhu yaʿṣimuka mina al-nāsi inna Allāha lā yahdī al-qawma al-kāfirīn

Al-Māʾidah 5:67

Al-Ma'idah 5:67

“O Messenger, convey what has been sent down to you from your Lord. If you do not, you have not conveyed His message. The God protects you from people. The God does not guide the disbelieving people.”

يَعْصِمُكَ derives from the simple verb عَصَمَ; the attached كَ refers to the Messenger.

مِنَ النَّاسِ specifies protection against people.

This qualification matters: the verse identifies the protection's object. It cannot without further evidence be expanded into absolute immunity from every illness, bodily injury, human error, or incident throughout a lifetime.

Conversely, the promise should not be narrowed until it loses any clear meaning.

In the command to convey the message, The God assures the Messenger of His protection. Conveying and protection against human threats are therefore connected.

The verb itself does not specify the exact means, every situation covered, or how the protection occurs.

The lexical meaning is clear: The God protects the Messenger against people. A detailed account of this promise's relationship to every event of his life goes beyond the root's meaning.

Does عِصْمَة mean absolute infallibility?

Theology also uses عِصْمَة technically for immunity from sin or error. Different views concern its extent and whom it includes.

The Quran does not use مَعْصُوم as a description of a Prophet or Messenger, and the Al-Ma'idah verse speaks of protection against people.

The root alone therefore cannot establish a theory of every Messenger's absolute immunity from all error.

Nor does the absence of the term مَعْصُوم by itself prove that divine protection from sin or error never occurs.

That question requires independent consideration of verses on Prophets and Messengers, guidance, warnings, correction, and divine protection.

Lexically, عِصْمَة means preservation and protection; context determines its object and scope.

6. يَعْصِمُكُمْ: others cannot prevent divine will

The third occurrence of the simple verb appears in Al-Ahzab:

قُلْ مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يَعْصِمُكُمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ إِنْ أَرَادَ بِكُمْ سُوءًا أَوْ أَرَادَ بِكُمْ رَحْمَةً ۚ وَلَا يَجِدُونَ لَهُمْ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ وَلِيًّا وَلَا نَصِيرًا

qul man dhā alladhī yaʿṣimukum mina Allāhi in arāda bikum sūʾan aw arāda bikum raḥmatan wa-lā yajidūna lahum min dūni Allāhi waliyyan wa-lā naṣīrā

Al-Aḥzāb 33:17

Al-Ahzab 33:17

“Say: Who could protect you against The God if He intended harm for you or intended mercy for you? They will find neither a protector nor a helper apart from The God.”

يَعْصِمُكُمْ refers to protecting the people addressed, but the sentence is a question.

It asks who could protect them against divine will.

The conditional clause includes both intended harm and intended mercy.

The question concerns more than preventing an unwelcome event: others cannot control or alter divine will concerning either harm or mercy.

No one can rely on their own power to prevent that will from being realized.

Yet the verb does not mean absolute control. The inability to control divine will emerges from the question, condition, and context; the verb still concerns preservation and protection.

Protection in Quranic language is thus a relationship whose agent, object, and relevant danger must be identified.

7. عَاصِم: a protector

عَاصِم is the root's active participle, naming someone or something that prevents harm or provides protection.

It occurs in two verses besides Hud 11:43.

Yunus 10:27 says of those who have earned evil deeds: مَا لَهُمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ مِنْ عَاصِمٍ, meaning they have no protector against The God.

Ghafir 40:33 likewise has مَا لَكُمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ مِنْ عَاصِمٍ: you have no protector against The God.

Both occurrences are within negative constructions.

The second مِنْ, directly before عَاصِمٍ, reinforces the comprehensiveness of the negation: there is no protector whatsoever in that situation. It differs from مِنَ اللَّهِ earlier in the clause.

This should not be taken as denying every kind of protection in every circumstance of life.

Hud concerns the flood and divine command.

Yunus concerns recompense for deeds and humiliation in the hereafter.

Ghafir warns of a day when people flee, turning their backs, with no protector against The God.

The basic meaning of عَاصِم remains, while the danger and circumstances differ between verses.

8. اعْتَصَمَ: holding fast and seeking refuge

Form VIII اعْتَصَمَ concerns grasping, holding fast, and taking refuge in someone or something.

A person turns toward a means or source regarded as providing protection, support, or stability.

Someone about to fall may grasp a rope. The rope provides support; gripping it illustrates اعْتِصَام.

Quranic usage extends beyond a physical rope to humanity's relationship with The God and His guidance.

وَمَنْ يَعْتَصِمْ بِاللَّهِ

وَكَيْفَ تَكْفُرُونَ وَأَنْتُمْ تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْكُمْ آيَاتُ اللَّهِ وَفِيكُمْ رَسُولُهُ ۗ وَمَنْ يَعْتَصِمْ بِاللَّهِ فَقَدْ هُدِيَ إِلَىٰ صِرَاطٍ مُسْتَقِيمٍ

wa-kayfa takfurūna wa-antum tutlā ʿalaykum āyātu Allāhi wa-fīkum rasūluhu wa-man yaʿtaṣim bi-Allāhi fa-qad hudiya ilā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm

Āl ʿImrān 3:101

Al Imran 3:101

“How can you disbelieve while The God's verses are recited to you and His Messenger is among you? Whoever holds fast to The God has been guided to a straight path.”

يَعْتَصِمْ appears with the preposition بِـ.

The construction expresses holding to and depending upon the source in which one seeks refuge.

The stated result is guidance to a straight path.

Holding fast to The God is therefore connected here with guidance and avoiding error.

It should not be restricted to physical protection or guaranteed material comfort: the verse itself names the result as فَقَدْ هُدِيَ إِلَىٰ صِرَاطٍ مُسْتَقِيمٍ.

Nor is holding fast merely uttering The God's name or claiming allegiance. The recitation of His verses and the presence of His Messenger connect it with accepting His guidance.

9. Holding fast together: attachment and unity

An important occurrence appears in Al Imran:

وَاعْتَصِمُوا بِحَبْلِ اللَّهِ جَمِيعًا وَلَا تَفَرَّقُوا ۚ وَاذْكُرُوا نِعْمَتَ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذْ كُنْتُمْ أَعْدَاءً فَأَلَّفَ بَيْنَ قُلُوبِكُمْ فَأَصْبَحْتُمْ بِنِعْمَتِهِ إِخْوَانًا وَكُنْتُمْ عَلَىٰ شَفَا حُفْرَةٍ مِنَ النَّارِ فَأَنْقَذَكُمْ مِنْهَا ۗ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ آيَاتِهِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ

wa-ʿtaṣimū bi-ḥabli Allāhi jamīʿan wa-lā tafarraqū wa-udhkurū niʿmata Allāhi ʿalaykum idh kuntum aʿdāʾan fa-allafa bayna qulūbikum fa-aṣbaḥtum bi-niʿmatihi ikhwānan wa-kuntum ʿalā shafā ḥufratin mina al-nāri fa-anqadhakum minhā kadhālika yubayyinu Allāhu lakum āyātihi laʿallakum tahtadūn

Āl ʿImrān 3:103

Al Imran 3:103

“Hold fast, all together, to The God's rope, and do not become divided. Remember The God's favor upon you when you were enemies and He joined your hearts, so that through His favor you became brothers. You were on the edge of a pit of fire, and He saved you from it. Thus The God makes His verses clear to you, so that you may be guided.”

اعْتَصِمُوا is a plural imperative; بِحَبْلِ اللَّهِ identifies what is to be grasped.

The direct meaning of حَبْل is a rope, something grasped or used to connect and support.

Here حَبْلِ اللَّهِ images humanity's connection with divine guidance and covenant; it should not be restricted to a physical rope.

Al Imran 3:101 directly has بِاللَّهِ; Al Imran 3:103 has بِحَبْلِ اللَّهِ.

Holding fast to The God can thus be explained through holding to the guidance and connection He provides.

The context of divine verses, guidance, and avoiding disbelief supports the rope's association with His guidance and message.

حَبْل alone does not establish a specific person, institution, family, or religious group that must be followed in every circumstance.

The role of جَمِيعًا

The imperative is accompanied by جَمِيعًا.

It instructs all the people addressed to hold fast to The God's rope.

وَلَا تَفَرَّقُوا then commands them not to become divided.

The subject therefore includes communal bonds as well as individual protection.

The expression names حَبْلِ اللَّهِ as the shared point of attachment; it does not explicitly name a human person or group. Identifying a particular referent requires further evidence.

People may gather for a common purpose. If truth and justice provide their criterion, personal differences need not destroy their bond.

If every faction instead places loyalty to its leader or group above truth, affiliation itself can cause division.

This illustrates attachment to a common reference, rather than eliminating every disagreement or making everyone identical.

The verse continues with their former enmity and the connection The God established between their hearts.

Holding fast is therefore also connected with preventing the rupture of human bonds and a return to hostility.

Social unity belongs to the verse's context and outcome; it is not the standalone lexical definition of اعْتِصَام.

10. Holding fast to The God and turning away from hypocrisy

Al-Nisa places holding fast alongside repentance, reform, and sincere devotion:

إِلَّا الَّذِينَ تَابُوا وَأَصْلَحُوا وَاعْتَصَمُوا بِاللَّهِ وَأَخْلَصُوا دِينَهُمْ لِلَّهِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ مَعَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ ۖ وَسَوْفَ يُؤْتِ اللَّهُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ أَجْرًا عَظِيمًا

illā alladhīna tābū wa-aṣlaḥū wa-ʿtaṣamū bi-Allāhi wa-akhlaṣū dīnahum li-Allāhi fa-ulāʾika maʿa al-muʾminīna wa-sawfa yuʾti Allāhu al-muʾminīna ajran ʿaẓīmā

Al-Nisāʾ 4:146

Al-Nisa 4:146

“Except those who repent, make amends, hold fast to The God, and devote their religion sincerely to The God: those are with the believers. The God will give the believers a great reward.”

This follows discussion of the hypocrites.

Four actions are mentioned together:

Repentance, reform, holding fast to The God, and devoting religion sincerely to Him.

They are related but should not be reduced to one meaning.

Repentance concerns turning back from a wrong course.

Reform concerns correcting conduct and its effects.

Holding fast to The God concerns attachment and seeking refuge in Him.

Sincere devotion concerns directing religious allegiance purely toward Him.

Holding fast is therefore one component of a person's real return.

Someone may regret past behavior yet continue to treat misleading loyalties and criteria as their ultimate reference. Such a return is not equivalent to holding fast to The God.

Holding fast should not replace making amends: the verse states both separately.

Preservation and attachment here concern choosing the right reference and remaining on the path of reform.

Al-Nisa 4:175 also joins belief with holding fast, followed by divine mercy, bounty, and guidance. The pronoun in بِهِ has been understood as referring to The God or to the clear light mentioned in Al-Nisa 4:174. The proposed translation here follows the first reading; the second also relates attachment to His guidance.

These uses place holding fast within faith, reform, and guidance, beyond an inward feeling of shelter alone.

11. Holding fast and divine guardianship

The end of Al-Hajj connects the command with The God's guardianship.

Al-Hajj 22:78, after commanding striving for The God, stating that He has imposed no hardship in religion, and commanding salat and zakat, says:

وَاعْتَصِمُوا بِاللَّهِ هُوَ مَوْلَاكُمْ

wa-ʿtaṣimū bi-Allāhi huwa mawlākum

Al-Ḥajj 22:78

“Hold fast to The God; He is your Protector.”

The continuation praises Him as an excellent Protector and Helper.

This connection places holding fast alongside His guardianship and help.

Holding fast, guardianship, and help are distinct terms.

اعْتِصَام emphasizes attachment and seeking refuge.

Guardianship concerns protection and supportive association.

Help concerns providing assistance.

The ideas are connected, but each retains its distinct semantic contribution.

وَمَا جَعَلَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ مِنْ حَرَجٍ places this attachment within a religion in which The God says He has imposed no hardship upon the people addressed.

Holding fast should therefore not become a pretext for binding people to groundless hardships or invented human commands.

12. فَاسْتَعْصَمَ: resistance to a wrongful demand

An important occurrence appears in Joseph's story:

قَالَتْ فَذَٰلِكُنَّ الَّذِي لُمْتُنَّنِي فِيهِ ۖ وَلَقَدْ رَاوَدْتُهُ عَنْ نَفْسِهِ فَاسْتَعْصَمَ ۖ وَلَئِنْ لَمْ يَفْعَلْ مَا آمُرُهُ لَيُسْجَنَنَّ وَلَيَكُونًا مِنَ الصَّاغِرِينَ

qālat fa-dhālikunna alladhī lumtunnanī fīhi wa-la-qad rāwadtuhu ʿan nafsihi fa-staʿṣama wa-la-in lam yafʿal mā āmuruhu la-yusjananna wa-la-yakūnan mina al-ṣāghirīn

Yūsuf 12:32

Yusuf 12:32

“She said: This is the one about whom you blamed me. I did seek to seduce him, but he resisted and guarded himself. If he does not do what I command, he will certainly be imprisoned and be among the humiliated.”

فَاسْتَعْصَمَ is Form X.

It follows رَاوَدْتُهُ عَنْ نَفْسِهِ, the woman's account of trying to draw Joseph into her desire.

The verb indicates his resistance and refusal to yield.

Here the family concerns guarding oneself against behavior one refuses to perform.

Form X alone does not establish a request for someone else's protection. In this usage it expresses restraint and refusal.

Someone offered a bribe might reject it and maintain that decision. This illustrates restraint and resistance conceptually.

Resisting a demand and lifelong immunity from every error remain different matters.

فَاسْتَعْصَمَ reports Joseph's conduct in a particular situation; the word alone does not define his whole life in terms of infallibility.

The following verses

In Yusuf 12:33, Joseph asks his Lord for protection from their scheme, saying that unless it is turned away he may incline toward them.

In Yusuf 12:34, The God answers and turns their scheme away from him.

The verses show two connected matters: Joseph's restraint, and divine response and protection.

They should not be collapsed into اسْتَعْصَمَ itself. Yusuf 12:32 states his refusal; Yusuf 12:34 describes the scheme being turned away.

This distinction clarifies the relationship between human responsibility and choice and divine assistance.

13. عِصَم: bonds holding parties together

Al-Mumtahanah employs the root socially, in a passage concerning marriage:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا جَاءَكُمُ الْمُؤْمِنَاتُ مُهَاجِرَاتٍ فَامْتَحِنُوهُنَّ ۖ اللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِإِيمَانِهِنَّ ۖ فَإِنْ عَلِمْتُمُوهُنَّ مُؤْمِنَاتٍ فَلَا تَرْجِعُوهُنَّ إِلَى الْكُفَّارِ ۖ لَا هُنَّ حِلٌّ لَهُمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحِلُّونَ لَهُنَّ ۖ وَآتُوهُمْ مَا أَنْفَقُوا ۚ وَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ أَنْ تَنْكِحُوهُنَّ إِذَا آتَيْتُمُوهُنَّ أُجُورَهُنَّ ۚ وَلَا تُمْسِكُوا بِعِصَمِ الْكَوَافِرِ وَاسْأَلُوا مَا أَنْفَقْتُمْ وَلْيَسْأَلُوا مَا أَنْفَقُوا ۚ ذَٰلِكُمْ حُكْمُ اللَّهِ ۖ يَحْكُمُ بَيْنَكُمْ ۚ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ

yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū idhā jāʾakumu al-muʾminātu muhājirātin fa-mtaḥinūhunna Allāhu aʿlamu bi-īmānihinna fa-in ʿalimtumūhunna muʾminātin fa-lā tarjiʿūhunna ilā al-kuffāri lā hunna ḥillun lahum wa-lā hum yaḥillūna lahunna wa-ātūhum mā anfaqū wa-lā junāḥa ʿalaykum an tankiḥūhunna idhā ātaytumūhunna ujūrahunna wa-lā tumsikū bi-ʿiṣami al-kawāfiri wa-sʾalū mā anfaqtum wa-l-yasʾalū mā anfaqū dhālikum ḥukmu Allāhi yaḥkumu baynakum wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm

Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:10

Al-Mumtahanah 60:10

“Believers, when believing women come to you as emigrants, test them. The God knows their faith best. If you find them to be believers, do not return them to the disbelievers. These women are not lawful for those men, nor those men for these women. Give the men what they have spent. There is no blame upon you for marrying these women when you give them their bridal dues. Do not retain marriage bonds with disbelieving women. Ask for what you have spent, and let them ask for what they have spent. That is The God's judgment; He judges between you. The God is Knowing, Wise.”

عِصَم is the broken plural of عِصْمَة.

Here عِصْمَة refers to marriage: a relationship holding spouses together socially and legally.

This fits the early usage in the verse attributed to Urwah ibn al-Ward.

عِصَم should not be translated here as protection from sin or infallibility.

Nor does it mean the women themselves: it denotes marriage bonds.

The role of تُمْسِكُوا

وَلَا تُمْسِكُوا بِعِصَمِ الْكَوَافِرِ combines two lexical families.

تُمْسِكُوا belongs to م س ك, concerning holding and not releasing.

عِصَم belongs to ع ص م and names the marital bonds.

The natural translation is:

“Do not retain marriage bonds with disbelieving women.”

This should not be reduced to “do not keep the women,” since the marriage bonds are what the expression directly addresses.

The full verse provides the circumstances: believing emigrant women, examining their faith, not returning them, marriage status, and both parties' financial rights.

The lexical meaning should not be turned into a general law about all family relationships or everyone with differing religious beliefs.

The verse addresses marriage in a particular situation while also discussing financial rights.

Maintaining or dissolving a marital bond is distinct from owning a person.

Lexically, عِصْمَةُ النِّكَاح names the bond maintaining marriage, not one spouse's absolute dominion over the other.

14. Distinguishing related roots

ع ص م and ح ف ظ

ح ف ظ concerns preserving, keeping, watching over, and preventing loss.

Central uses of ع ص م emphasize preventing harm, falling, separation, or danger, and grasping a source of support.

Memorizing a text so it is not forgotten has a clearer connection with حِفْظ.

Grasping a rope to avoid falling more vividly illustrates اعْتِصَام.

The families can overlap in protection; there is no absolute boundary between them.

ع ص م and و ق ي

و ق ي concerns guarding against something potentially harmful.

وِقَايَة can involve shielding oneself or placing a barrier between a person and danger.

ع ص م also concerns protection, but the اعْتِصَام branch particularly emphasizes grasping a supporting means or source.

Suitable clothing protecting someone from cold fits the field of وِقَايَة.

Grasping a rope while falling offers a clear image of اعْتِصَام.

These distinctions emerge from usage, rather than an absolute rule separating every derivative.

ع ص م and م س ك

م س ك concerns grasping and holding.

ع ص م can also concern holding, with preventing harm or rupture especially important in its central uses.

Al-Mumtahanah 60:10 combines the families:

وَلَا تُمْسِكُوا بِعِصَمِ الْكَوَافِرِ

wa-lā tumsikū bi-ʿiṣami al-kawāfir

Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:10

Here holding is the action, while عِصَم names the bonds being retained.

The two words therefore perform different roles.

ع ص م and ع ر و

عُرْوَة, under ع ر و, concerns a handhold or something one can grasp.

Al-Baqarah 2:256 has الْعُرْوَةِ الْوُثْقَىٰ.

عُرْوَة names the handhold; وُثْقَى expresses its firmness.

اعْتِصَام instead highlights the act of grasping a supporting means.

The conceptual connection is clear, but one names a handhold and the other emphasizes attachment or preservation.

ع ص م and ف ص م

ف ص م concerns breaking and rupturing a connection.

After الْعُرْوَةِ الْوُثْقَىٰ, Al-Baqarah 2:256 says لَا انْفِصَامَ لَهَا: it has no break.

Breaking a bond conceptually contrasts with maintaining it and preventing separation.

Their resemblance in spelling does not make the roots identical.

ع ص م concerns preservation and preventing harm or separation; ف ص م concerns breaking and rupture.

ع ص م and ع ص ي

They share their first two consonants but differ in meaning and usage.

عَصَى under ع ص ي concerns disobedience and defiance.

ع ص م concerns protection, preservation, and holding fast.

Someone may seek refuge in The God to avoid disobedience or restrain themselves from wrongdoing; that ethical connection does not make the roots identical.

عَصَا, “staff,” also belongs to another family and should not be equated with عِصَام because of similar sounds.

15. Translation proposals for Forqan

One fixed equivalent should not be imposed on every derivative.

“Protect” or “preserve” fits the simple verb; “hold fast” or “seek refuge” fits Form VIII; “resist” or “restrain oneself” fits the Quranic Form X; and “marriage bonds” fits عِصَم.

The simple verb

يَعْصِمُكَ — Al-Ma'idah 5:67

Brief root meaning: preserving and protecting against harm.

Meaning in this verse: protects you from people.

Proposed translation:

وَاللَّهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاسِ

wa-Allāhu yaʿṣimuka mina al-nās

Al-Māʾidah 5:67

“The God protects you from people.”

Meaning in this verse: protects me from the danger of water.

Proposed translation:

يَعْصِمُنِي مِنَ الْمَاءِ

yaʿṣimunī mina al-māʾ

Hūd 11:43

“Protects me from the water.”

يَعْصِمُكُمْ — Al-Ahzab 33:17

Meaning in this verse: protects you against divine will.

يَعْتَصِمْ — Al Imran 3:101

“Holds fast to The God.”

Form X

Brief meaning in this branch: guarding oneself and refusing to yield to a demand.

Meaning in this verse: resisted and refused.

“I did seek to seduce him, but he resisted and guarded himself.”

The active participle

عَاصِم — Yunus 10:27, Hud 11:43, and Ghafir 40:33

Brief meaning of this form: a preserver or protector.

Meaning in these verses: someone or something protecting against harm or dangerous consequences.

Proposed translation:

“Protector” or “preserver.”

The broken plural

عِصَم — Al-Mumtahanah 60:10

Brief meaning in this branch: a bond establishing and preserving a relationship.

Meaning in this verse: marriage bonds.

Proposed translation:

وَلَا تُمْسِكُوا بِعِصَمِ الْكَوَافِرِ

wa-lā tumsikū bi-ʿiṣami al-kawāfir

Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:10

“Do not retain marriage bonds with disbelieving women.”

Here عِصَم should not be rendered “immunity from sin,” “purity,” or “ownership of a spouse.”

Conclusion

ع ص م concerns preserving, restraining, and protecting against bodily harm, falling, danger, or the rupture of a bond.

Early evidence includes supporting tools such as a waterskin's strap, grasping a rope, and maintaining marriage bonds.

The simple Quranic verb concerns protection. Noah's son expects a mountain to protect him from water, but it does not save him against divine command. Al-Ma'idah 5:67 promises protection to the Messenger in the context of conveying the message.

Quranic اعْتَصَمَ concerns holding fast to The God and His guidance. Al Imran connects it with guidance and communal bonds; Al-Nisa with repentance, reform, and sincerity.

اسْتَعْصَمَ in Joseph's story concerns his resistance to a wrongful demand.

عِصَم in Al-Mumtahanah 60:10 names marriage bonds, relationships that can persist or be dissolved.

Thus preserving and preventing harm or rupture form the semantic core of ع ص م. Quranic uses variously foreground the protector, holding to a protective means, resisting a wrongful demand, or the bond maintaining a relationship.

Translation should preserve these distinctions. The lexical meaning of protection should not, without independent examination, be turned into a theory of absolute immunity from every error. Each verse's structure and context establish the protection's object and scope.
