Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root Ḥ W Ṭ

To surround, encompass, take something from all sides, enclose it fully; and in some uses, to guard, watch over, or besiege

Important Forms from This Root

حَاطَ / يَحُوطُ (ḥāṭa / yaḥūṭu)

ḥāṭa / yaḥūṭu; surrounded, guarded, watched over, or took care from the sides.

أَحَاطَ / يُحِيطُ (aḥāṭa / yuḥīṭu)

aḥāṭa / yuḥīṭu; encompassed from all sides, surrounded fully, or had comprehensive grasp.

مُحِيط (muḥīṭ)

muḥīṭ; encompassing, surrounding, fully aware, fully powerful, or all-enclosing.

أُحِيطَ (uḥīṭa)

uḥīṭa; was surrounded, was overtaken, was enclosed from every side.

حَائِط (ḥāʾiṭ)

ḥāʾiṭ; wall, or a walled garden whose perimeter has been enclosed.

Method Note

The root Ḥ W Ṭ moves between two fields that may first seem different: protection and guarding on one side, enclosure and siege on the other. These meanings are not actually unrelated. They come from the same material image: something takes the perimeter around something else. If the surrounding is for preservation, the meaning approaches protection. If it closes the way out, the meaning approaches siege and entrapment.

In the Qur’an, this root often appears in the sense of encompassing knowledge, encompassing power, encompassing punishment, or a condition surrounding a human being. So “encompassing” is not merely being near. It means that something so surrounds the matter that remaining outside it, hiding from it, or escaping it is not easy, and in some contexts not possible.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of Ḥ W Ṭ is taking the perimeter around something, so that the thing is inside a circle, enclosure, field, or authority. This encompassing can be spatial, like a wall around a garden. It can be protective, like someone guarding what is inside. It can be intellectual, like knowledge that grasps all sides of a matter. It can also be punitive or besieging, like punishment that closes the way of escape.

The direction of the encompassing matters. When the encompassing belongs to The God’s knowledge and power, it means total reach, knowledge, and authority. When it concerns sin, punishment, or loss, it means being trapped in a condition and having the exits closed.

Material Image

The material image is a wall surrounding a garden, house, or settlement. حَائِط (ḥāʾiṭ) is close to this picture: a wall, or a garden known by its surrounding wall. Such a wall can create safety inside, prevent entry from outside, and if someone is trapped within it, restrict the way out.

The image then expands. When knowledge encompasses something, it does not know only one corner of it; it accounts for its sides, limits, and relations. When power encompasses, no point lies outside its field. When sin encompasses a person, sin is no longer one isolated act; it has become the surrounding environment in which the person is enclosed.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, حَاطَهُ (ḥāṭahu) and يَحُوطُهُ (yaḥūṭuhu) can mean to guard, protect, or look after. The one who surrounds something may be keeping harm away from it. From here, حِيَاطَة (ḥiyāṭah) and حَوْطَة (ḥawṭah) come near the field of care, prudence, and management. حَائِط (ḥāʾiṭ) is a wall or a walled garden: something whose perimeter is enclosed.

Therefore, Ḥ W Ṭ is not originally negative. Encompassing can be merciful and protective, or threatening and besieging. The context decides whether the surrounding is for preservation or for being overtaken.

Qur’anic Usage

The God Encompasses the Deniers

In the parable of ṣayyib, after darknesses, thunder, lightning, thunderbolts, and fear of death, the Qur’an says: وَاللَّهُ مُحِيطٌ بِالْكَافِرِينَ (wallāhu muḥīṭun bil-kāfirīn); The God encompasses the deniers 2:19. This is a precise closing line. They fear the thunderbolts, put their fingers in their ears, and try to escape the danger; but The God surrounds the deniers from every side.

Here, مُحِيط (muḥīṭ) means that they are not outside the field of The God’s knowledge, power, and account. Fear of thunderbolt is partial; The God’s encompassing is total. They may close their ears, but they cannot step outside His encompassing reality.

Sin Encompassing a Human Being

In بَلَىٰ مَن كَسَبَ سَيِّئَةً وَأَحَاطَتْ بِهِ خَطِيئَتُهُ فَأُولَٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ (balā man kasaba sayyiʾatan wa-aḥāṭat bihi khaṭīʾatuhu fa-ulāʾika aṣḥābu al-nār), sin has become something that surrounds the person 2:81. This is not a simple isolated slip. It is a sin that has taken the person’s perimeter and enclosed him within its condition.

The phrase أَحَاطَتْ بِهِ (aḥāṭat bihi) matters. A person may commit a wrong and still see a way back, repentance, and exit from that state. But when sin encompasses, the wrong has become an environment. The person is inside a structure that limits his sight, choice, and movement.

Punishment and Fire Encompassing

In إِنَّا أَعْتَدْنَا لِلظَّالِمِينَ نَارًا أَحَاطَ بِهِمْ سُرَادِقُهَا (innā aʿtadnā lil-ẓālimīna nāran aḥāṭa bihim surādiquhā), the fire is described with its surrounding enclosure encompassing the wrongdoers 18:29. The image does not present punishment as a distant flame. It presents it as an environment into which a person is enclosed.

Here, encompassing means that the way out is closed. Punishment is not on one side so that one can flee from another. Its enclosure surrounds the person. This use reveals the besieging side of the root.

Loss Encompassing Wealth and Fruit

In the story of the owner of two gardens, the Qur’an says: وَأُحِيطَ بِثَمَرِهِ (wa-uḥīṭa bithamarihi) 18:42. His fruit was encompassed. Loss or destruction came around his produce and overtook it. A simple translation like “his fruit was destroyed” gives the result, but it loses the image of uḥīṭa.

His wealth and fruit are trapped in an enclosure of loss. It is not as if a small strike hit one point; the produce is overtaken from the sides until no rescue remains. This use shows that encompassing can describe the overtaking of wealth, yield, or the visible result of human effort.

The God’s Encompassing Knowledge

In وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ قَدْ أَحَاطَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عِلْمًا (wa-anna allāha qad aḥāṭa bikulli shayʾin ʿilman), encompassing is attached to knowledge 65:12. The God encompasses every thing in knowledge. This means His knowledge is not partial, surface-level, or one-sided. The limits, sides, relations, hidden aspects, and manifest aspects of everything are within His knowledge.

This is one of the root’s most precise layers. When knowledge encompasses, nothing remains outside its field. A human being may know a matter from one angle, but encompassing knowledge means all sides are included.

Human Inability to Encompass The God’s Knowledge

In Āyat al-Kursī, the Qur’an says: وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِّنْ عِلْمِهِ إِلَّا بِمَا شَاءَ (walā yuḥīṭūna bishayʾin min ʿilmihi illā bimā shāʾa) 2:255. Human beings do not encompass anything of His knowledge except what He wills. This verse marks the boundary of human knowledge. Human beings can know, discover, and understand, but their knowledge is not independently encompassing.

The difference between knowing and encompassing matters here. A person may know something to a degree without grasping all its dimensions. Encompassing knowledge is possible only by what The God opens. In this verse, Ḥ W Ṭ becomes a language of intellectual humility.

Denying What They Have Not Encompassed in Knowledge

In بَلْ كَذَّبُوا بِمَا لَمْ يُحِيطُوا بِعِلْمِهِ (bal kadhdhabū bimā lam yuḥīṭū biʿilmihi), the Qur’an speaks of people who denied what they had not encompassed in knowledge 10:39. The verse gives a major epistemic principle: a person should not rush to deny something whose sides he has not yet understood.

Here, encompassing stands against hasty judgment. Denial without encompassing knowledge is active ignorance: the person not only does not know, but issues a decisive judgment without knowing. This matters for Forqan’s method as well. Understanding roots and verses requires caution, reflection, and refusal to claim complete grasp too quickly.

The God Encompasses Human Actions

In many verses, The God is described as مُحِيط (muḥīṭ) regarding what people do, such as وَكَانَ اللَّهُ بِمَا يَعْمَلُونَ مُحِيطًا (wakāna allāhu bimā yaʿmalūna muḥīṭan) 4:108. This encompassing is not only outward recording. The action, its intention, effect, direction, concealment, and consequences are within The God’s knowledge.

No action falls outside the circle of The God’s knowledge and account. A person may hide an action from people, but cannot move it outside The God’s encompassing knowledge.

Difference from Nearby Words

Ḥifẓ is preserving and preventing loss. Preservation may happen from inside or outside, but it does not necessarily carry the image of taking the perimeter. Ḥ W Ṭ, when it approaches protection, keeps the image of surrounding and guarding from the sides.

Ḥaṣr is restricting and narrowing a field. It often carries the weight of limitation and confinement. Encompassing can be protective or besieging. Ṭawq is closer to a ring, collar, or band around the neck. Dawr points to aroundness or circling, but not necessarily to enclosing authority or comprehensive reach.

Shaml means including or covering, but it may lack the spatial and siege-like image of Ḥ W Ṭ. ʿIlm is knowledge, but encompassing knowledge is knowing from every side, not partial awareness. Akhḏ is taking or seizing; encompassing may come before taking, or accompany it by closing the ways out.

Translation Method

مُحِيط (muḥīṭ) should not always be translated only as “encompassing” unless the reader understands what is being carried by the word. In some contexts, the translation needs explanation: surrounding from every side, encompassing in knowledge and power, or overtaking and enclosing. When applied to The God, muḥīṭ often refers to comprehensive knowledge, power, and account. When applied to sin and punishment, it points to being trapped in a condition whose exits are closed.

In knowledge-related verses, encompassing means grasping all sides. Therefore, لَمْ يُحِيطُوا بِعِلْمِهِ (lam yuḥīṭū biʿilmihi) should not be reduced to “they did not know it.” Better: “they had not encompassed it in knowledge,” or “they had not grasped all its sides.”

Forqan Translation Suggestions

وَاللَّهُ مُحِيطٌ بِالْكَافِرِينَ (wallāhu muḥīṭun bil-kāfirīn): The God encompasses the deniers; the deniers are not outside the encompassing knowledge and power of The God.

أَحَاطَتْ بِهِ خَطِيئَتُهُ (aḥāṭat bihi khaṭīʾatuhu): his sin encompassed him; his wrongdoing so surrounded him that he became trapped within it.

أَحَاطَ بِهِمْ سُرَادِقُهَا (aḥāṭa bihim surādiquhā): its enclosure surrounded them; its surrounding canopy enclosed them from every side.

وَأُحِيطَ بِثَمَرِهِ (wa-uḥīṭa bithamarihi): his fruit was encompassed by loss; his produce was overtaken from every side and destroyed.

أَحَاطَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عِلْمًا (aḥāṭa bikulli shayʾin ʿilman): He encompasses every thing in knowledge; His knowledge surrounds every thing from every side.

لَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِّنْ عِلْمِهِ (lā yuḥīṭūna bishayʾin min ʿilmihi): they do not encompass anything of His knowledge; they do not bring any part of His knowledge into complete grasp except what He wills.

بِمَا لَمْ يُحِيطُوا بِعِلْمِهِ (bimā lam yuḥīṭū biʿilmihi): what they had not encompassed in knowledge; what they had not grasped from all sides.

Summary

The root Ḥ W Ṭ points to taking the perimeter around something. This surrounding can be protection if it preserves and guards, or siege if it closes the way out. In the Qur’an, the root moves into knowledge, power, punishment, sin, loss, and human action.

In Forqan’s reading, encompassing means that nothing remains outside the surrounding field. When The God is muḥīṭ, nothing is outside His knowledge, power, and account. When sin encompasses, the person becomes enclosed in the environment of wrongdoing. When punishment encompasses, escape is closed. And when a human being denies without encompassing knowledge, the judgment comes from haste and ignorance, not complete understanding.
