Short Introduction to the Root

The root ر ف ع moves in the semantic field of raising, lifting, and elevating. Its core is the transfer of something from a lower level, place, or rank to a higher level, place, or rank.

In the Qur’an, this raising is sometimes fully tangible and spatial: raising the Mount, raising the foundations of the House, raising the heaven, or placing someone upon a throne. At other times, the subject is non-material, such as voice, degrees, or mention. In these cases, the same image of “upwardness” remains, but the object and the context determine the kind of height.

In the verse:

وَرَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ

The direct meaning of رَفَعْنَا is “We raised / We lifted up.” فَوْقَكُم specifies the place and relation: “above you.” The connection of this scene with the covenant can make the awe and weight of that covenant prominent in reflection on the verse, but that meaning does not come from the root رفع itself.

Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

Among the most important Qur’anic forms of this root are the following:

رَفَعَ / رَفَعْنَا / يَرْفَعُ / نَرْفَعُ

He raised, We raised, he raises / raises.

رُفِعَتْ

Was raised; passive construction.

رَافِع

The one who raises; as in رَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ.

رَافِعَة

Raising; in the expression خَافِضَةٌ رَافِعَةٌ.

رَفِيع

High-ranking, elevated, or having height; in the divine description رَفِيعُ الدَّرَجَاتِ.

مَرْفُوع / مَرْفُوعَة

Raised, elevated, or lifted up, depending on the described object and the context.

This root appears in the Qur’an in various verbal and descriptive forms. Sharing the same root does not mean that all these forms have one kind of height or one ethical result.

The Semantic Core of the Root

The foundational core of ر ف ع can be stated as follows:

Raising something or placing it at a higher level, place, or rank.

This core develops several main branches in the Qur’an:

  • Spatial elevation: the Mount, the heaven, the foundations of the House, the throne.
  • Increasing the height of the voice.
  • Raising rank or degree.
  • Elevating name and mention.
  • Raising a person toward a destination or place specified by the verse.

Thus the root itself does not mean “honor,” “virtue,” “awe,” “nearness,” or “spiritual rank.” These concepts may arise from the subject and the context.

The Material Image of the Root

The simplest image of this root is lifting or extending something upward.

In building a structure, foundations rise. In raising a mountain or the heaven, something comes to stand in a higher position relative to a lower level. In raising the voice, its intensity or audible level rises.

This tangible experience allows language to speak of non-material matters through “height” as well. Human beings naturally understand a higher station, a higher degree, or a more elevated name through the vertical direction. The Qur’an uses this linguistic capacity; but this semantic extension should not cause every use of رفع to be made physical, nor every non-material use to be treated as merely metaphorical. The subject of each verse is decisive.

Use of the Root in Arabic

In Arabic, رفع stands opposite lowering or being low, and is used for bodies, voices, structures, station, rank, and other matters.

In the grammatical terminology that formed after the development of the science of grammar, “رفع” also became the name of one of the inflectional cases. But this later technical use should not be inserted into the base meaning of Qur’anic verses. In the root article, the center of study is the Qur’anic usage itself.

Raising the Mount

وَإِذْ أَخَذْنَا مِيثَاقَكُمْ وَرَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ

“And when We took your covenant and raised the Mount above you.”

Al-Baqarah 2:63

The structure of the verse is clear:

رَفَعْنَا: We raised.

فَوْقَكُم: above you.

الطُّورَ: the object; the thing that was raised.

Therefore, “above” is not contained inside رفع itself; فوق creates this relation.

The joining of raising the Mount with taking the covenant creates a serious and awe-filled scene. In reflection on the verse, one may say that this raising highlights the weight and awe of the covenant; but this contextual result should not be added to the lexical meaning of رفعنا.

A close structure is also seen in al-Baqarah 2:93 and an-Nisa 4:154.

Raising the Foundations of the House

وَإِذْ يَرْفَعُ إِبْرَاهِيمُ الْقَوَاعِدَ مِنَ الْبَيْتِ وَإِسْمَاعِيلُ

“And when Ibrahim and Ismail were raising the foundations of the House.”

Al-Baqarah 2:127

Here يرفع expresses the movement or extension of the foundations upward. The translation “were raising” or “were lifting up” preserves the image of the word.

The sanctity of the House, worship, and prayer come from the context of the verses, not from the root رفع itself.

Raising the Heaven

اللَّهُ الَّذِي رَفَعَ السَّمَاوَاتِ بِغَيْرِ عَمَدٍ تَرَوْنَهَا

“The God is the One who raised the heavens without pillars that you see.”

Ar-Rad 13:2

And:

وَالسَّمَاءَ رَفَعَهَا وَوَضَعَ الْمِيزَانَ

“And the heaven He raised, and He set the balance.”

Ar-Rahman 55:7

And:

رَفَعَ سَمْكَهَا فَسَوَّاهَا

“He raised its height and proportioned it.”

An-Naziat 79:28

And:

وَإِلَى السَّمَاءِ كَيْفَ رُفِعَتْ

“And to the heaven, how it was raised.”

Al-Ghashiyah 88:18

These verses preserve the image of height, but they do not explain the physical quality of the heaven or the mechanism of this raising. Therefore, no specific cosmological theory should be extracted from the root رفع.

The Contrast Between ر ف ع and و ض ع

Ar-Rahman 55:7 places two different movements beside one another:

وَالسَّمَاءَ رَفَعَهَا وَوَضَعَ الْمِيزَانَ

رفع about the heaven: raising and elevating.

وضع about the balance: setting and placing.

This contrast is useful for understanding the field of رفع, because it shows that رفع is not merely “placing”; rather, it contains placing or transferring in an upward direction.

Even so, one should not conclude from this pairing that وضع is the absolute opposite of رفع in every use. In this verse, two verbs come together, but each has its own object and semantic field.

Raising the Voice

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَرْفَعُوا أَصْوَاتَكُمْ فَوْقَ صَوْتِ النَّبِيِّ

“O you who have faith, do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet.”

Al-Hujurat 49:2

The subject of raising here is voice. The word فوق specifies the comparative relation.

This verse shows that the root is not only about bodily location; the height of voice too can be expressed within the same image of “upwardness.”

Raising Degrees

نَرْفَعُ دَرَجَاتٍ مَّن نَّشَاءُ

“We raise in degrees whomever We will.”

Al-Anam 6:83

And:

وَرَفَعَ بَعْضَكُمْ فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ دَرَجَاتٍ

“And He raised some of you above others in degrees.”

Al-Anam 6:165

And:

يَرْفَعِ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنكُمْ وَالَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْعِلْمَ دَرَجَاتٍ

“The God raises those of you who have faith and those who were given knowledge by degrees.”

Al-Mujadilah 58:11

In these uses, degrees specifies the kind of height. Here the rising is an increase of rank, not a simple movement of a body in space.

The exact meaning of each degree must be understood from the context of that verse; the root رفع alone does not say whether the degree is financial, social, scholarly, or afterlife-related.

Elevating Mention

وَرَفَعْنَا لَكَ ذِكْرَكَ

“And We elevated your mention for you.”

Ash-Sharh 94:4

The object is ذكر. Therefore, the translation should preserve this word as much as possible.

In Persian, “We made your name and remembrance elevated in renown” is more natural than “We raised your remembrance,” but the root explanation must show that the original image is still “raising.”

“Fame,” “rank,” or “belovedness” may be effects of this elevation of mention, but they should not replace ذكر itself in translation.

ر ف ع Beside ص ع د

إِلَيْهِ يَصْعَدُ الْكَلِمُ الطَّيِّبُ وَالْعَمَلُ الصَّالِحُ يَرْفَعُهُ

“Toward Him ascends the good word, and righteous work raises it.”

Fatir 35:10

This verse brings two nearby fields together:

صعود: going up.

رفع: raising.

This difference is clear in this verse, but it should not be turned into an absolute rule for all Arabic without examining all uses of the two roots.

Concerning the referent of the pronoun in يرفعه and the way “righteous work” is connected to “the good word,” there are different analyses; the root article does not need to make one of them decisive without a definite proof.

Raising Persons

Isa

إِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَىٰ إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ

“When The God said: O Isa, I am taking you in full and raising you toward Me.”

Al Imran 3:55

And:

بَلْ رَفَعَهُ اللَّهُ إِلَيْهِ

“Rather, The God raised him toward Himself.”

An-Nisa 4:158

رفع gives the meaning of raising, and إلى / إليه specifies the direction or goal of this raising.

But the particle إلى by itself does not determine the quality of this movement. From the combination raising + toward Him, one can understand orientation “toward Him,” but whether this raising is bodily, spatial, rank-related, or of another quality is not made definite by the preposition and the root alone.

At the level of contextual reflection, one may say that “toward Him” is compatible with nearness and a higher station, but this reflection should not replace the direct meaning of the construction.

Idris

وَرَفَعْنَاهُ مَكَانًا عَلِيًّا

“And We raised him to a high place.”

Maryam 19:57

Here مكانًا عليًّا specifies what is said about the result of raising. عليًا describes height; رفع itself is the act of raising.

The quality of this place and its manner are not determined by the root itself.

Derivatives Connected to Divine Names or Descriptions

رَفِيعُ الدَّرَجَاتِ

رَفِيعُ الدَّرَجَاتِ ذُو الْعَرْشِ يُلْقِي الرُّوحَ مِنْ أَمْرِهِ عَلَىٰ مَنْ يَشَاءُ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ

“High in degrees, Owner of the Throne; He casts the Spirit from His command upon whomever He wills from His servants.”

Ghafir 40:15

In this verse, رَفِيع is a description of The God. This form comes on the pattern فعيل and is connected with the field of height and elevation.

The construction رَفِيعُ الدَّرَجَاتِ has more than one possible grammatical and semantic explanation. It may be connected to the exaltedness and high rank of The God, or to being the possessor of high degrees and the one who raises ranks. The construction itself is brief, and one interpretation should not be made exclusive without a decisive clue.

The important point for Forqan is that the Qur’anic form is رَفِيعُ الدَّرَجَاتِ. If the later tradition used the definite form الرَّفِيع as a divine name, it should be kept distinct from the exact Qur’anic form.

The material meaning of “height” should also not be physicalized here. When رفيع is a description of The God, the field of the root connects to exaltedness and high rank, not to a body being at a higher point.

رَافِعَة in Contrast With خَافِضَة

خَافِضَةٌ رَّافِعَةٌ

“Lowering and raising.”

Al-Waqiah 56:3

In this construction, lowering and raising stand directly opposite one another.

This pairing is one of the clearest witnesses for the foundational direction of رفع: raising in contrast with lowering.

But what or whom the Event lowers and raises is examined from the context of the surah and should not be placed inside the lexical definition of the two words.

مَرْفُوع and مَرْفُوعَة

وَالسَّقْفِ الْمَرْفُوعِ

“And the raised roof.”

At-Tur 52:5

فِيهَا سُرُرٌ مَرْفُوعَةٌ

“In it are raised couches.”

Al-Ghashiyah 88:13

فِي صُحُفٍ مُّكَرَّمَةٍ ۝ مَّرْفُوعَةٍ مُّطَهَّرَةٍ

“In honored pages; elevated and purified.”

Abasa 80:13–14

The passive participle shows that something is in a state of being “raised.” But the kind of height is specified by the described object and the context.

For the roof and the couches, height can be spatial. For the pages, given honored and purified, elevation of rank can also be involved.

Comparison With Nearby Roots

ر ف ع Compared With ع ل و

رفع focuses more on the act of raising or transferring to a higher place.

علو tends more toward being high, exalted, or superior itself.

In:

وَرَفَعْنَاهُ مَكَانًا عَلِيًّا

Both fields come together:

رفعناه: We raised him.

مكانًا عليًا: a high place.

This pairing clarifies the difference in tendency between the two roots.

ر ف ع Compared With ص ع د

صعود more often expresses going upward; رفع more often expresses raising something upward.

Fatir 35:10 is a good witness for this difference.

ر ف ع Compared With خ ف ض

خفض moves in the direction of lowering, and رفع in the direction of raising.

The contrast خافضة رافعة makes this relation clear.

ر ف ع Compared With و ض ع

وضع tends toward placing and setting; رفع toward raising.

In ar-Rahman 55:7, the heaven is “raised” and the balance is “set.” This pairing clarifies the boundary between the two verbs without making them absolute opposites in every use.

ر ف ع and ف و ق

فوق is not a verb synonymous with رفع; it expresses the relation of being above something.

In:

وَرَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ

رفعنا is the act of raising, and فوقكم specifies the resulting position.

Grammatical and Structural Notes

The verb رفع is transitive in many Qur’anic uses, and its object determines what has been raised:

رَفَعْنَا ... الطُّورَ

Object: the Mount.

رَفَعَ أَبَوَيْهِ

Object: his father and mother.

رَفَعَ السَّمَاوَاتِ

Object: the heavens.

تَرْفَعُوا أَصْوَاتَكُمْ

Object: your voices.

Other elements in the sentence specify added layers:

فوقكم: spatial relation.

على العرش: place of being set.

إليه: direction or goal.

درجات: type of rank-height.

مكانًا عليًا: description of destination or place.

Therefore, the meaning of a preposition, adverbial expression, or object should not be placed inside the root itself.

Challenges of Understanding and Translation

Two extremes must be avoided.

First, translating every use as physical raising. This is not natural in “raising degrees” and “raising mention.”

Second, turning every use into “spiritual elevation” or “granting rank.” This removes the material meaning in the raising of the Mount, the heaven, and the foundations of the House.

The suitable path is to preserve the shared image of “upwardness,” and then understand its kind from the object and the context.

Also, in uses related to the heaven, Isa, Idris, or afterlife matters, one should not extract from رفع itself a physical or metaphysical quality that the verse has not stated.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For رَفَعَ in material use:

“raised,” “lifted up,” “elevated.”

For وَرَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ:

“And We raised the Mount above you.”

For يَرْفَعُ إِبْرَاهِيمُ الْقَوَاعِدَ:

“Ibrahim was raising / lifting up the foundations of the House.”

For وَرَفَعَ أَبَوَيْهِ عَلَى الْعَرْشِ:

“He raised his father and mother upon the throne.”

“He seated them on the throne” can be a natural explanation of the result, not a full replacement of the root image.

For لَا تَرْفَعُوا أَصْوَاتَكُمْ:

“Do not raise your voices / do not lift them higher.”

For raising degrees:

“raised in degrees,” “raised his rank,” depending on the structure of the verse.

For وَرَفَعْنَا لَكَ ذِكْرَكَ:

“We made your name and mention elevated in renown.”

For رَفِيعُ الدَّرَجَاتِ:

“High in degrees” or a more natural rendering based on the final analysis of the construction, while keeping a separate explanation of its structure.

For مرفوع:

“raised,” “lifted up,” or “elevated,” according to the thing being described.

Meaning in the Forqan Verse Under Study

وَرَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ

Short root meaning:

raising, lifting up, elevating.

Meaning in this verse:

We raised; the Mount was lifted above them.

Contextual reflection:

The placement of this raising in the scene of taking the covenant highlights the awe and weight of that covenant. This layer is understood from the whole scene, not from the lexical meaning of رَفَعْنَا alone.

Conclusion

The root ر ف ع moves around the axis of raising and placing something at a higher level, place, or rank.

In material uses, the Mount, the heaven, the foundations of the House, and persons are raised. In other uses, voice, degrees, and mention are understood within the same image of height.

The Qur’an also uses the derivative رَفِيع in description of The God: رَفِيعُ الدَّرَجَاتِ. In this use, the field of “height” connects to exaltedness and elevation, without being reduced to bodily height.

The pairing of رفع with خفض، وضع، علو، صعود and فوق clarifies its borders further: رفع is the act of raising itself; the kind, direction, and result of this raising are understood from the other elements of the sentence.

The final definition of this root can be stated as follows:

ر ف ع expresses raising, lifting up, or transferring something from a lower level, place, or rank to a higher level, place, or rank. The kind of this height — spatial, vocal, rank-related, or meaningful — becomes clear from the object, grammatical structure, and context of the verse.
