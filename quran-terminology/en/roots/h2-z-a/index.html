Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root H Z A

Mocking, belittling, humiliating, making something weightless, and treating seriously placed truth as a game

Morphological forms of this root

The root ه ز أ (H Z A) does not simply mean laughing or joking. A joke can be harmless and human, but istihzāʾ means lowering the rank of something, making it appear weightless, and turning a serious matter into mockery and play.

General meaning of the root H Z A

The root ه ز أ (H Z A) points to mocking, deriding, belittling, and making something or someone appear worthless. The core issue is not laughter itself, but reducing the weight and honor of the one being mocked.

When someone treats something as huzuwan, he behaves as though it has no serious value. Something that should be met with understanding, responsibility, respect, or serious attention becomes play, a joke, humiliation, or an object of laughter.

For this reason, mockery can be more dangerous than simple ignorance. The ignorant person may not know, but the mocker often hears or encounters something, then instead of understanding or answering, he makes it light in order to escape its seriousness.

Usage of the root in Arabic

Classical Arabic says هَزِئَ به (haziʾa bh) or هَزِئَ منه (haziʾa mnh): he mocked him or took him in derision. It also says اِسْتَهْزَأَ به (astahzaʾa bh): he treated him with mocking and belittling contempt.

الهُزْء (al-huzʾ) and الهُزُؤ (al-huzuʾ) mean mockery and derision. المَهْزَأَة (al-mahzah) is something or a situation turned into a field of mockery. المُسْتَهْزِئ (al-mustahziʾ) is the one who performs this action.

The material image of hazʾ: thinness, lifelessness, and loss of weight

In some material Arabic usages, الهُزْأَة (al-huzah) is used for a camel that, through old age, illness, cold, weakness, or thinness, has lost its flesh and strength and become frail and lifeless.

This image is important. Something that should have strength, flesh, weight, and movement has become thin, weak, and ineffective. The same sense appears in language and ethics: the mocker tries to make something heavy, serious, and alive appear thin, lifeless, light, and unimportant.

When the signs of God, faith, messengers, or believers are mocked, the mocker tries to reduce the social weight of truth. He cannot destroy truth, but he tries to make it appear weak and ineffective in the eyes of people.

The linguistic image of hazʾ: making a serious matter light

The main behavioral image of this root is making something light. A person lowers something from its serious station and places it in the field of laughter, humiliation, or play. In istihzāʾ, the other side is not merely rejected; it is made small.

Someone who denies a sign may enter open disagreement. But someone who mocks a sign tries to break its weight in the eyes of others. Instead of facing truth, he turns the space of understanding into a space of laughter and contempt.

For this reason, mockery can become a social tool: through derision, one creates group pressure, embarrasses the believer, makes truth seem unimportant, and blocks the path of taking the message seriously.

Examples outside the Qur’an

Arabic says استهزأ بخصمه (asthzʾ bkhṣmh): he mocked his opponent, humiliated him, and tried to lower his rank.

It says استهزأ بالكلام (asthzʾ bālklām): he did not take the speech seriously and treated it mockingly.

It says استهزأ بالقانون (asthzʾ bālqānwn): he treated the law as though it had no binding force or honor. This shows that mockery is not always laughing at a person; it can be belittling an order, covenant, law, or truth.

Morphological and semantic forms

The simple form is هَزِئَ / يَهْزَأُ (haziʾa / yahzaʾu): he mocked or derided.

اِسْتَهْزَأَ / يَسْتَهْزِئُ (istahzaʾa / yastahziʾu) belongs to Form X. In this form, the mocking conduct becomes stronger and more deliberate, as though the person intentionally pulls something into the field of ridicule and makes it appear light and dishonored.

مُسْتَهْزِئ (mustahziʾ) is the active participle, the one who performs this behavior. مُسْتَهْزِئُونَ (mustahziʾūn) is its masculine plural: mockers, deriders, or those who make truth appear light.

The difference between joking, ridicule, and istihzāʾ

Joking can be natural, harmless, affectionate, and human. In healthy joking, the honor of a person or truth is not broken.

Ridicule more directly points to humiliating laughter at someone or something. It contains lowering and making small.

Istihzāʾ is usually deeper and more dangerous, because it can become a method of escaping truth. The mocker makes truth into a plaything instead of answering it; he makes it light instead of understanding it.

Usage of the root in the Qur’an

In the Qur’an, the root ه ز أ (H Z A) appears mostly in contexts of dealing with signs, messengers, believers, religion, or divine promises. Some take the signs of God as play and mockery, some mock the messengers, and some belittle the faith of the believers.

These uses show that mockery in the Qur’an is not merely an unpleasant social behavior. It is a form of opposition to truth. The mocker does not want to enter the serious weight of the message. He pulls the message down into joke, humiliation, and play in order to escape its claim.

Mustahziʾūn in the verses of hypocrisy

In the opening verses of Sūrah al-Baqarah, the hypocrites meet the believers and say, “We believe.” But when they withdraw privately to their devils, they say: إِنَّا مَعَكُمْ إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُسْتَهْزِئُونَ (innā maʿakum innamā naḥnu mustahziʾūna). This appears in 2:14.

Here, mustahziʾūn does not simply mean “we were joking.” They are saying: we are not truly with the believers; our statement of faith was not serious; we were taking them and their faith as a game.

This word uncovers the face of hypocrisy. In the field of laqū, the hypocrite takes on the face of faith. In the field of khalaw, he returns to his devils. With mustahziʾūn, he reveals that faith was used as a mask for social play.

Mockery and making faith light

In the verse of hypocrisy, mockery is directed at the believers and their faith. The hypocrite says “we believe” with his tongue, but privately says that he was only mocking. Faith on his tongue is a tool, not truth.

This behavior lowers faith from its serious place. Faith should reveal the direction of the heart, action, and loyalty of the human being; the hypocrite turns it into a performative sentence.

Mockery against truth

In the Qur’an, mockery often stands against truth. Truth is firm, weighty, standing, and real. When truth comes, the human being should face it, examine it, and clarify his relation to it.

But the mocker, instead of facing truth, tries to make it look light and hollow. He cannot remove the actual weight of truth, but he tries to turn it, in his eyes and the eyes of others, into play and derision.

Mockery of signs and messengers

In the Qur’an, messengers were also mocked. This shows that mockery in the face of truth is not new. When a messenger brings a weighty truth, those who do not want to face it may mock it.

In 6:10, messengers before you were mocked, but that which they mocked surrounded those who mocked.

Allāh yastahziʾu bihim and the return of action to the actor

In the continuation of the verses of hypocrisy, 2:15 says: اللَّهُ يَسْتَهْزِئُ بِهِمْ (allāh yastahziʾu bihim). This should not be understood as human mockery or belittling laughter attributed to God. God is beyond human games.

The deeper meaning is that the structure they built is returned upon them. They imagine they have played the believers, but in reality they are left in the field of respite, arrogance, and blindness.

In Arabic and Qur’anic expression, the recompense of an action is sometimes named with the action itself. “God mocks them” means that the recompense of their mockery returns to them with precision and justice.

Ḥāqa bihim and truth surrounding mockery

The Qur’an says: وَحَاقَ بِهِمْ مَا كَانُوا بِهِ يَسْتَهْزِئُونَ (waḥāqa bihim mā kānūā bihi yastahziʾūna): what they used to mock surrounded them. A clear example appears in 6:10.

This expression is precise. Through mockery, the mocker is as though building a bubble around himself so that he does not feel the weight of truth. But truth does not disappear through mockery. Eventually the very thing that was belittled surrounds the human being.

Ḥāqa means something arrived, surrounded, and closed the way of escape. Mockery does not weaken truth; it only increases the human being’s distance from readiness before truth.

Mockery, devils, and hidden hypocrisy

The word mustahziʾūn in Sūrah al-Baqarah comes after two important movements: laqū and khalaw. They say “we believe” in the field of encounter with the believers, but in private with their devils they say: we are with you; we were only mocking.

This shows that mockery here is not merely individual behavior. It is part of the network of hypocrisy. Their devils are the center that confirms and strengthens this lightening of truth. In privacy, the language of hypocrisy emerges from behind the curtain.

Translation suggestions for Forqan

هَزِئَ / يَهْزَأُ (haziʾa / yahzaʾu): mocked; derided; belittled.

اِسْتَهْزَأَ / يَسْتَهْزِئُ (istahzaʾa / yastahziʾu): took in mockery; derided; made weightless; treated with contempt and play.

مُسْتَهْزِئ (mustahziʾ): mocker; derider; one who makes a serious matter light and weightless.

إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُسْتَهْزِئُونَ (innamā naḥnu mustahziʾūna): we were only mocking; we were only taking them as a game; we did not take their faith seriously.

هُزُوًا / هُزْءًا (huzuwanā / huzanā): mockery; derision; plaything; something treated lightly and without honor.

اللَّهُ يَسْتَهْزِئُ بِهِمْ (allāh yastahziʾu bihim): God returns their mockery upon them; God exposes the emptiness of their scheme against themselves.

وَحَاقَ بِهِمْ مَا كَانُوا بِهِ يَسْتَهْزِئُونَ (waḥāqa bihim mā kānūā bihi yastahziʾūna): the truth they belittled surrounded them; what they mocked enclosed them.

Summary

The root ه ز أ (H Z A) in Arabic points to mocking, deriding, and belittling. The issue is not merely laughter; it is lowering rank, breaking weight, and turning a serious matter into play.

In the Qur’an, this root appears mostly in relation to the human response to signs, messengers, faith, believers, or divine promises. Mockery in this field is a method of escaping the seriousness of truth.

Truth does not disappear through mockery. Eventually the very truth that was belittled surrounds the human being as consequence and judgment.
