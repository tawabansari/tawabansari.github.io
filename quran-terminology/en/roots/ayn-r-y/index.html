Core meaning: being naked or uncovered; a person or thing lacking a covering that covers it or previously covered it.

1. Introduction and meanings in early Arabic

The triliteral Arabic root ع ر ي concerns nakedness, lack of covering, and being bare. It applies to the human body as well as land, animals, and other things that can bear a covering.

In early Arabic, عَرِيَ means to become naked or to be uncovered. عَارٍ describes someone without covering, and عُرْيَان is another form expressing nakedness. عَرَاء denotes open ground without something to screen or shelter it.

The meaning is not limited to an absence of clothing. An unsaddled horse, for example, can be called فَرَسٌ عُرْيٌ. Such a horse does not necessarily lack its natural bodily covering: it lacks the saddle that might otherwise be on its back.

الْعَرَاء also denotes open, unsheltered ground. Land has no clothing to remove; the relevant absence is that of cover, shade, or something screening it from the open surroundings.

These uses support the following definition of the core meaning:

ع ر ي denotes being bare or uncovered: something lacks a covering that, in the situation concerned, might cover, protect, or screen it from view or exposure.

Protection and concealment are possible functions of a covering; they should not all be included in every derivative's meaning.

2. Evidence from poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period

An early witness appears in poetry attributed to al-Nabighah al-Dhubyani, a pre-Islamic poet:

أَتَيْتُكَ عَارِيًا خَلَقًا ثِيَابِي
عَلَى خَوْفٍ تُظَنُّ بِيَ الظُّنُونُ

ataytuka ʿāriyan khalaqan thiyābī / ʿalā khawfin tuẓannu biya al-ẓunūnu

“I came to you bare, my clothes worn out, in fear, with suspicions cast upon me.”

Here عَارِيًا is combined with خَلَقًا ثِيَابِي: the speaker calls himself bare while also mentioning his worn clothing.

This combination shows that nakedness can extend beyond the absolute absence of clothes. Someone without adequate or sufficient covering may describe their condition this way. عَارٍ therefore need not always mean that not a single piece of covering remains on the body.

Another witness appears in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic poet Abu Du'ad al-Iyadi:

فَبِتْنَا عُرَاةً لَدَى مُهْرِنَا
نُنَزِّعُ مِنْ شَفَتَيْهِ الصَّفَارَا

fa-bitnā ʿurātan ladā muhrinā / nunazziʿu min shafatayhi al-ṣafārā

Approximate rendering, following the “bare” reading: “We spent the night bare beside our colt, pulling dry prickly vegetation from its lips.” The disputed word is discussed below; “on the alert” is an interpretive explanation, not its certain direct equivalent.

The verse contains عُرَاة, but its precise interpretation is disputed. Some explanations relate it to nakedness or readiness after putting aside clothing; others connect it with عُرَواء, a fit of shivering or chill. A variant also reads قِيَامًا, “standing,” instead of عُرَاةً.

The witness is useful for examining the range of interpretations, but its variant wording and disputed meaning make it unsuitable as the basis for a definitive new definition.

Overall, عَارٍ in poetry attributed to the pre-Islamic period and Classical Arabic lexical accounts supports nakedness and lack of covering as an old, established meaning of this family.

الْعَرَاء as open ground has clear Quranic examples and lexical explanations. Without independent evidence, however, not every feature of this noun denoting a place can be assumed to have been used identically throughout the pre-Islamic period.

3. Lexical and Quranic forms

Important forms include عَرِيَ, يَعْرَى, عَارٍ, عُرْيَان, عُرْي, عَرَاء, أَعْرَى, and تَعَرَّى.

The forms can perform different functions in a sentence:

عَرِيَ and يَعْرَى express being naked or uncovered.

عَارٍ and عُرْيَان describe a person or thing without covering.

أَعْرَى can mean to strip or leave something uncovered.

تَعَرَّى is used for becoming naked or removing one's covering.

الْعَرَاء names open, unsheltered ground without screening or protective cover.

The Quran uses two forms in three occurrences: تَعْرَىٰ in Ta Ha 20:118, and الْعَرَاءِ in Al-Saffat 37:145 and Al-Qalam 68:49.

A morphological distinction matters: تَعْرَىٰ in Ta Ha is the second-person imperfect of عَرِيَ يَعْرَى, not the Form V verb تَعَرَّى. Their written resemblance should not obscure the difference in structure.

4. تَعْرَىٰ: nakedness contrasted with having a covering

The first Quranic occurrence discussed here is in The God's address to Adam:

إِنَّ لَكَ أَلَّا تَجُوعَ فِيهَا وَلَا تَعْرَىٰ

inna laka allā tajūʿa fīhā wa-lā taʿrā

Ṭā Hā 20:118

Ta Ha 20:118

“It is granted to you that you shall neither hunger there nor be naked.”

تَعْرَىٰ denotes becoming naked or remaining uncovered. It appears alongside تَجُوعَ: one concerns hunger and the other nakedness.

The following verse adds two further conditions:

وَأَنَّكَ لَا تَظْمَأُ فِيهَا وَلَا تَضْحَىٰ

wa-annaka lā taẓmaʾu fīhā wa-lā taḍḥā

Ṭā Hā 20:119

Ta Ha 20:119

“And that you shall neither thirst there nor be exposed to the sun.”

Together these verses mention four conditions: hunger, nakedness, thirst, and exposure to sunlight.

The passage describes Adam's enjoyment of conditions meeting basic needs: food against hunger, clothing against nakedness, and provision against thirst and exposure to the sun.

تَعْرَىٰ itself does not establish that nakedness here necessarily means poverty, social exclusion, or loss of moral standing. Such conditions may accompany nakedness elsewhere, but the verb's direct meaning is being uncovered.

Nakedness in Adam's story

Later in the same story:

فَأَكَلَا مِنْهَا فَبَدَتْ لَهُمَا سَوْآتُهُمَا وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِنْ وَرَقِ الْجَنَّةِ وَعَصَىٰ آدَمُ رَبَّهُ فَغَوَىٰ

fa-akalā minhā fa-badat lahumā sawʾātuhumā wa-ṭafiqā yakhṣifāni ʿalayhimā min waraqi al-jannati wa-ʿaṣā ādamu rabbahu fa-ghawā

Ṭā Hā 20:121

Ta Ha 20:121

“Then they both ate from it, and their private parts became visible to them, and they began joining leaves from the Garden over themselves. Adam disobeyed his Lord and went astray.”

This verse helps explain تَعْرَىٰ because it describes the exposure of سَوْآت and the attempt to cover the body with the Garden's leaves.

In Ta Ha 20:118, freedom from nakedness is part of Adam's provision in the Garden. In Ta Ha 20:121, a new situation leads Adam and his wife to cover themselves.

سَوْآت and عُرْي are nevertheless distinct words. Here سَوْآت denotes bodily parts whose exposure prompts an attempt to cover them; عُرْي is the broader notion of being uncovered.

Al-A'raf 7:22 describes the same event through the exposure of سَوْآت and their covering with leaves from the Garden.

This connection gives تَعْرَىٰ in Adam's story a clear relation to bodily covering. Replacing that direct physical meaning with an exclusively symbolic sense, such as loss of guidance or spiritual standing, does not fit the context.

5. الْعَرَاء: open, uncovered ground

The root's second Quranic form is الْعَرَاءِ.

عَرَاء denotes open, exposed ground without screening or shelter. It is semantically connected with nakedness, but describes a place rather than a human body.

A person may be naked because they lack clothes; a horse may be عُرْي because it lacks a saddle; land may be عَرَاء because it is open and unsheltered.

The root thus does not invariably indicate the absence of one particular kind of covering. The subject and context determine which covering is relevant.

عَرَاء should not automatically be equated with a desert, seashore, or ground entirely devoid of every kind of plant. Some lexical explanations stress the absence of trees or screening vegetation. Such places may exemplify it, but the translation “open, unsheltered ground” preserves the shared sense without specifying the entire landscape.

6. الْعَرَاء in Jonah's story

The first occurrence of الْعَرَاءِ discussed here is in Jonah's story:

فَنَبَذْنَاهُ بِالْعَرَاءِ وَهُوَ سَقِيمٌ

fa-nabadhnāhu bi-l-ʿarāʾi wa-huwa saqīm

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:145

Al-Saffat 37:145

“So We cast him onto open, unsheltered ground while he was ill.”

الْعَرَاءِ identifies the place where Jonah was cast. نَبَذْنَاهُ expresses the casting, while بِالْعَرَاءِ locates it.

وَهُوَ سَقِيمٌ describes Jonah's physical condition at that time.

The verse thus depicts an ill person in an open, unsheltered place. Read together with the following verse, the absence of environmental cover becomes a significant part of the scene.

The following verse says:

وَأَنْبَتْنَا عَلَيْهِ شَجَرَةً مِنْ يَقْطِينٍ

wa-anbatnā ʿalayhi shajaratan min yaqṭīn

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:146

Al-Saffat 37:146

“And We caused a plant of yaqṭīn to grow over him.”

Its position after الْعَرَاءِ creates a meaningful connection: Jonah is first cast onto open, unsheltered ground, and then a plant is grown over him.

The sequence can suggest that the plant provided cover or shelter. الْعَرَاءِ itself does not mean “a need for shade,” however, and Al-Saffat 37:146 does not detail every function of the plant.

Nor does الْعَرَاءِ alone establish that Jonah was cast onto a seashore, a particular island, or a specific desert. A precise geographical identification needs independent evidence.

The word's clear meaning here is open, unsheltered ground.

7. الْعَرَاء in Al-Qalam

The noun's other occurrence is in Al-Qalam:

لَوْلَا أَنْ تَدَارَكَهُ نِعْمَةٌ مِنْ رَبِّهِ لَنُبِذَ بِالْعَرَاءِ وَهُوَ مَذْمُومٌ

lawlā an tadārakahu niʿmatun min rabbihi la-nubidha bi-l-ʿarāʾi wa-huwa madhmūm

Al-Qalam 68:49

Al-Qalam 68:49

“Had a favor from his Lord not reached him, he would have been cast onto open, unsheltered ground while blameworthy.”

الْعَرَاءِ retains its spatial meaning, but the sentence differs from Al-Saffat 37:145.

In Al-Saffat, فَنَبَذْنَاهُ reports Jonah's being cast out. In Al-Qalam, لَوْلَا أَنْ تَدَارَكَهُ نِعْمَةٌ مِنْ رَبِّهِ introduces a counterfactual situation: what would have happened had his Lord's favor not reached him?

The distinction matters. At first glance, one verse may seem to report his being cast onto open ground while the other denies it.

The remainder of each sentence clarifies the difference.

In Al-Saffat 37:145 he is cast out while سَقِيمٌ, “ill.”

In Al-Qalam 68:49 the hypothetical outcome includes وَهُوَ مَذْمُومٌ, “while blameworthy.”

Al-Qalam should therefore not be read merely as denying any casting onto open ground. Its conditional construction concerns being cast out in the state described by مَذْمُومٌ, whereas his Lord's favor intervened in his fate.

الْعَرَاءِ means open, unsheltered ground in both verses. Their difference arises from the verbs, conditional structure, and Jonah's state, rather than a change in the noun's meaning.

8. Distinctions from related roots

ع ر ي (ʿariya) and ع ر و (ʿarā)

These roots are close in written form and in parts of their semantic range, but their Quranic uses should be distinguished in morphological analysis.

ع ر ي in the uses examined here concerns nakedness and lack of covering.

ع ر و, in forms such as اعْتَرَاكَ and عُرْوَة, concerns reaching or affecting someone, or something to which one holds fast.

In Hud 11:54, اعْتَرَاكَ بَعْضُ آلِهَتِنَا بِسُوءٍ concerns evil afflicting a person.

In Al-Baqarah 2:256 and Luqman 31:22, الْعُرْوَةِ الْوُثْقَىٰ denotes a firm handhold to which a person holds fast.

Resemblance between consonants does not justify transferring these uses to the meaning of nakedness.

Some older dictionaries discuss the families together because of their closely related structures and alternation of weak consonants. That arrangement does not make all their forms and meanings identical.

ع ر ي (ʿariya) and ك ش ف (kashafa)

كَشْف concerns removing a covering, setting aside an obstacle, or revealing something.

عَرِيَ more often expresses the state of being uncovered.

Someone might remove a covering and expose something, an action expressible with كَشْف. The thing now lacking cover might be described as عَارٍ.

This is a tendency, not an absolute separation between their semantic fields.

ع ر ي (ʿariya) and س ت ر (satara)

س ت ر concerns covering and keeping something concealed.

عُرْي, by contrast, expresses the uncovered state.

Covering does not always mean providing clothes. A wall, curtain, plant, or natural barrier can screen something. The lack of covering in عَرَاء can accordingly describe a place.

ع ر ي (ʿariya) and ع و ر (ʿawira)

ع و ر occurs in the Quran in forms such as عَوْرَة, concerning private parts, vulnerability, or a place requiring cover and protection.

It should not be identified with ع ر ي.

Nakedness may expose the private parts, but “private parts” and “nakedness” are not the same concept.

In Al-Ahzab 33:13, for instance, some people describe their homes as عَوْرَة. The claim concerns vulnerable or unprotected houses, not bodily nakedness.

Semantic proximity should therefore not lead to merging the two roots.

9. Translation proposals for Forqan

The following renderings suit the Quranic forms:

تَعْرَىٰ — Ta Ha 20:118

“Be naked”

This concise, natural rendering fits Adam's story. Poverty, deprivation, or loss of spiritual covering need not be inserted into the translation itself.

الْعَرَاءِ — Al-Saffat 37:145

“Open, unsheltered ground”

In the verse's phrase:

فَنَبَذْنَاهُ بِالْعَرَاءِ

fa-nabadhnāhu bi-l-ʿarāʾ

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:145

“So We cast him onto open, unsheltered ground.”

الْعَرَاءِ — Al-Qalam 68:49

Again, “open, unsheltered ground.”

In the phrase:

لَنُبِذَ بِالْعَرَاءِ

la-nubidha bi-l-ʿarāʾ

Al-Qalam 68:49

“He would have been cast onto open, unsheltered ground.”

“Bare ground” also preserves the root's image and is understandable, but “open, unsheltered ground” conveys the spatial character more clearly.

A brief root meaning for Forqan word boxes may read:

Brief root meaning: Being naked or uncovered; lacking a covering.

For the contextual meaning of each derivative:

تَعْرَىٰ: Be naked.

الْعَرَاءِ: Open, unsheltered ground.

Conclusion

The Arabic root ع ر ي denotes nakedness and lack of covering. Its early uses show that this extends beyond the human body to animals, land, and other things.

In Ta Ha 20:118, تَعْرَىٰ concerns human nakedness within a passage addressing basic needs.

الْعَرَاءِ in Al-Saffat 37:145 and Al-Qalam 68:49 denotes open, unsheltered ground, a place where Jonah's physical condition and fate are significant to the narrative.

The two uses share a semantic core: absence of covering. One concerns an uncovered person; the other, a place without screening or shelter.

Understanding this root requires preserving nakedness and lack of covering while allowing the context to determine the covering concerned and the consequences of its absence. Forms such as عُرْوَة and اعْتَرَى should not be equated with the Quranic derivatives of ع ر ي without separate analysis.
