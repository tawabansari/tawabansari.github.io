Qur'anic Root

The Qur'anic Root TT AYN M (Ṭ ʿ M)

Tasting, eating, food, feeding, and real access to nourishment

Morphological forms of this root

Root
Foundational meaning
ط ع م (ṭ ʿ m)

Receiving something through the mouth, tasting, eating, and being nourished by food.
Verb
Taste/eat

He ate, tasted, or consumed something through the mouth.
Noun
Food

This English mirror follows the same order and argument of the Persian article, while rendering the root study naturally for English readers.

Short Meaning of the Root

The root ط ع م (ṭ ʿ m) points to receiving something through the mouth, tasting, eating, and benefiting from it as food. From this core, طَعَام (ṭaʿām) means a substance or share prepared for eating, and إِطْعَام (iṭʿām) means making food available to another.

In the Qur'an, this root is used for tasting and eating, for providing food, and for the social relation between food and the human being in need.

Important Forms of the Root

طَعِمَ (ṭaʿima) is the past verb and, depending on context, may mean “he ate,” “he tasted,” or “he consumed something through the mouth.” يَطْعَمُ (yaṭʿamu) is its present form: “he eats” or “he tastes.”

طَعْم (ṭaʿm) is taste, the quality received when a substance meets the mouth and tongue. طُعْم (ṭuʿm) may refer to food, a food-share, or something given for nourishment. طَعَام (ṭaʿām) names what is eaten: food, edible matter, or a food-share.

طَاعِم (ṭāʿim) is the active participle, meaning one who eats. مَطْعُوم (maṭʿūm) means something eaten or prepared for eating; this form does not occur in the Qur'an. أَطْعَمَ (aṭʿama), Form IV, means to feed, to make eating possible, or to deliver food to another. يُطْعِمُ (yuṭʿimu) means “he feeds.” إِطْعَام (iṭʿām) is the verbal noun: feeding, food-giving. أُطْعِمَ (uṭʿima) is the passive: “he was fed.” اِسْتَطْعَمَ (istaṭʿama) means to ask for food; this form does not occur in the Qur'an.

The most important Qur'anic forms of this root are forms of طَعِمَ (ṭaʿima), forms of أَطْعَمَ (aṭʿama), طَعَام (ṭaʿām), إِطْعَام (iṭʿām), and طَاعِم (ṭāʿim).

Linguistic Note

The core of ط ع م (ṭ ʿ m) is receiving a substance through the mouth as food or taste. This field may include several stages: contact with the mouth and tasting, swallowing and eating, the body benefiting from food, and delivering edible matter to another.

Not every use of the root expresses all four stages. Sometimes only tasting is in view, sometimes full eating, and sometimes providing food to someone else. In the story of Ṭālūt, لَمْ يَطْعَمْهُ (lam yaṭʿamhu) is said about water, so طعم (ṭaʿm) is not limited to solid food. By contrast, in فَإِذَا طَعِمْتُمْ (fa-idhā ṭaʿimtum), the action is eating food.

طَعَام (ṭaʿām) is not the act of eating, but the thing upon which eating occurs. إِطْعَام (iṭʿām) is not merely placing food into another person’s mouth; it is delivering food to them in a way that makes eating actually possible.

Material Image of the Root

The clearest image is a substance placed in the mouth. The tongue receives its quality, then the substance is swallowed and the body benefits from it.

In this image, طعم (ṭaʿm) begins with a brief contact, but it can extend to full nourishment. For this reason, the same root may be translated sometimes as “tasting” and sometimes as “eating.”

In إطعام (iṭʿām), the image moves from the eater to the giver: food leaves one person’s possession or store and becomes accessible to another. Thus feeding is a material and real relation. As long as food has not actually become accessible to another, the full meaning of إطعام (iṭʿām) has not been fulfilled.

Historical Note and Ancient Arabic Usage

In classical Arabic, طعم (ṭaʿm) was used both for tasting and for eating. It could be used for food and drink, although in many structures طعم (ṭaʿm) and شرب (shurb) were distinguished.

الطَّعْم (al-ṭaʿm) meant taste: the quality the tongue receives from a substance. طعام (ṭaʿām) is originally a general word for food. In some commercial or livelihood contexts it could refer specifically to grain or wheat, but that restricted usage must not be imposed on all Qur'anic occurrences.

إطعام (iṭʿām) also had a broader range than placing a prepared meal before someone. Providing edible matter, a food-share, or real access to food can all fall within this field. In a final version, if reliable and datable evidence from pre-Islamic poetry for particular uses of طعم (ṭaʿm), طعام (ṭaʿām), and إطعام (iṭʿām) is found, it may be added.

Qur'anic Usage of the Root

1. Tasting and drinking water

فَمَنْ شَرِبَ مِنْهُ فَلَيْسَ مِنِّي وَمَنْ لَمْ يَطْعَمْهُ فَإِنَّهُ مِنِّي إِلَّا مَنِ اغْتَرَفَ غُرْفَةً بِيَدِهِ

So whoever drinks from it is not of me; and whoever does not taste it is of me, except one who scoops a handful with his hand.

Al-Baqarah 2:249

In لَمْ يَطْعَمْهُ (lam yaṭʿamhu), the object is water. This use shows that the root is not restricted to solid food. “Does not taste it” is more natural than “does not eat it,” but the point is not merely surface contact of the tongue with water. The verse concerns using and drinking from the water as a test.

2. Eating food

فَإِذَا طَعِمْتُمْ فَانْتَشِرُوا وَلَا مُسْتَأْنِسِينَ لِحَدِيثٍ

So when you have eaten, disperse, and do not remain seeking conversation.

Al-Ahzab 33:53

Here طعم (ṭaʿm) refers to eating food. The object is omitted because the context makes it clear. Translating it as “when you have tasted” would not carry the full action of eating. The suitable rendering is: “when you have eaten food.”

3. What has been eaten or drunk

لَيْسَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ جُنَاحٌ فِيمَا طَعِمُوا

There is no blame upon those who have believed and done righteous deeds in what they consumed.

Al-Ma'idah 5:93

Given the context, فِيمَا طَعِمُوا (fīmā ṭaʿimū) can include both eating and drinking. A more explanatory translation is “in what they ate or drank.” This usage shows the breadth of طعم (ṭaʿm) compared with أكل (akl).

4. General food

فَلَنْ نَصْبِرَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامٍ وَاحِدٍ

We will not endure one food.

Al-Baqarah 2:61

طعام (ṭaʿām) here is not the name of one specific substance; it refers to a monotonous food or a repeated dietary pattern. Thus طعام (ṭaʿām) may indicate both an edible substance and a pattern of food.

5. The food of the People of the Book

الْيَوْمَ أُحِلَّ لَكُمُ الطَّيِّبَاتُ وَطَعَامُ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ حِلٌّ لَكُمْ وَطَعَامُكُمْ حِلٌّ لَهُمْ

Today the good things have been made lawful for you; the food of those who were given the Book is lawful for you, and your food is lawful for them.

Al-Ma'idah 5:5

In the language of the verse itself, طعام (ṭaʿām) is a general word for food. It does not by itself mean “slaughtered animal” or “slaughtered meat.” If meat is one instance of food, that instance is known from custom or another indicator, not from the root ط ع م (ṭ ʿ m) itself.

The reciprocal structure is important: their food for you, and your food for them. The structure speaks about the possibility of food-sharing between two communities.

6. All food

كُلُّ الطَّعَامِ كَانَ حِلًّا لِبَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ إِلَّا مَا حَرَّمَ إِسْرَائِيلُ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ

All food was lawful for the Children of Israel, except what Israel made unlawful for himself.

Al-Imran 3:93

كُلُّ الطَّعَامِ (kullu al-ṭaʿām) expresses the generality of food. This structure shows that طعام (ṭaʿām), linguistically, is not restricted to meat, grain, or any single substance.

7. The command to feed the needy

فَكُلُوا مِنْهَا وَأَطْعِمُوا الْبَائِسَ الْفَقِيرَ

So eat from them, and feed the distressed poor.

Al-Hajj 22:28

In this verse, eating and feeding come together: eat yourselves, and feed the needy. Feeding here is the real transfer of food-benefit to the person in need.

8. Feeding despite love for the food

وَيُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِ مِسْكِينًا وَيَتِيمًا وَأَسِيرًا

And they feed the food, despite love for it, to the needy, the orphan, and the captive.

Al-Insan 76:8

The expression يُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ (yuṭʿimūna al-ṭaʿām) is natural Arabic: they deliver food to another so that the other may eat. In English one may say either “they feed the needy, the orphan, and the captive,” or “they deliver food to the needy, the orphan, and the captive.”

9. Feeding without expectation of return

إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ اللَّهِ لَا نُرِيدُ مِنْكُمْ جَزَاءً وَلَا شُكُورًا

We feed you only for the face of The God; we desire from you neither repayment nor thanks.

Al-Insan 76:9

In نُطْعِمُكُمْ (nuṭʿimukum), the recipient is directly attached to the verb. The food itself is omitted because the previous verse has already mentioned it. This shows that أطعم (aṭʿama) can take the recipient directly: “we feed you.”

10. Feeding as expiation

فَكَفَّارَتُهُ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ

Its expiation is feeding ten needy people from the middle and standard of what you feed your families.

Al-Ma'idah 5:89

In this verse, إطعام (iṭʿām) is a defined responsibility, and its measure is compared with the food of one’s family.

أوسط (awsaṭ) should not be reduced to “the cheapest acceptable food.” The root و س ط (w s ṭ) points to the middle and central position, and in some uses the center can be valued because of balance, stability, or prominence. But the word itself does not prove that the meaning is always “the best and most expensive.”

The practical standard in the verse is clear: مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ (mā tuṭʿimūna ahlīkum), what you feed your families. Therefore the expiation must not come from humiliating, negligible food outside the family’s real level of living. At the same time, the verse does not necessarily demand the most exceptional and luxurious meal the family ever eats. In this context, أوسط (awsaṭ) is best understood as the normal, balanced, and meaningful center of the family’s food standard.

11. Fidya: the food of a needy person

وَعَلَى الَّذِينَ يُطِيقُونَهُ فِدْيَةٌ طَعَامُ مِسْكِينٍ

And upon those who can bear it with difficulty is a fidya: the food of one needy person.

Al-Baqarah 2:184

طَعَامُ مِسْكِينٍ (ṭaʿāmu miskīn) expresses the food-share related to one needy person. The genitive relation can indicate specification or attachment: food for a needy person, the food-share of a needy person. This structure connects the food to the need and position of the poor person and moves it beyond an undefined act of giving.

12. Feeding on a day of hunger

أَوْ إِطْعَامٌ فِي يَوْمٍ ذِي مَسْغَبَةٍ

Or feeding on a day possessed of hunger.

Al-Balad 90:14

Here the value of feeding is connected to a condition of shortage and widespread hunger. Feeding on such a day means transferring a vital substance to someone whose access to food has been disrupted.

13. The food of the needy person

وَلَا يَحُضُّ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ الْمِسْكِينِ

And he does not urge toward the food of the needy.

Al-Haqqah 69:34

وَلَا يَحُضُّ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ الْمِسْكِينِ

And he does not encourage toward the food of the needy.

Al-Ma'un 107:3

وَلَا تَحَاضُّونَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ الْمِسْكِينِ

And you do not urge one another toward the food of the needy.

Al-Fajr 89:18

In these verses the Qur'an does not say إطعام المسكين (iṭʿām al-miskīn), feeding the needy person. It says طعام المسكين (ṭaʿām al-miskīn), the food of the needy person. This difference is meaningful. The center of the expression is not only the voluntary act of the giver; the food itself is placed in relation to the needy person.

The Arabic genitive can express ownership, specification, entitlement, relation, or function. Therefore, from the grammar alone, one cannot prove with certainty that every such food is already legally owned by the poor person in a fully realized sense. But in these contexts, the neutral translation “giving food to the poor” weakens part of the force of the expression. The Qur'an speaks of food that has a direct relation to the needy person and must reach them.

Thus طعام المسكين (ṭaʿām al-miskīn) at minimum points to a strong social specification and entitlement: food that is recognized as the share and need of the poor person, not merely a gift the wealthy may choose to give if they feel like it. This meaning aligns with other Qur'anic structures that recognize a share and right for the needy in the wealth of those who possess. However, the exact legal form of ownership, when it is created, and how it is implemented cannot be extracted from the genitive relation alone.

So one can firmly say that the food of the needy in these verses is not random charity; securing it is a social responsibility; and failing to urge society toward it is a sign of moral and social collapse. But the phrase “usurping the already specified legal property of the needy” requires additional legal evidence beyond the genitive phrase itself.

14. He feeds and is not fed

قُلْ أَغَيْرَ اللَّهِ أَتَّخِذُ وَلِيًّا فَاطِرِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَهُوَ يُطْعِمُ وَلَا يُطْعَمُ

Say: Shall I take as protective authority other than The God, Originator of the heavens and the earth, while He feeds and is not fed?

Al-An'am 6:14

The verbal contrast is precise: يُطْعِمُ (yuṭʿimu) is active; He is the agent of feeding. لَا يُطْعَمُ (lā yuṭʿamu) is negated passive; He is not placed in the position of one to whom food is delivered.

This structure is not only an abstract statement about needlessness. Its material image is two different positions in the network of nourishment. The being that receives food is in a position of objecthood and biological dependence. The God is presented as the agent of feeding, while any placement of Him in the position of receiving food is negated. The present verb may indicate ongoing agency, but one should not build a complete theory of natural mechanisms from the tense alone. The verse directly says that He feeds and is not food-receiving.

The closer translation is: “He feeds and is not fed.” This is more precise than “He does not need food,” because it preserves the contrast between active and passive. The explanation of needlessness may come afterward.

15. Food and drink

وَالَّذِي هُوَ يُطْعِمُنِي وَيَسْقِينِ

And the One who feeds me and gives me drink.

Ash-Shu'ara 26:79

Here feeding and giving drink are distinguished: إطعام (iṭʿām) is delivering food, while سقي (saqy) is delivering drink. This distinction shows that even though طعم (ṭaʿm) may sometimes be used with water, in a contrastive structure it may be restricted to food.

16. Feeding against hunger

الَّذِي أَطْعَمَهُمْ مِنْ جُوعٍ وَآمَنَهُمْ مِنْ خَوْفٍ

The One who fed them from hunger and secured them from fear.

Quraysh 106:4

The preposition مِنْ (min) marks the condition or danger from which feeding removes them: He fed them out of hunger. Here feeding is not merely a single bite or meal; it is the provision of food against the collective danger of hunger.

17. Human responsibility in feeding

وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمْ أَنْفِقُوا مِمَّا رَزَقَكُمُ اللَّهُ قَالَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَنُطْعِمُ مَنْ لَوْ يَشَاءُ اللَّهُ أَطْعَمَهُ

And when it is said to them, Spend from what The God has provided you, those who disbelieved say to those who believed: Shall we feed one whom, if The God willed, He would have fed?

Ya-Sin 36:47

In this verse, attributing provision to The God is used as a pretext for denying human responsibility. Two forms of the root stand opposite one another: أَنُطْعِمُ (anuṭʿimu), “shall we feed?” and أَطْعَمَهُ (aṭʿamahu), “He would have fed him.” The structure shows that The God’s ultimate agency in provision does not erase the responsibility of human intermediaries.

Food Means Food, Not Slaughtered Meat

طعام (ṭaʿām) is a general word for edible matter or food-share. The Qur'an uses it for the food of the Children of Israel, the food of the People of the Book, family food, the food of the needy, expiation and fidya, guest food, and the food of Paradise and Hell.

Therefore طعام (ṭaʿām) is not linguistically synonymous with slaughtered meat. Meat can be one instance of food, but the word itself includes all edible substances.

Distinguishing Similar Uses

Tasting appears in Al-Baqarah 2:249, where طعم (ṭaʿm) is used with water. Eating appears in Al-Ahzab 33:53, where the verb refers to eating food. Consumption of food and drink appears in Al-Ma'idah 5:93, where طعم (ṭaʿm) can include both.

طعام (ṭaʿām) names the substance or share that is eaten. إطعام (iṭʿām) is providing and delivering food to another. Satisfying hunger is a possible result of feeding, not its necessary meaning in every occurrence. Feeding may occur even when the amount does not produce complete fullness.

In طعام المسكين (ṭaʿām al-miskīn), food is specified in relation to the needy person. This relation goes beyond a completely unstructured act of giving, though the details of legal ownership are not determined by the genitive construction alone.

Syntactic and Structural Notes

وَمَنْ لَمْ يَطْعَمْهُ (wa-man lam yaṭʿamhu): يطعم (yaṭʿam) is jussive after لم (lam), and the pronoun ه (hu) returns to the water: whoever does not taste it.

فَإِذَا طَعِمْتُمْ (fa-idhā ṭaʿimtum): the object is omitted because food is known from the context: when you have eaten food.

يُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ (yuṭʿimūna al-ṭaʿām): the verb is Form IV and الطعام (al-ṭaʿām) is its object: they deliver food to another.

نُطْعِمُكُمْ (nuṭʿimukum): the pronoun كم (kum) is the recipient: we feed you.

إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَاكِينَ (iṭʿāmu ʿasharati masākīn): إطعام (iṭʿām) is a verbal noun performing the work of the verb: feeding ten needy people.

طَعَامُ مِسْكِينٍ (ṭaʿāmu miskīn): the genitive can indicate specification or entitlement: the food of one needy person, the food-share of the needy person. But not every genitive automatically proves complete and already realized ownership.

مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ (min awsaṭi mā tuṭʿimūna ahlīkum): من (min) marks a part or type from the family’s standard food. أوسط (awsaṭ) points to the central and normal standard of family nourishment: neither humiliating food nor necessarily the most luxurious exceptional meal.

يُطْعِمُ وَلَا يُطْعَمُ (yuṭʿimu wa-lā yuṭʿam): the first verb is active and the second passive: He feeds and is not fed. The syntax places the agent of feeding opposite the being who would be nourished.

قُلْ لَا أَجِدُ فِي مَا أُوحِيَ إِلَيَّ مُحَرَّمًا عَلَىٰ طَاعِمٍ يَطْعَمُهُ

Say: I do not find, in what has been revealed to me, anything forbidden to an eater who eats it.

Al-An'am 6:145

طاعم (ṭāʿim) is the active participle, and يطعمه (yaṭʿamuhu) is the act of eating that very substance. The ruling concerns a person consuming something as food.

Difference from Nearby Roots

ط ع م (ṭ ʿ m) differs from أ ك ل (ʾ k l). أكل (akl) points to eating and consuming matter and can be extended metaphorically to consuming wealth. طعم (ṭaʿm) is more directly connected to the mouth, taste, and tasting; therefore it can be used for a small amount or even for water.

It differs from ذوق (dhawq), which points to experiencing the quality of something and in the Qur'an extends to experiencing punishment, mercy, and consequences. طعم (ṭaʿm) remains more strongly tied to food and nourishment.

It differs from شرب (shurb), which is specifically drinking. طعم (ṭaʿm) may sometimes be used about water, but in structures such as يطعمني ويسقين (yuṭʿimunī wa-yasqīn), food and drink are distinguished.

It differs from رزق (rizq). Provision is any benefit that reaches a being; food is one instance of provision, but not every provision is food.

It differs from قوت (qūt), which is sustenance that maintains the body and meets basic need. طعام (ṭaʿām) is broader and can include any edible substance.

It differs from nourishment in the biological sense, غذو (gh-dh-w), which emphasizes growth and bodily maintenance through food. طعم (ṭaʿm) focuses on eating and the edible substance, while nourishment emphasizes the biological result.

It differs from شبع (shabaʿ), being full. إطعام (iṭʿām) is the act of feeding; fullness is a result that may follow. It differs from جوع (jūʿ), hunger, which is the body’s need for food. طعام (ṭaʿām) is the material answer to that need, and إطعام (iṭʿām) is the act of delivering it.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

طَعِمَ (ṭaʿima): ate, tasted, consumed food or drink. يَطْعَمُ (yaṭʿamu): eats, tastes. طَعْم (ṭaʿm): taste, tasted quality, tasting. طَعَام (ṭaʿām): food, meal, edible matter, food-share where context requires. طَاعِم (ṭāʿim): eater, one who consumes something as food. أَطْعَمَ (aṭʿama): fed, delivered food, made eating possible. إِطْعَام (iṭʿām): feeding, delivering food, making food accessible.

For وَمَنْ لَمْ يَطْعَمْهُ (wa-man lam yaṭʿamhu): “and whoever does not taste it.” For فَإِذَا طَعِمْتُمْ (fa-idhā ṭaʿimtum): “so when you have eaten food.” For طَعَامُ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ (ṭaʿāmu alladhīna ūtū al-kitāb): “the food of those who were given the Book.”

For the expiation verse: “Its expiation is feeding ten needy people from the normal and worthy food you feed your families.” For طَعَامِ الْمِسْكِينِ (ṭaʿām al-miskīn), the closer rendering is “the food of the needy,” and the explanatory rendering is “the food-share of the needy.” For وَهُوَ يُطْعِمُ وَلَا يُطْعَمُ (wa-huwa yuṭʿimu wa-lā yuṭʿam), the main suggestion is: “He feeds and is not fed,” because this preserves the active-passive contrast. For الَّذِي أَطْعَمَهُمْ مِنْ جُوعٍ (alladhī aṭʿamahum min jūʿ): “the One who fed them from hunger.”

Linguistic Summary

The root ط ع م (ṭ ʿ m) points to receiving something through the mouth, tasting, eating, and delivering food to another. Its material image is a substance whose taste is received and which enters the mouth to nourish the body.

In the Qur'an, طعم (ṭaʿm) can express tasting water, eating food, or consuming food and drink. طعام (ṭaʿām) names the edible substance or food-share, and إطعام (iṭʿām) is the act of making it available to another.

In يُطْعِمُ وَلَا يُطْعَمُ (yuṭʿimu wa-lā yuṭʿam), agency in feeding stands opposite passivity and biological dependence. In طعام المسكين (ṭaʿām al-miskīn), food is related to the needy person through specification and entitlement, moving beyond accidental charity, though the details of legal ownership are not determined by the genitive alone. In مِنْ أَوْسَطِ مَا تُطْعِمُونَ أَهْلِيكُمْ (min awsaṭi mā tuṭʿimūna ahlīkum), the measure is the family’s real and worthy food standard: neither the lowest food nor necessarily the most luxurious meal.

The distinctive feature of this root is the connection between tasting, eating, food, feeding, and real human access to edible nourishment.
