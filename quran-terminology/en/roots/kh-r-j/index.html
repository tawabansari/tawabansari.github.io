Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root KH R J

Coming out, bringing out, drawing something from concealment, and moving it from a closed field into visibility, effect, and responsibility

Important Forms from This Root

خَرَجَ / يَخْرُجُ (kharaja / yakhruju)

came out, went out, left a bounded place.

أَخْرَجَ / يُخْرِجُ (akhraja / yukhriju)

brought out, drew out, made something emerge from concealment.

خُرُوج (khurūj)

coming out; in some verses, the coming out on the Day of Resurrection.

مَخْرَج (makhraj)

a way out, an exit, an opening from constraint.

خَرْج / خَرَاج (kharj / kharāj)

payment, revenue, output, or what is drawn out from wealth, land, or capacity.

Method Note

The root KH R J may look simple at first: to come out. But in the Qur’an, this coming out is often more than ordinary movement from one place to another. A boundary is usually involved. Something is inside, concealed, confined, covered, or held within a limited field; then it crosses that boundary and enters the field of appearance.

This gives the root a wide Qur’anic range. It appears for birth, for vegetation and provision coming out of the earth, for being brought out of darknesses into light, for expulsion and displacement, and for human beings coming out in the Resurrection. In all of these uses, something previously hidden, closed, or unseen is brought into visibility, effect, responsibility, or judgment.

Central Meaning

The central meaning of KH R J is passing from inside to outside, crossing the boundary of concealment. This passage is not always spatial. A seed comes out from the soil and appears as a plant. A human being is brought out from the mother’s womb and enters the field of perception and experience. A believer is brought out from darknesses toward light. A people may also be driven out from home or land, where khurūj becomes expulsion and loss of place.

In each case, the thing no longer remains in its previous state. A boundary is broken or opened, and what was hidden or contained enters the field of action, seeing, judgment, or responsibility. For this reason, KH R J is important for understanding how capacities, truths, and consequences become manifest in the Qur’an.

Material Image

The material image can be seen in a seed hidden inside the soil. As long as it remains there, the eye sees nothing. But when water and the right conditions arrive, the soil opens and the shoot comes out. This is not mere relocation. The hidden capacity of the seed has reached appearance.

Another image is a person coming out from a house, enclosure, or city. Before leaving, he belonged to a defined field. After coming out, he enters another field and new relations appear. The Qur’an uses this concrete image in deeper layers: coming out from the womb into the world, from graves into Resurrection, from darknesses into light, and from constraint into opening.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, KH R J is used for coming out of a place, but also for the yield or output of property and land. From this field, خَرَاج (kharāj) can mean revenue, produce, or payment: something that comes out from land, wealth, or human capacity.

This connection shows that khurūj is not only leaving a place. Land produces what comes out of it. Wealth may have an output. And when a human being enters the field of trial, what is inside him becomes outward behavior and effect.

Qur’anic Usage

Bringing the Human Being Out from the Mother’s Womb

The Qur’an says: وَاللَّهُ أَخْرَجَكُم مِّن بُطُونِ أُمَّهَاتِكُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ شَيْئًا (wallāhu akhrajakum min buṭūni ummahātikum lā taʿlamūna shayʾā) 16:78. The verse reminds the human being of being brought out from the concealment of the womb into the field of the world.

The human being begins in a hidden and enclosed space, knowing nothing. Then The God gives hearing, sight, and hearts, so the human being enters the field of knowledge, gratitude, and responsibility. This khurūj is not only biological birth. It is the beginning of reception and accountability.

Bringing Provision Out from the Earth

Surat al-Baqarah says: وَأَنزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَأَخْرَجَ بِهِ مِنَ الثَّمَرَاتِ رِزْقًا لَّكُمْ (wa-anzala mina al-samāʾi māʾan fa-akhraja bihi mina al-thamarāti rizqan lakum) 2:22. Water descends from the sky, and through it fruits are brought out as provision.

The earth has capacity within it, but that capacity is not made visible without water and divine governance. The coming out of fruits is the transformation of a hidden possibility in the soil into usable provision for the human being.

Bringing the Living Out from the Dead

The Qur’an says: يُخْرِجُ الْحَيَّ مِنَ الْمَيِّتِ وَيُخْرِجُ الْمَيِّتَ مِنَ الْحَيِّ (yukhriju al-ḥayya mina al-mayyiti wayukhriju al-mayyita mina al-ḥayy) 30:19. Here khurūj operates at a level deeper than ordinary growth or birth.

The living comes out from what appears dead or without life, and the dead also comes from the living. The verse shows that the relation between life and death is in The God’s hand; the human being sees only the surface of this exchange.

Coming Out from Darknesses into Light

In the context of guidance, the Qur’an says: اللَّهُ وَلِيُّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا يُخْرِجُهُم مِّنَ الظُّلُمَاتِ إِلَى النُّورِ (allāhu waliyyu alladhīna āmanū yukhrijuhum mina al-ẓulumāti ilā al-nūr) 2:257. This is not spatial movement. It is movement in understanding and guidance.

In darknesses, the human being is caught in layers of obscurity, confusion, and inability to distinguish. The God brings believers out of that state toward light, meaning toward a field where truth can be seen, understood, and followed.

Ikhrāj as Expulsion and Loss of Place

KH R J is not always positive in the Qur’an. Sometimes it appears as ikhrāj, expulsion under pressure and injustice: وَإِخْرَاجُ أَهْلِهِ مِنْهُ أَكْبَرُ عِندَ اللَّهِ (wa-ikhrāju ahlihi minhu akbaru ʿinda allāh) 2:217.

Here expelling the people of a sanctuary or land is not merely physical relocation. It is the removal of security, home, the right to live, and social place. When khurūj is forced through injustice, it becomes driving human beings out of the ground of their life.

Makhraj: A Way Out from Constraint

Surat al-Ṭalāq says: وَمَن يَتَّقِ اللَّهَ يَجْعَل لَّهُ مَخْرَجًا (waman yattaqi allāha yajʿal lahu makhrajā) 65:2. Makhraj is a way out from a closed situation.

A human being may be trapped in pressure, crisis, or a narrow field of visible causes and see no path. The verse says that taqwā gives a person a makhraj: a way to come out of what appears closed. This way out may not match immediate human calculation, but in the verse’s logic, taqwā places the person within the field of opening.

The Day of Coming Out

The Qur’an names the Resurrection as the Day of Coming Out: ذَٰلِكَ يَوْمُ الْخُرُوجِ (dhālika yawmu al-khurūj) 50:42. Here khurūj means coming out from the graves into the field of accounting.

The human being moves from hidden, buried, and silent condition into open judgment. This use connects KH R J to the final disclosure of what had been hidden.

Khurūj as Social Appearance

In the story of Qarun, the Qur’an says: فَخَرَجَ عَلَىٰ قَوْمِهِ فِي زِينَتِهِ (fakharaja ʿalā qawmihi fī zīnatih) 28:79. Qarun came out before his people in his adornment.

This is not merely walking among people. It is the public display of wealth, position, and social power. He brought what he possessed out from the private field into the public gaze. Here khurūj means appearing on the social stage and displaying status.

Kharj and Kharāj: Financial Output

In Surat al-Kahf, the people say to Dhū al-Qarnayn: فَهَلْ نَجْعَلُ لَكَ خَرْجًا (fahal najʿalu laka kharjā) 18:94. In Surat al-Muʾminūn: أَمْ تَسْأَلُهُمْ خَرْجًا فَخَرَاجُ رَبِّكَ خَيْرٌ (am tasʾaluhum kharjan fakharāju rabbika khayr) 23:72.

Here kharj or kharāj is what comes out from wealth or capacity as payment, cost, or revenue. This meaning is still connected to the root: something is drawn out from a source.

Difference from Nearby Words

Ẓahara focuses more on appearing and becoming visible, without necessarily showing movement from inside to outside. KH R J usually carries the crossing of a boundary. Badā means to become apparent to sight or understanding, but KH R J emphasizes movement out from a hidden, closed, or confined place.

Baʿatha means to raise, send, or bring forth into motion. In resurrection, baʿth and khurūj come close: baʿth focuses on being raised, while khurūj focuses on coming out into the visible field. Nashara means to spread out or disperse. Khurūj may precede nashr: something first comes out of concealment, then spreads across the earth, society, or the field of sight.

Aẓhara means to make apparent, but it may lack the image of crossing a boundary. أَخْرَجَ (akhraja) keeps the sense of drawing something out from an inner, hidden, or closed condition.

Translation Method

KH R J should not always be forced into one English equivalent. “Bring out” is often correct, but not always enough. If the verse speaks about earth and provision, “bring out” or sometimes “cause to grow forth” may be better. If it speaks about darknesses and light, the translation should show movement from obscurity and confusion into clear discernment.

If the verse speaks of social injustice, “expel,” “drive out,” or “displace” may carry the force better. If the verse speaks of Resurrection, “coming out into the open field of accounting” may make the meaning clearer.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

أَخْرَجَكُم مِّن بُطُونِ أُمَّهَاتِكُمْ (akhrajakum min buṭūni ummahātikum): He brought you out from the wombs of your mothers.

فَأَخْرَجَ بِهِ مِنَ الثَّمَرَاتِ رِزْقًا لَّكُمْ (fa-akhraja bihi mina al-thamarāti rizqan lakum): through it, He brought out fruits as provision for you.

يُخْرِجُ الْحَيَّ مِنَ الْمَيِّتِ (yukhriju al-ḥayya mina al-mayyit): He brings the living out from the dead.

يُخْرِجُهُم مِّنَ الظُّلُمَاتِ إِلَى النُّورِ (yukhrijuhum mina al-ẓulumāti ilā al-nūr): He brings them out from darknesses into light; He moves them from states of obscurity and confusion into the light of discernment.

وَإِخْرَاجُ أَهْلِهِ مِنْهُ (wa-ikhrāju ahlihi minhu): expelling its people from it; displacing its people from their place.

يَجْعَل لَّهُ مَخْرَجًا (yajʿal lahu makhrajā): He makes for him a way out; He opens for him a path out of constraint.

ذَٰلِكَ يَوْمُ الْخُرُوجِ (dhālika yawmu al-khurūj): that is the Day of Coming Out into the open field of accounting.

فَخَرَجَ عَلَىٰ قَوْمِهِ فِي زِينَتِهِ (fakharaja ʿalā qawmihi fī zīnatih): he came out before his people in his adornment; he appeared before his people in display.

فَهَلْ نَجْعَلُ لَكَ خَرْجًا (fahal najʿalu laka kharjā): shall we assign you a payment? shall we give out from our wealth as a cost for you?

Summary

The root KH R J in the Qur’an is the language of passing from concealment into appearance. Something is hidden in the womb, soil, darkness, grave, home, city, wealth, or inner self; then it comes out from that boundary and enters the field of effect, sight, judgment, or responsibility.

This root reminds the human being that concealments are not permanent. The seed comes out from the earth. Provision is brought out from the soil. The human being comes out from the mother’s womb. What is inside a person appears in social action. Finally, all human beings reach the Day of Coming Out, when hidden things are brought into the open. In Qur’anic logic, khurūj is not merely outward movement; it is the arrival of a thing at a stage where it can no longer remain in its former concealment.
