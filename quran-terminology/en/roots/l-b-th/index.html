Remaining, lingering, and the passage of time: from brief stays to long periods, and the difference between elapsed time and human perception

The Arabic root L B TH denotes remaining, lingering, or spending a period in a place or condition. Labitha can describe someone staying somewhere, delaying movement or departure, or continuing in a particular state. The period may be moments, days, years, or a very long span.

Crucially, the verb itself does not specify how long the stay lasts. Duration comes from accompanying words and context. The Quran describes both a short delay before an action and prolonged stays in prison, a cave, among a people, or in an otherworldly condition.

Labth does not itself entail conscious waiting, willingness to remain, or inability to leave. Someone may stay voluntarily, be imprisoned, sleep, or perceive elapsed time differently after death and revival. The verb can express duration in all these situations without defining the nature of the condition.

The root occurs 31 times in 27 Quranic verses, in three main constructions: the basic verb لَبِثَ in various perfect and imperfect forms, the form V verb تَلَبَّثُوا, and the active participle لَابِثِينَ.

These uses illuminate remaining through time, the difference between actual duration and human perception, delay before action, residence under different conditions, and remaining in passages about the hereafter.

1. Historical meaning in early Arabic

Classical Arabic لَبِثَ يَلْبَثُ means to remain or linger. لَبِثَ بِالْمَكَانِ describes staying or residing in a place, as does لَبِثَ فِي الْمَكَانِ.

Recorded verbal nouns include لَبْث, لُبْث, لَبَث, لَبَاث, and لُبَاث. Their different vocalizations do not imply separate roots.

Early dictionaries explain the family through مُكْث and إِقَامَة: remaining, stopping, or continuing to be present in a place or condition.

Labth is not restricted to residence. In constructions such as مَا لَبِثَ أَنْ فَعَلَ it concerns delay before an action.

For example, مَا لَبِثَ أَنْ جَاءَ means that he did not delay coming, or that it was not long before he came.

The basic meaning of lingering remains, but its negation indicates no significant interval between the events.

Witnesses from Arabic poetry and usage

The Umayyad poet Jarīr says:

وَقَدْ أَكُونُ عَلَى الْحَاجَاتِ ذَا لَبَثٍ
وَأَحْوَذِيًّا إِذَا انْضَمَّ الذَّعَالِيبُ

wa-qad akūnu ʿalā al-ḥājāti dhā labathin / wa-aḥwadhiyyan idhā inḍamma al-dhaʿālīb

“I may be deliberate in attending to affairs, yet swift and resourceful when the base fellows gather.”

Labath here refers to taking time rather than rushing into action.

The poet contrasts two ways of behaving: some situations call for deliberation, others for speed and agility.

Here labth concerns delay or a pause before acting, rather than simply staying in a place.

The witness also shows that lingering is neither a virtue nor a fault by itself. Its value depends on the situation and outcome.

Jarīr was an Umayyad poet, however, so his poetry does not establish pre-Islamic usage.

Arabic proverbial tradition also preserves:

لَبِثْ قَلِيلًا يَلْحَقِ الْهَيْجَا حَمَلُ

labith qalīlan yalḥaqi al-hayjā ḥamal

“Wait a little until Ḥamal reaches the battlefield.”

Some versions replace يَلْحَق with يُدْرِك, and the imperative also varies in transmission. The saying promises the imminent arrival of a force and sometimes serves as a threat.

Here labth is waiting for an event that has not yet occurred.

Accounts differ about the original speaker and the saying's date. It is associated with both pre-Islamic figures and people of the early Islamic period, so this wording cannot be dated securely to pre-Islamic times without further investigation.

Despite that limitation, the proverb illustrates how remaining and waiting could be applications of labth in early Arabic.

Lingering in movement and slowness of conduct

لَبِث and لَابِث are also recorded for someone who lingers or acts slowly.

An early Arabic fragment says:

غَرَّكِ مِنِّي شَعَثِي وَلَبَثِي
وَلِمَمٌ حَوْلَكِ مِثْلُ الْحُرْبُثِ

gharraki minnī shaʿathī wa-labathī / wa-limamun ḥawlaki mithlu al-ḥurbuth

“My dishevelment and slowness have misled you, together with the locks around you, like the ḥurbuth plant.”

The lexical explanation describes an elderly speaker unable to keep pace because of weakness. The dark locks of the young people around the addressee are compared to ḥurbuth; the line is not necessarily describing the addressee's own hair.

Labath here concerns slow, lingering movement. Physical weakness may prevent someone from keeping up with others.

Slowness is not a fixed meaning of every form of this root; it comes from the person's situation and the passage.

The fragment's historical attribution is also insufficiently clear to establish a pre-Islamic date.

Limits of the historical evidence

These poems and proverbs attest a semantic field of remaining, stopping, delaying, and waiting in early Arabic. The Quran itself clearly records the family in the Arabic of the first Islamic century.

Independently dated pre-Islamic poetic evidence is not equally available for every branch of this root.

A definite story about its earliest origin is therefore unwarranted: for example, that it originally meant sitting in one place and later expanded to waiting or slow movement.

What Arabic usage and the Quran establish is remaining or lingering through a span of time.

The root's core can be defined as remaining or continuing to be present in a place or condition, or lingering before changing that condition or doing something else.

The root alone specifies neither duration, cause, nor manner.

2. Morphology: remaining, lingering, and delaying

Labitha: remaining in a condition

لَبِثَ is a basic triliteral verb, appearing in several Quranic forms.

لَبِثَ is third-person singular, لَبِثُوا third-person plural, لَبِثْتُ first-person singular, and لَبِثْنَا first-person plural.

لَبِثْتَ and لَبِثْتُمْ address a singular and plural subject respectively.

The imperfect forms include يَلْبَثُونَ and يَلْبَثُوا.

They differ in person, number, tense, and syntactic construction, rather than representing separate lexical meanings.

For instance, لَبِثْتُ means “I remained” or “I lingered,” while لَبِثُوا means “they remained.”

Other sentence elements identify the situation and duration.

Talabbathū: delaying an action

تَلَبَّثُوا occurs once in the Quran, in Al-Aḥzāb 33:14.

It belongs to form V, structurally distinct from basic لَبِثَ.

In that verse, talabbuth concerns delay or a pause before acting.

Form V can modify how an action is expressed, but its pattern alone does not make every talabbuth deliberate, prolonged, or psychologically hesitant.

إِلَّا يَسِيرًا specifies the short delay in this verse.

Lābithīn: those who remain

لَابِث is the active participle of لَبِثَ, describing someone who lingers or remains somewhere.

Al-Nabaʾ 78:23 contains its plural لَابِثِينَ.

Morphologically, the word describes those in a state of remaining.

The participle itself does not specify duration; the accompanying expression must be examined.

In this verse, أَحْقَابًا expresses the periods of remaining.

Recognized readings also transmit لَبِثِينَ here. Without the active participle's alif, it differs structurally from لَابِثِينَ, but in this context likewise describes those who remain.

The reading difference does not create two groups with separate destinies; morphology does not justify that conclusion.

Further forms and their Quranic status

Arabic also records أَلْبَثَ, لَبَّثَ, تَلَبَّثَ, and اسْتَلْبَثَ.

أَلْبَثَهُ and لَبَّثَهُ can mean causing another person to remain or delay.

اسْتَلْبَثَ sometimes concerns finding a wait long or something slow to arrive.

These forms illustrate the family's morphological range, but should not all be presented as Quranic derivatives.

Of these forms, only تَلَبَّثُوا occurs in the Quran.

3. Remaining in human life: residence, imprisonment, and brief delay

One clear use describes the duration of someone's presence in a place or among a people.

Muhammad's life among his people

Yūnus 10:16 says:

فَقَدْ لَبِثْتُ فِيكُمْ عُمُرًا مِنْ قَبْلِهِ أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ

fa-qad labithtu fīkum ʿumuran min qablihi a-fa-lā taʿqilūn

Yūnus 10:16

“I have already lived among you for a lifetime before it. Will you not reason?”

لَبِثْتُ فِيكُمْ concerns Muhammad's life and presence among his audience.

عُمُرًا describes the length of that presence.

He appeals to his earlier life among the people, a life known to them.

Labth here describes living among a community, rather than a momentary physical presence.

His social familiarity and life history emerge from the whole verse. The root itself means neither good reputation, truthfulness, nor proof of his divine mission.

Moses in Midian

Ṭā Hā 20:40 says:

فَلَبِثْتَ سِنِينَ فِي أَهْلِ مَدْيَنَ ثُمَّ جِئْتَ عَلَىٰ قَدَرٍ يَا مُوسَىٰ

fa-labithta sinīna fī ahli madyana thumma jiʾta ʿalā qadarin yā mūsā

Ṭā Hā 20:40

“Then you remained for years among the people of Midian, and then came at an appointed time, O Moses.”

لَبِثْتَ describes Moses' stay among the Midianites.

سِنِينَ indicates that it continued for several years.

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:27 and Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:29 also discuss this period in relation to his residence and subsequent return.

In Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:18, Pharaoh says to Moses:

وَلَبِثْتَ فِينَا مِنْ عُمُرِكَ سِنِينَ

wa-labithta fīnā min ʿumrika sinīn

Al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:18

“And you remained among us for years of your life.”

Pharaoh speaks of the period Moses lived in his surroundings.

The two uses share a feature: labth can describe years spent within a community.

The verb does not define the person's relationship with that community. Moses' life in Pharaoh's environment and his residence in Midian differed in circumstances and purpose, although both are expressed with labth.

Joseph in prison

Yūsuf 12:42 says:

فَلَبِثَ فِي السِّجْنِ بِضْعَ سِنِينَ

fa-labitha fī al-sijni biḍʿa sinīn

Yūsuf 12:42

“So he remained in prison for several years.”

لَبِثَ accompanies فِي السِّجْنِ.

بِضْعَ سِنِينَ gives a duration of several years without specifying one exact number.

This shows that labth need not describe voluntary remaining.

Joseph remained imprisoned, but the verb does not mean that he chose or welcomed his condition.

Labth should therefore not be restricted to voluntary residence.

Joseph's patience and endurance emerge from his character and conduct in the narrative, rather than from labth meaning patience.

Noah and the long period among his people

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:14 says:

فَلَبِثَ فِيهِمْ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ إِلَّا خَمْسِينَ عَامًا

fa-labitha fīhim alfa sanatin illā khamsīna ʿāman

Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:14

“And he remained among them a thousand years less fifty years.”

The duration among his people is explicit: nine hundred and fifty years.

This matters because labth can describe a very long period.

Length is supplied by أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ إِلَّا خَمْسِينَ عَامًا, not by the root itself.

The same root elsewhere describes a very short interval. A prolonged stay is therefore not part of its fixed meaning.

Abraham: no delay in hospitality

Hūd 11:69 says:

قَالُوا سَلَامًا قَالَ سَلَامٌ فَمَا لَبِثَ أَنْ جَاءَ بِعِجْلٍ حَنِيذٍ

qālū salāman qāla salāmun fa-mā labitha an jāʾa bi-ʿijlin ḥanīdh

Hūd 11:69

“They said: Peace. He said: Peace. It was not long before he brought a roasted calf.”

This use is particularly instructive syntactically.

مَا negates لَبِثَ.

فَمَا لَبِثَ أَنْ جَاءَ means that he did not delay bringing the food, or soon brought it.

Here labth concerns the interval before an action.

It should not be restricted to staying in a place: the emphasis is prompt action without significant delay.

Al-Aḥzāb 33:14 presents a related use:

ثُمَّ سُئِلُوا الْفِتْنَةَ لَآتَوْهَا وَمَا تَلَبَّثُوا بِهَا إِلَّا يَسِيرًا

thumma suʾilū al-fitnata la-ātawhā wa-mā talabbathū bihā illā yasīran

Al-Aḥzāb 33:14

“Then, had they been asked to engage in turmoil, they would have complied and would have delayed only a little.”

The context describes a group unprepared to stand firm under pressure and danger.

In this reading of the passage, تَلَبَّثُوا concerns delay before acting, with إِلَّا يَسِيرًا specifying its brevity. The reference of بِهَا is also interpreted in relation to remaining in the city; that question is distinct from the root's basic meaning.

The conduct differs morally: Abraham hastens to offer hospitality, while the other group readily gives way to turmoil.

Brief delay or swift action is neither good nor bad in itself. Moral significance comes from the action and context.

Remaining briefly after Muhammad's departure

Al-Isrāʾ 17:76 says:

وَإِذًا لَا يَلْبَثُونَ خِلَافَكَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

wa-idhan lā yalbathūna khilāfaka illā qalīlan

Al-Isrāʾ 17:76

“And then they would remain after you only a little.”

The context concerns attempts to expel Muhammad from his land.

لَا يَلْبَثُونَ negates remaining, while إِلَّا قَلِيلًا excepts a short duration.

Brevity comes from the combination, not the independent meaning of yalbathūn.

The verb can thus describe a group's continued presence after a particular event.

4. Remaining in the cave: actual duration and human perception

In Al-Kahf, the root appears repeatedly in the story of young people who sought refuge in a cave.

These passages offer a clear Quranic example of remaining in relation to awareness of elapsed time.

Reckoning the duration

Al-Kahf 18:12 says:

ثُمَّ بَعَثْنَاهُمْ لِنَعْلَمَ أَيُّ الْحِزْبَيْنِ أَحْصَىٰ لِمَا لَبِثُوا أَمَدًا

thumma baʿathnāhum li-naʿlama ayyu al-ḥizbayni aḥṣā li-mā labithū amadan

Al-Kahf 18:12

“Then We raised them, so that We might know which of the two groups had more accurately reckoned the length of their stay.”

لَبِثُوا describes their remaining; أَمَدًا concerns its duration or temporal extent.

Remaining is one thing; knowing its precise duration is another.

Someone may have stayed somewhere without being able to determine the length correctly.

Asking about duration

Al-Kahf 18:19 says:

قَالَ قَائِلٌ مِنْهُمْ كَمْ لَبِثْتُمْ قَالُوا لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍ

qāla qāʾilun minhum kam labithtum qālū labithnā yawman aw baʿḍa yawm

Al-Kahf 18:19

“One of them said: How long have you remained? They said: We have remained a day or part of a day.”

The same verse continues:

قَالُوا رَبُّكُمْ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا لَبِثْتُمْ

qālū rabbukum aʿlamu bi-mā labithtum

Al-Kahf 18:19

“They said: Your Lord knows best how long you have remained.”

The young people are uncertain, or differ, about their stay's duration.

They first suggest a day or part of one, then attribute more exact knowledge to their Lord.

Human experience or perception of time need not match actual duration.

The verb labth does not establish that time literally stopped or that physical laws changed for them.

It expresses remaining over a period. How that period is experienced or perceived is a separate question.

Three hundred years and nine more

Al-Kahf 18:25 says:

وَلَبِثُوا فِي كَهْفِهِمْ ثَلَاثَ مِائَةٍ سِنِينَ وَازْدَادُوا تِسْعًا

wa-labithū fī kahfihim thalātha miʾatin sinīna wa-zdādū tisʿan

Al-Kahf 18:25

“And they remained in their cave three hundred years, and added nine.”

The duration is stated numerically.

ثَلَاثَ مِائَةٍ سِنِينَ وَازْدَادُوا تِسْعًا gives three hundred years and nine more.

Al-Kahf 18:26 then says:

قُلِ اللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا لَبِثُوا

quli allāhu aʿlamu bi-mā labithū

Al-Kahf 18:26

“Say: The God knows best how long they remained.”

This recalls divine knowledge of their stay.

Reading the verses together requires preserving both the stated number and the emphasis on divine knowledge.

Disagreements about the number of youths or their stay's duration should not alter the clear meaning of labth.

Throughout, labth means remaining; the information speakers possess about its duration changes.

Another account with the same distinction

Al-Baqarah 2:259 uses the root three times:

قَالَ كَمْ لَبِثْتَ قَالَ لَبِثْتُ يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍ قَالَ بَلْ لَبِثْتَ مِائَةَ عَامٍ

qāla kam labithta qāla labithtu yawman aw baʿḍa yawmin qāla bal labithta miʾata ʿām

Al-Baqarah 2:259

“He said: How long did you remain? He said: I remained a day or part of a day. He said: Rather, you remained a hundred years.”

The account concerns someone whom The God caused to be dead for a hundred years and then raised.

He imagines that a much shorter period has passed.

Labth describes an interval involving his death and subsequent revival.

The root cannot therefore be restricted to conscious presence, wakefulness, or continuous bodily activity.

Using labth does not necessarily mean that someone was aware of passing time throughout the period.

This matters alongside the cave story: in both, elapsed time differs from the participants' perception.

The resemblance does not establish that the youths' condition was identical to that of the person in Al-Baqarah 2:259. Each account has its own wording and information.

The shared point is that the duration of a condition and someone's awareness of that duration are different matters.

5. Labth in resurrection passages: the past appearing brief

Passages about resurrection repeatedly use L B TH to describe human perceptions of past life or a previous stay.

They relate to the earlier accounts while asking a broader question: how do people view their past after encountering another stage of existence?

A brief portion of the day

Yūnus 10:45 says:

وَيَوْمَ يَحْشُرُهُمْ كَأَنْ لَمْ يَلْبَثُوا إِلَّا سَاعَةً مِنَ النَّهَارِ يَتَعَارَفُونَ بَيْنَهُمْ

wa-yawma yaḥshuruhum ka-an lam yalbathū illā sāʿatan mina al-nahāri yataʿārafūna baynahum

Yūnus 10:45

“On the day He gathers them, as though they had remained only a brief portion of the day, they will recognize one another.”

كَأَنْ marks a comparison or perceived appearance.

Their previous life need not have lasted only that short interval; it appears so brief when they are gathered. Sāʿah here need not denote the modern, precisely measured sixty-minute hour.

Al-Aḥqāf 46:35 likewise says:

كَأَنَّهُمْ يَوْمَ يَرَوْنَ مَا يُوعَدُونَ لَمْ يَلْبَثُوا إِلَّا سَاعَةً مِنْ نَهَارٍ

ka-annahum yawma yarawna mā yūʿadūna lam yalbathū illā sāʿatan min nahār

Al-Aḥqāf 46:35

“On the day they see what they are promised, it will be as though they had remained only a brief portion of a day.”

كَأَنَّهُمْ is again an important indication.

The past appears brief when they encounter the divine promise.

Brevity comes from the comparison and إِلَّا سَاعَةً مِنْ نَهَارٍ, not labth itself.

A day or part of a day

Al-Muʾminūn 23:112 asks:

كَمْ لَبِثْتُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ عَدَدَ سِنِينَ

kam labithtum fī al-arḍi ʿadada sinīn

Al-Muʾminūn 23:112

“How many years did you remain on earth?”

In the next verse they answer:

لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍ فَاسْأَلِ الْعَادِّينَ

labithnā yawman aw baʿḍa yawmin fa-sʾali al-ʿāddīn

Al-Muʾminūn 23:113

“We remained a day or part of a day; ask those who count.”

Al-Muʾminūn 23:113

Al-Muʾminūn 23:114 says:

إِنْ لَبِثْتُمْ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا لَوْ أَنَّكُمْ كُنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ

in labithtum illā qalīlan law annakum kuntum taʿlamūn

Al-Muʾminūn 23:114

“You remained only a little, if only you had known.”

These verses present earthly duration as brief against the horizon of the hereafter.

قَلِيلًا does not mean that the actual earthly years never existed.

The initial question mentions a count of years; the responses emphasize that period's brevity from the standpoint of the next stage.

Ten days or one day

Ṭā Hā 20:103 says:

يَتَخَافَتُونَ بَيْنَهُمْ إِنْ لَبِثْتُمْ إِلَّا عَشْرًا

yatakhāfatūna baynahum in labithtum illā ʿashran

Ṭā Hā 20:103

“They whisper among themselves: You remained only ten.”

The following verse says:

إِذْ يَقُولُ أَمْثَلُهُمْ طَرِيقَةً إِنْ لَبِثْتُمْ إِلَّا يَوْمًا

idh yaqūlu amthaluhum ṭarīqatan in labithtum illā yawman

Ṭā Hā 20:104

“When the most judicious of them in conduct says: You remained only a day.”

Ṭā Hā 20:104

Two different estimates of the past are expressed.

One gives “ten,” commonly understood as ten days or nights in this context, and the other one day. The noun counted by عَشْرًا is not explicitly stated in the first verse.

These numbers occur in people's speech; they should not both be presented as precise, simultaneous measurements of the actual duration.

Labth does not cause their disagreement; their estimates differ.

Brief remaining and human judgment

Al-Rūm 30:55 says:

وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ السَّاعَةُ يُقْسِمُ الْمُجْرِمُونَ مَا لَبِثُوا غَيْرَ سَاعَةٍ

wa-yawma taqūmu al-sāʿatu yuqsimu al-mujrimūna mā labithū ghayra sāʿah

Al-Rūm 30:55

“On the day the Hour arises, the guilty will swear that they remained only a short while.”

This is explicitly the oath of the guilty.

In Al-Rūm 30:56, those given knowledge and faith answer:

لَقَدْ لَبِثْتُمْ فِي كِتَابِ اللَّهِ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ الْبَعْثِ

la-qad labithtum fī kitābi allāhi ilā yawmi al-baʿth

Al-Rūm 30:56

“You certainly remained, according to the record of The God, until the day of resurrection.”

This contrast matters.

The guilty people's claim of brevity should not be accepted as a definitive measurement without considering the response.

The verses distinguish human knowledge of the past from what is known and recorded by The God.

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:46 also says:

كَأَنَّهُمْ يَوْمَ يَرَوْنَهَا لَمْ يَلْبَثُوا إِلَّا عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَاهَا

ka-annahum yawma yarawnahā lam yalbathū illā ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā

Al-Nāziʿāt 79:46

“On the day they see it, it will be as though they had remained only an evening or its morning.”

This expression too begins with كَأَنَّهُمْ.

It portrays the past appearing brief, without changing labth's meaning. Al-Isrāʾ 17:52 similarly says وَتَظُنُّونَ إِنْ لَبِثْتُمْ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا: “and you will think you remained only a little.” Here تَظُنُّونَ explicitly identifies the people's estimate.

What these passages share

Resurrection passages use the same verb to express remaining and elapsed duration.

Yet the durations described vary: a short while, a day, ten understood as days, or a little time.

This does not establish a special kind of physical time in the hereafter whose laws are definitively known to us.

Nor should the difference in human perceptions of time be overlooked.

People's reckoning and perception of duration can change with their condition and horizon of knowledge.

The revived person's story and that of the cave youths show the same distinction.

An important connection among these uses is the difference between remaining for a period and being able to know or estimate it accurately.

6. Remaining in exceptional conditions and the hereafter

Further uses show remaining in unusual, involuntary, or otherworldly conditions.

The jinn and knowledge of Solomon's death

Sabaʾ 34:14 says:

فَلَمَّا خَرَّ تَبَيَّنَتِ الْجِنُّ أَنْ لَوْ كَانُوا يَعْلَمُونَ الْغَيْبَ مَا لَبِثُوا فِي الْعَذَابِ الْمُهِينِ

fa-lammā kharra tabayyanati al-jinnu an law kānū yaʿlamūna al-ghayba mā labithū fī al-ʿadhābi al-muhīn

Sabaʾ 34:14

“When he fell, it became clear to the jinn that, had they known the unseen, they would not have remained in the humiliating punishment.”

لَبِثُوا accompanies فِي الْعَذَابِ الْمُهِينِ.

Remaining here concerns the continuation of a painful, humiliating condition.

Labth is not limited to a geographical location.

A person or being may remain in a condition described as punishment, suffering, or deprivation.

The pain comes from الْعَذَابِ الْمُهِينِ, rather than from labth itself.

Jonah and remaining in the fish's belly

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:143 and Al-Ṣāffāt 37:144 say:

فَلَوْلَا أَنَّهُ كَانَ مِنَ الْمُسَبِّحِينَ لَلَبِثَ فِي بَطْنِهِ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ

fa-lawlā annahu kāna mina al-musabbiḥīna la-labitha fī baṭnihi ilā yawmi yubʿathūn

Al-Ṣāffāt 37:143; Al-Ṣāffāt 37:144

“Had he not been among those who glorify, he would have remained in its belly until the day they are raised.”

The lām in لَلَبِثَ marks the answer to لَوْلَا; it should not be described merely as an independent emphasis detached from the conditional construction.

The sentence is conditional: فَلَوْلَا ... لَلَبِثَ.

Remaining until resurrection is the consequence presented had the stated condition been absent.

It cannot therefore be translated as a report of what actually happened to Jonah.

Labth again means remaining; إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ gives the duration.

This does not make every occurrence of labth mean remaining until resurrection or forever.

Lābithīna fīhā aḥqābā: periods of remaining in Hell

Al-Nabaʾ 78:21, Al-Nabaʾ 78:22, and Al-Nabaʾ 78:23 say:

إِنَّ جَهَنَّمَ كَانَتْ مِرْصَادًا ۝ لِلطَّاغِينَ مَآبًا ۝ لَابِثِينَ فِيهَا أَحْقَابًا

inna jahannama kānat mirṣādan / li-l-ṭāghīna maʾāban / lābithīna fīhā aḥqāban

Al-Nabaʾ 78:21; Al-Nabaʾ 78:22; Al-Nabaʾ 78:23

“Hell has indeed been a place of ambush, a destination for transgressors, remaining in it for long ages.”

لَابِثِينَ describes those remaining in Hell.

أَحْقَابًا is the plural of ḥuqub and denotes long periods.

Length comes from aḥqābā, not lābithīn itself.

Two issues must remain distinct.

First, lābithīn alone does not mean remaining forever.

Second, aḥqābā alone does not establish that their stay must end after a specified number of periods.

A plural does not restrict the number to a stated figure, nor does this plural alone establish endlessness.

Other Quranic expressions concerning otherworldly duration and destiny include خَالِدِينَ, أَبَدًا, إِلَّا مَا شَاءَ, and statements of divine will.

Their relationship requires a separate study of enduring existence and the relevant verses.

The lexical meaning of labth should not be altered to prove a predetermined conclusion about punishment's duration.

In Al-Nabaʾ it means remaining; aḥqābā describes long periods. Assessing the ultimate outcome requires a broader network of verses.

7. Comparison with neighboring roots

L B TH and M K TH: remaining and pausing

Labth and makth are among Arabic's closest semantic families.

Both can describe remaining, stopping, or lingering.

Al-Naml 27:22 says:

فَمَكَثَ غَيْرَ بَعِيدٍ

fa-makatha ghayra baʿīd

Al-Naml 27:22

“Then he remained only a short while.”

The passage concerns the hoopoe's return to Solomon. The subject of مَكَثَ has been understood as either the hoopoe or Solomon; the short delay is clear in either interpretation.

Hūd 11:69 says of Abraham:

فَمَا لَبِثَ أَنْ جَاءَ بِعِجْلٍ حَنِيذٍ

fa-mā labitha an jāʾa bi-ʿijlin ḥanīdh

Hūd 11:69

“It was not long before he brought a roasted calf.”

Both passages describe brief remaining or delay.

They do not show that makth always means a short wait and labth always prolonged residence.

The Quran uses labth for very long periods and brief delays; makth likewise appears in different settings.

A tendency can be described cautiously: Quranic labth frequently accompanies elapsed duration and reckoning time; makth can denote stopping and remaining and sometimes connects with waiting or deliberate pausing.

This is not an absolute linguistic rule.

L B TH and TH W Y: remaining and residing

ثَوَىٰ concerns remaining, dwelling, or residence.

Moses' story provides a clear comparison.

Ṭā Hā 20:40 says:

فَلَبِثْتَ سِنِينَ فِي أَهْلِ مَدْيَنَ

fa-labithta sinīna fī ahli madyan

Ṭā Hā 20:40

“Then you remained for years among the people of Midian.”

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:45, however, says:

وَمَا كُنْتَ ثَاوِيًا فِي أَهْلِ مَدْيَنَ تَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِنَا

wa-mā kunta thāwiyan fī ahli madyana tatlū ʿalayhim āyātinā

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:45

“You were not residing among the people of Midian, reciting Our signs to them.”

Both concern residence among the Midianites, but their contexts differ.

Ṭā Hā recounts Moses' stay as part of his life.

Al-Qaṣaṣ addresses Muhammad, who was not present among the Midianites to learn those past events through residence there.

Both roots can concern residence. Thawā sometimes foregrounds dwelling at a place, while labth, especially in these passages, foregrounds time spent in a condition.

This is a tendency rather than an absolute boundary.

L B TH and Q W M: remaining and standing or establishing

Q W M has a wide Quranic range: rising, standing, establishing, maintaining, and making something firm.

Iqāmah can mean residence in Arabic, but that is only one use of Q W M.

Labth centers on remaining over time; iqāmah in many Quranic uses also concerns establishing and maintaining.

أَقَامَ الصَّلَاةَ cannot simply mean lingering in ṣalāh, just as لَبِثَ فِي السِّجْنِ cannot mean establishing the prison.

L B TH and KH L D: remaining and enduring

Quranic khulūd concerns enduring continuance and in some constructions accompanies أَبَدًا.

Labth also means remaining, but does not itself specify duration or endlessness.

In Al-Baqarah 2:259, a person remains a hundred years.

In Hūd 11:69, Abraham makes no significant delay.

Al-Nabaʾ 78:23 describes remaining for long periods.

These examples show the range of durations labth accommodates.

Khulūd has its own semantic field of enduring continuance; its forms too must be read in context.

Labth alone proves neither an end nor everlasting duration.

L B TH and ʾ M D: remaining and its duration

Al-Kahf 18:12 places لَبِثُوا and أَمَدًا in one expression.

Labithū describes remaining, while amad concerns its duration or temporal extent.

Their combination shows that the root labth does not itself contain a number or quantity of time.

Amad, yawm, sanah, ʿām, sāʿah, or another expression can specify duration separately.

Translation should distinguish the act of remaining from the stated amount of time.

8. Translation proposals for Forqan

The primary equivalent of لَبِثَ is “remained” or “stayed.” Where duration or delay is prominent, “lingered” or “delayed” may fit.

لَبِثَ: he remained, lingered, or continued in a condition for a time.

لَبِثْتُ: I remained or lingered.

لَبِثْتَ: you remained or lingered, singular.

لَبِثْتُمْ: you remained or lingered, plural.

لَبِثُوا: they remained or lingered.

لَبِثْنَا: we remained or lingered.

يَلْبَثُونَ: they remain or linger.

تَلَبَّثُوا: they lingered or delayed; Al-Aḥzāb 33:14 discusses delay in the situation described.

لَابِثِينَ: those remaining; “remaining in it” is natural in Al-Nabaʾ 78:23.

The selected equivalent must fit the sentence's construction.

In Yūsuf 12:42:

“So he remained in prison for several years.”

In Ṭā Hā 20:40:

“Then you remained for years among the people of Midian.”

In Hūd 11:69:

“It was not long before he brought a roasted calf.”

In Al-Baqarah 2:259:

“How long did you remain?”

In Al-Kahf 18:25:

“And they remained in their cave three hundred years, and added nine.”

In Al-Aḥzāb 33:14:

“And they would have delayed only a little.”

In Al-Nabaʾ 78:23:

“Remaining in it for long ages.”

In Al-Ṣāffāt 37:144:

“He would have remained in its belly until the day they are raised.”

The basic translation should not replace labth with sleeping, dying, being imprisoned, consciously waiting, being compelled, or remaining forever unless other words in the verse express that meaning.

Constructions such as كَأَنَّهُمْ لَمْ يَلْبَثُوا must retain the comparison. “As though they had not remained” differs from “they actually did not remain.”

9. Summary

L B TH concerns remaining, lingering, and spending time in a place or condition. Historical uses include residence, waiting, slow conduct, and delay before action.

None of these secondary characteristics is the fixed meaning of every occurrence.

The Quran uses this family for years in prison, life among a people, staying in a cave, a short delay before action, continuing in a painful condition, and remaining in the hereafter.

Its temporal range is broad. Hūd 11:69 negates significant delay; Al-Baqarah 2:259 gives a hundred years; Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:14 gives nine hundred and fifty; Al-Nabaʾ 78:23 describes long periods.

The root itself therefore specifies neither brevity nor length.

Al-Baqarah 2:259, Al-Kahf, and resurrection passages reveal another distinction: actual duration and human perception of that duration need not coincide.

A very long period may seem brief. People may not know how much time passed or may estimate it differently. This does not change labth's lexical meaning; it reflects the situation in which remaining occurred.

In otherworldly passages too, labth alone establishes neither finitude nor eternity. Duration and outcome must be considered through temporal qualifications, sentence structure, and other relevant verses.

The root L B TH means remaining or continuing to be present in a place or condition through time, and lingering before changing that condition or acting otherwise; the root alone does not determine brevity, length, choice, awareness, or the eventual outcome.

This definition accommodates prolonged residence and brief delay while distinguishing labth from sleep, death, conscious waiting, and everlasting duration, and recognizing its overlap with residence and pausing.

The root's significance extends beyond counting periods. People encounter passing time throughout life and beyond, but their understanding need not match what elapsed. Time, the duration of remaining, the person's condition, and awareness of that duration must be distinguished.
