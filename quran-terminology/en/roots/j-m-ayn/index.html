Foundational Meaning

The root J M ʿ means gathering scattered things, bringing parts together, collecting people, or placing multiple things into one set. Its focus is removing dispersion and bringing parts to one point, one time, one place, or one shared state.

Jamʿ is close to waṣl, but they are not the same. Waṣl means joining two things so distance and rupture do not remain between them. Jamʿ means gathering several things or people together. Things may be gathered, yet not truly joined. Jamʿ brings together; waṣl establishes connection among them.

In the Qur'an, J M ʿ has material, social, historical, ethical, and eschatological meanings. People are gathered, wealth is gathered, the Qur'an is gathered, peoples are gathered on the Day of Resurrection, and everything comes into a state of gathering before The God.

Qur'anic Uses

The root J M ʿ appears in the Qur'an in many forms: verbs such as جَمَعَ (jamaʿa), يَجْمَعُ (yajmaʿu), نَجْمَعُ (najmaʿu), and جَمَعْنَا (jamaʿnā); forms such as أَجْمِعُوا (ajmiʿū) for gathering decision and force; اجْتَمَعَ (ijtamaʿa) for coming together; and nouns such as جَمْع (jamʿ), جَمِيع (jamīʿ), أَجْمَعِين (ajmaʿīn), جَامِع (jāmiʿ), مَجْمُوع (majmūʿ), مَجْمَع (majmaʿ), and جُمُعَة (jumuʿah).

In Ali 'Imran 3:9: رَبَّنَا إِنَّكَ جَامِعُ النَّاسِ لِيَوْمٍ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ (rabbanā innaka jāmiʿu al-nāsi li-yawmin lā rayba fīh), our Lord, You are the gatherer of people for a day in which there is no doubt.

In Ali 'Imran 3:103: وَاعْتَصِمُوا بِحَبْلِ اللَّهِ جَمِيعًا وَلَا تَفَرَّقُوا (wa-iʿtaṣimū bi-ḥabli Allāhi jamīʿan wa-lā tafarraqū), hold fast to the rope of The God all together and do not divide.

In Al-Hashr 59:14: تَحْسَبُهُمْ جَمِيعًا وَقُلُوبُهُمْ شَتَّىٰ (taḥsabuhum jamīʿan wa-qulūbuhum shattā), you think them gathered as one, while their hearts are scattered.

In Al-Qiyamah 75:17: إِنَّ عَلَيْنَا جَمْعَهُ وَقُرْآنَهُ (inna ʿalaynā jamʿahu wa-qur'ānah), upon Us is its gathering and its recitation.

In At-Taghabun 64:9: يَوْمَ يَجْمَعُكُمْ لِيَوْمِ الْجَمْعِ (yawma yajmaʿukum li-yawmi al-jamʿ), the day He gathers you for the Day of Gathering.

In Al-Humazah 104:2: الَّذِي جَمَعَ مَالًا وَعَدَّدَهُ (alladhī jamaʿa mālan wa-ʿaddadah), the one who gathered wealth and counted it.

In Al-Ma'arij 70:18: وَجَمَعَ فَأَوْعَىٰ (wa-jamaʿa fa-awʿā), and gathered, then stored away.

In Yunus 10:71: فَأَجْمِعُوا أَمْرَكُمْ وَشُرَكَاءَكُمْ (fa-ajmiʿū amrakum wa-shurakā'akum), gather your affair and your partners.

In Taha 20:64: فَأَجْمِعُوا كَيْدَكُمْ (fa-ajmiʿū kaydakum), gather your plot.

In Al-Kahf 18:61: فَلَمَّا بَلَغَا مَجْمَعَ بَيْنِهِمَا (fa-lammā balaghā majmaʿa baynihimā), when they reached the meeting point between the two.

Jamʿ as Ending Dispersion

The core of J M ʿ is ending dispersion. Things are separate, scattered, or distant; then they are brought into one point, one state, one group, or one aim.

This gathering may be physical, such as people gathering in one place. It may be inward, such as hearts gathered around one matter. It may be mental and decisional, such as agreement upon a plan. It may be eschatological, such as all human beings gathered for account.

So jamʿ is not always only bodily assembly. Wherever dispersion is turned into a shared state, the semantic field of J M ʿ is present.

Jamʿ and All-Togetherness

One important use of this root is the word جَمِيع (jamīʿ). Jamīʿ means all, everyone, all together, without dispersion or exception. The Qur'an often uses جَمِيعًا (jamīʿan) to show that a ruling, event, or call includes all.

In Ali 'Imran 3:103, جَمِيعًا (jamīʿan) stands against division: hold fast to the rope of The God all together and do not divide. It is not merely each individual holding separately; the gathering itself forms around the rope of The God.

This verse shows that Qur'anic gathering, when centered on truth, stands against division. But if gathering is centered on falsehood, plotting, or injustice, it has no sacred value. Being gathered is not holy by itself; the axis of gathering matters.

Outward Gathering and Scattered Hearts

One of the most precise verses about the difference between outward gathering and true gathering is Al-Hashr 59:14: تَحْسَبُهُمْ جَمِيعًا وَقُلُوبُهُمْ شَتَّىٰ (taḥsabuhum jamīʿan wa-qulūbuhum shattā).

The Qur'an says you think them gathered and united, but their hearts are scattered. This verse shows that numeric and outward gathering does not mean true unity. A group may appear under one front, one slogan, one political structure, or one shared identity, while inwardly their hearts and directions are scattered.

The Qur'an is not deceived by the appearance of numbers. True gathering needs a shared axis, truthfulness, covenant, ethical direction, and connection of hearts. If hearts are شَتَّىٰ (shattā), outward gathering is only a shell of unity.

Jamʿ and Division

Jamʿ in the Qur'an often stands against division and scattering. But this contrast must be understood carefully. The Qur'an calls human beings to gather upon truth, not blindly upon tribe, party, power, or inherited custom.

In Ali 'Imran 3:103, the axis of gathering is حَبْلِ اللَّهِ (ḥabl Allāh), the rope of The God. Unity is Qur'anic when it is around the rope of The God. If people gather on fanaticism, injustice, shirk, or identity-worship, that is not Qur'anic gathering. It may look like unity outwardly, but in reality it is assembly upon falsehood.

So Qur'anic jamʿ means gathering around truth from The God, not merely being many in number or standing with a group.

Gathering in the Hereafter

One of the most important Qur'anic fields of J M ʿ is the Hereafter. The God is جَامِعُ النَّاسِ (jāmiʿu al-nās): the gatherer of people for a day in which there is no doubt. Resurrection is يَوْمِ الْجَمْعِ (yawm al-jamʿ), the day when worldly dispersions end and all stand before truth.

In the world, human beings are scattered across times, places, languages, classes, powers, and identities. In the Hereafter, this outward dispersion is removed. All are brought into one field of account.

This gathering is not only the gathering of bodies. It is the gathering of deeds, intentions, results, testimonies, and the truth of human life. On the Day of Gathering, no one hides behind the dispersions of the world.

Gathering and Account

The gathering of Resurrection is connected with account. When The God gathers human beings, He does not place them together aimlessly. Gathering is for truth to become manifest and for judgment.

For this reason, divine gathering is tied to justice. The one who was scattered, hidden, fleeing, or powerful in the world becomes present in a gathering where escape and concealment no longer have meaning. Everything is gathered so truth may become clear.

Gathering in the Hereafter is therefore the return of the human being to the complete field of responsibility.

Gathering Wealth

The Qur'an also speaks of gathering wealth. In Al-Humazah 104:2 it says: الَّذِي جَمَعَ مَالًا وَعَدَّدَهُ (alladhī jamaʿa mālan wa-ʿaddadah). In Al-Ma'arij 70:18 it says: وَجَمَعَ فَأَوْعَىٰ (wa-jamaʿa fa-awʿā). This use shows that gathering is not always positive.

In Sūrat Al-Humazah, gathering wealth is paired with counting it. This pairing matters. The problem is not mere ownership. The problem is a state in which a person gathers wealth, counts it again, attaches the heart to it, and draws from hoarding a feeling of permanence, superiority, or self-sufficiency.

This kind of gathering removes wealth from the flow of good and social responsibility. Wealth is no longer a means of service, giving, zakat, and repair. It becomes something a person stores, counts, and uses to imagine themself great.

Gathering wealth becomes dangerous when wealth is gathered not for building and repair, but for counting, hoarding, boasting, and feeling self-sufficient. Such gathering stands against zakat and the flow of good.

Gathering the Qur'an

In Al-Qiyamah 75:17, the Qur'an says: إِنَّ عَلَيْنَا جَمْعَهُ وَقُرْآنَهُ (inna ʿalaynā jamʿahu wa-qur'ānah). This verse is very important. The God places the gathering of the Qur'an upon Himself. This means its gathering, coherence, preservation, and being conveyed in a recitable and receivable form belong to divine guarantee.

In this verse, gathering comes before recitation: first jamʿ, then qur'ān. This order can show that receiving the Qur'an is not merely reading scattered pieces. The God Himself guarantees its gathering, preservation, and becoming recitable.

This verse shows that, in the Qur'an's own view, revelation is not scattered speech without preservation. The God guards the process of its gathering and recitation. This gathering is not only physical collection of words; it includes preserving relation, order, conveyance, and the capacity of revelation to be read.

From this angle, gathering the Qur'an approaches the root W Ṣ L. Waṣl of speech means conveying speech in connected form; gathering the Qur'an means gathering and ordering it for reception and recitation.

Gathering Command and Plot

In verses such as Yunus 10:71 and Taha 20:64, the root J M ʿ appears as أَجْمِعُوا أَمْرَكُمْ (ajmiʿū amrakum) and أَجْمِعُوا كَيْدَكُمْ (ajmiʿū kaydakum). Here gathering means bringing together force, thought, plan, will, and decision.

This use shows that jamʿ is not only gathering people. It can gather energies and decisions. A person or group may gather its affair, meaning that it removes dispersion in decision and action and concentrates all its force on one path.

But again, the axis matters. Sometimes gathering one's affair is for truth; sometimes it is for plotting. In Taha 20:64, the magicians are told to gather their plot. Gathering itself is not good or bad; its direction determines its value.

Jamʿ and Assembly

The form ijtamaʿa in the Qur'an points to coming together. Assembly is the state of individuals or forces standing together. But assembly is not the same as true unity. A group may gather in one place while hearts and aims remain scattered.

The Qur'an shows this difference clearly: تَحْسَبُهُمْ جَمِيعًا وَقُلُوبُهُمْ شَتَّىٰ (taḥsabuhum jamīʿan wa-qulūbuhum shattā). The outward appearance of gathered numbers can deceive, but if hearts are scattered, that gathering does not have real strength. Numeric gathering differs from heart-gathering and gathering upon truth.

Majmaʿ

The word مَجْمَع (majmaʿ) refers to a place of gathering or meeting point. In the story of Moses, مَجْمَعَ بَيْنِهِمَا (majmaʿa baynihimā) is the point where two seas meet. This use shows the spatial layer of the root: a place where two flows meet or come together.

Majmaʿ can be a place of convergence: a point where two paths, two flows, or two domains meet. This fits the root's core: bringing or arriving at a shared point.

Jumuʿah

The word جُمُعَة (jumuʿah) comes from this root and is tied to gathering. In Qur'anic culture, the day of Jumuʿah is known through the gathering of believers for the remembrance of The God and collective salat. In Sūrat Al-Jumuʿah, the call to salat on the day of Jumuʿah comes, and believers are called to hasten toward remembrance of The God.

This use shows that gathering in the Qur'an is not only bodies standing together. Jumuʿah must be gathering around remembrance of The God, not merely social assembly. If gathering is emptied of remembrance, awareness, and responsibility, only the shell of assembly remains.

Difference From Nearby Roots

J M ʿ and W Ṣ L are close, but not the same. Jamʿ means gathering several things together. Waṣl means establishing connection among them. People may gather, yet not be joined; they may be in one place while hearts, aims, and covenants are severed.

J M ʿ is also close to Ḥ SH R, but not the same. Jamʿ can be any kind of gathering: calm, natural, social, mental, or eschatological. Ḥashr carries more of the sense of summoning, driving out, bringing forth, and making people present in a field of account or assembly.

J M ʿ differs from A L F. Jamʿ means bringing together; ta'līf means creating affinity, harmony, and intimacy among hearts or parts. There may be gathering without affinity. Gathering bodies is easier than joining hearts in affinity.

J M ʿ also differs from ḥashd. Ḥashd is closer to gathering an army or group in a state of mass mobilization. Jamʿ is broader and may apply to wealth, the Qur'an, people, the Day of Resurrection, decision, or parts of a thing.

Good Gathering and Bad Gathering

One important point in the Qur'an is that gathering is not good or bad by itself. The value of gathering depends on its axis and destination.

Gathering around the rope of The God is a guiding gathering. Gathering people for the Day of Account is divine and just. Gathering the Qur'an is preserved and merciful. But gathering wealth for hoarding, gathering a plot for deception, or gathering forces for falsehood is dangerous.

The Qur'an does not make number and assembly alone into a measure of truth. A crowd may be large and still be on falsehood. A group may be small and still be on truth. The criterion is the direction of gathering.

Human gathering becomes Qur'anic when it leads to responsibility, remembrance, justice, loyalty, and repair. If human beings gather together while remaining irresponsible toward truth, the poor, the orphan, the oppressed, covenant, and The God, then the gathering is only a gathering of bodies.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For جَمَعَ (jamaʿa): “gathered,” “brought together,” or, in the context of wealth, “hoarded.”

For جَمِيعًا (jamīʿan): “all together,” “all of them,” or “together as a whole.”

For جَامِعُ النَّاسِ (jāmiʿu al-nās): “gatherer of the people.”

For يَوْمِ الْجَمْعِ (yawm al-jamʿ): “the Day of Gathering,” or “the day when all are gathered.”

For جَمْعَهُ وَقُرْآنَهُ (jamʿahu wa-qur'ānah): “its gathering and its recitation,” or, in explanation, “its gathering, ordering, and being made recitable.”

For جَمَعَ مَالًا وَعَدَّدَهُ (jamaʿa mālan wa-ʿaddadah): “gathered wealth and counted it,” with the tone of hoarding and attachment kept in view.

For أَجْمِعُوا أَمْرَكُمْ (ajmiʿū amrakum): “gather your affair,” or “concentrate your decision and force.”

For مَجْمَعَ بَيْنِهِمَا (majmaʿa baynihimā): “the meeting point between the two,” or “the point where the two flows gather.”

Summary

The root J M ʿ in the Qur'an points to gathering and ending dispersion. This gathering may concern human beings, wealth, decision, revelation, the Day of Resurrection, or parts of a thing.

J M ʿ means gathering; bringing all together; moving from dispersion into a set; placing people or parts in a shared field; collecting wealth or resources; gathering people for account; gathering the Qur'an and preserving the order of receiving it; and gathering around one affair, decision, or destination.

From the Qur'anic perspective, gathering has value when it is centered on truth. Gathering around The God, remembrance, justice, and repair is guiding. Gathering around wealth-worship, plotting, power, or falsehood can move the human being away from truth.

The root may be summarized this way: J M ʿ means gathering scattered things and placing them in one set, field, or shared destination. Jamʿ is not the same as waṣl: jamʿ brings together, while waṣl connects. If gathering is centered on truth, it stands against division and dispersion; if it is centered on falsehood, it is only an unguided mass.
