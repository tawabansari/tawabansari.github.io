1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ب ع د moves in the semantic field of distance, separation, afterwardness, being set apart, and moving out of nearness.

This distance may be spatial, temporal, sequential, moral and spiritual; or it may be expressed as rejection, destruction, and being far from mercy.

In many verses, بَعْدَ is simply an adverb of time or sequence and means "after." In other verses, ba'id describes the distance of something, such as a distant punishment, a far error, or a return that deniers consider far-fetched.

In expressions such as:

بُعْدًا لِلْقَوْمِ الظَّالِمِينَ

Far removal and destruction be upon the wrongdoing people.

Al-Muminun 23:41

the root goes beyond simple distance and approaches being far from mercy, the end of presence, and the destruction of a wrongdoing group.

Even so, this root should not be turned into a theory about loss of energy, voltage drop, or moving out of range of a transmitter. Those are modern images, not the lexical meaning of the Qur'an.

The shortest statement of this root is:

being far or becoming far in place, time, order, or spiritual relation; and in some contexts, complete rejection and destruction.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The semantic core of ب ع د is:

something standing at a distance from something else, whether in place, time, order, or spiritual relation.

This distance does not always mean complete severance. Something may simply be farther away while some connection remains.

In some contexts, distance becomes so severe that it approaches rejection, destruction, or the loss of return.

So one can distinguish between simple distance, temporal distance, distance along a route or between stations, distance from truth, and final distance with destruction.

The root ب ع د by itself does not speak about "energy transmission" or "signal loss." Such ideas may sometimes help as examples, but they do not replace the lexical core.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The simple image of the root is two points with increasing distance between them.

As distance increases, arrival becomes more difficult, the time of movement grows, direct contact becomes less possible, and what was near appears far.

This image can also move into time. When we say that something happened after something else, we establish an order or temporal distance between two events.

In the moral sense, a human being may also become distant from truth, mercy, or the right path. But this distance is not necessarily geographical. A person may be in the same place physically while being distant from truth in belief and action.

The best shared image is:

an increase of distance between two positions, two times, two states, or two directions.

6. Use of the Root in Early Arabic

In Arabic, بَعُدَ المكانُ means the place became far or was far.

بَعُدَ الرجلُ may point to a man becoming distant from a place or a relationship.

بَعْدَ ذلك means after that.

البعيد is something distant.

بُعْدًا له can be a curse of removal, rejection, or destruction.

In some early uses, بَعِدَ, with kasrah on the middle radical, comes close to perishing or destruction. This can be understood as complete removal from life, community, or mercy.

But one must not conclude from this branch that every distance equals death or complete severance.

7. History of Understanding, Misuse, or Semantic Restriction

One restriction is to understand ب ع د only as geographical distance.

In the Qur'an, distance can be spiritual:

ضَلَالًا بَعِيدًا

A far error.

An-Nisa 4:116

This expression is not about kilometers or physical location; it points to severe distance from the path of truth.

The opposite error is to turn every use of the root into a spiritual concept. Many verses only express temporal sequence with ba'd and need no moral interpretation.

Another error is building a theory of "system resistance." Distance in some natural systems can affect transfer, but the Qur'an does not define ب ع د in such technical language.

The correct method is to ask in each verse: is the distance spatial, temporal, sequential, spiritual, or a final curse and rejection?

8. Methodological Note: Difference Between BUD and BA'DA

BUD is a noun and indicates farness or distance.

BA'DA is an adverb and means "after."

For example:

مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا جَاءَتْهُمُ الْبَيِّنَاتُ

After the clear signs had come to them.

Ali Imran 3:105

Here the issue is not geometric or spiritual distance; it is temporal and event sequence.

Also, ba'd does not always show causal dependence. That a second event came after a first event does not necessarily mean the first directly caused the second.

So three things must be distinguished: temporal afterwardness, narrative sequence, and causal dependence.

The word ba'd itself can show the first two; causality must be understood from context.

9. A Short Look at Related Semitic Languages

Forms close to ب ع د in Semitic languages are connected with distance and separation.

This kinship shows that the meaning of distance belongs to old layers of this language family.

But claims such as saying that this root originally meant "leaving the city center" or "systemic isolation" cannot be established without direct evidence.

Arabic and Qur'anic usage is enough and primary for determining the meaning in this article.

10. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

A. Budan for 'Ad

أَلَا بُعْدًا لِعَادٍ قَوْمِ هُودٍ

Be aware: far removal and destruction be upon 'Ad, the people of Hud.

Hud 11:60

Budan in this verse is not merely spatial distance. It is a curse-like and judgmental expression and announces the end of the people of 'Ad.

Translations such as "far removed be the people of 'Ad," "destruction be upon the people of 'Ad," or "far from mercy be the people of 'Ad" can carry the context.

B. Budan for the wrongdoing people

فَبُعْدًا لِلْقَوْمِ الظَّالِمِينَ

So far removal and destruction be upon the wrongdoing people.

Al-Muminun 23:41

This expression comes after the destruction of a people. Distance here is connected with their removal from the scene of life and their being far from mercy.

It should not be translated as "system resistance becoming infinite."

C. Far error

وَمَنْ يُشْرِكْ بِاللَّهِ فَقَدْ ضَلَّ ضَلَالًا بَعِيدًا

And whoever associates partners with The God has indeed gone astray into a far error.

An-Nisa 4:116

Ba'idan shows severe distance from the path of guidance.

This distance is not spatial. A person may be physically near people of faith but distant in belief and direction of life.

D. A far schism

وَإِنَّ الَّذِينَ اخْتَلَفُوا فِي الْكِتَابِ لَفِي شِقَاقٍ بَعِيدٍ

And those who differed concerning the Book are in a far schism.

Al-Baqarah 2:176

Shiqaq ba'id is a deep opposition and a distance from agreement and truth.

Ba'id highlights the severity and spread of the separation.

E. A far place

وَهُمْ يُنَادَوْنَ مِنْ مَكَانٍ بَعِيدٍ

And they are called from a far place.

Fussilat 41:44

Here distance can form an image of inability to hear, respond, or receive clearly.

The structure of the verse speaks of a distance that makes communication difficult, but the word ba'id itself simply means "far."

F. A far return

ذَٰلِكَ رَجْعٌ بَعِيدٌ

That is a far return.

Qaf 50:3

The deniers of resurrection consider return after death far-fetched.

Here ba'id may mean far from their imagination, unlikely in their view, or difficult for them to accept.

The verse reports their view; it does not mean that resurrection is actually far from divine power.

G. A punishment seen as far or near

إِنَّهُمْ يَرَوْنَهُ بَعِيدًا
وَنَرَاهُ قَرِيبًا

They see it as far, and We see it as near.

Al-Ma'arij 70:6-7

This contrast shows that ba'id can describe human perception of the time of occurrence.

The deniers imagine the punishment as far away, but in the scale of divine knowledge and decree it is near.

H. After seeing what they loved

مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا أَرَاكُمْ مَا تُحِبُّونَ

After He showed you what you loved.

Ali Imran 3:152

Min ba'd ma shows the order of events.

The verse states that disobedience and dispute happened after seeing signs of victory.

This construction by itself does not mean "processing dependence of the second stage on the first"; it states order and context.

I. Asking for distance in journeys

فَقَالُوا رَبَّنَا بَاعِدْ بَيْنَ أَسْفَارِنَا وَظَلَمُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ

So they said: Our Lord, place greater distance between our journeys; and they wronged themselves.

Saba 34:19

The people of Saba had continuous, safe, and close journeys, but they failed to value the blessing and asked for greater distance.

Ba'id bayna asfarina means make our stations or journeys far from one another.

This verse is less about "breaking a supply chain" than about ingratitude toward ease of travel and safety of route.

J. Distance between the two easts

يَا لَيْتَ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَكَ بُعْدَ الْمَشْرِقَيْنِ

If only there were between me and you the distance of the two easts.

Az-Zukhruf 43:38

This expression conveys a wish for the greatest distance from the misleading companion.

Bu'd al-mashriqayn gives an image of a very great distance.

K. After faith or after clear signs

The construction min ba'd appears many times in the Qur'an: مِنْ بَعْدِ إِيمَانِكُمْ, مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا جَاءَتْهُمُ الْبَيِّنَاتُ, and مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا عَقَلُوهُ.

In such verses, the moral weight increases because deviation happened after knowledge, faith, or the arrival of a sign.

Ba'd itself only shows sequence; the intensity of responsibility comes from the content of the earlier event.

11. Important Derivatives in the Qur'an

بَعِيد

An adjective for distance. This distance may be spatial, temporal, possible in human perception, or spiritual.

بَعْدَ

An adverb of time or sequence, translated as "after."

بُعْدًا

In curse-like expressions, it means farness, rejection, and destruction.

بَاعِدْ

An imperative from form III, indicating the creation of distance between two things.

بَعِدَ

In some Arabic uses, it indicates destruction or complete removal, but the exact form and context must be examined in each verse.

12. Grammatical and Structural Notes

BA'DA as an adverb

Ba'd is usually in an idafa construction, and the word or clause after it identifies what the later event followed:

مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا جَاءَهُمُ الْعِلْمُ

مِنْ بَعْدِ

The particle min can highlight the beginning of the later stage: after that.

بَعِيد

It is an adjective and follows what it describes: ضَلَالًا بَعِيدًا, مَكَانٍ بَعِيدٍ, شِقَاقٍ بَعِيدٍ.

بُعْدًا لِـ

This is a curse-like construction:

بُعْدًا لِلْقَوْمِ الظَّالِمِينَ

The lam identifies the group upon whom distance and destruction are declared.

بَاعِدْ بَيْنَ

The verb with bayna identifies the two sides between which distance is placed.

13. B AYN D Compared with Q R B

Qurb is nearness and the smallness of distance.

Bu'd is farness and the largeness of distance.

These two can stand opposite one another in place, time, or spiritual relation.

In Al-Ma'arij 70:6-7:

يَرَوْنَهُ بَعِيدًا
وَنَرَاهُ قَرِيبًا

They see it as far, and We see it as near.

Al-Ma'arij 70:6-7

there is a direct contrast between bu'd and qurb.

But divine nearness and human distance from The God must not be understood as physical distance. The God is not confined in place. Nearness and distance in these cases relate to mercy, knowledge, response, obedience, and spiritual direction.

14. B AYN D Compared with N HAMZA Y

Na'a points to moving away, withdrawing, and turning aside.

Bu'd expresses the distance or farness itself.

One may say:

Na'a: the act of withdrawing and moving away.

Bu'd: the state in which distance has come about.

But this difference is not absolute in every context.

15. B AYN D Compared with H J R

Hajr indicates leaving, separating, and cutting companionship.

Bu'd emphasizes the distance that exists between two sides.

Hajr may lead to bu'd, but not every bu'd is the result of hajr. Distance may be natural, temporal, or spatial.

16. Specialized Note: Being Far from The God

The Qur'an does not present The God as a being confined in place, so that a human could become geometrically distant from His essence or near to Him.

So when we speak of spiritual nearness or distance, the intended meaning is not spatial distance.

A human being becomes distant through wrongdoing, shirk, denial, and turning away from the path of guidance. This distance takes place in the human being and his direction, not in divine knowledge and encompassing.

The God does not become absent from the human being, but a person may ignore His signs, turn away from mercy and guidance, and place himself through action on a path far from rightness.

So "being far from truth" does not mean that the message of guidance no longer reaches the person physically. He may hear the verses, yet refuse them through arrogance and partisanship.

Spiritual distance is more a problem of orientation, will, and acceptance than a problem of "signal range."

The expression dalalan ba'idan can also show that return from such a path has become more difficult, because the person has traveled a great distance in wrong choices and beliefs. But as long as the opportunity of life remains, one should not infer from this expression that return is absolutely impossible.

In the constructions of min ba'd, there is also an important moral point. Deviation that occurs after knowledge, sign, or covenant is not the same as deviation before awareness.

This intensity does not come from the word ba'd itself; it comes from the fact that the person chose another path after the truth became clear.

17. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

بُعْد

Distance. Farness.

"System resistance" is not a translation of the word.

بَعِيد

Far. Distant. Far from truth, in spiritual context. Far-fetched or difficult to imagine, in the right context.

"Outside the range of effect" can only be a limited explanation, not the base translation.

بَعْدَ

After.

"Dependent on completion of the previous stage" goes beyond the meaning of the word.

بُعْدًا لِـ

Far removed be. Far from mercy be. Destruction be upon them, according to context.

بَاعِدْ بَيْنَ

Place distance between them. Make them far from one another.

ضَلَالًا بَعِيدًا

A far error. An error far away from the path of truth.

مَكَانٍ بَعِيدٍ

A far place.

18. Conclusion

The root ب ع د indicates farness and distance.

This distance may be spatial, temporal, sequential, or spiritual.

Ba'id describes something that is far. This farness may relate to place, time, possibility in human perception, or distance from truth.

Ba'd means "after" and shows sequence between two events. This sequence does not necessarily prove causal dependence or that the second stage is conditioned upon the first.

Budan in expressions such as budan lil-qawm al-zalimin is a curse of distance, rejection, and destruction upon wrongdoers.

In ba'id bayna asfarina, the people of Saba showed ingratitude toward the ease and nearness of routes and asked for greater distance. The verse speaks of ingratitude and the loss of the blessing of safe travel.

Dalalan ba'idan expresses severe distance from the path of guidance, not physical exit from the range of a message.

Distance from The God is also not spatial. He is not confined in place. Through choice, wrongdoing, and turning away, the human being becomes distant from guidance and mercy, while divine knowledge and encompassing still include him.

Comparing ب ع د with ق ر ب shows that the two stand opposite each other in place, time, and meaning, but in theological usage they must not be measured by geometric distance.

Expressions about resistance, voltage, frequency, and energy transfer are modern examples; they do not form the lexical meaning of the root.

The shortest statement of the core of this root is:

being far or becoming far in place, time, order, or spiritual relation; and in some contexts, complete rejection and destruction.
