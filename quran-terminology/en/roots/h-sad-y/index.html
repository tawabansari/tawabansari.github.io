From pebbles and counting to comprehensive enumeration and recording

The Arabic root ح ص ي has two familiar semantic fields: حَصَى, meaning pebbles, and iḥṣāʾ, meaning accurately counting and keeping track of something so that its quantity or number is not overlooked. The two have acquired connections in Arabic, but without historical investigation neither can be declared the certain origin of the other.

Within this family, حَصَاة denotes a single pebble, and أَحْصَى الشَّيْءَ means to count, keep track of, and encompass the number of something. In some usages, iḥṣāʾ is also associated with the ability to perform or complete a task: someone unable to encompass something may be unable to count all of it, keep its quantity accurately in view, or manage it fully.

In the Qur’an, the root occurs only within the family of iḥṣāʾ. Sometimes people are instructed to count a period accurately; sometimes their inability to enumerate all of The God’s blessings is discussed; elsewhere the comprehensive enumeration of deeds and beings is attributed to The God.

The Qur’anic forms are تُحْصُوهَا, أَحْصَى, أَحْصَاهَا, أَحْصَاهُمْ, أَحْصَيْنَاهُ, أَحْصَاهُ, أَحْصُوا, and تُحْصُوهُ. The morphological analysis of أَحْصَى in Al-Kahf 18:12 is disputed and will be examined separately.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: eleven occurrences and the analysis of aḥṣā in Al-Kahf 18:12

1. Semantic cores and early Arabic usage

Ḥaṣā: pebbles and large numbers

حَصَى denotes pebbles, with حَصَاة as its singular. حَصَيَات is a plural of حَصَاة, and أَرْضٌ مَحْصَاةٌ describes land containing many pebbles.

Arabic also uses this word to express numerical abundance. نَحْنُ أَكْثَرُ مِنْهُمْ حَصًى means that we outnumber them, not necessarily that we possess more pebbles.

Al-Aʿshā’s poetry contains the following:

وَلَسْتَ بِالْأَكْثَرِ مِنْهُمْ حَصًى
وَإِنَّمَا الْعِزَّةُ لِلْكَاثِرِ

wa-lasta bi-l-akthari minhum ḥaṣan / wa-innamā al-ʿizzatu li-l-kāthiri

Meaning: You are not the most numerous among them; strength and ascendancy belong to the one with greater numbers.

In this verse, حَصًى stands for number or quantity. The poet does not mean actual pebbles, but a multitude of people. لَسْتَ addresses another person rather than making a first-person statement about the poet himself; the abundance concerns the number of supporters and allies.

The witness is recorded in Al-Aʿshā’s Dīwān and in lexical and literary sources, demonstrating the numerical use of ḥaṣā in early literary Arabic.

Khizānat al-Adab: Al-Aʿshā’s witness, its addressee, and the number of supporters

The connection between pebbles and large numbers is understandable: pebbles are small, numerous units, and the abundance of things can be compared to them. This comparison does not, however, prove that أَحْصَى necessarily developed from a historical method of counting with pebbles. That claim requires independent evidence.

Iḥṣāʾ: counting with accuracy and comprehensiveness

Al-ʿAyn explains iḥṣāʾ through knowledge encompassing the number of something and investigating it exhaustively. Exhaustiveness here means pursuing the count so that no relevant part is omitted.

Kitāb al-ʿAyn: iḥṣāʾ and exhaustive enumeration

Lisān al-ʿArab likewise connects iḥṣāʾ with counting and preservation. أَحْصَى الشَّيْءَ is recorded both for counting something and for encompassing it.

The important distinction is that a person may begin counting something without being able to finish or keep track of all its parts.

For example, someone can begin counting the grains of a field, yet their abundance or a lack of sufficient information may prevent a complete count. Counting has begun, but comprehensive enumeration has not been achieved.

In many usages, therefore, iḥṣāʾ emphasizes accurate, comprehensive counting, particularly when contrasted with incomplete counting, forgetting, or human inability.

Iḥṣāʾ and ability

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, Ibn Fāris identifies one principle of this family as الْعَدّ وَالْإِطَاقَة: counting and ability.

Ibn Fāris, Maqāyīs al-Lughah: prevention, counting and ability, and pebbles

In Arabic, لَا أُحْصِيهِ sometimes means “I cannot manage it” or “I cannot keep complete track of it.”

In Asās al-Balāghah, Al-Zamakhsharī also mentions هَذَا أَمْرٌ لَا أُحْصِيهِ, explaining it through inability to carry out and fully control a task.

Asās al-Balāghah: inability to manage or keep complete track

This usage shows that iḥṣāʾ is not restricted in every context to counting objects. “Being able” should not, however, replace counting and accurate tracking everywhere. In verses about recording deeds or enumerating people, encompassing their number and recording them accurately are more apparent.

Ḥaṣāh: judgment, firmness, and self-restraint

Besides a pebble, حَصَاة appears in Arabic expressions concerning sound judgment and firmness of opinion.

ذُو حَصَاةٍ describes a wise, considered person, while ثَابِتُ الْحَصَاةِ is used for someone firm in judgment.

A verse attributed to Kaʿb ibn Saʿd al-Ghanawī, and in some sources to Ṭarafah, reads:

وَأَنَّ لِسَانَ الْمَرْءِ مَا لَمْ تَكُنْ لَهُ
حَصَاةٌ عَلَى عَوْرَاتِهِ لَدَلِيلُ

wa-anna lisāna al-marʾi mā lam takun lahu / ḥaṣātun ʿalā ʿawrātihi la-dalīlu

Meaning: A person’s tongue, unless restrained by sound judgment, reveals his faults.

حَصَاة here does not mean an actual pebble, but the judgment and steadiness that restrain a person’s speech.

Lexicographers offer different explanations connecting this usage with a pebble’s solidity or with the ability to assess and control oneself. These explanations are useful, but do not establish the precise history of this figurative meaning.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ: Kaʿb ibn Saʿd al-Ghanawī’s witness on judgment

This branch does not occur in the Qur’an as ḥaṣāh. Judgment and firmness of opinion should therefore not be inserted directly into the translation of أَحْصَى in Qur’anic verses.

Lisān al-ʿArab: the differing attribution to Kaʿb and Ṭarafah

Other branches that must be distinguished from iḥṣāʾ

In examining families with ḥāʾ, ṣād, and a final weak consonant, Ibn Fāris also records حَصْو for preventing or withholding.

In that branch, حَصَوْتُهُ, with final wāw, means to restrain or deprive someone. Its proximity in a dictionary is not sufficient reason to identify it with the Qur’anic root ح ص ي.

حَصَاة is also recorded for a hard piece of musk and for a stone formed within the body. These usages concern a body’s shape and hardness, but do not determine the principal meaning of iḥṣāʾ in the Qur’an.

2. Qur’anic forms and the distinction between iḥṣāʾ and counting

All eleven Qur’anic occurrences concern counting, recording numbers, determining quantity, or comprehensively accounting for what is counted.

In all these cases, the underlying form is the form IV أَحْصَى, with the qualification that أَحْصَى in Al-Kahf 18:12 may also be analyzed as an elative expressing comparative or superlative degree.

The Qur’an places ع د د and ح ص ي alongside each other several times. These combinations show a shared field of counting, while their emphases are not always identical.

Ibrāhīm 14:34: counting blessings and inability to encompass them

وَآتَاكُمْ مِنْ كُلِّ مَا سَأَلْتُمُوهُ وَإِنْ تَعُدُّوا نِعْمَتَ اللَّهِ لَا تُحْصُوهَا إِنَّ الْإِنْسَانَ لَظَلُومٌ كَفَّارٌ

wa-ātākum min kulli mā saʾaltumūhu wa-in taʿuddū niʿmata allāhi lā tuḥṣūhā inna al-insāna la-ẓalūmun kaffārun

Translation: And He gave you of everything you asked of Him. If you count The God’s blessing, you cannot enumerate it all. Indeed, the human being is deeply unjust and ungrateful.

Here تَعُدُّوا belongs to ع د د, and تُحْصُوهَا to ح ص ي.

Their juxtaposition brings out a subtle distinction. People can undertake to count blessings, but cannot count and encompass them all completely.

Beginning a count, in other words, is not the same as reaching a complete total.

The verse does not say that people cannot count any blessing. They can recognize and name many blessings, but comprehensively encompassing them all exceeds their ability.

Nor is this inability, by itself, a mathematical proof that the number of blessings is infinite. The verse concerns the limits of human capacity to enumerate divine blessings completely.

Al-Naḥl 16:18 uses the same expression for human inability to encompass The God’s blessing. Its ending differs, emphasizing The God’s forgiveness and mercy.

In these two verses, iḥṣāʾ therefore means complete enumeration, not merely awareness that blessings exist or gratitude for them.

Maryam 19:94: comprehensive and emphatic counting

لَقَدْ أَحْصَاهُمْ وَعَدَّهُمْ عَدًّا

la-qad aḥṣāhum wa-ʿaddahum ʿaddan

Translation: He has certainly encompassed them all and counted them one by one.

أَحْصَاهُمْ and عَدَّهُمْ عَدًّا occur alongside each other in this verse.

أَحْصَاهُمْ indicates comprehensive knowledge of the number of those being discussed. عَدَّهُمْ عَدًّا emphasizes the act of counting itself through the verbal noun عَدًّا.

This combination shows that iḥṣāʾ and ʿadd are not unrelated acts. Both concern counting, and here they reinforce each other.

Nevertheless, أَحْصَاهُمْ can be examined for its emphasis on completeness and the omission of no one, whereas عَدَّهُمْ عَدًّا directly emphasizes their being counted.

This must not become an absolute rule that every ʿadd is incomplete and every iḥṣāʾ complete. The distinction here emerges from the use of the two verbs and the sentence’s emphatic construction.

Al-Jinn 72:28: encompassing and enumerating

The end of Al-Jinn 72:28 contains أَحَاطَ بِمَا لَدَيْهِمْ وَأَحْصَىٰ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ عَدَدًا.

The verse likewise brings several related concepts together: encompassing what is with the messengers, and enumerating everything with respect to number.

أَحَاطَ is from ح و ط and concerns encompassing. أَحْصَى is from ح ص ي and appears here together with عَدَدًا.

أَحْصَىٰ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ عَدَدًا thus concerns comprehensively accounting for things’ number and quantity. أَحْصَى should not, however, be converted into unrestricted knowledge or unrestricted encompassing in every context.

Preserving the distinction between roots matters: encompassing, knowing, counting, and keeping complete track can occur together in a statement, but each contributes its own meaning.

3. Enumerating durations and quantities: time, waiting periods, and human ability

Al-Kahf 18:12: calculating the duration of the stay

ثُمَّ بَعَثْنَاهُمْ لِنَعْلَمَ أَيُّ الْحِزْبَيْنِ أَحْصَىٰ لِمَا لَبِثُوا أَمَدًا

thumma baʿathnāhum li-naʿlama ayyu al-ḥizbayni aḥṣā li-mā labithū amadan

Translation: Then We raised them so that We might know which of the two groups had counted the duration of their stay more accurately.

The verse concerns the Companions of the Cave. أَحْصَى here relates to determining the duration of their stay accurately. “So that it might become clear,” sometimes used to render لِنَعْلَمَ, is an interpretive explanation; the direct wording means “so that We might know.”

There are two grammatical analyses of this word.

In one analysis, أَحْصَى is a form IV past verb: “counted” or “determined the duration accurately.” أَمَدًا can then be its object, referring to the duration of the stay.

Al-Zamakhsharī defends this analysis because he does not consider forming an elative from a derived verb appropriate here. Some other grammarians also prefer it.

In the second analysis, أَحْصَى is an elative: “more accurate in counting” or “better able to determine the number.” The comparison between the two groups becomes more explicit in this reading.

This reading also has grammatical advocates. The four-letter form of أَحْصَى alone does not suffice to dismiss it categorically, since the conditions for forming elatives from derived verbs are disputed.

In either analysis, the principal issue remains clear: determining the duration of the stay accurately.

The morphological difference therefore affects how the comparison is expressed, but does not remove iḥṣāʾ here from the field of calculating and keeping track of duration.

Al-Durr al-Maṣūn: past-verb and elative analyses in Al-Kahf 18:12

Al-Ṭalāq 65:1: counting the waiting period

Al-Ṭalāq 65:1 contains the instruction وَأَحْصُوا الْعِدَّةَ.

أَحْصُوا is a plural form IV imperative, with الْعِدَّةَ as its object.

Iḥṣāʾ here is a practical responsibility: the waiting period must be calculated and kept track of correctly so that the prescribed rights and limits are not applied incorrectly.

This shows that iḥṣāʾ does not always concern an enormous or uncountable set. Sometimes it concerns a specified duration that people must count accurately.

وَأَحْصُوا الْعِدَّةَ does not itself establish the length of the waiting period. Its duration, conditions, and differences between circumstances must be drawn from the relevant verses themselves.

The contribution of ح ص ي here is thus the instruction to count and observe the duration accurately, not to determine every legal detail of divorce.

Al-Muzzammil 73:20: inability to maintain an exact measure

After discussing different portions of the night and The God’s measuring of night and day, Al-Muzzammil 73:20 contains عَلِمَ أَنْ لَنْ تُحْصُوهُ.

In this context, iḥṣāʾ concerns measuring and accurately maintaining the quantity of night or night vigil.

Some lexicographers explain the expression as inability to keep precise track of times. Others, considering the difficulty of night vigil, also discuss inability to perform it.

The two explanations are related, but distinct. Inability to determine and observe the exact portion of night differs from absolute inability to undertake any night vigil or recitation.

The verse continues by instructing people to recite what is manageable and mentions illness, travel, and other human conditions. This shows that differences in people’s actual abilities and circumstances are relevant.

لَنْ تُحْصُوهُ can therefore be understood in connection with inability to keep complete track of and observe the intended measure. The verb alone does not imply that everyone is incapable of every amount of night worship.

This illustrates the lexical connection between iḥṣāʾ and ability: encompassing something is sometimes more than counting it theoretically; it also requires the ability to keep accurate track and observe it.

4. Iḥṣāʾ in recording deeds and divine knowledge

Al-Kahf 18:49: omitting nothing small or great

Al-Kahf 18:49 speaks of a record whose contents alarm the offenders. They ask what kind of record leaves nothing small or great without having accounted for it.

أَحْصَاهَا appears here with the pronoun هَا, referring to the small and great matters under discussion.

Iḥṣāʾ in this context concerns comprehensive recording and accounting. The verse emphasizes that nothing relevant to the reckoning has been omitted.

The subject differs from calculating duration or counting people. There the issue was number or time; here it is human conduct and its recording in the record of reckoning.

The common element remains complete accounting and the omission of no relevant part.

The verse also says that people find what they have done present before them. That presence belongs to the verse’s broader account of reckoning; all its characteristics should not be placed solely within the lexical meaning of أَحْصَاهَا.

Al-Mujādilah 58:6: iḥṣāʾ contrasted with forgetting

يَوْمَ يَبْعَثُهُمُ اللَّهُ جَمِيعًا فَيُنَبِّئُهُمْ بِمَا عَمِلُوا أَحْصَاهُ اللَّهُ وَنَسُوهُ وَاللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ شَهِيدٌ

yawma yabʿathuhumu allāhu jamīʿan fa-yunabbiʾuhum bi-mā ʿamilū aḥṣāhu allāhu wa-nasūhu wa-allāhu ʿalā kulli shayʾin shahīdun

Translation: On the day when The God raises them all and informs them of what they have done: The God has fully recorded it, while they have forgotten it; and The God is witness over everything.

أَحْصَاهُ اللَّهُ is contrasted with وَنَسُوهُ in this verse.

People may forget their past conduct; here human forgetting is placed against divine comprehensive accounting.

This contrast shows that iḥṣāʾ in such a context is more than numerically counting a few actions: preserving and recording what has been done is also involved.

أَحْصَاهُ cannot, however, mean that The God has only just become aware of it. The verse speaks of The God’s comprehensive recording and witnessing, not the acquisition of new knowledge.

Nor does human forgetting here mean that everyone is always unaware of all their deeds in every circumstance. The contrast shows the limitations of human memory beside comprehensive divine accounting.

Yā Sīn 36:12: iḥṣāʾ in a clear imām

إِنَّا نَحْنُ نُحْيِي الْمَوْتَىٰ وَنَكْتُبُ مَا قَدَّمُوا وَآثَارَهُمْ وَكُلَّ شَيْءٍ أَحْصَيْنَاهُ فِي إِمَامٍ مُبِينٍ

innā naḥnu nuḥyī al-mawtā wa-naktubu mā qaddamū wa-āthārahum wa-kulla shayʾin aḥṣaynāhu fī imāmin mubīnin

Translation: Indeed, We bring the dead to life and write what they have sent ahead and their traces; and We have fully recorded everything in a clear, clarifying imām.

نَكْتُبُ and أَحْصَيْنَاهُ appear alongside each other here.

نَكْتُبُ speaks of writing people’s deeds and traces, while أَحْصَيْنَاهُ emphasizes the comprehensiveness and completeness of the recording.

The object of أَحْصَيْنَاهُ is also كُلَّ شَيْءٍ. The statement’s scope is therefore broader than only the deeds mentioned at the verse’s beginning.

فِي إِمَامٍ مُبِينٍ identifies a setting or framework in which this accounting is expressed. Imām belongs to another root, however, and its characteristics should not be inserted into the definition of iḥṣāʾ.

The verse emphasizes comprehensive recording and accounting. The verb أَحْصَيْنَا alone does not determine the material form, technology, or mechanism of this recording.

Al-Nabaʾ 78:29: iḥṣāʾ and writing

وَكُلَّ شَيْءٍ أَحْصَيْنَاهُ كِتَابًا

wa-kulla shayʾin aḥṣaynāhu kitāban

Translation: And We have fully committed everything to a written record.

Like Yā Sīn 36:12, this verse speaks of comprehensively accounting for كُلَّ شَيْءٍ.

The combination of أَحْصَيْنَاهُ and كِتَابًا shows that recording has a clear role in this usage.

The meaning of kitāb and its grammatical relationship to the verb are separate questions. Iḥṣāʾ itself does not mean paper, a notebook, or a particular material instrument.

Everything is the subject of iḥṣāʾ here, whereas Al-Kahf 18:49 focuses on small and great deeds recorded in the record of reckoning. The difference in the scope of these statements must be preserved.

5. Distinctions from related roots

Ḥ Ṣ Y (ḥaṣā) and ʿ D D (ʿadad)

These roots share the field of counting, and the Qur’an places them alongside each other several times.

ʿAdd concerns counting and determining number. In many usages, iḥṣāʾ emphasizes the count’s comprehensiveness, accurate accounting, and the omission of no relevant part.

In Ibrāhīm 14:34 and Al-Naḥl 16:18, people can begin counting blessings but cannot encompass them all. In Maryam 19:94, iḥṣāʾ and ʿadd occur together and reinforce each other.

They are therefore not completely interchangeable synonyms. Neither should an absolute boundary be imposed that excludes overlap in every usage.

Study of ʿ D D

Ḥ Ṣ Y (ḥaṣā) and Ḥ S B (ḥisāb)

Ḥisāb concerns counting, calculation, assessment, and drawing conclusions from quantities. Its Qur’anic uses range broadly from measurement to examining deeds.

Iḥṣāʾ especially emphasizes accurate and comprehensive accounting for number or quantity.

In an audit, all items may first be enumerated and the final reckoning then carried out on that basis. The example clarifies the concepts’ relationship, but does not mean the Qur’an prescribes this fixed technical sequence in every context.

Study of Ḥ S B

Ḥ Ṣ Y (ḥaṣā) and Ḥ F Ẓ (ḥifẓ)

Ḥifẓ concerns keeping, caring for, and preventing loss or forgetting.

In contexts such as Al-Mujādilah 58:6, iḥṣāʾ accompanies preservation and complete recording: what has been accounted for is not omitted despite human forgetting.

Not every act of preservation is counting, however, and not every instance of iḥṣāʾ means physically protecting something.

Al-Ṭalāq 65:1 concerns accurately counting the waiting period, whereas keeping and protecting people or objects can be expressed through other roots.

Study of Ḥ F Ẓ

Ḥ Ṣ Y (ḥaṣā) and Ḥ Ṣ B (ḥaṣab)

These families approach one another in the field of stones and pebbles, but are distinct roots.

حَصَى denotes pebbles, while حَصْب and حَاصِب concern the hurling of stones or pebbles, or a stone-hurling phenomenon.

“Hurling stones” therefore cannot be transferred to the Qur’anic verb أَحْصَى. The Qur’anic uses of ح ص ي concern counting and recording, not throwing stones.

Study of Ḥ Ṣ B

Ḥ Ṣ Y (ḥaṣā) and Ḥ Ṣ L (ḥaṣala)

Ḥaṣala concerns gathering, separating, and identifying the yield or outcome of something.

Iḥṣāʾ can likewise produce an accurate result, but emphasizes counting and comprehensively accounting for parts that can be enumerated or recorded.

Identifying the outcome of a calculation, for example, may be expressed through ḥuṣūl, whereas counting all its data relates more closely to iḥṣāʾ.

Their overlap in arriving at a result or accounting for a collection does not make their roots or all their usages identical.

6. Translation proposals for Forqan

A single fixed equivalent is best avoided across all verses. Morphology, the verb’s object, and context determine whether “count,” “enumerate,” “account for fully,” “record,” or “keep accurate measure” is most appropriate.

For لَا تُحْصُوهَا in Ibrāhīm 14:34 and Al-Naḥl 16:18, “you cannot enumerate it all” is suitable. It preserves counting while conveying human inability to reach a complete total.

أَحْصَى in Al-Kahf 18:12 concerns accurately determining the stay’s duration. “Had counted more accurately” suits the elative reading; if the verbal analysis is preferred, “had correctly calculated the duration of their stay” is more appropriate.

أَحْصَاهَا in Al-Kahf 18:49 can be translated “has fully recorded and accounted for them,” since the record of reckoning and the omission of nothing small or great are central.

أَحْصَاهُمْ in Maryam 19:94 can be rendered “has comprehensively accounted for them” or “has accurately encompassed their entire number.” وَعَدَّهُمْ عَدًّا in the same verse can be translated “and counted them one by one,” preserving the distinction as far as possible.

In Yā Sīn 36:12, Al-Mujādilah 58:6, and Al-Nabaʾ 78:29, complete recording and accounting suit the context. Iḥṣāʾ concerns what is not omitted and is connected with writing, deeds, or divine knowledge.

وَأَحْصُوا الْعِدَّةَ in Al-Ṭalāq 65:1 is best rendered “and count the waiting period accurately.” This expresses the audience’s practical responsibility without adding a new legal provision.

أَحْصَىٰ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ عَدَدًا in Al-Jinn 72:28 can be translated “has comprehensively encompassed the number of everything” or “has completely enumerated everything with respect to number.”

In Al-Muzzammil 73:20, given the measuring of night and the difficulty of observing the precise amount, “you cannot measure and observe it exactly” appropriately explains the expression. The base translation may retain “you cannot keep exact account of it,” with its referent clarified in the explanation.

Conclusion

The Arabic root ح ص ي has two familiar semantic fields: حَصَى, pebbles, and iḥṣāʾ, accurate counting and accounting. Numerical abundance, sound judgment, and firmness of opinion are also recorded in early Arabic. They cannot all be reduced to one origin without historical evidence.

In the Qur’an, the root belongs to the family of iḥṣāʾ. People are commanded to count the waiting period accurately, but are described as unable to enumerate all of The God’s blessings. The Companions of the Cave narrative concerns the precise duration of the stay; verses about reckoning connect iḥṣāʾ with completely recording deeds and omitting none.

Brief root meaning: The Qur’anic branch’s semantic core is accurate, comprehensive counting and accounting for the number, quantity, or parts of something; in relevant contexts it conveys complete recording without omission.

In human usages, iḥṣāʾ may be achieved or exceed a person’s capacity. When attributed to The God, comprehensive enumeration, knowledge, and complete recording are emphasized. “Knowledge,” “preservation,” “writing,” and “reckoning” should not, however, be treated as fixed synonyms of the root itself: each contributes separately within the network of verses.

To translate ح ص ي accurately, first establish what is being accounted for: blessings, duration, people, deeds, the waiting period, or everything. Then choose the sense suited to that context. This preserves the root’s shared core while preventing iḥṣāʾ in every verse from being reduced to a general word such as “knowing” or “counting.”
