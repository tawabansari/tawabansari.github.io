Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root ʿ W N

Help, support, gaining strength, cooperation, seeking assistance, refusing self-sufficiency, and discerning the right direction of aid

Forms Studied from This Root

On this page, the root ʿ W N is studied semantically, then the main Qur’anic forms of this root are explained separately:

  • aʿāna / aʿīnūnī — Form IV; he helped, help me, giving support in completing a task
  • taʿāwanū — Form VI; cooperate, help one another
  • nastaʿīn / istaʿīnū — Form X; we seek help, seek help, asking for support and taking a source of strength
  • mustaʿān — passive participle / descriptive form; the one from whom help is sought, or the one with whom help is sought
  • ʿawān — noun / adjective; middle, neither old nor young, between two states

Note: forms such as yuʿīn, yataʿāwanūn, istaʿāna, and ʿawn are known in Arabic, but the direct Qur’anic forms highlighted for this root are the forms listed above. This page focuses on the Qur’anic forms of the root ʿ W N.

ʿAwān must also be explained carefully. In its Qur’anic usage, it does not mean helping; it refers to being in the middle, between two states. So the forms of this root should not all be flattened into one simple meaning.

General Meaning of the Root ʿ W N

The root ʿ W N in the Qur’an points to the field of help, assistance, support, becoming a backing, strengthening, cooperation, and seeking help. This root shows that the human being is not self-sufficient or independent. A person needs help to stand, act, endure, serve God, and move on the path of truth.

Help in the Qur’an is not only material or outward help. A person may need bodily help, financial help, social support, moral support, or inward help for patience, understanding, guidance, and resistance against falsehood.

But the Qur’an also makes clear that not every kind of help is good. Cooperation can be in the direction of goodness and taqwā, or in the direction of sin and aggression. So the act of helping is not enough by itself; the direction of help matters.

From here, the root ʿ W N brings a person to an important question: from whom do I seek help, for what do I seek help, and when I help others, to which path am I adding strength?

Help Beyond Simple Assistance

In everyday language, help often means doing something for someone or making a problem easier. But in the Qur’an, help can carry a deeper meaning.

Help means that a person, in weakness, need, responsibility, or a difficult path, finds a support. That support may complete their ability, open the way, preserve their patience, or save them from falling into inability.

When a person seeks help from God, they are not merely saying, “solve my problem.” They are admitting: I am not independent; I cannot walk the path correctly without You; servanthood is not completed without Your help; even the ability to stand upon truth is from You.

For this reason, help in the Qur’an is tied to humility. The arrogant person sees himself as sufficient, but the awakened person knows that his strength is limited and that every true strength is ultimately under God’s lordship.

aʿāna / aʿīnūnī: Giving Help in Completing a Task

The form aʿāna and usages such as aʿīnūnī point to helping in the completion of a task. This kind of help may take place at the human level: someone asks others to help so that a rightful, heavy, or difficult work can be completed.

This usage shows that the Qur’an does not deny human cooperation and assistance. People can and should help one another in rightful works. Human help, when placed correctly, is part of divine and social ethics.

But this help must be understood in its proper place. Human beings may be means of help, but the final source of ability and possibility is God. If a person sees the help of others as independent, he may move away from tawḥīd. And if he denies the help of others altogether, he may flee social responsibility.

So human help in the Qur’an is neither absolute nor worthless. Its value lies in being placed in the path of truth, justice, reform, and trust.

nastaʿīn: Seeking Help and Rejecting Self-Sufficiency

Nastaʿīn is one of the most important forms of this root. It means “we seek help,” but it is not merely a request for assistance in a small problem.

In nastaʿīn, the human being admits that for servanthood, guidance, patience, understanding, reform, and passing through weakness, help is needed. He does not see himself as independent or self-sufficient.

Seeking help from God means taking God as the final support. It means knowing that causes, people, bodily strength, reason, knowledge, experience, and planning all have value, but none of them is independent of God.

In this sense, nastaʿīn protects the human being from two dangers: laziness and abandoning effort, and arrogance and self-sufficiency. A person strives, but places final reliance upon God.

So nastaʿīn is not only a phrase for the moment of prayer; it is an etiquette of life. In every truthful path, a person must know that without God’s help, his strength is incomplete.

istaʿīnū: The Call to Seek Help Correctly

The form istaʿīnū is a call to seek help. This call shows that in hardship and responsibility, a person should not rely only on his own force.

The Qur’an calls the human being to seek help through the right path. Seeking help may be connected with patience, prayer, trust in God, supplication, consultation, effort, and righteous companionship. Seeking help means that a person accepts his need and seeks the right way to complete that need.

This point is important: asking for help is not a humiliating weakness. Because the human being is limited, he needs help. The real weakness is to deny one’s need and imagine oneself self-sufficient.

So istaʿīnū calls the human being to active humility: a humility that calls upon God and also moves on the path of truth.

mustaʿān: The One from Whom True Help Is Sought

Mustaʿān points to one from whom help is sought, or the one with whom true help is sought. In Qur’anic usage, this word brings the human being back to God as the final support.

When God is called mustaʿān, it means that in hardship, falsehood, injustice, grief, weakness, and the complexity of affairs, true help is sought from Him.

This does not deny causes. A person may seek help from people, plan, use tools, and cooperate with others. But in the depth of tawḥīd, no cause is independent. All causes are under God’s ownership, knowledge, and lordship.

So mustaʿān gives the human being peace: if all outward paths are closed, God is not closed. If people do not understand, God knows. If powers oppress, God witnesses. And if the human being becomes weak, true help is with Him.

taʿāwanū: Social Cooperation and the Moral Direction of Help

Taʿāwanū points to cooperation and mutual help. This word shows the social dimension of the root ʿ W N. Human beings do not only seek help from God; on earth, they must also help one another in the right direction.

But the Qur’an does not praise cooperation absolutely. If cooperation is in the right path, it is good; if it is in the path of corruption, it strengthens sin and injustice.

This is very important for society. Many acts of oppression are not carried out by one oppressor alone; they are carried out through networks of cooperation. One commands, one executes, one stays silent, one justifies, one profits, and one hides the truth. All of this can become a kind of cooperation upon falsehood.

So taʿāwanū is not merely a call to help; it is a call to discern the direction of help.

Cooperation upon Birr and Taqwā

The Qur’an affirms cooperation upon birr and taqwā. Birr is close to broad goodness, righteousness, faithfulness to truth, and righteous action. Taqwā points to conscious self-guarding, ethical care, and responsible orientation before God.

Cooperation upon birr and taqwā means that a person places his strength in service of good. If someone helps justice, helps the needy, clarifies truth, defends the oppressed, or moves society toward purity and responsibility, he is in the field of right cooperation.

This cooperation may be individual, familial, social, intellectual, economic, or political. The criterion is whether this help brings things closer to truth, justice, mercy, taqwā, and reform.

So helping is not always merely a good feeling. Help must be measured by the criterion of truth.

Cooperation upon Sin and Aggression

The Qur’an forbids cooperation upon ithm and ʿudwān. Ithm points to sin, moral burden, and an act that stains the human being. ʿUdwān is close to aggression, transgression, crossing the limit, and injustice.

This warning is very important, because people sometimes help falsehood in the name of loyalty, tribe, family, state, religion, work, or interest. They may say: we only followed orders, we only remained silent, we only accompanied, we only gave small help; but that small help may become part of a structure of injustice.

Cooperation upon sin and aggression is not only direct participation in oppression. Justifying oppression, hiding truth, supplying tools for oppression, promoting falsehood, or normalizing corruption can also belong to this field.

So the root ʿ W N makes the human being responsible: every strength we give to another must be examined by the path to which it is added.

ʿAwān: Being in the Middle and Its Semantic Separation from Help

ʿAwān appears in the Qur’an in a meaning different from help. It describes something that is neither old nor young, but between two states.

This usage shows that not every form of one root always collapses into one simple semantic branch. ʿAwān should not be forced back into the meaning of “help.” Here, the semantic field is being in the middle, middle age, or being between two limits.

This is important for the Forqan method. We should not take a word from a root and force it into our preferred or familiar meaning. Each form must be understood in its own place, with its own context, and with linguistic precision.

So ʿawān should be mentioned on the root page ʿ W N, but explained separately from the branch of seeking help and cooperation.

Divine Help and Human Help

In the Qur’an, help is spoken of both from God and from human beings. But these two are not on the same level.

God’s help is source-level, final, and tied to His knowledge and wisdom. God can open the way, strengthen the heart, give patience, provide causes, repel hostility, and keep the human being on the path of truth.

Human help is limited, relative, and entrusted. A person can help another, but he himself is in need of God. No human being is an independent source of help.

This distinction preserves tawḥīd while not destroying social responsibility. A person must help others, but he must not see himself as the absolute savior. He should receive help from others, but he should not see them as independent of God.

So human help is sound when it is understood under divine help.

Help, Servanthood, and Guidance

The root ʿ W N is deeply connected to servanthood and guidance. A person needs help in order to serve God. Servanthood is not only intention; it is a path that requires patience, action, purity, decision, and steadfastness.

A person may know the truth but not have the strength to stand upon it. He may know the right path, but weaken before fear, pressure, habit, or weakness. Here, seeking help becomes meaningful.

The human being also needs help for guidance. Guidance is not only knowing the path; it is also the ability to walk the path. Whoever seeks help from God is in fact asking Him to complete understanding, strength, patience, and stability within him.

So help in the Qur’an is not only the solving of outward problems; it is also the building of inward strength for the path of truth.

The Danger of Relying on Wrong Forms of Help

Just as seeking the right help can save a person, relying on wrong forms of help can mislead him.

If a person seeks help for injustice, builds a network for falsehood, or relies on powers that distance him from God, help becomes corruption.

Also, if a person becomes heedless of God and sees causes as independent, outward help can approach hidden shirk. Causes matter, but they are not independent. Human beings can help, but they do not own the final result.

So the root ʿ W N calls the human being to precision: from whom do you seek help? For what task? And when you help others, are you strengthening truth or falsehood?

Summary

The root ʿ W N in the Qur’an invites the human being to recognize help, support, cooperation, seeking assistance, and his true need before God. This root shows that the human being is not self-sufficient and needs help for servanthood, guidance, patience, reform, and carrying out the path of truth.

aʿāna and aʿīnūnī speak of helping in the completion of a task. Nastaʿīn and istaʿīnū speak of seeking help and taking support. Mustaʿān reminds us that true help is sought with God. Taʿāwanū shows the social dimension of help and clarifies that cooperation must be upon birr and taqwā, not upon sin and aggression. ʿAwān is a separate branch of this root, pointing to being in the middle and standing between two limits.

If the root ʿ W N is understood correctly, the human being recognizes both his need for God and his responsibility in helping others. In the Qur’anic view, help is not merely assistance; it is directing strength. And every strength must be examined: is it serving truth or falsehood, taqwā or aggression, servanthood to God or servanthood to other than God?
