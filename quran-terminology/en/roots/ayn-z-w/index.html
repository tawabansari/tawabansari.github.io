Central meaning: attributing someone or something to another; associating a person with a family, lineage, or group. The Quranic noun denotes distinct groups of people.

1. The root in early Arabic

The triliteral root ع ز و concerns attribution and affiliation: connecting someone or something with an origin, family, group, or reference point.

In early Arabic, عَزَا الرَّجُلَ إِلَى أَبِيهِ means attributing a person to his father. عَزَا نَفْسَهُ إِلَى قَوْمٍ means that someone attributed himself to a people and declared his affiliation with them.

An affiliation may be genuine or merely claimed. عَزَا does not itself guarantee its truth; it expresses the act of attribution and identification of a connection.

اعْتَزَى is used for declaring affiliation with a lineage or group. In Arab tribal society, this could serve various purposes, from identifying ancestry and social standing to requesting support during conflict.

عِزْوَة is also recorded for affiliation, family connection, and the name or attribution by which someone identifies himself.

Someone saying that he belonged to a particular lineage or family thereby associated himself with that group. Calling its name for help in danger or conflict gave that affiliation a practical function.

These situations should nevertheless be distinguished. Declaring affiliation is the lexical sense; seeking assistance, mutual defense, and tribal solidarity are possible social functions.

Another important noun is عِزَة, a group of people. Recorded plurals include عِزُون, عُزُون, and عِزًى. This branch occurs in the Quran as عِزِينَ.

Here the word concerns people gathered together as a group distinct from other groups.

Two levels should therefore be distinguished: عَزَا expresses attribution and declared affiliation, while عِزَة names a group. Lexical sources explain a derivational connection, but the verb's meaning cannot simply replace the noun's meaning in the Quranic verse.

2. Early poetic evidence and its dating

One early witness is a verse attributed to Bishr ibn Abi Khazim, a pre-Islamic poet:

نَعْلُو الْقَوَانِسَ بِالسُّيُوفِ وَنَعْتَزِي
وَالْخَيْلُ مُشْعَرَةُ النُّحُورِ مِنَ الدَّمِ

naʿlū al-qawānisa bi-l-suyūfi wa-naʿtazī / wa-l-khaylu mushʿaratu al-nuḥūri mina al-dam

“We strike helmet crests with swords and proclaim our affiliation, while the horses' throats and upper chests are stained with blood.”

نَعْتَزِي occurs in a battle setting. The poet speaks of fighting and declaring group affiliation.

Such a declaration in a tribal setting could do more than give a family name. Naming a lineage or group could identify fellow affiliates, gather allies, and signal a request for support during conflict.

These functions follow from the social setting. نَعْتَزِي itself does not mean fighting, striking, or winning; it denotes declaring affiliation.

Early lexical sources attribute this verse to Bishr ibn Abi Khazim. On that attribution, it is evidence of pre-Islamic usage. The date of this particular composition has not been independently established in this review, and it cannot establish every detail of the root's pre-Quranic usage.

Another witness is attributed to al-Ra'i al-Numayri, an Umayyad-period poet:

فَلَمَّا الْتَقَتْ فُرْسَانُنَا وَرِجَالُهُمْ
دَعَوْا يَا لَكَعْبٍ وَاعْتَزَيْنَا لِعَامِرِ

fa-lammā iltaqat fursānunā wa-rijāluhum / daʿaw yā la-Kaʿbin wa-iʿtazaynā li-ʿĀmir

“When our horsemen and their men met, they called upon Ka'b, and we proclaimed our affiliation with Amir.”

اعْتَزَيْنَا again concerns tribal affiliation. Each group is identified by the name to which it declares allegiance. The verse has variant transmissions; the wording followed here is quoted in lexical sources.

Declaring affiliation on the battlefield could identify groups and request support. Since this poet lived in the Umayyad period, however, the verse illustrates continuation after the Quran's revelation rather than independently proving pre-Islamic usage.

Together, these witnesses show attribution to a group, family, or lineage as an established early Arabic meaning. They do not imply that every group named by a related noun necessarily rests on blood or tribal ties.

3. Lexical and Quranic forms

Important forms include عَزَا, يَعْزُو, عَزْو, عِزْوَة, اعْتَزَى, اعْتِزَاء, and عِزَة.

عَزَا and يَعْزُو express attributing someone or something to another.

عَزْو is the verbal noun of this act and means attribution or ascription.

عِزْوَة concerns a person's affiliation, especially the connection by which they are known or identify themselves.

اعْتَزَى is Form VIII; in the use discussed here, it expresses declaring affiliation or identifying oneself with a group.

عِزَة names a group of people; its plural occurs in the Quran as عِزِينَ.

Lexical sources also record forms with final ya, such as عَزَيْتُهُ alongside عَزَوْتُهُ. This morphological overlap does not justify transferring every use of ع ز ي to ع ز و without historical and linguistic examination.

عَزَاء and تَعْزِيَة are also recorded for endurance in affliction and consolation. Some dictionaries discuss these alongside ع ز و, but consolation and endurance should be assessed separately from attribution and grouping.

Under the root classification followed here, the sole Quranic occurrence is عِزِينَ in al-Ma'arij 70:37. Alternative derivational analyses are discussed below.

This matters because the Quranic occurrence is neither عَزَا nor اعْتَزَى, but a plural noun describing groups of people.

4. عِزِينَ: separate groups

عِزِينَ is the plural of عِزَة.

عِزَة denotes people recognized as a group, such as a gathering distinguishable from other gatherings.

Some lexical accounts reconstruct عِزْوَة as its original form. In that analysis, the final waw is omitted and compensated for by the final ta.

Its historical form and exact derivation have different explanations. Some connect it with attribution and group affiliation; others suggest consolation and the comfort people find in one another.

The noun's established meaning is clearer: a group of people distinct from other groups.

Lexical sources use عِزِينَ for people gathered in separate groups.

This may initially seem contradictory: how can a word relate both to gathering and dispersal?

The distinction lies in two levels of organization.

People gathered within one group are together relative to that group's members, while the group itself may be separate from others.

For example, people at a gathering may sit in several circles instead of forming a single assembly. Each circle contains a gathering, but the circles together form separate groups.

عِزِينَ therefore does not mean complete individual dispersal with no gathering at all. It can describe people assembled in distinct groups.

This distinction matters for translating the Quranic verse.

The morphology of عِزِينَ

عِزِينَ is accusative in al-Ma'arij 70:37, generally analyzed as a circumstantial expression describing the people.

It is inflected like the sound masculine plural: waw and nun in the nominative, ya and nun in the accusative and genitive.

This inflection does not make it an ordinary sound masculine plural of an adjective referring to rational masculine beings. Its singular is عِزَة, meaning a group.

The word's visible form and its meaning therefore require distinct examination.

In the familiar morphological analysis, the final ta of عِزَة compensates for an omitted final root letter. Its two visible initial letters do not make it a two-letter root or a derivative of ع ز ز.

5. The occurrence in al-Ma'arij 70:37

The preceding verse is directly relevant:

فَمَالِ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا قِبَلَكَ مُهْطِعِينَ

fa-māli alladhīna kafarū qibalaka muhṭiʿīn

Al-Maʿārij 70:36

Al-Ma'arij 70:36

“What is the matter with those who disbelieve, hastening toward you?”

It continues:

عَنِ الْيَمِينِ وَعَنِ الشِّمَالِ عِزِينَ

ʿani al-yamīni wa-ʿani al-shimāli ʿizīn

Al-Maʿārij 70:37

Al-Ma'arij 70:37

“From the right and from the left, in separate groups.”

These two verses describe a continuous scene.

Al-Ma'arij 70:36 concerns those who disbelieve, positioned toward the addressee in the condition described as مُهْطِعِينَ.

Al-Ma'arij 70:37 clarifies their arrangement: on the right and left, in distinct groups.

عِزِينَ describes how the people are arranged, rather than naming a particular people, tribe, or belief.

It does not specify how many people each group contains, whether they belong to one family, or why they are separate. Those details do not follow from the word alone.

عَنِ الْيَمِينِ وَعَنِ الشِّمَالِ specifies the groups' directions. In the construction, this phrase can concern how they gather or approach.

عِزِينَ can thus be understood as people positioned toward the addressee in several separate groups, on the right and left.

By itself, عِزِينَ does not mean hostility, mockery, conspiracy, or arrogance. Such conduct may be discussed in relation to the historical setting or the people concerned, but it is not part of the noun's lexical meaning.

The following verse states:

أَيَطْمَعُ كُلُّ امْرِئٍ مِنْهُمْ أَنْ يُدْخَلَ جَنَّةَ نَعِيمٍ

a-yaṭmaʿu kullu imriʾin minhum an yudkhala jannata naʿīm

Al-Maʿārij 70:38

Al-Ma'arij 70:38

“Does every person among them hope to be admitted into a garden of bliss?”

This question moves beyond physical arrangement to challenge their conduct and expectations.

The connection between the verses should not restrict عِزِينَ to unbelieving, arrogant groups or claimants to paradise. Grouping is the word's meaning; disbelief and expectations of admission belong to the surrounding passage.

Grouping and social affiliation

The proposed connection of عِزَة with عِزْوَة and affiliation offers one explanation for the naming of these groups; it is not a settled historical derivation.

People identifying with a particular family, lineage, or community may gather beside its members in social situations and form a distinct group.

At a large assembly, for example, kinship, friendship, shared interests, or other affiliations may produce several circles. Each is internally connected yet distinguishable from the others.

This illustration clarifies the word's range but does not prove that the groups in al-Ma'arij 70:37 were necessarily organized by tribal affiliation.

The possible origin of a name, the established meaning of a noun, and the particular situation in a verse should remain distinct.

6. Related roots and distinctions

ع ز و and ع ز ز

These roots share their first two letters but not identical meanings.

The branch of ع ز و discussed here concerns attribution and affiliation; Quranic عِزِينَ denotes separate groups.

ع ز ز, by contrast, concerns strength, honor, predominance, and difficulty of access or overcoming.

The distinction between عِزَة and عِزَّة is especially important.

عِزَة, with an undoubled zay, names a group; عِزَّة, with doubled zay, belongs to ع ز ز and concerns strength and honor.

For example, the Quran says:

بَلِ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا فِي عِزَّةٍ وَشِقَاقٍ

bali alladhīna kafarū fī ʿizzatin wa-shiqāq

Ṣād 38:2

Sad 38:2

“Rather, those who disbelieve are in arrogance and opposition.”

عِزَّة here belongs to ع ز ز, not ع ز و.

Written resemblance, particularly without vowel signs and doubling marks, should not erase the distinction in root and meaning.

ع ز و and ن س ب

نسب concerns lineage, kinship, and attributing a person to a family or origin.

عزو can likewise attribute someone to a father or family, but its range extends beyond blood relationships.

Arabic can use عزو to attribute a saying to its speaker or a report to its source without implying kinship.

In this comparison, عزو emphasizes attribution and نسب one's lineage or familial connection. However, verbal uses of نسب also extend beyond kinship, so this is not an absolute boundary.

The families overlap, but their full lexical ranges are not identical.

ع ز و and ف ر ق

ف ر ق concerns separation and differentiation.

عِزِينَ names groups that may be positioned separately.

Al-Ma'arij 70:37 depicts several groups on the right and left. Their arrangement relates to separation, but عِزِينَ is not a verb expressing separation or dispersal.

فَرَقَ can express the act of separating, whereas عِزِينَ describes distinct groups.

ع ز و and ج م ع

ج م ع concerns gathering and coming together.

عِزَة is also a group, but the noun alone does not specify its membership count or size.

Quranic عِزِينَ describes an array of separate groups: there is a gathering within each group, while the groups remain distinct.

Gathering and grouping are therefore related without being entirely identical concepts.

ع ز و and ع ص ب

عُصْبَة, from ع ص ب, denotes people joined together who may possess collective strength.

Lexical sources sometimes explain عِزَة through عُصْبَة, since both can denote a group of people.

عُصْبَة foregrounds connection and solidarity, whereas عِزِينَ in al-Ma'arij denotes separate groups.

This does not make every عِزَة devoid of solidarity or every عُصْبَة necessarily related by blood. Independent evidence and context determine each word's particular meaning.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

The sole Quranic form under this root classification is عِزِينَ in al-Ma'arij 70:37.

Suitable renderings include “in groups,” “group by group,” and “in separate groups.”

Given the verse's brevity and reference to right and left, the principal proposal is:

عَنِ الْيَمِينِ وَعَنِ الشِّمَالِ عِزِينَ

ʿani al-yamīni wa-ʿani al-shimāli ʿizīn

Al-Maʿārij 70:37

“From the right and from the left, in separate groups.”

This is concise and natural while preserving the arrangement in distinct groups.

“Scattered” alone is less precise because it may suggest each individual standing apart, whereas the noun denotes groups.

“Tribal groups” and “fanatical groups” add information unsuitable for direct translation: the verse does not specify their internal ties or motives.

Proposal for the Forqan word box:

Word: عِزِينَ

Root: ع ز و

Word type: Plural noun, plural of عِزَة; inflected like the sound masculine plural, accusative with ya; a circumstantial expression here.

Brief root meaning: Attribution and affiliation with a person or group; a distinct group of people.

Meaning in this verse: Separate groups of people.

Conclusion

ع ز و concerns attributing or affiliating someone or something with a particular reference point. The connection may be familial, tribal, social, or an attribution of speech or information to its source.

Early اعْتِزَاء can declare affiliation with a family or group, an act that could request support or identify people during conflict in tribal society.

In the classification followed here, عِزَة denotes a group of people; its Quranic plural is عِزِينَ.

Al-Ma'arij 70:37 describes people arranged in separate groups on the right and left. It means neither complete individual dispersal nor necessarily tribal solidarity, but groups distinguishable from one another.

Thus, the verbal branch principally denotes attribution and declared affiliation, while Quranic عِزِينَ means separate groups. These levels are related in the derivational analysis followed here, but the kind of affiliation or motive for forming each group should not be inserted into the noun without evidence.
