Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root M Ḥ Q

Erasing, gradual reduction, wearing away, removing blessing, and the disappearance of a thing until it reaches decrease or complete effacement

Important Forms from This Root

م ح ق

The root field; effacement, decrease, and the loss of effect and blessing.

مَحَقَ / يَمْحَقُ

Erased; reduced; removed; stripped of blessing; wore down.

مَحْق

Effacement; reduction; gradual ruin; loss of effect and blessing.

مَحَاق

The waning and hiding of light, especially of the moon.

مَمْحُوق

Effaced; diminished; stripped of blessing and moving toward ruin.

Central Meaning

The root M Ḥ Q refers to being erased, reduced, worn away, gradually ruined, and stripped of blessing. It usually does not show only sudden destruction; more often it points to erosion, reduction, gradual effacement, and the disappearance of effect and lasting presence.

In this root, something may still exist outwardly, while its truth, blessing, durability, light, or power of growth is being lost.

Linguistic Note

The root M Ḥ Q is connected with reduction after outward existence. Something is there, but from within or through time it decreases, its light goes away, its effect is erased, or its durability disappears.

This root differs from simple destruction. Something may be destroyed all at once, but maḥq more often shows a state in which something is gradually emptied of effect, blessing, light, or truth.

For this reason, maḥq can be subtle on the surface. A person may see a larger number, while that number has no blessing or lasting force in reality.

Material Image of the Root

One important image of this root is the waning of moonlight. When the moon moves from fullness toward maḥāq, the body of the moon is not destroyed, but its light for the people of earth becomes gradually hidden until darkness.

This image matters for understanding the root. Maḥq means that something still appears to be there, but its light, effect, or strength is decreasing.

Another image is gradual wearing and erosion, like a garment worn or washed until its threads become thin and weak, or a tool slowly worn down through repeated use. This kind of destruction is not sudden; it happens inwardly and through repetition.

So maḥq is not merely wiping away. It is a gradual decrease and loss of brightness, strength, texture, blessing, or effect.

Maḥq and Ribā

The Qur’an says: يَمْحَقُ اللَّهُ الرِّبَا: God wears down ribā. This wording appears in 2:276.

This expression is very precise. Ribā is outward increase: wealth seems to grow, the number rises, the claim becomes larger. But the Qur’an says God makes that very increase undergo maḥq, meaning He strips it of blessing, durability, health, and true effect.

So ribā may grow numerically, but in truth and society it is wearing down. The number rises, but trust falls. Wealth becomes larger, but mercy becomes smaller. The claim grows, but life becomes tighter and narrower.

This is the secret of the contrast between ribā and maḥq: ribā is outward increase, but its end is reduction, darkness, and erosion.

Maḥq and Charities

In the same context, the Qur’an says: وَيُرْبِي الصَّدَقَاتِ: God causes charities to grow. This contrast is very deep. Ribā outwardly increases, but it undergoes maḥq. Charity outwardly decreases, but it grows.

The Qur’anic measure is not only the surface number. A person may give something and the number of his wealth decreases, while blessing, effect, life, and true growth increase. Another person may take something and the number of his wealth increases, while its reality moves toward maḥq and erosion.

So maḥq stands opposite true growth, not merely opposite numerical increase.

Maḥq and Blessing

The root M Ḥ Q stands in inverse relation to blessing. Blessing means lastingness, pure increase, good effect, and sound growth. Maḥq means the loss of that lastingness and effect.

Sometimes something is not small in quantity, but it has no blessing. A person has wealth, but no peace. He has income, but no good effect. He has power, but no durability. This state is close to maḥq: the appearance remains, but the life and effect have gone.

It is the same in transaction. If an economic relation is built on lying, concealment, deception, or pressure, numerical profit may appear, but trust, the soul of the market, is worn away. When trust is eroded, trade itself becomes weak from within.

Maḥq and Truth

Semantically, maḥq can also be understood beside the disappearance of falsehood and the manifestation of truth. Falsehood may have a large appearance for a time, but because it is not rooted in truth, it is gradually emptied of effect and lastingness.

When truth becomes clear, false increases and inflated structures are gradually scraped down until their emptiness becomes visible. From this angle, maḥq is not only external destruction; sometimes it is the exposure of rootlessness in something that once appeared large and stable.

Ribā is like this too. If ribā-based increase stands on injustice and pressure, it may appear as growth at first, but before truth and justice it is worn down from within.

Maḥq and Economy

In economy, maḥq can mean the loss of blessing and health in a financial flow. If money increases but trust disappears, if assets rise but people’s lives become tighter, if wealth gathers in one point while society becomes weak, that outward increase can be moving toward maḥq.

So maḥq is not only the destruction of money. It is the destruction of healthy relation, trust, balance, and the real effect of wealth.

In this sense, يَمْحَقُ اللَّهُ الرِّبَا means that ribā-based increase cannot build sound and lasting growth. Even if it rises outwardly, it carries erosion within itself.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

مَحَقَ / يَمْحَقُ (maḥaqa / yamḥaqu): erased; reduced; stripped of blessing; wore down; removed.

مَحْق (maḥq): effacement; decrease; erosion; gradual ruin; loss of effect and blessing.

مَحَاق (maḥāq): waning and hiding of light, especially of the moon.

مَمْحُوق (mamḥūq): effaced; worn down; stripped of blessing; emptied from within and moving toward ruin.

يَمْحَقُ اللَّهُ الرِّبَا: God strips ribā of blessing; God wears down and destroys ribā-based increase; God draws ribā toward effacement and decrease.

وَيُرْبِي الصَّدَقَاتِ: and He causes charities to grow; and He makes giving fruitful and increased.

Summary

The root M Ḥ Q refers to being erased, reduced, worn away, losing effect, and being stripped of blessing. It does not only show sudden destruction; it also contains gradual decrease, effacement of effect, wearing away of texture, and collapse of blessing.

In the discussion of ribā, this root is crucial. Ribā is outwardly increase, but the Qur’an says God subjects it to maḥq. That means an increase built upon injustice, pressure, and imbalance is eventually emptied of effect, blessing, and durability.

Opposite this, charity outwardly reduces, but God causes it to grow. The Qur’anic criterion is not a surface number; it is true growth, blessing, justice, and life.

Therefore, maḥq is the name of the end of false increase: something rises, but from within it loses its light, texture, and blessing until it is finally effaced.
