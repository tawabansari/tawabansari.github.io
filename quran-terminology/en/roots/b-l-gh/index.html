Qur'anic Root

The Qur'anic Root B L GH

Reaching, arriving at a limit, and delivering a message to its destination and completion of proof

Morphological forms of this root

Root

ب ل غ (b l gh)

Verb
Reaching a limit
بَلَغَ / يَبْلُغُ (balagha / yablugh)

Reaching a place, time, stage, age, maturity, death, or a threshold of responsibility.

Delivering a message
بَلِّغْ / أُبَلِّغُ / أَبْلَغُوا (balligh / uballighu / ablaghū)

Delivering revelation, messages, and the Lord’s communication to the point of reception and completion of proof.
Noun and adjective
Delivery and completion
بَلَاغ / بَالِغ / بَالِغَة (balāgh / bāligh / bālighah)

Delivery, the delivered message, something that has reached, something complete, and a proof that has reached its fullness.

In this entry, ب ل غ (b l gh) is understood in close relation to ب ي ن (b y n): بيان (bayān) clarifies the boundary of meaning, while بلاغ (balāgh) delivers that clarified and bounded message to its destination and recipient.

Foundational Meaning

The root ب ل غ (b l gh) originally points to reaching, reaching the destination, reaching a limit, the completion of a path, and delivering something to its place of reception or realization. In this root, there is a movement or process that advances until it reaches its limit, destination, stage, ear, heart, time, maturity, or result.

ب ل غ (b l gh) has a close relationship with ب ي ن (b y n), but they are not the same. ب ي ن (b y n) clarifies the boundary and separates meaning from ambiguity. ب ل غ (b l gh) delivers the message or movement to its destination. So بيان (bayān) is clarity and boundary-making, while بلاغ (balāgh) is the delivery of the bounded message to the point of reception and completion of proof. When these two come together, as in البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn), in the Forqan method the expression speaks of “delivering that which is mubīn”: delivering the Book, the Qur'an, the Criterion, the clear and clarifying message.

The important forms of this root in the Qur'an include بلغ (balagha), يبلغ (yablugh), بلغوا (balaghū), بلغت (balaghat), بلّغ (balligh), أبلغ (ablagha), أبلغكم (uballighukum), بلاغ (balāgh), بالغ (bāligh), بالغة (bālighah), and مبلغ (mablagh). بلغ (balagha) basically means “it reached.” When it is used for age, limit, place, time, or a stage, it means reaching that limit or destination. When it is used for message and mission, it takes the meaning of delivering and conveying. بلاغ (balāgh) can name the act of delivery, the delivered message, or the completed delivery. بالغ (bāligh) means reaching, reached, complete, or arrived at its destination. بالغة (bālighah) can refer to something complete, reaching, and fully establishing proof.

The linguistic core of ب ل غ (b l gh) is reaching a limit or delivering to a destination. Sometimes the human being reaches the stage of maturity and responsibility. Sometimes a message reaches its addressee. Sometimes the divine command reaches its realization. Sometimes the soul reaches the boundary of departure. Sometimes speech reaches in such a way that no excuse of ambiguity remains.

The material image of this root is a traveler who crosses the road and reaches the station; or a fruit that passes beyond unripeness and reaches ripeness; or a message that leaves the sender, travels the path, and reaches the receiver. As long as the message has not reached its destination, بلاغ (balāgh) is not complete. بلاغ (balāgh) means delivering until the message stands in the position of reception.

In classical Arabic usage, ب ل غ (b l gh) is connected to reaching a destination, reaching a limit, becoming complete, bodily or intellectual maturity, and delivering news or a message. This root is used both in material movement and in delivering speech, news, command, and mission. In the final version, if a reliable witness from pre-Islamic poetry or ancient Arabic usage is found, it can be added.

Spatial Reaching

One clear field of this root is spatial reaching:

فَلَمَّا بَلَغَا مَجْمَعَ بَيْنِهِمَا نَسِيَا حُوتَهُمَا

So when the two reached the meeting-place between them, they forgot their fish.

Al-Kahf 18:61

Here, بلغ (balagha) points to spatial arrival. Moses and his companion travel a path until they reach a specific point. This simple use shows the core of the root: movement until destination.

In the same surah it says:

حَتَّىٰ إِذَا بَلَغَ مَغْرِبَ الشَّمْسِ

Until, when he reached the setting-place of the sun.

Al-Kahf 18:86

And:

حَتَّىٰ إِذَا بَلَغَ مَطْلِعَ الشَّمْسِ

Until, when he reached the rising-place of the sun.

Al-Kahf 18:90

In these verses too, بلغ (balagha) is reaching a point in travel and movement. This same material layer becomes important in the meaning of mission: the message must reach its destination.

Reaching Age, Maturity, and Responsibility

The second field is reaching age, maturity, and a limit of responsibility:

وَلَا تَقْرَبُوا مَالَ الْيَتِيمِ إِلَّا بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ أَشُدَّهُ

And do not approach the property of the orphan except in the best manner, until he reaches his strength.

Al-An'am 6:152

In this verse, يبلغ أشده (yablugha ashuddahu) means that the person reaches a stage of firmness, strength, maturity, and ability to carry responsibility. Maturity in this context is not merely a bodily sign; it is reaching a level of strength and ripeness at which a person’s rights should no longer remain in the hands of a guardian.

In Surah An-Nisa it says:

وَابْتَلُوا الْيَتَامَىٰ حَتَّىٰ إِذَا بَلَغُوا النِّكَاحَ فَإِنْ آنَسْتُم مِّنْهُمْ رُشْدًا فَادْفَعُوا إِلَيْهِمْ أَمْوَالَهُمْ

And test the orphans until, when they reach marriage; then if you perceive maturity in them, hand over their property to them.

An-Nisa 4:6

This verse is very important for understanding ب ل غ (b l gh). The Qur'an does not say that only the body reaches a stage; rather, reaching marriage is accompanied by testing and observing maturity. بلغوا النكاح (balaghū al-nikāḥ) means that they reach a threshold where marriage becomes relevant for them, but handing over financial rights is also conditioned upon perceiving maturity.

In this verse, three elements come together: reaching the limit, testing, and observing maturity. This structure shows that Qur'anic maturity for responsibility is not merely a biological event; maturity must be accompanied by growth, discernment, trustworthiness, and the ability to manage life.

From here an important conclusion follows: An-Nisa 4:6 is not compatible with child marriage. If the Qur'an, for handing over an orphan’s property, mentions both reaching the threshold of marriage and observing maturity as a condition, then marriage cannot be meaningful for a child who is unripe, without maturity, without the power of decision, and still under guardianship. Marriage in the Qur'an is a contract of a responsible human being, not an imposition upon a child.

If a child even needs a test of maturity to receive their own property, how could that child enter a contract heavier than property, a contract connected to body, psyche, consent, family, responsibility, and shared life? Therefore, بلغوا النكاح (balaghū al-nikāḥ) must be understood as reaching the real limit of marriage, not merely reaching a number or a bodily sign.

In Surah An-Nur it says:

وَإِذَا بَلَغَ الْأَطْفَالُ مِنكُمُ الْحُلُمَ فَلْيَسْتَأْذِنُوا

And when the children among you reach sexual maturity, let them ask permission.

An-Nur 24:59

Here too, ب ل غ (b l gh) points to passing beyond childhood and entering a new stage of privacy, responsibility, and social etiquette. Before this limit, children are treated as children. When they reach الحلم (al-ḥulum), the rule of privacy and asking permission changes regarding them. So the Qur'an distinguishes between childhood and the stage of responsibility.

Therefore, in Forqan translation, بلغوا النكاح (balaghū al-nikāḥ) should not be translated simply and dangerously as “they reached marriage age,” if that expression in common culture is reduced to a number or bodily puberty. It is better to say: “until they reach the threshold of marriage” or “until they reach the limit of marriage.” The explanation should add: that is, they reach a stage in which maturity, discernment, ability to carry responsibility, informed consent, and real readiness for contract and shared life are observable in them.

The Divine Command Reaching Its End

The third field is the divine command or decree reaching its end:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ بَالِغُ أَمْرِهِ

Indeed, The God brings His command to its destination.

At-Talaq 65:3

This expression is very important. بالغ أمره (bālighu amrihi) means that The God brings His command to completion, destination, and realization. No obstacle can stop the command of The God halfway. Here ب ل غ (b l gh) points to final realization and the divine command reaching its end.

In another verse it says:

حِكْمَةٌ بَالِغَةٌ

A wisdom that has reached and become complete.

Al-Qamar 54:5

حكمة بالغة (ḥikmatun bālighah) means a wisdom that has reached the limit of fullness and delivery of proof; it is not incomplete, unfinished, or unripe wisdom. This expression is semantically close to بيّنة (bayyinah) and تبيين (tabyīn): a clear proof must reach the point at which responsibility becomes complete.

Reaching the Boundary of Death

The fourth field is reaching the boundary of death:

فَلَوْلَا إِذَا بَلَغَتِ الْحُلْقُومَ

So why, when it reaches the throat...

Al-Waqi'ah 56:83

And:

كَلَّا إِذَا بَلَغَتِ التَّرَاقِيَ

No indeed; when it reaches the collarbones.

Al-Qiyamah 75:26

In these verses, بلغ (balagha) points to the soul or life reaching the boundary of departure and the end of worldly life. Here ب ل غ (b l gh) is reaching the final point of a worldly path. When the human being reaches this limit, the field of ordinary choice and return comes to an end.

Delivery of Message and Mission

The fifth field is the delivery of the message and the mission:

يَا أَيُّهَا الرَّسُولُ بَلِّغْ مَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ مِن رَّبِّكَ

O Messenger, deliver what has been sent down to you from your Lord.

Al-Ma'idah 5:67

This verse is one of the key verses of the root ب ل غ (b l gh). The object of بلّغ (balligh) is clear: ما أنزل إليك من ربك (mā unzila ilayka min rabbika), what has been sent down to you from your Lord. So the Messenger’s بلاغ (balāgh) is the delivery of the sent-down revelation, not the addition of an independent source alongside it.

The continuation of the verse says:

وَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلْ فَمَا بَلَّغْتَ رِسَالَتَهُ

And if you do not do so, then you have not delivered His message.

Al-Ma'idah 5:67

Here the mission is tied to delivery. Being a Messenger has no meaning without بلاغ (balāgh). Mission, in the Qur'anic station, means carrying the message and delivering it to the addressee.

Delivery by Earlier Messengers

The sixth field is the بلاغ (balāgh) of earlier messengers:

أُبَلِّغُكُمْ رِسَالَاتِ رَبِّي وَأَنصَحُ لَكُمْ

I deliver to you the messages of my Lord, and I give you sincere counsel.

Al-A'raf 7:62

And:

أُبَلِّغُكُمْ رِسَالَاتِ رَبِّي وَأَنَا لَكُمْ نَاصِحٌ أَمِينٌ

I deliver to you the messages of my Lord, and I am for you a sincere and trustworthy counselor.

Al-A'raf 7:68

In these verses, the prophets present themselves as deliverers of the messages of their Lord. They are not independent sources of lordship or lawmaking separate from The God; they deliver the messages of the Lord. The trustworthiness of the Messenger is precisely that he does not make the message his own and does not place himself in the position of the Sender.

The God’s Witnessing of the Delivery of Messages

The seventh field is The God’s witnessing over the delivery of the messages:

لِّيَعْلَمَ أَن قَدْ أَبْلَغُوا رِسَالَاتِ رَبِّهِمْ

So that He may know that they have delivered the messages of their Lord.

Al-Jinn 72:28

Here أبلغوا رسالات ربهم (ablaghū risālāti rabbihim) means that the messages have been delivered to their destination. This verse again shows that the central matter of the messengers is delivering the messages of the Lord. إبلاغ (iblāgh) means delivering the message until the proof stands in its proper place.

Balagh as Message and Completion of Proof

The eighth field is بلاغ (balāgh) as message and completion of proof:

هَٰذَا بَلَاغٌ لِّلنَّاسِ وَلِيُنذَرُوا بِهِ

This is a balagh for the people, and so that they may be warned by it.

Ibrahim 14:52

In this verse, the Qur'an or the sent-down message is a بلاغ (balāgh) for people. That is, the message has been delivered to people, and its function is warning, tawḥīd, and awakening. بلاغ (balāgh) here is not only the act of delivery; it is also the delivered message itself.

Al-Balagh Al-Mubin

The ninth field is البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn):

فَهَلْ عَلَى الرُّسُلِ إِلَّا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ

So is there anything upon the messengers except delivering that which is mubīn?

An-Nahl 16:35

And also:

وَمَا عَلَى الرَّسُولِ إِلَّا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ

And there is nothing upon the Messenger except delivering that which is mubīn.

An-Nur 24:54

In common translation, البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn) is usually translated as “clear delivery” or “manifest communication.” This reading is possible from the angle that المبين (al-mubīn) can be an adjective for البلاغ (al-balāgh). But in the Forqan method, because the Qur'an repeatedly identifies itself as كتاب مبين (kitāb mubīn) and قرآن مبين (qur'ān mubīn), the closer and more precise translation is:

البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn) = delivering that which is mubīn.

That is, the Messenger’s duty is to deliver that same مبين (mubīn): the clear Book, the clear Qur'an, the Criterion, and the sent-down Reminder. This reading is fully consistent with other verses, because the Qur'an itself says: بلّغ ما أنزل إليك من ربك (balligh mā unzila ilayka min rabbika). Therefore بلاغ مبين (balāgh mubīn) means delivering the sent-down message that is itself clear and clarifying.

This point is important for understanding obedience to the Messenger. If the Messenger’s duty is to deliver that which is مبين (mubīn), obedience to the Messenger in the station of message returns to obedience to the delivered message; not to obedience to every historical report, later narration, or lawmaking outside the Qur'an. The Messenger is commanded to deliver revelation, and revelation in the Qur'an is itself called the clear Book and the clear Qur'an.

Balagh and the People’s Responsibility

The tenth field is بلاغ (balāgh) in relation to the responsibility of the people:

فَإِن تَوَلَّوْا فَإِنَّمَا عَلَيْكَ الْبَلَاغُ

So if they turn away, then upon you is only the delivery.

Al-Imran 3:20

In this verse, بلاغ (balāgh) shows the limit of the Messenger’s responsibility. The Messenger is obligated to deliver; compulsion, domination over people’s faith, and guaranteeing the result are not upon him. This meaning is in harmony with verses such as “there is no compulsion in religion” and “you are not a guardian over them.”

In another verse it says:

فَإِنَّمَا عَلَيْكَ الْبَلَاغُ وَعَلَيْنَا الْحِسَابُ

So upon you is only the delivery, and upon Us is the accounting.

Ar-Ra'd 13:40

Here two domains are separated: upon the Messenger is delivery; the accounting belongs to The God. This boundary is very important because it prevents the Messenger from being turned into the keeper of people’s faith or the final judge of hearts. The Messenger delivers the message; The God accounts; the human being is responsible for the response.

Balagh and Obedience

The eleventh field is بلاغ (balāgh) and obedience:

وَأَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ وَاحْذَرُوا ۚ فَإِن تَوَلَّيْتُمْ فَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّمَا عَلَىٰ رَسُولِنَا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ

And obey The God and obey the Messenger and be cautious; then if you turn away, know that upon Our Messenger is only delivering that which is mubīn.

Al-Ma'idah 5:92

This verse is very central for the Forqan method. The verse brings obedience to The God and obedience to the Messenger, but immediately explains the limit of the Messenger’s mission: upon Our Messenger is only البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn). This means that obedience to the Messenger in this station is not separate from his message; his message is delivering that which is مبين (mubīn).

So using the obedience verses to construct an independent and endless authority outside the Qur'an conflicts with the continuation of the verse itself. The Qur'an itself explains that the Messenger, in the station of mission, is the deliverer of that which is مبين (mubīn).

The same meaning appears in Surah At-Taghabun:

وَأَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ ۚ فَإِن تَوَلَّيْتُمْ فَإِنَّمَا عَلَىٰ رَسُولِنَا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ

And obey The God and obey the Messenger; then if you turn away, upon Our Messenger is only delivering that which is mubīn.

At-Taghabun 64:12

Here too, the verse does not separate obedience to the Messenger from بلاغ مبين (balāgh mubīn). The Messenger is commanded to deliver that which is مبين (mubīn). Therefore obedience to the Messenger is obedience to the message of the mission.

Distinguishing the Uses

One must distinguish between the uses of ب ل غ (b l gh). بلغ (balagha) in travel means reaching a place. بلغ (balagha) in growth means reaching the limit of maturity, firmness, or responsibility. بلغ (balagha) in death means reaching the boundary of the end of life. بلغ (balagha) in the divine command means the command reaching its realization. بلّغ (ballagha) and أبلغ (ablagha) in mission mean delivering the message of The God. بلاغ (balāgh) can be the delivery itself, the delivered message, or the completion of proof. البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn), in the Forqan method, means delivering that which is مبين (mubīn).

Structural Notes

Structurally, the object of بلاغ (balāgh) is very important. بلّغ ما أنزل إليك (balligh mā unzila ilayka) means: deliver what has been sent down to you. أبلغكم رسالات ربي (uballighukum risālāti rabbī) means: I deliver to you the messages of my Lord. أبلغوا رسالات ربهم (ablaghū risālāti rabbihim) means: the prophets delivered the messages of their Lord. عليك البلاغ (ʿalayka al-balāgh) means: your responsibility is delivery, not compulsion or control over the result. البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn), in the Forqan method, means delivering that which is مبين (mubīn), the clear and clarifying message.

Difference from Nearby Roots

ب ل غ (b l gh) differs from several nearby roots. وصل (waṣala) points to joining and connecting two things; ب ل غ (b l gh) points to reaching a destination or delivering to a destination. وصل (waṣala) may create connection, but بلغ (balagha) emphasizes complete arrival. أتى (atā) is coming; بلغ (balagha) is reaching a limit or destination after a path. جاء (jā'a) is coming or arriving; بلغ (balagha) is reaching the target point or full limit. أعلن (aʿlana) is making public; بلّغ (ballagha) is delivering a message. بيّن (bayyana) is clarifying and setting the boundary of meaning; بلغ (balagha) is delivering that meaning to the addressee. ذكر (dhikr) is reminder and reminding message; بلاغ (balāgh) is the delivery of that message to the people.

Translation Suggestions for Forqan

For translation in Forqan, ب ل غ (b l gh) must be translated according to context. بلغ (balagha) can be “reached,” “reached the limit,” “reached the destination,” or “reached the stage.” يبلغ أشده (yablugha ashuddahu) means “until he reaches his firmness.” بلغ النكاح (balagha al-nikāḥ) is better translated as “reached the threshold of marriage” or “reached the limit of marriage,” together with an explanation of maturity and responsibility. بلغت الحلقوم (balaghat al-ḥulqūm) means “it reached the throat.” بلّغ (balligh) and أبلغ (ablagha) in mission mean “to deliver.” بلاغ (balāgh) can be “delivery,” “the delivered message,” “balagh,” or “completed delivery.” البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn), in the Forqan method, is better translated as “delivering that which is mubīn.” حكمة بالغة (ḥikmatun bālighah) can be “a wisdom that has reached and become complete” or “a wisdom that fully establishes proof.” إن الله بالغ أمره (inna Allāha bālighu amrihi) means “The God brings His command to its destination and realization.”

Linguistic Summary

Linguistic summary: the root ب ل غ (b l gh) points to reaching, reaching a limit, delivering, and completing the path to the destination. Its material image is a caravan, a message, a fruit, or a soul reaching its intended point or final limit. In the Qur'an, this root extends from spatial reaching to human maturity, from the arrival of death to the realization of the divine command, and from the delivery of the messages of the prophets to البلاغ المبين (al-balāgh al-mubīn). Its relation to ب ي ن (b y n) is this: ب ي ن (b y n) clarifies the boundary and separates meaning from ambiguity; ب ل غ (b l gh) delivers that meaning and message to the addressee and destination. From this root, the Qur'an shows that human responsibility requires real arrival at the limit of maturity, and the Messenger’s mission is delivering that which is مبين (mubīn): delivering the clear Book and clear Qur'an, not constructing an independent authority outside revelation.
