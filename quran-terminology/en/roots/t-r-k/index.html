Qur’anic Root

The Qur’anic Root T R K

Leaving, abandoning, letting go, leaving something behind, and changing one’s active relation to it

Important Forms from This Root

تَرَكَ / يَتْرُكُ

taraka / yatruku; left, abandoned, let go, left behind; leaves or leaves behind.

تُرِكَ / يُتْرَكُ

turika / yutraku; was left or abandoned; is left, left alone, or left to itself.

تَرْك

tark; leaving, abandoning, letting go, ceasing.

تَارِك

tārik; one who leaves, abandons, or ceases.

مَتْرُوك

matrūk; left, abandoned, left behind.

تَرِكَة / تَرِيكَة

tarikah / tarīkah; what remains after a person; or, lexically, something left behind.

Central Meaning

The root T R K means leaving, abandoning, letting go, ceasing from something, and leaving it in its place or state. It does not only mean walking away from something. Sometimes it means leaving a thing and going. Sometimes it means ceasing to do an action. Sometimes it means leaving something behind for another. Sometimes it means leaving someone to a condition so that its consequence becomes clear.

Two main directions appear together in T R K: cutting off active attachment, action, or control; and the remaining of something or some effect after that cut. Leaving is not always destroying. Often, leaving means that something remains, but the person’s active relation to it no longer continues.

Linguistic Note

The semantic core of T R K is removing one’s hand from something while leaving it. The root carries a separation from action, holding, attachment, or direct control. A person no longer continues an action, no longer pursues something, or removes it from his immediate control.

Yet this separation is not necessarily the destruction of the thing. When someone leaves something, the thing may remain. What changes is the person’s relation to it. From here, the meaning of tarikah becomes clear: what a person leaves behind after going.

Material Image

The material image of this root is a person placing something somewhere and separating from it. Someone leaves a house; the house is not destroyed, but he is separated from it. Someone leaves wealth behind; the wealth does not vanish, but remains after him. Someone leaves an action; that action is no longer continued by him.

Three elements appear in this image: something exists, a person withdraws from it or leaves it, and that thing or its effect remains somewhere. So tark is both separation and leaving behind.

In some material uses of Arabic, tarīkah is used for something like a husk, body, or tool left behind: something whose active owner or life has departed while it remains in the scene. This layer should be handled carefully and only with sound evidence, but semantically it fits the root’s center.

Classical Arabic Note

In classical Arabic, taraka is used for leaving, abandoning, letting go, leaving behind, ceasing, and passing something behind. It had a wide everyday use: leaving a house, leaving wealth, leaving a road, leaving work, leaving war, leaving speech, or leaving something for others.

The use of tarikah shows that the root is not only connected to abandonment in a negative sense. It is also connected to what remains, inheritance, and consequence. When a person goes, something may remain from him: wealth, effect, name, responsibility, or consequence. All of these may be understood within the field of T R K.

Qur’anic Usage

Leaving and Abandoning

One basic use of the root is leaving or abandoning something. In this meaning, a person no longer follows an action, thing, or condition. His active relation with it is cut. This can be positive or negative. Leaving idols, sin, or falsehood is positive. Leaving truth, responsibility, or the right path is negative. The root itself has no fixed moral color; context gives its value.

Leaving Something Behind

The Qur’an uses this root for what a person leaves behind in inheritance: مَا تَرَكَ الْوَالِدَانِ وَالْأَقْرَبُونَ 4:7. Here, taraka does not mean a simple voluntary abandonment. It means the property that remains after a person has left the world.

This use shows that T R K is tied to remaining effect and inheritance. Someone has gone, but something remains accessible to others.

Leaving in Darkness

In Surat al-Baqarah, the Qur’an says: وَتَرَكَهُمْ فِي ظُلُمَاتٍ لَّا يُبْصِرُونَ 2:17. Here, tarakahum means He left them, abandoned them, or left them in that state.

The important point is that leaving here is not merely going away. It is leaving someone in a condition. تَرَكَهُمْ فِي ظُلُمَاتٍ means He left them remaining in darkness and unable to see.

Not Being Left Without Testing

At the beginning of Surat al-ʿAnkabūt, the Qur’an says: أَحَسِبَ النَّاسُ أَن يُتْرَكُوا أَن يَقُولُوا آمَنَّا وَهُمْ لَا يُفْتَنُونَ 29:2. Here, yutrakū appears in the passive. It does not only mean that they are set free. It means being left to themselves without testing and without the inner truth being made manifest.

The Qur’an rejects this assumption. A claim of faith is not simply left alone; trial reveals its measure.

Leaving Someone to Consequence

Sometimes leaving means leaving a person to himself or to the consequence of his own action. This kind of leaving is not immediate destruction, but being left in the path one has chosen. Whoever rejects light may be left in his own darkness, not randomly, but in relation to his choice and its consequence.

Leaving a Lasting Memory and Effect

The Qur’an uses a repeated expression about some prophets: وَتَرَكْنَا عَلَيْهِ فِي الْآخِرِينَ. It appears for Noah in 37:78, for Abraham in 37:108, and for Moses and Aaron in 37:119.

In this structure, the object is not explicitly stated. From the context, it is understood that something like a good mention, lasting praise, enduring effect, or elevated name was left among later generations. This use shows an important dimension of the root: leaving is not only abandoning a physical thing; it may also be leaving an intangible but living effect in history.

Difference from Nearby Roots

W DH R is close to leaving and abandoning, but T R K is wider and more clearly includes leaving something behind. KH L Y is more tied to making empty, leaving alone, or leaving free. H J R emphasizes leaving with separation, distance, or severing relation.

N B DH is closer to throwing away, casting off, or putting behind, sometimes with intensity or contempt. Ṣ R F concerns turning away or redirecting. KH DH L is abandoning someone from help, especially when help is needed. B Q Y is remaining itself, while leaving may result in something remaining.

Translation Method

In Forqan translation, taraka should not always be translated only as “left.” If the context is separation from something, “left” or “abandoned” may be suitable. If the context is inheritance, “left behind” is more precise. If the context is being placed in a condition, as in تَرَكَهُمْ فِي ظُلُمَاتٍ, “left them in darknesses” or “left them remaining in darknesses” is better.

If the context is ceasing an action, “ceased” or “gave up” may fit. If the context is consequence and abandonment, “left to” may be more accurate. If the context is leaving an effect in history, “left behind,” “left as a lasting mention,” or “placed as an enduring memory” may be more natural.

Forqan Translation Suggestions

تَرَكَ (taraka): left, abandoned, let go, left behind, placed as a remaining effect.

يَتْرُكُ (yatruku): leaves, abandons, lets go, leaves behind.

يُتْرَكُ (yutraku): is left, is abandoned, is left to itself.

تَرْك (tark): leaving, abandonment, ceasing, letting go.

تَارِك (tārik): one who leaves, one who abandons.

مَتْرُوك (matrūk): left, abandoned, left behind.

تَرِكَة (tarikah): inheritance, remaining property, what remains after a person.

وَتَرَكَهُمْ فِي ظُلُمَاتٍ: He left them in darknesses; He left them remaining in darknesses.

وَتَرَكْنَا عَلَيْهِ فِي الْآخِرِينَ: We left for him a lasting mention among later generations; We left his name and effect among those who came after.

Linguistic Summary

The root T R K denotes leaving, abandoning, ceasing, and leaving something behind. In this root, a relationship changes: a person no longer holds, pursues, continues, or actively controls something. Yet the thing or its effect may still remain.

For this reason, leaving is not only going away. Sometimes it is leaving behind. It is not only severing; sometimes it is leaving someone to consequence. It is not only abandonment; sometimes it is inheritance. In 2:17, تَرَكَهُمْ فِي ظُلُمَاتٍ means He left them in darknesses and left them remaining in that condition, not merely that He moved away from them.

The central image is this: the hand is withdrawn from something, the active relation is cut, and the thing or its effect remains in a certain state.
