Qur’anic root Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ

Becoming manifest after concealment; truth emerging clearly, and something becoming established or distinct; other uses concerning movement and settling

The Arabic root ح ص ح ص concerns something becoming manifest after concealment. حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ describes truth emerging clearly after obscurity, denial, or concealment.

Early lexicons also record حَصْحَصَ for moving something until it settles, movement over the ground, and certain movements of a camel. حَصْحَصَة can name the movement itself.

Other usages involve swift or difficult movement and repeatedly pursuing a matter. The family therefore extends beyond the familiar expression حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ.

These senses should not all be assigned one certain original image. Lexicographers offer differing explanations of the relationship to triliteral ح ص ص and reduplicated forms.

The root occurs only once in the Qur’an: حَصْحَصَ in Yūsuf 12:51.

After questions about Joseph and the women’s response, the Egyptian official’s wife acknowledges her earlier action and says:

الْآنَ حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ

al-āna ḥaṣḥaṣa al-ḥaqqu

“Now the truth has become clear.”

This does not mean that a new truth has come into existence. An existing reality, previously concealed or inadequately recognized, is now disclosed by a central participant.

We must distinguish truth existing, truth becoming manifest, and someone acknowledging or admitting it.

1. Morphological structure and forms

The root consists of four radicals: ḥāʾ, ṣād, ḥāʾ, and ṣād.

It repeats the pair ح ص.

In Qur’anic morphological analysis, حَصْحَصَ is a basic quadriliteral verb on the pattern فَعْلَلَ. The Corpus label “Form I” here denotes the basic quadriliteral,not the basic triliteral pattern.

This differs structurally from triliteral فَعَلَ.

Lexicons often discuss it under ح ص ص, while the Qur’anic morphological index classifies its four-radical structure as ح ص ح ص.

Two questions should therefore remain separate:

The classification used in early dictionaries.

The Qur’anic verb’s own morphological structure.

Connecting it with حَصَّ does not make the Qur’anic verb triliteral in form.

Important recorded or morphologically possible forms include:

  • حَصْحَصَ ـ يُحَصْحِصُ: Became clear or manifest; elsewhere moved, shifted something, or persisted intensely in a matter.
  • حَصْحَصَة: Truth becoming clear after concealment; also moving or adjusting something, especially until it settles.
  • تَحَصْحَصَ: In some early uses, clung to the ground or settled; not necessarily manifestation of truth.
  • حِصْحِص: In a lexical use, earth and stones.
  • حِصْحَاص / حَصْحَاص: Earth or gravel forms should be distinguished from the movement adjective. In قَرَبٌ حَصْحَاصٌ, the initial fatḥah form describes swift, unflagging travel.
  • حَصْحَاص: A place name recorded in geographical and lexical works.
  • حُصْحُص / حُصْحُوص: Someone who examines the details of affairs.
  • مُحَصْحِص: An active participle of حَصْحَصَ, also attested in lexicons. Its sense follows the verb’s particular usage; it does not necessarily mean a revealer of truth.

Not all occur in the Qur’an. Some are restricted and should not be presented as a large body of common early derivatives.

The only Qur’anic form is حَصْحَصَ.

It is a third-person masculine singular past verb in the basic quadriliteral pattern.

الْحَقُّ is its subject in Yūsuf 12:51.

The construction therefore means “the truth became clear.”

It is intransitive here: the truth itself becomes manifest, rather than someone directly revealing an object through a transitive verb.

Forms such as يُحَصْحِصُ, حَصْحَصَة, تَحَصْحَصَ, and مُحَصْحِص do not occur in the Qur’an.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: one basic quadriliteral verb in Yūsuf 12:51

2. Semantic branches in early Arabic

Becoming manifest after concealment

Kitāb al-ʿAyn, one of the earliest Arabic dictionaries, states:

الْحَصْحَصَةُ: بَيَانُ الْحَقِّ بَعْدَ كِتْمَانِهِ

al-ḥaṣḥaṣatu bayānu al-ḥaqqi baʿda kitmānihi

“Ḥaṣḥaṣah is the manifestation of truth after its concealment.”

This definition matters especially for the Qur’anic usage.

It distinguishes two conditions.

First, a truth exists but remains concealed.

Second, that truth becomes clear.

This is not simply learning a new piece of news. It can concern removing what prevented recognition of an existing reality.

Lisān al-ʿArab gives the same explanation and connects حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ with truth becoming manifest.

Existence and visibility are distinct.

An event may have occurred without others knowing it.

Someone may also know the truth while concealing or contradicting it.

Reliable evidence or an admission may make that reality clear to others.

This fits Yūsuf 12:51.

Kitāb al-ʿAyn and Lisān al-ʿArab: truth becoming clear after concealment

حَصْحَصَ الشَّيْءُ: becoming clear or established

Ibn Fāris records this expression in Maqāyīs al-Lughah for something becoming clear.

He places it in the branch concerning clarity and establishment.

He also records moving something until it settles securely.

These usages concern different aspects.

One removes obscurity and makes something clear.

The other concerns something settling after movement or adjustment.

A semantic connection can be seen between a truth becoming distinctly established and an object settling in place.

This does not mean that every sentence combines physical movement and the emergence of truth.

The verb’s subject and context determine the sense.

In Yūsuf 12:51, الْحَقُّ is the subject.

Manifestation of truth is therefore appropriate, rather than bodily displacement on the ground.

Moving something until it settles

Kitāb al-ʿAyn also explains حَصْحَصَة as moving one thing within another until it settles securely.

Lisān al-ʿArab records حَصْحَصْتُ التُّرَابَ for moving earth from side to side.

This movement may involve turning over, shifting, or arranging something.

Someone might move earth or gravel to seat something securely in it.

This usage does not itself mean revealing a concealed truth.

It concerns physical movement and settling.

The branch belongs in lexical research without automatically entering the translation of حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ.

A poetic witness concerning a camel’s movement and settling

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ and Lisān al-ʿArab transmit a verse attributed to Ḥumayd ibn Thawr al-Hilālī:

وَحَصْحَصَ فِي صُمِّ الْحَصَىٰ ثَفِنَاتِهِ
وَرَامَ الْقِيَامَ سَاعَةً ثُمَّ صَمَّمَا

wa-ḥaṣḥaṣa fī ṣummi al-ḥaṣā thafinātihi / wa-rāma al-qiyāma sāʿatan thumma ṣammamā

“It braced its calloused pads against the hard gravel, tried for a while to rise, then firmly committed itself.”

ثَفِنَات names parts of a camel’s body that contact the ground when kneeling and become hardened by pressure.

حَصْحَصَ concerns the animal’s movement and bracing itself.

There is an important variation in transmission.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ transmits صُمِّ الصَّفَا in the first line and وَنَاءَ بِسَلْمَىٰ نَوْءَةً ثُمَّ صَمَّمَا in the second.

The variation in both lines makes identifying the source necessary for an exact citation. حَصْحَصَ is present in both inspected dictionary transmissions; no firm claim about a differing dīwān reading is made here without inspecting it.

This should be presented as a lexical witness with variant wording, rather than a uniform text in all sources or a securely dated pre-Qur’anic witness.

It mainly illustrates the lexicographers’ explanation of a camel moving and bracing itself.

Ḥumayd is known as a mukhaḍram, living before Islam and into the early Islamic period. This particular verse’s date is uncertain.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ and Lisān al-ʿArab: variant transmissions of Ḥumayd ibn Thawr’s witness

حَصْحَصَ as travelling or moving quickly

Lisān al-ʿArab also uses حَصْحَصَة for movement across the ground.

It quotes this line:

لَمَّا رَآنِي بِالْبَرَازِ حَصْحَصَا

lammā raʾānī bi-al-barāzi ḥaṣḥaṣā

“When he saw me in the open, he hurried into motion.”

Kitāb al-Afʿāl and the خلبص entry in Lisān al-ʿArab attribute this rajaz to ʿUbayd al-Murrī and continue:

فِي الْأَرْضِ مِنِّي هَرَبًا وَخَلْبَصَا

fī al-arḍi minnī haraban wa-khalbaṣā

“Fleeing from me across the ground, he hurried away.”

Here the verb concerns flight and movement.

This differs from truth becoming manifest in Yūsuf.

The subject here is a person fleeing another.

In Yūsuf 12:51, the subject is الْحَقُّ.

The different subjects help identify the appropriate meaning.

The rajaz is attributed to ʿUbayd al-Murrī; some sources name him ʿUbayd ibn Nashbah al-Murrī and place him among early poets.

Attribution and textual variants should nevertheless remain visible. A quotation in later dictionaries does not itself establish the exact date of composition.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ, khalbaṣa: the rajaz attributed to ʿUbayd al-Murrī

حَصْحَصَة and persistent pursuit

Lisān al-ʿArab transmits from Ibn Shumayl:

مَا تَحَصْحَصَ فُلَانٌ إِلَّا حَوْلَ هَذَا الدِّرْهَمِ لِيَأْخُذَهُ

mā taḥaṣḥaṣa fulānun illā ḥawla hādhā al-dirhami li-yaʾkhudhahu

“He keeps circling around this dirham to obtain it.”

The explanation concerns sticking close, repeatedly approaching, and insisting on attaining something.

Movement and pursuit can thus accompany persistence in some uses.

This does not make the Qur’anic حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ mean that truth persistently tries to obtain something.

Repeated pursuit is another usage with a different subject and construction.

حِصْحِص: earth and stones

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ and Lisān al-ʿArab record حِصْحِص, with initial kasrah, for earth and stones.

Its letters relate to the quadriliteral structure, but its meaning differs from the verb of manifestation.

The terrain sense concerns earth and gravel.

The Qur’anic sense concerns a truth becoming clear.

The noun does not establish that truth is likened to earth or stones in Yūsuf 12:51.

That image is absent from the verse itself.

حُصْحُص: someone who examines details

Lexicons record رَجُلٌ حُصْحُصٌ and حُصْحُوصٌ for someone who examines the details of affairs.

This can involve inquiry, investigation, and attention to particulars.

These words do not occur in the Qur’an.

حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ should therefore not become “someone investigated every detail.”

The truth becomes manifest; the story’s context identifies those contributing to its disclosure.

Lisān al-ʿArab: movement, persistence, and other branches

3. The relationship between ح ص ح ص and ح ص ص

The quadriliteral verb and the triliteral family

حَصَّ belongs to triliteral ح ص ص.

Lexicons record several branches for that root.

Ibn Fāris identifies three principles: a share or portion, clarity and establishment, and removal or diminution.

حِصَّة means a share of a whole.

حَصَّ الشَّعْرَ concerns cutting or shortening hair.

اِنْحَصَّ الشَّعْرُ can concern hair disappearing.

These belong to the triliteral family and should not be presented as Qur’anic derivatives of ح ص ح ص.

حَصْحَصَ remains quadriliteral in structure.

Early lexicographers nevertheless propose semantic and derivational connections with ح ص ص.

First explanation: truth becoming distinct from falsehood

Some lexical and exegetical explanations relate the verb to حِصَّة.

That noun means a defined portion of a whole.

On this explanation, truth’s share or position becomes distinct from falsehood’s.

The central image is differentiation.

Truth mixed with or concealed beneath falsehood becomes clear as the two are distinguished.

This is semantically compatible with Joseph’s story.

A semantic explanation nevertheless differs from proof of historical origin.

Proximity of meaning does not establish that the verb first meant dividing shares and later acquired the manifestation sense.

Second explanation: a covering being removed

Another explanation connects the verb with حَصَّ in the sense of removing or shortening hair.

Early Arabic uses حَصَّتِ الْبَيْضَةُ شَعْرَ الرَّأْسِ for hair being worn away through contact with a helmet.

Al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī considers this branch in explaining حَصْحَصَ.

Something previously covered becomes visible after the covering disappears.

Truth’s disclosure can thus be compared with removing what prevented it from being seen.

This does not prove that the verb originated in shaving hair.

It is one proposed lexical explanation, rather than a certain historical derivation.

Maqāyīs al-Lughah and Al-Rāghib: proposed connections with Ḥ Ṣ Ṣ

Does reduplication necessarily indicate intensity?

حَصْحَصَ repeats ح ص.

Other quadriliteral verbs also repeat a component, such as كَبْكَبَ and كَفْكَفَ.

Al-Ṭabarī and Al-Rāghib compare حَصْحَصَ with such patterns.

This does not make every reduplicated quadriliteral necessarily mean repeated action, extreme intensity, or permanent continuation.

In some words, repeated letters can accompany intensity, repetition, or extent.

Other words have established senses that cannot be extracted from their shape alone.

حَصْحَصَ in Yūsuf 12:51 concerns truth becoming manifest.

The word does not specify how many times it emerged or how many stages preceded it.

The narrative establishes the sequence, rather than the repeated letters alone.

Al-Ṭabarī: truth becoming manifest and comparison of reduplicated patterns

4. The occurrence in Yūsuf 12:51

Truth becoming manifest through renewed inquiry

Verse text:

قَالَ مَا خَطْبُكُنَّ إِذْ رَاوَدْتُنَّ يُوسُفَ عَنْ نَفْسِهِ قُلْنَ حَاشَ لِلَّهِ مَا عَلِمْنَا عَلَيْهِ مِنْ سُوءٍ قَالَتِ امْرَأَتُ الْعَزِيزِ الْآنَ حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ أَنَا رَاوَدْتُهُ عَنْ نَفْسِهِ وَإِنَّهُ لَمِنَ الصَّادِقِينَ

qāla mā khaṭbukunna idh rāwadtunna yūsufa ʿan nafsihi qulna ḥāsha li-Allāhi mā ʿalimnā ʿalayhi min sūʾin qālati imraʾatu al-ʿazīzi al-āna ḥaṣḥaṣa al-ḥaqqu anā rāwadtuhu ʿan nafsihi wa-innahu la-mina al-ṣādiqīna

Proposed Persian translation, rendered here in English:

“He said: What was your affair when you sought to draw Joseph to your desire? They said: Exalted is The God; we know of no wrongdoing on his part. The official’s wife said: Now the truth has become clear; I sought to draw him to my desire, and he is indeed among the truthful.”

حَصْحَصَ is a past verb.

الْحَقُّ is its subject.

الْآنَ specifies the time of manifestation.

The expression has three clear components:

First, now.

Second, becoming manifest.

Third, the truth.

Its direct meaning is therefore:

“Now the truth has become clear.”

The preceding events explain the significance of this statement.

Why does disclosure matter at this stage?

Yūsuf 12:26, Yūsuf 12:27, Yūsuf 12:28, and Yūsuf 12:29 introduce claims and evidence concerning Joseph and the official’s wife.

Joseph attributes the alleged conduct to the woman herself.

Evidence concerning his shirt then becomes relevant.

Yūsuf 12:35 nevertheless describes a decision to imprison him after signs had appeared.

This matters for حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ.

Evidence may clarify truth while those in power refuse to acknowledge or act upon it.

Joseph’s imprisonment does not establish that the accusation was true.

In Yūsuf 12:50, when the king’s messenger comes to bring Joseph, Joseph asks for inquiry into the women who cut their hands.

Yūsuf 12:51 reopens the question.

The women say they know of no wrongdoing by Joseph.

The official’s wife then admits her action.

الْآنَ حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ has a clear place in that setting.

A truth previously entangled in claims, concealment, and wrongful decisions is now disclosed.

Does truth come into existence or become manifest?

This is a central distinction.

The official’s wife does not say the reality of the event first came into existence at that moment.

What happened existed before her statement.

Joseph had already spoken of it.

The narrative had also presented evidence.

Now a central participant admits her own action.

حَصْحَصَ thus concerns an existing truth becoming manifest.

This also fits Kitāb al-ʿAyn: truth becoming clear after concealment.

“Now truth was created” or “truth was manufactured” would be wrong.

Becoming manifest or clear is appropriate.

The official’s wife’s admission

She continues:

أَنَا رَاوَدْتُهُ عَنْ نَفْسِهِ

anā rāwadtuhu ʿan nafsihi

This is a direct admission.

The woman attributes responsibility for the action to herself.

She then says:

وَإِنَّهُ لَمِنَ الصَّادِقِينَ

wa-innahu la-mina al-ṣādiqīna

She identifies Joseph as truthful.

These statements explain the preceding declaration.

Stating the reality and affirming Joseph’s account make the truth manifest.

حَصْحَصَ itself does not mean “confessed.”

Confessing is the woman’s act.

Truth becoming clear is the outcome she declares.

The phrase alone should not become “the woman confessed her sin.”

That information comes from the following clauses and the wider story, rather than the verb itself.

The women’s testimony and the wife’s admission

The women first say:

مَا عَلِمْنَا عَلَيْهِ مِنْ سُوءٍ

mā ʿalimnā ʿalayhi min sūʾin

The statement concerns the limits of their knowledge.

They know of no wrongdoing on Joseph’s part.

The official’s wife, however, admits her own action.

Two different statements are present:

Denying knowledge of wrongdoing by Joseph.

Directly acknowledging an action and affirming his truthfulness.

The distinction matters for disclosure.

حَصْحَصَ alone supplies no complete legal rule about all testimony and confessions.

The verse reports these statements in a particular event.

General evaluation of testimony, admissions, and evidence requires separate study.

الْآنَ: the time of disclosure

الْآنَ matters at the beginning of the expression.

It refers to the present time.

The event occurred earlier, while the admission happens at this stage.

“Now” identifies the time of disclosure, rather than the original event’s time.

An event and the disclosure of its truth can occur at different times.

حَصْحَصَ itself does not mean many years passing or a long delay.

The narrative establishes the interval.

Does the expression mean a final judicial determination?

The truth is disclosed through a central participant’s words.

The expression alone does not name a court judgement or all procedures of a trial.

The account contains questions, answers, signs, and an admission.

These can be studied in relation to establishing truth and examining an accusation.

Modern legal terminology and procedures should not be imposed on the verb without direct evidence.

Lexically, it concerns becoming manifest.

The narrative establishes how this happened and its social consequences.

5. Comparison with related roots

Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ and Ḥ Ṣ Ṣ: a quadriliteral verb and a share or separation

حَصْحَصَ is quadriliteral in structure.

Lexicons treat حِصَّة and حَصَّ under triliteral ح ص ص.

Explanations connect the two through truth becoming distinct from falsehood or something appearing after removal of a covering.

The semantic connection is understandable.

It does not import every sense of ح ص ص, such as a share, hair removal, or diminution, into the Qur’anic verb.

Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ and Ḥ Q Q: manifestation and truth

الْحَقُّ in Yūsuf 12:51 comes from ح ق ق.

حَصْحَصَ is an independent verb from ح ص ح ص.

Something being true and becoming manifest are different.

Truth may exist while concealed.

Someone may also claim that truth has emerged without that claim being correct.

In Joseph’s story, the woman’s statement accompanies acknowledging her own conduct and affirming his account.

The narrative therefore supports disclosure in this setting.

The verb should not become “created truth” or “conferred truthfulness.”

Study of Ḥ Q Q

Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ and B Y N: becoming manifest or clear

بَانَ and تَبَيَّنَ belong to ب ي ن.

The family concerns separation, clarity, and manifestation in its different uses.

Lexicographers repeatedly explain حَصْحَصَ through تَبَيَّنَ.

The verbs overlap in becoming clear.

This does not make all their forms complete synonyms.

ب ي ن occurs widely in the Qur’an, while ح ص ح ص occurs once.

“Became clear” suits the verb in Yūsuf 12:51.

Study of B Y N

Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ and Ẓ H R: concealment and emergence

ظَهَرَ can concern becoming visible or manifest.

Lisān al-ʿArab explains حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ through ظَهَرَ وَبَرَزَ.

The families overlap in this use.

ظ ه ر also has other senses, including the back, emergence, and prevailing.

Those should not be transferred into حَصْحَصَ itself.

Study of Ẓ H R

Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ and K SH F: manifestation and removing a covering

كَشَفَ concerns removing a covering or revealing something.

Lexicographers explain the truth expression through removal of what had concealed it.

Disclosure may therefore be the action or process leading to manifestation.

The Qur’anic verb describes the truth itself becoming clear.

الْحَقُّ is the subject; the clause does not name an agent directly revealing an object through a transitive construction.

It should not be recast without reason as “someone discovered the truth.”

Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ and Ṣ D Q: truth and truthful speech

صَدَقَ concerns truthfulness and speaking truly.

The official’s wife calls Joseph truthful in Yūsuf 12:51.

The verse thus connects disclosure of truth with his truthfulness.

They remain different concepts.

حَصْحَصَ describes truth becoming manifest.

الصَّادِقِينَ describes people in terms of truthful speech.

The disclosure here confirms the truth of Joseph’s statement.

Study of Ṣ D Q

Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ and Ḥ Ṣ L: manifestation and obtaining or gathering

ح ص ل is an independent root concerning obtaining, gathering, or a result emerging.

Al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:10 uses حُصِّلَ for what is in the breasts.

Its theme of hidden matters being brought out relates to the present discussion.

حُصِّلَ nevertheless comes from ح ص ل, rather than ح ص ح ص.

Similar meaning should not merge the roots.

Ḥ Ṣ Ḥ Ṣ and Ḥ Ṣ Y: clarity and counting

أَحْصَىٰ belongs to ح ص ي and concerns counting or knowing the quantity comprehensively.

حُصْحُص also describes attention to details in Arabic.

The thematic proximity does not make the truth expression in Yūsuf 12:51 mean numerically counting truth.

The verse concerns disclosure of an event’s reality, rather than counting objects.

6. Interpretive questions and semantic development

Does حَصْحَصَ always concern truth becoming manifest?

No.

Early Arabic records movement and shifting too.

The rajaz attributed to ʿUbayd al-Murrī concerns a person moving and fleeing.

Lexicons also use the family for moving earth and a camel bracing itself.

Manifestation of truth is one established use, rather than the whole family’s meaning.

The Qur’anic subject الْحَقُّ and the story’s disclosure make that sense appropriate here.

Did the truth not previously exist?

No.

The event had already happened.

Joseph had already spoken of it.

The renewed inquiry now produces an explicit admission.

What changes is the truth’s visibility to others, rather than the past reality itself.

Translation should retain the distinction.

Does the verb necessarily indicate gradual disclosure?

It does not impose that requirement.

A story may reveal truth after a succession of events.

Repeating ح ص does not alone establish that the verb always denotes several gradual stages.

The narrative began earlier and reaches an admission in Yūsuf 12:51.

Its unfolding comes from the story, rather than the root’s structure alone.

Does the verb mean confessing?

Not directly.

The official’s wife confesses.

حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ expresses the truth becoming manifest.

Truth can emerge through an admission, testimony, observation, or other evidence.

The verb should not be restricted to one means of disclosure.

The admission matters in Joseph’s story, but its role comes from context.

Does disclosure always mean justice is implemented?

No.

Truth may emerge without those in power acting accordingly.

The story had already presented signs before Joseph’s imprisonment.

Knowing truth and implementing justice are distinct.

The verb here describes truth becoming manifest.

It does not establish that every social consequence occurred at that same instant.

Is this exclusively a judicial term?

Yūsuf 12:51 uses it in an inquiry resembling examination of an accusation.

Lexicons do not define it as a technical name for a court, judge, or formal decision.

It also occurs in other settings.

It should not be restricted to “issuing a final judgement.”

The truth emerges; the story establishes the inquiry’s form and consequences.

7. Translation proposals for Forqan

For حَصْحَصَ in Yūsuf 12:51, “became clear” is a suitable concise equivalent.

“Became evident” is also natural and accurate.

“Became manifest” can express emergence from concealment.

“Became established and clear” approaches one lexical explanation, but may insert two concepts for one verb.

“Became distinct from falsehood” reflects some analyses but is more explanatory than the basic equivalent.

For the full expression:

الْآنَ حَصْحَصَ الْحَقُّ

al-āna ḥaṣḥaṣa al-ḥaqqu

The proposed rendering is:

“Now the truth has become clear.”

This is short, natural, and faithful to the sentence’s structure.

A contextual explanation can add that the women’s words and the wife’s admission clarified Joseph’s case.

That explanation should not replace the verb itself.

Non-Qur’anic derivatives also require their respective senses.

حَصْحَصَ الشَّيْءُ in the manifestation branch means “became clear or evident.”

حَصْحَصَ التُّرَابَ concerns moving or turning over earth.

In describing movement over the ground, the verb can mean travelling or moving.

حَصْحَصَة concerns truth emerging after concealment in one use, and physical movement in another.

Forqan’s word box can retain two distinct parts:

Brief root meaning: “Becoming clear after concealment; settling or becoming distinct; and, in other uses, movement and shifting.”

Meaning in Yūsuf 12:51: “The truth of Joseph’s case becoming manifest through the women’s statement and the official’s wife’s admission.”

The box should not define the verb directly as confession, a trial, or issuing judgement.

Al-Nihāyah: the attested active participle muḥaṣḥiṣ in another sense

Those belong to the story’s events, while the root is broader.

8. Conclusion

ح ص ح ص is a reduplicated quadriliteral Arabic root.

Its sole Qur’anic occurrence is the past verb حَصْحَصَ.

Its clear meaning in Yūsuf 12:51 is truth becoming manifest.

Kitāb al-ʿAyn explains truth emerging after concealment; Maqāyīs al-Lughah relates it to clarity and establishment.

Other lexical branches concern shifting, moving something until it settles, travelling quickly, and repeatedly pursuing something.

Poetry attributed to ʿUbayd al-Murrī and Ḥumayd ibn Thawr illustrates some uses. Wording and attribution vary, particularly for Ḥumayd’s verse, and the witnesses require caution.

Lexicographers also connect the verb with triliteral ح ص ص through explanations such as truth’s share becoming distinct or a concealing covering being removed.

These are semantic explanations, rather than proof of historical origin.

The expression’s place in Joseph’s story is clear.

The event’s reality already existed.

Joseph had spoken of it.

In the renewed inquiry, the women deny knowing wrongdoing by Joseph and the official’s wife admits her own conduct.

The truth becomes manifest in that setting.

Manifestation differs from coming into existence, just as an admission differs from the verb’s lexical meaning.

حَصْحَصَ in Yūsuf 12:51 concerns a truth becoming clear after it had remained concealed or obscure.

“Now the truth has become clear” is therefore a suitable Forqan rendering.

It preserves the Arabic meaning and leaves admissions, evidence, Joseph’s truthfulness, and the distinction between knowing truth and implementing justice for independent reflection on the verse.
