Qur'anic Root

Qur'anic Root AYN TH W

Engaging in corruption and disturbing sound order in a way that harms people’s rights and collective life.

1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ع ث و in the Qur'an moves in the semantic field of causing corruption, spreading ruin, disturbing right order, and harming the soundness of collective life.

The proposed short meaning of the root is: engaging in corruption and disturbing an existing order and soundness, in a way that harms people's rights and the foundations of collective life.

This root appears in the Qur'an five times and only in the plural prohibitive verb form تَعْثَوْا. In all cases, the verb is accompanied by the phrase فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ:

وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ

And do not engage in corruption and ruin in the earth.

This fixed pattern shows that, in Qur'anic usage, عثو is a corruption-producing behavior whose effect extends to the earth, society, resources, people's rights, and public relations.

The weakening of the foundations of trust, justice, and social order can be understood as one of the outcomes of عثو. But "hollowing out from within" or "moth-eating" is not the direct and established meaning of this root. If such images are used, they should be presented only as explanatory similes.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

عَثَا / يَعْثُو

It means: he corrupted, spread ruin, or moved along a path of corruption.

تَعْثَوْا

A present-tense verb under prohibition, addressed to a group: do not corrupt; do not engage in ruin; do not spread corruption and disruption in the earth.

This is the only Qur'anic form of the root.

عُثُوّ / عُثِيّ

Verbal nouns reported in Arabic for this verb. These forms do not occur in the Qur'an.

عَاثٍ

An active participle meaning one who practices عثو and proceeds in corruption. This form also does not occur in the Qur'an.

The absence of these derivatives in the Qur'an is only a morphological report. It should not be used to conclude that عثو is intrinsically only collective, or that the Qur'an deliberately avoids naming an individual as عاثي.

3. Reference Card for the Qur'anic Derivative

Qur’anic derivativeUsed in Qur’an: yes
تَعْثَوْا

Do not engage in corruption-producing behavior in the earth, and do not spread ruin by disturbing right, justice, and sound order.

This verb in the Qur'an always appears as a plural prohibition; is accompanied by فِي الْأَرْضِ; and is joined with مُفْسِدِينَ, which clarifies the corruption-producing direction of the behavior.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The defensible semantic core of the root is: engaging in corruption and moving forward in conduct that harms soundness, order, or existing rights.

In Qur'anic usage, عثو is not merely the name of a small, isolated error. The root appears in contexts connected with public resources, social life, settlement, economic exchange, and responsibility before the Last Day.

This feature can show that عثو emphasizes behavior that spreads corruption and has consequences beyond a limited slip. Still, very extreme intensity, hiddenness, openness, or total destruction should not be placed inside the basic definition in every use.

The kind of عثو is known from the context: misuse of blessing and resources; disturbing the order of access; diminishing people's rights; corruption in measure and weight; misuse of power and settlement; or separating worship from social responsibility.

5. The Material Image of the Root

For ع ث و in the Qur'an there is no direct material image like splitting stone, drinking water, or a spring coming out.

The Qur'anic image of this root is mostly social: a community has water, food, land, houses, technical ability, and economic exchange; but people, instead of preserving equity and rights, use that same environment for transgression, reducing others' shares, disturbing trust, and spreading corruption.

For explaining the outcome of عثو, one may speak of a building whose inner bonds are weakened and whose firmness is lost. One may also compare corruption to a harm that wears down the foundation of a system before the final result becomes visible.

But these are analytical similes, not the material etymology of the word.

The word عُثّه, meaning moth, is built from the letters ع ث ث. Its sound resemblance to ع ث و is not enough to prove a derivational link. Therefore moth-eating should not be presented as the original or historical image of the root ع ث و.

6. Use of the Root in Arabic

In lexical sources, عَثَا and عَثِيَ are reported for corrupting, spreading ruin, and going far in corruption.

Raghib al-Isfahani suggests a tendency of difference between عيث and عثي: عيث more often for corruption that is sensed directly, and عثي more often for corruption that is known through judgment, outcome, or evaluation.

Raghib himself treats the two as close to one another. Therefore this difference should not be turned into an absolute boundary that makes every عثو hidden and every عيث visible or material.

With caution, one may say that عثو in Qur'anic usage appears more in the field of behavioral and social corruption. But because the Qur'an does not use the root ع ي ث, no precise Qur'anic boundary between these two roots can be extracted.

Arabic also reports forms such as عَثْوَة and أَعْثَى concerning the thickness or dishevelment of hair. This lexical branch should not be merged into the Qur'anic meaning of corruption as one unified image without clear evidence.

7. Challenges in Understanding and Rendering

Treating عثو and فساد as completely identical

In all five verses, تَعْثَوْا is accompanied by مُفْسِدِينَ. This co-occurrence shows the two words are very close, but the presence of both may indicate emphasis, clarification of the direction of the behavior, or the result of that behavior.

A cautious tendency may be considered: عثو emphasizes engaging and proceeding in ruin-producing behavior; افساد emphasizes causing corruption and removing soundness. Still, this boundary should not be treated as certain.

Adding hidden or open corruption to the base definition

The root itself does not decide that the corruption must be hidden or must be open. Shortchanging, reducing rights, violating resources, and social corruption can happen in different forms. Therefore hiddenness or openness should be removed from the base definition.

Making a certain link with عيث

The closeness of عثو and عيث in lexical sources is important, but the distinction between sensible and non-sensible corruption is a lexical proposal, not a proven rule for all uses of language.

Examples such as a wolf attacking a flock or material raiding may clarify the use of عيث, but they are not enough to prove that عثو is intrinsically only intellectual or spiritual corruption.

Connecting عثو with moth-eating

عُثّه and عَثّ relate to moth and its eating away, but their root is ع ث ث. The root under study is ع ث و.

The image of moth-eating may be literarily attractive for explaining gradual corruption, but it must not be introduced as the lexical origin of عثو or as the missing link of the root.

Restricting عثو to economic crime

The two verses about Shu'ayb place عثو in the context of measure, weight, and people's rights. But two other verses bring it after the blessings of water, food, land, and settlement, and the verse in Al-'Ankabut connects it with worship and the Last Day.

Economic corruption is therefore one clear instance of عثو, not the whole meaning of the root.

Turning عثو into complete collapse

عثو can weaken the foundation of a society, but the word itself does not necessarily mean final destruction. Collapse, ruin, or punishment may be the result of continued corruption.

8. Methodological Note

In examining each verse, one should ask: after which blessing, command, or warning does the prohibition of عثو come? Which right or order is at risk? What range does فِي الْأَرْضِ give to the behavior? What role does مُفْسِدِينَ play in explaining the quality or result of عثو? Is the instance economic, social, related to resources, or a combination of these? Which meaning comes from the root itself and which result comes from the context?

These three levels should be kept distinct: direct meaning: engaging in corruption and spreading ruin; contextual instance: shortchanging, reducing rights, misusing blessing, or disturbing public order; outcome: weakening trust, justice, security, settlement, and the foundations of collective life.

The outcome must not replace the direct meaning of the root.

9. Qur'anic Uses of the Root

عثو after being given water and provision

وَإِذِ اسْتَسْقَىٰ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِ فَقُلْنَا اضْرِبْ بِعَصَاكَ الْحَجَرَ فَانْفَجَرَتْ مِنْهُ اثْنَتَا عَشْرَةَ عَيْنًا قَدْ عَلِمَ كُلُّ أُنَاسٍ مَشْرَبَهُمْ كُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا مِنْ رِزْقِ اللَّهِ وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ

And when Moses sought water for his people, We said: Strike the stone with your staff. Then twelve springs burst forth from it. Each group knew its drinking place. Eat and drink from the provision of The God, and do not engage in corruption and ruin in the earth.

Al-Baqarah 2:60

The prohibition of عثو comes after several blessings and a clear order: water was provided; twelve springs appeared; each group's drinking place became known; and permission was given to eat and drink from the provision of The God.

This sequence shows that receiving blessing and resources is not a license for transgression, disturbing order, or corruption. The verse itself does not restrict the specific instance of عثو; the prohibition can include every kind of corruption after receiving blessing.

عثو after power, settlement, and construction

وَاذْكُرُوا إِذْ جَعَلَكُمْ خُلَفَاءَ مِنْ بَعْدِ عَادٍ وَبَوَّأَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ تَتَّخِذُونَ مِنْ سُهُولِهَا قُصُورًا وَتَنْحِتُونَ الْجِبَالَ بُيُوتًا فَاذْكُرُوا آلَاءَ اللَّهِ وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ

And remember when He made you successors after Aad and settled you in the earth. You make palaces from its plains and carve the mountains into houses. So remember the favors of The God, and do not engage in corruption and ruin in the earth.

Al-A'raf 7:74

The people of Thamud had access to land, building power, plains, and mountains. The prohibition of عثو shows that technical progress and outward settlement are not, by themselves, signs of moral soundness.

The act of building structures or carving houses in the mountains should not itself be treated as عثو. These are mentioned in the verse as abilities and resources of the people. Corruption forms in the way they behave toward blessing, people, and divine command.

عثو in the context of measure, weight, and people's rights

وَيَا قَوْمِ أَوْفُوا الْمِكْيَالَ وَالْمِيزَانَ بِالْقِسْطِ وَلَا تَبْخَسُوا النَّاسَ أَشْيَاءَهُمْ وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ

And O my people, fulfill the measure and the balance with equity; do not diminish people's things; and do not engage in corruption and ruin in the earth.

Hud 11:85

This is one of the clearest contexts of عثو. Before the prohibition, three measures are given: completing the measure; completing the weight with equity; and not diminishing the rights and property of people.

Shortchanging is not merely the harm of one transaction. If it becomes a common practice, it damages trust, justice, and the security of exchange. Still, عثو should not be translated only as shortchanging. Shortchanging is one of its instances in this context.

The repeated prohibition in Shu'ayb's speech

أَوْفُوا الْكَيْلَ وَلَا تَكُونُوا مِنَ الْمُخْسِرِينَ وَزِنُوا بِالْقِسْطَاسِ الْمُسْتَقِيمِ وَلَا تَبْخَسُوا النَّاسَ أَشْيَاءَهُمْ وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ

Fulfill the measure and do not be among those who cause loss. Weigh with the straight balance. Do not diminish people's things, and do not engage in corruption and ruin in the earth.

Ash-Shuara 26:181-183

In these verses, عثو comes after three clear forms of economic injustice: making the measure deficient; making the weight incorrect; and diminishing people's rights.

The phrase فِي الْأَرْضِ expands the outcome beyond an individual transaction. Corruption in exchange can harm the public structure of trust and justice.

عثو in connection with worship and the Last Day

وَإِلَىٰ مَدْيَنَ أَخَاهُمْ شُعَيْبًا فَقَالَ يَا قَوْمِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَارْجُوا الْيَوْمَ الْآخِرَ وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ

And to Madyan We sent their brother Shu'ayb. He said: O my people, worship The God, keep the Last Day in view, and do not engage in corruption and ruin in the earth.

Al-Ankabut 29:36

In this verse, three directions are placed together: worship of The God; regard for the Last Day; and avoiding عثو in the earth.

This connection shows that worship and belief in final accountability are not separate from social conduct. A claim of faith cannot justify earthly corruption and violation of people's rights.

The precise meaning of وَارْجُوا الْيَوْمَ الْآخِرَ should be determined in the separate study of the root ر ج و.

10. Examination of the Qur'anic Form تَعْثَوْا

The root ع ث و appears in the Qur'an only in the form تَعْثَوْا.

This form is a present-tense verb; it is prohibited by لَا; it is addressed to a group; and it ends with the wāw of plurality.

The repeated structure in five verses creates a clear pattern:

وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ

And do not engage in corruption and ruin in the earth.

The plural address fits the tribal and social contexts of the verses. Still, it cannot be concluded that an individual alone is incapable of عثو. An individual can also produce corruption that spreads into a social structure.

11. Grammatical and Structural Notes

The prohibitive لَا

In all cases, لَا is the prohibitive particle, and it makes the present verb jussive. The verse is not merely reporting the existence of corruption; it is preventing the audience from entering, continuing, or spreading it.

فِي الْأَرْضِ

فِي الْأَرْضِ indicates the environment and range of the behavior. In this structure, the earth may include the arena of human life, resources, social relations, and economic dealings. But in the base translation it should not be restricted to one specific instance such as state, market, environment, or political system.

مُفْسِدِينَ

Mُفْسِدِينَ is grammatically a circumstantial accusative connected to the subject of تَعْثَوْا. A literal English rendering such as "while being corrupters" may suggest that the audience is already fixed as corrupters. The structure is better conveyed as: do not engage in عثو in a corruption-producing way; do not commit an عثو that creates corruption; do not engage in ruin-producing behavior in the earth.

Mُفْسِدِينَ clarifies the direction and quality of the عثو being prohibited. It can be understood as an emphatic state, or as clarifying the result and nature of the behavior.

The co-occurrence of عثو and افساد

The co-occurrence of these two words may serve emphasis, clarification of the nature of عثو, or statement of its result. From this structure alone, one cannot conclude that عثو is always hidden corruption; that افساد is always open corruption; or that one is always the fixed cause and the other always its fixed result.

12. ع ث و Compared with Near Roots

ع ث و compared with ف س د

فساد denotes ruin, the disturbance of soundness, and the transformation of a sound state into an unhealthy one. عثو in the Qur'an always appears beside افساد and emphasizes engaging and moving forward in corruption-producing conduct. Suggested tendency: فساد names corruption itself or the act of corrupting; عثو highlights involvement in a path that spreads corruption. The limited Qur'anic evidence does not allow this boundary to be stated absolutely.

ع ث و compared with ع ي ث

عیث is also related in Arabic to corruption and spreading ruin. Raghib tends to use عیث for corruption that is sensed, and عثی for corruption known through judgment and result. This is a tendency, not an absolute distinction. It may be possible that عیث in some language uses fits material destruction more closely and عثو fits behavioral and social corruption more closely, but turning that possibility into a rule requires broader evidence. The Qur'an does not use the root ع ي ث, so no direct Qur'anic contrast between the two is available.

ع ث و compared with ع ث ث

عُثّه, meaning moth, belongs to the structure ع ث ث. عثو is from ع ث و. The similarity of the first two letters and the fitting image of gradual corruption are not enough to prove common derivation. Moth-eating can be a simile for hidden erosion, but it is not part of the lexical definition of عثو.

ع ث و compared with ب خ س

بخس points to diminishing another's right, value, or property. In Hud 11:85 and Ash-Shuara 26:183, بخس comes before the prohibition of عثو. بخس is a specific behavior; عثو is a wider title for the social corruption of which بخس can be one instance.

ع ث و compared with خ س ر

خسران relates to loss, reduction, and forfeiture. In Ash-Shuara 26:181, الْمُخْسِرِينَ comes before the prohibition of عثو. Causing loss can refer to harm in measure, while عثو shows the wider range of corruption.

ع ث و compared with ط غ ي

طغیان denotes exceeding bounds and rebellion. عثو emphasizes corruption-producing behavior in the earth. Rebellion may prepare the ground for عثو, and عثو may be one manifestation of rebellion, but the two roots are not synonyms.

ع ث و compared with ع د و

تعدي denotes crossing a boundary and violating a right. عثو can arise from a set of transgressions that corrupt public order and soundness, but عثو is not only crossing one specific boundary.

ع ث و compared with ه ل ك

هلاك relates to destruction, perishing, or a harmful end. عثو is corruption-producing behavior, and destruction may be the outcome of its continuation. Complete destruction should not be placed inside the definition of عثو.

13. عثو and Misuse of Blessing

The first two uses of the root come after a reminder of blessing: water, food, and ordered access in Al-Baqarah 2:60; and land, houses, plains, and mountains in Al-A'raf 7:74.

This co-occurrence shows that corruption does not necessarily arise from poverty or lack of means. عثو can form in the middle of possession, power, and outward settlement.

Blessing can open two paths before human beings: responsible use, gratitude, and preservation of right; or arrogance, transgression, and spreading corruption.

A society may have buildings, resources, technical knowledge, and economic power. But if equity and people's rights are damaged in it, outward settlement does not prevent عثو.

This conclusion is understood from the context of the verses, not from the one-word lexical meaning of عثو.

14. عثو and the Erosion of Social Foundations

In the verses of Shu'ayb, economic corruption is not limited to the amount missing from one transaction. Repeated short measure, manipulation of weight, and diminishing rights can weaken public trust, destroy the value of covenant and exchange, expose weaker people to harm, and replace equity with deception.

At this level one can say that عثو weakens the foundation of society. This weakness may appear in trust, justice, and people's relations before final ruin becomes visible.

Comparing such corruption to an erosion that weakens something from within is possible as literature. But it must not be attributed to the word عُثّه or moth and presented as a definite etymology.

15. Translation Suggestions for Forqan

تَعْثَوْا

Possible equivalents: do not engage in corruption; do not practice ruin; do not spread corruption and disruption; do not turn toward ruinous conduct.

The full phrase: وَلَا تَعْثَوْا فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ

First suggestion: and do not engage in corruption and ruin in the earth. This is natural, but it partly merges عثو and مفسدین into one phrase.

Second suggestion: and do not commit corruption-producing behavior in the earth. This conveys the explanatory role of مفسدین better, but compresses the image of the two Arabic words.

Third suggestion: and in the earth, do not engage in ruinous conduct as corrupters. This preserves the two layers, but it is somewhat heavy in English.

The more suitable choice for the main Forqan text is: and do not engage in corruption and ruin in the earth.

In the linguistic note one can explain: the Arabic text uses both the verb عثو and the description مفسدین, emphasizing ruin-producing and corruption-spreading conduct.

Short definition for Forqan

Engaging in corruption and disturbing an existing order and soundness, in a way that harms people's rights and the foundations of collective life.

16. Final Definition of the Qur'anic Form

تَعْثَوْا

Do not engage in corruption-producing behavior in the earth, and do not spread ruin by disturbing right, equity, and sound order.

17. Conclusion

The root ع ث و in the Qur'an is rare but highly coherent in structure and context. It appears five times and only in the plural prohibitive form تَعْثَوْا, and in every case it is accompanied by فِي الْأَرْضِ مُفْسِدِينَ.

Qur'anic usage shows that عثو is corruption-producing behavior with social consequences; it is prohibited after access to water, food, land, and building power; in Shu'ayb's speech it is connected with measure, weight, equity, and people's rights; in Al-Ankabut 29:36 it is joined with worship of The God and regard for the Last Day; it is not restricted to one particular crime; مُفْسِدِينَ clarifies and emphasizes the corruption-producing direction of the behavior; the root itself does not determine whether the corruption is hidden or open; weakening the foundation of trust and justice is an understandable outcome of عثو, not its certain material meaning; the link between عثو and عُثّه or moth-eating is not derivationally established; and the difference between عثو and عیث should be stated cautiously as a tendency.

The final definition of the root is: ع ث و denotes engaging in corruption and spreading ruin in the earth; behavior that harms sound order, people's rights, responsible use of blessing, and the foundations of social soundness. The form and range of this corruption are known from the context of each verse.
