Core meaning: the upper arm, which contributes to the strength and stability of arm movement. By extension, a helper, supporter, or source of strength for a person or group. Other branches concern supporting parts of structures and cutting trees or their branches.

1. The root and its meaning in early Arabic

The triliteral root ع ض د is associated in its primary bodily use with عَضُد, the upper arm. This is the part of the human arm between the elbow and shoulder, involved in movement, lifting, carrying, and exerting force.

In early Arabic, عَضُد names this body part. Dictionaries also record vocalizations such as عُضُد, عَضْد, and عُضْد. In their primary use, these variants name the same limb.

Because the upper arm contributes to the strength and stability of hand movement, Arabic also uses the word for supporting strength and a person who provides assistance.

For example, فُلَانٌ عَضُدِي does not mean that someone has become part of the speaker's body. It means that he is the speaker's helper and support.

عَضَدَهُ and عَاضَدَهُ are also used for helping and strengthening someone. اعْتَضَدَ بِفُلَانٍ means to seek help and gain strength through another person.

These uses transfer the image of the arm to human relationships. Just as the upper arm strengthens the hand in its work, someone can increase another person's capacity through companionship and assistance.

Assistance and companionship should not, however, be restricted to a single form. Help may involve physical strength, knowledge, speech, defense, advice, or providing resources.

أَعْضَاد also denotes lateral and supporting parts of certain structures, including the sides of a watering trough or frameworks that help hold a structure together.

In another branch, عَضَدَ الشَّجَرَ means to cut trees or their branches, while مِعْضَد names a cutting implement used for this work.

Some of these uses may be associated with an arm, a branch, or a projecting part of a larger whole. Such resemblance alone does not establish that every meaning developed from one historical image.

A precise account of ع ض د must therefore distinguish its attested branches. For its Quranic uses, عَضُد means the upper arm and, by extension, a helper or support that strengthens someone.

2. Early poetic evidence and attribution

One important early example is a verse attributed in some collections to Tarafah ibn al-Abd and in other transmissions to Aws ibn Hajar:

أَبَنِي لُبَيْنَى لَسْتُمُ بِيَدٍ
إِلَّا يَدًا لَيْسَتْ لَهَا عَضُدُ

a-banī Lubaynā lastumu bi-yadin / illā yadan laysat lahā ʿaḍudu

“Sons of Lubayna, you are no hand, except a hand that has no upper arm.”

The poet compares a group of people to a hand without an upper arm.

The image emphasizes weakness and lack of support. A hand detached from its arm loses its normal strength and function. The poet uses this image to describe the weakness of those addressed.

عَضُد still names a body part here, but the relationship between arm and hand expresses the weakness or strength of a human group.

The verse thus attests more than the word “arm”: it illustrates its figurative use to explain strength and support.

Early sources also transmit variants of the second half of the verse. These textual and attribution differences prevent certainty about every detail or its author. Both poets to whom it is attributed belong to the pre-Islamic period, and the image is attested in early sources.

Another example occurs in a poem attributed to Labid ibn Rabi'ah:

رَاسِخُ الدِّمْنِ عَلَى أَعْضَادِهِ
ثَلَمَتْهُ كُلُّ رِيحٍ وَسَبَلْ

rāsikhu al-dimni ʿalā aʿḍādihi / thalamathu kullu rīḥin wa-sabal

“Old deposits are firmly lodged along its sides; every wind and rainfall has breached it.”

The poet describes a watering trough long removed from regular use and maintenance.

أَعْضَاد refers to the sides of the trough: parts forming or supporting its body and edges.

This use shows the extension of the name of the upper arm to parts of material structures.

Just as the arm has a structural role in the body, the sides of a trough belong to its structure.

This does not make every side or wall an عَضُد. Such structural uses are attested in particular examples, and their scope must be determined from evidence.

Labid lived both before and after the advent of Islam. The date of this particular verse has not been independently established in this study. It is evidence for early Arabic, but does not by itself establish composition before the Quran's revelation.

These examples illuminate two uses: the arm as a body part and image of strength, and parts contributing to the structure of a constructed object.

3. Semantic branches and lexical forms

Important forms include عَضُد, أَعْضَاد, عَضَدَ, يَعْضُدُ, عَاضَدَ, مُعَاضَدَة, اعْتَضَدَ, عَاضِد, عَضِيد, عِضَادَة, and مِعْضَد.

These forms do not all have the same meaning.

The upper arm and body parts

عَضُد names the upper arm, between elbow and shoulder.

أَعْضَاد is its plural.

عِضَاد and مِعْضَد can also name something fastened or worn on the upper arm, such as an armlet.

This branch concerns the body part and objects associated with it.

عَضُد sometimes occurs alongside سَاعِد. Although their broader uses can overlap, anatomical terminology distinguishes عَضُد, between shoulder and elbow, from سَاعِد, normally between elbow and wrist.

This distinction helps clarify the root's bodily image.

Assistance and strengthening

In the branch concerning assistance, عَضَدَ فُلَانًا means to help and strengthen someone.

عَاضَدَهُ can likewise express support and cooperation in a task.

مُعَاضَدَة names this cooperation and support.

اعْتَضَدَ بِفُلَانٍ means that someone relied on another person's help and gained strength through it.

Here the arm becomes an image of accompanying and supporting strength.

The Form III pattern of عَاضَدَ does not require equal assistance in both directions in every situation. The extent and direction of assistance depend on syntax and context.

Lateral and supporting parts

أَعْضَادُ الْحَوْضِ is attested for the sides and structural parts of a watering trough.

عِضَادَتَا الْبَابِ names the two lateral members of a doorframe.

These uses concern the lateral and supporting components of a structure.

“Side” alone is not a complete definition, however: it cannot explain the Quranic sense of helper or supporter without accounting for the semantic connection.

Cutting trees and branches

In classical Arabic, عَضَدَ الشَّجَرَ is used for cutting trees or their branches.

عَضِيد and مَعْضُود may describe a branch or tree that has been cut.

مِعْضَد and مِعْضَاد also name implements for cutting branches.

Cutting and severing are central in this branch.

It should not be equated with helping: cutting a branch separates part of the plant, rather than necessarily supporting or strengthening it.

Different branches within a lexical family do not justify an excessively general definition such as “affecting the surroundings of things.” That formulation is neither precise nor distinctive.

Disease or injury of the upper arm

عَضَد, with a short a after ض, also names a disease or injury affecting a camel's upper arm in the dictionaries.

This use occurs in a poem attributed to al-Nabighah al-Dhubyani:

شَكَّ الْفَرِيصَةَ بِالْمِدْرَى فَأَنْفَذَهَا
شَكَّ الْمُبَيْطِرِ إِذْ يَشْفِي مِنَ الْعَضَدِ

shakka al-farīṣata bi-al-midrā fa-anfadhahā / shakka al-mubayṭiri idh yashfī mina al-ʿaḍad

“With its pointed horn it pierced the flesh near the shoulder and drove it through, as a veterinarian pierces when treating disease of the upper arm.”

In this image, a wild bull gores a dog with its horn. الْعَضَد names the disease in the comparison with veterinary treatment, not assistance or support.

This use concerns the bodily area affected by the disease. A body part's name can thus underlie the name of an affliction of that part.

The disease sense does not occur in the Quran and should not be imported into the translation of عَضُد there.

4. Quranic forms

The root ع ض د occurs in only two Quranic verses, both using the noun عَضُد.

عَضُدَكَ — Al-Qasas 28:35

This combines عَضُد with the singular second-person suffix كَ: “your arm.”

عَضُدًا — Al-Kahf 18:51

This is the same noun, indefinite and without a pronoun suffix. In context it means a helper or supporter.

The verbs عَضَدَ, عَاضَدَ, and اعْتَضَدَ do not occur in the Quran.

This matters because both verses derive their meaning from the noun عَضُد and the sentence structure, rather than from a verb directly meaning “to help.”

شَدَّ in Al-Qasas 28:35 belongs to ش د د, not ع ض د. Strengthening in this verse is expressed through the combination of two lexical families.

5. سَنَشُدُّ عَضُدَكَ بِأَخِيكَ: strengthening Moses through Aaron

One of the root's two Quranic uses occurs in the story of Moses.

For the mission entrusted to him, Moses asked The God to send his brother Aaron with him.

In Al-Qasas 28:34, Moses explains that Aaron is more eloquent than he is and asks that his brother accompany him as a helper who confirms his words.

The divine response follows:

قَالَ سَنَشُدُّ عَضُدَكَ بِأَخِيكَ وَنَجْعَلُ لَكُمَا سُلْطَانًا فَلَا يَصِلُونَ إِلَيْكُمَا ۚ بِآيَاتِنَا أَنْتُمَا وَمَنِ اتَّبَعَكُمَا الْغَالِبُونَ

qāla sa-nashuddu ʿaḍudaka bi-akhīka wa-najʿalu lakumā sulṭānan fa-lā yaṣilūna ilaykumā bi-āyātinā antumā wa-mani ittabaʿakumā al-ghālibūn

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:35

Al-Qasas 28:35

“He said: We will strengthen your arm through your brother and grant you both authority, so they will not reach you. Through Our signs, you and those who follow you will prevail.”

عَضُدَكَ directly means “your arm,” but سَنَشُدُّ عَضُدَكَ بِأَخِيكَ is a clear figurative expression.

The God is not promising Moses an increase in the physical size or muscular strength of his arm. He promises to strengthen him through his brother's companionship.

Moses bears a mission and responsibility, while Aaron stands alongside him as a helper.

Just as the upper arm supports the hand's capacity to act, Aaron's companionship increases Moses' capacity to fulfill his mission.

The role of سَنَشُدُّ

سَنَشُدُّ belongs to ش د د and concerns making firm and strong. The prefix سَـ marks futurity: “We will strengthen.”

Here The God speaks of strengthening Moses' arm.

Strengthening is thus expressed by the combination of شَدَّ and عَضُد.

عَضُد names the arm and can figuratively suggest support, while the act of strengthening is expressed by سَنَشُدُّ.

This distinction matters in word-by-word translation.

Translating عَضُدَكَ on its own as “your power” removes the image of the arm.

Treating it solely as a physical limb obscures the phrase's figurative meaning.

“We will strengthen your arm through your brother” therefore preserves the wording and conveys the intended sense.

The role of بِأَخِيكَ

The preposition بِـ in بِأَخِيكَ identifies the means or agent of strengthening.

The God says He will strengthen Moses' arm through his brother.

Aaron is thus the means of assisting and strengthening Moses in this construction.

This does not restrict the lexical meaning of عَضُد to a brother or relative.

Moses had requested his brother, and The God identifies that person as the means of strengthening him. Elsewhere in Arabic, someone other than a brother can be a person's عَضُد or support.

Kinship is a feature of Aaron's role here, not a fixed component of the root's meaning.

Aaron's assistance and physical strength

In Al-Qasas 28:34, Moses mentions Aaron's eloquence and asks that he be sent with him to confirm his words.

The assistance sought is therefore not limited to physical strength.

Sometimes a task calls for another person's bodily strength; sometimes it calls for knowledge, skill, or eloquence.

For example, someone may have a truthful claim before a powerful authority yet struggle to express and defend it clearly. A knowledgeable and articulate companion can strengthen that person's capacity.

Such a helper acts as the person's arm, not necessarily by taking over the task, but by strengthening the ability to fulfill it.

Likewise, requesting Aaron does not remove Moses' responsibility. The God gives both a mission and authority, while Moses retains his own role.

Having support does not imply absolute incapacity or transfer one's responsibility to another person.

عَضُد and سُلْطَان

The verse continues:

وَنَجْعَلُ لَكُمَا سُلْطَانًا

wa-najʿalu lakumā sulṭānan

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:35

سُلْطَان is a separate word concerning authority, prevailing power, or proof.

The verse thus mentions two matters: strengthening Moses through Aaron and granting authority to both.

These meanings should not all be folded into عَضُد itself.

عَضُد supplies the image of support and strengthening. Authority, protection from the enemies' reach, and victory are expressed elsewhere in the verse.

It is therefore incorrect to define عَضُد on its own as certain victory or complete immunity from every danger.

6. عَضُدًا in Al-Kahf 18:51

The other Quranic use occurs in Surah Al-Kahf.

The preceding verse, Al-Kahf 18:50, recounts Iblis' disobedience to his Lord and asks how people take him and his offspring as guardians instead of The God, although they are their enemies.

The next verse states:

مَا أَشْهَدْتُهُمْ خَلْقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَلَا خَلْقَ أَنْفُسِهِمْ وَمَا كُنْتُ مُتَّخِذَ الْمُضِلِّينَ عَضُدًا

mā ash-hadtuhum khalqa al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍi wa-lā khalqa anfusihim wa-mā kuntu muttakhidha al-muḍillīna ʿaḍudan

Al-Kahf 18:51

Al-Kahf 18:51

“I did not make them witness the creation of the heavens and earth, nor their own creation; nor would I take those who mislead as My supporting arm.”

عَضُدًا is used figuratively here: a helper or support.

The sentence differs in structure from the account of Moses.

In Al-Qasas 28:35, The God says He will strengthen Moses' arm through Aaron.

In Al-Kahf 18:51, He says He does not take those who mislead as His arm.

One provides support for a human being; the other denies taking those who mislead as support for The God.

This distinction is essential to understanding the root precisely.

مُتَّخِذَ الْمُضِلِّينَ عَضُدًا

مُتَّخِذ belongs to أ خ ذ and means one who takes or appoints something to a particular role.

الْمُضِلِّينَ refers to those who lead others astray.

عَضُدًا expresses the role to which they are not appointed: helper and supporter.

The phrase therefore says that The God does not appoint those who mislead as His helpers or support.

عَضُدًا is singular although الْمُضِلِّينَ is plural.

This does not deny only one supporter while allowing several. Here عَضُد can name the supporting role without specifying the number of persons.

Persian likewise allows “he did not make them his arm” without suggesting that all the people became one body part or one person.

The referent of “those who mislead”

Al-Kahf 18:50 mentions Iblis and his offspring. The nearest antecedent of the pronouns in أَشْهَدْتُهُمْ and أَنْفُسِهِمْ is connected with that context.

At the end of the verse, however, الْمُضِلِّينَ replaces a repeated pronoun.

This identifies them specifically by their activity of misleading.

عَضُد itself does not imply that every supporter misleads. That description comes from الْمُضِلِّينَ.

عَضُد still means helper or supporter; context identifies those excluded from that role.

Does The God need a helper?

For human beings, a helper can compensate for a deficiency or increase someone's capacity.

When The God denies taking those who mislead as His arm, the image does not imply a human arm, a need for assistance, or dependence on another's strength.

The human expression of helper and support serves here to deny participation by, and dependence on, those who mislead.

The preceding statement denies making them witness the creation of the heavens and earth or their own creation.

The verse thus denies the standing and share attributed to beings to whom people assign roles and authority instead of The God.

This also connects with Saba 34:22, which denies the alleged deities independent ownership, participation in the heavens and earth, and a supporting role for The God.

وَمَا كُنْتُ مُتَّخِذَ الْمُضِلِّينَ عَضُدًا should therefore not be reduced to a refusal to use wicked helpers while implying a basic need for assistance in creating the world.

The context concerns The God's sole creative authority and the error of taking others as partners and guardians instead of Him.

That conclusion comes from the verses together, while the lexical meaning of عَضُد remains helper and support.

7. Comparing the two Quranic uses

Al-Qasas 28:35 and Al-Kahf 18:51 use the same word for two different relationships.

In Al-Qasas, Moses is a human being entrusted with a difficult mission. Aaron's companionship increases his capacity to fulfill it.

In Al-Kahf, The God is the Creator of the heavens and earth and denies taking helpers from among those who mislead.

The first use provides support for a human being.

The second denies taking those who mislead as support for The God.

A word's lexical meaning alone does not determine the condition or needs of the sentence's subject.

Saying that a human being “needs another's arm” may express limited capacity or the difficulty of a responsibility.

But the statement that The God does not take those who mislead as His arm does not express such human need. In context, it denies their participation and any dependence on them.

This comparison does not make ع ض د literal in one verse and wholly unrelated in meaning in the other.

Both involve the image of an arm and support. They differ in who receives assistance or has it denied of them, and in the matter addressed by each verse.

8. Comparison with neighboring roots

ع ض د and أ ز ر

These families are closely related in the story of Moses.

In Ta Ha 20:31, Moses asks The God:

اشْدُدْ بِهِ أَزْرِي

ushdud bihi azrī

Ṭā Hā 20:31

“Strengthen my support through him.”

In Al-Qasas 28:35, the response to the request for Aaron's companionship states:

سَنَشُدُّ عَضُدَكَ بِأَخِيكَ

sa-nashuddu ʿaḍudaka bi-akhīka

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:35

“We will strengthen your arm through your brother.”

These expressions are close in their sense of strengthening and assistance.

عَضُد draws on the arm, while أَزْر concerns strength and support and is also explained through the image of the back.

This does not establish that ع ض د always means help from someone's side while أ ز ر always means help from behind.

Such an exact spatial division does not follow from the verses.

Both express strengthening and support, through different images and sentence structures.

ع ض د and ر د أ

In Al-Qasas 28:34, Moses asks that Aaron be sent with him as رِدْءًا.

رِدْء concerns a helper and supporter who accompanies someone and provides assistance.

In the next verse, The God says He will strengthen Moses' arm through Aaron.

The request and response concern the same matter: Aaron's companionship to strengthen Moses.

رِدْء and عَضُد nevertheless have different constructions.

رِدْء in Moses' request describes Aaron's assisting role.

عَضُد in the response is Moses' arm, strengthened through his brother's companionship.

They overlap in assistance but express it from different perspectives.

ع ض د and ع و ن

ع و ن concerns helping and providing assistance.

عَضُد can also figuratively name a helper.

عَضُد names a body part that becomes an image of support and strength, whereas عَوْن expresses assistance more directly.

Someone may ask another person to help with a task; this request can be expressed with ع و ن.

A dependable supporter may figuratively be called that person's “arm.”

This does not establish that عَضُد always means permanent help and عَوْن always temporary help.

The roots do not categorically establish that distinction.

ع ض د and ن ص ر

نَصْر concerns assistance against difficulty, opposition, or an enemy.

عَضُد can likewise concern assistance, but its central image is an arm and support increasing someone's capacity.

Aaron's companionship can strengthen Moses and help him face Pharaoh's opposition.

عَضُد alone does not, however, mean victory over an enemy.

Assistance can also occur where there is no enemy or battle at all.

نصر and عضد are thus close in the field of assistance without being identical in every use.

ع ض د and ش د د

These families occur together in سَنَشُدُّ عَضُدَكَ.

شَدَّ concerns making firm, strengthening, and intensifying.

عَضُد names the upper arm and figuratively denotes support.

One thus expresses the act of strengthening, the other what is strengthened.

Translation should preserve that distinction.

شَدَّ and عَضُد are independent roots. Their combination in a phrase does not transfer the complete meaning of both to either one.

ع ض د and ض د د

Their sound resemblance can cause confusion.

ع ض د concerns the arm, support, and other material uses.

ض د د concerns opposition, contrast, and standing against something.

عَضُد as supporter and ضِدّ as opponent therefore belong to independent roots.

Two shared letters or similar pronunciation do not establish a derivational relationship.

ع ض د and ع ض ض

ع ض ض concerns biting and gripping with the teeth.

ع ض د names the arm in its Quranic use.

Because the roots differ only in their final consonant, morphological and word-by-word study must distinguish them carefully.

Thus عَضَّ, “to bite,” and عَضُد, “arm,” cannot be merged into one definition without evidence.

9. Translation proposals for Forqan

The two Quranic uses of عَضُد should be translated according to their different sentence structures.

عَضُدَكَ — Al-Qasas 28:35

Brief root meaning: Upper arm; figuratively, a helper or support that increases someone's capacity.

Meaning here: Your arm; in “strengthening the arm,” strengthening and supporting Moses.

Suggested translation:

سَنَشُدُّ عَضُدَكَ بِأَخِيكَ

sa-nashuddu ʿaḍudaka bi-akhīka

Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:35

“We will strengthen your arm through your brother.”

This preserves the image of the arm, with strengthening expressed by سَنَشُدُّ.

“We will help you through your brother” conveys the general sense but omits part of the verse's lexical image.

عَضُدًا — Al-Kahf 18:51

Brief root meaning: Upper arm; helper and support.

Meaning here: Helper and supporter; a role denied to those who mislead in this verse.

Suggested translation:

وَمَا كُنْتُ مُتَّخِذَ الْمُضِلِّينَ عَضُدًا

wa-mā kuntu muttakhidha al-muḍillīna ʿaḍudan

Al-Kahf 18:51

“Nor would I take those who mislead as My supporting arm.”

This makes assistance clear while retaining the original image of عضد.

Adding “partners in creation” to the translation of عَضُدًا itself would be inappropriate: that conclusion follows from the whole verse and its context, not the word's direct meaning.

Nor should عَضُدًا be reduced to “power,” since this construction names the role of helper or supporter.

Conclusion

ع ض د is associated with عَضُد, the human upper arm. The limb contributes to the strength and stability of hand movement, and its image supports the use for a helper who strengthens another person.

Early evidence shows the use of the arm to express strength and support, and of the same family for lateral and supporting parts of structures.

Other branches concern cutting trees or branches, implements, and afflictions associated with the arm. These uses should be recognized without being reduced to one historical origin without evidence.

عَضُد occurs twice in the Quran.

In Al-Qasas 28:35, The God promises to strengthen Moses' arm through his brother Aaron. Human companionship and support can strengthen someone through another person's abilities and assistance.

In Al-Kahf 18:51, The God denies taking those who mislead as His supporting arm. The context denies their participation and independent standing in creation; it should not be understood as bodily need or divine dependence.

The primary meaning of ع ض د is “upper arm,” and its established figurative meaning is “a helper or support that increases someone's strength.” Both Quranic uses retain the image of the arm: one strengthens a human being through a helper; the other denies taking those who mislead as support for The God.

Forqan's translation should retain the image of the arm where possible and distinguish strengthening, assistance, authority, and victory to the extent supported by syntax and context.
