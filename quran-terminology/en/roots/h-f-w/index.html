The Qur’anic Root Ḥ F W

Qur’anic and lexical forms

(ḥafiyyun)

(ḥafiyyan)

(fa-yuḥfikum)

From thorough inquiry and insistence to attention, honour, and kindness

In Arabic, Ḥ F W has several semantic branches extending from intense attention and inquiry to kindness and honouring another person. «حَفِيّ» can describe someone who has asked extensively about a subject and become familiar with its details. It can also describe someone who pays particular attention to another and treats that person kindly and generously.

Within this family, iḥfāʾ concerns persistence and insistence in a request. In another use, ḥafāwah concerns attentive care and warmly welcoming someone. Although these meanings appear different, unusual attention and persistence can be seen in the branches concerning inquiry and human conduct. The entire root should nevertheless not be reduced to one meaning without evidence.

In Arabic, the family also concerns being without footwear and the skin of the feet becoming worn through walking. Prevention and complete removal or very close trimming of hair are also recorded. «احْتَفَأَ», meaning uprooting papyrus, contains a hamzah and belongs to the separate family Ḥ F ʾ; it should not be merged with this root.

Ḥ F W occurs three times in the Qur’an. In Al-Aʿrāf 7:187, «حَفِيٌّ» appears in a question about the Hour. In Maryam 19:47, the same word describes the Lord’s kindness and attention toward Abraham. In Muḥammad 47:37, «فَيُحْفِكُمْ» concerns demanding with insistence and pressure.

Qur’anic forms: «حَفِيٌّ» in Al-Aʿrāf 7:187, «حَفِيًّا» in Maryam 19:47, and «فَيُحْفِكُمْ» in Muḥammad 47:37.

Quranic Arabic Corpus: the three occurrences of Ḥ F W and their forms

Other recorded Arabic forms include «حَفَا», «حَفِيَ», «حَافٍ», «حَفَاوَة», «حِفَايَة», «إِحْفَاء», «تَحَفَّى», «احْتَفَى», and «اسْتَحْفَى», but they do not occur in these forms in the Qur’an.

1. Semantic fields and early Arabic usage

In Maqāyīs al-Lughah, under the family ḥafā, Ibn Fāris distinguishes three principles: preventing, pursuing a question exhaustively, and being without footwear.

Maqāyīs al-Lughah: prevention, exhaustive inquiry, and being barefoot

This division shows that neither kindness nor insistence alone can define the root. Each branch has its particular uses, and the historical relationship between all of them is not conclusively established.

Al-ʿAyn also treats forms associated with Ḥ F W and Ḥ F Y alongside one another. This matters for roots with a final weak radical: some derivatives and verbal nouns show wāw, while others show yāʾ.

The yāʾ in ḥafiyy therefore does not by itself justify separating it from the family in which ḥafāwah and iḥfāʾ are discussed. This study gathers the Qur’anic forms under Ḥ F W while preserving differences in form and lexical branch.

Kitāb al-ʿAyn: Ḥ F W, Ḥ F Y, and the separate hamzated Ḥ F ʾ

Ḥafiyy: persistent in inquiry and well informed

In a familiar use, «حَفِيّ» describes someone who has asked extensively about a subject and persistently sought to know it.

Ibn Fāris explains this through «اسْتِقْصَاءُ السُّؤَالِ»: asking and pursuing the inquiry thoroughly.

«اسْتَحْفَى عَنِ الشَّيْءِ» is likewise recorded for thorough inquiry into something.

In this branch, ḥafiyy does not mean simply any knowledgeable person. Knowledge can be acquired in different ways, whereas this use concerns attentive, persistent inquiry.

Some sources accordingly explain ḥafiyy as «عالِمٌ بِالشَّيْءِ», someone knowledgeable about a subject.

Here, however, knowledge can be the outcome of inquiry; the word is not merely a synonym of ʿālim in every use.

This distinction is particularly important in Al-Aʿrāf 7:187.

Ḥafāwah: attending to and honouring another

In another branch, «حَفِيَ بِفُلَانٍ» denotes paying particular attention to someone, treating that person kindly, and attending to their needs.

«تَحَفَّى بِهِ» and «احْتَفَى بِهِ» can likewise concern a warm welcome, honouring someone, and gracious care.

Al-Aṣmaʿī explains ḥafiya bi-fulānin as attending to someone’s needs and treating that person fittingly and generously.

Lisān al-ʿArab also explains ḥafāwah through abundant attention, asking after a person, and honouring them.

Here the person’s circumstances receive attention, which may include asking after them. This semantic closeness alone does not establish the historical direction of development.

For example, someone who warmly receives a guest, asks how they are, and works to meet their needs may be described through this family.

Ḥafāwah does not necessarily require kinship or longstanding friendship: an unfamiliar guest can also receive such care.

Nor does warm behaviour and abundant attention invariably establish inward sincerity. Someone may show attention out of real affection or to obtain an advantage. Intention cannot be determined from the word alone.

Arabic records the proverb «مَأْرَبَةً لَا حَفَاوَةً»: behaviour arising from personal need or advantage rather than sincere affection and concern.

This proverb shows that social usage can distinguish attentive behaviour from the actor’s real motive.

Al-Ṣiḥāḥ: ḥafāwah and the proverb maʾrabatan lā ḥafāwatan

Iḥfāʾ: insistence and exhaustive pursuit

«أَحْفَى فِي السُّؤَالِ» means insisting strongly in questioning or requesting.

Lisān al-ʿArab relates iḥfāʾ to ilḥāf and insistence in a request.

This can range from repeated questioning about a subject to persistently requesting something from another person.

For example, someone who repeatedly questions another and is not satisfied with a short answer may be described as «مُحْفٍ».

Here insistence is not inherently good or bad. Thorough inquiry may help establish the truth, while excessive insistence can exhaust another person or put them under pressure.

The ethical value of iḥfāʾ therefore depends on the request’s subject, the degree of insistence, and the other person’s circumstances, rather than on the root alone.

In Muḥammad 47:37, this branch concerns demanding wealth.

Ḥafiya and ḥāfin: being without footwear

In Arabic, «حَافٍ» describes someone walking without shoes or other footwear.

«حَفِيَتِ الْقَدَمُ» is also recorded for a foot whose skin has become thin or damaged through extensive walking.

Al-ʿAyn distinguishes walking without footwear from the foot becoming worn through walking.

The conditions can be related but are not identical. Someone may walk barefoot without yet injuring their feet, while prolonged walking can wear down the sole or its protective surface.

«حَفِيَ الْفَرَسُ» is used of a horse whose hoof has become worn through walking.

This material use does not occur in the Qur’an but matters for understanding the broader lexical family.

Thorough removal and taking away

Arabic records «أَحْفَى شَارِبَهُ» for trimming a moustache very short or close to its roots.

In this use, trimming continues close to the skin.

Other uses of iḥfāʾ likewise concern removing or reducing something extensively.

A resemblance can be seen between trimming hair thoroughly and pursuing a request exhaustively. That resemblance does not by itself prove that either use historically developed from the other.

A semantic explanation should be distinguished from a historical claim about a word’s origin.

Ḥafw: preventing

Ibn Fāris records «حَفَوْتُ الرَّجُلَ مِنْ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ» for depriving a person of something or withholding it from him.

Lisān al-ʿArab likewise records «حَفَاهُ مِنْ كُلِّ خَيْرٍ» for withholding good from a person.

Alongside this, ḥafāhu is also reported with the meaning of giving.

These different uses show that a single meaning should not be extracted from a form without examining its construction and context.

The prevention branch is not directly present in the three Qur’anic occurrences. Thus ḥafiyy in Al-Aʿrāf or Maryam should not be translated as depriving or preventing.

Lisān al-ʿArab: prevention, giving, hair, attention, and insistence

2. Early poetic witnesses: inquiry and worn feet

A poem attributed to Al-Aʿshā includes the following:

فَإِنْ تَسْأَلِي عَنِّي فَيَا رُبَّ سَائِلٍ
حَفِيٍّ عَنِ الْأَعْشَى بِهِ حَيْثُ أَصْعَدَا

fa-in tasʾalī ʿannī fa-yā rubba sāʾilin / ḥafiyyin ʿani al-aʿshā bihi ḥaythu aṣʿadā

Meaning: “If you ask about me, how many an inquirer has persistently asked about Al-Aʿshā and where he has gone.”

Ḥafiyy here describes someone persistent in questioning and seeking news.

In this verse, ʿani al-aʿshā identifies the subject of inquiry, but its dependence on ḥafiyy is not certain. In Sharḥ Abyāt Mughnī al-Labīb, Al-Baghdādī attaches ʿan to sāʾil and bihi to ḥafiyy.

The verse is therefore a useful witness for persistent inquiry, but does not alone prove an identical grammatical relationship to «كَأَنَّكَ حَفِيٌّ عَنْهَا» in Al-Aʿrāf 7:187.

Al-Baghdādī: ʿan attached to sāʾil and bihi to ḥafiyy in Al-Aʿshā’s witness

Another part of the same poem reads:

فَآلَيْتُ لَا أَرْثِي لَهَا مِنْ كَلَالَةٍ
وَلَا مِنْ حَفًى حَتَّى تُلَاقِيَ مُحَمَّدَا

fa-ālaytu lā arthī lahā min kalālatin / wa-lā min ḥafan ḥattā tulāqiya muḥammadā

Meaning: “I swore not to pity her for fatigue or her worn feet, until she reaches Muḥammad.”

Here ḥafan concerns the animal’s feet becoming worn through prolonged travel.

The same poem thus contains two different uses of this family: ḥafiyy for thorough inquiry and ḥafan for wear caused by travel.

This difference confirms that one fixed translation cannot explain every use of the root.

The poem appears in Al-Aʿshā’s collected poetry and literary accounts of the early Islamic period. Since this passage itself mentions travelling to Muḥammad, it should not be presented as certainly predating Qur’anic revelation without investigating its date. These uses nevertheless matter as literary Arabic associated with the period of revelation.

Al-ʿAyn: Al-Aʿshā’s footsoreness witness and the first verse’s variant wording

Lexical accounts also record taḥaffā bihi for warmly receiving and caring for a guest. Such uses show the branch of attention and honouring; they need not all be restricted to scholarly inquiry.

3. «كَأَنَّكَ حَفِيٌّ عَنْهَا»: Al-Aʿrāf 7:187

يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ السَّاعَةِ أَيَّانَ مُرْسَاهَا قُلْ إِنَّمَا عِلْمُهَا عِنْدَ رَبِّي لَا يُجَلِّيهَا لِوَقْتِهَا إِلَّا هُوَ ثَقُلَتْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ لَا تَأْتِيكُمْ إِلَّا بَغْتَةً يَسْأَلُونَكَ كَأَنَّكَ حَفِيٌّ عَنْهَا قُلْ إِنَّمَا عِلْمُهَا عِنْدَ اللَّهِ وَلَكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ النَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

yasʾalūnaka ʿani al-sāʿati ayyāna mursāhā qul innamā ʿilmuhā ʿinda rabbī lā yujallīhā li-waqtihā illā huwa thaqulat fī al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi lā taʾtīkum illā baghtatan yasʾalūnaka ka-annaka ḥafiyyun ʿanhā qul innamā ʿilmuhā ʿinda allāhi wa-lākinna akthara al-nāsi lā yaʿlamūna

Translation: They ask you about the Hour, when it will occur. Say: “Knowledge of it belongs only to my Lord. None but He reveals it at its time. It weighs heavily in the heavens and the earth; it comes to you only suddenly.” They ask you as though you were fully informed about it. Say: “Knowledge of it belongs only to The God, but most people do not know.”

Al-Aʿrāf 7:187

Here ḥafiyyun describes Muḥammad as imagined by the questioners.

They ask when the Hour will arise, continuing their questions as though Muḥammad possessed precise knowledge of it.

The meaning of ḥafiyy in this context

Given the Arabic use of ḥafiyy for a persistent inquirer, ḥafiyyun ʿanhā can describe someone who has asked and investigated extensively and become familiar with the details.

In this reading, the questioners treat Muḥammad as though he knew the time of the Hour.

This fits the surrounding clauses: the verse twice stresses that knowledge of the Hour is with The God.

“As though you were fully informed about it” is therefore an appropriate contextual rendering.

Ḥafiyy should not, however, be treated throughout Arabic as simply equivalent to ʿālim. Thorough knowledge associated with persistent inquiry is one familiar use of the root relevant here.

Another reading: as though close to the questioners

Early commentaries also transmit another reading.

According to it, the questioners behave as though Muḥammad has a close, friendly relationship with them and should therefore give them special information about the Hour.

Here ḥafiyy is understood as kind and attentive toward a person.

Some reports associate this reading with Quraysh asking Muḥammad to disclose special knowledge of the Resurrection’s time because of their kinship.

These historical details are not stated in the verse itself, however, and should not enter the translation as the direct meaning of ḥafiyy.

The verse has ḥafiyyun ʿanhā, not ḥafiyyun bihim. Nevertheless, that difference alone cannot dismiss the second reading: its proponents attach ʿanhā to yasʾalūnaka and understand an omitted bihim with ḥafiyy.

The pronoun hā in ʿanhā refers to the Hour. The first reading connects the phrase with ḥafiyy to foreground inquiry about it; the second attaches it to the questioning. Preference for the first reading should rest on the construction and context as a whole, rather than the mere presence of ʿan.

After considering both views, Al-Ṭabarī likewise prefers extensive inquiry about the Hour and knowledge of it.

Al-Ṭabarī, Al-Aʿrāf 7:187: both readings and preference for inquiry about the Hour

Thorough inquiry and knowledge of the unseen

A key point is that asking extensively about a subject does not necessarily make its answer accessible.

People can repeatedly ask about something, but repetition cannot turn their questions into knowledge if the necessary information has not been made available to them.

Here Muḥammad is instructed to say that knowledge of the Hour belongs only to The God.

Ḥafiyy should therefore not be understood as someone empowered to obtain every hidden truth through personal effort.

Nor does someone’s religious or social standing by itself establish that they must know every unseen matter, even if others assume so.

In this context, the questioners expect knowledge from Muḥammad which the verse reserves to The God.

This epistemic conclusion comes from the verse as a whole; ḥafiyy itself means neither possessing nor lacking knowledge of the unseen.

4. «إِنَّهُ كَانَ بِي حَفِيًّا»: Maryam 19:47

قَالَ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكَ سَأَسْتَغْفِرُ لَكَ رَبِّي إِنَّهُ كَانَ بِي حَفِيًّا

qāla salāmun ʿalayka sa-astaghfiru laka rabbī innahu kāna bī ḥafiyyan

Translation: He said: “Peace be upon you. I shall ask my Lord to forgive you; He has always been kind to me.”

Maryam 19:47

Ḥafiyyan occurs here too, but its context differs from Al-Aʿrāf 7:187.

The speaker is Abraham, who responds calmly after hearing his father’s threat and promises to ask his Lord to forgive him.

He then says of his Lord: «إِنَّهُ كَانَ بِي حَفِيًّا».

Particular attention and kindness

Here ḥafiyy describes treating another with attention, kindness, and gracious care.

Bī complements this description, showing that Abraham speaks of his Lord’s relationship with him.

Arabic ḥafiya bi-fulānin can describe someone attending to another’s needs and treating them generously.

“Has been kind to me” or “has treated me with gracious attention” therefore fits this verse.

Ḥafiyy does not itself mean mercy in its broadest sense. Raḥmah belongs to a separate lexical family and has a broader semantic field.

Ḥafiyy foregrounds attention and care toward someone: that person receives particular concern and attendance.

Does ḥafiyy mean every prayer is answered?

Some commentarial explanations connect the expression with The God’s kindness in answering Abraham’s prayer.

That is understandable alongside Abraham’s mention of seeking forgiveness and prayer, but should not become the lexical meaning of ḥafiyy itself.

The word alone does not guarantee acceptance of every particular request, without conditions and at every time.

In Maryam 19:47 Abraham speaks of his Lord’s kindness toward him, and in Maryam 19:48 he expresses hope concerning his prayer.

Al-Tawbah 9:114 separately explains Abraham’s asking forgiveness for his father, its reason, and its limits.

Ḥafiyy therefore does not establish that the request for his father’s forgiveness was necessarily accepted. That issue requires the related verses to be considered together.

Al-Ṭabarī, Maryam 19:47: the Lord’s kindness and Abraham’s prayer

A divine description and the word’s human uses

In Arabic, ḥafiyy can also describe an attentive, kind human being.

When used of The God, the meaning of gracious attention remains, but human behaviour’s bodily and psychological features should not all be attributed to Him.

For example, people may attend to someone out of worry, need, loneliness, or fear of losing them. Such motives do not follow from the divine description ḥafiyy.

Nor does The God’s kindness here mean that He was unaware of Abraham’s circumstances and learned about them by asking.

Human attention sometimes involves asking after someone; the divine use concerns gracious care, rather than acquiring new knowledge.

The two uses of ḥafiyy in Al-Aʿrāf and Maryam

In Al-Aʿrāf 7:187, ḥafiyyun ʿanhā concerns precise inquiry and knowledge about a subject.

In Maryam 19:47, ḥafiyyan occurs with bi- for the Lord’s relationship with Abraham.

Both involve particular attention and care, but their objects differ.

In Al-Aʿrāf, the object is something being investigated; in Maryam, it is a person receiving kindness.

The preposition and context therefore matter decisively for translation.

Ḥafiyy in Al-Aʿrāf cannot simply be rendered “kind,” just as in Maryam it cannot mean “asking extensively” or “well informed about the details.”

5. «فَيُحْفِكُمْ»: Muḥammad 47:37

إِنْ يَسْأَلْكُمُوهَا فَيُحْفِكُمْ تَبْخَلُوا وَيُخْرِجْ أَضْغَانَكُمْ

in yasʾalkumūhā fa-yuḥfikum tabkhalū wa-yukhrij aḍghānakum

Translation: If He asks you for it and presses you insistently, you will be miserly, and He will bring out your hidden resentments.

Muḥammad 47:37

Fa-yuḥfikum is a form IV imperfect verb, coordinated with yasʾalkumūhā in the condition. It is jussive, marked by deletion of its final weak letter. Kum is the object pronoun, and the understood subject refers to the Lord mentioned in the context.

Here iḥfāʾ concerns a request accompanied by insistence and pressure.

Insistence in demanding wealth

Muḥammad 47:36 discusses worldly life and the reward of faith and mindfulness. It then states that The God does not demand the addressees’ wealth from them.

Muḥammad 47:37 continues conditionally: if their wealth is demanded with insistence and pressure, their miserliness becomes apparent and their hidden resentments emerge.

In this context, fa-yuḥfikum is more than ordinary asking.

A request involving iḥfāʾ goes beyond expressing a need or wish and becomes emphatic pursuit and pressure that burdens the other party.

Renderings such as “presses you insistently” or “puts pressure on you in demanding it” therefore fit the context.

The verb itself, however, does not mean taking all wealth. Some commentarial explanations infer that from the demand’s severity and its financial subject; iḥfāʾ does not mean demanding everything in all its uses.

Al-Ṭabarī, Muḥammad 47:37: pressing a demand for wealth and bringing out resentment

Is iḥfāʾ always an improper demand?

Here iḥfāʾ appears in a hypothetical situation resulting in miserliness and the disclosure of hidden resentments.

This use does not establish that every emphatic request in Arabic is improper.

Persistent inquiry to clarify a truth may be fitting, whereas excessive pressure to obtain another person’s wealth may have harmful consequences.

Nor does the verse say that requesting wealth is wrong in every amount and circumstance. Muḥammad 47:38 describes an invitation to spend in The God’s way.

An invitation to spend, demanding wealth, and demanding with great pressure and insistence should therefore be distinguished.

Iḥfāʾ contributes the intensity of the request, rather than specifying every financial and social rule.

Iḥfāʾ and the disclosure of resentment

The rest of the verse states that hidden resentments are brought out in this situation.

This result is expressed through the condition and the addressees’ behaviour as a whole.

Iḥfāʾ itself does not mean disclosing resentment. What is brought out is separately expressed through «وَيُخْرِجْ أَضْغَانَكُمْ».

Al-Durr al-Maṣūn: fa-yuḥfikum coordinated with the conditional verb and active yukhrij

Three matters should therefore remain distinct: demanding wealth, pressure and insistence in the demand, and the addressees’ response.

This distinction shows that Ḥ F W concerns the manner of requesting here, rather than miserliness, resentment, or every psychological consequence.

6. Distinctions from related roots and translation proposals

Ḥ F W (ḥafā) and S ʾ L (saʾala)

Suʾāl concerns asking questions and making requests.

In the inquiry branch, iḥfāʾ emphasises persistence and insistence in that activity.

Al-Aʿrāf 7:187 contains both yasʾalūnaka and ḥafiyyun. The questioners ask about the Hour as though Muḥammad had himself persistently sought knowledge of it and knew its details.

Muḥammad 47:37 likewise places yasʾalkumūhā beside fa-yuḥfikum. The first expresses asking, while the second adds its intensity and insistence.

These occurrences show the closeness of suʾāl and iḥfāʾ without making their contributions identical.

Study of S ʾ L

Ḥ F W (ḥafā) and L Ḥ F (laḥafa)

Ilḥāf occurs in Al-Baqarah 2:273 in «لَا يَسْأَلُونَ النَّاسَ إِلْحَافًا».

It concerns insistence and persistence in a request.

Lexical sources sometimes explain iḥfāʾ and ilḥāf through each other, since both can denote an intense, repeated request.

This semantic closeness does not make the roots identical.

Ilḥāf comes from L Ḥ F, while iḥfāʾ belongs to Ḥ F W.

Muḥammad 47:37 uses fa-yuḥfikum, which must be understood through its own form and context. Al-Baqarah 2:273 concerns insistence in asking people.

The verses illuminate a shared field of insistence while preserving the difference between the words.

Ḥ F W (ḥafā) and L Ṭ F (laṭafa)

L Ṭ F concerns gentleness, subtlety, and kindly treatment.

Ḥafiyy in Maryam 19:47 can likewise be translated as “kind” or “graciously attentive.”

However, ḥafiyy in Al-Aʿrāf 7:187 concerns thorough inquiry, while the verb from the same family in Muḥammad 47:37 expresses strong insistence in demanding.

Kindness may therefore suit one use of ḥafiyy, but does not define the whole family of Ḥ F W.

Ḥ F W (ḥafā) and K R M (karama)

Karam concerns generosity, nobility, and worth.

In human conduct, ḥafāwah can likewise accompany honouring a guest and generous treatment.

Ḥafāwah, however, foregrounds particular attention and warm care, while karam has a broader field encompassing giving, standing, and other qualities.

Nor does particular attention to someone always involve giving money.

This matters in Maryam 19:47. Ḥafiyy describes the Lord’s gracious attention toward Abraham and should not be confined to one kind of material gift.

Ḥ F W (ḥafā) and Kh F Y (khafiya)

Kh F Y is a separate root concerning hiddenness and not being apparent.

In Maryam 19:3, khafiyyan occurs in «نِدَاءً خَفِيًّا», describing a hidden or quiet call.

Ḥafiyyan in Maryam 19:47, however, belongs to Ḥ F W and concerns attention and kindness.

These words differ in writing only in their first letter, but have distinct semantic fields.

Ḥafiyy therefore does not mean hidden, nor khafiyy kind. Root assignment and translation must preserve the distinction.

Ḥ F W (ḥafā) and Ḥ F F (ḥaffa)

In Qur’anic usage, Ḥ F F concerns surrounding and being situated around something.

In Al-Kahf 18:32, palms are placed around two gardens; in Al-Zumar 39:75, angels are described around the Throne.

Ḥ F W, however, concerns thorough inquiry, kindness, and insistence in demanding in the Qur’an.

These families share their first two letters, but differ in their final radical and uses.

Ḥafiyy in Maryam therefore cannot mean someone who has gathered in a ring around another. Attention and kindness have their own meaning here.

Study of Ḥ F F

Translation proposals for Forqan

In Al-Aʿrāf 7:187, «كَأَنَّكَ حَفِيٌّ عَنْهَا» can be rendered:

“As though you were fully informed about it.”

This fits the context and the repeated statement that knowledge of the Hour belongs exclusively to The God.

A lexical explanation may add that ḥafiyy here describes someone who has inquired attentively and persistently about a subject.

“As though you had asked extensively about it” is also close to the lexical image, but in Persian may leave the intended consequence, presumed thorough knowledge, insufficiently clear.

In Maryam 19:47, «إِنَّهُ كَانَ بِي حَفِيًّا» can be translated:

“He has always been kind to me.”

“He has always shown me gracious, particular attention” likewise makes the familiar meaning clearer.

“Asking extensively” or “well informed about the details” does not suit this setting: the subject is the Lord’s gracious care toward Abraham.

In Muḥammad 47:37, fa-yuḥfikum denotes insistence and pressure in a request.

“And presses you insistently” or “and puts pressure on you in demanding it” suits this verb.

For the full verse: “If He asks you for your wealth and presses you insistently in demanding it, you will be miserly, and He will bring out your hidden resentments” makes the sentence clear.

However, “your wealth” comes from the context and the pronoun referring back to the preceding verse, rather than from yuḥfikum itself.

Outside the Qur’an, ḥafāwah can be rendered “warm attention and honouring”; iḥtafā bihi, “welcomed him attentively and respectfully”; istaḥfā ʿani al-amr, “inquired thoroughly into the matter”; and ḥāfin, “without footwear” or “barefoot.”

Conclusion

Ḥ F W has several Arabic semantic branches. Ibn Fāris identifies preventing, exhaustive inquiry, and being without footwear as principles of this family. Other sources record kindness, honouring, attending to another’s needs, insistence in requesting, and thorough removal.

Its three Qur’anic occurrences fall within the field of attention, inquiry, and emphatic requesting.

In Al-Aʿrāf 7:187, ḥafiyyun ʿanhā concerns someone supposedly fully informed through persistent inquiry. In Maryam 19:47, ḥafiyyan describes the Lord’s kindness toward Abraham. In Muḥammad 47:37, fa-yuḥfikum concerns insistence and pressure in demanding.

These uses can be related through unusual attention and persistence, but this feature should not become a vague, compulsory definition of every branch.

In inquiry, intense attention can lead to thorough investigation and detailed knowledge. In human relationships, it can accompany kindness, honouring, and care. In demanding, persistence can become insistence and pressure on the other party.

Being without footwear, worn feet, and prevention are separate Arabic uses, and their historical relationship with inquiry and kindness has not been conclusively established.

Brief root meaning: Close attention and persistent pursuit; in Qur’anic usage, thorough knowledge, gracious kindness, or insistence in demanding, according to construction and context.

Preposition and context matter decisively in translation: ḥafiyyun ʿanhā concerns a subject; ḥafiyyan bī concerns gracious care toward a person; and yuḥfikum concerns pressure and insistence in requesting.

Preserving these differences prevents every occurrence from being confined to “kind,” “knowledgeable,” or “insistent.” Each derivative must be understood through Arabic usage and its position in the verse.
