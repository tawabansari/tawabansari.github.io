Qur'anic Root Q TH A

A specific edible fruit or vegetable similar to cucumber.

1. Short Introduction to the Root

The root ق ث أ in Arabic and Qur'anic usage points to a kind of edible fruit or vegetable close to cucumber or similar plants.

This root points to one specific instance of earth-grown produce, not to a general category of plants. Therefore, unlike some roots that carry a broad meaning, ق ث أ relates more to one particular kind of food.

The semantic core of the root can be stated as follows:

ق ث أ denotes a kind of edible fruit or vegetable similar to cucumber.

2. Important Forms and Derivatives of the Root

قِثَّاء

A kind of edible fruit or vegetable similar to cucumber.

قِثَّائِهَا

Its cucumbers, or similar fruits, that grow from the earth.

3. Reference Cards for Qur'anic Derivatives

قِثَّاء

An edible fruit or vegetable similar to cucumber.

قِثَّائِهَا

The cucumbers or similar fruits of a land or earth.

4. The Semantic Core of the Root

The core of this root rests on one main element:

pointing to one specific kind of plant-based food;

similarity to cucumber or related plants;

being placed among edible products of the earth.

Unlike roots such as ب ق ل, this root does not have the broad meaning of greens, but points to a specific instance.

5. The Material Image of the Root

The material image of this root is an elongated and watery fruit that grows from the earth and is consumed fresh.

This image includes features such as:

freshness and wateriness;

direct consumption as food;

growth in the earth as one of the agricultural products.

6. Use of the Root in Arabic

In Arabic, قِثَّاء refers to a kind of cucumber or similar plant that is consumed as food.

This word points specifically to one agricultural product and stands in contrast to broader words such as بقل.

7. Qur'anic Use of the Root

وَإِذْ قُلْتُمْ يَا مُوسَىٰ لَنْ نَصْبِرَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامٍ وَاحِدٍ فَادْعُ لَنَا رَبَّكَ يُخْرِجْ لَنَا مِمَّا تُنْبِتُ الْأَرْضُ مِنْ بَقْلِهَا وَقِثَّائِهَا وَفُومِهَا وَعَدَسِهَا وَبَصَلِهَا

And when you said: O Moses, we will not be patient with one kind of food; so call upon your Lord for us, that He may bring out for us from what the earth grows: its greens, its cucumbers, its garlic or wheat, its lentils, and its onions.

Al-Baqarah 2:61

In this verse, قِثَّائِهَا appears alongside other products of the earth and points specifically to cucumber or a similar fruit.

This usage shows that قثاء is one specific instance of earthly foods, mentioned alongside other products such as greens, lentils, and onions.

8. Semantic Notes

قثاء points to one specific kind of plant-based food, not to a general category;

the word is close to cucumber or similar plants;

in Qur'anic usage, it appears beside other products of the earth and shows part of the variety of foods.

9. Conclusion

The root ق ث أ in the Qur'an points to a kind of edible fruit or vegetable similar to cucumber, which grows from the earth and is consumed directly.

The final definition of the root is:

ق ث أ denotes a kind of edible fruit or vegetable similar to cucumber.
