  <section class="article-section" id="introduction">
    <h2>Introduction: relocating a Qur’anic word</h2>
    <p>One of the hidden distortions in understanding the Qur’an is to pull a Qur’anic word out of its proper place, place it somewhere else, and then use that relocation to change the meaning of the verses. In the discussion of obedience to the Messenger, one of the most important words that has suffered this fate is <span class="term">al-mubin</span>.</p>
    <p>The Qur’an repeatedly describes itself as <span class="term">kitab mubin</span>, a clear Book, and <span class="term">Qur’an mubin</span>, a clear Qur’an. In other words, <span class="term">mubin</span> is one of the central descriptions of the revealed revelation inside the Qur’an itself: a Book that is clear and clarifying, free from obscurity in itself, and able to separate the boundary between truth and falsehood for the human being. In the raw root study of <span class="term">b-y-n</span>, the same point was present: this root is connected with separation, the disclosure of boundaries, expression, proof, clarification, and making meaning clear through distinction. Clarity in this root is not merely light falling upon something; it is the separation of truth from falsehood, maturity from misguidance, revelation from conjecture, and clear message from confused speech.</p>
    <p>But the distortion began when <span class="term">al-mubin</span> was separated from its Qur’anic place, namely the <span class="term">clear Book</span> and the <span class="term">clear Qur’an</span>, and was placed in later systems of naming and religion-making in a way that weakened its connection to the Qur’an.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="al-haqq-al-mubin">
    <h2>Al-Haqq al-Mubin and the place of the description al-mubin</h2>
    <p>Regarding God, the Qur’an uses the precise and very elevated expression <span class="term">al-Haqq al-Mubin</span>, not <span class="term">al-mubin</span> independently and apart from <span class="term">al-Haqq</span> in that same context. The verse says:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">يَوْمَئِذٍ يُوَفِّيهِمُ اللَّهُ دِينَهُمُ الْحَقَّ وَيَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ الْحَقُّ الْمُبِينُ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/024-an-nur/025/">An-Nur 24:25</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>On that Day, God will pay them their true due in full, and they will know that God is the clear Truth.</p>
    <p>In this verse, <span class="term">al-mubin</span> describes <span class="term">al-Haqq</span>; God is the manifest and clarifying Truth. But when we come to the Qur’an and the Book, the expressions <span class="term">clear Book</span> and <span class="term">clear Qur’an</span> are repeated directly many times. This distinction matters. If this distinction is ignored, <span class="term">al-mubin</span> is separated from its revelatory place. Then, when the Qur’an says that the Messenger’s duty is <span class="term">al-balag al-mubin</span>, the mind no longer returns to the clear Qur’an itself. Instead, the way is opened for the “delivery of the Messenger” to be transferred to external, later, speculative, and disputed reports.</p>
    <p>This is the dangerous point. If the Messenger is commanded to deliver the <span class="term">clear delivery</span>, and the Qur’an calls itself the <span class="term">clear Book</span> and the <span class="term">clear Qur’an</span>, then the clearest Qur’anic understanding is that the Messenger was commanded to deliver this very <span class="term">mubin</span>: the clear Book, the clear Qur’an, the Criterion, and the Reminder. But if <span class="term">al-mubin</span> is separated from the Qur’an, the verses “obey God and obey the Messenger” are easily pulled away from obedience to God’s clear message and toward obedience to later reports. As a result, Bukhari, Muslim, the jurist, the hadith scholar, the school, occasions of revelation, Isra’iliyyat, and parallel legal systems sit in the position of explaining, completing, specifying, abrogating, and even ruling over the Qur’an.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="obedience-and-balag">
    <h2>The obedience verses and the boundary of clear delivery</h2>
    <p>First, the obedience verses must be read together with their endings. The Qur’an says in Surat an-Nur:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">قُلْ أَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ ۖ فَإِن تَوَلَّوْا فَإِنَّمَا عَلَيْهِ مَا حُمِّلَ وَعَلَيْكُم مَّا حُمِّلْتُمْ ۖ وَإِن تُطِيعُوهُ تَهْتَدُوا ۚ وَمَا عَلَى الرَّسُولِ إِلَّا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/024-an-nur/054/">An-Nur 24:54</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Say: obey God and obey the Messenger. If they turn away, upon him is only what he has been made to carry, and upon you is what you have been made to carry. If you obey him, you will be guided; and upon the Messenger there is nothing except the clear delivery.</p>
    <p>In this verse, obedience to the Messenger is not separated from the Messenger’s mission. The verse does not say that obedience to the Messenger means obedience to every report attributed to the Messenger centuries later. It does not say that obedience to the Messenger means obedience to later hadith books. It does not say that obedience to the Messenger means obedience to a legal and sectarian system. The verse brings obedience to the Messenger within the very context of messengerhood and then clarifies the boundary of messengerhood: upon the Messenger there is nothing except <span class="term">al-balag al-mubin</span>.</p>
    <p>The same logic appears in Surat al-Ma’idah:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَأَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ وَاحْذَرُوا ۚ فَإِن تَوَلَّيْتُمْ فَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّمَا عَلَىٰ رَسُولِنَا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/005-al-maidah/092/">Al-Ma’idah 5:92</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Obey God and obey the Messenger, and beware. If you turn away, then know that upon Our Messenger is only the clear delivery.</p>
    <p>Here also, the command to obey immediately ends with <span class="term">al-balag al-mubin</span>. This means that even when obedience is mentioned, the Qur’an does not allow the Messenger to be moved from the station of delivering God’s message to the station of an independent source of legislation beside God. The Messenger carries and delivers the message. He is commanded to deliver that which is clear. If people turn away, the Messenger’s duty remains clear: delivering the clear message; not guarding people’s faith, not compulsion in religion, not constructing a parallel law, and not handing the community over to later reports.</p>
    <p>The same principle is repeated again in Surat at-Taghabun:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَأَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ ۚ فَإِن تَوَلَّيْتُمْ فَإِنَّمَا عَلَىٰ رَسُولِنَا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/064-at-taghabun/012/">At-Taghabun 64:12</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Obey God and obey the Messenger. If you turn away, then upon Our Messenger is only the clear delivery.</p>
    <p>This repetition is not accidental. The Qur’an repeatedly explains obedience to the Messenger through the boundary of <span class="term">clear delivery</span>. This means that obedience to the Messenger in his station as Messenger is obedience to the clear message that was delivered. If someone wants to separate this obedience from <span class="term">clear delivery</span> and turn it into obedience to human and disputed reports, he has passed beyond the verse itself.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="messengers">
    <h2>A structural principle about all messengers</h2>
    <p>The same meaning is stated about all messengers:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">فَهَلْ عَلَى الرُّسُلِ إِلَّا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/035/">An-Nahl 16:35</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Is there anything upon the messengers except the clear delivery?</p>
    <p>And elsewhere:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">فَإِن تَوَلَّوْا فَإِنَّمَا عَلَيْكَ الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/082/">An-Nahl 16:82</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>If they turn away, then upon you is only the clear delivery.</p>
    <p>And also:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَإِن تُكَذِّبُوا فَقَدْ كَذَّبَ أُمَمٌ مِّن قَبْلِكُمْ ۖ وَمَا عَلَى الرَّسُولِ إِلَّا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/029-al-ankabut/018/">Al-‘Ankabut 29:18</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>If you deny, communities before you also denied; and upon the Messenger there is nothing except the clear delivery.</p>
    <p>And in Surat Ya-Sin:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَمَا عَلَيْنَا إِلَّا الْبَلَاغُ الْمُبِينُ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/036-ya-sin/017/">Ya-Sin 36:17</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>And upon us there is nothing except the clear delivery.</p>
    <p>This collection of verses creates a structural principle: the messengers are not owners of the religion, they are not independent legislators, they are not guardians over people’s faith, and they are not commanded to construct parallel traditions alongside the Book of God. Their duty is to deliver God’s clear message. Now the question must be asked: where does the Qur’an identify this <span class="term">mubin</span> that the Messenger is commanded to deliver?</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="book-quran-mubin">
    <h2>The clear Book and the clear Qur’an</h2>
    <p>The answer is clear: the Qur’an repeatedly identifies the Book and the Qur’an as <span class="term">mubin</span>.</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">الر ۚ تِلْكَ آيَاتُ الْكِتَابِ الْمُبِينِ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/012-yusuf/001/">Yusuf 12:1</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Alif Lam Ra. These are the verses of the clear Book.</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">الر ۚ تِلْكَ آيَاتُ الْكِتَابِ وَقُرْآنٍ مُّبِينٍ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/015-al-hijr/001/">Al-Hijr 15:1</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Alif Lam Ra. These are the verses of the Book and a clear Qur’an.</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">طس ۚ تِلْكَ آيَاتُ الْقُرْآنِ وَكِتَابٍ مُّبِينٍ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/027-an-naml/001/">An-Naml 27:1</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Ta Sin. These are the verses of the Qur’an and a clear Book.</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">طسم</p>
      <p class="arabic">تِلْكَ آيَاتُ الْكِتَابِ الْمُبِينِ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/026-ash-shuara/001/">Ash-Shu‘ara 26:1-2</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Ta Sin Mim. These are the verses of the clear Book.</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">طسم</p>
      <p class="arabic">تِلْكَ آيَاتُ الْكِتَابِ الْمُبِينِ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/028-al-qasas/001/">Al-Qasas 28:1-2</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Ta Sin Mim. These are the verses of the clear Book.</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَالْكِتَابِ الْمُبِينِ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/043-az-zukhruf/002/">Az-Zukhruf 43:2</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>By the clear Book.</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَالْكِتَابِ الْمُبِينِ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/044-ad-dukhan/002/">Ad-Dukhan 44:2</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>By the clear Book.</p>
    <p>And in Surat al-Ma’idah:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">يَا أَهْلَ الْكِتَابِ قَدْ جَاءَكُمْ رَسُولُنَا يُبَيِّنُ لَكُمْ كَثِيرًا مِّمَّا كُنتُمْ تُخْفُونَ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ وَيَعْفُو عَن كَثِيرٍ ۚ قَدْ جَاءَكُم مِّنَ اللَّهِ نُورٌ وَكِتَابٌ مُّبِينٌ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/005-al-maidah/015/">Al-Ma’idah 5:15</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>O People of the Book, Our Messenger has come to you, clarifying for you much of what you used to conceal of the Book and passing over much. There has come to you from God a light and a clear Book.</p>
    <p>These verses show that <span class="term">mubin</span> is one of the central names and descriptions of the Qur’an and the Book. The Qur’an calls itself <span class="term">mubin</span>: clear, clarifying, manifesting, separating boundaries, and bringing truth out of obscurity. So when the same Qur’an says that the Messenger’s duty is <span class="term">al-balag al-mubin</span>, the most natural and Qur’anic understanding is that the Messenger is commanded to deliver that same clear Book and clear Qur’an.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="tibyan-tabyin">
    <h2>The Book as tibyan and the Messenger’s tabyin</h2>
    <p>The Qur’an strengthens this meaning with other expressions as well. In Surat an-Nahl it says:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَنَزَّلْنَا عَلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ تِبْيَانًا لِّكُلِّ شَيْءٍ وَهُدًى وَرَحْمَةً وَبُشْرَىٰ لِلْمُسْلِمِينَ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/089/">An-Nahl 16:89</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>And We sent down the Book to you as a clarification for every thing, and as guidance, mercy, and good news for those who submit.</p>
    <p>Here the Book itself is <span class="term">tibyan</span>, clarification. Therefore the Messenger’s clarification cannot be separate from the Book or against the Book. The Messenger clarifies what was revealed, and the Book itself is clarification. These two must not be placed against each other. If we use the Messenger’s clarification as an excuse for reports outside the Qur’an to rule over the Qur’an itself, then we have turned clarification into the opposite of clarification.</p>
    <p>An-Nahl 16:44 must also be read within this framework:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ وَالزُّبُرِ ۗ وَأَنزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ الذِّكْرَ لِتُبَيِّنَ لِلنَّاسِ مَا نُزِّلَ إِلَيْهِمْ وَلَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/044/">An-Nahl 16:44</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>With clear signs and written messages; and We sent down the Reminder to you so that you may clarify to the people what was sent down to them, and so that they may reflect.</p>
    <p>This verse does not say that the Messenger creates a law separate from the Qur’an. It does not say that later books of reports become an independent source of religion. It says that the Reminder was sent down to the Messenger so that he may clarify for the people that which was sent down to them. Therefore the Messenger’s clarification is inside revelation and in service of revelation, not outside revelation and ruling over revelation. The same point was present in your raw material: the Messenger’s clarification is connected to <span class="term">ma nuzzila ilayhim</span>, “what was sent down to them,” and must not be turned into producing a parallel religion or an independent law beside revelation.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="distortion">
    <h2>The distortion caused by separating al-mubin from the Qur’an</h2>
    <p>Now we can see the distortion more clearly. When <span class="term">al-mubin</span> is separated from the clear Qur’an, people no longer read the verses “obey God and obey the Messenger” within the framework of clear delivery. Instead of asking what clear message the Messenger delivered, they ask what narrators attributed to the Messenger. Instead of understanding obedience to the Messenger as obedience to the message, they turn it into obedience to historical and speculative collections. Instead of the clear Book being the criterion, hadith, report, occasion of revelation, jurisprudence, consensus, and the sayings of a school become the criterion.</p>
    <p>This is exactly the path earlier communities also followed. Jews and Christians did not set their books aside all at once. Rather, layer by layer, tradition, interpretation, priestly legislation, report, council, commentary, and human authority were placed over the Book of God. Gradually, the divine text was buried beneath human tradition. In the end, human beings made law in the name of God, declared things lawful and unlawful, and placed their own speech in the station of God’s speech.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="ahl-al-kitab">
    <h2>The slip of the People of the Book and legislative partners</h2>
    <p>The Qur’an critiques exactly this illness:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">اتَّخَذُوا أَحْبَارَهُمْ وَرُهْبَانَهُمْ أَرْبَابًا مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ وَالْمَسِيحَ ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ وَمَا أُمِرُوا إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا إِلَٰهًا وَاحِدًا ۖ لَّا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ۚ سُبْحَانَهُ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/009-at-tawbah/031/">At-Tawbah 9:31</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>They took their scholars and monks as lords besides God, and the Messiah son of Mary, while they were commanded only to worship one God. There is no god except Him. Glory be to Him above what they associate.</p>
    <p>This verse is not only about prostrating to scholars and monks. The main danger is that human authority is placed in the position of divine authority in the realm of religion, legislation, and lawful and unlawful. When a scholar, jurist, hadith transmitter, priest, monk, narrator, or school can make something religiously binding that God did not permit in His Book, the human being has in practice made a legislative partner for God.</p>
    <p>The Qur’an states the same meaning even more openly in Surat ash-Shura:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">أَمْ لَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ شَرَعُوا لَهُم مِّنَ الدِّينِ مَا لَمْ يَأْذَن بِهِ اللَّهُ ۚ وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةُ الْفَصْلِ لَقُضِيَ بَيْنَهُمْ ۗ وَإِنَّ الظَّالِمِينَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/042-ash-shuraa/021/">Ash-Shura 42:21</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Translation:</p>
    <p>Or do they have partners who have legislated for them from the religion what God did not authorize? If not for the word of decision, judgment would have been made between them; and the wrongdoers will have a painful punishment.</p>
    <p>This verse is the heart of the discussion. If the Qur’an is clear and the Messenger is commanded to deliver that clear message, then no report, school, jurisprudence, or historical tradition has the right to construct law beside the Qur’an. If a report comes and forbids what God did not forbid in the Qur’an, or makes obligatory what God did not make obligatory, or restricts or abrogates a clear Qur’anic ruling through report, this is no longer clarification. It is legislation without God’s authorization.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="israiliyyat">
    <h2>Isra’iliyyat and the displacement of Qur’anic authority</h2>
    <p>Through this same gate, Isra’iliyyat entered. When the clear Book is no longer seen as sufficient and as the final criterion, the human being goes looking for stories, occasions of revelation, reports, narrations from the People of the Book, stories of Ka‘b al-Ahbar and Wahb ibn Munabbih, and external details. Then these heard reports gradually cast their shadow over the understanding of the Qur’an. Instead of the Qur’an correcting previous scriptures and historical reports, historical reports come and explain, restrict, direct, and even spiritually distort the Qur’an. This is distortion in the religion of God; not necessarily through changing the letters of the Qur’an, but through changing the authority of the Qur’an.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="conclusion">
    <h2>Conclusion: returning religious authority to God</h2>
    <p>The Qur’an is clear about itself. It is the clear Book. It is the clear Qur’an. It is light and a clear Book. It is clarification. The Messenger’s delivery is also clear delivery. Therefore the correct path is to understand “obey God and obey the Messenger” within this Qur’anic system: obedience to God and the Messenger is obedience to the clear revelation and the clear message that the Messenger delivered. Any other speech that wants to rule over this Book, present itself as its necessary supplement, or push its verses aside with speculative reports has not remained in its proper place.</p>
    <p>Returning <span class="term">al-mubin</span> to the Qur’an is not a small lexical discussion; it is returning religious authority to God. When we return <span class="term">mubin</span> to the clear Book, the Messenger stands in his proper place: the deliverer of God’s message, not the source of a law separate from God. Hadith also stands in its proper place: a human and historical report, not preserved revelation and not ruler over the Qur’an. Jurisprudence also stands in its proper place: human understanding, not God’s religion. And the community is saved from the same path on which the previous People of the Book slipped: placing the speech of human beings where the speech of God belongs.</p>
  </section>
