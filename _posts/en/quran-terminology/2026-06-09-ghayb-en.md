<h1>Faith in the Unseen; the Logical Path to the Straight Way and the Gateway to Quranic Guidance</h1>

<p class="subtitle">Why does the Quran begin the guidance of the mindful with faith in what is unseen?</p>

<h2 id="intro">The central question</h2>

<p>At the beginning of Surah Al-Baqarah, after “Alif Lam Mim,” the Quran introduces itself in this way: “This is the Book in which there is no rayb; guidance for the mindful.” Then it describes the first sign of the mindful: “those who believe in the unseen.”</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/002/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Baqarah 2:2
  </a>
  ,
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/003/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Baqarah 2:3
  </a>
</p>

<h2 id="baqarah-opening">The opening of Al-Baqarah and the place of faith in the unseen</h2>

<p>This order is highly significant. Before the Quran speaks of salat, spending, revelation, or the Hereafter, it first speaks of faith in the unseen. This means that the door of Quranic guidance opens for the one who has not limited truth to matter and sensory perception.</p>

<p>If a human being begins by saying that nothing exists except what the eye sees and what material instruments can measure, then he has not yet entered the main space of Quranic guidance.</p>

<h2 id="meaning">What does faith in the unseen mean?</h2>

<p>Faith in the unseen does not mean accepting superstition, shutting down reason, or believing without evidence in every unseen claim. In the Quran, the unseen is the realm hidden from the direct sight and full grasp of the human being; but denying it merely because it is unseen is itself a major claim that requires evidence.</p>

<p>The human being does not directly see many things, yet effects, order, reason, morality, experience, and signs can guide him toward their existence.</p>

<p>From here, the first question begins: Is reality only this sensory world, or is there also truth beyond it?</p>

<h2 id="logical-path">The logical path: from the possibility of the unseen to the possibility of revelation</h2>

<p>If God does not exist, the search for religion in its true meaning comes to an end; because in that case, there is no final conscious, knowing, just, and responsive source from whom the human being can seek guidance.</p>

<p>Morality may still be understood as a social contract, a human feeling, or a collective interest; but behind it there would no longer be an absolute, knowing, and just source.</p>

<p>But if God exists, the next question appears: Is God one or multiple?</p>

<h2 id="one-god">If God exists, is He One?</h2>

<p>If multiple gods are assumed, one must ask: What is their relationship to one another? Are they in complete harmony, or in conflict? Is one dominant over another? Are power, knowledge, mercy, and justice divided among them?</p>

<p>If multiple final and independent wills govern existence, how can the order of the world and the unity of truth be explained? The Quran points to this very logic: if there were gods in the heavens and the earth besides The God, their order would fall into corruption.</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/021-al-anbiya/022/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Anbiya 21:22
  </a>
</p>

<h2 id="communication">If God is One, does He communicate with the human being?</h2>

<p>If God is One, the next question is this: Does this One God communicate with the human being or not?</p>

<p>If God is aware, living, knowing, and wise, is He indifferent toward the human being, morality, suffering, oppression, prayer, choice, and human destiny? If God is assumed to be completely indifferent, then the search for guidance from Him no longer has a clear meaning.</p>

<p>But if God is responsive, merciful, wise, and guiding, the next question is this: How does God communicate with the human being?</p>

<p>This communication can occur through signs in the horizons and within the self: signs in the world and inside the human being. The Quran repeatedly calls the human being to reflect on the heavens, the earth, the self, history, death, life, provision, order, and the signs of creation.</p>

<p>This communication can also occur through revelation: a clear divine message to orient reason, morality, worship, and society. The Quran says that God will show His signs in the horizons and within the selves of human beings until it becomes clear that it is the truth.</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/041-fussilat/053/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Fussilat 41:53
  </a>
</p>

<p>So the logical path proceeds in this way: Is there a truth beyond matter? If there is, is that truth God? If it is God, is God One? If He is One, does He communicate with the human being? If He communicates, is this communication guiding, ethical, just, and recognizable?</p>

<h2 id="criteria">How is divine guidance recognized?</h2>

<p>At this stage, the human being must ask: If God guides, how is His guidance recognized?</p>

<p>Divine guidance must be consistent with justice, mercy, wisdom, truth, and human growth. A guidance that removes moral responsibility, sanctifies oppression, makes tribe, race, or birth the criterion of superiority, shuts down reason, or portrays God as unjust cannot be consistent with the Lordship of The God.</p>

<p>The Quran does not make birth, tribe, language, or inherited religious identity the criterion of honor. Rather, it presents taqwa as the criterion of honor before The God.</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/049-al-hujurat/013/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Hujurat 49:13
  </a>
</p>

<p>From here it becomes clear that faith in the unseen is the beginning of the path, but not the end of the path. After accepting the unseen, the human being must ask: Which word, which Book, which call, and which path is truly closer to God?</p>

<p>Which guidance makes the human being more just, truthful, pure, responsible, merciful, and aware? Which text calls the human being to worship the One God, to righteous action, justice, ihsan, patience, truthfulness, and accountability before God?</p>

<h2 id="quran">The Quran in the field of guidance</h2>

<p>In this field, the Quran does not present itself merely as a tribal religious text. It presents itself as guidance, furqan, remembrance, light, and a criterion that separates truth from falsehood.</p>

<p>This means that the Quran does not ask the human being to rely merely on a name, inheritance, tribe, or birth. Rather, it calls him to examination, tadabbur, righteous action, and return to God.</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/185/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Baqarah 2:185

  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/005-al-maidah/015/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">

  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/025-al-furqan/001/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Furqan 25:1
  </a>
</p>

<h2 id="atheism">The place of dialogue with atheism</h2>

<p>For this reason, Quranic discussion with atheism has its own place. If someone has not yet accepted the principle of the unseen, then the discussion with him is preliminary: Is reality only matter or not? Does morality have a final foundation or not? Can order, awareness, reason, and the human fitrah be explained without any aware and wise source?</p>

<p>This discussion may be important, but it is not yet the same discussion with which the Quran begins building taqwa at the opening of Al-Baqarah.</p>

<p>At the beginning of Al-Baqarah, the Quran begins the path of guidance for the mindful with a human being who has not closed the door to the unseen. Such a human being is ready to hear about salat, spending, revelation, the Hereafter, responsibility, and reform of the self. But the one who considers every unseen matter impossible from the start is still standing at the threshold before Quranic guidance.</p>

<p>Therefore, it is not fitting for believers to spend most of their energy in exhausting arguments, mockery, sarcasm, or verbal warfare with atheists. The Quran calls us to the best form of dialogue, gentle speech, wisdom, good exhortation, and proof; not to humiliation or ridicule.</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/125/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    An-Nahl 16:125
  </a>
  ,
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/029-al-ankabut/046/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-‘Ankabut 29:46
  </a>
</p>

<p>If dialogue takes place, it must be based on etiquette, reason, clear authority, and respect for human dignity.</p>

<h2 id="believers">The main field of Quranic dialogue</h2>

<p>The main field of Quranic work begins after faith in the unseen: dialogue with those who take God, revelation, morality, and accountability before truth seriously.</p>

<p>In this field, discussion must be grounded in the Quran, reason, justice, mercy, and clear authority. Those who believe in the unseen must move beyond bias, blind imitation, inherited superiority, and sectarianism, and ask themselves: Which understanding presents God as more just, wise, merciful, and free from imperfection? Which understanding is more consistent with the Quranic text, moral fitrah, and divine justice?</p>

<h2 id="birth">Faith in the unseen and the danger of inherited salvation</h2>

<p>Here, the issue of birth also becomes important. The Quran does not tie guidance and honor to race, family, tribe, language, or inherited religious identity. If a person considers himself more saved only because he was born into a Muslim family, then he has turned guidance into the luck of birth.</p>

<p>But the Quran places responsibility upon capacity, intention, action, taqwa, and accountability. God does not burden any soul beyond its capacity, and God does not wrong people at all. Therefore, any understanding of religion that portrays God as unjust, tribal, or discriminatory must be examined again before the Quran.</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/286/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Baqarah 2:286
  </a>
  ,
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/010-yunus/044/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Yunus 10:44
  </a>
</p>

<p>This point is not the main subject of this article, but it is a natural consequence of the same principle: faith in the unseen must not become blind prejudice. Faith in the unseen means that the human being accepts that truth is greater than the eye and greater than the environment of birth. Just as the atheist should not reduce the world to sensory material things, the believer should not confine God within the boundaries of his tribe, inherited sect, or group narrations.</p>

<p>From the Quranic view, the human being must believe in the unseen; but this faith must lead him to humility, taqwa, justice, salat, spending, and righteous action. If faith in the unseen makes a person arrogant, sectarian, merciless, or certain of inherited salvation, then it has left its Quranic path.</p>

<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>

<p>Faith in the unseen, then, is the gateway. But after entering through this gateway, the human being must be trained by the Quran. The Quran takes him from a general belief in God toward salat, spending, faith in revelation, certainty in the Hereafter, taqwa, and falah.</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/003/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Baqarah 2:3
  </a>
  ,
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/004/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Baqarah 2:4
  </a>
  ,
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/005/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Baqarah 2:5
  </a>
</p>

<p>The conclusion is this: The Quran begins its guidance with a human being who is not closed before the unseen. Faith in the unseen means accepting the possibility of a truth beyond matter, and being ready to hear guidance from the One God. This faith should lead the human being to just searching, ethical dialogue, tadabbur, and measuring guidance by truth; not to fruitless argument, humiliation of others, or inherited certainty of salvation.</p>

<p>The one who believes in the unseen has not finished the work; he has only entered the field of guidance. Now he must see which path brings him closer to taqwa, justice, truthfulness, mercy, and falah. The Quran presents itself for this very field: a Book without rayb, guidance for the mindful; those who believe in the unseen.</p>
