Introduction

Shukr is one of those words that flows often on our tongues, but its meaning has frequently been confined to a few short phrases. When we hear good news, recover from illness, escape danger, acquire wealth, or see one of our wishes fulfilled, we say: “al-hamdu lillah,” “thank God,” or “thanks be to God.”

These statements can be sincere and beautiful. Bringing a blessing to the tongue and relating it to God is part of shukr. The problem begins when the whole of shukr is reduced to these words, as though the human being can receive a blessing, mention God’s name with his tongue, and then ruin that same blessing, use it for oppression, or employ it in a direction incompatible with its truth, while still considering himself grateful.

In the logic of the Qur’an, shukr is not merely a verbal reaction after the arrival of some good. Shukr is a relationship between gift, recognition, and response. Something reaches the human being; he sees it and does not ignore it; he relates it to its true source; he understands its value and responsibility; then he makes the effect of this recognition visible in his choices, behavior, and way of using it.

So shukr is not only that a person says, “This blessing is from God.” Shukr is that this recognition also changes the direction of his behavior.

If knowledge is a blessing, its shukr is not only saying, “O God, thank You that I learned.” Acting on the basis of what one knows and not polluting knowledge with deception are also part of shukr.

If power is a blessing, its shukr is not only prostrating after reaching a position. It is preventing oppression and using power justly.

If wealth is a blessing, its shukr is not only saying “al-hamdu lillah” after financial gain. It is keeping wealth clean, observing the rights of others, and using it responsibly.

If hearing and sight are blessings, their shukr is not only being happy about bodily health. It is hearing truth and seeing what may not be pleasant for the human being to see, but brings responsibility.

In the Qur’an, shukr turns blessing from a personal privilege into an ethical responsibility.

Shukr Begins with Seeing

Before shukr begins with the tongue, it begins with seeing.

The ungrateful human being is not always someone who openly denies blessing. Sometimes he has the blessing and uses it, but no longer sees it. Health appears ordinary until it is damaged. Security is imagined to be a natural right until it is lost. The love of parents, the opportunity for education, clean water, the ability to move, to hear, to see, and to think can become so ordinary in life that their presence leaves the field of human awareness.

The first stage of shukr is bringing blessing out of the darkness of habit.

The grateful human being knows that what he has is not merely the product of his independent ability. Human effort is real and should not be denied, but the very ability to strive is also a gift. A person may work for years to acquire knowledge, yet he did not create from nothing the mind, memory, health, time, teacher, book, and opportunity that opened the path of learning for him.

Shukr begins from this recognition: the human being does not see himself as the absolute source and independent owner of everything.

Even so, seeing and recognizing alone are not enough. If blessing has no effect on the kind of response the human being gives, shukr has not yet reached its completion. What has been received must become visible in the way it is used.

The Material Image of Shukr: When the Effect of Receiving Does Not Remain Hidden

In older Arabic usage, the root sh-k-r was not used only in the context of human gratitude. It was also used about an animal in whose body a small amount of food leaves a visible effect: it grows, or gives more milk.

The main point in this image is not the smallness of the food or the largeness of the product. The point is that what was received did not remain without effect. Food entered the body, and its result became visible in the animal’s being.

This image can help us understand human shukr. A blessing should not enter the human being’s life while leaving no right effect in him. If someone gains knowledge but his bigotry, arrogance, and deception increase, the proper effect of knowledge has not appeared in him. If he gains power but his ability to suppress and impose increases, the blessing has not reached an appropriate response in him. If he becomes wealthy but his greed, arrogance, and cruelty increase, a few religious phrases cannot make him grateful.

At the same time, this image should not be turned into a technical formula. Shukr does not mean the highest output from the smallest resources. Shukr is not the same as economic efficiency or increased productivity.

Sometimes the shukr of a blessing lies not in consuming more, but in consuming less. Sometimes the shukr of power lies not in using it, but in refraining from imposing power. Sometimes the shukr of the tongue lies not in speaking, but in a silence that prevents harm. Sometimes the shukr of wealth lies not in increasing it, but in giving, purifying it from the rights of others, or stopping a harmful form of consumption.

So the measure of shukr is not the size of the result, but the correctness and proportion of the response.

Blessing Is Not Only Wealth and Comfort

In common understanding, blessing is mostly connected with money, house, health, children, success, or the fulfillment of desires. But the Qur’an sees the field of giving much more broadly.

Life is a blessing.

Forgiveness and a second opportunity are blessings.

Guidance is a blessing.

Hearing, sight, and the heart are blessings.

The ability to distinguish, the power to decide, and the possibility of repentance are blessings.

Security, rescue from oppression, and pure provision are blessings.

Power, influence, and position can also be blessings, even while they are tests at the same time.

Therefore shukr cannot be limited to a reaction after receiving wealth or comfort. Every gift places a specific question before the human being:

If I have been given another opportunity, will I repeat my previous behavior?

If a truth has become clear to me, will I still continue a habit whose wrongness I have understood?

If I have the ability to distinguish, will I think for myself, or hand the responsibility of reason to others?

If I have been rescued from oppression, will I myself become a new oppressor?

If I have security, will I ignore the insecurity of others?

Every blessing carries with it a responsibility proportionate to itself.

Shukr and Kufr: Seeing or Covering

One of the most important ways to understand shukr in the Qur’an is to pay attention to its contrast with kufr:

فَاذْكُرُونِي أَذْكُرْكُمْ وَاشْكُرُوا لِي وَلَا تَكْفُرُونِ

Fa-dhkuruni adhkurkum, wa-shkuru li wa-la takfurun.

So remember Me, and I will remember you; and show shukr to Me, and do not commit kufr toward Me.

Al-Baqarah 2:152

If shukr were only saying “thank you,” then kufr would also have to be only not saying thank you, or a kind of verbal discourtesy. But the contrast in the verse is deeper.

Kufr is connected with covering, ignoring, and rejecting. A human being may see a truth, but cover it; receive a blessing, but live as though he had not seen it; have a path made clear to him, but refuse to accept its consequence.

Shukr moves in the opposite direction. The grateful person does not ignore the gift or the truth. He recognizes it and allows this recognition to become visible in his response.

So shukr and kufr are not merely two kinds of speech; they are two different positions before truth.

One sees, recognizes, and responds.

The other covers, denies, or refuses the fitting response.

For this reason, a person may say “al-hamdu lillah” often while, in action, covering the blessing. Someone who has knowledge but hides truth in order to preserve his position may give thanks with his tongue, while his behavior comes close to kufr toward the blessing. Someone who enjoys security but does not see the suffering of the insecure has accepted blessing only for himself and has covered its responsibility.

Forgiveness: An Opportunity That Must Be Seen in a New Life

After recalling the forgiveness of the Children of Israel, the Qur’an says:

ثُمَّ عَفَوْنَا عَنْكُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

Thumma ‘afawna ‘ankum min ba‘di dhalika la‘allakum tashkurun.

Then We pardoned you after that, so that you might show shukr.

Al-Baqarah 2:52

Shukr here is not only a feeling of relief after forgiveness. If an individual or a society is forgiven but continues the same past path, the effect of forgiveness has not appeared in life.

Forgiveness is not an opportunity to continue the same past; it is an opportunity to build a different future.

A person may be saved from danger, pass through illness, have a ruined relationship repaired again, or be forgiven for an error. At that moment he says, “O God, thank You.” But if after some time everything returns to its previous form, his tongue has shown shukr, but his life has not.

The shukr of forgiveness is that the human being uses the new opportunity to build a new life. If he has been forgiven, he too should become more ready to forgive. If he has been given another opportunity, he should not ruin it like the first. If he has been saved from the consequence of his error, he should reform the cause of that error.

Shukr for Provision: Not Only Eating, but How It Is Acquired and How It Is Used

The Qur’an says:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ وَاشْكُرُوا لِلَّهِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ

Ya ayyuha alladhina amanu, kulu min tayyibati ma razaqnakum, wa-shkuru lillahi in kuntum iyyahu ta‘budun.

O you who have believed, eat from the pure things We have provided for you, and show shukr to God, if it is Him alone that you worship.

Al-Baqarah 2:172

In this verse, shukr is placed beside eating from pure provision. This pairing is not meaningless. Shukr for provision is not only that the human being mentions God’s name before or after food. The provision itself must be pure, and the way it is used must remain compatible with the worship of God.

Wealth acquired through oppression does not become pure by saying, “O God, thank You.”

Food obtained through the right of a worker, deception of a customer, economic corruption, or seizure of the rights of others does not become shukr through a prayer before eating.

The Qur’an says elsewhere:

فَكُلُوا مِمَّا رَزَقَكُمُ اللَّهُ حَلَالًا طَيِّبًا وَاشْكُرُوا نِعْمَتَ اللَّهِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ

Fa-kulu mimma razaqakumu Allahu halalan tayyiban, wa-shkuru ni‘mata Allahi in kuntum iyyahu ta‘budun.

So eat from what God has provided for you, lawful and pure, and show shukr for the blessing of God, if it is Him alone that you worship.

An-Nahl 16:114

Shukr for provision begins with recognizing its source, and reaches the purity of the way it is acquired, the purity of consumption, avoidance of waste, and regard for the rights of others.

The human being cannot separate the direction of blessing from its truth. If provision is a means for preserving life, then grateful use of it must also preserve life, not lead, through greed, corruption, and ruin, to harm against others.

Shukr for Guidance: Knowing the Truth Is Not Enough

After speaking of guidance, the Qur’an says:

وَلِتُكَبِّرُوا اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَاكُمْ وَلَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

Wa-li-tukabbiru Allaha ‘ala ma hadakum wa-la‘allakum tashkurun.

And so that you may magnify God for what He has guided you to, and so that you may show shukr.

Al-Baqarah 2:185

Guidance itself is a blessing, but shukr for guidance is not only that a person says, “O God, thank You that I understood the truth.”

Shukr for guidance is that after the path has become clear, the human being moves in that same direction. If a truth has been made clear to him but his life does not change at all, the effect of guidance has not appeared in him.

This matter has special importance in religion. A person may recognize the error of an inherited belief, see an oppression that he previously considered normal, or have a new meaning from the Qur’an become clear to him. At that moment he may thank God for showing him the truth. But real shukr begins when he is willing to pay the cost of following the truth as well.

One who recognizes truth but hides it in order to preserve income, reputation, position, or comfort has not yet fulfilled the shukr of guidance.

The Qur’an says:

إِنَّا هَدَيْنَاهُ السَّبِيلَ إِمَّا شَاكِرًا وَإِمَّا كَفُورًا

Inna hadaynahu as-sabil, imma shakiran wa-imma kafura.

We guided him to the path, whether he is grateful or deeply covering and ungrateful.

Al-Insan 76:3

The path is shown, but the response is still entrusted to the human being. The grateful one is the one who accepts the clarified truth and allows it to reform his life. The kafur is the one who covers it or flees from its consequence.

The more clearly truth becomes visible to the human being, the greater his responsibility becomes. Not knowing is not the same as not wanting to know. Error before the path becomes clear is not equal to continuing the same behavior after recognizing it.

Guidance becomes shukr when it becomes choice.

Shukr for Hearing, Sight, and the Heart

The Qur’an says:

وَاللَّهُ أَخْرَجَكُمْ مِنْ بُطُونِ أُمَّهَاتِكُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ شَيْئًا وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَالْأَفْئِدَةَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

Wa-Allahu akhrajakum min butuni ummahatikum la ta‘lamuna shay’an, wa-ja‘ala lakumu as-sam‘a wa-l-absara wa-l-af’idah la‘allakum tashkurun.

And God brought you out from the wombs of your mothers while you knew nothing, and He made for you hearing, sight, and hearts, so that you may show shukr.

An-Nahl 16:78

Hearing, sight, and the heart are not merely biological capacities; they are instruments of recognition. The human being does not come into the world with complete knowledge. Hearing, seeing, experiencing, thinking, and measuring gradually bring him out of ignorance.

The shukr of the ear is not only being happy about the ability to hear. The ear becomes grateful when it hears truth, does not ignore the speech of the oppressed, and does not place itself only at the disposal of a voice that confirms its previous beliefs.

The shukr of the eye is not only seeing beautiful things. The eye must also see oppression, recognize signs, and not flee from seeing what brings responsibility.

The shukr of the heart is also not merely a spiritual feeling. In the language of the Qur’an, the heart is connected with understanding, discernment, and orientation. If the human being places the tools of recognition in the service of bigotry, deception, and selfishness, he has turned the blessing away from its proper direction.

Having the instrument alone is not shukr for it. An instrument becomes grateful when it is used in the path of recognizing truth and responding responsibly to it.

Power: A Blessing That Is Also a Test

One of the deepest verses on shukr appears in the story of Solomon. When the throne of the Queen of Sheba was brought before him, he said:

هَٰذَا مِنْ فَضْلِ رَبِّي لِيَبْلُوَنِي أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ

Hadha min fadli Rabbi li-yabluwani a-ashkuru am akfur.

This is from the favor of my Lord, to test me whether I show shukr or commit kufr.

An-Naml 27:40

Solomon does not see the blessing only as a reward; he sees it as a test.

This view differs sharply from common understanding. We often imagine that whoever has more wealth, power, knowledge, or influence must necessarily have received special divine approval. But the Qur’an says that the same favor can be a field of trial.

The main question is not what has been given to the human being; the question is what the human being does with it.

The shukr of power is that the human being knows power is not his absolute property. He must preserve the right of the weak, avoid tyranny, and place his ability in the service of justice.

Someone who prostrates in gratitude after reaching office, but suppresses opponents, consumes public wealth for his own benefit, and silences truth, may have shown shukr with his tongue, but his power bears witness against his shukr.

The verse continues:

وَمَنْ شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشْكُرُ لِنَفْسِهِ وَمَنْ كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّي غَنِيٌّ كَرِيمٌ

Wa-man shakara fa-innama yashkuru li-nafsih, wa-man kafara fa-inna Rabbi ghaniyyun karim.

Whoever shows shukr only shows shukr for himself, and whoever commits kufr, then my Lord is Rich and Generous.

God does not benefit from the human being’s shukr. It is the human being who, through shukr, repairs his own relation with the truth of the blessing and is saved from being ruined by that very blessing.

Power, if it is not met with shukr, devours its owner.

Wealth, if it is not met with shukr, makes the human being a slave of greed.

Knowledge, if it is not met with shukr, produces arrogance.

Religion, if it is not met with shukr, becomes a tool of domination.

Blessing does not always raise the human being; sometimes it merely reveals what was hidden inside him.

Increase in Blessing Is Not Always a Sign of Divine Approval

One of the most famous verses of shukr is this:

لَئِنْ شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ وَلَئِنْ كَفَرْتُمْ إِنَّ عَذَابِي لَشَدِيدٌ

La-in shakartum la-azidannakum, wa-la-in kafartum inna ‘adhabi la-shadid.

If you show shukr, I will surely increase you; and if you commit kufr, My punishment is severe.

Ibrahim 14:7

This verse is often understood transactionally: the human being says a sentence of thanks, and God, in return, increases his possessions.

But the verse does not say exactly what is increased. “I will increase you” is broader than increase in wealth. Increase may appear in blessing, capacity to use it, awareness, trust, peace, barakah, or a new opening.

If shukr is the right response to blessing, then increase can also be connected with the expansion of the capacity for good. One who uses knowledge correctly gains greater ability to understand. One who preserves trust becomes worthy of greater trust. One who uses wealth responsibly builds relationships and a society in which greater good becomes possible.

But one must not conclude from the other side that every material increase is a sign of shukr or of God’s approval. The Qur’an also speaks of a condition in which the human being, in his heedlessness and denial, gradually receives more opportunity and means:

وَالَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا سَنَسْتَدْرِجُهُمْ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

Wa-alladhina kadhdhabu bi-ayatina sanastadrijuhum min haythu la ya‘lamun.

Those who denied Our signs, We will gradually draw them on from where they do not know.

Al-A‘raf 7:182

Increase is not always reward; sometimes it is the continuation of the test. A person’s wealth, influence, or power may increase while that very increase plunges him deeper into self-deception, corruption, and heavier responsibility.

So neither scarcity is always a sign of God’s anger, nor abundance always a sign of His approval. The criterion is how the human being responds.

Wealth that destroys peace and justice is only an increased number.

Power that makes its owner dependent on fear and suppression is not a healthy blessing.

Knowledge that takes the human being farther from truth is not an increase in awareness.

Qur’anic increase should not be measured only by quantity; the quality of benefit and the direction of its effect also matter.

Shukr as One Face of Wisdom

The Qur’an says about Luqman:

وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا لُقْمَانَ الْحِكْمَةَ أَنِ اشْكُرْ لِلَّهِ

Wa-laqad atayna Luqmana al-hikmata ani-shkur lillah.

And We certainly gave Luqman wisdom: show shukr to God.

Luqman 31:12

The connection of shukr with wisdom is not accidental. Wisdom is not merely having abundant information; it is seeing everything in its proper place, recognizing relations, and responding in proportion to truth.

Shukr has the same structure. The human being must know what he has received, where it came from, what value it has, and what responsibility it creates.

Ingratitude often forms through the reversal of relations. The human being turns what is a means into an end. He imagines a blessing that is a trust to be his absolute property. He attributes a success in whose formation many factors played a role solely to his own genius and personal merit. What he should share with others, he counts as a sign of his own superiority; and what he should protect, he turns into a tool for boasting.

Shukr returns these relations to their proper place. The grateful human being does not deny his own effort, but he also does not see himself as the independent source of success.

The verse continues:

وَمَنْ يَشْكُرْ فَإِنَّمَا يَشْكُرُ لِنَفْسِهِ وَمَنْ كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَنِيٌّ حَمِيدٌ

Wa-man yashkur fa-innama yashkuru li-nafsih, wa-man kafara fa-inna Allaha ghaniyyun hamid.

Whoever shows shukr only shows shukr for himself, and whoever commits kufr, God is Rich and Praiseworthy.

Luqman 31:12

The command of shukr is not for completing God; it is for reforming the human being. Shukr protects him from arrogance, waste, greed, and self-deception. From this angle, shukr is not only one of the actions of the wise human being; it is itself a form of wisdom.

Shukr to God and Shukr to Parents

In the continuation of the counsel regarding parents, the Qur’an says:

أَنِ اشْكُرْ لِي وَلِوَالِدَيْكَ إِلَيَّ الْمَصِيرُ

Ani-shkur li wa-li-walidayk; ilayya al-masir.

Show shukr to Me and to your parents; to Me is the destination.

Luqman 31:14

This verse shows that shukr is not used only in the human being’s relation with God. The human being must also not ignore those who have had a role in bringing good, nurturing, and supporting him.

Shukr to parents is not only saying respectful words. Paying attention to their suffering, behaving with dignity, caring for them when they need it, and not forgetting their efforts are practical forms of this shukr.

But shukr to parents does not mean unconditional obedience. The next verse says that if they pressure the human being toward shirk, he must not obey them, while still keeping company with them in this world in a recognized and good way.

The Qur’an distinguishes between shukr, respect, and absolute obedience.

The human being can recognize someone’s good, observe his right, and respond appropriately to his kindness, while not following him in error. Past goodness does not make today’s error true.

Shukr must not become a tool of domination, neither in the family nor in society.

Shukr Must Be Seen in Action

One of the clearest verses about the reality of shukr is addressed to the family of David:

اعْمَلُوا آلَ دَاوُودَ شُكْرًا وَقَلِيلٌ مِنْ عِبَادِيَ الشَّكُورُ

I‘malu ala Dawuda shukra, wa-qalilun min ‘ibadiya ash-shakur.

O family of David, act in shukr; and few of My servants are deeply grateful.

Saba 34:13

The verse does not say, “O family of David, say thanks.” It says: “Act in shukr.”

The family of David had power, government, knowledge, craft, and broad means. The fitting response to such giving could not be only verbal praise. Those means had to be seen in the kind of governance, behavior toward people, and use of power.

If a ruler prostrates in gratitude after victory but tramples the rights of people, his action is not shukr.

If a wealthy person holds a thanksgiving gathering but his wealth has been acquired through exploitation or corruption, his table cannot hide the truth of the path of that wealth.

If a scholar thanks God for his knowledge but uses his knowledge to deceive people, his religious phrases do not show the effect of the blessing in the right direction.

Perhaps one reason grateful people are few is that verbal shukr is easy, while practical shukr is costly.

Saying “thank God” takes only a few moments, but using power justly, keeping wealth clean, remaining faithful to truth, and acting on the basis of knowledge involve the whole life of the human being.

Many love the blessing, but do not want its responsibility.

Why Is Practical Shukr Difficult?

Verbal shukr is inexpensive, but practical shukr takes a cost from the selfishness of the human being.

The shukr of wealth may mean giving, observing the right of the worker, and limiting one’s own consumption.

The shukr of power may mean accountability, accepting critique, and giving up part of one’s control.

The shukr of knowledge may force a person to speak a truth that harms his position or popularity.

The shukr of freedom may ask the human being to stand for the freedom of those who do not think like him.

The shukr of forgiveness may force him to leave a past habit.

The shukr of love may require loyalty, care, and setting aside selfishness.

The human being flees from practical shukr because real shukr is not only admission of blessing; it is acceptance of the responsibility that blessing places on his shoulders.

For this reason, shukr passes beyond the boundary of good feeling and confronts the selfishness of the human being. The human being wants to have the blessing, but he does not want to see his freedom in using it limited. He wants to be owner, not trustee; beneficiary, not accountable.

Shukr breaks this illusion.

Shukr in Hardship Is Not the Denial of Pain

Sometimes a person who is suffering is told: “Be grateful; perhaps this calamity is a hidden blessing.” Such a statement may come from good intention, but it is not always just or precise.

The Qur’an does not force the human being to call oppression itself, illness itself, loss itself, or calamity itself a blessing. Pain is pain. Oppression is oppression. The death of a loved one does not cease to be suffering by changing its name.

Shukr in hardship does not mean denying pain.

A human being can complain of illness while not ignoring what still remains in his possession.

He can be angry at oppression and still recognize the ability to resist, speak, or help others as a valuable gift.

He can mourn and still not forget the love he has received.

He can strive to remove calamity and, at the same time, recognize the opportunity to decide, the path of treatment, the presence of a friend, or the possibility of return.

Shukr in hardship returns the human being’s gaze from total loss to the remaining possibility; but it does not beautify pain and does not silence the protest of the oppressed.

One cannot say to someone who has been oppressed, “Be grateful and do not speak.” If he has the ability to defend, protest, and change, using this ability may itself be a form of shukr.

Sabr here also does not mean passive surrender. Active sabr is holding oneself on the right path under pressure. Shukr in hardship is difficult without such steadiness, because the human being must see pain, not deny it, and at the same time not allow it to swallow all of his awareness.

Nor should every calamity be called a “hidden blessing.” A human being may grow after a difficult experience, but his growth does not make the crime of the oppressor good.

Hamd and Shukr Are Not the Same

In common language, “al-hamdu lillah” and “thank God” are often used almost interchangeably. There is closeness between hamd and shukr, but their semantic fields are not completely identical.

Hamd is closer to praising goodness, perfection, or a praiseworthy act. The human being can praise God because of His mercy, wisdom, and justice, even before mentioning a specific blessing in relation to himself.

Shukr is more directly placed before a gift, kindness, guidance, or good that has reached the human being. In shukr, there is a relation between the giver, the gift, and the response of the receiver.

One can say that hamd praises goodness and perfection more broadly, while shukr responds to received good. But this distinction should not be turned into a rigid dualism. Hamd too can have an effect in life, and shukr too can be accompanied by the tongue.

Saying “al-hamdu lillah” is a great and true statement, but by itself it does not fulfill the whole responsibility of shukr. The tongue can praise God while the behavior of the human being still remains unanswered toward His gift.

Doing Good Must Not Depend on People’s Thanks

The Qur’an speaks of people of goodness who feed the needy and say:

إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ اللَّهِ لَا نُرِيدُ مِنْكُمْ جَزَاءً وَلَا شُكُورًا

Innama nut‘imukum li-wajhi Allah; la nuridu minkum jaza’an wa-la shukura.

We feed you only for the face of God; we desire from you neither reward nor thanks.

Al-Insan 76:9

They do not deny the value of thanks. The issue is that they do not make their good dependent on the reaction of the receiver.

If someone does good only when his name is mentioned, when he is thanked, or when he gains social credit, his action has become dependent on people’s response. When appreciation is absent, he may stop doing good or take it back through reminders of favor and humiliation.

This does not mean that people’s gratitude is unimportant. The Qur’an itself reminds us of shukr to parents. Ignoring the kindness of others is not a virtue. But the good-doer also must not make the needy person indebted and humiliated by demanding thanks.

Shukr is the moral right of the person who does good, but it must not be the price of his help.

When God Is Called Shakir and Shakur

The Qur’an does not use the root sh-k-r only about the human being. God too is described as Shakir and Shakur:

وَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ شَاكِرٌ عَلِيمٌ

Wa-man tatawwa‘a khayran fa-inna Allaha Shakirun ‘Alim.

Whoever voluntarily adds good, God is Shakir and Knowing.

Al-Baqarah 2:158

And:

مَا يَفْعَلُ اللَّهُ بِعَذَابِكُمْ إِنْ شَكَرْتُمْ وَآمَنْتُمْ وَكَانَ اللَّهُ شَاكِرًا عَلِيمًا

Ma yaf‘alu Allahu bi-‘adhabikum in shakartum wa-amantum, wa-kana Allahu Shakiran ‘Alima.

What would God do with your punishment if you show shukr and believe? And God is Shakir and Knowing.

An-Nisa 4:147

God being Shakir cannot mean exactly what it means about the human being. God is not the receiver of a blessing from outside Himself and has no need of the good of His servants.

In this use, God is the One who does not ignore the good and effort of the human being. He knows it, does not waste it, accepts it, and responds to it in a fitting way.

The combination “Shakir and Knowing” is also important. God’s response is built on complete knowledge. He does not see only the outward form of the action. Intention, ability, difficulty of the path, and what remains hidden from others are not overlooked by Him.

We also read:

إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ شَكُورٌ

Inna Allaha Ghafurun Shakur.

Indeed, God is Forgiving and Shakur.

Ash-Shura 42:23

Before this description comes:

وَمَنْ يَقْتَرِفْ حَسَنَةً نَزِدْ لَهُ فِيهَا حُسْنًا

Wa-man yaqtarif hasanatan nazid lahu fiha husna.

Whoever acquires a good deed, We increase for him goodness in it.

God being Shakur shows that the limited good of the human being is not lost with Him. A human being may do a small action, but God does not ignore it and can increase its response.

From this same semantic field, the Qur’an calls the effort of the human being mashkur:

فَأُولَٰئِكَ كَانَ سَعْيُهُمْ مَشْكُورًا

Fa-ula’ika kana sa‘yuhum mashkura.

Those are the ones whose effort was accepted and responded to.

Al-Isra 17:19

And:

وَكَانَ سَعْيُكُمْ مَشْكُورًا

Wa-kana sa‘yukum mashkura.

And your effort was accepted and responded to.

Al-Insan 76:22

A mashkur effort is an effort that has not been ignored and has not been wasted.

This picture of God is very important. God does not ask the human being for shukr so that something may be added to Him, yet He Himself does not forget the smallest real good of the human being.

Blessing Is Not Absolute Ownership

One of the obstacles to shukr is the imagination of absolute ownership. The human being says:

This wealth is mine.

This knowledge is mine.

I acquired this position myself.

This body, time, and ability belong to me, and I will use them however I wish.

But shukr brings the gift close to a trust. Trust does not mean that the human being has no authority or benefit. He can enjoy the blessing, develop it, and build his life with it. But his authority is not unlimited, because every blessing is located inside a network of rights and responsibilities.

A person’s wealth may be the result of his effort, but society, security, the work of others, roads, education, and economic opportunity also have a share in it.

His knowledge may be the result of years of study, but language, books, teachers, and the inheritance of previous human beings made it possible.

Political and social power does not arise without the trust or companionship of others.

Even the human body is in his possession in such a way that he must care for it, not destroy it without reason.

Shukr makes these connections visible. Ingratitude covers them and pulls the human being into the illusion of independence.

The Shukr of Each Blessing Is Proportionate to That Blessing

One fixed form of shukr cannot be set for all blessings. Each gift has its own responsibility.

The shukr of knowledge is truth-seeking and scholarly trustworthiness.

The shukr of power is justice and restraining tyranny.

The shukr of wealth is purity in acquisition, moderation, and observance of the rights of others.

The shukr of freedom is defending the freedom of those who are deprived of it.

The shukr of security is not indifference to the insecurity of others.

The shukr of the tongue is truthfulness and refraining from harm.

The shukr of the pen is clarification and avoidance of deception.

The shukr of love is loyalty and care.

The shukr of forgiveness is reforming behavior.

The shukr of opportunity is using it at the right time.

The shukr of guidance is following the truth that has become clear.

The shukr of life is not merely remaining alive; it is living in a direction that preserves life in oneself and in others.

A behavior may be the shukr of one blessing but not enough for another. Giving wealth may be shukr for wealth, but the shukr of knowledge is not completed by financial giving. Knowledge too must be used in the right direction.

Shukr is not one single prescription for all gifts; it is the fitting response to each gift.

Is Saying “Thank God” Worthless?

This critique does not mean that verbal shukr is worthless.

Saying “thank God” can be a moment of awareness. The tongue helps the human being remember the gift, distance himself from arrogance, and not forget the source of blessing. Sincere speech can be the beginning of shukr.

The problem appears when the beginning is imagined to be the end.

If words are accompanied by recognition and action, they have their place. But if the tongue is separated from life, the phrase of shukr can become a religious covering.

A human being may give thanks for unlawful wealth.

He may perform a prostration of thanks for an oppressive victory.

He may praise God for a position he gained through deception.

He may give thanks for his own rescue while remaining indifferent to those who were destroyed in the same incident.

In such cases, mentioning God’s name does not purify the behavior. It may even sanctify oppression in the human being’s mind.

Verbal shukr is sincere when the human being is ready to ask himself:

What does this blessing ask of me?

What right has come upon me with it?

How can I prevent it from becoming corruption?

Conclusion

Shukr in the Qur’an is much broader than saying “al-hamdu lillah” or “thank God” after receiving good news. These phrases can be part of shukr, but they are not the whole of it.

Shukr begins with seeing the gift. The human being does not lose it in daily habit, does not imagine himself to be its absolute source, and recognizes the responsibility that has come with it. Then he makes this recognition visible in his speech, choice, and action.

For this reason, shukr for provision is connected with purity in earning and consumption. Shukr for guidance is joined with following truth. Shukr for the tools of recognition appears in responsible seeing, hearing, and thinking. Shukr for power is justice and refraining from oppression. Shukr for forgiveness is building a different life after a second opportunity. Shukr for knowledge is trustworthiness and acting according to what is known.

The verse:

اعْمَلُوا آلَ دَاوُودَ شُكْرًا

O family of David, act in shukr.

is one of the clearest summaries of this meaning. Shukr must be seen in life.

The placing of shukr opposite kufr also shows that the matter is not only verbal courtesy. The grateful one recognizes gift and truth and gives a right response to it. The kafir covers it, ignores it, or refuses the fitting response.

Shukr is not for meeting a need of God. Through shukr, the human being is saved from being ruined by the blessing. Blessing without shukr can become a failed test: knowledge becomes arrogance, power becomes tyranny, wealth becomes greed, and religion becomes a tool of domination.

God too is called Shakir and Shakur, not because He needs human blessing, but because He does not ignore the good and effort of His servants, does not waste it, and responds to it.

Real shukr can be summarized as follows:

Shukr is recognizing a gift, not ignoring its value, relating it to its true source, and showing a responsible and proportionate response in awareness, speech, and action.

Therefore, every time we say “thank God,” we must also ask ourselves the unwritten continuation of that sentence:

Now, with what has been given to me, what will I do?
