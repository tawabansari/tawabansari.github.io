<h1>The Disconnected Letters and the Boundary of Human Knowledge Before the Quran</h1>

<p class="subtitle">Stopping, reflecting, and submitting before a text whose certain meaning The God has not opened for us</p>

<h2 id="intro">The central question</h2>

<p>The disconnected letters are among the most visible, yet most silent, signs of the Quran. These letters appear at the beginning of several chapters. They are recited openly, preserved generation after generation, and remain part of the Quranic text; yet the Quran has not opened their certain meaning for us.</p>

<p>This is where the central question appears: What does the human being do before a part of the divine text whose certain meaning he does not know? Does he stop and recognize the boundary of his knowledge? Or does he create meaning to calm his own anxiety, then impose that meaning upon others?</p>

<h2 id="mutashabihat">The disconnected letters and Quranic etiquette before mutashabihat</h2>

<p>This should not lead us to denial, dismissal, or unsupported code-making. The sound path is the path the Quran itself teaches us regarding matters that are mutashabihat: to stop where certain knowledge has not been given to us, and to submit before a truth revealed from The God.</p>

<p>The Quran warns that those in whose hearts is deviation pursue its ta’wil; while those firmly rooted in knowledge say: We believe in it; all of it is from our Lord.</p>

<blockquote class="quran-quote" dir="rtl" lang="ar">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/003-ali-imran/007/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">

  </a>

</p>

<h2 id="claim">The claim of knowing the meaning of the letters</h2>

<p>Some have said that The God and the Messenger know the meaning of these letters. If this is stated only as a possibility, it may not be impossible in reason. But if it is presented as a certain interpretive principle, it requires clear evidence.</p>

<p>The Quran itself does not state this claim explicitly. Nor do we possess a clear, certain, and reliable report in which the Prophet explained the meaning of the disconnected letters as a necessary interpretation for the community.</p>

<p>Quranic caution, therefore, requires avoiding unsupported claims. What has not been established for us cannot be attributed to the text of The God as certain knowledge.</p>

<h2 id="hadith">The disconnected letters and the claim of hadith necessity</h2>

<p>This silence is important, because the disconnected letters are among the most hidden linguistic elements of the Quran. If the guidance-function of the Quran depended on hadith explanations of all its parts, then the disconnected letters should have been among the first places where a clear and certain Prophetic explanation was transmitted.</p>

<p>These letters stand at the beginning of twenty-nine chapters, are recited aloud, and do not carry an ordinary lexical meaning for the reader. Yet we do not possess a certain Prophetic key for decoding them.</p>

<p>This places the claim of the absolute necessity of hadith for understanding the Quran under serious examination. Our point is not to deny the historical value of all reports. Rather, it is to critique the claim that the Quran, in its function as guidance, is incomplete or unintelligible without hadith.</p>

<p>If that claim were true, the disconnected letters should have been the clearest example of the Quran’s need for hadith. Yet the reality is that the Quran has been recited, preserved, and has guided without the decoding of these letters, while presenting itself as a Book in which there is no rayb.</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/002/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    Al-Baqarah 2:2
  </a>
</p>

<h2 id="baqarah">The opening of Surah Al-Baqarah and the absence of rayb in the Book</h2>

<p>The opening of Surah Al-Baqarah is especially worthy of reflection. First comes “Alif Lam Mim”; then immediately after it, the Quran says: “This is the Book in which there is no rayb, a guidance for the mindful.”</p>

<p>This means that the presence of letters whose certain meaning is not clear to us does not harm the Book’s function as guidance. The clarity of the Quran does not mean that the human being has mastery over every layer of the text. Rather, it means that the Book guides the human being toward the path of truth, while also showing him the limits of his own knowledge.</p>

<h2 id="views">The diversity of later views</h2>

<p>In later periods, many views emerged regarding the disconnected letters. Some considered them names of The God; some saw them as divine oaths; some treated them as symbolic abbreviations; some viewed them as signs of the Quran’s inimitability; and some connected them to numerical values.</p>

<p>The diversity of these views itself shows that we are not dealing with a certain teaching transmitted from the Prophet. If a clear Prophetic explanation necessary for the community’s understanding had existed, such wide dispersion in meaning would not have developed.</p>

<p>Human speculation, even when motivated by a desire to understand, must not take the place of certain knowledge. There is a difference between a reflection offered with humility and a claim imposed as the certain meaning of the Quran.</p>

<h2 id="numbers">Numerical and symbolic attempts</h2>

<p>Numerical and symbolic attempts must also be approached with serious caution. Any theory that must set aside part of the Quran in order to prove itself no longer treats the Quran as the criterion; rather, it makes the Quran serve the theory.</p>

<p>For example, in some calculations attributed to Rashad Khalifa and the connection of the Quran to the number 19, the numerical system is made to appear valid only when the final verses of Surah At-Tawbah, verses 128 and 129, are removed from the accepted text of the Quran. Such a method is not compatible with submission before the Quranic text.</p>

<p class="verse-reference">
  <a href="{{ '/quran-reflection/en/009-at-tawbah/128/' | relative_url }}" style="color: inherit; text-decoration: none;">
    At-Tawbah 9:128

</p>

<p>The Quran is the measure; it is not raw material for constructing a numerical system.</p>

<h2 id="hurufism">The experience of letter-centered movements</h2>

<p>History also shows that claiming ownership over the meaning of letters can lead to dangerous paths. In some movements, such as Hurufism, letters became a field for esoteric claims, personal authority, and special spiritual ranks.</p>

<p>Hurufism, associated with Fadlallah Astarabadi in the late eighth century AH, was a movement in which letters and numbers gained a central role in uncovering hidden meanings and secrets of existence. The reader may research this movement separately, but this brief mention is enough to show the danger: when the human being moves beyond “I do not know” and grants himself authority over hidden meaning, he may move from tadabbur into unsupported claim, and from reflection into authority-building.</p>

<h2 id="submission">A school of epistemic submission</h2>

<p>So the central question regarding the disconnected letters is not only: “What do they mean?” The deeper question is: What does the human being do before a part of the divine text whose certain meaning he does not know?</p>

<p>Does he stop and say that its knowledge is with The God? Or does he create meaning to calm his own anxiety, then impose that meaning upon others?</p>

<p>In our view, the disconnected letters are a school of epistemic submission. They teach the human being that Quranic faith is not only in understanding; at times, it is also in recognizing the boundary of one’s own understanding.</p>

<p>The Quran calls us to tadabbur, but tadabbur is not the same as claiming ownership over the unseen dimension of the text. Tadabbur means following the clear signs, examining the coherence of the Book, receiving guidance, and remaining humble before what The God has not opened for us.</p>

<h2 id="clarity">The clarity of the Quran and the boundary of human grasp</h2>

<p>These letters also show that the Quran can be both clear and layered beyond full human grasp. The clarity of the Quran does not mean every detail of it has been placed under the control of the human mind. Rather, it means that the path of guidance, furqan, remembrance, and return to The God is clear within it.</p>

<p>The Quran describes itself as guidance for people and furqan, while also speaking of mutashabihat. These two truths do not contradict one another.</p>

<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>

<p>In the end, the disconnected letters are not a weakness in the Quran; they expose the limit of the human being. They show that the Book of The God both calls the human being to think and restrains him from unsupported claims.</p>

<p>The one who stops before these letters has not abandoned reason. Rather, he has protected his reason from trespassing into a realm that has not been made clear to him.</p>

<p>So the best statement before these letters is the Quranic etiquette itself: We believe in it; all of it is from our Lord.</p>
