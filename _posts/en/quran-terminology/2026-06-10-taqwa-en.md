  <section id="intro">
    <h2>Introduction</h2>

    <p>
      One of the words that has been greatly reduced in common translations of the Qur’an is the word <strong>taqwa</strong>. In many translations, taqwa is simply rendered as “fear.” Although this translation may sometimes show part of the effect of taqwa, it does not fully carry its original and root meaning.
    </p>

    <p>
      Taqwa in the Qur’an is not merely an emotion of fear. Taqwa is an inner conscious state: care, self-guarding, moral wakefulness, responsible orientation, and placing a shield between oneself and collapse.
    </p>

    <p>
      If we understand taqwa only as “fear,” then religion becomes a fear-centered relationship. But if we understand taqwa from its Qur’anic and linguistic root, religion becomes a path of awareness, responsibility, choice, purity, and salvation.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="root">
    <h2>The Linguistic Root of Taqwa</h2>

    <p>
      The word <strong>taqwa</strong> comes from the root <strong>و ق ی</strong>. This root is connected to the meaning of <strong>wiqāyah</strong>: to preserve, to guard, to protect, to cover, to take a shield, and to keep oneself away from harm.
    </p>

    <p>
      In simple language, taqwa means that a human being places a conscious shield between themselves and danger.
    </p>

    <p>
      But this danger is not only external danger. A human being must sometimes guard themselves from injustice, arrogance, falsehood, lust, greed, unfairness, blind imitation, worship of power, worship of people, and forgetfulness of God. Therefore, taqwa means that a person knows where they must stand, what they must guard themselves against, and before which truth they must regulate themselves.
    </p>

    <p>
      From here, it becomes clear that translating taqwa as “fear” is very limited. Fear may sometimes awaken a person, but taqwa is not merely being afraid. Taqwa means being consciously watchful.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="fear">
    <h2>Does Taqwa Mean Fear?</h2>

    <p>
      To see the depth of this issue, let us look at verse 52 of Surah An-Nahl:
    </p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic-quote">
        وَلَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَلَهُ الدِّينُ وَاصِبًا ۚ أَفَغَيْرَ اللَّهِ تَتَّقُونَ
      </p>
      <p>
        “And to Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth; and to Him belongs the deen continually. So do you place other than God as the center of your taqwa?”
      </p>
      <p>
        <a href="/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/052/">An-Nahl 16:52</a>
      </p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>
      The common translation usually says: “Do you fear other than God?”
    </p>

    <p>
      But if we pay attention to the root <strong>و ق ی</strong>, the question of the verse is deeper. The verse is not only asking: “Do you fear other than God?” Rather, it is asking: when everything belongs to God, and the deen continually belongs to Him, why do you place the center of your caution, care, orientation, and regulation of life in other than God?
    </p>

    <p>
      This difference is not small. If we take taqwa only as fear, religion becomes an emotional and passive state. But if we understand taqwa as conscious care, the verse frees the human being from hidden servitude before other than God.
    </p>

    <p>
      A person sometimes regulates their behavior based on fear of people; sometimes based on fear of leaders, scholars, social traditions, systems of power, family, tribe, or public judgment. In such a state, the center of taqwa is transferred from God toward creation.
    </p>

    <p>
      The Qur’an critiques this. Taqwa means that the center of a human being’s care and accountability must be God — not people, not power, not tradition, not social fear, and not religious structures that place themselves in God’s position.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="worship">
    <h2>Worship Is for Reaching Taqwa</h2>

    <p>
      From the beginning of Surah Al-Baqarah, the Qur’an makes the purpose of worship clear:
    </p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic-quote">
        يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ اعْبُدُوا رَبَّكُمُ الَّذِي خَلَقَكُمْ وَالَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ
      </p>
      <p>
        “O people, worship your Lord — the One who created you and those before you — so that you may reach taqwa.”
      </p>
      <p>
        <a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/021/">Al-Baqarah 2:21</a>
      </p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>
      This verse is very important. God does not need our worship. A human being does not add anything to God through worship. God is God before our worship and without our worship: complete, free of need, living, sustaining, and the Owner of all things.
    </p>

    <p>
      Therefore, worship does not benefit God; its benefit returns to the human being. The verse says: worship, <strong>so that you may reach taqwa</strong>. Meaning, the fruit of worship is the formation of taqwa within the human being.
    </p>

    <p>
      If worship does not make a person more watchful, more just, more pure, more truthful, more responsible, and more awake, then one must reflect again on the meaning of that worship. Qur’anic worship must build a shield within the human being: a shield against collapse, injustice, cruelty, arrogance, heedlessness, and servitude to other than God.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="fasting">
    <h2>Fasting Is Also for Taqwa</h2>

    <p>
      The Qur’an expresses this same truth about fasting:
    </p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic-quote">
        يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ
      </p>
      <p>
        “O you who have attained faith, fasting has been written upon you, as it was written upon those before you, so that you may reach taqwa.”
      </p>
      <p>
        <a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/183/">Al-Baqarah 2:183</a>
      </p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>
      So fasting is not merely hunger. If fasting is only enduring hunger and thirst, but it does not keep a person away from falsehood, injustice, anger, wastefulness, worship of desire, humiliation of others, and cruelty, then it has not yet reached its Qur’anic destination.
    </p>

    <p>
      Fasting is a training in taqwa. In fasting, the human being learns that they can control their immediate desire. They can say to their body: no. They can say to their nafs: you do not own me. This training, if understood correctly, frees the human being from servitude to the stomach, habit, desire, anger, and momentary impulses.
    </p>

    <p>
      So fasting is a school of self-guarding. And conscious self-guarding is the heart of the meaning of taqwa.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="sacrifice">
    <h2>Taqwa and Sacrifice: God Does Not Need Meat and Blood</h2>

    <p>
      The Qur’an also makes this same principle clear regarding sacrifice:
    </p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic-quote">
        لَن يَنَالَ اللَّهَ لُحُومُهَا وَلَا دِمَاؤُهَا وَلَـٰكِن يَنَالُهُ التَّقْوَىٰ مِنكُمْ
      </p>
      <p>
        “Neither their meat reaches God, nor their blood; rather, what reaches Him is the taqwa from you.”
      </p>
      <p>
        <a href="/quran-reflection/en/022-al-hajj/037/">Al-Hajj 22:37</a>
      </p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>
      This verse is one of the clearest verses about God’s freedom from need regarding the outer form of worship. God does not need meat. He does not need blood. He does not need religious display. What has value is the taqwa that takes shape within the human being.
    </p>

    <p>
      So if sacrifice, worship, prayer, fasting, Hajj, giving, or any other religious act does not bring the human being to taqwa, the outer form of the act remains, but its spirit has been lost.
    </p>

    <p>
      God does not want performance from us. He wants awareness, truthfulness, care, and inner reform. Worship is alive when it brings the human being closer to the state of taqwa.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="justice">
    <h2>Taqwa and Justice</h2>

    <p>
      Taqwa is not only an individual relationship between the human being and God. Taqwa must also be seen in social justice. The Qur’an says:
    </p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic-quote">
        اعْدِلُوا هُوَ أَقْرَبُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ
      </p>
      <p>
        “Be just; that is closer to taqwa.”
      </p>
      <p>
        <a href="/quran-reflection/en/005-al-maidah/008/">Al-Ma’idah 5:8</a>
      </p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>
      This verse shows that taqwa without justice is an incomplete claim. A person cannot consider themselves muttaqī while acting unjustly in judgment, hostility, disagreement, power, family, trade, religion, or politics.
    </p>

    <p>
      Even hostility toward a group must not take a person away from justice. Taqwa means that a human being remains watchful over truth even in anger, disagreement, and enmity. This is the inner shield: a shield that does not allow a person to fall away from justice in the name of religion, tribe, sect, politics, or revenge.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="clothing">
    <h2>Taqwa and the Inner Clothing of the Human Being</h2>

    <p>
      In Surah Al-A’raf, after speaking about outward clothing, the Qur’an points to a deeper truth:
    </p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic-quote">
        وَلِبَاسُ التَّقْوَىٰ ذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ
      </p>
      <p>
        “And the clothing of taqwa — that is better.”
      </p>
      <p>
        <a href="/quran-reflection/en/007-al-araf/026/">Al-A’raf 7:26</a>
      </p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>
      Clothing covers the human body and protects it from exposure and harm. But the human being does not only have a body. The nafs, heart, ethics, and character of the human being also need covering and protection.
    </p>

    <p>
      The clothing of taqwa means an inner covering that protects the human being from moral nakedness. A person may have outward clothing, yet be morally naked: cruel, arrogant, false, unjust, enslaved to desire, or obsessed with power. The Qur’an reminds us that true covering is not only fabric; taqwa is also clothing.
    </p>

    <p>
      This verse again shows that taqwa is not merely fear. Taqwa is covering, protection, beauty, and inner guardianship within the human being.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="salvation">
    <h2>Taqwa and Salvation from the Fire</h2>

    <p>
      Taqwa in the Qur’an is not merely an ethical virtue; it is also the path of salvation. In Surah Maryam, it says:
    </p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic-quote">
        ثُمَّ نُنَجِّي الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوا وَّنَذَرُ الظَّالِمِينَ فِيهَا جِثِيًّا
      </p>
      <p>
        “Then We save those who had taqwa, and We leave the wrongdoers therein, fallen on their knees.”
      </p>
      <p>
        <a href="/quran-reflection/en/019-maryam/072/">Maryam 19:72</a>
      </p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>
      Here, taqwa is directly connected to salvation. This is very important. What saves the human being from the final collapse is not merely a religious name, a claim of faith, group affiliation, or the outward form of worship; rather, it is that state of care and self-guarding that protects the human being from injustice, corruption, denial, arrogance, and irresponsibility.
    </p>

    <p>
      In contrast to taqwa, the verse speaks of the wrongdoers. This shows that the opposite of taqwa is not merely “not being afraid”; rather, it is injustice. Meaning, the one who lacks taqwa loses their inner shield and moves closer to injustice: injustice against oneself, injustice against people, injustice against the earth, injustice against truth, and injustice in relation to God.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="ihsan">
    <h2>Taqwa and Ihsan</h2>

    <p>
      The Qur’an also connects taqwa with ihsan:
    </p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic-quote">
        إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوا وَالَّذِينَ هُم مُّحْسِنُونَ
      </p>
      <p>
        “Indeed, God is with those who have taqwa and those who are doers of ihsan.”
      </p>
      <p>
        <a href="/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/128/">An-Nahl 16:128</a>
      </p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>
      Taqwa does not only hold a person back from evil; it must also move them toward better goodness. Taqwa is like a shield that keeps the human being from falling, and ihsan is like a higher direction that brings the human being to beauty in action.
    </p>

    <p>
      The person of taqwa does not only say: “I did not sin.” They ask: “What is the best thing I can do?” This is where taqwa comes close to ihsan. Taqwa is care, and ihsan is the most beautiful response of the human being to that care.
    </p>

    <p>
      Therefore, the complete fruit of taqwa is that a person moves away from evil and comes closer to the best action — not out of blind fear, but out of awareness, love, responsibility, and the presence of God in the conscience.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="guidance">
    <h2>Taqwa and the Guidance of the Qur’an</h2>

    <p>
      Now we can understand why Surah Al-Baqarah says at the beginning:
    </p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic-quote">
        ذَٰلِكَ الْكِتَابُ لَا رَيْبَ ۛ فِيهِ ۛ هُدًى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ
      </p>
      <p>
        “This is the Book; there is no doubt in it; a guidance for the muttaqīn.”
      </p>
      <p>
        <a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/002/">Al-Baqarah 2:2</a>
      </p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>
      The Qur’an was sent down for all people, but its guidance becomes alive in the one who has the state of taqwa. Meaning, the one who is ready for care, ready for reform, ready to hear the truth, and who takes a shield against their own ego, arrogance, imitation, and desire.
    </p>

    <p>
      An unguarded heart can make excuses even from the verses of God. But a heart of taqwa finds the way through those same verses.
    </p>

    <p>
      So taqwa is a precondition for the guidance becoming alive within the human being. The Qur’an is like rain; but land that is closed, stony, arrogant, or polluted does not benefit from rain in the same way as land that is ready and receptive.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section id="summary">
    <h2>Summary</h2>

    <p>
      Taqwa in the Qur’an is not merely fear.
    </p>

    <p>
      Taqwa means that the human being places themselves in a state of conscious care. It means building a shield between oneself and collapse. It means knowing that one is accountable before God, staying away from injustice, corruption, and arrogance, and regulating one’s life not according to fear of people, but according to awareness of God.
    </p>

    <p>
      Worship is for reaching taqwa. Fasting is for reaching taqwa. Sacrifice and rituals without taqwa do not reach God. Justice is closer to taqwa. Taqwa is the inner clothing of the human being. Taqwa is the shield of salvation from the Fire. And taqwa becomes more complete when it brings the human being closer to ihsan and the best action.
    </p>

    <p>
      So the muttaqī is not someone who merely fears. The muttaqī is someone who is awake, watchful, self-guarding, moving closer to justice, moving away from injustice, and placing the center of their accountability only in that One Worshiped God.
    </p>

    <p>
      <strong>Taqwa means living with a shield of awareness before God.</strong>
    </p>
  </section>
