  <section class="article-section" id="introduction">
    <h2>The question of qada and qadar</h2>
    <p>When we think about qada and qadar, divine decree and measured destiny, the discussion can be approached in two parts. The first part is reflective and rational. Its purpose is not to prove a complete philosophical model of existence, but to free the mind from quick assumptions, to help us see the greatness of The God’s creation, to widen our understanding of justice and choice, and to remind the human being of the limits of human knowledge before such matters.</p>
    <p>The second part returns directly to the Qur’an. In the end, the final criterion of truth for us is the speech of The God, not speculation and not human theory.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="two-extremes">
    <h2>1. Two extremes: total determinism and absolute autonomy</h2>
    <p>In discussions about free will, determinism, qadar, and compulsion, two extreme views often appear.</p>
    <p>The first view says that everything has already been determined and the human being has no real choice. According to this view, since The God knows the beginning, the middle, and the end of everything, human life is like a movie already recorded. A person simply acts out a role without truly choosing anything. If this were true, then trial, responsibility, and even justice would become difficult to understand.</p>
    <p>The second view stands at the opposite end. It says that the human being is completely free and fully creates his own destiny. In this view, The God knows what will happen, but this knowledge plays no role in the formation of the human path; He is imagined almost as an aware observer.</p>
    <p>But if we look carefully and fairly, reality is not fully captured by either extreme.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="unchosen-frame">
    <h2>2. The human being begins inside an unchosen frame</h2>
    <p>The truth is that the human being enters this world within conditions he did not choose. No one chose the time of his birth, the land in which he was born, the family in which he grew, the body with which he entered the world, the abilities he received, or the limitations he carried. No one chose the language, culture, society, economic condition, inherited wounds, or intellectual environment that surrounded him at the beginning.</p>
    <p>Yet inside this very frame, The God has placed real choice. These choices may not be unlimited, but they are real. At every stage of life, the human being meets possible paths, and he decides which path to take.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="real-choice">
    <h2>3. Real choice inside real limits</h2>
    <p>Consider a person born without legs. He did not choose that condition. He may live in a society that does not properly support people with special needs. He may lack money, social support, accessibility, or equal opportunity. All of these realities stand outside his initial choice.</p>
    <p>But even within such conditions, choice still exists. He may surrender or strive. He may take a righteous path or a destructive one. Each choice opens a new door to consequences and further choices.</p>
    <p>This means that human freedom should not be imagined as the freedom of a being without limits. The human being enters the world inside a frame, but within that frame he still makes real choices. That is why responsibility has meaning.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="yusuf">
    <h2>4. Yusuf: unchosen circumstances and moral choice</h2>
    <p>To understand this more deeply, we can look at the story of Yusuf, peace be upon him. Yusuf was sold as a child by the actions of his brothers. He did not choose to be thrown into the well. He did not choose to be taken into the market of slavery. If, instead of being bought into the house of the Egyptian official, he had been bought by a corrupt and cruel person and raised in a polluted environment, would the shape of his life have remained exactly the same? Or could the pressure of those conditions have opened another kind of path?</p>
    <p>This example shows that environment truly matters. At the same time, human will and moral choice also have their place. Yusuf chose purity, patience, and truthfulness inside conditions he did not create, and those choices shaped the rest of his life.</p>
    <p>The same principle can be seen in history. Major events are shaped by many human choices, social conditions, political pressures, and hidden factors. If surrounding people had chosen differently, history may have unfolded differently. Reality is not conditions alone and not choice alone; it is the interaction of conditions and choices.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="limited-knowledge">
    <h2>5. Prayer, possible paths, and the limits of human knowledge</h2>
    <p>When The God calls Himself Rabb al-‘alamin, Lord of all worlds, the phrase itself opens our awareness to a creation greater than what human beings experience directly. Just as the material universe is far larger than earlier human beings imagined, there may be dimensions of reality that remain beyond our present ability to grasp fully.</p>
    <p>As a thought experiment, not as a definite claim about the structure of creation, we can imagine that there may be paths of existence other than the one we know. There may be realities in which certain historical tragedies did not unfold as they did here, or in which people lived under different conditions, with different openings, tests, and possible choices.</p>
    <p>Perhaps this is not one path only, but countless possible paths of existence, each with its own conditions and possibilities, and in each one human beings still choose and live with the results of their choices.</p>
    <p>Perhaps prayer is also not merely a word or wish, but a bridge by which The God directs a human life toward conditions in which better choices, deeper growth, and fuller understanding become possible.</p>
    <p>The point of this reflection is not to make a firm claim about the architecture of creation. The point is to remember that human knowledge is deeply limited. What we see of reality is only a small part of a truth far greater than us. The creation of The God can be wider and deeper than the human mind can fully contain.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="field-of-possibility">
    <h2>6. Qada and qadar as a field of possibility, not the denial of choice</h2>
    <p>Perhaps this is why the human being must finally submit to the justice of The God; not with a weak submission, but with the submission that comes from understanding that The God’s knowledge is complete while human knowledge is partial. The God sees every condition, every possible path, every choice, and every consequence. The human being sees only a small portion.</p>
    <p>If a person trusts that The God is just and wrongs no one, even when he does not understand the full wisdom behind an event, that trust may be part of the real meaning of faith.</p>
    <p>This is why The God says that He does not burden any soul beyond its capacity. Only He knows the true capacity of every human being and the full conditions surrounding that person. In this world, people often judge by visible actions because their knowledge is limited. The God judges with perfect knowledge: knowledge of every possible result of every possible choice within every possible condition.</p>
    <p>If we summarize this in the language of qada and qadar, we can say: qada and qadar do not mean the removal of human choice. They point to a divine order in which the conditions, possibilities, and paths of each human life are encompassed by the knowledge of The God, while the choice of path within that frame is still entrusted to the human being.</p>
    <p>The God knows the starting point, all possible paths, and the outcome of every choice. Yet the human being chooses among those paths and meets the result of that choice. Qada and qadar are the frame and field of possibility. Free will is the human movement inside that field. What finally happens is neither blind compulsion nor absolute independence, but the realization of real human choices inside an order already encompassed by the complete knowledge of The God.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="pharmacy">
    <h2>7. The pharmacy example: condition, law, knowledge, and choice</h2>
    <p>Imagine a person born with diabetes. This condition was not something he chose. He did not choose the way his body would form or the weakness with which he would enter the world. This represents the part of qada and qadar that stands outside human choice.</p>
    <p>Now imagine that this person enters a pharmacy. There are many medicines there. Some help control his condition, some do nothing, and some can make him worse. The pharmacist knows the laws of the body, the effect of each medicine, and the result of each choice. Yet the final choice belongs to the patient. He may choose the right medicine, ignore treatment, or choose something that harms him. The result of each choice belongs to a system already built upon real laws, but the act of choosing is still his.</p>
    <p>In the absolute knowledge of The God, not only are the choices this person makes in his present condition known, but all possible paths he could have taken under other conditions are also known. The God knows what kind of person he would have become if he had not been born with this illness, what choices he would have made, what paths he would have taken, and what the result of each would have been. The God knows whether he would have been more grateful or more heedless in ease, more just or more oppressive in greater power.</p>
    <p>For this reason, divine judgment is not based merely on the visible shape of a person’s life. It is based on The God’s complete knowledge of real capacity, real conditions, and all possible openings available to that human being. The God does not judge a person by comparing him superficially with others, but by knowing his own true capacity.</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا</p>
      <p class="translation">The God does not burden any soul except according to its capacity.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/286/">Al-Baqarah 2:286</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>This means that illness, limitation, or hardship is not in itself a sign of injustice. It is part of the specific conditions of that person’s test. The justice of The God is that He knows not only what a person did, but what he could have done, what he would have done under other conditions, and what was truly within his capacity.</p>
    <p>In the pharmacy example, a just judge would not look only at how quickly the patient recovered. He would look at what choice the patient made in light of the real condition of his body. The God judges with complete and flawless knowledge.</p>
    <p>In this example, the initial illness represents the conditions into which a person enters without choosing them: the part of qada and qadar outside human control. The medicines represent possible paths. The pharmacist’s knowledge represents divine knowledge of all paths and their results. The patient’s choice represents human free will.</p>
    <p>So qada and qadar do not mean that the human being is forced to walk one fixed path. They mean that all paths, conditions, and consequences are already known to The God, while the real movement among those paths is made by the human being through choice. The human being does not create the laws, but inside those laws he chooses.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="divine-knowledge">
    <h2>8. The Qur’an and the all-encompassing knowledge of The God</h2>
    <p>Now we turn directly to the Noble Qur’an, because the Qur’an itself explains the relation between divine knowledge, human choice, trial, and justice in judgment.</p>
    <p>The God first describes the reach of His knowledge:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَعِندَهُ مَفَاتِحُ الْغَيْبِ لَا يَعْلَمُهَا إِلَّا هُوَ ۚ وَيَعْلَمُ مَا فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ ۚ وَمَا تَسْقُطُ مِن وَرَقَةٍ إِلَّا يَعْلَمُهَا وَلَا حَبَّةٍ فِي ظُلُمَاتِ الْأَرْضِ وَلَا رَطْبٍ وَلَا يَابِسٍ إِلَّا فِي كِتَابٍ مُّبِينٍ</p>
      <p class="translation">With Him are the keys of the unseen; none knows them except Him. He knows what is on land and sea. No leaf falls except that He knows it, nor a grain in the darknesses of the earth, nor anything moist or dry, except that it is in a clear record.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/006-al-anam/059/">Al-An‘am 6:59</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>This verse shows that The God’s knowledge is complete. No event, choice, or condition stands outside His knowledge.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="test">
    <h2>9. Worldly life as trial</h2>
    <p>The same Qur’an that speaks of The God’s absolute knowledge also explains the purpose of worldly life:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">الَّذِي خَلَقَ الْمَوْتَ وَالْحَيَاةَ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْغَفُورُ</p>
      <p class="translation">He created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in action, and He is The Mighty, The Forgiving.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/067-al-mulk/002/">Al-Mulk 67:2</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>When The God says that the purpose of life is trial, this itself shows that human action is real and meaningful. A trial has meaning only where real choice exists.</p>
    <p>The God also explains the nature of this trial:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَلَنَبْلُوَنَّكُم بِشَيْءٍ مِّنَ الْخَوْفِ وَالْجُوعِ وَنَقْصٍ مِّنَ الْأَمْوَالِ وَالْأَنفُسِ وَالثَّمَرَاتِ ۗ وَبَشِّرِ الصَّابِرِينَ ۝ الَّذِينَ إِذَا أَصَابَتْهُم مُّصِيبَةٌ قَالُوا إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ</p>
      <p class="translation">We will surely test you with something of fear, hunger, loss of wealth, lives, and fruits. Give good news to the patient, those who, when a calamity reaches them, say: We belong to The God, and to Him we return.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/155/">Al-Baqarah 2:155-156</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>This verse shows clearly that hardship, including the loss of life, wealth, health, or security, belongs to the reality of trial. It is not, by itself, a sign of injustice.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="responsibility">
    <h2>10. Human responsibility and what the soul earns</h2>
    <p>At the same time, the Qur’an directly places responsibility for choice upon the human being:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَقُلِ الْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّكُمْ ۖ فَمَن شَاءَ فَلْيُؤْمِن وَمَن شَاءَ فَلْيَكْفُرْ</p>
      <p class="translation">Say: The truth is from your Lord. So whoever wills, let him believe; and whoever wills, let him cover the truth.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/018-al-kahf/029/">Al-Kahf 18:29</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">كُلُّ نَفْسٍ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ رَهِينَةٌ</p>
      <p class="translation">Every soul is held in pledge for what it has earned.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/074-al-muddaththir/038/">Al-Muddaththir 74:38</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَأَن لَّيْسَ لِلْإِنسَانِ إِلَّا مَا سَعَىٰ ۝ وَأَنَّ سَعْيَهُ سَوْفَ يُرَىٰ</p>
      <p class="translation">And that the human being has nothing except what he strives for, and that his striving will soon be seen.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/053-an-najm/039/">An-Najm 53:39-40</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>These verses show that the human being has real choice and that his actions are the basis of judgment.</p>
    <p>Yet the Qur’an also makes clear that human will is not outside the will of The God:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">لِمَن شَاءَ مِنكُمْ أَن يَسْتَقِيمَ ۝ وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَن يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ</p>
      <p class="translation">For whoever among you wills to go straight; and you do not will except that The God, Lord of all worlds, wills.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/081-at-takwir/028/">At-Takwir 81:28-29</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>This verse expresses the balance we reached earlier: the human being wills and chooses, but this willing occurs within an order encompassed by The God.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="shakilah">
    <h2>11. Shakilah: a Qur’anic key to condition and choice</h2>
    <p>At this point, the Qur’an adds a more subtle key, perhaps one of the most important verses for understanding the relation between condition, personality, and action:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">قُلْ كُلٌّ يَعْمَلُ عَلَىٰ شَاكِلَتِهِ فَرَبُّكُمْ أَعْلَمُ بِمَنْ هُوَ أَهْدَىٰ سَبِيلًا</p>
      <p class="translation">Say: Each acts according to his shakilah, and your Lord knows best who is more guided in way.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/017-al-isra/084/">Al-Isra 17:84</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>Shakilah is more than a simple trait. It points to the formed shape of a person: nature, upbringing, environment, experience, and previous choices all gathered into a living inner form. No two human beings enter the world with exactly the same formation, and no two human beings become exactly the same through life. A person raised in safety and affection has one shakilah; a person raised in poverty, violence, illness, or humiliation has another.</p>
    <p>This verse gathers the example of Yusuf and the pharmacy example into one Qur’anic sentence: the human being acts from his shakilah, from the point to which unchosen conditions and previous choices have brought him. Yet the action remains his action; it is not something merely forced upon him.</p>
    <p>The most important part is what follows: “your Lord knows best who is more guided in way.” Only The God truly knows who is more guided, because only The God knows from what point each person began and how far each person actually moved. Human beings usually judge by visible action. The God knows the real starting point, the real burden, the real capacity, and the real striving.</p>
    <p>Someone who begins from a harder shakilah and takes a small step may, before The God, be more guided than someone who begins from an easier shakilah and takes a larger visible step. Judgment is not based on the surface speed of recovery, but on the real condition of the soul.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="wus">
    <h2>12. Capacity, justice, and divine judgment</h2>
    <p>The central principle of divine justice in judgment is stated clearly:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا ۚ لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتْ وَعَلَيْهَا مَا اكْتَسَبَتْ</p>
      <p class="translation">The God does not burden any soul except according to its capacity. For it is what it has earned, and against it is what it has earned.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/286/">Al-Baqarah 2:286</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">لَا نُكَلِّفُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا وَلَدَيْنَا كِتَابٌ يَنطِقُ بِالْحَقِّ وَهُمْ لَا يُظْلَمُونَ</p>
      <p class="translation">We do not burden any soul except according to its capacity, and with Us is a record that speaks with truth, and they will not be wronged.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/023-al-muminun/062/">Al-Mu’minun 23:62</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>These verses, together with the verse of shakilah, form a complete picture. The God knows each person’s nature, conditions, formation, striving, and real capacity. He judges on that basis, not by a shallow comparison between people.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section" id="akhirah">
    <h2>13. This world in relation to the Hereafter</h2>
    <p>The Qur’an also corrects our view of worldly life:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">وَمَا هَذِهِ الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا إِلَّا لَهْوٌ وَلَعِبٌ ۚ وَإِنَّ الدَّارَ الْآخِرَةَ لَهِيَ الْحَيَوَانُ ۚ لَوْ كَانُوا يَعْلَمُونَ</p>
      <p class="translation">This worldly life is nothing but diversion and play, and the home of the Hereafter is truly the life, if they only knew.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/029-al-ankabut/064/">Al-‘Ankabut 29:64</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">بَلْ تُؤْثِرُونَ الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا ۝ وَالْآخِرَةُ خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَىٰ</p>
      <p class="translation">Rather, you prefer the worldly life, while the Hereafter is better and more lasting.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/087-al-ala/016/">Al-A‘la 87:16-17</a></p>
    </blockquote>

    <p>These verses show that worldly life is brief and passing in relation to the Hereafter, and final judgment takes place within a frame much greater than this limited life.</p>
    <p>Finally, The God states the foundation of His justice:</p>

    <blockquote class="quran-quote">
      <p class="arabic">إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَظْلِمُ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ</p>
      <p class="translation">Indeed, The God does not wrong by the weight of an atom.</p>
      <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/004-an-nisa/040/">An-Nisa 4:40</a></p>
    </blockquote>
  </section>

  <section class="article-section conclusion" id="conclusion">
    <h2>Conclusion</h2>
    <p>According to the clear text of the Qur’an: The God knows all things; worldly life is a place of trial; the human being has real choice; each person acts from his shakilah; and The God knows better than anyone who is more guided in way. The God judges every human being according to real capacity and real conditions, and His final judgment is completely just.</p>
    <p>This means that no suffering, no limitation, and no personal formation stands outside the knowledge, justice, and precise reckoning of The God. No human being will be held accountable beyond what was truly within his capacity.</p>
    <p class="closing">Qada and qadar are not blind compulsion. They are the divine field of possibility, knowledge, law, and justice; and human free will is the real movement of the human being within that field.</p>
  </section>
