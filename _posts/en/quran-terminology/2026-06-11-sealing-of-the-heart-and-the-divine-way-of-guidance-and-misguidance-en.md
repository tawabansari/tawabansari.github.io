  <h2 id="introduction">Introduction: the difficult question of the sealing of the heart</h2>

  <p>One of the most difficult questions in understanding the Noble Qur’an is this: when The God says that He has sealed hearts, or that He guides some and misguides others, what does that mean? Does The God guide one person and misguide another without criterion, without justice, and merely according to arbitrary will? Is the human being, from the beginning, the victim of a decision imposed in advance? Is a sealed heart closed from the start, without any choice or responsibility?</p>

  <p>A superficial reading may lead a person to this dangerous conclusion, as though The God makes some people fortunate without reason and makes others ruined without reason. But such a reading is incompatible with Qur’anic justice, with human responsibility, and with many other verses of the Qur’an. The Qur’an itself does not allow us to read the verses of guidance and misguidance apart from divine ways, human choice, repeated denial, prejudice, injustice, corruption, and conscious turning away.</p>

  <p>The Qur’an explains the Qur’an: guidance and misguidance are in the hand of The God, but not in the sense of disorder or arbitrariness. The God owns all causes, laws, ways, effects, and outcomes. He is the final cause of all effects, yet His acts run according to wisdom, justice, knowledge, and the fixed ways of creation. The permission of The God and the hand of The God in the world are not outside law and wisdom; rather, they are the deep order of existence, the ways of creation, and the spiritual and moral algorithms that The God has placed within the human being and the world.</p>

  <p>So when the Qur’an says that The God guides someone or misguides someone, we must ask: whom does The God guide? Whom does He misguide? And according to which divine way?</p>

  <h2 id="baqarah-six-seven">The foundational verse: sealing after conscious rejection</h2>

  <p>At the beginning of Surah Al-Baqarah, after introducing the Qur’an as guidance for the muttaqin, The God speaks of a group for whom warning and not warning have become the same:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">إِنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا سَوَاءٌ عَلَيْهِمْ أَأَنذَرْتَهُمْ أَمْ لَمْ تُنذِرْهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ ۝ خَتَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَعَلَىٰ سَمْعِهِمْ ۖ وَعَلَىٰ أَبْصَارِهِمْ غِشَاوَةٌ ۖ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ</p>
    <p class="translation">Indeed, those who disbelieved, it is the same for them whether you warn them or do not warn them; they will not believe. The God has sealed their hearts and their hearing, and over their sight is a covering, and for them is a great punishment.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/">Al-Baqarah 2:6–7</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>These verses are not about every uninformed person, every questioner, every doubter, or every person to whom the truth has not yet become clear. The verse says: “those who disbelieved.” In the language of the Qur’an, kufr is not merely not knowing; it is covering. The kafir is one who covers a truth that should be seen, denies a blessing that should be recognized, and pushes aside a sign that should be taken seriously.</p>

  <p>Therefore, the sealing of the heart in this context is not the beginning of the story; it is the result of a condition. First comes kufr, covering, the ineffectiveness of warning, and the closing of the ear before truth. Then comes the divine seal as the result and stabilization of that same path.</p>

  <p>This point is extremely important: The God does not seal, without reason, a heart that is still alive, seeking, fair, and ready to accept truth. The sealing of the heart is the end of a path of deviation, not the beginning of divine injustice.</p>

  <h2 id="only-fasiqin">The God misguides only the defiantly corrupt through His examples</h2>

  <p>In the same opening of Al-Baqarah, the Qur’an states another important principle: through one sign, The God guides one group and misguides another; but immediately explains who falls under divine misguidance:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَسْتَحْيِي أَن يَضْرِبَ مَثَلًا مَّا بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا ۚ فَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا فَيَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّهُ الْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّهِمْ ۖ وَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا فَيَقُولُونَ مَاذَا أَرَادَ اللَّهُ بِهَـٰذَا مَثَلًا ۘ يُضِلُّ بِهِ كَثِيرًا وَيَهْدِي بِهِ كَثِيرًا ۚ وَمَا يُضِلُّ بِهِ إِلَّا الْفَاسِقِينَ</p>
    <p class="translation">Indeed, The God is not ashamed to strike an example, whether of a mosquito or what is above it. As for those who believe, they know that it is the truth from their Lord. But as for those who disbelieve, they say: What did The God intend by this as an example? He misguides many by it and guides many by it; and He misguides by it none except the defiantly corrupt.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/">Al-Baqarah 2:26</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse is a key to understanding the issue. The God says that through one sign, one example, one verse, one truth, two kinds of response emerge. For the believer seeking truth, the same sign increases understanding. For the arrogant and defiantly corrupt person, the same sign becomes an excuse for denial. But the Qur’an does not allow us to say that The God misguides whomever He wants without reason. The verse states explicitly: “He misguides by it none except the defiantly corrupt.”</p>

  <p>So divine misguidance is not without rule. Defiant corruption, leaving the path of truth, breaking covenant, cutting what The God commanded to be joined, and causing corruption on the earth are the soil of misguidance.</p>

  <p>The next verse explains these defiantly corrupt people:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">الَّذِينَ يَنقُضُونَ عَهْدَ اللَّهِ مِن بَعْدِ مِيثَاقِهِ وَيَقْطَعُونَ مَا أَمَرَ اللَّهُ بِهِ أَن يُوصَلَ وَيُفْسِدُونَ فِي الْأَرْضِ ۚ أُولَـٰئِكَ هُمُ الْخَاسِرُونَ</p>
    <p class="translation">Those who break the covenant of The God after its firm binding, and cut what The God commanded to be joined, and cause corruption on the earth. Those are the losers.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/">Al-Baqarah 2:27</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This means that divine misguidance is the wise response of the divine order to a moral and existential condition, not an initial injustice against the human being.</p>

  <h2 id="when-they-deviated">When they deviated, The God caused their hearts to deviate</h2>

  <p>One of the clearest Qur’anic verses about the divine way of guidance and misguidance is the fifth verse of Surah As-Saff:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">وَإِذْ قَالَ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِ يَا قَوْمِ لِمَ تُؤْذُونَنِي وَقَد تَّعْلَمُونَ أَنِّي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ إِلَيْكُمْ ۖ فَلَمَّا زَاغُوا أَزَاغَ اللَّهُ قُلُوبَهُمْ ۚ وَاللَّهُ لَا يَهْدِي الْقَوْمَ الْفَاسِقِينَ</p>
    <p class="translation">And when Moses said to his people: O my people, why do you harm me while you know that I am the Messenger of The God to you? So when they deviated, The God caused their hearts to deviate. And The God does not guide the defiantly corrupt people.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/061-as-saff/005/">As-Saff 61:5</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse gives a clear Qur’anic formula: first, they knew that Moses was the Messenger of The God. Second, despite this knowledge, they harmed him. Third, they themselves “deviated.” Fourth, then The God caused their hearts to deviate. Fifth, The God does not guide the defiantly corrupt people.</p>

  <p>Here, there is no room left for a superficial reading. The verse does not say that The God first caused their hearts to deviate, and then they became misguided. It says: “when they deviated, The God caused their hearts to deviate.”</p>

  <p>This is the divine way. The human being who knowingly turns away from truth is left by The God within that very crookedness, and the result of his choice is made to run upon his heart. This is not injustice. This is the law-governed justice of creation.</p>

  <h2 id="desire-as-god">The sealing of the heart and taking desire as a god</h2>

  <p>Another verse that explains the matter with great precision is in Surah Al-Jathiyah:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">أَفَرَأَيْتَ مَنِ اتَّخَذَ إِلَـٰهَهُ هَوَاهُ وَأَضَلَّهُ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ وَخَتَمَ عَلَىٰ سَمْعِهِ وَقَلْبِهِ وَجَعَلَ عَلَىٰ بَصَرِهِ غِشَاوَةً فَمَن يَهْدِيهِ مِن بَعْدِ اللَّهِ ۚ أَفَلَا تَذَكَّرُونَ</p>
    <p class="translation">Have you seen the one who took his desire as his god, and The God misguided him upon knowledge, and sealed his hearing and his heart, and placed over his sight a covering? So who will guide him after The God? Will you not remember?</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/045-al-jathiyah/023/">Al-Jathiyah 45:23</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse also clearly shows that the sealing of the heart does not occur without background. The person whose heart is sealed is one who has taken his desire as his god: meaning that appetite, prejudice, ego, power, reputation, tribal tradition, inherited religion, or inner desire has become his final criterion. He measures truth not by evidence, but by desire.</p>

  <p>The verse even says: “upon knowledge.” This means that his problem is not mere ignorance. He has reached a position where he can understand, but he does not want to accept. Truth has come near to him, but desire prevents him from submitting. If such a person is misguided, his misguidance is the result of that inner choice.</p>

  <p>Here we must say: if the human heart is filled with desire, prejudice, self-worship, tribe-worship, religion-worship, personality-worship, and fear of breaking inherited tradition, there remains no space for the light of truth to enter.</p>

  <h2 id="covenant-breaking">Hearts are sealed because of covenant-breaking and disbelief</h2>

  <p>In Surah An-Nisa, the Qur’an also connects the sealing of the heart to covenant-breaking, disbelief, and grave wrongful speech:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">فَبِمَا نَقْضِهِم مِّيثَاقَهُمْ وَكُفْرِهِم بِآيَاتِ اللَّهِ وَقَتْلِهِمُ الْأَنبِيَاءَ بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ وَقَوْلِهِمْ قُلُوبُنَا غُلْفٌ ۚ بَلْ طَبَعَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهَا بِكُفْرِهِمْ فَلَا يُؤْمِنُونَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا</p>
    <p class="translation">So because of their breaking of their covenant, their disbelief in the signs of The God, their killing of the prophets without right, and their saying, “Our hearts are wrapped.” Rather, The God sealed them because of their disbelief, so they do not believe except a little.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/004-an-nisa/155/">An-Nisa 4:155</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>Again, the verse says: “because of their disbelief.” This means that the sealing of the heart is the result of disbelief, covenant-breaking, denial of signs, and violence against the carriers of truth. This sealing is not an initial injustice; it is the historical and moral result of standing against truth.</p>

  <h2 id="rust-on-hearts">Rust upon hearts is the result of what people earn</h2>

  <p>In Surah Al-Mutaffifin, the Qur’an gives another image of the closing of the heart. This time it speaks of “ran”: rust, stain, a covering that settles over the heart:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">كَلَّا بَلْ رَانَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِم مَّا كَانُوا يَكْسِبُونَ ۝ كَلَّا إِنَّهُمْ عَن رَّبِّهِمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ لَّمَحْجُوبُونَ ۝ ثُمَّ إِنَّهُمْ لَصَالُو الْجَحِيمِ ۝ ثُمَّ يُقَالُ هَـٰذَا الَّذِي كُنتُم بِهِ تُكَذِّبُونَ</p>
    <p class="translation">No; rather, what they used to earn has rusted over their hearts. No; indeed, from their Lord that Day they will be veiled. Then indeed, they will enter the Hellfire. Then it will be said: This is what you used to deny.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/083-al-mutaffifin/014/">Al-Mutaffifin 83:14–17</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse is very subtle. It does not say that The God darkened their hearts without reason. It says: “what they used to earn.” The act of the human being, the choice of the human being, the habit of the human being, the repetition of the human being, and the earning of the human being all affect the heart. With every sincere choice, the heart becomes more luminous; with every conscious denial, it becomes darker.</p>

  <p>So the sealing of the heart must be understood as a spiritual law. Just as the body changes through repeated habits, the heart also takes shape through repeated choices. A person who repeatedly sees truth and covers it gradually loses the ability to see. This is from the way of The God.</p>

  <h2 id="blocked-understanding">The sealing of the heart and the closing of understanding</h2>

  <p>In several places, the Qur’an speaks of people whose hearts have been sealed because they turned away from understanding and deep comprehension:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">رَضُوا بِأَن يَكُونُوا مَعَ الْخَوَالِفِ وَطُبِعَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ</p>
    <p class="translation">They were pleased to be with those who remained behind, and their hearts were sealed, so they do not understand.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/009-at-tawbah/087/">At-Tawbah 9:87</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse connects the sealing of the heart to satisfaction with remaining behind from responsibility and to the loss of understanding. The issue is not merely not knowing; a person can choose a condition that gradually extinguishes the capacity for understanding.</p>

  <p>In Surah Ali ‘Imran, the Qur’an also explains the divine way by which what is inside human beings becomes exposed in the field of trial:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">ثُمَّ أَنزَلَ عَلَيْكُم مِّن بَعْدِ الْغَمِّ أَمَنَةً نُّعَاسًا يَغْشَىٰ طَائِفَةً مِّنكُمْ ۖ وَطَائِفَةٌ قَدْ أَهَمَّتْهُمْ أَنفُسُهُمْ يَظُنُّونَ بِاللَّهِ غَيْرَ الْحَقِّ ظَنَّ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ ۖ يَقُولُونَ هَل لَّنَا مِنَ الْأَمْرِ مِن شَيْءٍ ۗ قُلْ إِنَّ الْأَمْرَ كُلَّهُ لِلَّهِ ۗ يُخْفُونَ فِي أَنفُسِهِم مَّا لَا يُبْدُونَ لَكَ ۖ يَقُولُونَ لَوْ كَانَ لَنَا مِنَ الْأَمْرِ شَيْءٌ مَّا قُتِلْنَا هَاهُنَا ۗ قُل لَّوْ كُنتُمْ فِي بُيُوتِكُمْ لَبَرَزَ الَّذِينَ كُتِبَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْقَتْلُ إِلَىٰ مَضَاجِعِهِمْ ۖ وَلِيَبْتَلِيَ اللَّهُ مَا فِي صُدُورِكُمْ وَلِيُمَحِّصَ مَا فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ بِذَاتِ الصُّدُورِ</p>
    <p class="translation">Then, after distress, He sent down upon you security, a drowsiness covering a group of you, while another group became anxious only about themselves, thinking about The God other than the truth, the thought of ignorance. They said: Is there anything for us in this matter? Say: The matter belongs entirely to The God. They conceal within themselves what they do not reveal to you. They say: If there had been anything for us in this matter, we would not have been killed here. Say: Even if you had been in your homes, those for whom killing was written would have gone forth to their resting places; so that The God might test what is in your breasts and purify what is in your hearts. And The God knows what is within the breasts.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/003-ali-imran/154/">Ali ‘Imran 3:154</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse does not directly speak of the sealing of the heart, but it shows a divine way: The God exposes, tests, and sifts what is within the breasts. The human heart becomes visible in the face of events. Those who are present only outwardly reveal themselves in trials. Guidance and misguidance also pass through this field of choice, trial, and the exposure of the inner self.</p>

  <h2 id="guidance-increase">Guidance is from The God, but it flows upon a prepared heart</h2>

  <p>The Qur’an says that The God guides whom He wills; but the same Qur’an explains upon what kinds of hearts this guidance flows.</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">وَالَّذِينَ اهْتَدَوْا زَادَهُمْ هُدًى وَآتَاهُمْ تَقْوَاهُمْ</p>
    <p class="translation">And those who were guided, He increased them in guidance and gave them their taqwa.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/047-muhammad/017/">Muhammad 47:17</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse shows that guidance is cumulative. A person who takes the first step toward truth has the path opened further by The God. Guidance is not only a sudden moment; it is a path. Through sincerity, humility, listening, fairness, and submission before clear authority, a person enters the path; then The God increases him in guidance.</p>

  <p>Another verse expresses the same principle through the language of striving:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">وَالَّذِينَ جَاهَدُوا فِينَا لَنَهْدِيَنَّهُمْ سُبُلَنَا ۚ وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ لَمَعَ الْمُحْسِنِينَ</p>
    <p class="translation">And those who strive in Us, We will surely guide them to Our ways. And indeed, The God is with the doers of good.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/029-al-ankabut/069/">Al-‘Ankabut 29:69</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse shows that divine guidance is connected with inner striving, sincere effort, and ihsan. The person who strives in the way of The God is someone willing to confront his own self, prejudice, fear, inherited assumptions, and mental comfort. Such a person is guided by The God to His ways.</p>

  <p>The Qur’an also says:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">مَا أَصَابَ مِن مُّصِيبَةٍ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ ۗ وَمَن يُؤْمِن بِاللَّهِ يَهْدِ قَلْبَهُ ۚ وَاللَّهُ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ</p>
    <p class="translation">No affliction strikes except by the permission of The God. And whoever believes in The God, He guides his heart. And The God is Knowing of all things.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/064-at-taghabun/011/">At-Taghabun 64:11</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>Here, the guidance of the heart is connected to belief. A heart that opens itself before The God finds guidance even within events. But a heart that is already closed and does not want to accept truth even if it becomes clear: how can such a heart be guided?</p>

  <h2 id="following-pleasure">The God guides those who follow His pleasure</h2>

  <p>Regarding the light of the Book and divine guidance, the Qur’an says:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">يَهْدِي بِهِ اللَّهُ مَنِ اتَّبَعَ رِضْوَانَهُ سُبُلَ السَّلَامِ وَيُخْرِجُهُم مِّنَ الظُّلُمَاتِ إِلَى النُّورِ بِإِذْنِهِ وَيَهْدِيهِمْ إِلَىٰ صِرَاطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ</p>
    <p class="translation">By it, The God guides whoever follows His pleasure to the paths of peace, and brings them out from darknesses into the light by His permission, and guides them to a straight path.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/005-al-maidah/016/">Al-Ma’idah 5:16</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse is also very clear. Divine guidance belongs to those who follow the pleasure of The God. This means that their hearts are seeking the pleasure of The God, not the victory of a sect, preservation of inherited tradition, self-justification, defense of personalities, or maintenance of power. The God takes such people, through the Book, out from darknesses into light.</p>

  <h2 id="arrogance">The God turns the arrogant away from His signs</h2>

  <p>One of the most dangerous barriers to guidance is arrogance. The Qur’an says:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">سَأَصْرِفُ عَنْ آيَاتِيَ الَّذِينَ يَتَكَبَّرُونَ فِي الْأَرْضِ بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ وَإِن يَرَوْا كُلَّ آيَةٍ لَّا يُؤْمِنُوا بِهَا وَإِن يَرَوْا سَبِيلَ الرُّشْدِ لَا يَتَّخِذُوهُ سَبِيلًا وَإِن يَرَوْا سَبِيلَ الْغَيِّ يَتَّخِذُوهُ سَبِيلًا ۚ ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ كَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا وَكَانُوا عَنْهَا غَافِلِينَ</p>
    <p class="translation">I will turn away from My signs those who are arrogant upon the earth without right. And if they see every sign, they will not believe in it; and if they see the way of right guidance, they will not take it as a way; and if they see the way of error, they will take it as a way. That is because they denied Our signs and were heedless of them.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/007-al-araf/146/">Al-A‘raf 7:146</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse shows precisely why some human beings are not guided even when they see signs. Their problem is not lack of signs; their problem is arrogance. If they see every sign, they do not believe in it. If they see the path of right guidance, they do not choose it. If they see the path of error, they take that path. Then the Qur’an gives the cause: because they denied the signs of The God and were heedless of them.</p>

  <p>So The God turns them away from His signs because they themselves have destroyed the correct way of encountering signs from within themselves.</p>

  <h2 id="unused-faculties">They have eyes, ears, and hearts, but do not use them</h2>

  <p>In another verse, the Qur’an shows that the issue of misguidance is not the absence of tools of knowledge, but the failure to use them:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">وَلَقَدْ ذَرَأْنَا لِجَهَنَّمَ كَثِيرًا مِّنَ الْجِنِّ وَالْإِنسِ ۖ لَهُمْ قُلُوبٌ لَّا يَفْقَهُونَ بِهَا وَلَهُمْ أَعْيُنٌ لَّا يُبْصِرُونَ بِهَا وَلَهُمْ آذَانٌ لَّا يَسْمَعُونَ بِهَا ۚ أُولَـٰئِكَ كَالْأَنْعَامِ بَلْ هُمْ أَضَلُّ ۚ أُولَـٰئِكَ هُمُ الْغَافِلُونَ</p>
    <p class="translation">And We have certainly created for Hell many of the jinn and humankind. They have hearts with which they do not understand, eyes with which they do not see, and ears with which they do not hear. Those are like cattle; rather, they are more astray. Those are the heedless.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/007-al-araf/179/">Al-A‘raf 7:179</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>If this verse is read apart from its own explanatory clauses, it may be misunderstood as compulsion. But the verse itself explains: they have hearts, but they do not understand with them; they have eyes, but they do not see with them; they have ears, but they do not hear with them. In other words, the tools were given, but they were not used. Heedlessness is the disabling of the tools of understanding.</p>

  <p>This is the condition of the person who has already decided not to see. Such a person may have eyes, but is not truly seeing; may have ears, but is not truly hearing; may have a heart, but is not truly understanding.</p>

  <h2 id="change-within">The God does not change the condition of a people until they change what is within themselves</h2>

  <p>If someone still assumes that guidance and misguidance in the Qur’an are unrelated to human choice, this verse clarifies the principle:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">لَهُ مُعَقِّبَاتٌ مِّن بَيْنِ يَدَيْهِ وَمِنْ خَلْفِهِ يَحْفَظُونَهُ مِنْ أَمْرِ اللَّهِ ۗ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُغَيِّرُ مَا بِقَوْمٍ حَتَّىٰ يُغَيِّرُوا مَا بِأَنفُسِهِمْ ۗ وَإِذَا أَرَادَ اللَّهُ بِقَوْمٍ سُوءًا فَلَا مَرَدَّ لَهُ ۚ وَمَا لَهُم مِّن دُونِهِ مِن وَالٍ</p>
    <p class="translation">For him are successive guardians before him and behind him, protecting him by the command of The God. Indeed, The God does not change what is in a people until they change what is within themselves. And when The God intends harm for a people, there is no turning it back; and they have no protector besides Him.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/013-ar-rad/011/">Ar-Ra‘d 13:11</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This is one of the most foundational verses of the divine way. Divine change is connected to the inner change of human beings and societies. The God does not change the condition of a people without rule. When human beings change themselves, their external path also changes. If they change themselves toward truth, humility, fairness, and reform, the path of guidance opens. If they change themselves toward prejudice, injustice, corruption, and denial, the divine way makes the result of that choice run upon them.</p>

  <p>The same meaning appears in Surah Al-Anfal:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ اللَّهَ لَمْ يَكُ مُغَيِّرًا نِّعْمَةً أَنْعَمَهَا عَلَىٰ قَوْمٍ حَتَّىٰ يُغَيِّرُوا مَا بِأَنفُسِهِمْ ۙ وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ</p>
    <p class="translation">That is because The God would not change a blessing He had bestowed upon a people until they change what is within themselves. And The God is Hearing, Knowing.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/008-al-anfal/053/">Al-Anfal 8:53</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>Therefore, the way of The God in guidance and misguidance is not disconnected from the inner state of the human being. The person’s inner direction, sincerity in seeking, or stubbornness and covering of truth plays a foundational role in the path of guidance and misguidance.</p>

  <h2 id="clear-authority">Guidance belongs to the heart that accepts clear authority</h2>

  <p>In the logic of the Qur’an, the guidance-ready person is the one who, when clear proof, a clear sign, and clear authority appear before him, accepts them. He has not decided in advance that only his religion, tribe, leader, jurist, commentator, or inherited tradition is true. He makes truth the criterion, not his own belonging.</p>

  <p>The Qur’an praises those who listen to speech and follow the best of it:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">الَّذِينَ يَسْتَمِعُونَ الْقَوْلَ فَيَتَّبِعُونَ أَحْسَنَهُ ۚ أُولَـٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ هَدَاهُمُ اللَّهُ ۖ وَأُولَـٰئِكَ هُمْ أُولُو الْأَلْبَابِ</p>
    <p class="translation">Those who listen to speech and follow the best of it. Those are the ones whom The God has guided, and those are the people of understanding.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/039-az-zumar/018/">Az-Zumar 39:18</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse gives the image of a guided heart: it listens, compares, and follows the best. It does not condemn speech before hearing it. It does not fear truth. If truth emerges from a place it did not expect, it still weighs it. Such a person is among the people of inner understanding.</p>

  <p>In contrast, the one who says, “I only accept what I received from my fathers, tribe, religion, group, or elders,” closes the path of guidance upon himself. The Qur’an repeatedly criticizes blind following of ancestors:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمُ اتَّبِعُوا مَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ قَالُوا بَلْ نَتَّبِعُ مَا أَلْفَيْنَا عَلَيْهِ آبَاءَنَا ۗ أَوَلَوْ كَانَ آبَاؤُهُمْ لَا يَعْقِلُونَ شَيْئًا وَلَا يَهْتَدُونَ</p>
    <p class="translation">And when it is said to them, “Follow what The God has sent down,” they say, “Rather, we follow what we found our fathers upon.” Even if their fathers did not understand anything and were not guided?</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/170/">Al-Baqarah 2:170</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse is directly connected to the issue of sealing the heart. Whoever makes his criterion “what we found our fathers upon,” rather than “what The God has sent down,” has imprisoned his heart in the past. If such a heart sees a clear sign, it first asks: does this agree with my inheritance? It does not first ask: is this true?</p>

  <h2 id="will-and-wisdom">Guidance and misguidance are by The God’s will, but His will is wise and law-governed</h2>

  <p>The Qur’an says:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">وَمَا أَرْسَلْنَا مِن رَّسُولٍ إِلَّا بِلِسَانِ قَوْمِهِ لِيُبَيِّنَ لَهُمْ ۖ فَيُضِلُّ اللَّهُ مَن يَشَاءُ وَيَهْدِي مَن يَشَاءُ ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ</p>
    <p class="translation">And We did not send any messenger except in the language of his people, so that he may make clear to them. Then The God misguides whom He wills and guides whom He wills. And He is the Mighty, the Wise.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/014-ibrahim/004/">Ibrahim 14:4</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>This verse attributes guidance and misguidance to The God, while also calling Him “the Mighty, the Wise.” This means that the will of The God is a wise will, not an arbitrary and lawless will. In addition, the verse says that messengers were sent in the language of their people “so that he may make clear to them.” Thus, before guidance and misguidance there is clarification and making clear.</p>

  <p>If this verse is read together with other verses, it becomes clear that “whom He wills” means within the framework of The God’s wise will, not injustice. The God guides the one who seeks truth, listens, has humility, and desires the path of divine pleasure. And the one who is defiantly corrupt, arrogant, covenant-breaking, desire-worshipping, and covering truth, He leaves within that person’s chosen path of misguidance.</p>

  <p>The Qur’an also says elsewhere:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَجَعَلَكُمْ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً وَلَـٰكِن يُضِلُّ مَن يَشَاءُ وَيَهْدِي مَن يَشَاءُ ۚ وَلَتُسْأَلُنَّ عَمَّا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ</p>
    <p class="translation">And if The God had willed, He would have made you one community. But He misguides whom He wills and guides whom He wills. And you will surely be questioned about what you used to do.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/093/">An-Nahl 16:93</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>The ending of the verse is extremely important: “you will surely be questioned about what you used to do.” If guidance and misguidance meant absolute compulsion and the removal of human responsibility, questioning people about their deeds would have no meaning. So divine will does not erase human responsibility; rather, it runs within the same order of responsibility.</p>

  <h2 id="expanded-and-tightened-chest">Expansion and constriction of the chest also follow a divine way</h2>

  <p>In Surah Al-An‘am, the Qur’an speaks of expansion of the chest and constriction of the chest:</p>

  <blockquote class="quran-quote">
    <p class="arabic">فَمَن يُرِدِ اللَّهُ أَن يَهْدِيَهُ يَشْرَحْ صَدْرَهُ لِلْإِسْلَامِ ۖ وَمَن يُرِدْ أَن يُضِلَّهُ يَجْعَلْ صَدْرَهُ ضَيِّقًا حَرَجًا كَأَنَّمَا يَصَّعَّدُ فِي السَّمَاءِ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يَجْعَلُ اللَّهُ الرِّجْسَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ</p>
    <p class="translation">So whoever The God wills to guide, He expands his chest for submission; and whoever He wills to misguide, He makes his chest tight and constricted, as though he were climbing into the sky. Thus The God places defilement upon those who do not believe.</p>
    <p class="source"><a href="/quran-reflection/en/006-al-anam/125/">Al-An‘am 6:125</a></p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>Again, the end of the verse is the key to understanding: “upon those who do not believe.” Tightness of the chest toward truth is a condition connected to not believing, resisting, and refusing to accept. The person ready for guidance, when he sees truth, even if it is heavy, finds his chest opened for submission before The God. But the one who sees truth as a threat to his constructed identity feels constricted by it. He is suffocated by truth because truth asks him to descend from his inner idols.</p>

  <h2 id="quranic-summary">Qur’anic summary: how does the sealing of the heart occur?</h2>

  <p>If the verses are read together, the picture becomes clear: The God does not seal the heart without reason. The sealing of the heart is the result of disbelief, covering truth, defiant corruption, covenant-breaking, desire-worship, arrogance, heedlessness, and repeated wrong action. Guidance is not lawless either. The God guides a heart that seeks truth, listens to speech, follows the best of it, seeks the pleasure of The God, and strives sincerely in the way of The God. Divine misguidance means the running of the result of human choice within the wise order of The God. Divine guidance means the opening of paths of light for a heart that has opened itself toward The God.</p>

  <p>So when the Qur’an says that The God guides whom He wills and misguides whom He wills, this “will” must be understood through the whole Qur’an. The will of The God is not blind, lawless, unjust, or arbitrary. The will of The God is His wise way, running according to knowledge, justice, and truth.</p>

  <p>The human being who has chosen prejudice in advance, treats his inherited religious assumptions as unchangeable, believes in inherited interpretations and human systems of thought instead of the speech of The God, refuses to step outside the path of ancestors and elders, and rejects every clear authority before examining it, is not fit for guidance; not because The God has wronged him, but because he himself has closed the path of light.</p>

  <p>But the human being who empties himself of prejudice, does not absolutize his assumptions, accepts clear proof wherever it appears with reason and fairness, and treats truth as greater than group, religion, tribe, personality, and inherited legacy: such a heart is the place of divine guidance.</p>

  <p>The God sends light to a heart that has left a window open for light. But a heart that has closed all its windows with prejudice, desire, arrogance, and blind imitation, if it remains in darkness, its darkness is the result of that closing.</p>

  <h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>

  <p>The sealing of the heart in the Qur’an does not mean an initial injustice from The God against the human being. The God does not deprive anyone of guidance without reason. The sealing of the heart is the result of a path: covering truth, breaking covenant, following desire, arrogance, heedlessness, and refusing clear signs.</p>

  <p>Guidance and misguidance are both in the hand of The God; but the hand of The God is the hand of wisdom, not injustice. The permission of The God in the world is His wise ways. Just as fire burns, falsehood darkens the heart. Just as light makes the eye see, sincerity prepares the heart for guidance. These are all among the signs of The God in the horizons and within the selves.</p>

  <p>Therefore, the human being must not say: if The God wants, He will guide me, and if He does not want, He will misguide me, so I am not responsible. The Qur’an does not accept such an excuse. The human being must ask: have I left my heart open for truth? If a verse goes against my inheritance, will I examine it? If clear authority appears before me, will I submit? Do I seek the pleasure of The God, or the victory of my own assumptions?</p>

  <p>The path of guidance begins here.</p>

  <p>A heart that places truth before itself: The God increases it in guidance. A heart that places itself before truth: that very self-worship becomes its seal.</p>

  <p>Peace.</p>
