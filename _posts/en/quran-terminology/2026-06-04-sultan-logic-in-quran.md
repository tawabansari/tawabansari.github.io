<div class="ltr" dir="ltr">
<p><strong>In the name of The-God, The-Merciful, The-Compassionate</strong></p>
<p>The Qur'an, in <a href="/quran-reflection/en/030-ar-rum/035/">Ar-Rum 30:35</a>, challenges the intellectual structure of those who fracture religion into sects and inherited claims:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>أَمْ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَـٰنًا فَهُوَ يَتَكَلَّمُ بِمَا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يُشْرِكُونَ</strong></p>
<p>Or have We sent down to them a sultan that speaks concerning what they associate with Him?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>To understand the force of this question, the word <strong>sultan</strong> cannot be flattened into a dead external object. The verse itself gives us markers: something sent down, something authoritative, and most strikingly, something that <strong>speaks</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="earlier-commentators">How Earlier Commentators Understood Sultan</h2>
<p>Classical and later commentators often read <strong>sultan</strong> as an external proof: a book, a written document, a miracle, an angelic authorization, legal dominance, or kingship.</p>
<p>Al-Tabari reads it mainly as textual evidence, narrative proof, or a miracle from God. Ibn Kathir continues this line and treats it as a speaking book that would legitimize what they associate with God. Ibn Taymiyyah restricts <strong>sultan</strong> across the Qur'an to revealed book, transmitted proof, or prophetic miracle. Later reformist and literary commentators also often remain in the same general frame: <strong>sultan</strong> as an external document, power, or warrant.</p>
<p>That reading captures part of the field, but it does not fully account for the living structure of <a href="/quran-reflection/en/030-ar-rum/035/">Ar-Rum 30:35</a>: <strong>a sultan that speaks</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="four-attributes">Four Structural Attributes of Sultan</h2>
<p>When the verse is read carefully, four structural attributes appear.</p>
<h3 id="sent-down">1. It Is Sent Down</h3>
<p><strong>Sultan</strong> is not manufactured from below by tribal custom, social pressure, inherited authority, or sectarian habit. It is connected to something sent down from above: a trans-human criterion within creation.</p>
<h3 id="indefinite">2. It Appears Indefinite</h3>
<p>The word appears as <strong>سُلْطَـٰنًا</strong>, indefinite. We are not dealing with the absolute essence of God's command, but with a mechanism, a law, or an embedded criterion through which truth can be measured.</p>
<h3 id="dominance">3. It Carries Dominance</h3>
<p>The root field of sultan carries force, authority, dominance, and penetration. When true sultan enters the arena, it cuts through superstition, inherited fear, false authority, and unsupported claims.</p>
<h3 id="speaks">4. It Speaks</h3>
<p>This is the most important feature in <a href="/quran-reflection/en/030-ar-rum/035/">Ar-Rum 30:35</a>. The Qur'an does not ask whether they possess a silent object. It asks whether they possess a <strong>sultan that speaks</strong>.</p>
<p>That means articulate authority: a criterion capable of exposing contradiction, clarifying claims, and stating truth coherently.</p>
<h2 id="four-dimensions">Four Qur'anic Dimensions of Sultan</h2>
<p>Across the Qur'an, the uses of <strong>sultan</strong> can be grouped into four broad dimensions.</p>
<h3 id="satan-truth">1. Sultan Between the Human Being, Satan, and Truth</h3>
<p><a href="/quran-reflection/en/017-al-isra/065/">Al-Isra 17:65</a> says:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>إِنَّ عِبَادِي لَيْسَ لَكَ عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَانٌ وَكَفَى بِرَبِّكَ وَكِيلًا</strong></p>
<p>Indeed, over My servants you have no sultan, and sufficient is your Lord as disposer of affairs.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Other verses in this field include <a href="/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/099/">An-Nahl 16:99</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/100/">An-Nahl 16:100</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/015-al-hijr/042/">Al-Hijr 15:42</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/034-saba/021/">Saba 34:21</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/014-ibrahim/022/">Ibrahim 14:22</a>, and <a href="/quran-reflection/en/037-as-saffat/030/">As-Saffat 37:30</a>.</p>
<p>In these verses, Satan has invitation, whisper, illusion, and manipulation, but he does not possess true <strong>sultan</strong> over God's servants. Sultan marks the boundary between objective authority and empty suggestion.</p>
<h3 id="false-names">2. Sultan Against Arbitrary Naming</h3>
<p><a href="/quran-reflection/en/007-al-araf/071/">Al-A'raf 7:71</a> says:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>أَتُجَادِلُونَنِي فِي أَسْمَاءٍ سَمَّيْتُمُوهَا أَنْتُمْ وَآبَاؤُكُمْ مَا نَزَّلَ اللَّهُ بِهَا مِنْ سُلْطَانٍ</strong></p>
<p>Do you dispute with me concerning names you have named, you and your forefathers, for which The-God has sent down no sultan?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Other related verses include <a href="/quran-reflection/en/053-an-najm/023/">An-Najm 53:23</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/012-yusuf/040/">Yusuf 12:40</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/010-yunus/068/">Yunus 10:68</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/007-al-araf/033/">Al-A'raf 7:33</a>, and <a href="/quran-reflection/en/018-al-kahf/015/">Al-Kahf 18:15</a>.</p>
<p>Here, <strong>sultan</strong> stands against invented names, inherited labels, sectarian constructions, and claims attributed to God without objective grounding.</p>
<h3 id="articulate-proof">3. Sultan as Articulate Exposition Against Shirk</h3>
<p><a href="/quran-reflection/en/030-ar-rum/035/">Ar-Rum 30:35</a> is central:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>أَمْ أَنْزَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَانًا فَهُوَ يَتَكَلَّمُ بِمَا كَانُوا بِهِ يُشْرِكُونَ</strong></p>
<p>Or have We sent down to them a sultan that speaks concerning what they associate with Him?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Other verses in this field include <a href="/quran-reflection/en/040-ghafir/056/">Ghafir 40:56</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/040-ghafir/035/">Ghafir 40:35</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/003-aal-imran/151/">Aal-Imran 3:151</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/022-al-hajj/071/">Al-Hajj 22:71</a>, and <a href="/quran-reflection/en/006-al-anam/081/">Al-An'am 6:81</a>.</p>
<p>This is where the Qur'an reveals the unique attribute: <strong>sultan speaks</strong>. It is not mere volume, rank, tradition, or emotional certainty. It is articulate authority that can withstand contradiction.</p>
<h3 id="dominance-breakthrough">4. Sultan as Dominance, Immunity, and Breakthrough</h3>
<p><a href="/quran-reflection/en/023-al-muminun/045/">Al-Mu'minun 23:45</a> says:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>ثُمَّ أَرْسَلْنَا مُوسَى وَأَخَاهُ هَارُونَ بِآيَاتِنَا وَسُلْطَانٍ مُبِينٍ</strong></p>
<p>Then We sent Musa and his brother Harun with Our signs and a manifest sultan.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>This wider network includes verses such as <a href="/quran-reflection/en/011-hud/096/">Hud 11:96</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/017-al-isra/080/">Al-Isra 17:80</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/028-al-qasas/035/">Al-Qasas 28:35</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/044-ad-dukhan/019/">Ad-Dukhan 44:19</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/040-ghafir/023/">Ghafir 40:23</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/017-al-isra/033/">Al-Isra 17:33</a>, <a href="/quran-reflection/en/027-an-naml/021/">An-Naml 27:21</a>, and <a href="/quran-reflection/en/055-ar-rahman/033/">Ar-Rahman 55:33</a>.</p>
<p>In this field, sultan is authority that breaks through, grants proof, protects from baseless attack, and establishes a decisive position.</p>
<h2 id="sultan-as-logic">Sultan as Logic</h2>
<p>When these verses are placed beside each other, <strong>sultan</strong> emerges as more than power and more than document. It is articulate authority. It is proof with force. It is a criterion that speaks.</p>
<p>This is why the connection with logic is powerful. The Arabic word <strong>mantiq</strong> is connected with <strong>n-t-q</strong>, speech and articulation. Logic is not merely private thought; it is ordered articulation that can expose contradiction.</p>
<p>So when the Qur'an asks whether they possess a <strong>sultan that speaks</strong>, it is asking whether their claim has an articulate, contradiction-resistant authority behind it.</p>
<h2 id="historical-language">The Historical Problem of Language</h2>
<p>Part of the difficulty is historical. Formal logic as a named discipline entered the Islamic intellectual world through translation and philosophical vocabulary. The word <strong>mantiq</strong> became the Arabic carrier for what Greek philosophy had called logos or logic.</p>
<p>But the Qur'an did not need to borrow the later technical term in order to point to the function. It had already placed the function inside its own vocabulary: <strong>sultan</strong> as decisive, articulate, truth-bearing authority.</p>
<h2 id="logic-and-human-being">Logic as the Sultan of the Human Being</h2>
<p>In this sense, logic is one expression of the human being's sultan. Through it, contradictions are exposed, false names are challenged, superstition is pierced, and creation becomes intelligible.</p>
<p>This is why blocking knowledge, reason, and education is not a small social crime. It is a blockage of access to sultan: the capacity to test claims, uncover patterns, measure reality, and refuse inherited falsehood.</p>
<h2 id="final-reflection">Final Reflection</h2>
<p>The Qur'an does not merely ask whether they possess a document. It asks whether they possess a <strong>sultan that speaks</strong>.</p>
<p>A living authority. A criterion. A logic. A force that exposes contradiction. A light that pierces illusion.</p>
<p>So the question remains: do we follow inherited names and sectarian shadows, or do we seek the sultan that speaks with truth?</p>
