Al-Baqarah 2:175

ۚ

Those are the ones who have bought misguidance in place of guidance, and punishment in place of forgiveness; how astonishing is their endurance of the Fire!

Our Reflection

What could a person gain in exchange for divine truth that would be worth losing God’s guidance and forgiveness? In Al-Baqarah 2:174, the Qur’an spoke of those who conceal revealed truth and receive a small price in return. Now it reveals the real value of that exchange: “اشْتَرَوُا الضَّلَالَةَ بِالْهُدَىٰ وَالْعَذَابَ بِالْمَغْفِرَةِ”. They have exchanged guidance for misguidance and forgiveness for punishment. However much money, rank, religious influence, or power they gain in this world through that exchange, they have received a paltry price compared with what they have lost. This is the reality to which “thamanan qalilan,” a small price, pointed in the preceding verse.

“Ula’ika,” those, connects the statement to the same concealers described in the preceding verse. “Ishtaraw,” they bought, also portrays this conduct as an exchange in which what a person gains is weighed against what they lose. They seek an advantage, but the Qur’an names the outcome of their choice as misguidance and punishment. This does not mean they explicitly desired punishment; rather, they chose a path with that outcome. In Al-Baqarah 2:160, the way of repentance, reform, and disclosure of truth had been opened. Persisting in concealment means continuing along that harmful path instead of returning and making amends.

This warning applies to life today as well. If a person recognizes a truth in God’s Book but conceals it to preserve income, rank, followers, or the standing of an inherited ruling, they have preferred a passing benefit to guidance. Defending a prohibition without evidence can also exemplify this conduct when it involves deliberately concealing the Qur’an’s clear statement. Yet not every difference in understanding the verses or every error in religious jurisprudence can be called deliberate concealment. The subject of this warning is knowing the truth and trading it for gain. Unfounded prohibitions are examined more fully in “Halal and Haram in the Qur’an: Divine Legislative Authority and the Danger of Associating Partners in Religion”.

The verse ends with the striking expression “فَمَا أَصْبَرَهُمْ عَلَى النَّارِ”. This is an exclamatory construction, and in its context of condemnation it does not praise their patience. The Qur’an evokes astonishment at the audacity and persistence of those who knowingly continue on a path ending in fire. Faced with such an exchange, one might ask: how could anyone be willing to lose the Lord’s guidance and forgiveness and choose punishment in return? This is the same fire to whose consumption the preceding verse likened the gain obtained by concealing truth. We do not know the nature of fire and punishment in the world to come, but the Qur’an’s warning about the outcome of such a choice is clear.

God’s guidance and forgiveness are worth more than any passing advantage or privilege. Someone who conceals revealed truth to preserve wealth, power, or standing has not merely withheld something from others; they have also endangered the course of their own life and its ultimate outcome. No gain from concealing truth is worth losing the guidance and forgiveness of The God. Fidelity to truth becomes apparent when a person preserves it even when it conflicts with personal interest and position, rather than relinquishing what is more enduring and valuable to retain what is transient.
