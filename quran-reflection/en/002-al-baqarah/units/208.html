Al-Baqarah 2:208

ۚ

O you who have believed, enter into peace, all of you, and do not follow Satan’s footsteps; indeed, he is an evident enemy to you.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verses, The God spoke of proportionate retribution and the pursuit of justice, the limits of confronting aggressors, the sanctity of the months of Hajj, the security of Abraham’s House, and the signs by which to recognize corrupt people and seekers of truth. He now calls all who have believed to a fundamental principle:

“يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا ادْخُلُوا فِي السِّلْمِ كَافَّةً”

“O you who have believed, enter into peace, all of you.”

“السِّلْم,” from the root س ل م, concerns soundness, peace, reconciliation, and submission. In the Qur’an, this lexical family is used both for peace among people and for submission to God.

Here the command addresses people who have already believed. Believing alone therefore does not necessarily mean that a person is also on the path of peace and soundness. The God asks the believers to enter into peace together and align their lives and conduct with it.

“كَافَّةً” also emphasizes that this command applies to everyone addressed. Peace should not be desirable only for members of our own family, people, denomination, or community while we leave the way open to hostility, discrimination, and aggression against others.

This command gains further significance in the continuity of the preceding verses. The God prohibited aggression in Al-Baqarah 2:190, opened the way to ending hostility in Al-Baqarah 2:192 and Al-Baqarah 2:193, and in Al-Baqarah 2:194 limited even the response to aggression to the measure of that aggression.

Rules concerning confrontation and war should therefore not become a pretext for making hostility the permanent principle of believers’ lives. Justice and defending the oppressed have their place, but the path to which The God calls the believers is one of peace, soundness, justice, and avoiding aggression.

This principle is also directly related to the place of the qibla and the Kaaba.

In Al-Baqarah 2:125, The God describes the House as “مَثَابَةً لِلنَّاسِ وَأَمْنًا”—a place of return for people and of security. In Al-Maidah 5:97, He also makes the Kaaba “قِيَامًا لِلنَّاسِ”—a means of support and stability for people.

The qibla and the Kaaba should signify turning toward The God, security, peace, and people gathering together—not religious prejudice, sectarianism, religious exclusivism, discrimination, and hostility toward other seekers of God.

How can one worship facing a House that God has established for people and their security, yet deny others access to it because their denomination or religious law differs?

How can one speak of Abraham’s way, yet treat that way as the property of a religious community and regard The God’s bounty as exclusive to that community?

In Al-Baqarah 2:136, the Qur’an commands belief in the revelation sent down to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, Moses, Jesus, and the other prophets, and says:

“لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِنْهُمْ”

“We make no distinction between any of them.”

Following Muhammad should therefore not turn into hostility toward followers of other prophets or contempt for their heritage of guidance. Differences in religious laws should not become an excuse for discrimination and hostility either, since the Qur’an itself speaks in Al-Maidah 5:48 of differing laws and paths appointed for communities.

These foundations are examined in “Din, Islam, and Shariah in the Qur’an”.

In Al-Hajj 22:40, the Qur’an also speaks of monasteries, churches, places of worship, and mosques in which God’s name is frequently remembered, and of preventing their destruction in the context of defending the oppressed.

Al-Baqarah 2:114 also condemns those who prevent God’s name from being remembered in His places of worship and strive to destroy them.

Thus, barring people from divine places of worship, destroying them, imposing belief, and denying people freedom to worship are incompatible with the call for believers to enter into peace.

Abraham’s House should bring people closer to the worship of the One God and to peace, rather than become a means of dividing them.

The true place of the qibla, the Kaaba, and Hajj is examined in detail in “Qibla, Wajh, and Hanif in the Qur’an”, “Hajj in the Qur’an: Abraham’s Way, Rites, and People’s Rights”, and “No Compulsion in Religion: The Messenger’s Duty Is to Convey”.

The God continues:

“وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا خُطُوَاتِ الشَّيْطَانِ”

“And do not follow Satan’s footsteps.”

“خُطُوَات” is the plural of “خُطْوَة,” meaning steps. This expression shows that following Satan can take place step by step.

At first, someone may merely consider themselves and their community superior; then belittle others’ beliefs; then impose restrictions and deprivation upon them; and finally justify discrimination, hostility, and even violence in religion’s name.

This gradual path can turn a society commanded to enter peace into one trapped in prejudice and division.

Al-Baqarah 2:169 identifies speaking about God without knowledge as one of Satan’s commands. Attributing unsupported rulings to God and turning religious interpretations into His definitive command are therefore also dangers to avoid.

In Aal Imran 3:103, the Qur’an commands everyone to hold fast to God’s rope and not become divided. In Ar-Rum 30:31 and Ar-Rum 30:32, it criticizes those who have divided their religion and become sects, each group pleased with what it possesses.

If every community seeks only its own peace and freedom while refusing to recognize others’ rights and freedom, it has not yet entered the peace to which The God calls the believers.

At the end, it says:

“إِنَّهُ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ مُبِينٌ”

“Indeed, he is an evident enemy to you.”

The God describes Satan as an evident enemy. People must take care that this enmity does not lead them away from justice and peace, especially when prejudice, claims of superiority, and oppression appear under the guise of defending religion.

After presenting rules concerning justice, confrontation, Hajj, and recognizing human conduct, this verse calls all believers to enter into peace. The qibla and the Kaaba should signify this same path: worship of the One God, security, peace, respect for the prophets, freedom to worship, and avoidance of discrimination and exclusivism. Anyone who pursues hostility, prejudice, and the exclusion of others in God’s name should measure their conduct against this clear Qur’anic command: “ادْخُلُوا فِي السِّلْمِ كَافَّةً”.
