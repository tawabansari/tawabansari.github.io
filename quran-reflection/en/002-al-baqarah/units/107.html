Al-Baqarah 2:107

ۗ

Do you not know that to The God belongs dominion over the heavens and the earth? And apart from The God, you have no guardian or helper.

Our Reflection

The preceding verse ended with this question:

“Do you not know that The God has power over all things?”

This verse immediately continues with the same construction:

“Do you not know that to The God belongs dominion over the heavens and the earth?”

First comes His power, then His dominion, and from there the discussion moves to guardianship and help. These four concepts appear in a context that has, for several verses, concerned the authority of The God to send down guidance.

Earlier, a group resented The God sending His bounty upon whomever He willed among His servants and said, “We believe in what was sent down to us.” This outlook draws an ethnic boundary around divine guidance: revelation is accepted, but supposedly it must come through the channel we expect.

The end of verse 106 breaks through that boundary: He has power over all things. If He wills to choose a new messenger, continue guidance, replace a sign or revelation, or bring something better or like it, His power is not subject to lineage, ethnicity, earlier scripture, or human religious expectations.

But power alone is not the whole answer. Verse 107 adds that the realm in which this power operates also belongs to Him:

“To Him belongs dominion over the heavens and the earth.”

Not only the heavens: the earth as well.

No independent part of existence lies outside His dominion. The world is not divided into two kingdoms, with heaven belonging to The God and earth forming the independent domain of another power.

This point is also significant alongside certain expressions in the scriptures of the People of the Book. John 12:31, 14:30, and 16:11 refer to Satan as “the ruler of this world”; 1 John 5:19 describes the world as under the influence of the evil one; and in Luke 4:6 Satan speaks of authority over the kingdoms of the world. Yet those same scriptures do not make him an independent owner on an equal footing with God: in Job 1:12, God Himself determines the limits of his authority.

The Qur’an draws the boundary more clearly. An-Nahl 16:99–100 says that Satan has no authority over those who believe and place their trust in their Lord; his authority is over those who take him as their protector. Even Satan’s “authority,” then, is not independent dominion. People can place themselves under his influence by following him, but the heavens and the earth remain the dominion of The God.

From here, the second half of the verse follows naturally:

“Apart from The God, you have no guardian or helper.”

In Az-Zumar 39:43–44, the Qur’an makes the same relationship explicit through intercession. Concerning those who have taken intercessors apart from The God, it asks:

“Even though they possess nothing?”

It then says that all intercession belongs to The God, because dominion over the heavens and the earth belongs to Him.

Saba 34:22 develops the same argument: those invoked apart from The God do not possess even an atom’s weight in the heavens or the earth, nor do they share in this dominion with Him. Al-An‘am 6:14 brings the question directly to guardianship: should I take a guardian other than The God, when He is the originator of the heavens and the earth?

The association of dominion, guardian, and helper in Al-Baqarah 2:107 is therefore meaningful. Someone who possesses nothing of their own in this dominion cannot establish an independent authority in the unseen against its owner.

The Arabic word for “guardian” (walī), from W L Y, is connected with nearness, continuity, guardianship, and protection. A “helper” (naṣīr), from N Ṣ R, is someone who comes to another’s aid. But the verse does not deny every friendship, act of cooperation, or form of help between people. The Qur’an itself calls believers guardians of one another and commands them to help and cooperate.

The decisive qualification is:

“Apart from The God.”

The issue arises when a being is placed in the role of a refuge in the unseen, an ultimate guardian, or a source of deliverance from calamity, relief, or help within the sphere of divine power.

This question is not directed only at the People of the Book. Followers of the Qur’an, too, can elevate a prophet, imam, saint, religious teacher, or righteous person to such a position that they make requests of that person which are not ordinary human requests for help. Those who accept this kind of appeal generally do not say that these servants possess power independently of The God; they understand that power to have been bestowed by Him and to remain dependent on Him. This distinction must be understood accurately: their position should not be presented as something they do not themselves claim.

Yet this explanation does not remove the verse’s question. If dominion belongs to Him, and He Himself says that you have no guardian or helper apart from Him, why should a human need for help in the unseen be directed to someone else?

Asking a person for help with something within their ability is a different matter. A patient seeks a physician’s help, someone suffering injustice asks another person for support, and a community cooperates in mutual defense. These are neither claims to control the unseen nor the establishment of a guardian against The God. The boundary changes when someone seeks refuge, help, or intervention beyond ordinary human ability from a person who is themselves a servant of The God within that same dominion.

Here the connection with verse 106 takes on further significance. If our reading of superseding and restoration in the preceding verse is correct, correction is not directed only at a command or a written text; the human conception of the One who commands may itself need to be purified. We may impose limits on The God: suppose that a prophet must come from a particular people, consider part of the world the independent domain of another power, or place His servants in a position from which we seek guardianship and help in the unseen.

These two verses remove those boundaries one after another:

He has power over all things.

Dominion over the heavens and the earth belongs to Him.

And you have no guardian or helper apart from Him.

The next verse brings its audience directly back to how they deal with the Messenger and compares their conduct with that of those who had encountered Moses before them. The discussion is therefore not merely about a theoretical understanding of The God. If power, dominion, guardianship, and help truly belong to Him, people cannot prescribe the path His guidance must take, construct a rival realm to His dominion, or hope to escape His command by relying on support outside Him.

This is our interpretation of the connection between these verses and their words. Absolute knowledge belongs to The God Himself, and He knows the original and complete interpretation of His words.
