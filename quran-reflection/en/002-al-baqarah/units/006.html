Al-Baqarah 2:6

Indeed, those who have committed covering and rejecting truth (kufr), it is the same for them whether you warn them or do not warn them; they will not believe.

Our Reflection

This verse comes after the description of the mindful in verses 2 to 5. At the beginning of the surah, the Qur’an introduced itself as a Book in which there is no doubt, and as guidance for the mindful — those who carry readiness, vigilance, sincerity, and orientation toward truth within themselves. Now the verse speaks of a group standing in the opposite condition: those who have covered or rejected truth.

Here, it is essential to understand that kufr in the Qur’an is not simple ignorance. This judgment does not apply to everyone who lacks knowledge, has not yet encountered the truth, or is sincerely searching. Kufr means covering, rejecting, or denying truth after truth has become clear, after signs have come, after the proof has been established, and after the path of understanding was open. For this reason, this verse is not speaking about the ignorant person, the questioner, the one in honest doubt, or the sincere seeker. It is speaking about those who have taken a posture of covering and resistance before clarified truth.

The phrase “it is the same for them” carries considerable weight. It means that warning them or not warning them has become equal. This equality does not mean that the warning is weak. Rather, it shows that the receiver of the warning is no longer in a state of receptivity. Just as rain, when it falls on living soil, brings forth growth, but when it falls on hard stone, does not penetrate it, the divine message affects a heart that still has an opening toward truth. But when a human being repeatedly covers truth, even warning becomes an ineffective sound to them.

Here, the role of the Prophet also becomes clear. The Prophet is entrusted with delivering the warning, not owning the result. He can warn, but he cannot force anyone into faith. The Qur’an repeats this principle many times: “upon the Messenger is only clear delivery,” as seen in Al-Mā’idah 5:99, An-Naḥl 16:82, An-Nūr 24:54, and At-Taghābun 64:12. Likewise, in Al-Ghāshiyah 88:21–22, the Prophet is described as a reminder, not as one who dominates over people.

Therefore, faith is the inner response of the human being to truth, not the product of external compulsion. In Al-Baqarah 2:256, the principle “there is no compulsion in religion” is stated, and in Yūnus 10:99, even the Prophet is asked whether he would compel people until they become believers. This shows that the Qur’anic path is not forced belief, but delivery, reminder, warning, and leaving the responsibility of choice open before the human being. This principle is examined separately in the article “There Is No Compulsion in Religion; the Prophet’s Duty Is Delivery”.

If the human being decides to live by their own assumptions, and to accept truth only where it agrees with desire, power, tradition, or self-interest, then divine warning will not affect them.

This verse also shows that the Qur’an is recited to all, but not all receive guidance from it. One and the same text can be guidance for the mindful, while exposing the rejection of those who cover truth. The problem is not in the Book; the problem is in the orientation of the human being before the Book. The Qur’an is light, but one who closes their eyes does not benefit from light.

So verse 2:6 is an invitation to watch over the inner self. It should not be read only about “others.” Whenever a human being covers a truth that has become clear to them because of tribal loyalty, fear, arrogance, self-interest, or habit, they are stepping onto the path of kufr. The real danger is that a person may gradually reach a point where warning and no warning become the same for them.
