Al-Baqarah 2:22

The One who made the earth for you as a resting spread, and the sky as a structure, and sent down water from the sky, then brought out through it fruits as provision for you. So do not set up rivals for the One God while you know.

Our Reflection

This verse continues directly from the previous verse. In verse 21, people were called to worship their Lord. In this verse, the Qur’an shows the reason for that call within the tangible structure of the world. Earth, sky, water, fruits, and provision are not mentioned as disconnected items; they appear together as one connected order that makes human life possible and sustains its continuation.

Firashan, from the root F R SH, means spreading out and preparing a bedding or resting place. The word speaks about the function of the earth, not its geometric shape. The verse is not saying that the earth is materially flat. It is saying that the earth has been made for the human being as a livable and usable spread. This linguistic precision matters, because firash should not be reduced to a superficial reading of “carpet.”

Then the verse speaks of as-sama'a bina'an: the sky as an established, ordered, higher structure. So the earth is not merely a bed, and the sky is not merely the space above our heads. Together, they are part of an order that makes life possible. After that, water is sent down from the sky, and through it fruits come forth and human provision is made available. This sequence shows a clear chain: resting spread, structure, water, fruit, and provision.

In anzala, the root N Z L carries the meaning of bringing down or causing something to descend from a higher level to a lower and receivable level. In this verse, water reaches the earth from the sky and appears in a form usable for life. This meaning fits the natural experience of rain, snow, and hail, and it can also remind a modern reader that water within the cosmic and earthly order is not a shallow or simple matter. But the verse itself rests first on the clear observable experience: water descends, and provision comes out from the earth.

The final axis of the verse is: “So do not set up rivals for the One God.” Andadan does not only mean stone idols or formal partners in power. Anything that a human being places as an independent support, source of meaning, source of security, or source of provision against the One God can enter this field. So shirk in this verse is not only a theoretical error; it is a displacement in the center of human trust.

The ending, “while you know,” is weighty. The Qur’an does not say: do not set up rivals for God because you do not know. It says: do not do this while you know. The signs of Lordship are visible in the structure of your life. The earth is your resting spread, the sky is the structure above you, water descends, fruits come forth, and provision is made available. If, after all this, human beings construct parallel supports, the problem is not merely ignorance. It is a distortion in trust, direction, and responsibility.

Cross-References
  • Al-Baqarah 2:21— Verse 21 gives the command to worship, and verse 22 shows its creational reason in earth, sky, water, and provision.
  • Ta-Ha 20:53— “The One who made the earth for you as a cradle”; the earth is described there as the cradle of life, which connects with the meaning of firash in Al-Baqarah 2:22.
  • Al-Anbiya 21:32— The sky is described as a protected roof, helping us understand “structure” in this verse.
  • An-Nahl 16:10-11— Water descending from the sky and the growth of plants and fruits; a broader unfolding of the same chain of provision.
  • Ar-Rum 30:40— Creation, provision, death, and life are attributed to the One God, while the false partners are denied power.
  • Ibrahim 14:30— Setting up rivals for the One God becomes a social means of leading people away from His path.
