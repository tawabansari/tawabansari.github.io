Al-Baqarah 2:103

ۖ

And if they had believed and guarded themselves, a reward from The God would certainly have been better for them, if only they knew.

Our Reflection

This verse sets another path against the choice described in the preceding verse. Had they believed instead of following the devils, and exercised mindful self-restraint with the knowledge placed at their disposal, they could have guarded themselves against rejecting truth and using that knowledge for magic, harm, and their own desires. Learning had given them capability, but possessing a capability is one thing; the purpose for which it is used is another. Before teaching, Harut and Marut warned: “Do not reject truth.” This verse expresses the same path from the human side through two verbs: “believed and guarded themselves”—faith accompanied by self-restraint.

Mindful guarding (taqwa) here is not merely fear. The Arabic word derives from W Q Y and carries the meanings of guarding, protecting, and taking a shield. Someone who has the power to do something but restrains themselves from misusing it demonstrates this mindful guarding in practice. This gives the concept particular significance after the account of Harut and Marut’s knowledge and power. For a fuller examination, see Taqwa in the Qur’an.

In the preceding verse, they bought something for which they sold themselves: “How evil was that for which they sold themselves.” Here, set against that transaction, stands “a reward from The God.” The Arabic word for reward here, mathūbah, comes from TH W B and is connected with returning. The same root gives thawāb, also meaning reward: something that returns to a person in response to their action. Instead of a transaction in which they themselves suffered loss, they could have chosen a path whose return came from The God and was better for them.

The verse again ends with the same sentence:

“If only they knew.”

The preceding verse had said, “They certainly knew.” The issue, then, is not a lack of information. A person sometimes knows something, yet that knowledge has no part in their choice. These people knew the outcome but chose what would harm them. Had that knowledge been alive in their faith, mindful guarding, and conduct, they would have chosen differently.
