Al-Baqarah 2:167

ۗ ۖ

And those who followed will say, “If only we could return, so that we might disown them just as they disowned us.” Thus The God shows them their deeds as causes of regret for them; and they will not come out of the Fire.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, the Quran spoke of leaders who disown their followers upon seeing the punishment, while the ties between them fall apart. It now shows the followers’ response: those who once trusted and obeyed those leaders wish to return once more to their former life.

What deserves attention, however, is the purpose of this return. They do not say, “If only we could return to repent, reform our conduct, or take the path of truth.” Rather, they say, “فَنَتَبَرَّأَ مِنْهُمْ كَمَا تَبَرَّءُوا مِنَّا”: so that we might disown them just as they disowned us. What they voice is a reciprocal reaction to their leaders’ conduct. They now wish to reject those whom they loved and followed in earthly life, just as they themselves have been rejected.

This distinction matters. In Al-Muminun 23:100, a person facing death asks to return in order to do the righteous work left undone. But in Al-Baqarah 2:167, the expressed wish is for reciprocal disavowal. Regretting the consequence of a choice does not necessarily mean repentance and reform. A person may regret the outcome of their following, yet still focus more on the conduct of the one who misled them than on their own responsibility for choosing.

The Quran therefore immediately says, “كَذَٰلِكَ يُرِيهِمُ اللَّهُ أَعْمَالَهُمْ حَسَرَاتٍ عَلَيْهِمْ”: thus God shows them their deeds as causes of regret. They face their own deeds, not only their leaders’ conduct. Al-Ahzab 33:67 likewise speaks of people who attribute their misguidance to obeying their great ones. Ibrahim 14:21 and Ibrahim 14:22 also make clear that followers cannot place the entire responsibility for their choices on those who invited them into error.

This does not remove the responsibility of misleading leaders, but neither does it free followers from personal responsibility. Someone who chose unquestioning following instead of weighing the truth in earthly life cannot change their past conduct on the Day of Reckoning merely by disowning their leader. The command in Al-Isra 17:36 not to follow what one has no knowledge of recalls precisely this responsibility during earthly life.

The end of the verse is also explicit: “وَمَا هُمْ بِخَارِجِينَ مِنَ النَّارِ”—they will not come out of the Fire. Wishing to return and declaring disavowal of their leaders no longer make amends for the past or remove the consequences of their conduct.

This verse warns people today: before following turns into regret, we must reflect on the soundness of the path and the credibility of those whose words we follow. No leader, scholar, narrator, or holder of authority can bear the responsibility for our religious choices in our place.
