Al-Baqarah 2:233

ۖ ۚ ۚ ۚ ۚ ۗ ۗ ۗ

And mothers breastfeed their children for two complete years, for whoever wishes to complete breastfeeding. And upon the one for whom the child is born rests their provision and clothing in an honorable manner. No soul is burdened beyond its capacity. Neither a mother should be harmed on account of her child, nor the one for whom the child is born on account of his child; and upon the heir rests the like of that. Then if the two wish to wean through mutual consent and consultation, there is no blame upon them. And if you wish to entrust your children to another for nursing, there is no blame upon you, provided you hand over what you have given in an honorable manner. And be mindful of The God, and know that The God sees what you do.

Our Reflection

Divorce can end the marital relationship, but it cannot abolish parenthood or the parents’ responsibility toward their child. This verse addresses the rights of pregnant women or women with infants after divorce. Its ruling may also apply generally, permitting the end of breastfeeding by mutual agreement and the employment of a wet nurse.

In the preceding verses, the Quran discussed divorce, the waiting period, the mutual rights of women and men, and separation with honorable conduct and kindness. It now turns to the child, because a woman and man may separate while the mother is pregnant or while they have an infant who still needs milk and care.

In such circumstances, the parents’ disagreement and separation must not erase the child’s rights or leave the mother alone with the entire responsibility for caring for and feeding the child.

“وَالْوَالِدَاتُ يُرْضِعْنَ أَوْلَادَهُنَّ حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ”

“And mothers breastfeed their children for two complete years, for whoever wishes to complete breastfeeding.”

“وَالِدَات” refers to mothers who have given birth, and “حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ” specifies a period of two complete years. However, the expression “لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ” shows that this period is for whoever wishes to complete the course of breastfeeding.

Here the Quran recognizes the mother’s right to breastfeed and care for her infant. Even after divorce, the basic principle is that the nursing child should remain with the mother and benefit from her milk and care. The father cannot use separation as a pretext to deprive the mother of breastfeeding against her wishes or make the child a means of pressuring her.

To fulfill this responsibility, however, the mother herself needs food, clothing, shelter, rest, and security; the Quran therefore immediately states the responsibility for providing her livelihood.

“وَعَلَى الْمَوْلُودِ لَهُ رِزْقُهُنَّ وَكِسْوَتُهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ”

“And upon the one for whom the child is born rests their provision and clothing in an honorable manner.”

“الْمَوْلُودِ لَهُ” refers to the child’s father. The Quran places responsibility for the nursing mother’s food and clothing upon him, even if the marital relationship has ended.

This responsibility is directly connected with the realities of the mother’s and infant’s lives. A mother who cares for her child day and night and breastfeeds needs adequate food, suitable living conditions, and safe shelter herself. She cannot be expected to nourish her child while struggling to obtain bread and her own necessities.

Providing for the nursing mother is part of meeting the needs of the very child who benefits from her milk and care.

A man who has divorced his wife therefore cannot disregard responsibility for her food and clothing during breastfeeding. If the mother is nursing their shared child, the father is likewise obliged to provide for her prescribed needs honorably.

In At-Talaq 65:6, God also states concerning housing for divorced women:

“أَسْكِنُوهُنَّ مِنْ حَيْثُ سَكَنْتُمْ مِنْ وُجْدِكُمْ وَلَا تُضَارُّوهُنَّ لِتُضَيِّقُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ”

“Lodge them where you yourselves dwell, according to your means, and do not harm them in order to constrain them.”

The man’s responsibility is thus not confined to providing some food. The mother and infant also need suitable housing, and the father must not use their necessities as a means of pressure.

Yet this responsibility, too, is assessed according to the father’s actual ability.

“لَا تُكَلَّفُ نَفْسٌ إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا”

“No soul is burdened beyond its capacity.”

“وُسْع” refers to a person’s ability and capacity. The Quran neither obliges the father to bear expenses beyond his means nor forces the mother to do something beyond her physical ability.

A father with a limited income must strive to meet the mother’s and child’s needs according to his actual resources, rather than face crushing debts and impossible expenses. Conversely, a wealthy man who can provide them with suitable living conditions must not use divorce as an excuse to evade his responsibility.

The same principle applies to the mother. If illness, physical weakness, or other circumstances prevent her from breastfeeding, a duty beyond her ability cannot be imposed upon her in the name of motherhood.

The Quran states this same principle in Al-Baqarah 2:286:

“لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا”

“The God does not burden any soul beyond its capacity.”

Despite this clear statement and the context of the divorce verses, in some jurisprudential traditions and religious laws accepted among Sunnis and the people of opinion and hadith, even jurists’ presuppositions and “amani” have been imposed upon the Quranic text, whereas those presuppositions should have been assessed and corrected against the Quran.

A clear example is Maliki jurisprudence, one of the four well-known schools in which Sunni followers take pride, where maintenance and clothing for a nonpregnant woman whose divorce is irrevocable are regarded as no longer due as marital rights. Al-Qurtubi, a Maliki commentator, initially acknowledges the verse’s connection with divorced women, yet when he reaches the command to provide food and clothing for the nursing mother, he favors the interpretation concerning women who remain married, in order to reconcile the verse with this juristic premise.

This reverses the criterion for understanding religion: the Quran should correct the jurist’s presuppositions, rather than the jurist making the Quran subordinate to his prior rulings and assumptions.

The Quran has already warned in Al-Baqarah 2:78 about “amani” and conjecture replacing knowledge of the Book, in Al-Baqarah 2:89 about denying truth after recognizing it, and in Al-Baqarah 2:213 about disagreement after clear evidence has arrived.

“لَا تُضَارَّ وَالِدَةٌ بِوَلَدِهَا وَلَا مَوْلُودٌ لَهُ بِوَلَدِهِ”

“Neither a mother should be harmed on account of her child, nor the one for whom the child is born on account of his child.”

This command continues the same principle prohibiting harm stated in Al-Baqarah 2:231, but this time in relation to the rights of parents and the child.

After divorce, disagreements between the woman and man may extend to the care and feeding of their child. Sometimes the father withholds necessities to pressure the mother; sometimes he threatens to take the child from her so that she is forced to accept his demands.

The mother, for her part, must not use the child as an instrument of revenge or to impose harmful demands upon the father.

A child is not an instrument of revenge against a former spouse. Disagreement between father and mother authorizes neither of them to use the child to hurt the other.

The child’s rights also matter here, because depriving the child of necessary nourishment, care, or affection merely to hurt the other parent harms the child first and foremost.

The father and mother may no longer be able to live together, but they must still cooperate concerning their child’s needs.

“وَعَلَى الْوَارِثِ مِثْلُ ذَٰلِكَ”

“And upon the heir rests the like of that.”

This expression shows that responsibilities concerning the child and preventing harm are also connected with the heir.

If the father dies, the child must not be deprived of essential support and care because of his absence. The Quran also connects the heir with a responsibility like that already stated.

The father’s death must therefore not become an excuse for the child’s and nursing mother’s needs to be forgotten amid disputes among heirs, the division of property, or relatives’ indifference.

Just as the man cannot disregard responsibility for his child during his lifetime, after his death the child’s associated rights must also be observed within the framework of the heir’s responsibility.

“فَإِنْ أَرَادَا فِصَالًا عَنْ تَرَاضٍ مِنْهُمَا وَتَشَاوُرٍ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا”

“Then if the two wish to wean through mutual consent and consultation, there is no blame upon them.”

“فِصَال,” from the root ف ص ل, is connected with separation and here refers to ending breastfeeding.

After stating the full period of breastfeeding, God also leaves open the possibility of ending it earlier; however, this decision must involve the consent and consultation of both parents.

The expression “عَنْ تَرَاضٍ مِنْهُمَا وَتَشَاوُرٍ” is very clear. The father has no right to impose an end to breastfeeding upon the mother unilaterally, nor should the mother disregard the child’s actual needs and the parents’ shared responsibility.

Illness or physical difficulties may prevent the mother from continuing to breastfeed, or the child’s circumstances may lead the parents to agree on gradually ending it. In such a situation, the Quran guides them toward a joint and informed decision.

The important principle is that ending breastfeeding must not result from unilateral coercion, post-divorce disputes, or disregard for the child’s needs.

“وَإِنْ أَرَدْتُمْ أَنْ تَسْتَرْضِعُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا سَلَّمْتُمْ مَا آتَيْتُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ”

“And if you wish to entrust your children to another for nursing, there is no blame upon you, provided you hand over what you have given in an honorable manner.”

“تَسْتَرْضِعُوا,” from the root ر ض ع, refers to arranging breastfeeding through another person. The Quran thus also permits employing a wet nurse.

However, this permission must not be understood separately from the preceding command concerning parental consent and consultation and the explicit prohibition against harming the mother.

The father has no right to separate a nursing infant from the mother against her wishes and entrust the child to a wet nurse solely because of divorce or disagreement with her. Such conduct can both violate the mother’s right to breastfeed and harm the mother and child.

If the mother cannot breastfeed, or the father and mother decide through mutual consent and consultation that another woman should nurse the child, the Quran leaves this path open, provided the rights associated with the agreement are respected honorably.

This flexibility shows that the Quran’s aim is not to force families into a single arrangement in every circumstance, but to meet the child’s needs while respecting the parents’ rights and abilities.

“وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ”

“And be mindful of The God, and know that The God sees what you do.”

The verse ends with the command to be mindful and a reminder that God sees human conduct.

In family disputes, one parent may conceal their financial means, pressure the other, or use the child’s needs to pursue personal demands. Sometimes an agreement is reached outwardly, while in reality one party has been forced to accept it because of financial dependence or family pressure.

Observing honorable conduct is therefore not confined to the outward form of contracts and agreements; honesty, genuine consent, avoiding harm, and fulfilling responsibility also matter.

God states:

“أَنَّ اللَّهَ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ”

“The God sees what you do.”

A father who fails to provide the mother’s and child’s necessities despite having the means, or a mother who uses the child to hurt the father, cannot hide the reality of their conduct behind family disagreements.

In this verse, the Quran organizes the nursing mother’s rights, the child’s needs, and the father’s financial responsibility as a continuation of the rulings on divorce. Breastfeeding is the mother’s right; providing her food and clothing is the father’s responsibility; no one is burdened beyond their capacity; and decisions about ending breastfeeding or employing a wet nurse must respect rights, parental consent, and consultation.

The father and mother may separate, but the child must not pay the price of their disagreement. Neither has the right to make the child an instrument of revenge or pressure against the other, because The God sees what they do.
