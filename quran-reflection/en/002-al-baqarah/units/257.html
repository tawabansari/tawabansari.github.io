Al-Baqarah 2:257

ۖ ۗ ۖ

The God is the guardian of those who have believed; He brings them out of darknesses into light. And those who have denied, their guardians are taghut; they bring them out of light into darknesses. They are the people of the Fire; they remain therein.

Our Reflection

In Ayat al-Kursi, God was presented through His absolute sovereignty and power. In the following verse, the Quran described freedom of faith and human choice, making clear that people can consciously reject taghut and believe in The God. Now this verse expresses the outcome of that choice and the path on which a person proceeds through two contrasting images:

«اللَّهُ وَلِيُّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا يُخْرِجُهُمْ مِنَ الظُّلُمَاتِ إِلَى النُّورِ»

“The God is the guardian of those who have believed; He brings them out of darknesses into light.”

Wali, from the root W L Y, is connected with nearness, connection, support, and taking responsibility. Guardianship in this verse is not merely a nominal relationship or a declaration of friendship; it is a relationship that influences the direction of a person’s movement and destiny.

Someone who consciously follows truth, sets taghut aside as a false authority, and places truth above selfishness, prejudice, and personal interest has, in reality, accepted the guardianship of The God. Such a person enters the path of divine guidance.

The Quran compares this guidance to emerging from darknesses and reaching light.

Darkness limits the ability to see and discern, while light reveals the way. People too may be caught in the darkness of ignorance, prejudice, deception, fear, or selfishness and be unable to recognize truth correctly. Through recognizing truth, thinking, correcting errors, and acting justly, the path becomes clearer to them.

This image also appears in Ibrahim 14:1, where bringing people out of darknesses into light is connected with the revealed Book and the Lord’s permission.

Divine guidance is therefore not merely a religious claim or a label applied to people; it is a reality that must affect their understanding, decisions, and conduct.

Following truth is movement along the path of the guardianship of The God; its fruit is emerging from darknesses into the light of guidance.

The Quran then describes the other side of this choice:

«وَالَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَوْلِيَاؤُهُمُ الطَّاغُوتُ يُخْرِجُونَهُمْ مِنَ النُّورِ إِلَى الظُّلُمَاتِ»

“And those who have denied, their guardians are taghut; they bring them out of light into darknesses.”

Here too, guardianship determines the direction of a person’s movement, but this time the direction is reversed.

A person who denies truth and instead follows rebellious desires, prejudice, or false authority may lose even the clear understanding they once possessed. When someone conceals a truth they have recognized to preserve their interests or standing, they gradually pave the way for justifying error, deceiving themselves, and wronging others.

This is movement from light into darknesses.

In the first part of the verse, God is described with the singular wali, while the second uses “أَوْلِيَاؤُهُمُ” and the plural verb “يُخْرِجُونَهُمْ”. Taghut can appear in different forms: a self-serving desire, a misleading leader, an oppressive power, or an authority that has usurped the place of truth.

The Quran also uses “light” in the singular and “darknesses” in the plural. This contrast creates a clear image of movement toward truth as opposed to becoming trapped in various darknesses.

Neither path, however, removes human choice. As the preceding verse declared, there is no compulsion in religion. People can choose truth and move in the light of divine guidance, or turn away from it and accept the authority of taghut. In either case, their choice has consequences.

For a detailed examination of the source of rebellion, the guardianship of taghut, and its inward and outward manifestations, see the separate study Taghut in the Quran: from Self-Worship to Domination over People.

The verse finally states:

«أُولَٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ»

“They are the people of the Fire; they remain therein.”

Here the Quran speaks not only of the worldly consequences of a wrong choice but also of its outcome in the Hereafter. Choosing between truth and taghut is neither trivial nor without consequence; it is a path people enter through their own will, and they will be responsible for its outcome.

Divine guardianship thus guides people toward understanding, light, and truth, while accepting the guardianship of taghut takes them away from the light of truth and traps them in the darknesses of error.
