Al-Baqarah 2:172

O you who believe! Eat of the pure and wholesome things We have provided you, and give thanks to God, if you worship Him alone.

Our Reflection

After warning against following Satan’s footsteps, speaking about God without knowledge, and giving ancestral tradition priority over revelation, the Quran returns to food. In Al-Baqarah 2:168, God invited all people to eat lawful and wholesome food; now He repeats this invitation to those who believe and adds gratitude. This repetition shows that faith should not prevent people from enjoying God’s wholesome provision. Being religious does not mean inventing groundless prohibitions and narrowing access to blessings. God has opened the way to use His provision, and in the next verse He states the principal forbidden foods and the ruling for necessity. No one therefore has the right to add personal preference, ethnic custom, or inherited rulings to the list of prohibitions without divine warrant.

Here God speaks of “طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ.” As examined in the independent study of the root Ṭ Y B, tayyib refers to something’s purity, pleasantness, goodness, and suitability in its context. Regarding food, this description directs attention to the cleanliness, wholesomeness, appropriate quality, and pleasantness of provision. An informed person with sufficient resources can also consider production methods, the health of water and soil, kind treatment of animals, and the rights of those involved in preparing food. This is responsible enjoyment of the Lord’s blessings. But if poverty, geography, or limited resources prevent access to better food, that lack does not itself make someone’s food forbidden. Being tayyib and being lawful are related but distinct concepts; food’s failure to reach the best possible quality cannot establish a divine prohibition.

The second command, “وَاشْكُرُوا لِلَّهِ,” completes the meaning of enjoying provision. Gratitude is not merely saying “thanks be to God” or mentioning the Lord’s name before and after eating. It begins with recognizing the blessing and its source and must also become evident in conduct. How can someone who enjoys God’s provision but wastes it, violates others’ rights in obtaining it, or remains indifferent to the hungry and needy fulfill the gratitude due for that blessing with a few words alone? In Saba 34:13, God commands David’s household to act in gratitude. True gratitude therefore means using blessings rightly, preventing their ruin, and bringing about kindness, justice, benefit, and well-being through them. This concept is examined more fully in “Gratitude in the Quran: From Saying Thanks Be to God to Responding Responsibly to Blessings.”

The verse’s conclusion connects gratitude directly with worship: “إِن كُنتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ.” Placing “إِيَّاهُ” before the verb of worship emphasizes devotion to God alone. If someone worships only The God, that servitude should also be visible in how they treat His blessings. Worship is not merely a verbal declaration of servitude or the performance of ceremonies; true servitude must become evident in obedience to God’s command, practical gratitude, kindness, justice, and avoidance of corruption. One cannot call God one’s only object of worship while turning His blessings into instruments of injustice and ruin, or declaring things lawful and forbidden for His servants without His permission.

Thus, this verse joins three truths: enjoying wholesome provision, gratitude expressed through action, and sincere worship of God. A grateful person is not merely a recipient of blessings; by using them rightly, that person makes their recognition and servitude evident in life.
