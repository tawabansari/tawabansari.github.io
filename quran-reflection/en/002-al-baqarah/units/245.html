Al-Baqarah 2:245

ۚ

Who will lend The God a good loan, so that He may multiply it for them many times over? And The God restricts and grants abundance, and to Him you will be returned.

Our Reflection

This verse follows the command to fight in God’s way in Al-Baqarah 2:244. Striving for truth and justice requires resources and financial support as well as resolve and steadfastness. Yet in this verse the Quran does not restrict the good loan to financing such a struggle.

“مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يُقْرِضُ اللَّهَ قَرْضًا حَسَنًا فَيُضَاعِفَهُ لَهُ أَضْعَافًا كَثِيرَةً”

“Who will lend The God a good loan, so that He may multiply it for them many times over?”

“Qarḍ,” from the root q-r-ḍ, is connected with setting aside part of one’s wealth and entrusting it to another. God has no need of human wealth; rather, He likens giving wealth in His way to a loan whose repayment He Himself guarantees—and many times over.

But why “قَرْضًا حَسَنًا”—a good loan?

Because not every act of giving is necessarily good. The wealth must have been acquired rightly, be spent for a good purpose, and be given without ostentation, reminders of generosity, or hurt. These principles are also stated in Al-Baqarah 2:262, Al-Baqarah 2:264, and Al-Baqarah 2:267.

This expression also occurs in Al-Maidah 5:12, Al-Hadid 57:11 and Al-Hadid 57:18, At-Taghabun 64:17, and Al-Muzzammil 73:20. Its use alongside charity, zakat, and other good deeds shows that a good loan is not limited to the costs of war; it also includes supporting those in need and spending wealth in good ways.

“وَاللَّهُ يَقْبِضُ وَيَبْسُطُ وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ”

“And The God restricts and grants abundance, and to Him you will be returned.”

“Qabḍ” is connected with taking and restricting, and “basṭ” with opening and expanding. A person’s wealth and resources do not last forever; sometimes one enjoys abundance and sometimes experiences hardship. Those who can help today should therefore not let the opportunity for doing good pass them by.

The promise of multiplying a good loan does not necessarily mean multiplying worldly wealth. The ending of the verse, “وَإِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعُونَ,” reminds us that human beings ultimately return to God and that the reward of their deeds remains preserved with Him.

A good loan is thus the good giving of wealth in God’s way, whether to help those in need, support the oppressed, or provide resources for a just struggle. Its value is not confined to the amount of wealth; the intention, the integrity of its source, the purpose, and the manner of giving also matter.

The God is the Creator and Owner of everything and has no need whatsoever of human wealth. If He wills, He can take back whatever He has given a person, as He says in this very verse: “وَاللَّهُ يَقْبِضُ وَيَبْسُطُ.” Yet despite His complete freedom from need and His power, out of kindness He calls human giving a “loan” and promises to return it many times over. What a beautiful expression! He places what is truly His own at human disposal, and when someone gives part of it for a good purpose, He honors it as though God had borrowed from that person.
