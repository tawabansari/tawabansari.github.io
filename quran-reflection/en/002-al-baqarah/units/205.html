Al-Baqarah 2:205

ۗ

And when he turns away, he strives in the land to spread corruption there and destroy cultivation and offspring; and The God does not love corruption.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:204, The God spoke of people whose speech is appealing and deceptive, and who even call God to witness what is in their hearts. He now describes one of their traits:

“وَإِذَا تَوَلَّىٰ سَعَىٰ فِي الْأَرْضِ لِيُفْسِدَ فِيهَا”

“And when he turns away, he strives in the land to spread corruption there.”

“تَوَلَّىٰ,” from the root و ل ي, here concerns turning away and turning one’s back. “سَعَىٰ,” from س ع ي, means striving and making an effort. The verse thus concerns someone who speaks pleasingly in others’ presence, but whose actions show something different once he turns away.

In the preceding verse, we gave examples of politicians, rulers, leaders, religious authorities, and public representatives who can win people’s trust through attractive promises. Yet assessing the soundness of those promises is not always easy. Determining whether an economic, industrial, social, or political program is genuinely practical and beneficial sometimes requires specialized knowledge, experience, and careful examination.

Ordinary people may be unable to tell whether promises to create thousands of jobs, end poverty, or develop the economy have a sound basis and real resources behind them, or are merely words intended to gain their support.

Here the Qur’an presents a clearer sign for recognizing such people: look at their actions and their consequences. Do they bring reform to the land, or produce corruption?

Of course, those who spread corruption are usually unwilling to call their behavior corrupt. In Al-Baqarah 2:11, The God speaks of people who, when told not to spread corruption in the land, reply:

“إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُصْلِحُونَ”

“We are only reformers.”

But the following verse, Al-Baqarah 2:12, makes clear that they themselves are the corrupters, even if they do not perceive it.

A claim to reform is therefore insufficient. Someone may present themselves as a reformer, a servant of the people, and society’s savior, while their policies and decisions destroy people’s lives and resources.

Continuing Al-Baqarah 2:205, The God gives a more specific sign:

“وَيُهْلِكَ الْحَرْثَ وَالنَّسْلَ”

“And destroys cultivation and offspring.”

“حَرْث,” from the root ح ر ث, concerns cultivation and farming. “نَسْل,” from ن س ل, refers to offspring, children, and the continuation of generations.

The important point is that the Qur’an does not restrict “offspring” here to humans or a particular kind of animal. Destroying offspring can encompass human life, animal life, and the continuation of living species. Alongside this, destroying farmland, food resources, and fertile land is also identified as a sign of corruption.

To recognize present-day examples of this corruption, we need not think only of someone who personally sets a field on fire or kills an animal. Sometimes a government decision or economic policy can harm the lives of thousands of people and other living beings.

For example, war and organized killing can bring a human population to destruction. Policies that forcibly suppress or erase a people’s language, identity, culture, symbols, and customs can also sever the connection between its generations and their history and heritage. These are examples of harm to the continuity of human social and cultural life.

Environmental examples are also clear. Uncontrolled deforestation can destroy animal habitats and vegetation. Excessive hunting may endanger the survival of an animal species. Unrestrained extraction of natural resources, without regard for protecting soil and water, can damage fertile land and harm nearby people and other living beings.

Water pollution, forest destruction, and the loss of plant and animal species are not merely technical or economic problems. When they result from corrupt human decisions and actions, they relate to this verse’s warning.

The Qur’an also says in Ar-Rum 30:41:

“ظَهَرَ الْفَسَادُ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِي النَّاسِ”

“Corruption has appeared on land and sea because of what people’s hands have earned.”

Human corruption can thus affect the whole extent of earthly life. This subject is examined in “Why Is Human Corruption Only on Earth?”.

Consequently, to recognize those who outwardly speak of reform and service, we must consider their policies, decisions, and the actual results of their work.

Do their programs improve people’s lives, or create conditions for more poverty, destruction, and displacement? Do they manage natural resources so that future generations can also benefit, or damage the land and environment for short-term gain? Do they protect people’s rights and identity, or endanger their lives and future to preserve their own power?

Beautiful speech and promises of reform should be measured against actions and their results, rather than the speaker’s fame, position, oaths, or claims of goodwill.

At the end, The God says:

“وَاللَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ الْفَسَادَ”

“And The God does not love corruption.”

This judgment is clear. However beautifully someone speaks and presents themselves as a reformer, corruption and the destruction of cultivation and offspring are incompatible with God’s way.

This verse teaches us not to be deceived by attractive speech, but to recognize people’s character through the effects of their actions. Someone who speaks of prosperity, justice, and progress to gain power, but whose policies destroy people, farmland, natural resources, and the offspring of living beings, cannot conceal the reality of their actions by claiming to bring reform. The God does not love corruption.

In Al-Baqarah 2:206, The God describes another trait of this same character: how he reacts when called to mindfulness. We will examine that trait in the reflection on the next verse.
