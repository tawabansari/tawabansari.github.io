Al-Baqarah 2:76

And when they meet those who have believed, they say: We have believed; but when some of them are alone with others, they say: Do you inform them of what The God (Allah) has opened (fataha) for you, so they may argue against you with it as proof (yuhajjukun) before your Lord and Nurturing Master (Rabb)? Do you then not use reason (ta'qilun)?

Our Reflection

This verse continues the path of the previous verse, but its language goes beyond a simple ethnic report. In verse 75, the subject was a group who heard the word of The God, used reason upon it, and then knowingly distorted it. In this verse, the same illness appears in another face: a claim of faith in public, and concealment in private.

The verse begins with an outward scene: when they meet those who have believed, they say that they have believed. The outward speech is the speech of faith. The language is the language of companionship. In the public space, they present themselves as standing with the people of faith.

But the Qur'an does not stop at appearance. It immediately opens the second scene: when some of them are alone with others. There the speech is no longer about faith. The issue becomes why they are telling the believers about a truth that The God has opened.

This change of language is the key to the verse. When they meet the people of faith, they say: we have believed. In the privacy of their own circle, they are worried that truth will become visible. They do not ask whether they have accepted the truth. They do not ask whether they should submit to what The God has opened. They ask why what The God has opened for them is being reported to others so that those others may use it as proof against them before their Lord.

So their problem is not a lack of awareness. They know that something from The God has been opened for them. They know that what they are hiding can become proof. They know that if this truth reaches the believers, they will no longer be able to hide behind claim, identity, or monopoly. For that reason, in private, their concern is not faith; it is the management of truth.

Here we must be careful: the Qur'an does not extend the statement to all Jews or all People of the Book. The previous verse had already said that this concerned a group from among them. That qualifier remains alive in the context. The subject is a conduct and a current, not the collective condemnation of a people. The Qur'an speaks with justice and does not strip faith from every Jew, every Christian, or every person among the People of the Book.

The Qur'an itself clarifies the criterion elsewhere. Those who believe, Jews, Christians, and Sabians, whoever believes in The God and the Last Day and does righteous action, their reward is with their Lord. So the name Jew, Christian, Sabian, or follower of the Qur'an, by itself, neither saves a person nor expels that person from the mercy of The God. The criterion is the reality of faith, the Last Day, and righteous action.

The Qur'an even says about the People of the Book that they are not all alike. Among them are people who recite the signs of The God, bow in surrender, believe in Him and the Last Day, command what is recognized as right, restrain from what is wrong, and hasten in good deeds. Therefore no one has the right to expel all People of the Book together from the circle of faith and righteousness.

But this Qur'anic justice should not dull the sharpness of the verse. Verse 76 speaks about people who outwardly say that they have believed, while privately they fear the disclosure of truth. This conduct is not limited to one people. It is the face of religious hypocrisy. It may occur among Jews, among Christians, among followers of the Qur'an, and in any God-aware community.

In this verse, the main issue is not ethnicity; it is truthfulness and hypocrisy. A Jew can be a believer if he remains faithful to The God, the Last Day, and righteous action. A Christian can be on the path of faith and righteousness if he is humble before The God, truth-seeking, and a doer of good. A hanif and monotheist can also be near the truth of faith if heart and action turn toward The God and good. But a follower of the Qur'an can also be a hypocrite if he has the language of faith in public and plays with truth in private.

For this reason, verse 76 has a deep similarity with the opening verses of the surah about the hypocrites. There too, the Qur'an spoke of people who, when they meet the people of faith, say that they have believed; but when they are alone with their devils, their language changes. Here the structure is the same: meeting believers, claiming faith; withdrawing into the inner circle, speaking differently.

This similarity is not accidental. The Qur'an shows us that hypocrisy is not imprisoned in one historical name. A hypocrite is not necessarily someone who belongs to one particular people. A hypocrite is someone who preserves the appearance of faith for people, but in private manages truth according to interest, fear, status, and group defense.

The expression of being alone, some with others, makes the scene very vivid. Privacy is where the veil of appearance falls away. Among people, speech is calculated. Before believers, the language of faith is used. But when insiders are alone with insiders, the real concern appears.

This privacy is not only a private gathering in the past. It can symbolize every closed religious space in which one thing is said to people and another thing is decided behind closed doors. Outwardly, there is the name of The God and faith. In private, there is fear of losing power, reputation, or group superiority.

Another key to the verse is what The God opened for them. The Qur'an does not say: what you made. It does not say: what you own. It says: what The God opened for you. Opening (fataha) means that something was closed or hidden and The God opened its way. Such a thing is not the private property of any group. It is a trust.

But opening in the Qur'an is not only simple opening. This root sometimes carries the color of judgment and decision, as when a prophet asks his Lord to open and judge between him and his people with truth. From this angle, what The God opened for them is not merely hidden information. It is as if a file of truth has been opened: a document that can function as proof and judgment.

This meaning fits perfectly with the continuation of the verse: they are worried that the believers may use what The God opened as proof against them before their Lord. So what The God opened is not merely a report for them; it is something with the power of judgment. If it becomes visible, it can remove the veil of claim and make truth speak against those who hide it.

Here the main pain becomes clear. They do not treat divine opening as a trust; they treat it as exclusive capital. It is as though they are saying: why are you telling others what The God opened for us? Why are you allowing them to build proof against us from this very truth? In other words, they do not want truth for the sake of truth; they want truth only as long as it protects their position.

What The God opens should free the human being. It should open the heart. It should clarify the path of guidance. But when the heart becomes hard and self-interested, it even wants to close divine opening. The God opens; the person trapped in hypocrisy covers. The God clarifies; he hides. The God gives truth as a trust; he turns it into a tool for defending his group.

Their question about informing others is also important. They are worried about telling, reporting, and conveying what The God has opened. The root of reporting and speaking (hadatha) is connected with coming into being, newness, report, and speech. In this verse, their anxiety is that knowledge kept inside a closed circle may become speech flowing among people. That is, truth may leave monopoly and enter public conversation.

Here the verse confronts us with a permanent illness of religious institutions: monopoly over knowledge. Those who imagine themselves owners of text and meaning fear truth reaching people. Not because truth is weak, but because if truth becomes clear, their monopoly becomes weak.

When people understand for themselves, they do not accept every claim. When people know what The God has opened, every religious authority cannot impose its own speech upon them in the name of The God. When the word of The God leaves the closed circle and enters the field of public understanding, the owners of monopoly are disarmed.

So their worry is that truth will become spoken and reported; that divine speech will leave the walls of control and move among the people of faith. When truth remains in a closed circle, it can be managed. But when it is spoken, heard, becomes a question, and becomes proof, it no longer remains easily in the hands of those who hold power.

The phrase about using it as proof before their Lord removes the veil further. They ask whether these things are being told so that the believers may use them as proof against them before their Lord. This means they themselves know that what they hide can become proof. They know that in what The God opened there is something that, if disclosed, leaves them answerless before their Lord.

In that same phrase, a very heavy contradiction is hidden. They speak of being before their Lord. That means they do not deny the divine court. They know there is a Lord. They know there is proof. They know that one day truth has weight before Him. Yet they imagine that if they hide the document of truth on earth and in social relations, they will also escape it before the Lord.

This is the theological foolishness of hypocrisy. They want to deal with The God by the logic of human courts. In earthly courts, if a document stays hidden, perhaps the claimant cannot prove the case. If the witness does not arrive, perhaps the case closes. If people do not know, perhaps power remains protected. But before the Lord it is not so. How can something The God Himself opened remain hidden from His knowledge?

Here their claim about using reason becomes absurd. They say to one another: do you not use reason? But they themselves have forgotten the simplest truth of faith: the Lord knows the hidden and the public. They have reduced reason to earthly calculation. They want, by hiding truth from people, to keep proof away from the court of The God as well. This is not reason; it is the illusion of reason.

The next verse breaks this illusion: The God knows what they keep secret and what they make public. This is the Qur'an's direct answer to their hidden private circle. You are worried that truth may reach the believers and become proof against you before your Lord; but do you not know that The God Himself already knows your hidden and your public?

So verse 77 is not an accidental continuation. It is the final blow to the logic of verse 76. They want to control truth. The Qur'an says truth is present before The God before it reaches human tongues. They fear disclosure before believers. The Qur'an reminds them that their most hidden speech is already open before The God.

The root of arguing with proof (hajja) is connected with proof and disputation. In this verse, they fear that believers will use the very thing The God opened for them as an argument against them. So the issue is not merely social discussion. It is the place of truth in the court of the Lord. Their speech shows that their conscience knows something has been hidden.

But the strangest part of the verse is its ending: do you then not use reason? This sentence is spoken by the very people who rebuke one another in private. The bitterness of the verse is here. They do not use reason as submission before truth; they turn reason into a tool of concealment.

In the previous verse, distortion came after they had used reason upon the word. Here the same logic continues. Reason should bind the human being to responsibility. It should restrain the self. It should keep the heart from playing with the sign. But in their language, using reason means calculating that telling the truth will harm us. It means: be alert and do not say something that can be used against us.

This is not reason; it is calculation without reverent awe. Qur'anic reason brings the human being closer to truth. But self-interested reason takes truth into the service of self-defense. They ask: do you not use reason? But what they mean is: why do you not understand that truth must not be disclosed? Why do you not understand that this divine opening can become proof against us?

Here the word reason becomes a bitter mirror. The same reason that should open the path of faith, when separated from reverent awe, can become an instrument of concealment. The human being understands, but does not use understanding to submit to truth. He understands in order to hide better. He understands in order to distort more precisely. He understands in order to block the path of proof.

This verse also shows that claiming faith is not enough. When they meet the believers, they say that they have believed. But the Qur'an also exposes the speech of their privacy. Faith is not a word for meetings with believers. Faith must remain true in privacy as well. If a person speaks the language of faith in the presence of believers, but privately fears the illumination of truth, that is not faith; it is a mask of faith.

From here, the connection of this verse to the broader discussion of faith becomes clear. Faith in the Qur'an is not a closed ethnic or religious identity. Jew, Christian, hanif, Sabian, and follower of the Qur'an, if they are faithful to The God, the Last Day, and righteous action, stand within the field of faith and the just judgment of The God. But hypocrisy too has its door open to everyone. No people is immune from it. No name automatically saves the human being from it.

A person may carry the name Jew and be truthful before The God. A person may carry the name Christian and hasten in good deeds. A person may carry the name one who submits (Muslim), recite the Qur'an, and yet in private hide or distort it for the benefit of power and sect. So the verse does not allow us to strip faith from all Jews. But it also does not allow us to confine hypocrisy and concealment to their past.

This is the justice of the Qur'an. The Qur'an neither calls all People of the Book pure and faultless, nor throws all of them outside faith. It neither considers all followers of the Qur'an saved merely by their name, nor closes the door of mercy upon sincere people and doers of righteousness from other communities. The criterion of The God is not outward names; it is the truth of the heart, truthfulness before truth, faith in the Last Day, and righteous action. For a wider discussion of the difference between religious identity and the reality of faith, see the article Mu'min or Muslim?.

For this reason, verse 76 can awaken every religious community. Wherever a group treats religious knowledge as its private property, the danger of this verse is alive. Wherever truth remains only in closed circles and people are denied direct access to it, the danger of this verse is alive. Wherever people of power say that the public should not know, because if they know they will use it as proof against us, the danger of this verse is alive.

This danger has also occurred in the history of the followers of the Qur'an. The verses of freedom of faith were recited, but structures of compulsion were built. Purifying social giving (zakat), instead of remaining purification, growth, and social responsibility, was turned in the hands of some systems into a tool of taxation and pressure. The word coverer of truth (kafir), which in the Qur'an carries the deep meaning of covering truth, was sometimes turned into a weapon of declaring intellectual and political opponents outside the faith. And the Qur'an, which came for guidance and reason, was turned in the hands of some groups into an instrument for controlling people.

These are exactly the moments in which one should fear the verse. Not blind fear, but conscious reverent awe. For The God has opened something for guidance, while the human being closes it for monopoly. The God has brought the word so that truth becomes clear, while the human being tries to keep that same truth in private so it does not become proof against his power.

Here the concept of concealment also comes close to the text. In another verse, the Qur'an censures those who hide clear signs and guidance after they have been made clear for people. This is the same illness as verse 76: truth has been clarified, but a group wants to stop its circulation. Verse 2:159 shows that concealing truth is not merely an academic error; it is betrayal of people's guidance.

Concealment and distortion belong to the same behavioral family, even though they are not identical. Distortion changes the direction of speech. Concealment hides the speech or sign itself. One turns truth away; the other blocks the way to truth. In verse 75, distortion after reason appeared. In verse 76, concealment and control of divine opening appear. In verse 77, the Qur'an announces that none of these acts of hiding lies outside the knowledge of The God.

So verse 76 is not merely a scene from a hidden conversation of a group in the past. The verse shows how religion is harmed from within. First, the outward appearance of faith is preserved. Then truth is controlled in private. Then people are deprived of knowing what The God has opened. And finally, this concealment is justified in the name of reason and interest.

True faith is the opposite of this. Faith is not afraid of the opening of The God. If The God has opened a truth, the believer does not flee from its clarification. If that truth is against himself, he must still bow before it. If the proof of The God is completed against his own group, he still does not sacrifice truth for the group. Faith means wanting truth to become visible, even if the first person who must be corrected is oneself.

A living question should emerge from this verse: what do I do with what The God has opened for me? Do I see it as a trust or as my property? Do I open it for people's guidance or close it to protect my position? If truth becomes proof against me or my group, do I accept it or hide it? Does my reason bind me to truth, or to my own interest?

The lesson of the verse is this: religious hypocrisy is not only lying with the tongue. Sometimes hypocrisy is keeping opened truth in private because one knows that, if it becomes visible, it will become proof against him. Such a person may say that he has believed when meeting believers, but in privacy he fears truth itself; not because he regards truth as great, but because truth threatens his position.

What The God has opened is a trust. No people, sect, religious class, government, interpreter, jurist, or holder of power has the right to treat it as private property. The word of The God and divine truth are for guidance, not monopoly. Whoever hides it so people cannot use it as proof shows that his problem is not ignorance; his problem is the disclosure of truth.

And if anyone imagines that he can hide truth from people and still remain secure before his Lord, the next verse answers him: The God knows what they conceal and what they disclose. So neither privacy lies outside His knowledge, nor does a public claim of faith deceive Him, nor does a document The God has opened disappear from the court of truth because human beings conceal it.
