Al-Baqarah 2:124

ۖ ۖ ۖ

And when Abraham’s Lord tested him with words and he fulfilled them, He said: I am appointing you as a leader for the people. He said: And from my descendants as well? He said: My covenant does not reach the wrongdoers.

Our Reflection

A few verses earlier, the Children of Israel were reminded of the distinction they had been given; then they heard that on the Day of Reckoning no soul can take another’s place. The discussion now turns to Abraham, the very forefather to whom great lines of descendants trace their ancestry. Yet before mentioning his offspring, the Qur’an speaks of Abraham’s own trial:

“When Abraham’s Lord tested him with words, and he fulfilled them.”

The verse does not say exactly what these “words” were. Elsewhere the Qur’an describes difficult trials Abraham faced, but this verse does not name them. What it brings to the fore is Abraham’s own response: “he fulfilled them.”

His standing therefore does not begin with lineage. Before the position comes the trial; before leadership comes faithfulness.

Then he hears:

“I am appointing you as a leader for the people.”

A leader (imam) is someone placed at the forefront whose way is followed. But from the outset, the wording here reaches beyond the boundary of a people: “for the people.” Not only for Abraham’s family, nor only for a people who would later regard themselves as his heirs.

This breadth continues in other verses. Muhammad is instructed to follow Abraham’s religious way, turning wholeheartedly to God (An-Nahl 16:123), and Aal ‘Imran 3:68 identifies those who followed Abraham as the people closest to him. Closeness to Abraham is not measured by a family tree alone; his way must also be followed.

At this point Abraham asks:

“And from my descendants as well?”

It is a natural request. Having received such a gift, he wants its good for his descendants too. God’s answer, however, neither excludes all his descendants nor makes all of them unconditional heirs to this position:

“My covenant does not reach the wrongdoers.”

This brief sentence is enough to remove lineage from its place as the ultimate criterion. Being a child of Abraham may be a great inheritance, but wrongdoing is not erased by a father’s name. Blood passes on ancestry; it does not, by itself, pass on the divine covenant.

The Qur’an does not deny that Abraham’s family was chosen. Prophethood and the Book were placed among his descendants, and many prophets arose from that line. Yet the same Qur’an says of Noah’s and Abraham’s descendants:

“Some of them are guided, but many of them are defiantly disobedient.” Al-Hadid 57:26.

Of the descendants of Abraham and Isaac, it also says:

“Among their descendants are those who do good and those who clearly wrong themselves.” As-Saffat 37:113.

One family origin, two different paths.

Even where the Qur’an speaks of leaders among the Children of Israel, its wording is “from among them”:

“We appointed from among them leaders who guided by Our command, when they persevered and were certain of Our signs.” As-Sajdah 32:24.

God’s answer to Abraham therefore does not reject his descendants; it rejects inherited immunity. Leaders and prophets did arise from his line, but descent alone does not make someone a leader toward guidance or a bearer of the covenant.

This is not only about the Children of Israel. A Muslim can repeat the same error under another name: placing their trust in a family, denomination, people, religious history, or closeness to holy figures, and gradually imagining that such affiliation creates a special entitlement before God. The verse leaves no room for that:

“My covenant does not reach the wrongdoers.”

No lineage turns wrongdoing into justice.

For this same reason, Abraham’s way extends beyond his descendants. Aal ‘Imran 3:68 does not ask whose veins carry Abraham’s blood; it asks who has followed him. A person can be descended from Abraham and move away from his way, while someone from another people can follow that same way of turning wholeheartedly to God.

Leadership in the Qur’an is not merely an honorary title either. The Qur’an speaks of leaders toward guidance, and elsewhere of leaders who call people toward the Fire. A leader’s worth is determined not by their title, but by the path toward which they lead people.

A fuller discussion of the Qur’anic meaning of a leader, Abraham’s distinctive standing, leaders toward guidance and error, the relationship between leadership and descent, and the question of leadership after Muhammad is presented in Imamah in the Qur’an: Abraham’s Example and the Question of Leadership.

Abraham became a leader for the people, yet even his children did not inherit the covenant merely by bearing his name.

Abraham’s way is not an inheritance acquired at birth; it is a path that must be walked.
