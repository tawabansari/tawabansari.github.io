Al-Baqarah 2:281

ۖ

And be mindful of a day when you will be returned to The God; then every person will be given in full what they have earned, and they will not be wronged.

Our Reflection

This verse follows the Quran’s commands concerning riba, financial rights, and justice. In Al-Baqarah 2:278, God addressed the believers directly and commanded them to relinquish their outstanding claims to riba. In Al-Baqarah 2:279, He emphasized preserving the principal and rejecting injustice on both sides; and in Al-Baqarah 2:280, He instructed that a debtor in hardship be granted time and explained the greater goodness of forgiveness.

Now He reminds those same addressees of a day when every human being must answer for their deeds.

“وَاتَّقُوا يَوْمًا تُرْجَعُونَ فِيهِ إِلَى اللَّهِ”

“And be mindful of a day when you will be returned to The God.”

“اتَّقُوا,” from the root W Q Y, points to care and self-restraint. The command is therefore not simply to fear the day of return, but to watch over today’s conduct with awareness of tomorrow’s accounting. For a broader understanding of this concept, see “What Is Taqwa in the Quran?”.

Although the context directs the verse’s address to believers, what follows does not confine responsibility to them.

“ثُمَّ تُوَفَّىٰ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ مَا كَسَبَتْ”

“Then every person will be given in full what they have earned.”

“كُلُّ نَفْسٍ” emphasizes the universality of this accounting. Every human being is responsible for what they have done.

“تُوَفَّىٰ,” from the root W F Y, points to rendering in full and leaving nothing short. “كَسَبَتْ,” from the root K S B, concerns what a person has acquired. Here it does not mean income and property alone, but the outcome of a person’s deeds, both good and bad.

The Quran uses the same expression in Aal Imran 3:25. In Aal Imran 3:161, after speaking about misappropriation of property, it also emphasizes that every soul will receive the full outcome of its deeds.

This reminder has particular importance in the context of the verses on riba. Someone may profit from others’ inability and accumulate wealth in this world without being held accountable for their conduct. Conversely, someone may reduce their own wealth by respecting others’ rights, giving, forgiving, or relinquishing a personal benefit. Yet divine accounting does not rest solely on the amount of wealth a person has accumulated; their deeds also enter the account.

“وَهُمْ لَا يُظْلَمُونَ”

“And they will not be wronged.”

This expression has a clear connection with the command in Al-Baqarah 2:279: “لَا تَظْلِمُونَ وَلَا تُظْلَمُونَ”—neither wronging nor being wronged.

In that verse, God commanded justice in financial relations among human beings; now He makes clear that no one will be wronged in the final accounting either.

Human beings may violate one another’s rights, conceal the truth, or evade accountability in this world, but no one will encounter injustice in divine judgment.

For a broader discussion of individual responsibility and the accounting of deeds, see “Intercession in the Quran: Divine Permission, Human Responsibility, and a Direct Relationship with God”.

The message of this verse is that faith in the day of return must become visible in a person’s conduct today. Respecting others’ rights, avoiding injustice, desisting from wrongdoing, and doing righteous deeds are responsibilities that do not disappear when life ends. Every human being will receive the outcome of their deeds before The God, and no one will be wronged.
