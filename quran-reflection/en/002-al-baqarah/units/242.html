Al-Baqarah 2:242

Thus The God makes His signs clear to you, so that you may use reason.

Our Reflection

After setting out a series of rulings concerning marriage, divorce, the mutual rights of spouses, bequests, and honorable conduct, God concludes this passage by recalling the purpose of explaining His signs: to make the truth clear so that human beings may understand it by using their reason.

“كَذٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ لَكُم ءايٰتِهِ”

“Thus The God makes His signs clear to you.”

“يُبَيِّنُ,” from the root b-y-n, refers to making things clear and revealing the distinctions between them. By explaining His signs, God clarifies rights, responsibilities, and the limits of human conduct so that truth is not confused with falsehood, nor proper conduct with wrongdoing.

This expression is directly connected with “The Quran’s Clarity and Illuminating Character”. The Quran presents itself as a clear and illuminating Book. In An-Nahl 16:89, it also calls it “تِبْيَانًا لِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ”—a Book that clarifies what is needed within the sphere of human guidance.

The same meaning is connected with “The Quran’s Completeness and Sufficiency for Religious Guidance”. God has explained in His Book what He has deemed necessary for guidance and for understanding human religious responsibilities. Al-Anam 6:114 likewise speaks of a Book sent down in detail. Thus, no one has the right to give human-made statements and rulings precedence over God’s clear signs in the name of completing religion.

“لَعَلَّكُم تَعقِلونَ”

“So that you may use reason.”

“تَعقِلونَ” comes from the root ʿ-q-l, which is associated with understanding, connecting what one knows, and restraining ill-considered judgment.

God explains His signs to human beings who have the capacity to think. Those addressed by the Quran are therefore not merely required to listen, memorize, or repeat its words; they must reflect on its signs, recognize their connections, understand the wisdom of its rulings, and reform their conduct in light of what they have understood.

The Quran’s illumination and human reasoning complement one another: God explains His signs, and human beings are responsible for understanding and applying them in life through reason, reflection, and avoidance of blind imitation.
