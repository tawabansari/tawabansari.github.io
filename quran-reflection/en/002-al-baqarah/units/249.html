Al-Baqarah 2:249

ۚ ۚ ۚ ۗ

Then, when Talut set out with the troops, he said: Indeed, The God will test you with a river; whoever drinks from it is not of me, and whoever does not taste it is of me, except one who scoops up a handful with his hand. So they drank from it, except for a few of them. And when he and those who believed with him had crossed it, they said: Today we have no strength to face Goliath and his troops. Those who thought they would meet The God said: How many a small group has overcome a large group by the permission of The God; and The God is with the steadfast.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verses, we saw that the Children of Israel wanted to fight and have a leader appointed, but expected someone from among their wealthy and noble people to be chosen. Despite Talut’s intellectual and physical qualifications, they objected to his selection. Their Prophet announced the divine choice to them, and The God even made the return of the Ark of the Covenant a sign of Talut’s kingship to grant them tranquility and reassurance.

For the Children of Israel, the Ark of the Covenant was not merely a historical relic; it symbolized their covenant, deliverance, and victory over Pharaoh and his troops. Recalling the remnants of the families of Moses and Aaron could renew their faith and confidence in The God who had previously rescued them from bondage. Now, too, a Prophet was among them, and the leader’s selection had been announced as coming from that same God. This continuity is connected with the Quranic principle “لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِنْهُمْ” in Al-Baqarah 2:136: faith in the Prophets and divine guidance should not be confined to one period or one Prophet. Nevertheless, signs, a symbol, a commitment, the presence of a Prophet, and past victory were not enough by themselves; faith and commitment had not become firmly established in the hearts of all Talut’s companions.

“فَلَمَّا فَصَلَ طَالُوتُ بِالْجُنُودِ”

“Then, when Talut set out with the troops.”

“فَصَلَ,” from the root f-ṣ-l, is connected with separating and separation. Here it refers to Talut’s departure with the troops from where they had been stationed.

“إِنَّ اللَّهَ مُبْتَلِيكُمْ بِنَهَرٍ”

“Indeed, The God will test you with a river.”

Before confronting the enemy, Talut tests his companions’ commitment. Those who had asked to fight and have a leader appointed now had to show in practice how much faith and confidence they had in God’s selection of Talut, the sign of the covenant and divine support, and the possibility of victory by His permission.

The condition of the test was clear: drinking from the river was prohibited, except for a handful of water. The aim was therefore not to deprive the troops of water entirely; rather, their commitment, faith, and obedience had to become evident in practice. Military discipline here was an expression of their commitment, not the ultimate purpose of the test.

“فَشَرِبُوا مِنْهُ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِنْهُمْ”

“So they drank from it, except for a few of them.”

Most of the companions did not pass this test, and only a small group remained faithful to their pledge and command. The central issue now is not the number who failed to obey, but the decision, morale, and faith of the small group who must decide whether to continue the struggle.

The outcome of this struggle depended on those individuals’ steadfastness. Had they too withdrawn from their responsibility, the struggle that led in Al-Baqarah 2:251 to victory and Goliath’s death at David’s hand would not have continued.

“لَا طَاقَةَ لَنَا الْيَوْمَ بِجَالُوتَ وَجُنُودِهِ”

“Today we have no strength to face Goliath and his troops.”

After crossing the river, as they faced Goliath’s power, a more difficult question arose: did this small group have the capacity to confront the enemy army?

Victory in war requires an accurate understanding of one’s own and the enemy’s capabilities, preparedness, and planning. Exaggerating or underestimating either side’s strength can lead to defeat and heavy casualties. Merely expressing concern about the army’s capacity therefore cannot be treated as a sign of lack of faith.

But the Quran goes on to introduce a group who, despite the difficult circumstances, did not lose hope or commitment and did not regard their small numbers as certain proof of defeat.

“قَالَ الَّذِينَ يَظُنُّونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُلَاقُو اللَّهِ كَمْ مِنْ فِئَةٍ قَلِيلَةٍ غَلَبَتْ فِئَةً كَثِيرَةً بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ”

“Those who thought they would meet The God said: How many a small group has overcome a large group by the permission of The God.”

What distinguished this group was their serious belief in meeting God, the same concept discussed in Al-Baqarah 2:46. They did not assess their responsibility solely by calculating the benefits and risks of worldly life; rather, they saw true victory in obeying The God and remaining faithful to the responsibility they had accepted.

This group did not deny the enemy’s numerical superiority, but neither did they regard their own small numbers as certain proof of defeat. They believed in the possibility of victory, not that the outcome was guaranteed in advance. That is why they made victory dependent on “God’s permission.”

In Al-Anfal 8:65 and Al-Anfal 8:66, the Quran likewise emphasizes perseverance and victory by divine permission while taking the troops’ actual weakness into account and lightening their obligation. Faith and confidence in divine support therefore do not replace an accurate understanding of one’s own and the enemy’s capabilities; rather, they give people strength not to abandon their rightful responsibility despite the difficulties.

“وَاللَّهُ مَعَ الصَّابِرِينَ”

“And The God is with the steadfast.”

This ending highlights the perseverance of that committed group. Despite their small numbers and the enemy’s strength, they did not abandon the responsibility they had accepted. In the next verse, too, when facing Goliath, they ask their Lord for patience, firm footing, and help.

What led in Al-Baqarah 2:251 to victory over Goliath and David’s role began with this small group’s steadfastness. Their faith did not remain merely an inward belief; it became decision, commitment, and action.

“Divine permission” concerns events taking place within the patterns and conditions established by God, while “patience” in this context refers to perseverance on the right path. These two concepts are examined more fully in Divine Permission in the Quran and Patience in the Quran.
