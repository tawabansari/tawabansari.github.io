Al-Baqarah 2:237

ۚ ۚ ۚ

And if you divorce them before touching them, while you have already prescribed an obligation for them, then half of what you have prescribed—unless the women themselves forgo it, or the one in whose hand is the marriage bond forgoes it. And that you forgo is nearer to mindfulness; and do not forget generosity between you. Indeed, The God sees what you do.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, the Quran spoke about separation before marital relations begin and respecting the woman’s rights. It now describes a situation in which the woman and man separate before marital relations take place, but the mahr has already been specified.

“فَنِصْفُ مَا فَرَضْتُمْ”

“Then half of what you have prescribed.”

In this situation, the Quran establishes half of the specified mahr as the woman’s right.

One understandable wisdom of this ruling is a person’s responsibility for the pledge and covenant they have entered and the hope they have created in the other person’s heart.

A woman who has accepted the marriage covenant may have become emotionally attached to her future husband, imagined their shared life, and planned for the future. If this covenant is broken before married life begins, all those hopes and commitments cannot be disregarded without any responsibility.

The Quran permits separation, but it does not allow the end of the marriage covenant to mean disregarding the other party’s rights.

It then opens the way for both parties to relinquish their entitlements:

“إِلَّا أَنْ يَعْفُونَ أَوْ يَعْفُوَ الَّذِي بِيَدِهِ عُقْدَةُ النِّكَاحِ”

“Unless the women themselves forgo it, or the one in whose hand is the marriage bond forgoes it.”

“يَعْفُونَ” is a feminine plural verb and refers to the women; however, “الَّذِي بِيَدِهِ عُقْدَةُ النِّكَاحِ” has a masculine singular structure and, in the context of divorce and financial rights in this verse, refers to the husband.

The woman can therefore freely relinquish the half of the mahr to which she is entitled; the man can likewise forgo the remaining half that he is not obliged to pay and give the woman the full mahr.

In this way, both parties can relinquish their financial entitlement and demonstrate goodwill and kindness through generosity.

“وَأَنْ تَعْفُوا أَقْرَبُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ وَلَا تَنْسَوُا الْفَضْلَ بَيْنَكُمْ”

“And that you forgo is nearer to mindfulness; and do not forget generosity between you.”

Forgiveness and generosity on the part of the woman or man can bring an end to the resentment and bitterness arising from the broken marriage covenant. Such a gesture may enable them to preserve respect and a good memory of one another despite their separation.

The Quran first specifies the financial right and then calls both parties to generosity and to remembering graciousness between them. A right may be claimed, but voluntarily relinquishing it can also provide a way to end bitterness and begin a period of peace.

This connection between rights, ma‘ruf, ihsan, and taqwa is examined in the study “Ma‘ruf, Munkar, and Fahsha in the Quran”.

“إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ”

“Indeed, The God sees what you do.”

The God knows the intentions and actions of both: the one who receives their entitlement and the one who relinquishes it in goodwill to preserve generosity between themselves and the other person.

The verse’s message is clear: the breakdown of a marriage covenant must not turn into a violation of rights or hostility. The Quran prescribes half the mahr, but alongside that financial right, it also opens the way to mutual generosity so that the woman and man can separate with goodwill and without resentment or bitterness.
