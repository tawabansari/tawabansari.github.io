Al-Baqarah 2:226

ۖ

For those who swear to withdraw from their wives, there is a waiting period of four months; then if they return, indeed, The God is Forgiving, Merciful.

Our Reflection

“لِلَّذِينَ يُؤْلُونَ مِنْ نِسَائِهِمْ”

“For those who swear to withdraw from their wives.”

In anger or hurt, a person sometimes says something whose effect remains in another’s heart for a long time. This matters especially in married life, because the words of wife and husband can bring each other peace or cause suffering and estrangement.

Sometimes, after a disagreement with his wife, a man swears that he will no longer have marital relations with her. At that moment he may be so angry that he himself does not realize how deeply his words affect her or what consequences they will have for their shared life.

The word “يُؤْلُونَ”, from the root أ ل و, concerns swearing an oath. In this verse, the subject is an oath a man swears to abstain from marital relations with his wife.

In Al-Baqarah 2:224, we read that we must not use the name of The God as an excuse to abandon goodness and reconciliation. In Al-Baqarah 2:225, the Quran also distinguished idle speech from what a person consciously cultivates in their heart.

Now this same responsibility is addressed within married life. A person must not deprive their spouse of the rights of shared life for an indefinite period because of an oath sworn during estrangement or disagreement.

“تَرَبُّصُ أَرْبَعَةِ أَشْهُرٍ”

“There is a waiting period of four months.”

“Tarabbus” denotes waiting and pausing in expectation of an outcome.

The God has set a period of four months for such an oath; four months is the maximum waiting period, not the minimum duration of separation.

If wife and husband reach an understanding after a few days or weeks, why must they remain apart for four months? The Quran has not commanded them to continue their estrangement compulsorily.

This period is an opportunity to step back from intense emotions, reflect on one’s words and decision, and consider the future of married life more calmly.

In family counseling today, a temporary, mutually agreed period apart is also sometimes considered for couples whose disagreements have reached an impasse, so that they can step away from tension and reflect on continuing the relationship. But such distance is useful when it helps understanding and decision-making, not when it becomes a means of punishing a spouse.

For example, during an argument a man tells his wife: “I will never approach you again!” Yet after a few days he calms down and realizes that he neither wanted separation nor wants their shared life to fall apart. Why should he keep himself and his wife suffering for months because of those words?

On the other hand, if a man persists in his decision, he has no right to rely on that oath to leave his wife waiting indefinitely.

The next verse, Al-Baqarah 2:227, speaks of deciding on divorce. Together, these two verses therefore clarify the path of return and the path of making a decision about separation.

Four months is an opportunity for reflection and resolving disagreement, not permission to leave a woman in uncertainty or deprive her of marital rights.

This meaning also accords with the command in Al-Baqarah 2:229, which speaks of retaining a spouse honorably or releasing her with kindness. In Al-Baqarah 2:231, The God also prohibits retaining women with the intention of causing harm.

“فَإِنْ فَاءُوا”

“So if they return.”

“فَاءُوا”, from the root ف ي أ, means returning.

This expression shows that the path of return is open. Someone who has distanced himself from his wife out of estrangement or even through a conscious decision can reconsider and take a step toward reconciliation.

Returning is more than breaking an oath; it can mark the beginning of repairing the relationship, making amends for hurt, and rebuilding affection and trust between wife and husband.

But such a return must respect the spouse’s rights. If someone reconciles only briefly and then again uses oaths and estrangement to hurt the other person, he has not yet addressed the underlying problem.

In Ar-Rum 30:21, The God presents affection and mercy as signs of the marital bond. Shared life should be a place of tranquility, not an arena of threats, revenge, and imposed suffering.

The purpose of marriage and the mutual responsibilities of wife and husband are examined in detail in the article “Quranic Marriage”.

“فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ”

“Then indeed, The God is Forgiving, Merciful.”

After speaking of oaths, withdrawal, and waiting, The God connects the path of return with the two attributes “غَفُورٌ” and “رَحِيمٌ”.

“Ghafur” speaks of abundant forgiveness, and “Rahim” of kindness and mercy.

A person may make a mistake in married life, speak out of pride, or make a decision they later regret. But The God does not close the path of reform and return to them.

If The God is forgiving and merciful, why should a person become so trapped in pride that, even after recognizing a mistake, they close the path of reconciliation to themselves?

The verse’s message is clear: a person’s words and oaths carry responsibility, especially when they affect a spouse’s rights and life. Four months is the upper limit of waiting to end uncertainty, not a command to continue separation. Whenever the path of reform and reconciliation becomes available, return must not be sacrificed to pride, for The God Himself is Forgiving and Merciful.
