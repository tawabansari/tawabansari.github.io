Al-Baqarah 2:224

ۗ

And do not make The God, through your oaths, an obstacle to doing good, practicing taqwa, and reconciling people; and The God is Hearing, Knowing.

Our Reflection

“وَلَا تَجْعَلُوا اللَّهَ عُرْضَةً لِأَيْمَانِكُمْ”

“And do not make The God an obstacle through your oaths.”

People sometimes swear out of anger, prejudice, resentment, or pride that they will not do something—even if it is good and commendable. Then, when an opportunity for reconciliation arises, they use that same oath as an excuse, saying: “I have sworn by God and can no longer go back on my word.”

For example, someone falls out with their brother and swears in God’s name never to help him again or take a step toward reconciliation. Although they know that forgiveness and repairing the relationship would be better, they take refuge in their oath to preserve their pride and resentment.

With this command, The God closes the way to such excuses. The name of The God must not become a pretext for abandoning good deeds and continuing hostility.

The word “عُرْضَةً” here refers to something that stands between a person and an action, becoming an obstacle or a pretext. The Quran does not want people to make their oaths a shield against what is right and good.

The God then recalls three matters:

“أَنْ تَبَرُّوا وَتَتَّقُوا وَتُصْلِحُوا بَيْنَ النَّاسِ”

“That you do good, practice taqwa, and reconcile people.”

“تَبَرُّوا” refers to goodness and doing good. If someone has sworn never again to help a relative in need, they should not place the oath above goodness.

In An-Nur 24:22, The God also prohibits swearing to withhold assistance from relatives, those in need, and emigrants, and calls for pardon and forgiveness.

“تَتَّقُوا” concerns taqwa and mindfulness of God. Taqwa calls on a person to put truth and justice before personal desires. An oath sworn out of pride cannot authorize setting taqwa aside.

“تُصْلِحُوا بَيْنَ النَّاسِ” refers to repairing relationships between people. Sometimes a person feels hurt by a friend or relative and swears never again to take a step toward resolving a dispute. But if an opportunity for reconciliation and repair arises, why should an oath stand in its way?

In An-Nisa 4:114, the Quran identifies reconciliation between people as a good deed, and in Al-Hujurat 49:10 it also commands reconciliation between brothers.

In Al-Maidah 5:89, The God has also explained the means of expiation for an oath. No one should therefore imagine that remaining faithful to a wrongful oath requires abandoning goodness and reconciliation.

An oath that obstructs goodness, taqwa, and reconciliation has no value before The God; it is empty speech, even if spoken in His name. We must not use God’s name to conceal our pride, resentment, and continuing disputes or to make excuses.

In the next verse, Al-Baqarah 2:225, the Quran also mentions “اللَّغْوِ فِي أَيْمَانِكُمْ”—idle speech in your oaths.

The present verse ends with these words:

“وَاللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ”

“And The God is Hearing, Knowing.”

The God hears our oaths and knows our intentions. He knows whether a person is truly seeking to fulfill a duty or has made His name a cover for their prejudice and hostility.

The verse’s message is clear: an oath must not take precedence over goodness, taqwa, and reconciliation. If a person has sworn in God’s name not to do a good deed, they must not violate God’s own command in order to preserve that oath.

Cross-references

  • Al-Baqarah 2:225: Idle oaths and responsibility for what the heart has earned.
  • Aal Imran 3:134: Restraining anger, pardoning people, and doing good.
  • An-Nisa 4:114: The value of reconciling people and doing good.
  • Al-Maidah 5:89: Rules concerning oaths and their expiation.
  • An-Nur 24:22: Prohibiting oaths to withhold assistance from relatives and those in need, and calling for pardon.
  • Al-Hujurat 49:9: The command to reconcile conflicting groups justly.
  • Al-Hujurat 49:10: The command to reconcile brothers and practice taqwa.
