Al-Baqarah 2:7

The God has sealed over their hearts and over their hearing; and over their sight is a covering; and for them is a great punishment.

Our Reflection

This verse continues and explains the condition of those mentioned in the previous verse. When warning and not warning become the same for someone, the matter is not merely that they do not hear a sound. It is a disorder in the human being’s tools of perception: heart, hearing, and sight.

In the Qur’an, the “heart” is not merely the center of emotions. The heart is the place of understanding, orientation, discernment, and moral decision. When the verse says that The God has sealed over their hearts, it should not be understood as an arbitrary deprivation imposed from the outset, as though The God closed the path of guidance against someone without cause. The Qur’an is not in conflict with divine justice. The sealing of the heart is the result of the human being’s path of covering truth. When a person rejects truth again and again, their ability to receive truth gradually becomes closed.

This principle becomes clearer in other verses. In Aṣ-Ṣaff 61:5, it is said that when they deviated, The God caused their hearts to deviate. This means that The God’s causing hearts to deviate is mentioned after people’s own choice to deviate. In Al-Jāthiyah 45:23, the Qur’an speaks of one who has taken his own desire as his god; then The God lets him go astray upon knowledge, seals his hearing and heart, and places a covering over his sight. This verse shows that sealing and misguidance come after a corrupt and chosen inner orientation.

Therefore, when the Qur’an says that The God guides whom He wills and lets go astray whom He wills, it should not be understood as randomness, injustice, or blind determinism. The will of The God in the Qur’an is not without wisdom or without law. The God acts through His consistent ways. Whoever is truth-seeking, humble, and ready for correction is placed on the path of guidance; and whoever repeatedly covers truth, acts arrogantly, and pushes signs aside in favor of desire or bias is left on the path of misguidance.

This can also be seen together with Al-Baqarah 2:26: The God guides many by the same example and lets many go astray by it, but the verse clarifies that He does not let any go astray by it except the fāsiqīn — those who break away from the right order. So divine misguidance is not baseless or accidental; it is connected to corruption, deviation, and the human being’s own orientation.

For this reason, the sealing of the heart in Al-Baqarah 2:7 is not an initial compulsion. It is the result of the divine way in response to persistent kufr. The human being covers truth; then covering truth becomes a habit of perception; and eventually, the heart, hearing, and sight no longer receive truth correctly. This subject is examined separately in the article “The Sealing of the Heart and the Divine Way of Guidance and Misguidance”.

Hearing (samʿ) appears in the singular in this verse, while qulūb — hearts — and abṣār — sights — are plural. This may indicate that the path of hearing the message is, in principle, one shared path: hearing truth. But hearts and sights — the centers of understanding and the angles of human perception — are multiple and scattered. Human beings see, analyze, and orient themselves in different ways; but when hearing truth becomes closed, the message no longer enters the system of understanding.

The covering over their sight (ghishāwah) prevents clear seeing. This does not mean physical blindness. They may have healthy eyes, see the signs, read the verses, and observe the world, yet their seeing does not become insight. The eye receives the image, but if the heart is sealed, the image does not become understanding or guidance.

In this verse, three levels of perception are presented: heart, hearing, and sight. For guidance, the human being does not need only the eye. One must hear, understand, and become inwardly oriented. If the heart is sealed, hearing truth becomes ineffective, and seeing signs can turn into denial or mockery.

The phrase “and for them is a great punishment” is the natural result of this condition. The great punishment is not merely an external penalty; it is the continuation of that great separation from truth. A human being who closes the path of receiving truth against themselves becomes deprived of light, guidance, tranquility, and correction. In the end, this closing of perception leads the human being to a punishment whose greatness comes from the greatness of separation from truth.

This verse should not cause despair for sincere seekers. A person who still asks, still fears, still searches, still examines their doubt honestly, and is still ready for correction has not reached a state of complete closure. The danger of this verse concerns the one who no longer wants to see, no longer wants to hear, and no longer wants to reform themselves before truth.

So Al-Baqarah 2:7 is an image of the human being’s perceptive collapse: when kufr continues, the heart is sealed, hearing truth is closed, sight is covered, and the human being inwardly loses the path of guidance. This is not a sudden ending; it is the result of a path that began with covering truth.
