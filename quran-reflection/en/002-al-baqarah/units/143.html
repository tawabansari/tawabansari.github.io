Al-Baqarah 2:143

ۗ ۚ ۗ ۚ

And thus We made you a community of the middle, so that you may be witnesses concerning people and the messenger may be a witness concerning you. We established the direction of prayer you previously followed only so that We may know who follows the messenger and who turns back on their heels. This was indeed burdensome, except for those whom The God guided. The God does not let your faith go to waste; indeed, He is deeply compassionate and merciful toward people.

Our Reflection

A few verses earlier, the Qur’an spoke of concealing testimony: someone possesses a truth from God but covers it up. It then freed people from captivity to the past: neither the greatness of our predecessors secures our salvation nor the mistakes of other people’s predecessors establish our superiority. Now a community is formed whose task is the very opposite of concealment: bearing witness.

The Qur’an says that this community has been made “a community of the middle” so that you may bear witness concerning people. Being in the middle is therefore a responsibility before it is a title of praise or superiority.

A witness cannot alter truth to favor their own community. If they make their own past so sacred that its mistakes cannot be seen, their testimony is compromised. If they paint other people’s past so darkly that it serves to elevate themselves, they have likewise abandoned the position of a just witness.

Being in the middle does not mean standing halfway between every pair of opinions. Truth is not always equidistant from two claims. A witness may testify entirely in favor of one side if that is where the truth lies. What matters is that prejudice, attachment, and self-interest do not distort the testimony.

The connection with verse 140 is clear. There, concealing testimony was condemned; here, a community is formed to give testimony. If this community conceals a truth to protect its religion, tradition, past, or superiority, it repeats precisely the error already condemned.

Nor is this testimony one-sided. The messenger bears witness concerning this community itself. A community of the middle cannot sit in judgment over others while exempting itself from scrutiny. The very community that must bear witness concerning people is itself subject to testimony.

The verse then presents the direction of prayer as a test. The direction the prophet previously followed became a means through which following and turning back would become evident in practice. The verse itself does not name the former direction, and understanding its reasoning does not require that name. The point is that a change takes place, revealing who moves with guidance and who turns back on their heels.

The image of “turning back on one’s heels” is vivid. People can become so attached to their previous position that when the path changes, they retreat instead of moving with guidance.

Here the connection with verse 141 also becomes evident. Shortly before this, the Qur’an said not to put the past in place of today’s responsibility. The direction of prayer confronts people with the same question: do they continue something merely because they followed it before, or are they ready to move when guidance points in another direction?

The verse does not conceal the difficulty of this change. It says that it was burdensome except for those whom God guided. Guidance is more than knowing a truth; sometimes it is the ability to let go of something familiar when a new direction becomes clear.

But changing direction does not mean that the past has been wasted:

God does not let your faith go to waste.

The Qur’an itself uses the word “faith”; outside accounts should not be used to reduce it to a narrower meaning. What people previously did sincerely, within the guidance available to them, does not necessarily lose its value when a new stage of guidance becomes clear.

This matters in our religious lives as well. Someone may sincerely hold an understanding for years, then realize through the Qur’an or new knowledge that they need to correct their course. A change in understanding today does not mean that their entire past was false and worthless. What matters is whether they move forward or turn back when truth becomes clearer.

From this perspective, being in the middle is also connected with freedom from blind attachments. A community of witnesses must not be imprisoned in its own past, just as it must not use other people’s past to construct a superior identity. Testimony has credibility when truth matters more than our attachments.

The connection between Abraham’s way, surrender, and testimony appears again in Al-Hajj 22:78, where the messenger bears witness concerning the community and the community concerning people. This shows that the community’s responsibility to bear witness is inseparable from that Abrahamic path: it continues the same surrender and sincerity at the level of a community.

The verse ends by turning our attention back to people, rather than emphasizing the community’s superiority: the one God is compassionate and merciful toward people. The community charged with bearing witness concerning them undertakes that responsibility in the light of God’s compassion and mercy toward those same people, without arrogance or contempt.

The lesson is clear: a community of the middle has meaning when it can testify to truth without partiality. It must neither magnify its own past until it conceals what is right nor diminish other people’s past until it imagines itself beyond criticism. If “a community of the middle” becomes an inherited title of superiority, it loses the very purpose for which it was placed in the middle.

Being in the middle is for the sake of bearing witness; testimony has value when truth matters more than our attachments.
