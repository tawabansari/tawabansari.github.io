Al-Baqarah 2:24

So if you do not do it, and you will never do it, then guard yourselves against the Fire whose fuel is people and stones; prepared for the coverers of truth.

Our Reflection

This verse gathers the final stage of the challenge raised in verse 23. After the Qur’an clarified the criterion, namely bringing an equivalent surah if there is doubt, it now states the result of facing that criterion: if you do not do it, and you will never be able to do it, then recognize your relation to the consequence of this denial.

The phrase “if you do not do it, and you will never do it” is not a simple negation. It establishes a structural condition. Lam points to an inability already realized in relation to the past and present, while lan negates the continuation of this ability into the future. So at the level of this verse, the issue is not a suspended possibility; it is a structural inability to produce an equivalent to the criterion presented in the previous verse.

The verse then moves from the level of a knowledge challenge to the level of existential protection: “then guard yourselves against the Fire.” Taqwa here, from the root W Q Y, does not mean mere psychological fear. It means forming a shield, preserving oneself, and creating a preventative relation to the consequence of a condition. The human being must set their relation to the result of denying truth in a way that protects them from it. In this sense, taqwa is an active state of care, not merely an emotional reaction.

The Fire is described as one “whose fuel is people and stones.” Waqud means the material of ignition: that by which fire becomes active and sustained. In this image, people and stones are not only caught in the Fire; they are part of its active material. This points to a deeper level of consequence: what forms against truth ultimately belongs to the same denial and rigidity that the human being has built within.

The pairing of people and stones also shows a structural equivalence. Elsewhere in the Qur’an, hardened hearts are compared to stones. When encounter with truth turns into persistent denial instead of acceptance, the inward structure of the human being approaches a level of rigidity comparable to stone. Here that equivalence moves from the inner level to the level of consequence.

At the end, “prepared for the coverers of truth” clarifies the direction of this structure. Kufr in this context does not mean simple ignorance or initial lack of awareness. It means covering truth after it has become clear. When the sign, argument, and possibility of recognition have been made available, but the human being covers and denies rather than accepts, the condition becomes kufr.

In continuation of verse 23, this verse shows that the challenge to bring an equivalent surah is not merely a theoretical debate. It leads to the way a human being must set their relation to the consequence of denying truth. The Qur’an does not leave the criterion at the level of claim. If someone does not accept that this text is revelation, they must be able to bring a structure equivalent to it.

In this same context, a living example can be seen in religious history. Alongside the Qur’an, human beings have built systems of transmission and reports, and at times they have given them a weight close to revelation, even in the form of reports attributed to God. But when such texts are examined linguistically, in sentence structure, density of meaning, word choice, patterns of divine transcendence, and inner coherence, their difference from the Qur’an becomes visible. This shows that the Qur’anic challenge is not merely to produce religious speech or a spiritual text. The matter is producing a structure equivalent to the speech of God.
