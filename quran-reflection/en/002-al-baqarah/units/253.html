Al-Baqarah 2:253

ۘ ۖ ۚ ۗ ۚ

These Messengers: We favored some of them over others; among them was one to whom The God spoke, and He raised some of them in degrees. And We gave Jesus, son of Mary, clear proofs and strengthened him with the Holy Spirit. And had The God willed, those who came after them would not have fought one another after the clear proofs had reached them; but they differed, so some believed and some disbelieved. And had The God willed, they would not have fought one another; but The God does whatever He wills.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, God identified Muhammad as one of His Messengers. Now He speaks of the distinctions He Himself has granted His Messengers.

«تِلْكَ الرُّسُلُ فَضَّلْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ»

“These Messengers: We favored some of them over others.”

All divine Messengers possess the distinction of messengership and guidance, but God has also distinguished some of them through particular abilities, responsibilities, and blessings.

This verse must be understood in harmony with the principle “لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِنْ رُسُلِهِ” in Al-Baqarah 2:285. Determining the distinctions of the Messengers belongs to God; His servants, however, have no right to discriminate among them out of ethnic, religious, or sectarian prejudice, or to invent ranks for them that God has not established. This subject is explained in the principle of non-discrimination among the Prophets.

«مِنْهُمْ مَنْ كَلَّمَ اللَّهُ وَرَفَعَ بَعْضَهُمْ دَرَجَاتٍ»

“Among them was one to whom The God spoke, and He raised some of them in degrees.”

The Quran gives examples of the Messengers’ special distinctions. An-Nisa 4:163 and An-Nisa 4:164 mention revelation to a number of Prophets, the granting of the Psalms to David, and God’s speaking to Moses: “وَكَلَّمَ اللَّهُ مُوسَىٰ تَكْلِيمًا”; “And The God spoke to Moses, speaking directly.”

«وَآتَيْنَا عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ الْبَيِّنَاتِ وَأَيَّدْنَاهُ بِرُوحِ الْقُدُسِ»

“And We gave Jesus, son of Mary, clear proofs and strengthened him with the Holy Spirit.”

Here God specifically identifies two distinctions of Jesus: receiving clear proofs and being strengthened with the Holy Spirit.

Aal Imran 3:49 and Al-Maidah 5:110 describe examples of Jesus’s clear signs: bringing forth a bird from clay, healing the congenitally blind and those afflicted with leprosy, and bringing the dead to life, all by the permission of The God. Yet the specific expression “أَيَّدْنَاهُ بِرُوحِ الْقُدُسِ” is not used in the Quran of anyone other than Jesus. To understand the distinctive nature of this strengthening, we must also examine Jesus’s coming into being and his connection with the spirit in the Quran.

Muhammad was asked about the nature of the “spirit,” a question that may also have been connected with Jesus. God commanded him to say: “قُلِ الرُّوحُ مِنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّي وَمَا أُوتِيتُمْ مِنَ الْعِلْمِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا” (Al-Isra 17:85): the spirit is of my Lord’s command, and you have been given only a little knowledge. Thus, aware of the limits of our knowledge, we now seek and examine what The God has made clear to us about the reality of the spirit in the Quran’s verses.

In Maryam 19:17, we read: “فَأَرْسَلْنَا إِلَيْهَا رُوحَنَا”: God sent His spirit to Mary. This spirit appeared before Mary in human form and identified himself as her Lord’s Messenger, sent to grant her a pure son (Maryam 19:19).

Al-Anbiya 21:91 also says of Mary: “فَنَفَخْنَا فِيهَا مِنْ رُوحِنَا”; “So We breathed into her of Our spirit.” In An-Nisa 4:171, the Quran also describes Jesus himself as “كَلِمَتُهُ أَلْقَاهَا إِلَىٰ مَرْيَمَ وَرُوحٌ مِنْهُ”: a word from God conveyed to Mary and a spirit from Him.

These connections invite careful reflection. In this reading, Jesus’s connection with the spirit is not limited to receiving revelation during his mission; it is present from the beginning of his existence. Jesus was born of Mary as a human being, but his creation was connected with the breathing of the divine spirit, and he himself is described as “a spirit from Him.” The Quran also speaks of the breathing of spirit in relation to Adam (Al-Hijr 15:29). Thus, the distinctive feature we are considering is not the breathing of spirit alone, but Jesus’s special strengthening with the Holy Spirit and the manifestation of his ability and awareness while still in the cradle.

Another distinctive feature of Jesus is that his awareness of divine truths was evident from the beginning of his life. In Maryam 19:30, while still in the cradle, he said:

«إِنِّي عَبْدُ اللَّهِ آتَانِيَ الْكِتَابَ وَجَعَلَنِي نَبِيًّا»

“Indeed, I am a servant of The God; He has given me the Book and made me a Prophet.”

Jesus’s knowledge and awareness therefore cannot be regarded solely as the product of gradual education and adult experience. In the cradle, he spoke of his servitude to God, the Book, and his prophethood.

This connection becomes clearer in Al-Maidah 5:110, where God says:

«إِذْ أَيَّدْتُكَ بِرُوحِ الْقُدُسِ تُكَلِّمُ النَّاسَ فِي الْمَهْدِ وَكَهْلًا»

“When I strengthened you with the Holy Spirit; you spoke to people in the cradle and in adulthood.”

In the same verse, the teaching of the Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel is also attributed to God. Strengthening with the Holy Spirit and speaking in the cradle are thus mentioned together, indicating a special gift whose effects were evident from the earliest stages of Jesus’s life.

The verses concerning the revelation of the Quran to Muhammad speak of Gabriel’s role in conveying revelation to his heart (Al-Baqarah 2:97) and of the Quran being brought down by the Holy Spirit (An-Nahl 16:102). In Jesus’s case, however, in addition to his strengthening with the Holy Spirit, his very coming into being is connected with the divine spirit. This difference in the Quran’s manner of expression matters.

Nevertheless, the nature of the spirit, how it was breathed into Mary, and the exact relationship between “Our spirit,” “a spirit from Him,” and “the Holy Spirit” are not fully clear to us. Al-Isra 17:85 also reminds us of the limits of human knowledge concerning the spirit. For a broader investigation, see Gabriel and the Spirit in the Quran.

«وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ مَا اقْتَتَلَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ بَعْدِهِمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا جَاءَتْهُمُ الْبَيِّنَاتُ وَلَٰكِنِ اخْتَلَفُوا»

“And had The God willed, those who came after them would not have fought one another after the clear proofs had reached them; but they differed.”

After describing the distinctions of the Messengers, the Quran turns our attention to the conduct of the people who came after them. Despite receiving clear proofs, they differed, and their differences even led to war.

This subject is connected with the principle of non-discrimination among the Prophets. When people make the Messengers symbols of ethnic and religious rivalry instead of following the truth, they may become enemies of one another in the name of those very divine Messengers.

«فَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ آمَنَ وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ كَفَرَ»

“So some of them believed, and some of them disbelieved.”

Despite the clear proofs, not everyone acted alike. Some accepted the truth and others denied it. Having divine guidance does not relieve human beings of responsibility for their choices and conduct.

«وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ مَا اقْتَتَلُوا وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يَفْعَلُ مَا يُرِيدُ»

“And had The God willed, they would not have fought one another; but The God does whatever He wills.”

God could have prevented their fighting, but His will has not been to compel people to believe or to prevent every difference among them. Yunus 10:99 also says that had the Lord willed, everyone on earth would have believed.

God’s granting distinctions to the Messengers is therefore one matter, and the conduct of their followers is another. God has given His Messengers different blessings and abilities, but people are responsible for not turning these differences into a pretext for prejudice, division, and war.
