Al-Baqarah 2:98

Whoever is an enemy to The God, His angels, His messengers, Gabriel, and Michael—indeed, The God is an enemy to those who reject the truth.

Our Reflection

The preceding verse spoke of hostility toward Gabriel, the one who had brought the Qur’an down upon the Prophet’s heart “by permission of The God.” Here it becomes clear that an agent acting by His permission cannot be separated from the source of the command:

“Whoever is an enemy to The God, His angels, His messengers, Gabriel, and Michael…”

The God, the angels, the messengers, Gabriel, and Michael appear in a single sequence. Selective hostility toward part of this chain is incompatible with a claim of complete loyalty to The God. The same problem seen in “We believe in what was sent down to us” can recur here: part of the guidance is accepted, while another part is set aside because it does not match our expectations and desires.

There is also a noteworthy point in the verse’s own construction. The Qur’an does not say “His angels, including Gabriel and Michael”; rather, it says:

“His angels, His messengers, Gabriel, and Michael.”

The angels, the messengers, Gabriel, and Michael are mentioned separately, joined by the conjunction “and.”

The common assumption is that Gabriel is the “angel of revelation”; yet nowhere does the Qur’an introduce Gabriel by name with the title “angel.” This verse, too, names him separately from “the angels.” That separation alone is not sufficient to determine Gabriel’s nature conclusively, but it shows that the familiar definition should not be treated as the Qur’an’s own wording.

The verses concerning the spirit give this question greater weight. The Qur’an repeatedly names the spirit and the angels separately and shows that they can be present together in the same scene. In the verses concerning revelation’s descent, Gabriel is also connected with the same setting in which the Qur’an elsewhere speaks of the trustworthy spirit and the holy spirit. In this respect, the Qur’anic evidence brings Gabriel closer to the sphere of “spirit” rather than explicitly placing him under the category of “angels.”

But this is where we must stop. About the spirit, the Qur’an says:

“Say: The spirit is of my Lord’s command, and you have been given only a little knowledge.”
Al-Isra 17:85

The spirit is of my Lord’s command, and only a little knowledge has been given to you.

We should therefore not fill a gap in our knowledge with another definitive description. If the Qur’an has not called Gabriel an “angel,” we need not add that title to him; and if it has not disclosed the nature of the spirit to us, we should not cross, through conjecture, the boundary that the Qur’an itself has left in place.

For a detailed examination of this subject and its Qur’anic evidence, see Gabriel and the Spirit in the Qur’an. The article When Qur’anic Silence Is Filled with Reports separately discusses how later commentaries have sometimes filled the Qur’an’s silence with reports and definitions drawn from outside it.

The verse ends with a clear judgment:

“Then indeed, The God is an enemy to those who reject the truth.”

The God is an enemy to those who reject the truth.

The judgment is not attached to the name of a people. The Qur’an does not say that The God is an enemy to the Children of Israel or to Jews; it says He is an enemy to those who reject the truth. Once again, the criterion is conduct toward truth, not lineage, ethnicity, or a religious label.

Nor should His enmity be understood as human resentment or emotional hostility. Someone who sets themselves against truth and the command of The God places themselves in a position whose consequence is divine opposition and judgment. This enmity is not personal, passing anger; it is the consequence of the path that the person rejecting the truth has chosen.

Faith cannot be made selective: accepting The God while setting aside what has come from Him, or an agent acting by His permission, because it conflicts with our wishes. At the same time, fidelity to the Qur’an requires that we say no more about the nature of those very agents than the Qur’an itself has said.
