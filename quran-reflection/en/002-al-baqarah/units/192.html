Al-Baqarah 2:192

So if they desist, indeed, The God is Forgiving and Merciful.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:191, The God stated the command to confront those who had fought the people addressed, expelled them from their homes, and caused fitnah. Now He says: “فَإِنِ انتَهَوا”—so if they desist.

This phrase makes clear the limit to continued fighting. If the aggressors desist from fighting, expulsion, and fitnah, war and revenge must no longer continue under the pretext of past hostility. The purpose of confronting them is to end oppression, not to perpetuate killing.

At the end, The God describes Himself as “غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ”—Forgiving and Merciful. Just as He has opened the way to confronting aggression, He also leaves open the way to ending hostility and returning.

An important point is that Al-Baqarah 2:191 used “وَاقْتُلُوهُمْ”, whereas in Al-Baqarah 2:193 we reach “وَقَاتِلُوهُمْ”. The difference between “اقتلوا” and “قاتلوا” is a world apart; we will examine this difference and its importance in the reflection on the next verse.

Cross-references
  • Al-Baqarah 2:190 — Fighting combatants and prohibiting transgression.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:191 — Confronting combatants and those who expel others, and the question of fitnah.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:193 — The difference between “قاتلوهم” and “اقتلوهم”, the purpose of fighting, and the condition for ending it.
  • An-Nisa 4:90 — Prohibiting aggression against those who withdraw from fighting and offer peace.
  • Al-Anfal 8:61 — Accepting peace when the other side inclines toward it.
