Al-Baqarah 2:120

ۗ ۗ ۙ

Neither the Jews nor the Christians will ever be pleased with you until you follow their religious way. Say: Indeed, the guidance of The God is the guidance. And if you follow their desires after the knowledge that has come to you, you will have no protector or helper against The God.

Our Reflection

The preceding verse defined the Messenger’s responsibility: he has been sent with the truth, brings good news, and gives warning, but is not responsible for the ultimate fate of those who refuse the truth. The present verse shows another side of that same boundary: someone who is not charged with forcing people to believe must not alter the truth to win their approval either.

“Neither the Jews nor the Christians will be pleased with you until you follow their religious way.”

Their approval here depends on accepting their way. As long as the Messenger says something that conflicts with their religious assumptions, the truth of what he says does not by itself guarantee their approval. The condition stated in the verse is clear: “until you follow their religious way.”

The response to this demand is brief:

“Say: the guidance of The God is the guidance.”

The question is no longer who will be pleased with you. God’s guidance is the criterion. The approval of a people, a religious community, or any human group cannot turn something that is not guidance into guidance; nor can people’s displeasure strip a truth of its validity.

But the verse distinguishes between a religious way (millah) and desires (ahwāʾ). It first says that they want you to follow their religious way, then warns:

“And if you follow their desires…”

The verse does not say that everything in the religious way of Jews or Christians is desire. It addresses the point at which what they want conflicts with the guidance and knowledge that have reached the Messenger. Beyond that point, following their wishes is no longer following truth; it is following desires.

The weight of the warning becomes clear in what follows:

“After the knowledge that has come to you.”

Before knowing, a person may err through ignorance. But when the truth has become clear to them and they still turn away from it to gain others’ approval, the problem is no longer simply a lack of knowledge. They have chosen between what they know and what others want from them.

This danger does not arise only through force or threats. The desire to be accepted can also draw a person gradually into compromise. Sometimes it is enough to know that speaking plainly will displease a group, or that questioning a widely held belief will damage one’s standing among them. At that point, knowledge and approval stand on opposite sides of a choice.

Later, in the discussion of the direction of prayer, the Qur’an repeats almost the same warning:

“If you follow their desires after the knowledge that has come to you, then you will surely be among the wrongdoers.” Al-Baqarah 2:145.

This warning is therefore not merely abstract. When the direction of prayer becomes an issue, the same conflict between knowledge and desires appears again.

Al-Ma’idah 5:48 draws the same boundary in different words:

“Do not follow their desires, turning away from the truth that has come to you.”

The next verse makes the warning sharper:

“Beware of them, lest they turn you away from some of what God has sent down to you.” Al-Ma’idah 5:49.

The principle is the same in all these verses: the truth and knowledge that have been received must not be set aside to win other people’s approval.

The closing words of Al-Baqarah 2:120 bring the warning to its fullest force:

“You will have no protector or helper against The God.”

This sentence does not say, “You have no protector or helper except God.” It concerns a situation in which someone follows others’ desires after knowledge has reached them. If a person turns away from truth to secure people’s support, those same people cannot protect them against God.

The word “protector” (walī) speaks of closeness, guardianship, and support; “helper” (naṣīr) speaks of aid. The verse closes off both possibilities: no one can take responsibility for protecting a person against God, and no one can rescue them from the consequences of such a choice.

Read together, Al-Baqarah 2:119 and 2:120 define the limits of the mission. The Messenger is not responsible for bringing everyone to faith; nor is he charged with retreating from truth to please them. His task is to convey the truth, not to buy people’s approval at the cost of what he knows.

Once truth has become clear to a person, other people’s approval cannot take its place.

Cross-References
  • Al-Baqarah 2:119— The Messenger has been sent with the truth, brings good news, and gives warning; he is not responsible for the ultimate fate of the companions of the blazing Fire.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:145— A closely matching warning against following desires after knowledge has come, linking that choice to wrongdoing.
  • Al-Ma’idah 5:48— The prohibition against following desires in place of the truth that has come to the Messenger.
  • Al-Ma’idah 5:49— The warning against being diverted from some of what God has sent down.
  • Al-Ma’idah 5:99— “The Messenger’s duty is only to convey the message”: his responsibility for delivering it.
  • Yunus 10:99— Rejecting the use of compulsion to make people believe.
  • Ar-Ra‘d 13:40— “Your duty is only to convey; the reckoning is Ours”: conveying belongs to the Messenger, and reckoning rests with God.
  • An-Najm 53:23— Following conjecture and what the self desires in contrast with guidance received from the Lord.
  • Al-Jathiyah 45:23— A warning about someone who takes their own desire as their deity.
