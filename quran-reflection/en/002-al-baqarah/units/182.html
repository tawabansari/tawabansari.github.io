Al-Baqarah 2:182

ۚ

So whoever fears deviation from what is right or sin on the part of a testator and brings about reconciliation among them, there is no sin upon that person. Indeed, The God is forgiving and merciful.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, The God declared it sinful to alter another person’s bequest. Now He opens the way to correct a bequest in which there is reason to fear deviation from what is right or sin. These two rulings do not conflict. Changing a bequest for personal gain or to deprive others of their rights is one thing; correcting it to prevent injustice and establish fairness is another.

The wisdom of this verse becomes particularly clear in the circumstances surrounding a person’s approaching death. The testator may be elderly, ill, or experiencing memory difficulties and trouble thinking and making decisions. They may be unable to assess all their relatives’ circumstances properly or may unintentionally depart from fairness in distributing property. On the other hand, someone may knowingly seek to violate a right out of anger, favoritism, or hostility. Through the expression “جَنَفًا أَوْ إِثْمًا”, the Quran takes account of the possibility of deviation from what is right and of sin; through “فَأَصْلَحَ بَيْنَهُمْ”, it leaves the way open to correction and reconciliation.

Those who know the testator’s circumstances can therefore help prevent injustice and bring about reconciliation among the people entitled to rights. This assistance should protect rights and uphold what is proper, rather than serve as a means of imposing other people’s wishes on an elderly or ill person. Old age and illness do not in themselves mean an inability to make decisions; what matters is the person’s actual condition and the possibility that others’ rights may be lost.

The ending of the verse with the two attributes “غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ” is also in keeping with opening this path to correction. The God is forgiving and merciful: He does not close the way to remedying mistakes and establishing justice, and He does not regard someone who acts to correct a bequest and prevent injustice as sinful.

Trustworthiness concerning a bequest does not mean preserving every error and injustice. Just as wrongfully altering a bequest is sinful, correcting it to uphold people’s rights and prevent oppression is a permissible and worthy act.
