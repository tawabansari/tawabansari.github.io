Al-Baqarah 2:163

ۖ

And your god is one God; there is no god except Him, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful.

Our Reflection

After the verses about concealing truth, disbelief, and their consequences, the Quran once again turns attention to the shared foundation of all divine calls: “وَإِلٰهُكُم إِلٰهٌ وٰحِدٌ”—your god is one God.

This truth is not confined to followers of a particular people, religious school, or revealed law. The God of Jews, Christians, and followers of the Quran’s revealed law is not three separate deities. The same one God to whom Abraham, Moses, and Jesus called people is the true God of all humanity. In Al-Ankabut 29:46, the Quran also explicitly tells the People of the Book that our God and your God is one. Differences in scriptures, revealed laws, and religious traditions do not turn that one God into separate gods.

“Ilah,” from the root A L H, means an object of worship: someone or something placed in the position of being worshipped. But through “إِلٰهٌ وٰحِدٌ” and then “لا إِلٰهَ إِلّا هُوَ,” the Quran denies every partner and other deity alongside that true God. Divine oneness is not merely a verbal acknowledgment that one God exists; it means accepting His oneness in servitude, ultimate reliance, and obedience. No people, religious school, or religious institution can claim exclusive possession of that one God.

The verse ends with the two attributes “الرَّحمٰنُ الرَّحيمُ.” Al-Rahman signifies God’s general and all-embracing mercy, from which all creatures benefit regardless of their religion or belief. Al-Rahim refers to His special and continuing mercy, connected with guidance, repentance, reform, and a person’s conscious turning toward God. Thus, the same one God whose general mercy embraces all also opens the way to receiving His special mercy.

These two attributes also appear at the beginning of Al-Fatihah. For a closer examination of the difference between general and special mercy, see the reflection on Al-Fatihah 1:1.
