Al-Baqarah 2:211

ۗ

Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We gave them; and whoever changes the blessing of The God after it has reached them, indeed, The God is severe in punishment.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:209, God warned against slipping after clear evidence had arrived. In Al-Baqarah 2:210, He questioned those who seem to await the coming of God and the angels and the settlement of the matter before accepting the truth. Now He recalls the history of the Children of Israel to illuminate human responsibility toward divine signs and blessings:

“سَلْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ كَمْ آتَيْنَاهُمْ مِنْ آيَةٍ بَيِّنَةٍ”

“Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We gave them.”

“Sal” is a command to ask, and “kam” here introduces a question that draws attention to the abundance of signs. This question does not merely seek a number; it confronts the Children of Israel with what they know of their own history.

In Al-Isra 17:101, God also speaks of the nine clear signs He gave Moses and commands that the Children of Israel be asked. These nine signs are a specific set of Moses’ signs; they do not mean that all the blessings and signs given to the Children of Israel were limited to that number.

Surah Al-Baqarah itself recalls many signs: deliverance from Pharaoh’s oppression, the opening of a path through the sea, receiving the Book and the Criterion, the shade of cloud, the provision of food and water, and the guidance of prophets (Al-Baqarah 2:49, Al-Baqarah 2:50, Al-Baqarah 2:51, Al-Baqarah 2:52, Al-Baqarah 2:53, Al-Baqarah 2:54, Al-Baqarah 2:55, Al-Baqarah 2:56, Al-Baqarah 2:57, Al-Baqarah 2:58, Al-Baqarah 2:59, Al-Baqarah 2:60, Al-Baqarah 2:61).

In Al-Baqarah 2:55, the demand to see God openly was raised, even though divine signs had already reached the Children of Israel. This conduct relates to the question in the preceding verse, Al-Baqarah 2:210, about waiting for God and the angels to come.

Thus, seeing many signs does not, by itself, guarantee that a person will accept the truth and act accordingly. The issue is not only the arrival of evidence; the human response to it also matters.

In Al-Jathiyah 45:16, Al-Jathiyah 45:17, God recalls giving the Children of Israel the Book, judgment, prophethood, and wholesome provisions. He then states that after clear evidence and knowledge reached them, they differed among themselves out of transgression.

These verses show that the human problem is sometimes neither a lack of evidence nor ignorance; pride, envy, a desire for superiority, and personal wishes may prevent the acceptance of truth. In An-Naml 27:14, the Quran also speaks of people who denied the signs out of injustice and arrogance despite their inner certainty.

God continues in Al-Baqarah 2:211:

“وَمَنْ يُبَدِّلْ نِعْمَةَ اللَّهِ مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا جَاءَتْهُ”

“And whoever changes the blessing of The God after it has reached them.”

“Yubaddil,” from the root B D L, means changing and putting one thing in place of another.

In Al-Baqarah 2:59, God also spoke of those who replaced the prescribed command and words with other words. In Al-Baqarah 2:61, the Children of Israel were questioned for seeking something inferior in place of something better.

Now, in Al-Baqarah 2:211, the subject is “changing the blessing of The God.” Yet this verse itself does not specify exactly what the person exchanges that blessing for.

To understand this matter, we must consider other Quranic usages.

In Ibrahim 14:28, He says:

“أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى الَّذِينَ بَدَّلُوا نِعْمَتَ اللَّهِ كُفْرًا وَأَحَلُّوا قَوْمَهُمْ دَارَ الْبَوَارِ”

“Have you not seen those who exchanged the blessing of The God for disbelief and brought their people into the abode of ruin?”

Here, what has taken the place of the blessing is explicitly identified as “disbelief.” Moreover, the consequences of the conduct of those making this exchange are not confined to themselves; they also lead their people toward ruin.

But why does the Quran speak of “blessing” in Al-Baqarah 2:211 after speaking of clear signs?

An “ayah” is a sign that guides a person toward recognizing the truth; a “ni‘mah” is a gift a person receives and for which they bear responsibility. A divine gift can be both a sign and a blessing.

For example, the deliverance of the Children of Israel from Pharaoh was both a sign of God’s power and guidance and a blessing that provided them with security and freedom. Receiving the Book likewise opened the way to recognizing truth and was a blessing whose guidance they were to follow.

In Al-Baqarah 2:40, Al-Baqarah 2:47, and Al-Baqarah 2:122, God repeatedly called the Children of Israel to remember His blessings. The question now arises: What did they do with all the signs and blessings they received?

Blessing is not limited to wealth, food, and comfort. Guidance, knowledge, security, freedom, ability, and power can also be blessings. Possessing them, however, is not in itself evidence of merit or divine approval of everything a person does.

In An-Naml 27:40, when Solomon received a great favor, he regarded it as a test of whether he would be grateful or ungrateful.

Thus, a blessing is not merely something one enjoys; it also brings responsibility.

Someone freed from oppression may become an oppressor. Someone who has received a Book of guidance may replace its clear words with human-made rulings. Someone endowed with knowledge and power may use them for deception and domination instead of service and justice.

In such situations, the blessing sometimes remains in a person’s possession while its use changes; at other times, wrongful conduct creates the conditions for losing that very blessing.

In Al-Anfal 8:53, God connects a change in the blessing He has bestowed upon a people with a change in what is within themselves. In Ibrahim 14:6, Ibrahim 14:7, recalling the blessing of deliverance from Pharaoh is also accompanied by a call to gratitude and a warning against ingratitude.

Misusing a blessing is therefore more than verbal ingratitude; it can lead to corruption, oppression, the destruction of opportunities, and the loss of that blessing for individuals and society.

This subject is examined in the studies “Gratitude in the Quran: From Saying ‘Thank God’ to Responding Responsibly to Blessings” and “Who Is a Kafir, and What Is Kufr?”.

Here we should also remember the verse’s connection with Al-Baqarah 2:209. That verse spoke of slipping after clear evidence had arrived; now the warning concerns changing a blessing after receiving it.

These two behaviors are not identical. A slip can result from deception or error, whereas changing speaks of alteration and replacement. The word “changing” alone does not establish every detail of a person’s intention. But when someone knows the truth and consciously sets it aside or exchanges it for disbelief and corruption, their responsibility becomes greater.

This responsibility is particularly significant for those whose knowledge, judgment, and leadership others trust. If a leader knowingly replaces clear guidance with another ruling and calls people to follow it, the harm of that conduct may reach many people.

Followers nevertheless remain responsible for their own choices and conduct. In Ibrahim 14:21, Ibrahim 14:22, during the exchange between followers and leaders on the Day of Reckoning, the Quran does not confine responsibility to one side.

There is another important point in the structure of Al-Baqarah 2:211.

God first names the Children of Israel, but then says:

“وَمَنْ يُبَدِّلْ نِعْمَةَ اللَّهِ”

“And whoever changes the blessing of The God.”

The conditional “man” extends the warning to anyone who acts this way, not only the Children of Israel.

This principle also accords with Aal Imran 3:103, Aal Imran 3:104, Aal Imran 3:105. In those verses, God reminds the believers of the blessing of united hearts and brotherhood, then warns them not to be like those who became divided and fell into disagreement after clear evidence had reached them.

Followers of the Quran must therefore also ask themselves: Do they use the Book God sent down to guide people in accordance with its truth, or do they conceal its clear words beneath sectarian prejudices, inherited rulings, and the authority of leaders?

This warning does not condemn all the Children of Israel or all followers of a religious law. The Quran itself says in Aal Imran 3:113, “لَيْسُوا سَوَاءً”: they are not all alike. The criterion is neither ancestry nor religious designation, but each person’s conduct toward the truth and blessing they have received.

At the end, He says:

“فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ شَدِيدُ الْعِقَابِ”

“Then indeed, The God is severe in punishment.”

“Shadid,” from the root SH D D, relates to strength and intensity, while “‘iqab,” from the root ʿ Q B, concerns punishment and the consequence that follows human conduct.

This conclusion shows that changing a divine blessing after receiving it is no small matter. A person is responsible for a blessing and how they use it, particularly when their conduct also misleads or harms others.

The consequences of such conduct may become apparent in this world: corruption, loss of security, the deterioration of social relations, and the violation of people’s rights. In Ar-Rum 30:41, the Quran also connects corruption on land and sea with what human hands have earned.

This relationship does not mean that every person or society experiencing suffering is necessarily receiving punishment for their own sin; the Quran also speaks of testing people through hardship (Al-Baqarah 2:155). Nor should divine punishment be confined to natural and social consequences; the Quran also speaks of reckoning and punishment in the Hereafter (Al-Baqarah 2:85, Al-Baqarah 2:86).

Despite the severity of this warning, the way back is not closed. As Al-Baqarah 2:160 states, someone who has concealed the truth can repent, reform their conduct, and make clear what they have hidden. In Al-Maidah 5:98, God also speaks of His forgiveness and mercy alongside the severity of punishment.

The Children of Israel received clear signs and many blessings, but possessing them did not, by itself, guarantee guidance and salvation. The same danger exists for anyone who receives God’s blessing and then uses it contrary to what is right or puts something else in its place.

The blessing of guidance is given so that people may recognize the truth and choose the right path. Gratitude for it is shown through faithfulness to truth, reforming one’s conduct, and respecting people’s rights, not through pride in the heritage of prophets, possessing a Book, or claiming religious superiority.

Whoever changes the blessing of The God after receiving it must accept responsibility for their conduct and its consequences. The God is severe in punishment; no title, ancestry, or religious authority can turn the replacement of clear truth into obedience to Him.
