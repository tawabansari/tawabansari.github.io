Al-Baqarah 2:239

ۖ

Then if you fear, on foot or riding; and when you are safe, remember The God, as He taught you what you did not know.

Our Reflection

How has a verse showing one of the clearest expressions of ease in religion been used to conclude that religion must be so demanding that a person has no excuse concerning prayer even in mortal danger? In this very verse, rather than imposing the usual forms of worship upon someone facing danger, God opens a path of ease.

In the preceding verse, Al-Baqarah 2:238, God commanded safeguarding the prayers and standing obediently before Him. He now clarifies how this safeguarding takes place amid fear and danger.

“فَإِن خِفتُم فَرِجالًا أَو رُكبانًا”

“Then if you fear, on foot or riding.”

“خِفتُم,” from the root خ و ف, refers to fear and apprehension of danger. This verse has sometimes been restricted to warfare, although the verse itself imposes no such restriction. God does not say “if you are at war,” but “if you fear.” This fear may arise during war, flight from an attack, an earthquake, a flood, a storm, a fire, or any other situation threatening human safety and life.

“رِجالًا” here means on foot, not men, because it is contrasted with “رُكبانًا,” meaning riders. The Quran thus permits someone facing danger to perform worship in a manner suited to that situation, even while moving, on foot or riding.

For example, imagine an earthquake, with people running out of buildings to save their lives; a severe storm overtaking a ship; or an aircraft suffering an accident in the sky and falling. In such moments, must a person concern themselves with arranging the usual conditions for prayer, standing in a particular place, and performing all its movements and formalities?

In such moments, people remember God more than at many other times. Cries of “God!” rise from every direction. Even someone who ordinarily considers themselves irreligious and does not believe in God may involuntarily utter God’s name when confronted with death.

In Yunus 10:22, the Quran likewise speaks of people who, when their ship is caught in a storm and surrounded by waves, call upon God sincerely and ask Him for deliverance. Al-Ankabut 29:65 also describes this turning toward God amid danger at sea.

In such a situation, more than the formalities of worship, a person needs its reality: remembering God, turning toward Him, and seeking the Lord’s help.

God has provided this very ease for His servants. Someone running to save their life is not compelled to stop moving and perform the usual prayer ritual. Someone escaping danger aboard a vehicle need not expose themselves to harm to perform the usual bowing and prostration. They can preserve their bond of worship and remembrance of the Lord as they are, leaving the usual form of worship until safety returns.

This ruling accords with the “Principle of Ease in Religion”. Al-Baqarah 2:185 states: “يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ بِكُمُ الْيُسْرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ الْعُسْرَ”; The God desires ease for you and does not desire hardship for you. In Al-Hajj 22:78, He states that He has placed no hardship or constraint upon you in religion. We also read in Al-Baqarah 2:286 that God does not burden anyone beyond their capacity.

Thus, safeguarding prayer, as commanded in the preceding verse, does not mean insisting on its usual formalities in every situation. The Quran itself distinguishes the reality of worship from how it is performed under different circumstances in human life. This subject is also examined in “Salat in the Quran: Meaning, Establishment, and the Practice of Prayer”.

“فَإِذا أَمِنتُم فَاذكُرُوا اللَّهَ كَما عَلَّمَكُم ما لَم تَكونوا تَعلَمونَ”

“And when you are safe, remember The God, as He taught you what you did not know.”

After danger passes and safety returns, a person can perform worship peacefully and in its usual manner. This is stated more explicitly in An-Nisa 4:103: “فَإِذَا اطْمَأْنَنتُمْ فَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ”; then when you are secure, establish prayer.

But safety must not lead to forgetting the Lord toward whom one turned in moments of danger. Many people call upon God in danger and promise gratitude, yet lapse into heedlessness again after being saved. In Yunus 10:23, the Quran recalls this very conduct after deliverance from danger at sea.

The concluding expression “كَمَا عَلَّمَكُم مَا لَمْ تَكُونُوا تَعْلَمُونَ” connects remembrance of God with His teaching and guidance. The Lord has taught people the way of servitude and what they did not know. They must safeguard this guidance in both hardship and ease.

The verse’s message is clear: God asks people to maintain their bond with Him even in danger, but He has not made fulfilling this command dependent upon formalities that may endanger their lives. Amid fear, He makes worship easy; after safety returns, He calls people to remember God and perform worship in peace. Divine religion is a path of servitude, not a means of imposing suffering and unnecessary hardship upon people.

  • Al-Baqarah 2:238: Safeguarding the prayers and standing obediently before God.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:286: Each person’s obligation limited to their capacity.
  • Aal Imran 3:191: Remembering God while standing, sitting, and lying on one’s side.
  • An-Nisa 4:101: Permission to shorten prayer during travel and fear of the enemy.
  • An-Nisa 4:102: Arranging congregational prayer in view of enemy danger and the need for security.
  • An-Nisa 4:103: Remembering God in different positions and returning to established prayer when secure.
  • Al-Maidah 5:6: Purification rulings and the emphasis that God does not wish to impose hardship.
  • Yunus 10:22: Calling sincerely upon God amid danger at sea.
  • Yunus 10:23: Forgetting one’s commitment and returning to wrongdoing after deliverance.
  • Al-Hajj 22:78: No hardship or constraint placed in religion.
  • Al-Ankabut 29:65: Turning sincerely toward God amid danger aboard a ship.
