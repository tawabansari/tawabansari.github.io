Al-Baqarah 2:254

ۗ

O you who have believed, spend from what We have provided you before a day comes in which there is neither trade, nor friendship, nor intercession; and the disbelievers are the wrongdoers.

Our Reflection

There is a simple yet meaningful principle in human life: every blessing we have been given reveals its true value and effect when we put it to use.

A person may possess abundant knowledge, brilliant talent, valuable artistic skill, or a good idea for improving people’s lives. But if they do not apply their knowledge, develop their art, or put their good ideas into practice, what benefit have they gained from possessing them? These abilities not only remain unfruitful; they may gradually weaken and even be lost.

This thought opens a way to understand one of the Quran’s repeated commands: “spending.”

«يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَنْفِقُوا مِمَّا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ»

“O you who have believed, spend from what We have provided you.”

From the beginning of Surat Al-Baqarah, in Al-Baqarah 2:3, the Quran identifies spending as a characteristic of the God-conscious: “وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنْفِقُونَ”. There we examined how provision is not limited to money and possessions. Knowledge, time, strength, skills, experience, reputation, opportunities, and other blessings that God has placed at a person’s disposal can also fall within the scope of provision.

“Spending,” or infaq, comes from the root N F Q and is connected with giving out, expending, and putting into circulation what one possesses. Spending is therefore not only giving money: a person can use their knowledge to teach, their abilities to serve, their art to raise awareness, and their thought and creativity to improve society.

The important distinction is that a person should not keep a blessing solely for themselves, but also use it for good and to meet the needs of others.

Knowledge that becomes teaching and healing, art that serves human beings, and a good idea that is put into practice are examples of provision being put into circulation.

This is our responsibility toward divine blessings. Every provision is also an opportunity to do good. For a broader examination, see the studies of the root R Z Q and the root N F Q.

«مِنْ قَبْلِ أَنْ يَأْتِيَ يَوْمٌ لَا بَيْعٌ فِيهِ وَلَا خُلَّةٌ وَلَا شَفَاعَةٌ»

“Before a day comes in which there is neither trade, nor friendship, nor intercession.”

But why does the Quran emphasize spending before that day arrives?

Because a person’s opportunity to use blessings and do good does not last forever. Today we may have ability, knowledge, possessions, and opportunities to serve; tomorrow, perhaps none of them will remain at our disposal.

In Al-Baqarah 2:245, we also saw that God asks people to give a goodly loan from their possessions and reminds them that He restricts and expands. Now this same responsibility is expressed through a reminder that life’s opportunities come to an end.

The day of account is no longer a setting for bargaining, obtaining new opportunities, or compensating for shortcomings through friendship and personal connections. In the Quran, intercession is not a way to bargain, influence divine judgment, or guarantee salvation. Rather, any intercession before The God is subject to His permission, knowledge, and right. For further explanation, see the study “Intercession in the Quran”. In the next verse, Al-Baqarah 2:255, all intercession is likewise made dependent on the permission of The God.

In Al-Munafiqun 63:10, the Quran also commands spending before death arrives, because a person who has lost the opportunity wishes for a little more time to give charity and be among the righteous.

Thus, today’s blessing brings today’s responsibility. Having talent for tomorrow is not enough. If we have the opportunity to do something good today, why postpone it to an uncertain time?

«وَالْكَافِرُونَ هُمُ الظَّالِمُونَ»

“And the disbelievers are the wrongdoers.”

Kufr in the Quran is not merely a matter of lacking a religious label; knowingly concealing and denying the truth is also part of its meaning. Someone who recognizes their blessings and responsibility yet knowingly refuses to fulfill their due wrongs both themselves and others. For further explanation, see “Who Is a Kafir, and What Is Kufr?”.

This verse confronts us with a simple but fundamental question:

To what extent have we put what we possess today to use in our own lives and the lives of others?

Have we turned our knowledge into action, our talents into service, and our good ideas into reality? Or have we merely taken comfort in possessing them?

A day will come when we no longer have the opportunity to use these blessings. While the opportunity remains, then, we should put what God has provided us into circulation in the right way.
