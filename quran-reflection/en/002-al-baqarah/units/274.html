Al-Baqarah 2:274

Those who spend their wealth by night and day, secretly and openly, have their reward with their Lord; and no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve.

Our Reflection

“الَّذِينَ يُنْفِقُونَ أَمْوَالَهُمْ بِاللَّيْلِ وَالنَّهَارِ سِرًّا وَعَلَانِيَةً”

“Those who spend their wealth by night and day, secretly and openly.”

In this expression, the Quran presents two contrasts: night and day, secretly and openly. These contrasts show that giving is not restricted to a particular time or one fixed manner. People can use their wealth for good whenever opportunity and need arise, whether others know about their giving or not.

“سِرًّا” refers to doing something secretly, and “عَلَانِيَةً” to its being open. Neither manner, in itself, is a sign of sincerity or ostentation.

In Al-Baqarah 2:271, the Quran regarded making charities public as good and concealing them when giving to the needy as better. Then, in Al-Baqarah 2:273, it drew our attention to people who, despite their need, do not reveal their poverty because of their restraint.

People should not label someone ostentatious merely on seeing their public giving, or spy on them to uncover hidden motives. The Quran forbids spying in Al-Hujurat 49:12.

In Al-Baqarah 2:272, God also reminded Muhammad that guiding people is not his responsibility. His task is to convey the message, give good news, and warn, not to impose faith or control people’s consciences. For a broader examination of this subject, see “There Is No Compulsion in Religion: The Messenger’s Task Is to Convey”.

Examining trustworthiness in the use of public donations and respect for the rights of those in need is, of course, different from prying into hidden intentions.

The Quran then says:

“فَلَهُمْ أَجْرُهُمْ عِنْدَ رَبِّهِمْ”

“Their reward is with their Lord.”

“Ajr” refers to the reward and recompense for an action. “عِنْدَ رَبِّهِمْ” connects their reward with their Lord.

This expression reminds us that a person does not need others’ approval, praise, or thanks for doing good. Someone may help people in need for years without anyone except the recipients knowing of their kindness. Such giving remaining unknown does not diminish its value with God.

The Quran made the same promise of reward with the Lord in Al-Baqarah 2:262 for those who spend their wealth in God’s way and do not follow their giving with reproach or injury.

Now the ending of that verse is repeated exactly:

“وَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ”

“And no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve.”

“Khawf” concerns fear and apprehension, and “ḥuzn” concerns sorrow. Through these two expressions, the Quran gives those who do good the glad news that their reward is secure with their Lord and that the ultimate outcome of their good deeds will not be accompanied by fear and sorrow.

This verse concludes the continuous discussion of giving. In these verses, the Quran called people to use their possessions rightly, help those in need, preserve their dignity, and put blessings into circulation for good.

In the next verse, Al-Baqarah 2:275, the subject of riba begins. This sequence prepares the way to examine two different directions in financial relations: using wealth for goodness and empowering others, and the financial relations about which the Quran warns in its discussion of riba.

The message of this verse is that people can spend their wealth for good at any time, openly or secretly. The value of giving should not be determined solely by its outward appearance or others’ judgments about the giver’s intentions. What matters is sincerity, right conduct, and respect for human dignity. The reward of those who do good is with their Lord: the same God who knows both public and hidden acts of goodness and has given them the glad news that no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve.
