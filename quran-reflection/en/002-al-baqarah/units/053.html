Al-Baqarah 2:53

And when We gave Musa the Book (al-kitāb) and the criterion of separation (al-furqān), so that you may be guided (tahtadūn).

Our Reflection

This verse comes right after pardon. In verse 51, the Children of Israel, after Musa went to the appointed meeting, took the calf and stood in the position of wrongdoing. In verse 52, God pardoned them after that great failure, but He said: “so that you may give thanks.” Now in verse 53, we see that divine pardon was not only the wiping away of the past. After pardon, the path of guidance was also placed before them again: God gave Musa the Book and the criterion of separation, so that they might be guided.

This order is deeply meaningful: the calf, pardon, then the Book and the criterion. After a failure, the human being does not only need to be forgiven; he also needs a standard of discernment. If the human being is pardoned but still does not know the difference between truth and falsehood, tawhid and image-worship, guidance and imitation, he may very quickly return to another form of the same calf.

The verse says: ātaynā Mūsā al-kitāb wa-al-furqān. The Book and the criterion come together. The Book is the recorded, written, ordered, and binding form of guidance: covenant, command, teaching, and a path given by God to the prophet. But the criterion of separation (furqān) is the ability to separate and discern: the power by which the human being distinguishes truth from falsehood, the road from the wrong road, light from darkness, and tawhid from deceptive forms.

Here we must distinguish between “having the Book” and “having the criterion.” A people may have the Book in their hands, while the criterion has not become active in their souls. A sacred text may be read, memorized, recited, used to build identity, even used for boasting over others; but if that text does not create within the human being the power to discern truth from falsehood, the Book remains at the level of writing and does not become criterion.

This danger is very clear after the story of the calf. They had seen a miracle, crossed the sea, observed the drowning of the people of Pharaoh, and after that were pardoned. But they still needed the criterion to become alive among them. Because the problem of the calf was not merely lack of information. It was failure in discernment. They could not distinguish between the One who is worshiped and a manufactured form, between guidance and collective excitement, between the divine covenant and a tangible point of reliance.

So the Book without the criterion can itself become a new danger. Just as the calf was a visible and sacred-looking form, the Book too, if read without khushu', taqwa, gratitude, and a pure intention, may become a textual calf: something the human being boasts about, but through which he is not guided. In such a condition, the Book is no longer a lamp for the road; it becomes a tool of superiority, argument, self-immunizing claims, and justification of wrongdoing.

From here, the connection of this verse with the earlier verses about the khashi'in becomes clear. In verse 45, it was said that patience and salat are weighty, except upon the khashi'in. In verse 46, the khashi'in were described as those who hold a serious and weighty sense that they will meet their Lord and that they are returning to Him. Such a person, when reading the Book, does not turn it into a tool of domination and pride, because he knows that one day he will meet his Rabb. He reads the Book under the shadow of return to God, not under the shadow of the ego, tribe, sect, or power.

The criterion becomes alive in such a soul. The one who is not khashi' may read the Book and still cover truth with falsehood. He may know the verse, but at the moment of benefit, partisanship, or fear, choose falsehood. But the khashi', because he sees himself before God, reads the Book with healthy fear, hope, gratitude, and responsibility. For him, the Book is not only a text; it is a mirror that must reform him.

For this reason, the Qur'an says in Al-Anfal 8:29: if you have taqwa, God will appoint for you a criterion of separation. This verse is a very important key. The criterion is not obtained merely by possessing a text; it becomes active through taqwa. Taqwa means that the human being is careful in reading, judging, ruling, power, and religious life; careful that desire, partisanship, benefit, fear, or sanctifying forms do not contaminate his understanding.

So every divine revelation, whether Torah, Gospel, or Qur'an, equips the human being with the criterion only when the human being approaches it with a right intention and a humbled heart. The Qur'an calls the Torah guidance and light. It also calls the Gospel guidance and light. It presents itself as guidance for people, and as clear proofs of guidance and criterion. But none of this means automatic guidance for every claimant of the Book.

Here the difference between the Qur'an and a shallow view of the Book becomes clear. The verse does not say: because the Book was given, therefore all were guided. It says: laʿallakum tahtadūn, so that you may be guided. This means the Book and the criterion open the gate of guidance, but the human being must respond. Guidance is a living relationship between God's gift and the human being's reception. God gives, but the human being must turn toward it with khushu', taqwa, gratitude, and seeking truth.

This is exactly what many forms of religiosity have lost. The human being assumes that because he has the true Book, he himself is also truth. But the Qur'an gives no such immunity. Having the Book does not remove the human being from critique and accountability. Someone may even cover truth with the Book; justify wrongdoing with a verse; humiliate people with law; and build a new calf in the name of revelation.

In this verse, al-kitāb and al-furqān are not identical, even though they come from one divine source. The Book is the recorded form of guidance; the criterion is the awakening of the power of discernment in the human being. The Book is like a map, but the criterion is the ability to read the map in the real field of life. If someone has a map but does not have eyes, reason, honesty, and a right intention, he can still lose the road.

For this reason, the criterion becomes necessary at the point where falsehood resembles truth. The calf, for the people of Musa, was not just a meaningless piece of metal. They accepted it as a deity and collective center. When falsehood wears the clothing of religion, sound, beauty, crowd, and sanctity, recognizing it becomes harder. In such a moment, merely having the Book in hand is not enough; the criterion must be active in the heart and mind.

This point is the same regarding Torah, Gospel, and Qur'an. If the Torah is read with khushu' and taqwa, it is light and guidance; if it is read without the criterion and without justice, it can become dry law, ethnic privilege, or a covering for power. The Gospel too, if read with seeking truth, is light and guidance; but if it becomes a closed identity and the sanctification of human beings, it falls away from its purpose. The Qur'an too, if read with taqwa and khushu', is guidance and criterion; but if read with the intention of domination, declaring others kafir (takfir), partisanship, and justifying wrongdoing, the reader remains deprived of the criterion, even if the verses are on his tongue.

From this angle, verse 53 is the continuing treatment for calf-worship. The calf was a failure in discernment; the Book and the criterion rebuild that discernment. The calf was a visible form without truth; the Book is written guidance; and the criterion is the light of discernment between the two. So after pardon, God did not leave them without a map. He gave Musa the Book and the criterion so that, after that bitter failure, the people might recognize the road again.

But laʿallakum tahtadūn still stands at the end of the verse. This means that even after the Book and the criterion, guidance is not forced automatically upon the people. The phrase carries a gentle warning: the road to guidance has been opened, but you must become people of guidance. If you are not khashi', if you do not give thanks, if you do not have taqwa, if you read the Book for the ego, tribe, and power, even that Book will not carry you to the destination.

As a result, verse 53 does not speak only about giving a sacred text. It speaks about giving the Book and the power of discernment; about the human being, after pardon, needing to learn how to separate the road from the wrong road. The Book is a great ni'mah, but if it does not reach the criterion, it may become a dangerous tool in the hand of an unclean human being. The criterion is the living spirit of discernment alongside the Book; and this spirit becomes alive in a heart that is khashi', grateful, has taqwa, and seeks guidance.

So after the calf and pardon, God opened the road again: He gave the Book, He gave the criterion, and He clarified the purpose: “so that you may be guided.” This verse teaches us that the real gratitude for pardon is not merely saying thanks. Real gratitude is that after being pardoned, the human being reads the Book rightly, awakens the criterion within himself, and no longer places visible, textual, or religious calves in the place of truth.

Cross-References
  • Al-Baqarah 2:51— Taking the calf and wrongdoing; the Book and the criterion in verse 53 treat that same failure in discernment.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:52— Pardon after calf-worship; verse 53 shows that after pardon, the path of guidance was opened through the Book and the criterion.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:45-46— The khashi'in are those who take their return to their Lord seriously; such khushu' is a condition for the right understanding of the Book and the criterion becoming alive.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:185— The Qur'an is described as guidance for people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion; a central witness for the relation between the Book, guidance, and the criterion.
  • Ali 'Imran 3:3-4— The sending down of the Torah, the Gospel, and the criterion; showing that the criterion stands within the continuity of divine revelations for distinguishing truth from falsehood.
  • Al-Ma'idah 5:44— The Torah is described in the Qur'an as guidance and light; a witness that earlier revelation also had the capacity for guidance.
  • Al-Ma'idah 5:46— The Gospel is also called guidance and light; showing that the principle of guidance in divine revelation is broader than one particular community.
  • Al-Ma'idah 5:48— The Qur'an confirms the earlier Books and stands as a guardian over them; useful for understanding the Qur'an's relation to Torah and Gospel.
  • Al-Anfal 8:29— If you have taqwa, God will appoint for you a criterion; an important witness that the criterion is connected to taqwa and the inner state of the human being.
  • Al-Anbiya 21:48— Musa and Harun were given the criterion, illumination, and remembrance for the people of taqwa; a direct verse about the criterion in the story of Musa.
  • Al-Furqan 25:1— The sending down of the Furqan upon God's servant as a warning to the worlds; showing the place of Furqan in the final revelation.
  • Al-Jathiyah 45:16— Giving the Book, judgment, and prophethood to the Children of Israel; connecting the ni'mah of the Book with their historical responsibility.
