Al-Baqarah 2:42

And do not clothe truth with falsehood, and do not conceal the truth while you know.

Our Reflection

This verse is the natural continuation of the previous verse. In verse 41, the Qur'an called the Children of Israel to have faith in what God had sent down, because this new revelation confirms what is with them. It then warned them not to be the first kafir toward it and not to trade God's signs for a small price. Now verse 42 shows how this kufr and bargaining happens in practice: first truth is covered and confused with falsehood, then truth is concealed.

The first key word is talbisu. It comes from the root L B S, a root tied to wearing, covering, blending, and making something confused. Clothing covers the body; but here the covering is not over the body, it is over truth. The Qur'an does not say they completely destroy truth, or that they do not recognize truth at all. It says they cover truth with falsehood. In other words, truth is still present, but it is placed inside a covering of falsehood in such a way that recognition becomes difficult for people.

This point is very close to the meaning of kufr. Kufr, too, is originally covering. But in verse 41 the warning was: do not be the first kafir toward the confirming revelation. In verse 42, the mechanism of this covering is explained. Kufr does not always appear as open denial and loud hostility. Sometimes truth is not named so that it can be explicitly denied. Rather, it is mixed with falsehood, wrapped in the clothing of falsehood, partly highlighted and partly hidden, or surrounded with so much wrong context, wording, and interpretation that its guiding force is silenced.

So wa-la talbisu al-haqqa bil-batil is not only a prohibition against simple lying. It is a prohibition against manufacturing religious ambiguity, engineering people's understanding, and covering truth with language that appears righteous. The most dangerous falsehood is not the one that openly presents itself as falsehood. More dangerous is falsehood that wears the clothing of truth, uses religious words, leans on Book, tradition, people, and office, but in practice darkens the path of truth for people.

Naked falsehood is usually easier to recognize. But falsehood that sits on top of truth carries a deeper danger. In such a state, people face something whose outward form is familiar, religious, and authoritative, while inwardly it has been polluted with interest, partisanship, power, concealment, or distortion. This is an epistemic trial: truth has not been fully removed, but it no longer reaches people in a pure and clear form.

Al-haqq in this verse comes with the definite article. This means the verse is not speaking about an undefined or relative truth, but about a known and clear truth in the field of revelation. In the Qur'an, truth is what corresponds with reality, justice, truthfulness, and God's command. In this context, truth can include the clear signs of revelation, recognition of the confirming message, God's judgment in the Book, and a truth of which the People of the Book are aware. Therefore, covering truth with falsehood is a betrayal of knowledge that exists with them.

In contrast, al-batil comes from the root B Ṭ L (B Tt L): something inwardly baseless, fruitless, collapsing, and lacking lasting truth. Falsehood may have form, voice, system, power, or religious covering, but in its foundation it is not firm. When falsehood is mixed with truth, the problem is not only that falsehood has entered. The problem is that truth also becomes unrecognizable to people. People no longer know which part of the speech is the light of revelation and which part is made from interest, power, partisanship, or distortion.

The order of the verses is also very precise. In verse 41, the motive and profit of bargaining was named: thamanan qalilan, a small price. A human being exchanges God's signs for a small interest: wealth, rank, influence, temporary security, approval of one's people, or political alliance. But in verse 42, the tool of that bargain becomes visible: first truth is made confused with falsehood, then truth is concealed. In other words, bargaining with the signs does not always begin with explicit denial. Sometimes it begins with creating ambiguity.

The result of this mixing comes in the second part of the verse: wa-taktumu al-haqq. Concealment comes from the root K T M: hiding, holding inside, and not disclosing something that should become visible. The order of the verse matters: first truth is covered with falsehood, then truth is concealed. Sometimes concealment is not direct. A person does not say, “I am hiding truth.” Instead, he first places truth inside a covering of falsehood, and then that covering itself causes truth to remain hidden from people's sight.

From here the difference between ignorance and concealment becomes clear. A person may not know, may not have understood, or may still be searching. That is not concealment. Concealment is when a human being knows truth but does not make it clear, or mixes it with falsehood in a way that removes its effect. For this reason, the ending of the verse is decisive: wa-antum ta'lamun, while you know.

This phrase makes the ethical weight of the verse heavy. The Qur'an is not speaking to people who have no knowledge at all. It speaks to those who know: bearers of the Book, preservers of the sign, and people who know the language of revelation. Therefore their error is not simple ignorance. It is a knowing error. With knowledge, they clothe truth in falsehood and then conceal it.

Here the concept of kafir can also be understood more precisely. In the logic of the Qur'an, a kafir is not simply someone who has remained uninformed from outside. Kufr takes on its serious ethical form when a human being is aware of truth and yet covers it. Verse 42 describes exactly this state: truth exists, knowledge of it exists, but falsehood is placed over it and then truth is hidden.

In the Medinan setting, this warning also has social and political importance. If the new community is to be built on security, truthfulness, and trust, it cannot live by concealing truth and mixing truth with falsehood. If a group that is People of the Book and aware of the signs makes truth confused among the people, the conflict does not remain merely theoretical. It becomes a breaking of trust, misuse of religion, a threat to social cohesion, and a preparation for hidden and dangerous alliances. From this angle, verse 42 is not only a doctrinal warning. It is a warning about epistemic corruption within society.

This verse is still read in continuation of the “small price” of the previous verse. When God's signs are traded for a small interest, the practical method is this: truth is covered with falsehood so the bargain does not become visible; then truth is concealed so people do not see reality. Selling the signs does not always begin with explicit denial. Sometimes it begins with expedient interpretations, calculated silences, selective use of verses, covering signs, and mixing truth with false speech.

So verse 42 is one of the most precise verses of the Qur'an about religious distortion. Distortion is not only changing the wording. Distortion can be covering truth with falsehood. It can be concealing truth while a person knows. It can be constructing an environment in which truth is no longer seen, even though it is still present in the text. This kind of distortion is more dangerous than open denial, because it is done with religious language and the appearance of knowledge.

At the end, the verse closes the escape route with wa-antum ta'lamun. If you did not know, the matter would be different. But when you know, mixing truth with falsehood and concealing truth is no longer a simple error. It is betrayal of knowledge. And betrayal of knowledge, in the logic of the Qur'an, is one of the great roots of kufr, corruption, and the misguidance of people.
