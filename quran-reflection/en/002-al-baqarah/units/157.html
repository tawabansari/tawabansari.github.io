Al-Baqarah 2:157

ۖ

Upon them are salawat and mercy from their Lord; and they are the rightly guided.

Our Reflection

The good news that God promised to the patient in Al-Baqarah 2:155 becomes clear in this verse. They are those who, when calamity strikes, do not forget the reality of servitude and return to their Lord. They may lose wealth, comfort, or even loved ones, but they do not lose their direction in life or their steadfastness in truth. God says of these very people: “أُولٰئِكَ عَلَيهِم صَلَوٰتٌ مِن رَبِّهِم وَرَحمَةٌ”; salawat and mercy from their Lord are upon them.

“Salawat from their Lord” is an important key to understanding the reality of salat in the Quran. In Al-Baqarah 2:153, believers were commanded to seek help through patience and salat, and heard the promise that The God is with the patient. Now, for the first time in the Quran’s order of verses, salawat is explicitly described as coming from the Lord upon His servants. This connection shows that the relationship of salat should not be understood only as a one-way movement from human beings toward God. Through establishing salat, remembrance, and supplication, servants turn toward their Lord, and God also relates to His servants through His salawat and care. Thus, within its broad Quranic scope, salat reveals a two-way relationship between the servant and the Lord.

Of course, belonging to the same word family does not mean that God and human beings perform the act in the same way. A human being supplicates to the Lord, but God does not pray to a higher being to request mercy. In Al-Ahzab 33:43, the salat of God and the angels upon believers is connected with bringing them out of darkness into light. Divine salawat can therefore be understood as the Lord’s care and bestowal of good, connected with His servants’ mercy and guidance, although its full nature has not been made clear to us. We have examined this subject in detail in Salawat in the Quran (Persian) and Salat in the Quran.

The Lord’s care for the patient is expressed through two terms: “salawat” and “mercy.” These concepts are connected, but are not necessarily synonymous. Amid life’s trials, God’s mercy is not limited to the immediate return of lost wealth or tangible compensation for every loss. In Al-Baqarah 2:154, the Quran even spoke of those killed in God’s path, without treating the end of their bodily life as the end of the whole reality of their existence. Loss and hardship should therefore not be taken as signs that a person is deprived of the Lord’s mercy, just as enjoying worldly blessings is not, by itself, evidence of being guided.

“وَأُولٰئِكَ هُمُ المُهتَدونَ”; and they are the rightly guided. This description concerns the same patient people introduced in the preceding verses. They do not merely know the right path; amid hardship, they maintain their orientation toward servitude and their responsibility. “Al-muhtadun” comes from the root H D Y, and in this context their being guided is connected with their practical steadfastness on the path of truth.

This description has a clear connection with the beginning of Al-Baqarah. In Al-Baqarah 2:3, Al-Baqarah 2:4, and Al-Baqarah 2:5, belief in the unseen, establishing salat, giving, belief in what was sent down to Muhammad and before him, and certainty in the Hereafter are characteristics of the mindful; they are described as being upon guidance from their Lord. In Al-Baqarah 2:177, faith, giving wealth to those in need, establishing salat, keeping promises, and patience in hardship also appear together, and those possessing these qualities are called truthful and mindful. Being guided therefore cannot be confined to a claim of belonging to a people, a religious law, or a religious community. The Quran places criteria of faith and conduct before everyone. In the present verse, however, the discussion specifically concerns the patient who remain steadfast on the path of truth during trials.

In Al-Baqarah 2:153, Al-Baqarah 2:154, Al-Baqarah 2:155, Al-Baqarah 2:156, and Al-Baqarah 2:157, a deep connection emerges between patience, salat, trials, salawat, mercy, and guidance. Servants are called to steadfastness and a relationship with their Lord; amid trials, they recall their orientation toward servitude; and God tells of His salawat and mercy upon them.

The rightly guided are not those who never suffer calamity, but those who do not lose the path of truth even amid calamity. Their steadfastness is a conscious response to their Lord’s guidance, and His salawat and mercy are an expression of a bond that remains unbroken even in hardship.
