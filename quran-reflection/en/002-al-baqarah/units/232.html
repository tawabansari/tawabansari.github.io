Al-Baqarah 2:232

ۗ ۗ ۗ

And when you divorce women and they reach the end of their term, do not prevent them from marrying their husbands if they agree among themselves honorably. Whoever among you believes in The God and the Last Day is admonished through this. That is more wholesome and purer for you; and The God knows, while you do not know.

Our Reflection

This verse addresses society—a society that must not obstruct goodness and reconciliation in other people’s lives through interference, prejudice, and obstruction.

In the preceding verses, the Quran stated the rights of women and men at divorce, and in Al-Baqarah 2:231 it prohibited retaining a woman to harm her and transgress. The discussion now turns to those outside the couple’s relationship whose interference can prevent their reconciliation and remarriage.

“وَإِذَا طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَبَلَغْنَ أَجَلَهُنَّ فَلَا تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ أَنْ يَنْكِحْنَ أَزْوَاجَهُنَّ إِذَا تَرَاضَوْا بَيْنَهُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ”

“And when you divorce women and they reach the end of their term, do not prevent them from marrying their husbands if they agree among themselves honorably.”

“تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ,” from the root ع ض ل, means restraining, obstructing, and placing under constraint. The same word appears in An-Nisa 4:19, where the Quran prohibits constraining women to reclaim their property.

In the present verse, after the waiting period ends, if the woman and her former husband agree to remarry honorably and within the divine limits, others must not obstruct them.

“إِذَا تَرَاضَوْا بَيْنَهُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ” provides a clear criterion: the honorable consent of the woman and man—not the consent of every relative, neighbor, and person who imagines themselves entitled to control their lives.

Sometimes a woman and man resolve their past disagreements after divorce and wish to remarry, but a father, brother, or other relatives obstruct them out of pride, resentment, or concern for family reputation. Instead of welcoming the repair of the relationship, they scatter thorns along its path.

But why should other people’s pride and prejudice take precedence over the consent and informed decision of two individuals?

The family can offer goodwill and advice, but it has no right to substitute its own resentments and wishes for the woman’s and man’s decision.

This issue is not confined to family relationships. In some societies, particularly where part of the population is unemployed and lacks healthy, constructive occupation, other people’s private lives become entertainment. Carry news from this house to that one; speak behind this person’s back and then another’s; interfere in this family’s life and make decisions for another family.

People manufacture rumors, alter others’ words, spread suspicion, and turn small disagreements into major hostilities. Sometimes the couple is ready to reconcile, but the gossip of those around them revives resentments and brings relations between the two families to an impasse.

A society that turns other people’s lives into a field of entertainment, gossip, and unwarranted interference ultimately makes life more constricted both for itself and for others.

In Al-Hujurat 49:6, the Quran commands examining a report before acting upon it, and in Al-Hujurat 49:12 it prohibits unwarranted suspicion, spying, and backbiting. These behaviors can destroy trust among people and make reconciliation difficult.

The same obstructive attitude sometimes appears in government offices and official and judicial institutions. People approach them to resolve disputes, register marriages, obtain documents, or secure their rights, but encounter baseless faultfinding, exhausting bureaucracy, unnecessary delays, and administrative corruption.

Sometimes a corrupt employee or official delays for weeks or months a matter that could easily and lawfully be completed. Demands for bribes, abuse of authority, and fabricated obstacles not only violate people’s rights but may also block the resolution of disputes and the repair of family relationships.

The duty of society and its institutions is to clear the path for right and justice, not to make people’s lives difficult and prevent goodness and reconciliation.

This principle is also connected with Al-Baqarah 2:224, where the Quran prohibited making an oath an obstacle to goodness, mindfulness, and reconciliation among people. In the present verse, too, no one should make prejudice, reputation, family custom, or administrative authority an obstacle to a marriage to which both parties honorably consent.

“ذَٰلِكَ يُوعَظُ بِهِ مَنْ كَانَ مِنْكُمْ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ”

“Whoever among you believes in The God and the Last Day is admonished through this.”

God connects this command with faith and human responsibility on the Last Day. Someone who believes in The God must also answer for the consequences of their interference in other people’s lives.

If someone causes conflict within a family through a rumor, prevents reconciliation through prejudice, or blocks right and reform by abusing power, they cannot detach themselves from responsibility for the harm they have caused.

In An-Nisa 4:114, reconciling people is also identified as a worthy deed. Faith should therefore bring a person closer to goodwill and reconciliation, not gossip, hostility, and obstruction.

“ذَٰلِكُمْ أَزْكَىٰ لَكُمْ وَأَطْهَرُ”

“That is more wholesome and purer for you.”

“أَزْكَىٰ,” from the root ز ك و, is connected with purity, growth, and flourishing, while “أَطْهَرُ,” from the root ط ه ر, emphasizes purity and the removal of impurity.

The expression “لَكُمْ,” “for you,” shows that the benefit of this command is not confined to the woman and man who wish to marry; society itself also benefits.

When people stop spreading rumors, interfering improperly, and creating obstacles, trust among families increases, hostilities diminish, and the path to cooperation and reconciliation remains open. Conversely, a society that continually interferes in other people’s lives itself becomes caught in suspicion and endless disputes.

“وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنْتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ”

“And The God knows, while you do not know.”

Those who judge other people’s lives with certainty may not even know the reality of their disagreements and agreements. They may have heard only one party’s account or a rumor, yet on that basis they seek to decide the future of two people.

Even the closest relatives do not necessarily know every reality of the couple’s life. God knows what is manifest and hidden, whereas human knowledge is limited, and people must not impose their conjectures and impressions upon others’ lives.

The verse’s message is clear: when a woman and man honorably consent to marriage and repairing their relationship, society must stop obstructing them. Families, those around them, and social institutions must clear the path for goodness and justice instead of spreading rumors, acting with prejudice, and making people’s lives difficult. Such conduct benefits not only those two individuals but also the purity, growth, and health of society itself.
