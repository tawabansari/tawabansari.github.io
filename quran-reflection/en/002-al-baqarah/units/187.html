Al-Baqarah 2:187

ۚ ۗ ۖ ۚ ۖ ۚ ۗ ۗ

Sexual relations with your wives have been made lawful for you on the night of siyam. They are a covering for you, and you are a covering for them. The God knew that you were betraying yourselves, so He accepted your repentance and pardoned you. Now have intimate relations with them and seek what The God has prescribed for you; and eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread. Then complete siyam until night; and do not have intimate relations with them while you are in devotional retreat in the mosques. These are The God’s bounds, so do not approach them. Thus The God makes His signs clear to people, so that they may attain taqwa.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:183, we explained that translating al-siyam as “fasting” does not convey the word’s full meaning. The basic meaning of the root ص و م is abstaining and stopping, but in that verse The God had not yet specified which activities this abstention concerned or at what times. Now, in Al-Baqarah 2:187, the answer becomes clear. The God Himself sets out the bounds of al-siyam: what is permitted at night, when abstention from food and drink begins and how long it lasts, and what restriction remains during devotional retreat. To understand the siyam prescribed in the Quran, therefore, we need not restrict the word’s meaning in advance to inherited interpretations; the Quran itself explains how this abstention is practiced.

This is one of the Quran’s clear and firmly established verses explaining the rulings of siyam. It does not require elaborate interpretations remote from the text. The God clearly states what He permits and what He restricts. People’s responsibility is to recognize and observe these bounds as stated, rather than adding to or subtracting from them in the name of religion.

The verse begins with a permission: “أُحِلَّ لَكُمْ لَيْلَةَ الصِّيَامِ الرَّفَثُ إِلَىٰ نِسَائِكُمْ”—sexual relations with your wives have been made lawful for you on the night of siyam. It then says: “هُنَّ لِبَاسٌ لَكُمْ وَأَنْتُمْ لِبَاسٌ لَهُنَّ”—they are a covering for you, and you are a covering for them.

This expression speaks of a close and reciprocal relationship between wife and husband. Just as clothing is close to the body, covers it, and protects it, spouses are close to one another in their shared life and can provide each other with comfort, support, and covering. The Quran does not present this relationship as one-sided: the wife is clothing for the husband, and the husband is also clothing for the wife.

The God then says that He knew the addressees were betraying themselves, so He accepted their repentance and pardoned them: “عَلِمَ اللَّهُ أَنَّكُمْ كُنْتُمْ تَخْتَانُونَ أَنْفُسَكُمْ فَتَابَ عَلَيْكُمْ وَعَفَا عَنْكُمْ”. With “فَالْآنَ بَاشِرُوهُنَّ”, He then states permission for intimacy with their wives and tells them to seek what The God has prescribed for them.

The verse itself describes a previous situation and a new permission, but details of that earlier situation should not be added to the text without evidence. What is clear is that The God knew the addressees’ conduct, opened the way to repentance and pardon, and now stated permission for intimacy. This permission is a command from The God Himself, not the result of human authority to change divine rulings. The distinction between relief explicitly stated by The God and claims that verses have been abrogated on the basis of outside sources is examined in the article “Abrogation in the Quran”.

The God then makes clear the time limits for eating and drinking:

“وَكُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَكُمُ الْخَيْطُ الْأَبْيَضُ مِنَ الْخَيْطِ الْأَسْوَدِ مِنَ الْفَجْرِ”

“And eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread.”

Here The God states the sign marking the start of daily abstention from food and drink: dawn becoming apparent and its light becoming distinct from the darkness of night. He then says:

“ثُمَّ أَتِمُّوا الصِّيَامَ إِلَى اللَّيْلِ”

“Then complete siyam until night.”

The Quran has therefore stated both the beginning and the end of this daily abstention. People may eat and drink until dawn becomes apparent, and afterward complete siyam until night.

These natural signs, however, do not appear in the same way everywhere on earth. In some northern and polar regions, days and nights are very long, and sometimes the usual boundary between light and darkness or the customary signs of dawn cannot be clearly distinguished. Under such conditions, siyam may be overwhelming or even harmful for some people.

The God, who created this very earth and knows His servants’ differing circumstances, also stated the provision of fidyah in Al-Baqarah 2:184. In the third reading of that verse, those for whom siyam lies at the limit of endurance and involves severe hardship can benefit from fidyah; for those able to perform siyam, the goodness of abstention remains.

There is therefore no need to devise new timetables on our own authority as divine commands to resolve difficulties arising from geographical conditions. The Quran has stated both the time signs of abstention and the limits of human ability and means of compensation. A detailed examination, including criticism of fatwas aligning siyam hours with Mecca or neighboring countries, appears in the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:184.

Another point in this verse may have received less attention in common understandings of fasting. It is sometimes assumed that siyam concerns only daytime and that all its restrictions end when night arrives. Yet The God uses “لَيْلَةَ الصِّيَامِ” from the beginning of the verse: the night also belongs to the period of siyam.

Here we must distinguish between the period of siyam and the kind of abstention required at different hours. The God permits eating, drinking, and marital intimacy at night, so the same daytime restrictions cannot be extended to the night without evidence. But permission for these activities does not mean that all bounds associated with siyam have ended.

The verse itself gives a clear example of this distinction:

“وَلَا تُبَاشِرُوهُنَّ وَأَنْتُمْ عَاكِفُونَ فِي الْمَسَاجِدِ”

“And do not have intimate relations with them while you are in devotional retreat in the mosques.”

Although intimacy with wives is permitted on the night of siyam, this same act remains forbidden for those in devotional retreat in the mosques. The God does not confine this restriction to daytime; He connects it with the state of retreat.

The nights of siyam are therefore also part of the period of this worship. Eating, drinking, and intimacy at night are permitted within the bounds The God has allowed, but someone in devotional retreat in the mosques must still abstain from marital intimacy. Al-siyam cannot therefore be reduced to daily hunger and thirst, with the assumption that all its devotional responsibilities end at nightfall. The Quran itself states different bounds for different times and circumstances.

Toward the end, The God says:

“تِلْكَ حُدُودُ اللَّهِ فَلَا تَقْرَبُوهَا”

“These are The God’s bounds, so do not approach them.”

Hudud, from the root ح د د, is connected with marking boundaries and separating domains. In this verse, The God has clarified the boundary between permitted and forbidden acts. People have no right to change these boundaries according to their own desires: neither to prohibit what The God has permitted nor to remove restrictions from what He has forbidden.

The expression “فَلَا تَقْرَبُوهَا” also deserves attention. The God does not merely speak of not crossing the bounds; He commands that people not approach them. This calls for conscious watchfulness regarding forbidden boundaries. Such care is directly connected with the purpose of siyam: cultivating taqwa and self-restraint.

Avoiding approach to divine bounds does not, of course, authorize creating new bounds. We cannot prohibit for everyone what The God has made lawful in the name of precaution, or add a command of our own that is absent from the Quran. Taqwa lies in fidelity to The God’s actual bounds, not in human-made severity.

The verse ends with:

“كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ آيَاتِهِ لِلنَّاسِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ”

“Thus The God makes His signs clear to people, so that they may attain taqwa.”

This ending reveals both the Quran’s manner of explanation and the purpose of siyam. The God Himself clarifies His signs so that people may recognize the bounds of His command. Just as Al-Baqarah 2:183 stated the purpose of siyam as “لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ”, here, after explaining its rulings and bounds, He returns once more to taqwa.

Devotional abstention approaches its purpose when people know what they should refrain from, at what time, and under what circumstances. Eating and drinking when The God has permitted them is not disobedience, just as abstaining from intimacy during retreat is obedience to His command. The standard is neither human desire nor inherited interpretation, but the bounds The God Himself has stated.

Siyam in the Quran is not merely daily hunger and thirst: it is conscious abstention within bounds The God Himself has clarified. Day and night each have their own rulings and permissions, and people move toward taqwa by recognizing and observing these very bounds. Mindfulness does not lie in adding hardship to The God’s command, but in knowing His boundaries and remaining faithful to what He Himself has prescribed.
