Al-Baqarah 2:161

Indeed, those who disbelieved and died while still disbelievers—upon them is the curse of The God, the angels, and all human beings.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:159, the Quran spoke of those who knowingly concealed the clear signs and divine guidance after God had explained them for people in the Book. Then, in Al-Baqarah 2:160, it opened the way back: “تَابُوا وَأَصْلَحُوا وَبَيَّنُوا”; they returned, reformed, and disclosed the hidden truth. A person therefore faces two paths in response to truth made clear to them and the warning against concealing it: to turn away from wrongful conduct and repair what they have damaged, or to persist in denying and covering truth.

Now, in Al-Baqarah 2:161, the discussion moves beyond concealment to disbelief and remaining in it until the end of life. “وَمَاتُوا وَهُمْ كُفَّارٌ” is an important qualification. The verse is not merely about someone who once disbelieved or concealed a truth, but about someone who did not turn away from disbelief and died in that state. The Quran thus distinguishes between someone who still has the opportunity to repent, reform, and return and someone whose life ended in that same state of disbelief. Deliberate concealment can be one expression of disbelief, but this verse no longer concerns concealment alone: it concerns persisting in disbelief until death.

The consequence of this choice is stated as follows: “أُولَٰئِكَ عَلَيْهِمْ لَعْنَةُ اللَّهِ وَالْمَلَائِكَةِ وَالنَّاسِ أَجْمَعِينَ”. “La‘n,” from L ‘ N, means expulsion and distancing. Divine cursing is not merely a verbal imprecation, but exclusion from God’s mercy and guidance, a consequence connected with a person’s own course and conduct. Someone who persists in disbelief enters a path, within divine patterns, that takes them further from truth and mercy. These patterns are not independent of God; they are the regularities of the created order of that same One God.

The expression “وَالنَّاسِ أَجْمَعِينَ” deserves attention. The Quran does not say the curse of believers or Muslims, but the curse of all human beings. Cursing here is therefore not confined to one religious group condemning another. Angels and people exist within God’s created order, and human conduct has real consequences within it. From this perspective, their curse can also be understood in connection with those divine patterns. Yet the verse does not explain how this cursing occurs, and it need not mean that every person knows a particular individual or actually pronounces a curse upon them in this life. Al-Ankabut 29:25 also speaks of people cursing one another at the Resurrection, even those who had previously shared a path of misguidance.

This verse reminds us that the criterion is not merely belonging to a religion, sect, or religious label, but a person’s conduct toward truth and the path on which they remain until life’s end. Nor does anyone have the right to identify another person as a definite instance of this verse merely because of religious disagreement. Knowledge of truth, rejection of it, and a person’s final state before God are not determined by religious labels.

As long as people have the opportunity to return, they can change their course. But if they end their lives on that same path of disbelief, they will also carry the consequence of their choice.
