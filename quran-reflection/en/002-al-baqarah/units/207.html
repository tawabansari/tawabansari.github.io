Al-Baqarah 2:207

ۗ

And among the people is one who exchanges his own self in seeking The God’s pleasure; and The God cares for His servants’ condition with the utmost kindness.

Our Reflection

In the preceding three verses, The God described people whose speech is appealing and deceptive, whose actions bring corruption and the destruction of cultivation and offspring, and whose pride and prejudice drive them to persist in sin when called to mindfulness.

He now presents another person in contrast to that character:

“وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَنْ يَشْرِي نَفْسَهُ ابْتِغَاءَ مَرْضَاتِ اللَّهِ”

“And among the people is one who exchanges his own self in seeking The God’s pleasure.”

Two words in this statement are especially important: “يَشْرِي” and “نَفْسَهُ”.

“يَشْرِي,” from the root ش ر ي, concerns buying, selling, and exchanging. In this verse, a person exchanges their own self in seeking God’s pleasure: they are willing to place what they value for themselves in exchange for something of greater value.

But “نَفْسَهُ” is not confined to life or physical existence. The self is the very subject of existence: the self that thinks, chooses, accepts responsibility, and experiences the results of its actions.

We should therefore not restrict “يَشْرِي نَفْسَهُ” to someone killed in God’s way. Sacrificing one’s life can be one instance of this exchange, but the verse does not impose that condition.

A person can devote their self—with their body, abilities, resources, identity, character, beliefs, prejudices, attachments, and responsibilities—to seeking God’s pleasure.

For example, someone who has defended a belief for years out of religious prejudice, upon recognizing that it is wrong, becomes willing to relinquish personal pride and standing and accept the truth.

A ruler who realizes that a decision has harmed people corrects the mistake instead of protecting power and reputation. Someone who could become wealthy through oppression and corruption forgoes that gain and chooses justice.

These, too, are examples of exchanging oneself: a person relinquishes personal desires and interests to draw closer to The God’s pleasure.

The difference between this character and the person described in the preceding verses now becomes clearer.

In Al-Baqarah 2:206, the corrupt person is unwilling to abandon pride and error because of “الْعِزَّةُ بِالْإِثْمِ”. The person in this verse, however, places God’s pleasure before personal desires and privileges.

One persists in sin to preserve personal pride and advantage; the other is willing to relinquish that same pride and advantage to remain on the path of truth.

The God closes the verse with an important description:

“وَاللَّهُ رَءُوفٌ بِالْعِبَادِ”

“And The God cares for His servants’ condition with the utmost kindness.”

“رَءُوفٌ” comes from the root ر أ ف. Compassion is related to mercy and emphasizes abundant kindness and consideration for another’s condition.

Here, The God describes Himself as “رَءُوفٌ بِالْعِبَادِ”: His servants and their circumstances receive His attention and kindness.

The God does not need people’s lives, wealth, or suffering for His own pleasure. When someone relinquishes wrongful gain, prejudice, or pride, that renunciation serves truth and goodness; it is not so that God may benefit from His servants’ deprivation and suffering.

The same expression, “رَءُوفٌ بِالْعِبَادِ,” also appears in Aal Imran 3:30, a verse that alerts people to the results of their actions. Divine compassion is thus also connected with guidance, warning, and human responsibility. God makes His servants aware of their actions’ consequences and places before them a path for avoiding harm.

In contrast to the corrupt character of the preceding three verses, this verse presents someone who does not see their own worth solely in preserving power, wealth, pride, and prejudices, but is willing to relinquish them for God’s pleasure. Such an exchange does not necessarily mean losing one’s life. Sometimes the hardest sacrifice is setting selfishness aside and accepting a truth that conflicts with our interests.

The God cares for His servants’ condition with the utmost kindness; seeking His pleasure should not be confused with preserving pride, spreading corruption, or trampling the rights of those same servants.
