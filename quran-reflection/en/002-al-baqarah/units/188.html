Al-Baqarah 2:188

And do not consume one another’s wealth wrongfully, nor bring it before rulers to consume a portion of people’s wealth sinfully, while you know.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verses, The God prescribed “al-siyam” for those who believe and identified attaining taqwa as its purpose. In the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:183, we explained that restricting the meaning of “al-siyam” to “fasting” reduces the broad concept of conscious restraint and abstention to one particular form of worship. The Quran then set out the limits of abstaining from eating, drinking, and sexual intimacy. But is human restraint limited to these actions alone?

Now, directly following these verses, The God speaks of another kind of restraint: “وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا أَمْوَالَكُمْ بَيْنَكُمْ بِالْبَاطِلِ”—do not consume one another’s wealth wrongfully. How can someone who abstains from eating and drinking to attain taqwa fail at the same time to refrain from wrongfully taking another person’s property? Does abstaining from food for a few hours achieve the purpose the Quran sets for siyam when a person violates others’ rights in transactions and financial relationships?

This command has a very broad scope and cannot be confined to one kind of transaction, one way of earning income, or one economic practice. “Consuming wealth wrongfully” can take place through deception in trade, taking others’ property, breach of trust, violating financial rights, abusing power, bribery, collusion, and many other methods. What unites these practices is obtaining wealth or benefit wrongfully from the property and rights of others. Their forms may change over time, but the fundamental command of The God remains clear: no one has the right to take another person’s property wrongfully.

One practical example in our time is the conduct of some traders during Ramadan itself. In the very month when people are called to devotional restraint, some sellers exploit increased need and demand to obtain greater profits. They raise the prices of food and essential goods without considering what poor and needy families can afford. For such a trader, Ramadan becomes an opportunity to profit further from others’ needs, instead of an opportunity for taqwa and kindness.

The Quran does not prohibit trade or lawful profit; in An-Nisa 4:29, it contrasts trade by mutual consent with consuming wealth wrongfully. Nor does every price increase automatically amount to wrongful consumption of wealth. Injustice becomes evident when a seller knowingly exploits necessity, shortages, deception, or people’s inability to choose freely in order to obtain wealth beyond what is rightfully theirs. The apparent consent of a buyer who has no other way to obtain bread for their family does not, by itself, establish that the seller’s conduct is just.

The verse is not confined to ordinary buying and selling. The God says: “وَتُدْلُوا بِهَا إِلَى الْحُكَّامِ لِتَأْكُلُوا فَرِيقًا مِنْ أَمْوَالِ النَّاسِ بِالْإِثْمِ وَأَنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ”. A person must not bring wealth or financial claims before rulers and those who adjudicate, using bribery, influence, or an unjust claim to take a portion of people’s property sinfully.

“تُدْلُوا”, from the root د ل و, is connected with lowering a bucket into a well and, in broader usage, also conveys presenting something to achieve a purpose. Here it concerns using wealth or approaching rulers to gain wrongful access to another person’s property. This may involve paying a bribe, presenting false documents, giving false testimony, abusing influence, or concealing the truth from the adjudicator.

Even if someone obtains another person’s property through power, law, or an authority’s ruling, that apparent victory does not establish the justice of their claim. A ruling may be issued in someone’s favor while that person knows better than anyone that they have no right to the property. Can a human ruling truly turn wrongfully obtained wealth into lawful wealth?

The closing words, “وَأَنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ”, intensify the responsibility of someone who acts knowingly. The God is not merely describing an improper transaction; He is speaking of someone who knows what they are doing and, despite that knowledge, strives to take others’ wealth through wrongful means.

The distinction between an inadvertent mistake and a deliberate violation of people’s rights matters here. Someone may make a mistake in a transaction and correct it after becoming aware of it. But someone who knows that property belongs to another and takes it through deception, influence, or misuse of an adjudicating authority cannot hide behind the appearance of a contract or ruling. Their responsibility also arises from their awareness and deliberate conduct.

Devotional restraint must therefore not be separated from economic justice and people’s rights. What value is there in a trader abstaining from eating and drinking during the day, yet failing to restrain greed and self-interest when selling food to someone in need? How can one speak of taqwa while exploiting others’ poverty and desperation to accumulate more wealth?

In Al-Baqarah 2:187, The God permitted eating and drinking at a specified time; in this verse, however, He prohibits consuming people’s wealth wrongfully. This distinction matters: a person may return to eating food after the daily period of abstention ends, but no time has been permitted for wrongfully consuming another person’s property.

The purpose of siyam, as stated in Al-Baqarah 2:183, is to attain taqwa. This taqwa must be evident in actual conduct—in transactions, pricing, respect for people’s rights, trustworthiness, and restraint from oppressive profiteering—just as it is evident in eating and drinking.

True restraint is not merely refraining from food for a few hours; it is also being able to keep one’s hands from others’ wealth, one’s greed from wrongful profit, and one’s power from violating people’s rights. Someone who abstains from food yet consumes others’ wealth wrongfully should ask what they have learned from siyam and what taqwa they have attained.

Cross-references
  • Al-Baqarah 2:183 — Siyam as restraint, with taqwa identified as its purpose.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:187 — The limits of abstaining from eating and drinking during siyam, and the return to the aim of taqwa.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:177 — The connection of righteousness and taqwa with generosity, the rights of those in need, and upright conduct.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:282 — The importance of recording debts and respecting financial rights in transactions.
  • An-Nisa 4:29 — The prohibition of consuming wealth wrongfully, and trade by mutual consent.
  • An-Nisa 4:58 — Returning trusts to their owners and judging justly between people.
  • An-Nisa 4:105 — Judging between people according to truth and not siding with those who betray trust.
  • An-Nisa 4:135 — Standing firmly for justice and truthful testimony, even against oneself and relatives.
  • Al-Maidah 5:8 — Justice even toward those with whom there is enmity.
  • Al-Maidah 5:42 — Condemnation of consuming unlawful gain and the importance of just judgment.
  • Al-Mutaffifin 83:1 — A warning to those who give short measure.
  • Al-Mutaffifin 83:2 — Those who demand full measure when receiving their due.
  • Al-Mutaffifin 83:3 — Those who give less when delivering what others are due.
  • Hud 11:85 — Giving full measure and weight justly, without diminishing people’s rights and property.
