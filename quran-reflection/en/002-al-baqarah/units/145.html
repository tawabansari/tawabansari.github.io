Al-Baqarah 2:145

ۚ ۚ ۚ ۙ

Even if you brought every sign to those who were given the Book, they would not follow your direction of prayer; nor will you follow their direction of prayer, nor do some of them follow the direction of others. If you followed their desires after the knowledge that has come to you, then you would certainly be among the unjust.

Our Reflection

The preceding verse established the community’s new direction toward the Sacred Mosque. Now the Qur’an states a reality plainly: this change will not remove every disagreement.

“Even if you brought every sign to those who were given the Book, they would not follow your direction of prayer.”

The verse does not say to keep supplying evidence until everyone finally accepts one direction. Signs and reasoning make truth clear, but they do not compel a person’s choice. Difference can remain.

The prophet’s position is also clear:

“Nor will you follow their direction of prayer.”

Accepting another person’s freedom does not mean losing one’s own direction. They are not compelled to accept Muhammad’s direction of prayer, and Muhammad does not abandon the clear direction established for him merely to achieve an appearance of agreement.

In one respect, this reasoning resembles Surah Al-Kafirun, without making the two audiences or issues identical. There, worship and religion are addressed in relation to the disbelievers; here, the discussion with the People of the Book concerns the direction of prayer and religious orientation. In both, genuine disagreement is not resolved through forced blending. Boundaries remain clear without either side being compelled to abandon its path to please the other.

The Qur’an does not even portray the People of the Book as a uniform bloc:

“Nor do some of them follow the direction of prayer of others.”

The shared designation “People of the Book” does not erase their internal differences. Religious directions can differ even among those grouped under one broad title. Disagreement should therefore not always be reduced to “us against them.”

Yet the verse does not conclude from these differences that truth is meaningless or that every direction is equivalent. Its central issue emerges at the end:

“If you followed their desires after the knowledge that has come to you…”

The central contrast here is between knowledge and desires.

The direction of prayer of the People of the Book is not itself called a desire or whim. A difference in direction is not, in itself, what is condemned. Desire enters when a group’s pressure, demands, or inclinations seek to replace the knowledge made clear to the prophet.

The same structure appeared earlier in Al-Baqarah 2:120: after knowledge has arrived, following others’ desires leads a person away from truth. Qiblah, Wajh, and Ḥanīf in the Qur’an examines the relationship between these two verses and the connections among a religious way, the direction of prayer, following, desires, and knowledge.

The verse thus distinguishes two things that are sometimes confused: tolerance of difference and retreat from truth. We can accept that others have their own direction of prayer and prescribed way without forcing them to become like us. But this freedom does not mean that we must abandon what we have recognized as true in order to gain their approval.

This also accords with Al-Ma’idah 5:48, where the Qur’an speaks of different prescribed ways and paths, and calls people to compete in good deeds rather than making everyone one community. Al-Hajj 22:67 likewise mentions a rite appointed for each community and discourages turning this difference into dispute. Religious differences can remain; the issue is to keep disagreement from becoming coercion, hostility, and submission to desires.

The ending is emphatic:

“Then you would certainly be among the unjust.”

Injustice here does not arise from different directions of prayer. It begins when, after receiving knowledge, someone sets aside a truth made clear to them in favor of others’ wishes and pressure.

This is the independence these verses establish: do not force others to accept your direction, and do not separate yourself from knowledge to obtain their approval.

A few verses earlier, each community was said to have what it had earned. The same principle appears here at the level of religious orientation. They are responsible for their choice and direction of prayer; the prophet is responsible for remaining faithful to the knowledge that has reached him.

The issue is the same for us. Respect for others is real when we can tolerate their differences, rather than respecting them only while they resemble us. Firmness has value when it rests on knowledge and honesty rather than prejudice.

The verse seeks neither compulsory unity nor an absence of principle. Difference can remain, but knowledge must not be sacrificed to desires.
