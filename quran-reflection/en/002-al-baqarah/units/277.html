Al-Baqarah 2:277

Indeed, those who believed and did righteous deeds, established salah, and gave zakat have their reward with their Lord; and no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:275, the Quran declared riba forbidden and trade lawful; in Al-Baqarah 2:276, it contrasted the eradication of riba with the growth of charities. Now it again recalls faith, righteous action, establishing salah, and giving zakat together.

“إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ”

“Indeed, those who believed and did righteous deeds.”

This connection has been expressed since the beginning of Al-Baqarah. In Al-Baqarah 2:3, faith, establishing salah, and giving were described as characteristics of the God-conscious. In Al-Baqarah 2:62 and Al-Baqarah 2:82, faith and righteous action also formed the basis of the divine promise.

Faith is not merely a verbal claim or religious affiliation; it must become apparent in human conduct. Righteous action likewise includes every right, constructive, and worthy deed consistent with truth, justice, and reform.

In the present context, avoiding riba and refraining from exploiting others’ needs are clear examples of righteous action. Using capital rightly, respecting both parties’ rights in a transaction, helping those in need, and preventing economic oppression also express people’s practical responsibility in relation to their faith.

For a fuller examination, see “The Principle of Faith in The God, the Hereafter, and Righteous Action”.

The Quran then says:

“وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ”

“And established salah and gave zakat.”

These two commands have also appeared repeatedly in this surah, including Al-Baqarah 2:43, Al-Baqarah 2:83, and Al-Baqarah 2:110.

“Iqāmah” refers to establishing and keeping something firm. Establishing salah goes beyond merely performing prayer ceremonially: performing prayer can be one form of establishing salah, but its entire meaning is not confined to movements and reciting words. Salah should keep the remembrance of God alive in a person’s life and affect their ethics and conduct. Zakat is also connected with purity and growth, and giving it is among people’s practical responsibilities toward blessings and society’s needs.

In Al-Baqarah 2:177, the Quran places these same concepts alongside faith, giving wealth, keeping commitments, and steadfastness within its broad definition of righteousness.

Thus, establishing salah and giving zakat are inseparable from righteous action and respecting people’s rights. A person cannot oppress others on the one hand and, on the other, regard worship or paying zakat as a substitute for justice and integrity.

For detailed study, see the book “Salah in the Quran: Its Meaning, Establishment, and the Manner of Prayer” and the study “Zakat and Its Historical Distortion”.

The verse ends with the familiar divine promise:

“لَهُمْ أَجْرُهُمْ عِنْدَ رَبِّهِمْ وَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ”

“Their reward is with their Lord; and no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve.”

This is the same promise stated in Al-Baqarah 2:62, Al-Baqarah 2:262, and Al-Baqarah 2:274.

The message of this verse is that faith, righteous action, establishing salah, and giving zakat are inseparable. In the context of riba, faith and righteous action must also become apparent in people’s economic relations: by avoiding oppression, respecting others’ rights, and using blessings for good. Such people have their reward with their Lord; no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve.
