Al-Baqarah 2:140

ۗ ۗ ۗ

Or do you say that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and the Tribes were Jews or Christians? Say: “Do you know better, or does The God?” And who is more unjust than someone who conceals testimony from The God that is with them? The God is not unaware of what you do.

Our Reflection

The preceding verses have, one after another, freed Abraham from the exclusive claims of later religious identities. He was devoted to God alone, surrendered to the Lord of all worlds, and followed a path marked by faith without discrimination, service, and sincerity toward God. Now the Qur’an asks more directly: do you call Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and the Tribes Jews or Christians?

This is more than a mistake in naming. It colors the past with later identities. People first construct their own religious mold, then fit earlier figures into it, and finally present their own construction as the very truth God intended.

In Al Imran, the Qur’an addresses this problem through a simple historical argument:

“People of the Book, why do you argue about Abraham when the Torah and the Gospel were only sent down after him? Will you not reason?” — Al Imran 3:65

Before the issue becomes a complicated theological dispute, the sequence of history itself challenges the claim. How can Abraham be confined within identities whose historical forms emerged after him?

This gives the answer in Al-Baqarah 2:140 its force: Do you know better, or does God?

The question establishes the authority by which we know. Divine truth is not required to accommodate our inherited identity; our identity and understanding must be tested against truth. If preserving our religion, ethnicity, or tradition requires us to reconstruct the past, the same question confronts us: do we know better, or does God?

But the gravest part of the verse follows: who could be more unjust than someone who conceals testimony from God that is already in their possession?

The Qur’an is not speaking of ignorance. It says that the testimony is with that person, and they conceal it.

Testimony (shahādah) belongs to the root SH H D, associated with presence, observation, awareness, and bearing witness. In contrast to the unseen, it concerns what is present and observable to a person, something to which they can testify. The issue here is therefore not simply a failure to discover truth. It is truth already in someone’s possession, covered up instead of disclosed.

The surrounding verses give this warning further weight. The Qur’an has said: do not discriminate among the prophets; do not make guidance the exclusive possession of a religious identity; God is our Lord and yours; everyone is responsible for their own actions; and make religion and service pure for Him. This is the direction the Qur’an has called the coloring of The God.

If people recognize these principles but conceal or neutralize them to protect the standing of their denomination, a transmitted account, a revered figure, or their community, they impose their own coloring on truth instead of allowing God’s coloring to transform their lives.

Here testimony stands against wishes and cherished notions. Earlier, the Qur’an called exclusive claims to salvation “their wishes.” Testimony concerns what is present before us and can be known; wishful thinking is the picture we want reality to match. The danger begins when desire and identity become so powerful that people conceal testimony to preserve a cherished belief.

This warning is not confined to Jews and Christians of the past. A Muslim, too, may read a clear verse but interpret it so that a predetermined conclusion remains unchanged, because its meaning conflicts with jurisprudence, a transmitted account, a denomination, or a favored idea. The Qur’an may reject discrimination among messengers, yet the reader still uses their own prophet to claim superiority; it may reject compulsion in religion, yet the reader finds a justification for coercion; it may identify God as everyone’s Lord, yet the reader acts as though God belongs exclusively to their community.

In such a case, the problem is not insufficient study. It is that what we know is not allowed to change what we have inherited as belief.

The verse ends by saying that the one God is not unaware of what you do. Concealment is more than a mental condition: it is an action. We recognize a truth and then do something with it: disclose it, cover it up, or reshape it until it no longer threatens our assumptions.

The lesson is clear and difficult. Freely seeking truth means more than having the right to ask questions. It also requires the courage to accept an answer, even when it corrects something we have held onto for years. Sincerity means refusing to sacrifice truth for our identity. The coloring of The God is where what God has made clear to us changes our own coloring, rather than our first choosing a color and then portraying God in its image.
