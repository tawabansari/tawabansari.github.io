Al-Baqarah 2:215

ۖ ۗ

They ask you what they should spend. Say: Whatever good you spend, let it be for parents, close relatives, orphans, those in need, and the stranded traveler; and whatever good you do, indeed, The God knows it.

Our Reflection

“يَسْأَلُونَكَ مَاذَا يُنْفِقُونَ”

“They ask you what they should spend.”

Infaq, from the root N F Q, relates to giving out, spending, and putting resources to use. In many Quranic verses, infaq concerns wealth and possessions. Yet in answering this question, God uses a broader word:

“قُلْ مَا أَنْفَقْتُمْ مِنْ خَيْرٍ”

“Say: Whatever good you spend.”

“Khayr” refers to what is good, beneficial, and valuable. Alongside money and possessions, people can also use their time, knowledge, experience, skills, and abilities to help others.

For example, a doctor can treat a patient in need free of charge; a teacher can teach a disadvantaged child; a builder can repair an impoverished family’s home; and a farmer can teach others how to cultivate crops and produce food. All these activities require resources and abilities that a person possesses and can put at the service of others.

Sometimes helping a family begins with bringing food, but if we can teach its members a skill through which they can earn a living, we may provide more lasting help. As the familiar proverb says, rather than always giving someone fish, it is better to teach them how to fish.

Someone who is hungry today, however, needs food, and their immediate need cannot be overlooked in the hope of future self-sufficiency. Sound assistance recognizes a person’s needs today while also, wherever possible, helping them gain ability and independence.

God then names those who should benefit from this good:

“فَلِلْوَالِدَيْنِ وَالْأَقْرَبِينَ وَالْيَتَامَىٰ وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ”

“Then let it be for parents, close relatives, orphans, those in need, and the stranded traveler.”

The verse begins with parents and close relatives, then names orphans, the needy, and stranded travelers. Their needs are not identical, and assistance should not always take the same form.

Elderly parents may need their children’s companionship and care more than money. An orphaned child may need guardianship, education, and support. For an unemployed person, learning a trade or gaining access to work can be valuable assistance. A stranded traveler may need transport, temporary shelter, or help reaching their destination above all else.

Therefore, the value of assistance lies not only in the amount of money given, but also in the benefit it brings to the recipient’s life.

In Al-Baqarah 2:177, these same groups are named among the recipients of wealth, and giving to them is connected with righteousness and mindfulness.

At the end of the verse, God says:

“وَمَا تَفْعَلُوا مِنْ خَيْرٍ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ بِهِ عَلِيمٌ”

“And whatever good you do, indeed, The God knows it.”

The verse begins with spending, but its conclusion broadens the discussion to every good deed. Even if we understand infaq in its financial sense, doing good is not limited to paying money.

Someone who spends an hour caring for an infirm patient, teaches a child to read and write, helps a person in need find work, or uses a skill to resolve a difficulty in another person’s life has done good, even without placing money in anyone’s hand.

The God knows every good deed, whether it is giving wealth, teaching a skill, treating a patient, building shelter, or spending time and effort helping a person in need. The value of these good deeds is not measured by money alone.

Cross-references
  • Al-Baqarah 2:177: Giving wealth to close relatives, orphans, and the needy as an expression of righteousness.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:180: Responsibility toward parents and close relatives through a bequest.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:219: Another question about spending and the answer al-‘afw.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:220: Improving orphans’ circumstances and attending to their welfare.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:262: Avoiding reminders of generosity and hurtful conduct after giving.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:272: The benefit of spending good returns to the giver.
  • An-Nisa 4:36: Doing good to parents, close relatives, orphans, and the needy.
  • Al-Isra 17:26: Respecting the rights of close relatives, the needy, and the stranded traveler.
  • Al-Insan 76:8: Giving food to the needy, the orphan, and the captive despite loving that food.
