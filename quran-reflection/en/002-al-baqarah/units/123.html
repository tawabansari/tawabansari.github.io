Al-Baqarah 2:123

And be mindful of a day when no soul will discharge anything on behalf of another; no compensation will be accepted from it, no intercession will benefit it, and they will not be helped.

Our Reflection

The preceding verse reminded the Children of Israel of the favor and distinction they had received. Now the same address takes them from their proud past to the Day of Reckoning. The gift was great, and the Book and prophets had been among them, but none of this was a covenant guaranteeing release from accountability.

“No soul will discharge anything on behalf of another.”

In this world we rely on others, and life cannot go on without mutual help. A father stands by his child, a friend answers a friend’s cry, and family members ease one another’s burdens. Yet there is an account that cannot be placed on someone else’s shoulders. Luqman 31:33 expresses this through the closest human bond: a father cannot take his child’s place, nor can a child take the father’s.

The same warning appeared earlier in Al-Baqarah 2:48. There, too, the reminder of the favor and distinction granted to the Children of Israel was followed by the closing of every route to false assurance. God’s favor brings responsibility; it does not bring a guarantee of salvation.

“No compensation will be accepted from it.”

In this world many things can be replaced. Wealth is lost and other wealth takes its place; a loss can be made good with a payment. But on that Day, responsibility cannot be bought off. There is nothing a person can offer and say: take this, and overlook what You had required of me.

The verse then says:

“No intercession will benefit it.”

This hope may be even more appealing than wealth or compensation: if I fall short, someone who has standing with God will save me. The verse does not allow such a hope to become insurance for salvation. No one can treat intercession as their own possession and, on that basis, take the responsibilities of their life lightly.

At the same time, elsewhere the Qur’an speaks of intercession that depends on God’s permission and approval—for example, in Al-Baqarah 2:255, Ta-Ha 20:109, Al-Anbiya 21:28, and Saba 34:23. So if there is intercession, it is not ours to guarantee for ourselves or our community. The authority remains God’s.

The detailed discussion of intercession, the verses that deny it and those that speak of permission, the meanings of covenant and approval, and the relationship between intercession and human responsibility is presented in the book Intercession in the Qur’an.

And finally:

“Nor will they be helped.”

The issue is no longer only compensation or an intermediary. No power can pull someone out of the consequences of their choice in defiance of God’s judgment. At this point, what remains is the reality of one’s actions and the responsibility each person has taken upon themselves.

These words come immediately after the reminder of the distinction granted to the Children of Israel. The connection is clear: possessing the Book, a history of prophets, a respected lineage, or a special standing with God does not by itself save anyone if it does not lead to faithfulness to truth. A gift cannot be turned into a privilege that removes a person’s accountability.

This illusion is still with us; only its form has changed. One person may draw assurance from the name of their religious denomination, another from their family, another from a community that considers itself destined for salvation, and another from devotion to a holy person. Sometimes we lean so heavily on these affiliations that they gradually take the place of our own responsibility. The verse wakes us from this dream: no one answers in our place, no compensation buys off our responsibility, and no intercession can be counted in advance as our unquestionable right.

Love for the righteous has its value, and hope in God’s mercy has its place; neither gives us leave to neglect our own lives. Each person must examine what they have done with the truth that has reached them: what they have accepted, what they have covered over, and what they have sent ahead.

Perhaps guarding ourselves against that Day begins today: instead of looking for someone to carry our burden, we must carry it rightly ourselves.
