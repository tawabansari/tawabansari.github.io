Al-Baqarah 2:265

ۗ

And the example of those who spend their wealth seeking the pleasure of The God and strengthening themselves from within is like a garden on high ground struck by heavy rain, yielding twice its produce; and if heavy rain does not reach it, then light rain; and The God sees what you do.

Our Reflection

“وَمَثَلُ الَّذِينَ يُنْفِقُونَ أَمْوَالَهُمُ ابْتِغَاءَ مَرْضَاتِ اللَّهِ وَتَثْبِيتًا مِنْ أَنْفُسِهِمْ”

“And the example of those who spend their wealth seeking the pleasure of The God and strengthening themselves from within.”

“Ibtighāʾ,” from the root B GH Y, refers here to seeking and consciously pursuing a goal. “مَرْضَاتِ اللَّهِ” is God’s pleasure. Thus, the giver does not part with wealth to gain people’s approval or fame, but seeks God’s pleasure.

Yet alongside this aim, the Quran includes another important expression:

“وَتَثْبِيتًا مِنْ أَنْفُسِهِمْ”

“And strengthening themselves from within.”

“Tathbīt,” from the root TH B T, means making firm and establishing. “مِنْ أَنْفُسِهِمْ” connects this firmness with the people themselves and their inner selves.

Here something beyond giving wealth is involved: by giving for good, a person not only helps others but also strengthens their own will, resolve, and character.

Wealth and the resources of life can become sources of attachment, anxiety, and fear of loss. A person may believe in goodness, yet when the time to give arrives, hesitate between keeping their possessions and helping another.

When a person consciously decides to part with wealth for others’ good, they have done more than perform a financial act: they have overcome their own hesitation and attachment and turned belief into conduct. Each time this choice is repeated consciously and sincerely, it can strengthen their will and capacity for subsequent good decisions.

Giving is therefore not merely a means of improving the recipient’s living conditions; the giver can also grow through this act, move away from excessive dependence on possessions, and attain greater independence and inner firmness.

This connection between action and firmness also appears in An-Nisa 4:66, where doing what one is advised to do is associated with goodness and “أَشَدَّ تَثْبِيتًا.” Firmness is therefore not merely the result of knowing or believing; acting upon what one recognizes as right also helps cultivate it.

The Quran then presents another parable to illustrate the condition of such givers:

“كَمَثَلِ جَنَّةٍ بِرَبْوَةٍ أَصَابَهَا وَابِلٌ فَآتَتْ أُكُلَهَا ضِعْفَيْنِ”

“Like a garden on high ground struck by heavy rain, yielding twice its produce.”

“جَنَّة” in this context is a lush garden, while “رَبْوَة,” from the root R B W, refers to elevated, raised ground.

This image stands opposite the smooth rock of the preceding verse. In Al-Baqarah 2:264, heavy rain washes away the soil on the rock’s surface and leaves it hard and bare. Here, however, the same rain reaches a fruitful garden and doubles its produce.

The character of the ground is therefore essential: the same rain that removes the soil from a rock’s surface increases the yield of a garden with suitable conditions.

The smooth rock in the preceding verse portrays giving emptied of its true value through ostentation, reproach, and injury. The garden in this verse, however, depicts giving that springs from seeking God’s pleasure and a person’s inner firmness.

The Quran then says:

“فَإِنْ لَمْ يُصِبْهَا وَابِلٌ فَطَلٌّ”

“And if heavy rain does not reach it, then light rain.”

“Wābil” is heavy, abundant rain, while “طَلّ” refers to light, fine rain.

In this part, alongside the character of the ground, the amount and intensity of the rain also receive attention. A garden with suitable conditions for growth yields twice its produce with heavy rain, and even if heavy rain does not reach it, light rain is also beneficial to its fruitfulness.

Thus, the two successive verses form a meaningful picture: in one, heavy rain washes away the rock’s surface soil and exposes its barrenness; in the other, the same rain increases the garden’s yield, and even light rain does not prevent it from bearing fruit.

Reflecting on this parable, we can see that giving founded on sound intention and inner firmness has the capacity for growth and fruitfulness, whereas giving mixed with ostentation and injury, like soil on the surface of rock, lacks such a firm foundation.

The verse finally says:

“وَاللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ”

“And The God sees what you do.”

“Baṣīr,” from the root B Ṣ R, denotes seeing and sight; as an attribute of God, it expresses His all-encompassing sight.

God knows human action and its reality. Two people may give the same amount of wealth, yet their intentions, resolve, and orientation may differ.

Giving in God’s way is therefore not limited to the growth of its outward fruits; this act can also strengthen the person from within. Just as a garden with suitable ground benefits from rain and bears fruit, a person who cultivates their intention and conduct toward goodness can grow in will, character, and the ability to do good.
