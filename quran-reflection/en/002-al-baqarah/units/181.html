Al-Baqarah 2:181

ۚ

So whoever alters it after hearing it, its sin rests only with those who alter it. Indeed, The God is All-Hearing and All-Knowing.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, The God prescribed a bequest for parents and close relatives. He now makes clear the responsibility of those who alter a bequest after hearing it. Anyone who has learned its contents has no right to tamper with it at will, conceal a share, or change the testator’s wishes for their own benefit or someone else’s.

The important point of the verse is that after a person dies, the record of their deeds is closed, and responsibility for changes others subsequently make to their bequest rests with those people themselves, not with the deceased. No one bears another person’s burden of sin—the same principle stated in Al-Anam 6:164. If others distort the bequest and someone’s right is lost, the sin of this alteration belongs to those involved in it.

The ending of the verse with the two attributes “سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ” is also significant. The God is All-Hearing and All-Knowing: He hears what is said and knows the truth of people’s deeds. The testator may no longer be alive to defend their wishes, and those left behind may remain unaware of the alteration, but no word or deed is hidden from The God’s knowledge.

Cross-references
  • Al-Baqarah 2:180 — The command to make a proper bequest for parents and close relatives.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:182 — Distinguishing correction of an unjust bequest from its sinful alteration.
  • Al-Anam 6:164 — Individual responsibility: no one bears another person’s burden of sin.
  • Al-Isra 17:15 — Each person is responsible for their own deeds; no one bears another’s burden.
  • An-Nisa 4:11 — Honoring a bequest and debt before distributing inheritance.
  • An-Nisa 4:12 — Honoring a bequest and debt, together with the prohibition of causing harm.
  • Al-Maidah 5:106 — Testimony at the time of a bequest and the need for trustworthiness in recording and conveying it.
