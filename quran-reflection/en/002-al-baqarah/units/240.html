Al-Baqarah 2:240

ۚ ۗ

And those among you who die and leave wives behind: a bequest for their wives, provision for up to a year without being expelled. Then if they leave, there is no blame upon you for what they honorably do concerning themselves; and The God is Invincible, Wise.

Our Reflection

A husband’s death must not mark the beginning of homelessness and deprivation for his wife. A woman who has lost her life partner may face worries about shelter, living expenses, and her future alongside the grief of loss. The home in which she has lived for years may be her only refuge, yet the heirs may wish to sell it in the hope of obtaining their inheritance shares sooner.

In this verse, God prescribes a special bequest to support such a woman, both recognizing her right to provision and preventing her expulsion.

“وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنْكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا وَصِيَّةً لِأَزْوَاجِهِمْ مَتَاعًا إِلَى الْحَوْلِ غَيْرَ إِخْرَاجٍ”

“And those among you who die and leave wives behind: a bequest for their wives, provision for up to a year without being expelled.”

“وَصِيَّةً,” from the root و ص ي, refers to an instruction and the entrusting of a responsibility whose implementation is assigned to others. “مَتَاعًا” conveys benefit and provision, while “إِلَى الْحَوْلِ” specifies its duration as up to one year. The expression “غَيْرَ إِخْرَاجٍ” joins this provision with a prohibition on expelling the woman from the home.

The verse therefore concerns more than dividing property after death; it provides time and resources for a woman who has lost her husband to continue her life.

In Al-Baqarah 2:234, God prescribed four months and ten days of waiting for women whose husbands have died. That ruling concerns the waiting period before entering a new marriage, whereas the present verse concerns provision and support for the woman for up to a year. Four months and ten days is the waiting period for remarriage; one year is the period of support contemplated for the woman in this bequest. These two rulings address two different needs.

The stated bequest is also distinct from the woman’s inheritance share. In An-Nisa 4:12, a wife’s share of her husband’s estate is specified as one quarter if he has no child and one eighth if he has a child. In the same verse, however, the Quran places the distribution of inheritance after a bequest or debt. A bequest and inheritance are therefore two separate rights, and the existence of one does not eliminate the other.

This distinction matters greatly in real life.

Imagine a man who has died and left a house. His wife lived in that home for years and is now alone, perhaps elderly or without an independent income. The children or other heirs decide to sell the house to receive their shares sooner. They may be thinking of their financial rights, but have they considered where this woman will go after the sale? Must she lose her shelter as well as her husband after years of married life?

“غَيْرَ إِخْرَاجٍ” matters in preventing precisely this kind of deprivation. The heirs have the right to know and claim their lawful shares, but their inheritance rights must not become a means of violating the woman’s right to residence and support. While her prescribed right to provision remains in force, claiming the inheritance must not cause her forced expulsion from the home.

This support is a benefit separate from the woman’s inheritance share. Her inheritance share is her right in the property left by her husband, whereas the bequest in this verse provides special support for the period after losing her spouse. A woman who inherits from her husband is not, for that reason, deprived of the provision prescribed in the bequest.

This meaning is also connected with the command to make a bequest in Al-Baqarah 2:180, where the Quran opens a way to address relatives’ differing needs through a bequest. A bequest can account for people’s actual circumstances, while inheritance determines their prescribed shares.

“فَإِنْ خَرَجْنَ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِي مَا فَعَلْنَ فِي أَنْفُسِهِنَّ مِنْ مَعْرُوفٍ”

“Then if they leave, there is no blame upon you for what they honorably do concerning themselves.”

In this part, the woman’s own agency becomes apparent.

In the first expression, the Quran spoke of “إِخْرَاج,” meaning the woman being expelled by others. Now, however, it says “خَرَجْنَ”: if the women themselves leave. The distinction matters: forcibly expelling the woman is prohibited, but remaining in that house is not imposed upon her either.

A woman may wish to live with her children or family after her husband’s death. Another may prefer to live in an independent home. A woman may also decide, after the prescribed waiting period ends, to remarry and form a new life.

The Quran recognizes the woman’s own authority to make such decisions within the framework of honorable conduct. “فِي أَنْفُسِهِنَّ” likewise emphasizes women’s decisions and actions concerning their own lives.

The choice to stay or leave therefore belongs to the woman herself, not her husband’s heirs. They cannot deprive her of freedom over her life under the pretext of supporting her, just as they cannot expel her from the home under the pretext of their inheritance shares.

Conversely, the woman’s voluntary departure does not, by itself, authorize invalidating the bequest altogether and depriving her of all the support rights it prescribes. Her right must be preserved in light of the bequest’s provisions and honorable conduct. Her freedom to choose where to live must not become a means of removing her financial rights.

This accords with Al-Baqarah 2:234, where the Quran recognizes the woman’s proper decisions about her life after the waiting period ends. An-Nisa 4:19 likewise prohibits treating women in a way that places them under others’ control against their will.

After her husband’s death, the woman does not become property or subject to his heirs’ authority. She is an independent person with the right to housing, support, and decisions about her future.

“وَاللَّهُ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ”

“And The God is Invincible, Wise.”

The verse ends with the two descriptions “Aziz” and “Hakim.” “Aziz” speaks of God’s power and invincibility, and “Hakim” of the wisdom and soundness of His command.

In this ruling, divine wisdom is manifest in supporting someone who may become vulnerable to others’ demands and interests after her husband’s death. Alongside determining financial rights, God has also opened the way to prevent homelessness, respect dignity, and preserve the woman’s agency.

The verse’s message is clear: a husband’s death must not deprive the woman of her home, support, and authority over her life. The one-year bequest is a right to support distinct from her inheritance share. The heirs cannot make her homeless to gain access to property sooner, and the woman herself is free to stay or leave. Justice is more than distributing wealth among heirs; respecting the rights and dignity of those who remain after the property owner’s death is also part of it.
