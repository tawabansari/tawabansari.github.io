Al-Baqarah 2:190

ۚ

And fight in the way of The God those who fight you, and do not transgress; indeed, The God does not love those who transgress.

Our Reflection

In this verse, The God accompanies the command to fight with two clear conditions: first, fighting must be “فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ”—in the way of The God. Second, it must be directed against those who fight you, and even against them you must not transgress.

In Al-Baqarah 2:178, The God also prescribed qisas concerning killing. In our reading, qisas, from the root ق ص ص, is connected with following up, and in cases of killing entails investigation, identifying responsibility, fair adjudication, and due legal process; it should not be reduced to the common understanding of “taking retaliation” and seeking revenge. The principle of pursuing justice is not limited to one kind of killing. No killing should be left without investigation, follow-up, and justice. After setting out qisas, The God also opened the way to pardon.

But what is the way of The God? Is it the way of dominating other lands, seizing people’s property, imposing beliefs, forcing people to accept religion, or expanding the power of a nation or government? No! The way of The God must be recognized through His own guidance, not through the desires of rulers, leaders, or groups that portray their wars as sacred.

From its opening, in Al-Fatihah, the Quran calls people to ask for guidance to “الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ” and then describes this path:

“صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ”

“The path of those You have blessed, not those who have incurred wrath, nor those who have gone astray.” (Al-Fatihah 1:7)

To recognize The God’s way, then, we must seek its examples in the Quran.

In Maryam, The God names Zakariya (Maryam 19:2), Yahya (Maryam 19:7), Maryam (Maryam 19:16), Isa (Maryam 19:34), Ibrahim (Maryam 19:41), Ishaq and Yaqub (Maryam 19:49), Musa (Maryam 19:51), Harun (Maryam 19:53), Ismail (Maryam 19:54), and Idris (Maryam 19:56). Then, in Maryam 19:58, He identifies them among the prophets and chosen ones whom He blessed, guided, and selected:

“أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ”

“Those are the ones whom The God has blessed.”

The model of the straight path is the way of these guided ones, not the way of conquerors, oppressors, or those who attach The God’s name to wars fought for power and possession.

The Quran also presents many examples of going astray. In Al-Maidah 5:77, it speaks of those who went to excess in religion and departed from the right way by following the desires of misguided people. In Al-Kahf 18:104, it describes people whose efforts have gone astray while they think they are doing good. In An-Nisa 4:116, associating partners with The God is called “ضَلَالًا بَعِيدًا”. In Al-Anam 6:116, following conjecture and a majority that judges by supposition is described as leading people astray from The God’s way. In Al-Araf 7:179, those who have hearts but do not understand, eyes but do not see, and ears but do not hear are called “أَضَلُّ”—further astray.

This is a very serious warning. A person may be utterly convinced that they are following The God’s way while actually departing from it. Even religious cries, claims of jihad, and belief in the sacredness of a goal do not, by themselves, establish that their conduct is right.

The Way of The God in the Context of Fighting

Considering the Quran’s verses together, fighting in The God’s way can be understood in four principal contexts:

1. Safeguarding the delivery of the message and freedom of thought and belief: the divine message must be able to reach people so that they can freely hear it, reflect on it, and decide for themselves. Someone who imposes their religion or religious law on others by force obstructs freedom of communication and choice. Such conduct is not in the way of The God; it approaches the way of taghut. War is not a means of compelling faith, but a response to aggression that destroys people’s freedom and security. (Al-Baqarah 2:256; Yunus 10:99; An-Nahl 16:125; Al-Hajj 22:39; Al-Hajj 22:40; An-Nisa 4:76)

The distinction between freedom to convey the message, freedom to accept or reject religion, and the prohibition of imposing belief is discussed in detail in “There Is No Compulsion in Religion: The Messenger’s Duty Is to Convey”.

2. Defending life, family, and property: when people are attacked and subjected to aggression, they have the right to defend their lives, families, and property. This defense must not turn into seizure or violations of others’ rights. (Al-Baqarah 2:190; Al-Hajj 22:39; Al-Hajj 22:40; Ash-Shura 42:41; Ash-Shura 42:42)

3. Defending those unable to defend themselves: the Quran explicitly speaks of men, women, and children trapped under oppression who ask for help to be freed. Defending these oppressed people is also an instance of fighting in The God’s way. (An-Nisa 4:75)

4. Responding to treaty-breaking and aggression: political, commercial, and wartime agreements must be respected. Breaking a pledge, especially when accompanied by aggression, can provide grounds for defense and resistance; but not every contractual dispute, by itself, authorizes war. The Quran requires honoring agreements with those who remain faithful to them and sets out resistance against those who break their agreements and initiate aggression. (At-Tawbah 9:4; At-Tawbah 9:12; At-Tawbah 9:13; Al-Anfal 8:58)

In all these situations, war is not the first resort. Before fighting, peaceful avenues—including dialogue, conveying the message with wisdom, debating in the best manner, social and political engagement, negotiation, and diplomacy—must be pursued to resolve disagreement and halt aggression. Fighting arises when aggression continues, people’s lives and rights are endangered, or treaty-breaking accompanied by aggression renders peaceful avenues fruitless. Even during war, if the other side inclines toward peace, the Quran commands that you incline toward peace. (An-Nahl 16:125; Al-Ankabut 29:46; Al-Anfal 8:61; An-Nisa 4:90)

Fighting Combatants, Not Punishing a People

The verse’s second condition is equally clear:

“الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ”

“Those who fight you.”

The God has not said to fight all followers of a religion, all members of an ethnic group, or all inhabitants of a land. Hostility toward a group of combatants does not justify accusing everyone who shares their religion, ethnicity, or homeland.

As we said in discussing qisas, addressing a killing must rest on pursuing the crime, identifying those responsible, and justice. In Al-Anam 6:164, The God also says: “وَلَا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ”—no bearer of a burden bears another’s burden.

Thus, one cannot hold all inhabitants of an area guilty for an army’s crime, raid people’s homes, kill children, take women and elderly people captive, and then call these acts revenge, qisas, or fighting in The God’s way. Someone who had no part in a crime must not be punished in place of its perpetrator.

“وَلَا تَعْتَدُوا”: A Boundary Even the Oppressed Have No Right to Cross

The end of the verse states this boundary explicitly:

“وَلَا تَعْتَدُوا إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ الْمُعْتَدِينَ”

“And do not transgress; indeed, The God does not love those who transgress.”

“تَعْتَدُوا” comes from the root ع د و, connected with transgressing, exceeding limits, and crossing a boundary. In this verse, “اعتداء” means exceeding permitted limits and violating others’ rights. Even in fighting, a person is therefore not permitted to cross the bounds of right and justice.

In Al-Maidah 5:2, the Quran states this same principle regarding those who had barred the Muslims from the Sacred Mosque:

“وَلَا يَجْرِمَنَّكُمْ شَنَآنُ قَوْمٍ أَنْ صَدُّوكُمْ عَنِ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ أَنْ تَعْتَدُوا”

“Do not let hatred of a people who barred you from the Sacred Mosque drive you to transgress.”

This verse is very clear. Even if a group previously oppressed you and prevented your entry into the Sacred Mosque, that injustice does not authorize you to violate the bounds of right and justice. Hostility and a history of oppression must not drive a person to transgression.

This warning is especially important for those who have been oppressed for years.

A people may have lived for many years under oppression, occupation, discrimination, and violence. Then, when they gain the ability to resist and achieve some victory, they may turn to transgression instead of establishing justice. They may attack people’s homes, kill innocent people, make women, children, and the elderly victims of revenge, record videos of their actions, and cry out the takbir at the same time.

Does shouting “Allahu akbar” turn the killing of innocent people into fighting in the way of The God? Does someone who was oppressed yesterday have the right to become an oppressor today?

No! Past oppression does not authorize present transgression. Victory over an oppressor does not give a person permission to inflict the same injustice on people who had no part in that crime.

Such people may achieve a short-lived military victory, yet through their conduct create the conditions for further violence, revenge, and destruction in this world, while also bearing responsibility before The God for the blood they have wrongfully shed. Someone may consider themselves victorious in this world while in truth having departed from the path of guidance.

The God has not said that your enemies’ transgression is wrong but yours is acceptable because you have been oppressed. His command is universal: “وَلَا تَعْتَدُوا”. His warning is also clear: “إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ الْمُعْتَدِينَ”.

Fighting in the way of The God is therefore recognized by its purpose, conditions, and limits, not its name or slogans. The God’s way is the way of guidance, justice, and taqwa. Someone fighting to defend what is right has no right to violate innocent people’s rights and thereby trample the very right they claim to defend.

No experience of oppression gives a person the right to oppress, and no victory makes transgression lawful. Someone who stands against oppression must take even greater care not to become an oppressor, for The God does not love those who transgress.
