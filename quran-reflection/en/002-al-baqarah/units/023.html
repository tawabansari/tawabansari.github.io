Al-Baqarah 2:23

And if you are in rayb about what We have sent down upon Our servant, then bring a surah from its like, and call your witnesses apart from the One God, if you are truthful.

Our Reflection

This verse returns to one of the most important links at the opening of Al-Baqarah. In verse 2, it was said: “This is the Book; there is no rayb in it.” In verse 23, the Qur’an speaks to those who have fallen into rayb before what has been sent down. There is no contradiction between the two verses. The rayb is not in the Book, but the human being can fall into rayb before the Book. Verse 23 shows the way to test that rayb: if your doubt is truthful and not only a cover for denial, then bring an equivalent text.

The word rayb here is not merely scientific doubt or honest questioning. Rayb is a state of wavering, suspicion, and inner disturbance that takes away the calm of recognition. The Qur’an does not reject questioning and reflection. It challenges a kind of rayb that stands before a clear sign but refuses to offer a clear standard for its claim. For this reason, the verse confronts doubt with an objective test.

In the previous verse, the speech was about “sending down water from the sky” for the life of the earth. In this verse, the same root N Z L appears at the level of revelation: “what We sent down.” This placement is very precise. In nature, water descends from a higher level to the earth and makes life possible. In revelation, meaning descends from the higher level to the level of human language and perception and makes guidance possible. This is not a superficial similarity. It shows that the Qur’an speaks, in both creation and instruction, through a shared pattern: mercy and meaning descend to a level at which the human being can receive them.

The expression “upon Our servant” is also very important. The Qur’an does not say that what was sent down came upon a king, magician, priest, or superhuman being. It says it came upon “Our servant.” This both raises the Prophet’s rank in relation to servanthood before God and denies any independent divine or human source within him. In this verse, the Prophet is not the source of revelation; he is the receiver of revelation. This connects the verse to the tawhid of the previous verses: just as provision does not come from an independent human source, revelation does not come from an independent human source.

The challenge is compressed in the phrase: “bring a surah from its like.” In a first reading, the pronoun can return to “what We sent down”: if you doubt that this text is revelation, then bring an equivalent surah. But because of the closeness of the wording, another layer can also point to “Our servant”: if you think this text is the product of the ordinary ability of a human being, then bring from a human being like him an equivalent text. In both readings, the challenge is the same: either bring an equivalent textual structure, or show that this level of meaning, order, guidance, and effect can be produced from a non-divine source.

The word surah is not merely a simple book division. From the root S W R, it is connected with wall, enclosure, tower, and a structure that marks off and gives identity. In the Qur’an, a surah is a meaningful and structural unit: a whole with boundary, identity, order, and inner coherence. Therefore the challenge is not to imitate a few sentences or produce elegant phrases. The demand is to bring an equivalent revelatory unit that can hold a comparable place in meaning, structure, guidance, coherence, and effect.

After that, the verse says: “and call your witnesses apart from the One God.” In the previous verse, the speech was about andad: constructed rivals and supports set up against God. Here the word shuhada appears. A witness is one who is present, judges, and testifies to the truth or falsehood of a claim. The Qur’an says: if you think this text is human, do not come alone. Call all your witnesses, judges, supporters, and people of discernment. But this call must be “apart from the One God”: enter the field with all your non-divine tools and show that your claim can actually be fulfilled.

The ending, “if you are truthful,” clarifies the measure of truthfulness. Truthfulness here is not merely a verbal claim. If they say this Book is not from God, the truth of that claim must appear in the ability to bring an equivalent example. In this way, verse 23 is the natural continuation of “there is no rayb in it” in verse 2. The Qur’an first declares the firmness of the Book, then places before the human being a way to test the claim.

Cross-References
  • Al-Baqarah 2:2— “This is the Book; there is no rayb in it.” Verse 2 declares the absence of rayb in the Book, while verse 23 shows how human rayb before the Book is to be tested.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:22— “He sent down water from the sky”; the connection between the descent of water in creation and the descent of revelation in guidance.
  • Yunus 10:38— “Then bring a surah like it”; the challenge of producing a surah like the Qur’an in response to the accusation of fabrication.
  • Hud 11:13— “Bring ten surahs like it”; the challenge expanded to ten surahs against the claim that the Qur’an is invented.
  • Al-Isra 17:88— Even if humanity and jinn gathered and supported one another, they could not bring the like of the Qur’an.
  • An-Nisa 4:82— If the Qur’an were from other than the One God, they would find much contradiction in it; connected to the absence of rayb and the inner coherence of the Book.
