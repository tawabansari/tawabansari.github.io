Al-Baqarah 2:234

ۖ ۗ

And those among you who die and leave wives behind—those women shall wait for four months and ten days. Then when their term has ended, there is no blame upon you for what they honorably do concerning themselves; and The God is aware of what you do.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:228, the Quran prescribed three waiting periods for divorced women and, in the same verse, spoke of not concealing what is in the womb and of the possibility of the woman and man returning with the intention of reconciliation. Now a different period is specified for a woman whose husband has died.

“وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنْكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنْفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا”

“And those among you who die and leave wives behind—those women shall wait for four months and ten days.”

“يَتَرَبَّصْنَ” is the same verb for waiting that appeared in Al-Baqarah 2:228, with the difference that there the waiting time was expressed as three periods, while here four months and ten days are prescribed.

Why does the Quran specify different periods for these two situations, and what wisdom might lie in this difference?

One understandable wisdom is to clarify pregnancy status before remarriage. A woman who has lost her husband may be pregnant without yet knowing it. If she marries another man before this becomes clear, disagreement may arise over the child’s parentage and which marital bond the child belongs to.

In an age without pregnancy tests and genetic methods, the passage of time mattered more in making pregnancy apparent. Clarity of parentage also affects the protection of the child’s rights, including family and inheritance rights.

Yet this wisdom also applies to divorce. The difference in waiting time therefore cannot be explained solely by the possibility of pregnancy.

Another important difference is that divorce allows the possibility of reconciliation and returning to the marriage, whereas the husband’s death ends that bond permanently.

In Al-Baqarah 2:228, the Quran connects the couple’s return with the intention of reconciliation:

“إِنْ أَرَادُوا إِصْلَاحًا”

“If they desire reconciliation.”

In At-Talaq 65:1, after stating the rulings on the waiting period, it also says:

“لَا تَدْرِي لَعَلَّ اللَّهَ يُحْدِثُ بَعْدَ ذَٰلِكَ أَمْرًا”

“You do not know; perhaps The God will bring about a new matter after that.”

Thus, alongside clarifying the condition of the womb, the waiting period in divorce offers an opportunity to reconsider separation and repair the relationship. This opportunity must not be endless or block the path to returning or forming a new life for an indefinite time.

In the case of death, however, there is no longer a possibility of returning to the same marriage. The woman necessarily faces a new situation in life, and if she wishes to marry again, she must enter a new covenant with another person.

A spouse’s death may also occur suddenly and without prior preparation. A woman who has lost her life partner may face legal, family, or economic difficulties alongside her grief and may turn hastily to a new marriage to escape these hardships.

In such circumstances, allowing time to consider a new bond may be wise, because marriage should not be merely a hurried reaction to bereavement, loneliness, or sudden pressures in life; it should rest on understanding, consent, and a considered decision.

This reading does not mean that a new marriage cannot help bring the woman peace or improve her circumstances. Nor does the Quran in this verse prescribe any specific period of remaining sorrowful, mourning ceremonies, wearing particular clothing, or withdrawing from society.

The subject of the verse is waiting before remarriage, not obliging the woman to mourn.

This becomes clearer in the next verse, Al-Baqarah 2:235. There the Quran permits an indirect expression of interest in proposing during the waiting period, but states:

“وَلَا تَعْزِمُوا عُقْدَةَ النِّكَاحِ حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ الْكِتَابُ أَجَلَهُ”

“And do not resolve upon the marriage bond until the prescribed term has reached its end.”

The four-month-and-ten-day waiting period is therefore directly connected with regulating the woman’s entry into a new marriage. Clarifying pregnancy status and providing an opportunity for a considered decision after the husband’s death may be among its wisdoms, although the precise reason for choosing four months and ten days is not stated in the verse itself.

“فَإِذَا بَلَغْنَ أَجَلَهُنَّ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِيمَا فَعَلْنَ فِي أَنْفُسِهِنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ”

“Then when their term has ended, there is no blame upon you for what they honorably do concerning themselves.”

After specifying the waiting period, the Quran recognizes the woman’s agency in deciding her future.

The expression “فِيمَا فَعَلْنَ فِي أَنْفُسِهِنَّ” refers to what women do concerning themselves, while “بِالْمَعْرُوفِ” states the criterion of proper conduct.

The address “فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ,” however, is directed to others: those who may consider themselves responsible for or entitled to control the woman’s decisions, including her deceased husband’s family and other members of society.

This command continues the principle in Al-Baqarah 2:232, where the Quran prohibited society from obstructing women’s marriages. It now states the same principle concerning a widow after the prescribed period has ended.

In some societies, a woman is expected not to remarry for many years after her husband’s death, or even for the rest of her life. If she decides to marry again, her husband’s family accuses her of disloyalty or says that she has forgotten the memory of her deceased husband.

Sometimes opposition to the woman’s marriage is connected with property, the care of children, or preserving the husband’s family’s influence, and is justified in the name of respect for the deceased.

Yet a widow is not her husband’s inheritance, such that control over her life passes to his family after his death. In An-Nisa 4:19, the Quran explicitly prohibits inheriting women against their will.

Affection and loyalty toward a deceased spouse do not mean being deprived of a new life. The woman can preserve the memories and dignity of her past life and, after the prescribed period ends, decide her future.

Families can support the woman during the difficult period of loss, but they have no right to turn her grief into a means of imposing solitude or preventing remarriage.

In Al-Baqarah 2:240, the Quran also speaks of the widow’s right to provision for up to a year without being expelled from the home. That ruling concerns support for the woman, whereas the present verse specifies the waiting period before a new marriage. The distinction between these two subjects is examined in the study “Abrogation in the Quran”.

“وَاللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ خَبِيرٌ”

“And The God is aware of what you do.”

“خَبِير,” from the root خ ب ر, denotes detailed awareness and knowledge of the reality of matters.

This warning is particularly directed toward those who interfere in a widow’s life. Relatives may outwardly speak of reputation, loyalty, or respect for the deceased, while in reality seeking to retain control over the woman’s life.

The God knows the reality of their intentions and actions.

The Quran prescribes four months and ten days of waiting for a widow. This period may be connected with clarifying the possibility of pregnancy before a new marriage and providing an opportunity for a considered decision after the loss of a spouse. But after it ends, the woman has the right to decide her future honorably. No family or society has the right to block her path to a new life in the name of mourning, reputation, or loyalty to her deceased husband.

Cross-references
  • Al-Baqarah 2:228: Three waiting periods after divorce, the condition of the womb, and returning with the intention of reconciliation.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:232: Prohibiting obstruction of women’s marriages after the waiting period.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:235: Marriage proposals and the restriction on contracting marriage until the prescribed term ends.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:240: The widow’s right to provision for up to a year without expulsion from the home.
  • An-Nisa 4:19: Prohibiting inheriting women against their will.
  • At-Talaq 65:1: Counting the waiting period and the possibility of new circumstances for reconciliation.
  • At-Talaq 65:4: The waiting period for pregnant women.
  • Al-Ahzab 33:49: No waiting period for divorce before marital contact.
