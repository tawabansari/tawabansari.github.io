Al-Baqarah 2:284

ۗ ۖ ۗ

To The God belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And whether you disclose what is in your hearts or conceal it, The God will call you to account for it; then He forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills; and The God is powerful over all things.

Our Reflection

This verse follows Al-Baqarah 2:283, where the Quran spoke of fulfilling trusts and not concealing testimony. Of someone who knowingly conceals testimony, it said, “فَإِنَّهُ آثِمٌ قَلْبُهُ”—his heart is sinful. Now the Quran reminds us that not only a person’s outward deeds, but also what they conceal within themselves, remains within God’s knowledge and accounting.

“لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ”

“To The God belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth.”

All existence belongs to God, and no power lies outside His sovereignty. A person may possess wealth, power, and influence in this world and evade accountability to others, but their ownership and power are limited. For a broader discussion, see the principle of the oneness of God in sovereignty.

The Quran then says:

“وَإِنْ تُبْدُوا مَا فِي أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَوْ تُخْفُوهُ يُحَاسِبْكُمْ بِهِ اللَّهُ”

“And whether you disclose what is in your hearts or conceal it, The God will call you to account for it.”

“تُبْدُوا,” from the root B D W, refers to disclosing, and “تُخْفُوهُ,” from KH F Y, to concealing. “يُحَاسِبْكُمْ,” from Ḥ S B, concerns calling to account.

For example, someone may cheat in a transaction, forge a document, conceal a truth, or violate another’s right through false testimony. They may act so skillfully that no one discovers the deception and they appear innocent before others. But a truth remaining hidden from human eyes does not mean that it is hidden from God.

This accounting is not confined to outward conduct; a person’s intentions and conscious decisions also matter. There is, however, a difference between passing, unwanted thoughts and what someone consciously cultivates in their heart. In Al-Baqarah 2:225, the Quran likewise distinguishes idle speech from what people’s hearts have earned. Aal Imran 3:29 also recalls God’s knowledge of what a person conceals or discloses.

It then says:

“فَيَغْفِرُ لِمَنْ يَشَاءُ وَيُعَذِّبُ مَنْ يَشَاءُ”

“Then He forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills.”

Divine accounting does not mean that every person will certainly be punished. God knows the reality of human deeds and intentions, and forgiveness and punishment are in His authority. In the Quran, the path of repentance and reform also remains open: in Al-Maidah 5:39, turning back after wrongdoing is connected with reforming one’s conduct. For further discussion, see the principle of repentance and reform.

The verse ends with “وَاللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ”, reminding us that God’s power encompasses all things.

The message of this verse is that a person may conceal the truth from others, but cannot escape the knowledge and accounting of The God. Honesty and avoiding injustice should therefore not depend solely on fear of exposure or worldly punishment; they should arise from faith, taqwa, and conscious human responsibility.

Cross-references
  • Al-Baqarah 2:225: The distinction between idle speech and what the heart consciously earns.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:281: Accounting for every soul’s deeds and the absence of injustice in divine judgment.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:282: Justice and honesty in recording financial obligations.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:283: The prohibition of concealing testimony and the sinful heart of the person who conceals it.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:286: Obligations according to capacity and responsibility for one’s own deeds.
  • Aal Imran 3:29: God’s knowledge of what is concealed or disclosed within the breasts.
  • An-Nisa 4:135: Just testimony, even against oneself and close relatives.
  • Al-Maidah 5:39: Repentance after wrongdoing and reforming one’s conduct.
  • Al-Ahzab 33:5: The distinction between unintended error and the heart’s deliberate intention.
