Al-Baqarah 2:203

ۚ ۚ ۗ

And remember The God during numbered days. Whoever hastens in two days incurs no sin, and whoever delays incurs no sin—for whoever practices mindfulness. And be mindful of The God, and know that you will be gathered to Him.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verses, The God spoke of performing the rites of Hajj, remembering Him, and asking for good in this world and the Hereafter. He now draws attention once more to remembering Him during the specified days of Hajj:

“وَاذْكُرُوا اللَّهَ فِي أَيَّامٍ مَعْدُودَاتٍ”

“And remember The God during numbered days.”

“مَعْدُودَاتٍ,” from the root ع د د, means counted; its feminine plural form in this expression also points to the small number of days. The bounds of these numbered days must be understood from the verse’s own context, which immediately speaks of hastening in two days and delaying. We see the same approach in Al-Baqarah 2:184, where “أَيَّامًا مَعْدُودَاتٍ” is connected through the continuing context to the month of Ramadan in Al-Baqarah 2:185. A detailed discussion appears in the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:184.

The clear command here is to remember The God during numbered days. In this statement itself, the Qur’an specifies neither a number of repetitions nor a particular mandatory formula for each day.

It then says:

“فَمَنْ تَعَجَّلَ فِي يَوْمَيْنِ فَلَا إِثْمَ عَلَيْهِ وَمَنْ تَأَخَّرَ فَلَا إِثْمَ عَلَيْهِ”

“Whoever hastens in two days incurs no sin, and whoever delays incurs no sin.”

This passage is a clear example of ease in the rules of Hajj.

The God describes two situations: someone who hastens in two days, and someone who delays. He then gives the same ruling for both:

“فَلَا إِثْمَ عَلَيْهِ”—“He incurs no sin.”

Thus, the Qur’an does not describe either choice as sinful in itself.

People’s circumstances differ. One person may hasten because of family responsibilities, travel difficulties, illness, accommodation costs, or a need to return sooner; another may be able to remain longer.

Here, The God allows room for both paths. Why, then, should a choice that the Qur’an itself declares free of sin be turned into sin and disobedience because of a custom or a juristic interpretation?

Nor should staying longer alone be taken as proof of greater mindfulness. Someone might remain at Hajj longer yet disregard others’ rights, while another returns earlier but has followed God’s command with greater sincerity and care.

The value of worship cannot be measured solely by the number of days a person remains in a place.

The God immediately says:

“لِمَنِ اتَّقَىٰ”

“For whoever practices mindfulness.”

This qualification matters. Ease concerning hastening or delaying does not remove the need to observe divine limits. Whether returning earlier or later, a person must preserve mindfulness.

Taqwa comes from the root و ق ي and concerns conscious care and self-protection. Here, it means observing people’s rights and God’s command in choosing the time of departure and in one’s conduct.

This concept is explained more fully in “What Is Taqwa in the Qur’an?”.

The verse continues:

“وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّكُمْ إِلَيْهِ تُحْشَرُونَ”

“And be mindful of The God, and know that you will be gathered to Him.”

“تُحْشَرُونَ,” from the root ح ش ر, means gathering and assembling. The God reminds people that eventually all will be gathered to Him.

At Hajj, people gather from different lands and communities, then disperse after completing the rites. Their final gathering, however, will be before The God. There, human responsibility is not confined to performing the outward forms of worship; conduct and mindfulness also matter.

This verse connects remembrance of The God, ease, and mindfulness. The God both commands remembrance of Him and declares hastening and delaying free of sin. People should therefore not add unfounded restrictions that make a form of worship difficult for themselves and others when God has allowed room within it.

Hajj is not valued by counting days or measuring the length of a stay. What matters is observing God’s command, people’s rights, and the mindfulness one must preserve during the journey and after returning. All will eventually be gathered to that same God.
