Al-Baqarah 2:248

ۚ

And their prophet said to them: Indeed, the sign of his kingship is that the chest will come to you; in it is tranquility from your Lord and a remnant of what the family of Moses and the family of Aaron left behind; the angels carry it. Indeed, in this there is a sign for you, if you are believers.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:247, we saw that the leaders of the Children of Israel objected to Talut’s selection because they saw superiority in wealth and social status. Their prophet explained the criteria for the divine choice in terms of Talut’s knowledge and physical ability. But for a community on the threshold of fighting, confidence in its leadership is vital, so The God, out of His bounty, sends them reassurance and peace of mind.

“إِنَّ آيَةَ مُلْكِهِ أَنْ يَأْتِيَكُمُ التَّابُوتُ فِيهِ سَكِينَةٌ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ”

“Indeed, the sign of his kingship is that the chest will come to you; in it is tranquility from your Lord.”

In the religious history of the Children of Israel, this chest is known as the Ark of the Covenant. It symbolizes their covenant with The God, recalling how He rescued them from Pharaoh’s bondage and fulfilled His covenant with them. The return of such a chest to a scattered and threatened community could recall the divine covenant, their liberation from bondage, and the hope of regaining independence and sovereignty.

“Sakīnah,” from the root s-k-n, is connected with tranquility and reassurance. In Al-Fath 48:4, God also speaks of sending tranquility into the hearts of believers. Here, the Quran states that the chest contained tranquility from their Lord. Its arrival could strengthen peace, solidarity, and confidence in the chosen leadership among the Children of Israel.

“وَبَقِيَّةٌ مِمَّا تَرَكَ آلُ مُوسَىٰ وَآلُ هَارُونَ”

“And a remnant of what the family of Moses and the family of Aaron left behind.”

This expression makes clear the chest’s connection with the legacy of Moses and Aaron. But the Quran does not specify exactly what those remnants were.

The written history of the Children of Israel, which existed before the Quran’s revelation, matters for understanding the background of this chest. Chapters 4 through 6 of the First Book of Samuel recount the Ark of the Covenant’s capture by an enemy and its return. Consulting this history does not conflict with a Quran-centered approach, since in Al-Maidah 5:48 the Quran describes itself as “مُصَدِّقًا لِمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ وَمُهَيْمِنًا عَلَيْهِ”: confirming the Scripture before it and serving as a guardian over it. Pre-Quranic history may therefore be studied, but ultimately its truthfulness must be assessed against what the Quran states.

“تَحْمِلُهُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ”

“The angels carry it.”

This part still raises a question for reflection. The Quran attributes the carrying of the chest to the angels, but does not describe how it took place.

One possibility is that the chest had passed beyond the reach of the Children of Israel and that the angels found and returned it, making its return a sign of the enduring covenant and divine support for that people. This remains only a possibility, however, because the verse identifies neither the chest’s previous location nor how it was carried.

It is worth mentioning that the Ark of the Covenant continues to hold special importance in Jewish religious tradition, and despite the passage of centuries, its whereabouts remain a subject of discussion and searching.

“إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لَكُمْ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ مُؤْمِنِينَ”

“Indeed, in this there is a sign for you, if you are believers.”

The Children of Israel had themselves sought leadership for fighting, but when God chose Talut contrary to their material criteria, they objected. Now the return of a chest connected with their covenant and religious history was a sign for accepting this choice and restoring confidence in the leadership.

The message of the verse is clear: for a society to stand firm, having a leader and resources is not enough; trust, tranquility, and solidarity are also necessary. Through a sign familiar to the Children of Israel, God confirmed Talut’s selection and provided the grounds for their reassurance.
