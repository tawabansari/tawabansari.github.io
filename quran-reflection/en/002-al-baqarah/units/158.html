Al-Baqarah 2:158

ۖ ۚ

Indeed, Safa and Marwah are among the signs of The God. So whoever performs hajj to the House or undertakes umrah incurs no sin in performing tawaf of the two. And whoever voluntarily does good, indeed, The God is appreciative and knowing.

Our Reflection

Safa and Marwah are two specific names that the Quran identifies as “sha‘a’ir Allah,” divine signs. This is the only verse that mentions them by name. The Quran gives no account of their exact location, natural features, or history, but makes their connection with pilgrimage to the House and umrah clear, recognizing their place among God’s sacred signs.

Divine signs are signs whose sanctity must be respected. In Al-Hajj 22:32, the Quran describes honoring God’s sacred signs as arising from mindfulness in the hearts; in Al-Maidah 5:2, it forbids violating their sanctity. Yet something being a sacred sign does not make every act connected with it obligatory. For example, in Al-Hajj 22:36, sacrificial animals are also counted among God’s sacred signs. A distinction must therefore be made between the sanctity of a sign and the rulings concerning it.

The word “يَطَّوَّفَ” comes from the root T W F, used in the Quran in contexts of moving around and coming and going. In An-Nur 24:58, another form of the same root describes ordinary movement among people, although its construction differs from the expression in this verse. In Al-Baqarah 2:158, “بِهِمَا” refers to both Safa and Marwah. The Quran speaks of tawaf in connection with these two, but this expression alone does not specify the exact form of movement. Neither a prescribed manner of going back and forth between them nor circular movement around each, with a fixed number of repetitions, can be derived from the expression itself.

Another important point is how the ruling is expressed. The Quran does not say that whoever performs hajj or umrah must perform tawaf of Safa and Marwah. Rather, it says: “فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِ أَنْ يَطَّوَّفَ بِهِمَا”; there is no sin upon that person in performing tawaf of the two. This expression denies that the act is sinful; it is not, by itself, a command making it obligatory. By contrast, Al-Hajj 22:29 uses an imperative for tawaf of the House. This difference in expression should not be overlooked.

The same manner of removing blame appears in Al-Baqarah 2:198, where the Quran declares seeking bounty and benefit during hajj to be without sin, without turning it into an obligatory duty. Of course, “la junah” alone does not establish that an act is never obligatory under any circumstances, because removing blame and determining obligation are separate questions. What is clear in Al-Baqarah 2:158 is the place of Safa and Marwah among divine signs and the absence of sin in tawaf connected with them. This verse alone, however, does not establish that this tawaf is obligatory, that it is a condition for the validity of hajj and umrah, or that seven passages and a particular manner of performance are required. Such obligations require independent Quranic evidence.

The verse ends by stating a broader principle: “وَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ شَاكِرٌ عَلِيمٌ”. The word “تَطَوَّعَ” comes from T W ‘ and does not share the root of “يَطَّوَّفَ”. The expression can encompass doing good willingly and adding to a prescribed amount. The closing sentence is connected with tawaf of Safa and Marwah, but is not limited to it: it includes every good deed a person willingly performs. God is appreciative and knowing: He values goodness and is aware of it.

Safa and Marwah, like God’s other sacred signs, have their own sanctity and place. Honoring these signs is part of respecting what God has honored. But respecting sacred signs is not the same as adding duties without clear Quranic evidence. A person may willingly perform additional good deeds, but turning an act into an obligatory divine command, or making it a condition for accepting others’ worship, requires evidence. This distinction both preserves the sanctity of the signs and avoids transferring the authority to prescribe religious law from The God to human beings.
