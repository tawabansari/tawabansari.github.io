Al-Baqarah 2:251

ۗ

So they defeated them by the permission of The God, and David killed Goliath; and The God gave him kingship and wisdom and taught him of what He willed. And were it not for The God repelling some people by means of others, the earth would fall into corruption; but The God is possessed of bounty toward all worlds.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verses, we saw that among the large number of the Children of Israel who sought leadership and struggle, only a small group remained faithful to their commitment to the end. They stood before Goliath’s army and asked their Lord for patience, firm footing, and victory. Now the Quran describes the outcome of that faith, resolve, and action.

«فَهَزَمُوهُمْ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ وَقَتَلَ دَاوُودُ جَالُوتَ»

“So they defeated them by the permission of The God, and David killed Goliath.”

That small group overcame Goliath’s army, and one of their fighters, David, killed Goliath. The significance of this minority’s steadfastness was such that David himself emerged from among these fighters: someone who later received kingship and whom the Quran includes among the Prophets (Al-Anam 6:84, Al-Anam 6:85, Al-Anam 6:86, Al-Anam 6:87, Al-Anam 6:88, Al-Anam 6:89).

Had that small group also withdrawn from the struggle like the others, this victory and the opportunity that arose for David would not have come about. We should therefore not forget their collective role because of the prominence of David’s name.

“بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ” reminds us that victory occurs within the laws, established patterns, and conditions that God has instituted. This concept is explained in the study Divine Permission in the Quran.

The Quran does not specify when or how David obtained kingship from Talut. However, the ancient Old Testament accounts in 1 Samuel chapter 31 and 2 Samuel chapters 2 and 5 relate that after the death of Saul, who is identified with Talut, David first became king over Judah and later over all the Children of Israel.

«وَآتَاهُ اللَّهُ الْمُلْكَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ وَعَلَّمَهُ مِمَّا يَشَاءُ»

“And The God gave him kingship and wisdom and taught him of what He willed.”

God did not give David only the power to rule; He also granted him wisdom and knowledge. In Sad 38:20, He says: “وَشَدَدْنَا مُلْكَهُ وَآتَيْنَاهُ الْحِكْمَةَ وَفَصْلَ الْخِطَابِ”: He strengthened his kingship and gave him wisdom and the ability to judge and speak decisively.

Knowledge enables us to understand phenomena and how to make use of them; wisdom, however, helps us know in what direction and for what purpose to use that knowledge. For example, knowledge can be used to build an atomic bomb, but wisdom calls for that same knowledge to serve life and the production of clean energy instead of destroying human beings.

This connection recalls the criterion for choosing Talut in Al-Baqarah 2:247: fitness for leadership does not rest on wealth and lineage alone; knowledge, ability, and wisdom matter in governing people’s affairs.

«وَلَوْلَا دَفْعُ اللَّهِ النَّاسَ بَعْضَهُمْ بِبَعْضٍ لَفَسَدَتِ الْأَرْضُ»

“And were it not for The God repelling some people by means of others, the earth would fall into corruption.”

Here the Quran goes beyond one group’s victory and describes one of the great divine patterns in human life: God prevents corruption from spreading and becoming pervasive through human beings themselves.

When power falls into the hands of oppressors and injustice, aggression, and the deprivation of people’s rights spread, the resistance of people who do good can restrain that power and prepare the way for its reform or replacement.

The same principle is stated in Al-Hajj 22:40. The Quran says that if God did not repel some people by means of others, monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques in which God’s name is often remembered would be destroyed. Restraining corruption is therefore not merely about preserving one people’s power or interests; protecting human rights, freedom of faith, and preventing oppression are also among its aims.

This divine pattern can be clearly observed in the history of nations and governments. Empires and kingdoms arise, grow, and reach the height of power; but when they practice oppression, prejudice, corruption, and aggression instead of justice, they create the conditions for resistance, decline, and replacement.

God has established this principle in human life, but its realization is not separate from human responsibility. If people surrender to corruption and wait for their circumstances to change without any effort or sacrifice, how can they expect the very restraint that God describes as occurring through people to take place?

Ar-Rad 13:11 also reminds us that God does not change a people’s condition until they change what is within themselves.

Firm determination, faith, trust in The God, and the action of people who do good are therefore among the factors through which this divine pattern is realized, just as victory over Goliath would not have occurred without the steadfastness and action of Talut’s small army.

«وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ ذُو فَضْلٍ عَلَى الْعَالَمِينَ»

“But The God is possessed of bounty toward all worlds.”

The end of the verse makes the wisdom of this principle clear. God speaks of His bounty toward all worlds, not only toward a people who have won a battle.

If no power stands against corruption and aggression, the rights, freedom, and security of other human beings will ultimately also be destroyed. It is part of divine bounty that people can prevent corruption from spreading by standing against oppression.

This verse teaches us that, while awaiting the victory of truth, we should not remain mere spectators of history. Just as Talut’s small group prepared the way for victory through faith, patience, and action, people who do good can also be the means through which divine restraint is realized and corruption is prevented from spreading throughout the earth.
