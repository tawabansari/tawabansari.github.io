Al-Baqarah 2:200

ۗ

Then, when you have completed your rites, remember The God as you remember your fathers, or with stronger remembrance. Among the people is one who says: Our Lord, grant us in this world; and in the Hereafter he has no share.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verses, The God spoke of the rites of Hajj, remembering Him, mindfulness, and seeking forgiveness. He now says:

“فَإِذَا قَضَيْتُمْ مَنَاسِكَكُمْ فَاذْكُرُوا اللَّهَ”

“Then, when you have completed your rites, remember The God.”

Hajj has actual rites that are performed and brought to completion; remembrance of The God, however, should not end when the rites end. Someone returning from Hajj should also put what they have learned of worship, mindfulness, and responsibility into practice in their life.

The place and purposes of these rites are examined in “Hajj in the Qur’an: Abraham’s Way, Rites, and People’s Rights”.

The God then says:

“كَذِكْرِكُمْ آبَاءَكُمْ أَوْ أَشَدَّ ذِكْرًا”

“As you remember your fathers, or with stronger remembrance.”

People feel attached to their fathers and ancestors. They remember them, keep their names alive, and sometimes take pride in their honors and achievements. Yet The God wants remembrance of Him to have a firmer place in human life.

“أَشَدَّ,” from the root ش د د, concerns intensity, strength, and firmness. The verse should therefore not be reduced to increasing the number of times one recites a remembrance, as though mentioning one’s father once required saying God’s name ten times. Remembrance of The God should be stronger in attention, attachment, sincerity, and its effect on life.

Respecting one’s fathers is commendable, but it should not become prejudice and blind adherence to their beliefs. In Al-Baqarah 2:170, the Qur’an criticizes precisely this unfounded following of the fathers’ path. In Al-Baqarah 2:134, it says, “لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتْ وَلَكُمْ مَا كَسَبْتُمْ”: theirs is what they earned, and yours is what you earn.

The greatness of our ancestors is therefore no proof that our beliefs are true. Each person must think about the truth and accept responsibility for their own actions.

Someone may take pride in their family, people, religious history, or leaders while neglecting God’s command concerning justice and the rights of others. Such a person should ask: in my life, is remembrance of The God stronger, or my attachment to the names and standing of fathers and leaders?

True remembrance of The God should not remain on the tongue alone; it should also affect a person’s choices and actions.

The verse continues:

“فَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَنْ يَقُولُ رَبَّنَا آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا”

“Among the people is one who says: Our Lord, grant us in this world.”

This person also calls upon The God, but their request is confined to this world.

People may ask The God for provision, health, children, well-being, and success. The Qur’an does not condemn earning a lawful livelihood: in Al-Baqarah 2:198, it declares that seeking the Lord’s bounty during Hajj incurs no blame.

The problem is not asking for worldly blessings; it is making this world one’s entire goal and forgetting the Hereafter.

For example, someone may go to Hajj wishing only for greater wealth, flourishing trade, or their children’s rise to position and power. But do they also ask The God for guidance, justice, forgiveness, and the ability to do good? Do they also work for their Hereafter?

At the end, The God says:

“وَمَا لَهُ فِي الْآخِرَةِ مِنْ خَلَاقٍ”

“And in the Hereafter he has no share.”

“خَلَاق” here means a share or portion. A person who confines all their desires and efforts to this world and sets aside the Hereafter remains deprived of a share in the Hereafter.

The next verse, Al-Baqarah 2:201, presents a prayer encompassing both this world and the Hereafter. The Qur’an thus does not call people to abandon this world; it teaches that this world must not take the place of the Hereafter.

Hajj ends with the completion of its rites, but remembrance of The God and the responsibility of worship do not end. People should not become so attached to ancestral honors that they forget the truth, or desire worldly blessings so much that the Hereafter disappears from their lives. The best fruit of Hajj is for remembrance of The God to become firmer in a person’s heart, thought, and conduct.
