Al-Baqarah 2:235

ۚ ۚ ۚ ۚ

And there is no sin upon you in what you express indirectly of a marriage proposal to women or conceal within yourselves. The God knows that you will mention them; but do not make secret arrangements with them; rather, you may speak appropriately. And do not resolve to conclude the marriage covenant until the prescribed period has ended. And know that The God knows what is within you, so beware of Him. And know that The God is Forgiving, Forbearing.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, God specified the waiting period for women after their husbands’ deaths and recognized their freedom to decide their own future once that period has ended. This verse addresses those who may wish to marry these women.

«وَلا جُناحَ عَلَيكُم فيما عَرَّضتُم بِهِ مِن خِطبَةِ النِّساءِ أَو أَكنَنتُم فى أَنفُسِكُم»

God considers neither concealing a wish to marry nor expressing it indirectly during the waiting period to be a sin.

A man may be interested in a woman whose husband has died or from whom she has separated. The woman, too, may wish to marry him, yet because of family ties, the man’s friendship with her former husband, fear of public judgment, or prevailing social traditions, neither may be able to express their interest. An opportunity to form a wholesome, healthy family may thus be lost, although God has not forbidden such a marriage.

A person can honor the memory of a deceased friend and, at the same time, marry that friend’s former wife by mutual consent after the prescribed period has ended. A woman’s marriage to another man likewise does not mean forgetting her deceased husband or betraying his memory.

Once the marriage covenant has ended and the prescribed period has been observed, a woman does not belong to her former husband or his family. As we discussed in the reflections on Al-Baqarah 2:232 and Al-Baqarah 2:234, society must not obstruct women’s remarriage. The Principle of Consent in Marriage must take precedence over other people’s expectations and attachments.

An example of the influence of social traditions on marriage appears in the account of Muhammad’s marriage to Zayd’s former wife in Al-Ahzab 33:37. Muhammad was concealing something within himself and feared people. We do not know whether there was mutual affection between Muhammad and that woman; however, God reproves fear of people and identifies removing constraints on believers marrying their adopted sons’ former wives as one purpose of this marriage. This marriage also had other legal and social dimensions concerning adoption, lineage, and inheritance, some of whose foundations we have examined in the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:220 and the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:180. We leave the full examination of this subject to the reflection on Al-Ahzab 33:37.

Society cannot forbid, in the name of morality, protective jealousy, loyalty, or religion, something that God has not forbidden. This issue is examined in detail in Halal and Haram in the Qur’an: Divine Legislative Authority.

«عَلِمَ اللَّهُ أَنَّكُم سَتَذكُرونَهُنَّ وَلٰكِن لا تُواعِدوهُنَّ سِرًّا إِلّا أَن تَقولوا قَولًا مَعروفًا»

God, who knows human nature, does not ask people to deny their natural feelings. Rather, He sets limits on the behavior arising from those feelings.

A woman who has recently lost her husband may face grief, loneliness, anxiety about her children’s future, and financial difficulties. The deceased man’s relatives, too, may still be mourning their loved one. It is inappropriate to add to their grief by raising the subject of marriage without due consideration, or to exploit the woman’s vulnerable circumstances to obtain a promise of marriage.

The Qur’an distinguishes between expressing an interest in marriage indirectly and making secret promises and arrangements. A person may express their interest respectfully, without pressuring the woman or forcing her into a premature promise or commitment.

The verse prohibits “تُواعِدوهُنَّ سِرًّا,” not every private conversation with women. The expression “إِلّا أَن تَقولوا قَولًا مَعروفًا” does not authorize secret promises and arrangements; rather, it opens the way for appropriate speech in contrast to the prohibited conduct.

As we have discussed in Ma‘ruf, Munkar, and Fahsha in the Qur’an, not everything customary among people is necessarily ma‘ruf. Ways of expressing interest in marriage may vary between cultures, but honesty, respect, regard for dignity, and avoidance of pressure and deception are constant standards by which to assess them.

The immediate context of the verse concerns widows. In the case of divorced women, the status of the previous covenant must also be considered. Al-Baqarah 2:228 states the possibility of returning to the marriage with the intention of reconciliation during the waiting period. Permission to express an interest in marrying a widow therefore cannot be treated as permission to interfere in a relationship that may still be restored.

«وَلا جُناحَ عَلَيكُم فيما عَرَّضتُم بِهِ مِن خِطبَةِ النِّساءِ أَو أَكنَنتُم فى أَنفُسِكُم»5

‘Azm, from the root “ع ز م,” concerns firm resolve and moving beyond hesitation. ‘Aqd, from the root “ع ق د,” is associated with tying, joining, and making something firm.

God distinguishes between a wish to marry, its indirect expression, a promise, and a firm resolve to conclude the marriage covenant. A person may hope for and think about a future marriage, but must not make a definitive decision to conclude the marriage covenant before the prescribed period ends.

There is no basis in this verse for prohibiting every purchase, gathering of information, or personal preparation for future life. However, if such steps are based on a definitive promise and agreement to marry before the prescribed period has ended, they would not accord with the limits stated in the verse.

In An-Nisa 4:21, God describes marriage as a solemn covenant. Such a covenant must rest on knowledge, consent, and conscious commitment, not haste, emotional pressure, or exploitation of others’ circumstances.

We have examined transparency, loyalty, and avoiding overlapping marital commitments in Qur’anic Marriage: From Biological Needs toward Spiritual Maturity. The Principle of Honoring Premarital Commitments likewise clarifies the importance of promises and the responsibility they entail before the marriage covenant is concluded.

«وَاعلَموا أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَعلَمُ ما فى أَنفُسِكُم فَاحذَروهُ»

At the beginning of the verse, God does not count concealing a wish to marry as a sin; at its end, however, He warns people that He knows what is taking place within them.

Two people may say the same words, yet one seeks to form a healthy family while the other seeks to exploit the woman’s circumstances. People can observe their outward words and conduct, but God also knows their hidden motives and intentions.

Al-Baqarah 2:225 also distinguishes between thoughtless oaths and what hearts consciously earn. Having a natural feeling is not the same as deliberately nurturing a deceitful intention.

«وَاعلَموا أَنَّ اللَّهَ غَفورٌ حَليمٌ»

After warning about observing the limits, God speaks of His forgiveness and forbearance. He knows human weaknesses, needs, and inner struggles, forgives people’s lapses, and does not hasten to punish.

God does not close the path to a healthy marriage; He closes the path to violating others’ rights. Neither should human feelings and desires lead to broken covenants and transgression of divine limits, nor should past attachments, social traditions, and fear of people prevent a wholesome, healthy covenant from being formed in the future.
