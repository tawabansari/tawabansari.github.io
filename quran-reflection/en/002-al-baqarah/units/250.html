Al-Baqarah 2:250

And when they came forth against Goliath and his troops, they said: Our Lord, pour patience upon us, make our steps firm, and help us against the disbelieving people.

Our Reflection

This verse is one of the beautiful prayers of the Quran. The Quran teaches us not only the words of prayer but also, by describing the circumstances of those who prayed, how to understand and put it into practice.

For this prayer to affect our lives and destiny, we must first understand its reality. To understand it, we need, as far as possible, to place ourselves in the circumstances of the Children of Israel, their Prophet, Talut, and his small army, and imagine how they felt when facing Goliath, what path they had traveled, and with what intention, faith, and commitment they prayed.

They were a people who sought deliverance and struggle, yet many withdrew when the time came to take responsibility. They objected to Talut’s selection because they preferred wealth and social standing to fitness in knowledge and physical strength. The God also returned the Ark of the Covenant to them as a sign of Talut’s kingship: an ark that recalled Moses and Aaron, deliverance from Pharaoh, and the victory of truth over oppression, and could revive tranquility and trust in the help of that same God within their hearts.

Nevertheless, having a Prophet, a capable leader, divine signs, and memories of past victories was not enough on its own. The test of the river showed that only a small group remained firm in their commitment. This small group, after weighing the difficulty of confronting Goliath’s army, did not lose their hope and faith and decided to continue the struggle.

«وَلَمَّا بَرَزُوا لِجَالُوتَ وَجُنُودِهِ»

“And when they came forth against Goliath and his troops.”

They were now in the field of action, beyond the stage of wishes, promises, and slogans. They had accepted responsibility, endured hardships, and stood before the enemy.

It was in this state that they prayed:

«رَبَّنَا أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًا»

“Our Lord, pour patience upon us.”

First they ask for patience: patience that gives them the strength to remain steadfast in a right decision. “أَفْرِغْ” evokes an image of pouring out and filling abundantly. They ask their Lord to grant them such patience that they will not retreat from their responsibility amid the hardships of the field.

«وَثَبِّتْ أَقْدَامَنَا»

“And make our steps firm.”

They have taken steps and now ask for those very steps to be made firm. They do not ask their Lord to take steps in their place; rather, they ask Him to make them steadfast on the path they have chosen.

«وَانْصُرْنَا عَلَى الْقَوْمِ الْكَافِرِينَ»

“And help us against the disbelieving people.”

In the Quran, “kufr” does not simply mean having no religion or not belonging to a particular religious community; it refers to concealing and denying truth after it has become clear. For further explanation, see “Who Is a Kafir, and What Is Kufr?”.

After asking for patience and firmness, they ask for help and victory. This sequence is meaningful: they first accepted responsibility and acted to fulfill it, then asked their Lord to help them.

Now it is our turn.

To recite this prayer with understanding and faith, we must compare our circumstances today with theirs, according to our understanding, resources, and abilities. We should examine our intention, will, capacity, ability to make decisions, and ultimately our conduct.

Do we deserve victory?

Whom have we chosen as our leader? How much have our personal preferences, wealth, lineage, ethnic affiliations, and prejudices influenced this choice? Today, when no Prophet is among us, do we assess our leaders’ behavior and conduct by Quranic principles, or do we only support those who say what pleases us?

What symbols of past victories and failures do we have? What do our heroes, stories, and narratives awaken within us? Do they recall patience, perseverance, faith in meeting The God, and the victory of truth over falsehood, or do they glorify the victory of oppressors over truth?

If our stories and narratives say that at the end of the world all people will be engulfed in darkness and falsehood will prevail over everything, does this not mean the victory of falsehood over truth? What place, then, do the Quran’s promises hold in our beliefs: in Al-Anbiya 21:105, that righteous servants will inherit the earth, and in Al-Mujadilah 58:21, where God speaks of His own victory and that of His Messengers?

Most importantly, have we ourselves taken steps for truth and justice? Have we been willing to forgo personal interests, prejudice, and comfort? When responsibility became difficult, did we remain steadfast like Talut’s small group?

This prayer is not confined to the battlefield. In Al-Araf 7:126, those who stood against Pharaoh’s threat after believing in Moses also prayed: “رَبَّنَا أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًا”. They too asked for patience to remain faithful to the truth they had come to recognize.

Whenever we find ourselves facing hardships on the path of truth, we can therefore ask our Lord for patience, steadfastness, and help with this understanding.

But before praying, we must look honestly at ourselves: Is our intention right? Is our path true? Have we assessed our abilities? Have we taken steps toward our goal? And have we remained faithful, to the best of our ability, to the responsibility we accepted?

Then our prayer will no longer be a mere repetition of words; it will arise from understanding, faith, commitment, and action.

Talut’s small army uttered this prayer while standing before the enemy. In the next verse, we see that they prevailed by the permission of The God and that David killed Goliath. This victory was not separate from the faith and commitment that had brought them to the field of struggle.
