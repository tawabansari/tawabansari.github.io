Al-Baqarah 2:162

ۖ

They remain in it; the punishment will not be lightened for them, nor will they be granted respite.

Our Reflection

This verse continues the discussion of those who died in a state of disbelief. In the preceding verse, the Quran described them as subject to the curse of God, the angels, and all people. It now says, “خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا”: remaining in it. The pronoun in “فِيهَا” refers back to “لَعْنَة” in the preceding verse. Thus, it speaks of remaining in that state of curse and exclusion from divine mercy.

The connection between this remaining and the phrase “لَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُمُ الْعَذَابُ” shows that the curse here is not merely a verbal imprecation, but is accompanied by real punishment. Being kept away from divine mercy may itself be an aspect of this punishment. In Aal Imran 3:87 and Aal Imran 3:88, the Quran also expresses this connection between curse, remaining, and punishment not being lightened. Yet the verse does not explain the precise nature of this punishment; it makes clear only that it will not be lightened for them.

The closing phrase, “وَلَا هُمْ يُنْظَرُونَ,” likewise means that they will not be granted respite. In Al-Baqarah 2:160, the way of repentance, reform, and return was open; but Al-Baqarah 2:161 spoke of those who died in that same state of disbelief. The opportunity to return during earthly life has now ended, and the consequence of that path becomes manifest in the hereafter. Remaining under the curse, punishment not being lightened, and receiving no respite are three connected expressions of this state.

This verse shows that the divine curse is not confined to the consequences of disbelief in earthly life; it can also be realized in the hereafter as lasting exclusion from mercy and punishment. But the manner of this remaining and the nature of punishment in a world beyond our present experience cannot be drawn in detail from this verse. The God knows its reality and manner best. And God knows best.
