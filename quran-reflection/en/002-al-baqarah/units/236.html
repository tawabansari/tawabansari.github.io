Al-Baqarah 2:236

ۚ ۖ

There is no blame upon you if you divorce women when you have not yet touched them or prescribed an obligation for them; and provide for them—the affluent according to his means, and the one of limited means according to his means—an honorable provision, a duty upon those who do good.

Our Reflection

Marriage is a firm covenant, but if a woman and man discover after entering it that they are incompatible, the Quran does not condemn them to continue the relationship by force.

“لَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِنْ طَلَّقْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ مَا لَمْ تَمَسُّوهُنَّ أَوْ تَفْرِضُوا لَهُنَّ فَرِيضَةً”

“There is no blame upon you if you divorce women when you have not yet touched them or prescribed an obligation for them.”

The verse concerns separation at the beginning of marriage, when marital relations have not yet taken place or the financial obligation, namely mahr, has not been determined. After the marriage contract, the woman and man may discover a significant incompatibility as they come to know one another and decide to end the marriage covenant.

Such separation is not in itself a sin. But two people being incompatible does not mean that either of them is a bad person.

Sometimes after a marriage ends, the man or his family justifies the separation by saying that the woman had a defect, was deficient, or was unworthy of being a wife. The woman or her family, in turn, may also discredit the man, speak abusively about him, or spread rumors.

These behaviors are inconsistent with honorable conduct and kindness. When two people have been unable to build a successful shared life, there is no need to destroy each other’s reputation and character to justify their separation.

In Al-Baqarah 2:229 and Al-Baqarah 2:231, the Quran emphasizes retaining honorably or releasing with kindness and honorable conduct. This principle applies even at the beginning of marriage.

“وَمَتِّعُوهُنَّ عَلَى الْمُوسِعِ قَدَرُهُ وَعَلَى الْمُقْتِرِ قَدَرُهُ”

“And provide for them—the affluent according to his means, and the one of limited means according to his means.”

The end of a marriage does not remove the responsibility to respect the woman’s rights. In this situation, the Quran commands the man to provide for the woman, but it does not set the same amount for everyone.

The affluent man is responsible according to his financial ability, and the man of limited means according to his resources. Thus, an impossible amount cannot be demanded from the latter, nor can the affluent evade his responsibility by paying a negligible sum.

The following verse, Al-Baqarah 2:237, separately addresses separation before marital contact when the financial obligation has already been determined.

“مَتَاعًا بِالْمَعْرُوفِ حَقًّا عَلَى الْمُحْسِنِينَ”

“An honorable provision—a duty upon those who do good.”

This expression connects the principle of respecting rights with honorable conduct and kindness.

The Quran establishes the woman’s entitlement to provision, but its appropriate form and amount may vary with economic circumstances, time, place, and living conditions.

This is one of the important uses of ma‘ruf in the Quran: divine principles and limits are fixed, but people may arrange details that the Quran has not specified in a particular form according to reason, fairness, proper custom, and the actual circumstances of life.

Of course, not every prevalent custom is ma‘ruf. If a culture normalizes discrediting women after separation, depriving them of financial rights, or imposing impossible expenses, the prevalence of such conduct cannot make it proper.

This distinction between fixed Quranic principles, ma‘ruf, munkar, fahsha, and changing custom is examined in detail in the study “Ma‘ruf, Munkar, and Fahsha in the Quran: Fixed Divine Principles and Changing Human Custom”.

The verse’s message is clear: if a marriage covenant ends early because of incompatibility, separation must not turn into hostility, slander, and destruction of one another’s reputation. The man must fulfill the woman’s entitlement to provision honorably and according to his means, and both parties must preserve each other’s respect and dignity. The end of a marriage is not the end of human responsibility toward rights and goodness.
