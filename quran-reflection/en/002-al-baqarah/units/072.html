Al-Baqarah 2:72

And when you killed (qataltum) a soul, a human life (nafs), then you fell into mutual blame-deflection (iddara'tum) about it; and The God (Allah) is bringing out (mukhrij) what you were concealing (taktumun).

Our Reflection

After the end of the cow story, the Qur'an opens a heavier scene: a killing has occurred, a truth has been hidden, and a society, instead of standing before the truth, has entered into dispute and blame-deflection. This verse shows that the matter was not only about slaughtering a cow; behind all those questions, delay, making things hard, and postponement, there was also hidden blood.

The verse does not say: one of you killed a soul. It says: “you killed a soul” (qataltum nafsan). Perhaps the final blow was struck by one person, but the address of the Qur'an is collective. This expression should not be taken lightly. In the logic of the Qur'an, a society is not polluted only by the direct hand of the killer; it also shares in the space of the crime through silence, concealment, group defense, protecting collective honor against truth, and pushing responsibility away from itself.

This point is not without precedent in this same surah. A little later, the Qur'an says to the Children of Israel: “you were killing yourselves” (taqtulun anfusakum). This collective language shows that killing, if it occurs in a collective atmosphere of corruption, partisanship, or injustice, is not only an individual act separate from society. A society that hides the killer, shifts the accusation, or sacrifices truth for group bonds has a share in the spread of death.

“A soul/self” (nafsan) is also a weighty word. The Qur'an speaks of killing a soul/self (nafs): a life, a human being, a life that carries sanctity. When a soul/self (nafs) is killed, it is not only a body that disappears; security, justice, trust, and the collective conscience are also wounded. If a society does not awaken before such blood, death does not remain in that body; it spreads into the morals of the society.

Then it says: “then you entered mutual blame-deflection about it” (then you mutually deflected blame fa-iddara'tum about it fiha). This word does not mean simple disagreement only. The root “repelling” (D R A) means to push away, drive off, repel, and thrust away from oneself. The structure of the verb is also important; its reciprocal form shows that the matter was two-sided or multi-sided. Each side was trying to push responsibility away from itself and drive it toward another. So the verse is not speaking of a simple accusation; it is speaking of collective blame-deflection.

Here the social illness becomes visible. A killing has occurred, but instead of seeking the truth, every group wants to show itself as clean. Instead of the blood of the innocent bringing them back to justice and confession, they become occupied with deflecting blame. This is the moment when a society moves away from truth: when the issue is no longer what the truth is, but how guilt can be kept away from us.

Then the verse says: “And The God is bringing out what you were concealing” (wa-The God Allah is bringing out mukhrij what you were concealing taktumun). The expression “bringing out” (mukhrij) is very strong. The Qur'an does not say only that The God once brought something out; through the active participle it says that The God is the One who brings out. This is not only a past incident; it is a divine attribute and way. Truth, however deeply it is buried under the soil of silence, fear, partisanship, or expediency, does not remain buried in the realm of The God.

“What you were concealing” (what you were kuntum concealing taktumun) also has an ongoing sense. It speaks of something you were hiding, something over whose concealment you were standing, and something you did not want to become manifest. This expression reminds us of another verse at the beginning of this same surah, where The God says in the story of Adam and the angels: “And I know what you reveal and what you were concealing.” From the beginning, the Qur'an shows that hidden and manifest are not the same before the knowledge of The God, but no hidden thing is outside His realm. What the human being covers does not remain covered before The God.

So verse 72 does not only open a murder case; it exposes the character of concealment. The problem is not only the spilled blood; it is a truth that is covered after the blood is spilled. The problem is not only the killer; it is a culture that, instead of making truth clear, buries it among group defenses, fears, expediencies, and blame-deflections.

In connecting this verse with the cow story, one must speak carefully. On one side, the Qur'an brings this scene after the verses of the cow, and the next verse, with the expression “with part of it,” leaves open the path of connection with that story. So it is not right to separate this section completely from the cow story. On the other side, the verse itself, with “and when,” opens a scene that also has independent meaning: killing, dispute, concealment, and the bringing out of truth.

Here two levels stand together. On the first level, we face an incident: a soul has been killed and a truth has remained hidden. On the second level, the Qur'an brings us to a larger law: The God brings out what human beings conceal. So whether we read these verses in close connection with the cow story, or whether we begin from the social and ethical angle of this verse itself, the main message is clear: hidden truth is not lost before The God.

This verse is also a mirror for today. Any society may restrict a killing only to the hand of the killer and ask itself: who struck? But the Qur'an opens a deeper question: who covered it? Who stayed silent? Who shifted the accusation? Who sacrificed truth for tribe, party, sect, family, power, or reputation? Sometimes the death of a soul begins with one blow; but it continues through a thousand silences and justifications.

The lesson of the verse is this: hidden blood does not remain silent. Buried truth does not die without sound. The One God is bringing out what human beings conceal. And before truth is dragged out through disgrace, society itself must return to justice, confession, and repair.
