Al-Baqarah 2:153

ۚ

O you who have believed, seek help through patience and prayer; indeed, The God is with those who remain steadfast.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, God directly called His servants to remember Him and give thanks, promising to remember them. Now He places before them two practical means of seeking help along the path of service: patience and prayer. The same Lord who called them to remember Him directly does not now refer them to sacred personalities or religious intermediaries. He calls them to steadfastness and a living relationship with Himself.

“Seek help” belongs to the root ʿ W N. In Al-Fatihah 1:5, people direct their worship and appeal for help to God; here, the practical way of seeking that help is explained. Patience and prayer are not two independent sources of power alongside God. Through steadfastness and a living relationship with the Lord, people place themselves on the path of His help. Asking God for help therefore does not mean abandoning responsibility and waiting. People must themselves walk the path God has shown.

This command is not new. In Al-Baqarah 2:45, the Children of Israel were also called to seek help through patience and prayer, and the Qur’an spoke of how burdensome this was except for the humble. Now believers are reminded of the same two principles. Patience and prayer are shared features of the prophets’ path. The Qur’an also speaks of prayer in connection with Moses, Abraham, Ishmael, and Jesus. A relationship with God and steadfastness in His way did not begin with Muhammad’s mission and do not belong exclusively to one religious community.

Patience belongs to the root Ṣ B R, associated with holding, restraining, exercising self-control, and remaining steadfast. Enduring illness, poverty, loss, and life’s difficulties expresses patience, but does not exhaust its meaning. Sometimes people must stand firm amid suffering; sometimes they must restrain themselves from something appealing but wrong. Someone who restrains anger from becoming injustice, resists the temptation of unlawful wealth, avoids envy over what they lack, values what they have, and refuses to abandon truth under pressure displays different forms of this steadfastness.

Patience is connected with contentment and gratitude, but is not identical to them. Grateful people recognize the value of their gifts; patient people do not let deprivation, greed, or hardship drive them from the right path. They may suffer and grieve, but do not surrender themselves to permanent bitterness, ingratitude, and harmful reactions. Patience also does not mean suppressing feelings or refusing to speak to God about pain. Although Jacob spoke of beautiful patience, in Yusuf 12:86 he shared his sorrow directly with his Lord. Job likewise spoke to God of his suffering in Al-Anbiya 21:83. Patience does not deny pain; it preserves life’s right direction amid pain and difficulty.

More importantly, not every kind of persistence is worthy. Sad 38:6 describes people urging one another to persist in devotion to their gods. They too insisted on their beliefs, but resistance alone did not establish their truth. Praiseworthy patience means remaining steadfast in truth, justice, mindfulness of God, and doing good. Al-‘Asr 103:3 places mutual counsel toward truth beside mutual counsel toward patience. Nor is patience a blanket command to endure oppression. Ash-Shura 42:39 speaks of defending oneself against injustice, while 42:43 also values patience joined with forgiveness. Sometimes patience appears in resistance and seeking justice, and sometimes in refraining from revenge and choosing forgiveness. For a broader examination, see Patience in the Qur’an: The Meaning of Steadfastness and the Criterion of Its Value.

Alongside patience, the Qur’an places prayer. Qur’anic prayer is not confined to “saying prayers” or repeating a set of words and movements. The Qur’an speaks of establishing prayer: sustaining a living, conscious, continuing relationship of worship with the one God. Standing, bowing, prostration, recitation, and remembrance have their place, but their purpose must not disappear into outward forms. Ta-Ha 20:14 connects establishing prayer with remembering God, while Al-‘Ankabut 29:45 identifies restraint from indecency and wrongdoing as an effect of prayer. Prayer should keep remembrance alive and affect thought, decisions, and conduct. This is examined in What Is Prayer in the Qur’an, and How Is It Established?.

Patience and prayer are distinct but connected. Sustaining prayer itself requires patience, as Ta-Ha 20:132 commands perseverance in prayer. Conversely, a living relationship with the Lord can strengthen people against difficulties and wrongful desires. Patience helps keep that relationship from being abandoned under life’s pressures; prayer keeps remembrance and responsibility alive along the path of steadfastness. Someone who establishes prayer yet treats people with injustice, deception, envy, and unfairness must ask what effect that relationship has had on their life.

The ending deserves particular attention. God first mentions both patience and prayer, but specifically promises His companionship to the patient: “The God is with those who remain steadfast.” This does not diminish prayer; it emphasizes steadfastness on the right path. Prayer must not become an isolated act whose ending also removes remembrance from conduct. Divine companionship is promised here to those remaining steadfast in faith and service, not to everyone who merely insists on their own wishes or beliefs.

This companionship does not mean God’s spatial presence beside a person. It concerns divine help and attention along the path of service. Nor should it be taken as a guarantee that patient people never fail or suffer, or that their worldly wishes will be fulfilled immediately. Patience does not promise a life without difficulty. Its value lies in remaining faithful to the right path even when the outcome remains unclear or suffering continues.

The following verses make this clearer. Al-Baqarah 2:155 speaks of testing people through fear, hunger, and losses of wealth, lives, and fruits. In 2:156, when calamity strikes, the patient recall that they belong to God and return to Him. Then 2:157 identifies these same people as recipients of their Lord’s blessings and mercy, and as guided. Patience in this sequence is therefore more than enduring an unpleasant event. It is the steadfastness of people who, amid trials, do not forget God, truth, and the path of return to Him.

The preceding verse told us to remember God and give thanks for His favors. This verse shows how remembrance and gratitude are to endure in life: through patience and prayer. No sacred intermediary can replace the servant’s direct relationship with the Lord, and outward worship cannot replace responsibility and steadfastness. God’s promise is clear: the one God is with those who remain steadfast.
