Al-Baqarah 2:21

O people, worship your Lord; the One who created you and those before you, so that perhaps you may become mindful and guarded.

Our Reflection

After completing the description of three human conditions at the beginning of the surah, this verse changes the level of address. The speech is no longer only about the mindful, the coverers of truth, or the hypocrites. The address is now directly to an-nas: human society at the broadest level of address. Here the Qur’an calls the human being, before every religious, ethnic, or group identity, as a responsible creature before their Lord.

The central command of the verse is: “Worship your Lord.” Worship here should not be understood as though the One God needs praise. Worship is the human response to the reality of Lordship: the conscious and practical ordering of one’s relation with the Lord who nurtures life, governs it, and places it on a path of growth and responsibility. For this reason, the verse first says “your Lord,” and then says, “the One who created you.” Creation points to the beginning of existence, but Lordship is the living and continuous flow of governance, nurture, and preservation. The Qur’an begins the call to worship from the closest relation between the human being and God: the Lord whose care surrounds human life at every moment.

The phrase “the One who created you and those before you” removes the human being from the illusion of isolated existence. The addressee is not a single individual cut off from history, but one link in a continuous chain of creation. The same Lord who created you also created those before you. Therefore worship in this verse is not a local, ethnic, or narrowly ritual reaction. It is a response to a shared truth in creation.

The ending, “so that perhaps you may become mindful and guarded,” is very important. Taqwa is not presented here as an automatic or forced result. It is the aim and possibility of worship. If worship is conscious, truthful, and grounded in recognition, it can bring the human being toward taqwa. But if it is performed as habit, display, or lifeless imitation, it does not necessarily bear that fruit.

Taqwa in the Qur’an is also not only fear. Fear may be one of the early layers of caution, but taqwa can be formed on the basis of love, faith, recognition, reason, and an awakened conscience. The fullest form of taqwa rests on faith; and iman, from the root A M N, is connected with security, peace, trust, and assurance. Deep taqwa, then, is a carefulness that rises from conscious trust in truth, not merely from anxiety or fear. Such taqwa brings a human being closer to goodness, excellence, and uprightness.

In connection with the previous verses, verse 21 is a return to the root. If the earlier verses portrayed different human conditions before guidance, darkness, light, denial, and hypocrisy, here the Qur’an calls everyone back to the shared foundation: worship your Lord, because He is the One who created you and those before you. This call, before it is a call to an identity, is a call to recognize the human being’s relation with the Lord.

Cross-References
  • Al-Baqarah 2:2— Guidance is introduced as guidance for the mindful. In verse 21, taqwa appears as the possible fruit of worship; the very quality that allows guidance to be received is itself nurtured through conscious worship.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:17-20— Before this verse, human instability before light and darkness was portrayed. Verse 21 moves beyond the analysis of these states and calls all people back to the shared foundation.
  • Al-A'raf 7:172— “Am I not your Lord? They said: Yes.” This connects to Lordship and the primordial human testimony before the Lord.
  • Adh-Dhariyat 51:56— The relation between creation and worship, and worship as the existential purpose of human and jinn life within the created order.
  • An-Nisa 4:1— A public address to people, linking taqwa with Lordship and reminding humanity of its shared origin.
  • Al-Hajj 22:78— Religion is not made a system of hardship and constriction; worship and taqwa should remain a path of conscious return to the Lord.
