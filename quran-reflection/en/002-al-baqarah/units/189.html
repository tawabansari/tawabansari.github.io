Al-Baqarah 2:189

ۖ ۗ ۗ ۚ

They ask you about the crescents. Say: they are markers of time for people and the Hajj. Righteousness is not entering houses from their backs; rather, righteousness belongs to the person who practices taqwa. Enter houses through their doors, and practice taqwa toward The God, so that you may prosper.

Our Reflection

In response to the question about the lunar crescents, The God says: “هِيَ مَوَاقِيتُ لِلنَّاسِ وَالْحَجِّ”—they are markers of time for people and the Hajj. The crescents enable people to recognize the passage of months, organize their calendar, and determine the timing of social and devotional activities and obligations, including the Hajj. In Yunus 10:5, the moon’s phases are also connected with knowing the number of years and reckoning. The Hajj, as stated in Al-Baqarah 2:197, is likewise associated with known months. This subject is discussed in detail in the article “Hajj in the Quran: Ibrahim’s Way, Rites, and People’s Rights”.

The God continues: “وَلَيْسَ الْبِرُّ بِأَنْ تَأْتُوا الْبُيُوتَ مِنْ ظُهُورِهَا وَلَٰكِنَّ الْبِرَّ مَنِ اتَّقَىٰ”—righteousness is not entering houses from their backs; rather, righteousness belongs to the person who practices taqwa. He then commands: “وَأْتُوا الْبُيُوتَ مِنْ أَبْوَابِهَا”—enter houses through their doors.

In its direct meaning, the recognized way into a house is its door. Yet a broader meaning can also be inferred from this command: in every undertaking, a person should use its reasonable, proper, right, and recognized approach, rather than deceitful, wrongful, or misguided routes.

For example, someone seeking admission to a university should enter through merit and proper admission, not bribery and cheating. Someone seeking a government appointment should obtain it through merit and fair competition, not by buying a position or violating others’ rights. Someone migrating to another country should follow lawful and recognized routes and, in their life there, respect people’s rights and just laws.

This principle matters even in a sacred undertaking such as the Hajj. A sacred destination does not justify a wrongful means of reaching it. A person cannot reach a destination through deception, bribery, violations of others’ rights, or evasion of divine limits and count the mere arrival as righteousness.

In Al-Baqarah 2:58, The God also commands the Children of Israel, when entering the town, to enter through the gate and humbly ask for forgiveness: “وَادْخُلُوا الْبَابَ سُجَّدًا وَقُولُوا حِطَّةٌ”. There too, entering the destination is connected with how people conduct themselves in relation to The God’s command.

The criterion for this conduct is made clear within the verse itself: “وَلَٰكِنَّ الْبِرَّ مَنِ اتَّقَىٰ”. Righteousness is not merely reaching a goal or performing an outward act; the way of reaching it must also accord with taqwa, justice, and respect for others’ rights. Likewise, in Al-Baqarah 2:177, righteousness is distinguished from outward appearances and connected with faith, upright conduct, and taqwa.

The verse ends: “وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ”—practice taqwa toward The God, so that you may prosper.

A person may reach a desired destination through cheating, bribery, or violations of others’ rights, but reaching the destination does not mean attaining true success. Falah means choosing the right destination and also taking the right path toward it.
