Al-Baqarah 2:3

Those who believe in the unseen, establish Salat, and spend from what We have provided them.

Our Reflection

After the Qur’an introduced itself as a Book without doubt and as guidance for the mindful, it now describes the first signs of those mindful ones. Mindful self-guarding in the Qur’an is not merely an inner feeling or a religious claim. This mindfulness has signs in life. In this verse, three foundational signs are mentioned: belief in the unseen, establishing Prayerful devotion, and spending from what God has provided.

The first sign is belief in the unseen. The unseen is that part of reality which is hidden from our direct sight, yet is not outside reality. God, the Hereafter, the Account, the angels, the promise of God, and many dimensions of existence belong to the realm of the unseen. The mindful person is not someone who only looks at the tangible world and immediate benefit. They know that life is not only what is immediately visible. Behind this world there is meaning, accountability, responsibility, and return to God.

But faith in the Qur’an is not merely a mental belief. The Arabic word for faith, īmān, belongs to the root ʾ M N, associated here with security, trust, and entrusted responsibility. A believer is one who comes to trust and have confidence in truth, and that trust becomes visible in action. This is why the Qur’an immediately connects faith with Salat and spending. If faith remains only on the tongue and does not become orientation, worship, ethics, responsibility, and service, it has not yet reached its Qur’anic meaning. For a fuller discussion of faith in the Qur’an, see: What Is Faith in the Qur’an?

The second sign is establishing Prayerful devotion. The Qur’an speaks of establishing prayer, rather than merely performing an act or reciting words. This difference matters. The Arabic term for establishing, iqāmah, means to establish, make firm, and keep standing. So Salat is not only a momentary act or the recitation of a few words; it must stand in a person’s life as a living practice that has an effect and gives direction.

The Qur’an itself clarifies the purpose of Prayerful devotion: “Establish prayer for My remembrance”. So Prayerful devotion is the living remembrance of God: returning human awareness to God, to truth, to responsibility, and to one’s place in the world. If this remembrance is separated from Prayerful devotion, only the form remains, not the spirit.

The Qur’an also gives Prayerful devotion a practical criterion: “Prayer restrains indecency and wrongdoing”. Therefore, a practice of prayer that does not affect a person’s ethics, speech, justice, mercy, trustworthiness, and behavior needs to be reconsidered. Establishing Salat means that connection with God must affect life, not remain separate from it. For a fuller discussion of Salat in the Qur’an, see: What Is Prayerful devotion in the Qur’an and How Is It Established?

The third sign is spending from God’s provision. The verse does not say they spend only from their money. It says: “from what We have provided them.” Provision is not only wealth. Knowledge, time, ability, skill, experience, social standing, physical strength, the ability to write, heal, build, and even the ability to listen to another person’s pain can all be from God’s provision. The mindful person does not keep what God has given only for themselves. They allow part of it to flow in the path of goodness, justice, help, reform, and service to others.

This verse shows that the guidance of the Qur’an becomes alive for those who carry these three directions together: their heart is open to the unseen, their relationship with God is established through Prayerful devotion, and their hand is not closed toward the creation of God. Faith, Prayerful devotion, and spending together form a balanced human being: one who is not only a person of belief, not only a person of private worship, and not only a person of social work without divine grounding; rather, their heart, worship, and service are joined together.
