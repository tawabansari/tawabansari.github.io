Al-Baqarah 2:204

And among the people is one whose speech in worldly life pleases you, and who calls The God to witness what is in his heart, while he is the most stubborn of adversaries.

Our Reflection

In this verse, The God describes people whose outward speech does not agree with the reality of their actions and inner selves:

“وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَنْ يُعْجِبُكَ قَوْلُهُ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا”

“And among the people is one whose speech in worldly life pleases you.”

Some people speak so beautifully, thoughtfully, and appealingly that listeners are easily captivated. They speak of justice, honesty, serving people, and reforming society, making promises as though they will solve every problem.

Today, this behavior can be observed among politicians, rulers, leaders, religious authorities, and elected representatives. When seeking public support, they speak of combating poverty and corruption, entrusting work to qualified people, creating jobs, expanding education, establishing justice, and improving people’s lives.

Sometimes they also exploit the suffering of ordinary people and those in need, stirring their emotions and making promises for which they have neither a sound plan nor the practical ability and will. Their aim is to gain trust, win popularity, and attain power.

The God continues:

“وَيُشْهِدُ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا فِي قَلْبِهِ”

“And he calls The God to witness what is in his heart.”

Such a person does not stop at attractive words; he also calls God to witness his sincerity and intentions in order to gain greater public trust.

He may repeatedly swear in God’s name, present himself as an honest servant of the people, and claim to intend nothing but good and reform. Yet invoking God’s name and swearing by Him are not, in themselves, evidence of truthfulness and purity of intention.

At the end, it says:

“وَهُوَ أَلَدُّ الْخِصَامِ”

“While he is the most stubborn of adversaries.”

“أَلَدُّ,” from the root ل د د, concerns stubbornness and obstinacy in hostility, while “خِصَام” refers to enmity and contention.

Thus, someone who outwardly speaks sweetly of goodwill and reform may in reality be deeply contentious and obstinate.

This verse teaches us not to make beautiful speech, attractive promises, or even invoking God as a witness our sole measure of a person’s honesty and fitness.

But how can such people be recognized? In the next two verses, Al-Baqarah 2:205 and Al-Baqarah 2:206, The God describes further traits. By examining their behavior, we can better recognize the difference between appealing speech and the reality of their actions.
