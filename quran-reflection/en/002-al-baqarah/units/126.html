Al-Baqarah 2:126

ۖ ۖ

And when Abraham said: My Lord, make this a secure city and provide its people with fruits and produce—those among them who believe in The God and the Last Day. He said: And whoever rejects truth, I will also let him enjoy a little; then I will compel him toward the punishment of the Fire. What an evil outcome!

Our Reflection

The preceding verse called the House a place of return and safety for people. Now Abraham asks for that same safety for the land in which this House stands:

“My Lord, make this a secure city.”

God’s House was not established in a vacuum. People live around it, and others come toward it from afar. They need safety to live and sustenance to remain. Abraham’s next prayer therefore concerns neither the splendor of the building nor the power of his family:

“Provide its people with fruits and produce.”

“Fruits and produce” should not be restricted to fruit grown in that very soil. The Qur’an itself says of the secure sanctuary:

“To it are gathered the fruits of every kind, as provision from Us.” Al-Qasas 28:57.

Abraham’s prayer, then, is not meant to turn the valley of Mecca into a lush orchard. Sustenance can reach this city from distant places, just as people come to the House from distant places. Ibrahim 14:37 gives the same prayer in his words: he has settled some of his descendants in an uncultivated valley beside the House and asks God to provide them with fruits and produce.

But Abraham’s prayer contains a qualification:

“Those among them who believe in The God and the Last Day.”

A few verses earlier, when Abraham asked about his descendants, he heard that God’s covenant does not reach wrongdoers. Here, too, when he speaks of provision, he limits his prayer to people of faith.

God’s answer, however, opens the world’s table more widely:

“And whoever rejects truth, I will also let him enjoy a little.”

Covenant and provision do not follow the same rule. The covenant does not reach wrongdoers, but rain, food, wealth, and the opportunity to live do not fall only upon believers’ homes. God gives the blessings of this world even to someone who rejects truth. The world’s table is broader than our religious boundaries.

This also breaks down an illusion: prosperity is not proof of God’s approval. A person may possess great wealth and power, live in a holy city, benefit from the House’s prosperity, and still have followed a path whose outcome is not good. Proximity to sacred things does not take the place of faith.

Perhaps the most unsettling part of the verse is precisely that “and whoever rejects truth” appears right here, in a discussion of the people of the very city where the House stands.

Rejection of truth is not something found only in distant lands among unfamiliar peoples. It can occur beside the Kaaba. It can arise in the shadow of sacred walls, amid religious rites, and within a society that regards itself as the guardian of religion. No geographical closeness to the House, religious title, or historical record makes a person immune to rejecting truth.

Here a difficult question confronts us:

If people turn a House that God established “for the people” into their sect’s property; if they compel people to follow one denomination and one particular religious law; if they claim success and salvation exclusively for their own community; if human constructions take the place of God’s command and are added to the pilgrimage rites in His name; if they block God’s servants from His mosques and prevent people from worshipping because of differences in belief—have they not rejected truth?

This question does not come out of nowhere. Of the Sacred Mosque itself, the Qur’an says:

“Those who have rejected truth and bar people from God’s way and from the Sacred Mosque, which We established for the people…” Al-Hajj 22:25.

The Qur’an itself places rejection of truth alongside barring people from God’s way and the Sacred Mosque. If that same conduct is repeated in religious dress, under a new name, or by people who regard themselves as custodians of sacred places, does its nature change as well?

Readers must answer for themselves, using the Qur’an’s criterion.

The broader discussion of what rejection of truth means in the Qur’an—whether it is merely the name of a religious identity or can also appear in covering truth and standing against it—is examined in Who Rejects Truth, and What Is Rejection of Truth?

This question also brings pilgrimage back to its foundation. Pilgrimage is not only the number of circuits, clothing, location, and movement. The House is for people; the city must be safe; Abraham’s way is connected with worshipping God alone and submitting to Him; and even someone who rejects truth is not deprived of God’s worldly provision. If the rites become a means of monopolizing religion, imposing belief, creating religious classes, or closing people’s way to God, we must ask what remains of Abraham’s pilgrimage.

Yet worldly enjoyment is not the end of the story:

“Then I will compel him toward the punishment of the Fire.”

“A little” does not mean that every person who rejects truth will necessarily receive little provision in this world. Elsewhere the Qur’an uses the same construction:

“We let them enjoy a little, then We compel them toward a severe punishment.” Luqman 31:24.

However long and full of blessings worldly life may appear to us, it is short beside the final outcome. Even abundant possessions can be “little,” because they do not last.

“What an evil outcome!”

The word translated “outcome” (maṣīr) is not merely a place to which someone returns; it is the end to which their path leads. The verse began with safety and provision, but closes by reminding us that worldly security and abundant blessings do not cancel the reckoning of the hereafter.

This same distinction breaks the claim to inherited or sectarian salvation. A community may consider itself chosen, possess the House, have wealth and power, and enjoy plentiful blessings; none of these alone is evidence of salvation. Paradise is not the property of one sect or one religious law. This subject is explored at length in Paradise Is Not the Monopoly of One Sect or One Religious Law.

God does not withhold His provision even from someone who rejects truth. Yet this very abundance must not deceive anyone into thinking that their path also has God’s approval.

One can live beside God’s House, eat from His table, bear the name of religion, and still turn away from His way.
