Al-Baqarah 2:201

And among them is one who says: Our Lord, grant us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, The God spoke of those who seek only the benefit of this world and have no share in the Hereafter. Now, in contrast to that request, He teaches us a prayer encompassing both this world and the Hereafter:

“رَبَّنَا آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً”

“Our Lord, grant us good in this world and good in the Hereafter.”

The first important point is the way this prayer addresses God. A person calls directly upon their Lord: “رَبَّنَا”—Our Lord.

This prayer introduces no intermediary, appoints no intercessor to convey the request to The God, and does not require a person to approach someone else first to bring their wish to the Lord.

The same direct relationship appears in Al-Baqarah 2:186, where The God says:

“فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌ أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ الدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ”

“Indeed, I am near; I answer the supplicant’s prayer when they call upon Me.”

To pray, then, a person needs no intermediary to carry their voice to The God. The Lord who created them hears their voice Himself.

The second point is asking for good in both lives.

Contrary to the common idea that a religious person must relinquish this world, regard poverty and deprivation as their natural lot, and expect all good only in the Hereafter, the Qur’an teaches us to ask the Lord for good in this world as well.

“حَسَنَةً,” from the root ح س ن, concerns goodness and what is good and commendable. Good in this world can encompass health, wholesome provision, a good family, beneficial knowledge, security, justice, peace, and a worthy life.

In Al-Araf 7:32, The God challenges the arbitrary prohibition of adornment and wholesome provisions. In An-Nahl 16:97, He speaks of “حَيَاةً طَيِّبَةً,” a wholesome life, for those who believe and act righteously.

Thus, religious devotion means living in this world with faith, justice, mindfulness, and responsibility, without forgetting the Hereafter; it does not mean abandoning this world.

The article “Trials and the Promise of Good in the Qur’an: Is This World the Believer’s Prison and the Disbeliever’s Paradise?” examines this subject in detail. The Qur’an neither presents worldly life as inherently the believer’s prison nor makes suffering and deprivation a condition of religious devotion.

Of course, worldly good is not confined to wealth and material comfort. Someone may possess abundant assets yet lack peace, justice, and healthy relationships. Conversely, enjoying worldly blessings should not distract a person from truth and the Hereafter.

For this reason, the prayer does not end with worldly good. It continues:

“وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً”

“And good in the Hereafter.”

People should strive in this world for a better life, the relief of poverty, resistance to oppression, and the flourishing of society. Yet they should remember that their lives are not confined to this world and that they will be accountable for their actions.

Finally, it says:

“وَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ”

“And protect us from the punishment of the Fire.”

“قِنَا” comes from the root و ق ي, the same root as taqwa, and concerns preservation and protection.

In this prayer, a person asks their Lord to protect them from the punishment of the Fire. Such a request accompanies personal responsibility for choosing the right path, doing righteous deeds, and avoiding oppression.

This short prayer brings together three fundamental requests: good in this world, good in the Hereafter, and protection from the punishment of the Fire. It neither sets this world aside nor forgets the Hereafter, and it places no intermediary between a person and their Lord for speaking to Him.

The Qur’an teaches us to call directly upon The God, strive for goodness and the flourishing of this life, and at the same time remember the outcome of our actions in the Hereafter. A worthy life in this world and happiness in the Hereafter are not opposing aspirations; both can have a place in one prayer and along one path of worship.
