Al-Baqarah 2:238

Guard the prayers and the middle prayer, and stand before The God in obedience.

Our Reflection

Amid verses discussing family relationships, the rights of wife and husband, and the responsibilities of life, the Quran commands safeguarding the prayers. People become occupied with work, family, disagreements, worries, and many other concerns, and amid all this may forget remembrance of their Lord and their responsibility as His servants. The command “حٰفِظوا,” from the root ح ف ظ, emphasizes guarding and care: preserving something valuable so that it is not neglected or forgotten.

“حٰفِظوا عَلَى الصَّلَوٰتِ وَالصَّلوٰةِ الوُسطىٰ”

“Guard the prayers and the middle prayer.”

“Salawat” is the plural of “salat.” This word family has a broad range of uses in the Quran, each clarified by its agent and context. In Al-Baqarah 2:157, salawat come upon servants from their Lord and are connected with mercy and guidance; in the present verse, however, the people addressed are commanded to guard the prayers and stand before God. The subject is therefore people’s acts of worship in prayer and safeguarding them.

In the Quran, “salat” is not confined to performing movements and reciting words; it is an actual act of worship that should keep remembrance of God and obedience to Him alive in a person’s life. In Ta-Ha 20:14, establishing prayer is connected with remembrance of God, and in Al-Ankabut 29:45, restraining indecency and wrongdoing is identified as one of its effects. Thus, guarding the prayers means both safeguarding the performance of worship and preserving its reality and effect in life. Someone who stands for prayer must also remember servitude to the Lord in conduct toward family, respect for others’ rights, and daily decisions.

After mentioning prayers in the plural, the Quran singles out “الصَّلوٰةِ الوُسطىٰ” for special attention. “Wusta,” from the root و س ط, can denote a middle position or worthiness and excellence. In this verse, it describes a particular prayer distinguished within the collection of prayers.

If we understand “wusta” in relation to the chronological order of the prayers, identifying it depends upon the number of daily prayers and their sequence. Other Quranic verses discuss prayer times, but this expression alone does not yield a definitive name for the middle prayer or the total number of daily prayers. In any case, the verse’s clear command is to guard the prayers and give special attention to the prayer mentioned. The wider discussion of prayer times and practice is examined in the study “Salat in the Quran: Meaning, Establishment, and the Practice of Prayer”.

“وَقوموا لِلَّهِ قٰنِتينَ”

“And stand before The God in obedience.”

“Qumu,” from the root ق و م, refers to rising and standing, while “qanitin,” from the root ق ن ت, denotes obedience and humble submission. Together, these expressions connect bodily standing in worship with an inward attitude of servitude. A person stands before the Lord to turn toward Him consciously and humbly, rather than having a body in a posture of worship while the heart and conduct remain far from God’s command.

In the next verse, Al-Baqarah 2:239, this same command continues even amid fear and danger. The connection between the two verses shows that guarding prayer is not confined to ease and tranquility. Even in difficult circumstances, a person must preserve their bond with the Lord, although the form of worship changes to suit their circumstances.

The verse’s message is that the prayers must have a protected place in human life. Worldly occupations, disagreements, and difficulties must not cause them to be forgotten; and standing before God must be accompanied by humility, obedience, and practical faithfulness to His way.
