Al-Baqarah 2:32

They said: Glory be to You; we have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Indeed, You are The All-Knowing, The Wise.

Our Reflection

This verse is the angels' response after the boundary of their knowledge had been made clear. In the previous verse, Adam was equipped with knowledge of the names, and the angels were placed before a reality they could not report. Now their reaction is not denial, argument, or insistence upon their earlier judgment. It is complete humility before the truth.

The expression subhanaka at the beginning of the response is very important. The angels first declare God free from every deficiency, error, lack of knowledge, or lack of wisdom. Before speaking about their own limitation, they purify the divine side from limitation. This is theology of transcendence: The God is not measured by the weakness, estimation, or limited calculation of created beings.

Then they say: la ilma lana illa ma allamtana. This sentence is the height of epistemic humility from the angels. They do not say that they have no role at all, but they make clear that whatever knowledge they possess is not independent from themselves. Their knowledge depends on divine teaching. So when something has not been taught to them, they stop before it and do not claim ownership over the truth.

From this angle, angelic knowledge is not a separate and local possession. It is knowledge whose meaning and function remain alive only while it is connected to the source of divine teaching. This admission keeps their channel of knowledge open. By saying this, they show that the system of divine agency is not built on independence from the source, but on connection, reception, obedience to command, and respect for the boundary of what has been given.

This verse shows that real submission is not only practical obedience; it is also acceptance of the boundary of knowledge. In verse 30, the angels asked. In verse 31, their knowledge-limitation became manifest. In verse 32, they themselves accept that limitation with adab and transcendence. This behavior is the opposite of fisq. The fasiq exits the preserving frame of truth; but when the angels see their boundary, they return into the orbit of truth and preserve the coherence of the system.

The ending of the verse, innaka anta al-alimu al-hakim, completes the structure of their response. The angels do not merely say that God knows. They say His knowledge is joined with wisdom. This means God does not only have more information; He also knows the place of each thing, the time of each thing, the role of each being, and the purpose of each stage. Divine knowledge is not raw and directionless information. It is knowledge joined with order, purpose, proportion, and wisdom.

Here, the attribute al-hakim gives the angels' response deeper meaning. In verse 30, they had seen the danger of corruption and bloodshed, but now they accept that this danger has not been left unrestrained before divine wisdom. In its physical root, wisdom is connected with restraining, making firm, and preventing disorder, as a rein restrains a force from leaving its course. So when the angels say al-hakim, they acknowledge that placing a khalifah on earth, despite the capacity for danger, belongs within a design whose purpose, balance, and restraint are known to The God.

Therefore, verse 32 gives a foundational lesson in the method of knowing: every being, even if pure, commissioned, and given to glorification, only knows according to what The God has taught it. The perfection of the angels is not that they know everything. It is that when they do not know, they stand humbly and return knowledge and wisdom to their true Owner.

Cross-References

  • Al-Baqarah 2:31— The teaching of the names to Adam and the disclosure of knowledge the angels did not possess.
