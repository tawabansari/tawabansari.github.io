Al-Baqarah 2:246

ۖ ۖ ۖ ۗ

Have you not considered the leaders of the Children of Israel after Moses, when they said to a prophet of theirs: Raise up a king for us, so that we may fight in the way of The God? He said: Might you refrain from fighting if fighting were prescribed for you? They said: Why should we not fight in the way of The God, when we have been driven from our homes and our children? But when fighting was prescribed for them, they turned away, except for a few of them; and The God knows the wrongdoers.

Our Reflection

Al-Baqarah 2:243 spoke of a large group who left their homes for fear of death. In this verse, the Quran recounts the experience of another group, from the Children of Israel after Moses, who had been driven from their homes and children and sought to fight.

“أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى الْمَلَإِ مِنْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ مِنْ بَعْدِ مُوسَىٰ”

“Have you not considered the leaders of the Children of Israel after Moses?”

“الْمَلَإِ” refers to the elders, leaders, and influential members of a community. The request therefore came from people who held social standing among their people and could play a role in decision-making and mobilizing them.

“إِذْ قَالُوا لِنَبِيٍّ لَهُمُ ابْعَثْ لَنَا مَلِكًا نُقَاتِلْ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ”

“When they said to a prophet of theirs: Raise up a king for us, so that we may fight in the way of The God.”

They wanted a leader who could organize their community for fighting. Their request shows that numbers and motivation alone are not enough; leadership and coordination also matter for collective action.

“قَالَ هَلْ عَسَيْتُمْ إِنْ كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الْقِتَالُ أَلَّا تُقَاتِلُوا”

“He said: Might you refrain from fighting if fighting were prescribed for you?”

Before accepting their request, their prophet confronts them with a serious question: when what you seek becomes an actual responsibility, will you remain firm in your decision?

This question reveals the difference between declaring readiness and acting upon it. Asking for leadership and speaking of fighting is easy; remaining committed to that decision when its difficulties and costs arrive is another test.

“قَالُوا وَمَا لَنَا أَلَّا نُقَاتِلَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَقَدْ أُخْرِجْنَا مِنْ دِيَارِنَا وَأَبْنَائِنَا”

“They said: Why should we not fight in the way of The God, when we have been driven from our homes and our children?”

They had a clear reason for their request: they had been driven away and separated from their homes and children. The issue here is not territorial conquest or seizing others’ property. Rather, these are people who have themselves suffered harm and seek leadership and fighting to confront the situation imposed upon them.

This is also connected with An-Nisa 4:75, where the Quran speaks of fighting in God’s way and helping men, women, and children who are subjected to oppression.

“فَلَمَّا كُتِبَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْقِتَالُ تَوَلَّوْا إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِنْهُمْ”

“But when fighting was prescribed for them, they turned away, except for a few of them.”

Here the answer to their prophet’s question becomes clear. When fighting was prescribed for them, most of those very people who had spoken confidently of its necessity turned away.

The Quran does not say that everyone did so; “إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِنْهُمْ” shows that a small group remained steadfast in their responsibility. Later in the same account, Al-Baqarah 2:249, Al-Baqarah 2:250, and Al-Baqarah 2:251 make clearer the role of faith and the steadfastness of a small group facing a larger force.

This account reminds us that being oppressed, having a leader, and even asking to fight do not, by themselves, demonstrate readiness or faithfulness to what is right. The principal measure is a person’s conduct when responsibility arrives.

“وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ بِالظَّالِمِينَ”

“And The God knows the wrongdoers.”

The ending of the verse shows that God knows the reality of human conduct. Those who complain of oppression must be careful not to wrong themselves and others by turning away from a responsibility they have accepted.

The message of this verse is clear: claims of steadfastness, requests for leadership, and talk of what is right are not enough; the value of a person’s decision becomes apparent when the time for action arrives. Sometimes a large population turns away from responsibility, while a small group, through faith and perseverance, opens a path to a society’s deliverance and restored dignity.
