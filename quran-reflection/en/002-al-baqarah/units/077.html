Al-Baqarah 2:77

Do they not know that The God (Allah) knows what they keep secret (yusirrun) and what they make public (yu'linun)?

Our Reflection

This verse is short, but its blow is very heavy. In the previous verse, a group in private was worried that what The God had opened for them might be reported to the believers and that the same truth might become proof against them before their Lord. Now the Qur'an breaks that hidden logic with one question: do they not know that The God knows what they keep secret and what they make public?

The verse does not say that people know all secrets. It does not say that the Prophet, or any caller, has the right to issue a final judgment over the hidden intentions of human beings. It does not say that Ahmad or Mahmud, an interpreter or jurist, a preacher or ruler, knows hearts. The Knower of secret and public is The God Himself. This boundary is extremely important. The Qur'an shows the mask of hypocrisy, but final knowledge of what is hidden in human beings belongs to The God.

This verse both exposes those who conceal and restrains other human beings from sitting in the place of The God. If someone outwardly claims faith while privately hiding truth, The God knows. But if someone is inwardly sincere and people judge him with their labels and guesses, The God knows that sincerity too. So the knowledge of The God is not only a threat to the concealer; it is also a refuge for the truthful human being.

The order of the words in the verse is precise: what they keep secret and what they make public. We usually say outward and inward, public and hidden, because the human eye first sees the outward and then guesses about the hidden. But in this verse, the Qur'an places the hidden before the public. In this context, the center of the illness is the private space. The religious appearance is only a covering over that private space.

In verse 76, their outward appearance was the claim that they had believed. But their hidden reality was different: why are you reporting to others what The God opened for you? So the root of the conduct was not in the public; it was in the hidden. The public was a role being played. The hidden was the place of the real decision. For this reason, the Qur'an first places its hand on that hidden layer: The God knows what they keep secret.

This placing of the hidden before the public leads us to a deeper understanding of hypocrisy. Hypocrisy is not only two different statements; hypocrisy is when the outward is separated from the inward, and the inward manages the outward for deception or benefit. In such a state, public speech does not come from a truthful heart; it comes from hidden calculation. The verse says that The God knows that hidden calculation, and then also knows the performance they make public.

Here their contradiction also becomes visible. In the previous verse, they spoke of the matter being before their Lord. That means their language still speaks of a Lord. They do not completely deny the divine court. But their conduct is as though, if they hide truth from people, no document will stand against them before their Lord either.

This is a kind of practical godlessness in the clothing of religiosity. This does not mean that we judge the inward state of individuals. It means that the conduct itself shows such a logic: a person speaks with his tongue about The God and the Lord, but in practice treats The God like an earthly ruler, as though if the documents do not reach people, The God will also remain unaware.

In earthly courts, perhaps a document can be hidden. Perhaps a witness can be silenced. Perhaps the flow of information can be closed. Perhaps if people do not know, no case will be formed. But before The God it is not so. He knows before people know. He knows the secret before speech becomes public. Even before a person can cover himself with religious appearance, the truth within him is present before The God.

For this reason, verse 77 is a direct answer to the foolishness of verse 76. They were afraid that the believers might use what The God had opened as proof against them before their Lord. The Qur'an says: do they not know that The God Himself knows what they keep secret and what they make public? That is, the proof of The God is not dependent on your management of information. The truth of The God does not disappear through your silence.

The verbs of the verse are in the present form: they keep secret and they make public. This is not only a report about one past moment. The verse speaks of an ongoing conduct: a habit of hiding and displaying. It speaks of a system that continually keeps one thing in private and displays another thing outwardly. This is the dual management of religion: one part for people, one part for insiders; one part public, one part hidden; one part for preserving the appearance of faith, one part for preserving power and monopoly.

Here we should not needlessly restrict the verse and say it refers only to what they hid from the Torah, or only to what they showed in their own favor. The near context may include such an example, but the wording of the verse speaks more broadly: what they keep secret and what they make public. The Qur'an has left the phrase open, so we should not narrow it.

This verse is about a general principle: The God knows the hidden and the public. All human beings are included in this principle: Jew, Christian, hanif, Sabian, follower of the Qur'an, scholar, jurist, interpreter, ruler, preacher, and every claimant of faith. No name takes a person outside the knowledge of The God. No title purifies the inward. No religious appearance can hide a polluted private space from the gaze of The God.

But this same principle must not become a tool of accusation. Because The God is the Knower of secrets, the human being has no right to rule over the secrets of other hearts. We can criticize outward conduct. We can examine speech, action, wrongdoing, concealment, distortion, and outward signs. But the final intention and hidden reality of the heart are in the knowledge of The God. This verse is both warning and boundary: warning for the hypocrite, and boundary for those who claim judgment over hearts.

From here it becomes clear that this verse must not become an excuse for stripping faith from all Jews or all People of the Book. The Qur'an itself has not given such permission. In other verses, it states the criterion: whoever believes in The God and the Last Day and does righteous action, his reward is with his Lord. The Qur'an also does not treat the People of the Book as all alike. It mentions among them people who recite the signs of The God, bow in surrender, believe in The God and the Last Day, and hasten in good deeds.

So the verse is not about the inherent condemnation of a people. It is about the danger of concealment in religion. This danger can be among Jews, among Christians, among followers of the Qur'an, and in every community that claims awareness of The God. The circle of hypocrisy is open before all religious names. No people or religion is safe from it by name alone.

In the logic of the Qur'an, the story of those before us did not come for ethnic mockery. The Qur'an turns history into a mirror. If it speaks of a group among the People of the Book, it is not so the audience of the Qur'an may see itself as purer. It is so that the same path of error may be recognized and not repeated. The religious human being is always in danger of seeing sin only in the history of others, not in his own present.

This verse is also connected with concealment. In another verse, the Qur'an censures those who hide clear signs and guidance after they have been made clear for people in the Book. This is the same illness whose root appeared in verse 76: a truth has been opened, but a group does not want that truth to reach people. Verse 77 reminds us that even if you deprive people of knowing, The God is not unaware of your concealment.

Concealment is not merely failing to say a simple report. When guidance should be clear for people and someone hides it, he stands between people and light. Such a person may outwardly regard himself as the guardian of religion, but in reality he is closing the way of the opening of The God. The God opens; he conceals. The God makes guidance clear for people; he keeps it inside closed private circles.

From this angle, verse 77 is short, but it stands at the center of a chain. In verse 75, the subject was distortion of the word of The God after reason. In verse 76, the subject was hiding what The God had opened. In verse 77, the Qur'an announces that hidden and public are both known to The God. And in verse 79, the discussion of writing and attributing speech to The God will become clearer.

So it is not necessary in this verse to reopen the whole discussion of distortion. The axis here is the knowledge of The God. The verse wants to break the root illusion: you cannot hide truth from The God by hiding it from people. You cannot make the private space of hypocrisy appear clean by displaying faith. You cannot cover the secret of power-seeking with the name of religion. The God knows.

Other verses state this truth with even greater intensity. The Qur'an says that people imagined The God did not know much of what they were doing, and that this assumption about their Lord led them to ruin. This is a root of collapse: the human being imagines that some part of his action remains outside the knowledge of The God. That assumption gives the heart boldness: boldness for concealment, boldness for duplicity, boldness for hiding truth.

Elsewhere it says: conceal your speech or make it public; He knows what is within the breasts. That means hidden and public make no difference to the knowledge of The God. The difference belongs to people. People see the public sooner, and may never see the hidden. But The God knows the inner reality of the breasts. Even if a person does not make speech public, and even if he keeps it in his heart, it is not outside the knowledge of The God.

The Qur'an goes even further and says that if you speak publicly, He knows the secret and what is more hidden than the secret. This means the knowledge of The God is not limited only to what the human being consciously hides. Sometimes there are motives inside the human being that are more hidden than the secret; things he himself may not yet have fully faced. But The God knows even that.

So verse 77 calls the human being to truthfulness. Truthfulness means that the public and private of the human being do not split apart. It means a person is not religious in the presence of people while privately calculating against truth. It means that if The God has opened something, he treats it as a trust, not as his property. It means that if a truth becomes proof against him or his group, he does not flee from it.

The lesson of the verse is clear: secrets are in the hand of The God. No human being has the right to consider himself the knower of hearts. But no concealer has the right to imagine that because people do not know, The God does not know either. Faith is not completed by outward display, and hypocrisy does not remain hidden by hiding from people.

The God knows what they keep secret and what they make public. So neither religious appearance is enough for salvation, nor does belonging to a people condemn a person, nor does belonging to a religious name make a person safe, nor does hiding truth remove it from the court of The God. The criterion is truthfulness before The God: in the hidden and in the public.
