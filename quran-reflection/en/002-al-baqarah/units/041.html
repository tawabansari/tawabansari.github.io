Al-Baqarah 2:41

ۖ

And have faith in what I have sent down, confirming what is with you; and do not be the first to cover it; and do not trade My signs for a small price; and Me alone, so have taqwa of Me.

Our Reflection

This verse is the direct continuation of the previous address to the Children of Israel. In verse 40, they were called to remember the favor, fulfill the covenant, and have rahbah of God. Now verse 41 shows one of the main fields of that faithfulness: having faith in what God has sent down, when this new revelation confirms what is already with them.

The setting of this verse must be understood carefully. Muhammad is building a new order in Medina: an order that, after the wars of Aws and Khazraj, tribal ruptures, and the insecurity of Yathrib, seeks to rebuild the city upon security, shared responsibility, and orientation toward The God. In this city, the Muhajirun and Ansar are not the only groups present. Jewish tribes are also part of the reality of Medina. They are People of the Book, preservers of a revelatory tradition, and participants in the social covenant of Medina. Therefore the Qur'anic address to them is not an address to absolute outsiders. It is an address to groups present in the field of Book, memory of revelation, and the social security of Medina.

Wa-aminu bima anzaltu must be read in this context. Faith in the Qur'an is not merely mental assent. The root A M N is tied to security, safety, trust, and becoming at peace. So the call to faith is not only the call to accept a theological proposition. It is the call to enter the divine and social field of security: before this new revelation, which comes from the same source of guidance, do not take a stance of hostility and covering. Recognize it in relation to your own Book, and stand in the field of security and truthfulness with the people of faith.

This verse does not say that the Children of Israel must suddenly abandon all of their law and erase their scriptural identity. Other verses show that the Qur'an does not declare the Torah and Gospel completely void. It speaks of guidance and light in them, calls the People of the Book to uphold what was sent down to them, and at the same time makes the Qur'an the criterion of confirmation, correction, and clarification of truth. Therefore the call of this verse is first a call to the shared foundations and to faithfulness to the same truth rooted in their own Book: tawhid, justice, not concealing truth, and receiving the divine sign when it becomes clear.

The phrase musaddiqan lima ma'akum is the key to the verse. The Qur'an does not say that what is with you is nothing. It does not say that your Book is entirely false and without light. Rather, it says that what I have sent down confirms what is with you. Ma'akum is very important: what is with you, in your possession, accessible to you, and present in your scriptural tradition. So the Qur'an is not dialoguing with a completely lost and absent Book. It speaks with a reality present among the People of the Book, although that reality needs confirmation, correction, purification from concealment, and return to the divine criterion.

Musaddiqan comes from the root Ṣ D Q, a root tied to truthfulness, firmness, and freedom from deviation. Confirmation here is not merely verbal approval. The new revelation aligns with the truthful and firm core of the previous revelation and makes visible and establishes the truth that remains in it. From this angle, the Qur'an does not stand against the truth of the Torah. It stands against distortion, concealment, misreading, and instrumental use of the Book.

For this reason, faith in the Qur'an for the People of the Book should not be understood as betrayal of the Torah, but as deeper faithfulness to the origin of revelation. If the Qur'an confirms the remaining truth in their Book, then denying it out of partisanship, interest, or social fear is in reality a severing from the very truth to which they claim loyalty. The Qur'an does not ask them to separate from God and the Book. It asks them to take their own Book so seriously that when a confirming witness comes from God, they do not cover it.

Then it says: wa-la takunu awwala kafirin bihi, do not be the first to cover it. Kufr, as clarified earlier, is not simply atheism or lack of information. It is covering truth after the sign has reached. This address is very weighty for the People of the Book, because compared with others they have the prior Book, the language of revelation, expectation of message, and recognition of signs. If they cover truth before others, their kufr is not merely denial of a new message. It is covering something they should recognize better than many others.

Awwala kafirin bihi can also be understood this way: the People of the Book must not stand in the front rank of opposition to the new revelation, especially when this new revelation confirms their own Book. Whoever knows the language of revelation, the lineage of prophethood, and the logic of signs carries heavier responsibility if he becomes a pioneer in covering. If the mushrikin of Makkah stood against the Qur'an from unfamiliarity with the Book, the People of the Book should not strengthen that same rank, because they were already familiar with revelation, prophethood, Book, and reckoning.

In the Medinan context, this warning is not only doctrinal; it also has political and social weight. The new community of Medina needed cohesion, faithfulness, and internal security in order to survive. If groups from the People of the Book, who share civic life and covenant with Medina, stand with external enemies and mushrikin against the new community instead of coexisting truthfully with it, this is no longer merely a difference of opinion. It turns the Book and the signs of God into tools of politics, influence, and power. From here the verse says: wa-la tashtaru bi-ayati thamanan qalila.

Wa-la tashtaru bi-ayati thamanan qalila means: do not trade My signs for a small price. The root SH R Y in Arabic belongs to the field of exchange and is one of the words of opposites; it can be used for buying and for selling. In both cases, the core is transfer and exchange: you give something and take something in return. The verse warns: do not bring God's signs into the market of interest.

A small price is not only cash or a small economic gain. Every interest placed against truth is a small price: preservation of religious rank, fear of losing influence, bargaining with enemy powers, approval of tribal elders, political alliance with mushrikin, or maintaining social superiority. Before the signs of God, all of this is small, because the human being exchanges a truth that should save him for a passing benefit.

Here the historical meaning of the verse can also be seen. In Medina, the issue was not only whether the People of the Book privately accepted or rejected the Qur'an. The issue was whether, in the field of the city's security, they would stand truthfully with the emerging community or exchange truth for expedient and political covenants. If they recognize God's signs but prefer alliance with the enemies of Medina, the mushrikin of Makkah, or tribal powers, that is the selling of the signs for a small price.

In the history of Medina, the problem of covenant-breaking by some groups and cooperation with the enemies of the city later led to heavy crises. Historical details must be spoken of carefully, because reports after the Qur'an always require scrutiny. But the Qur'anic principle is clear: breaking trust, cooperating with an enemy against a society with which one shares life and security, and turning truth into a tool of interest are among the great forms of betrayal toward God's signs. From the very beginning, the Qur'an blocks the Children of Israel in Medina from this dangerous path.

The verse ends: wa-iyyaya fattaqun. In the previous verse it said: wa-iyyaya farhabun, have rahbah of Me alone. Here it says: have taqwa of Me alone. Rahbah pointed more to awe and vigilance before greatness and consequence. Taqwa points to practical guarding, ethical watchfulness, and building an inner shield against falling. The root W Q Y means guarding, shielding, and protecting. So taqwa here means: guard yourselves from slipping toward bargaining, concealment, and covering truth.

Taqwa in this verse means: be watchful that fear of people, political interest, religious rank, or tribal alliances do not separate you from truth. The one who has taqwa does not sell truth for a small price. The one who has taqwa does not sacrifice security and truth for daily expediency. The one who has taqwa, when he finds the new revelation confirming his own Book, does not cover it.

Therefore, verse 41 is not a call to erase the People of the Book. It is a call to their truthfulness before their own Book. The Qur'an says to them: if you are truly People of the Book, if you remember God's favor, if you recognize the signs of revelation, then do not show hostility toward the confirming revelation. Do not be the first to cover it. Do not exchange God's signs for a small political, social, or religious gain. And in this field, have taqwa of God alone.
