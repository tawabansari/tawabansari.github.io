Al-Baqarah 2:222

ۖ ۖ ۖ ۚ

And they ask you about menstruation; say: it is discomfort. So keep apart from women during menstruation, and do not approach them until they become clean. Then, when they have cleansed themselves, go to them from the place that The God has commanded you. Indeed, The God loves those who return and those who seek cleanliness.

Our Reflection

In many patriarchal societies, menstruation—a natural phenomenon in women’s lives—has become a pretext for considering women impure and pushing them to the margins. This outlook existed before the Quran was revealed and continued after Islam in some religious rulings.

In Jewish tradition, Leviticus, chapter 15 regards even contact with a menstruating woman or the place where she sits or lies as a cause of ritual impurity.

In some Christian traditions, menstruating women were also barred from participating in sacred rites, as stated in the second canon of Dionysius of Alexandria.

In Zoroastrian tradition, Vendidad, chapter sixteen gives instructions for isolating a menstruating woman and keeping her apart from others.

In the hadith tradition after Islam, too, as in some Christian traditions, women have been prohibited during menstruation from acts of worship such as salat and siyam.

But what does the Quran say in response to these attitudes?

“وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْمَحِيضِ قُلْ هُوَ أَذًى”

“And they ask you about menstruation; say: it is discomfort.”

The Quran’s answer is brief and clear: “هُوَ أَذًى”—menstruation is suffering and discomfort.

Menstruation is one of the natural processes of a woman’s body and may be accompanied by bleeding, pain, weakness, and discomfort. The God has not called it a sin, spiritual impurity, or a sign of women’s inferiority.

The word “أَذًى” is also used in Al-Baqarah 2:196 for physical discomfort affecting the head—a condition for which The God provides a compensatory measure. Here, too, “أَذًى” denotes bodily suffering, not a defect in a woman’s character or faith.

The God then says:

“فَاعْتَزِلُوا النِّسَاءَ فِي الْمَحِيضِ وَلَا تَقْرَبُوهُنَّ حَتَّىٰ يَطْهُرْنَ”

“So keep apart from women during menstruation, and do not approach them until they become clean.”

Does this mean that a woman should be separated from her family, deprived of affection and companionship, or denied the right to worship?

The continuation of the verse makes the answer clear:

“فَإِذَا تَطَهَّرْنَ فَأْتُوهُنَّ مِنْ حَيْثُ أَمَرَكُمُ اللَّهُ”

“Then, when they have cleansed themselves, go to them from the place that The God has commanded you.”

The expression “فَأْتُوهُنَّ” and the reference to the place of intimacy show that the subject is sexual intercourse. The next verse, Al-Baqarah 2:223, continues this same marital context.

The command to keep apart in this verse therefore prohibits sexual intercourse during menstruation; it does not command removing a woman from her family, society, or worship.

This restriction makes sense in light of a woman’s physical discomfort and consideration for her comfort and hygiene. Rather than isolating her because of menstruation, the Quran asks the man to observe the limits of sexual relations during this time.

The two expressions “يَطْهُرْنَ” and “تَطَهَّرْنَ” are also important, since these very words are sometimes used to consider a woman impure and forbid her to worship.

“يَطْهُرْنَ” refers to becoming free of the menstrual condition and the ending of bleeding; “تَطَهَّرْنَ” denotes cleansing oneself afterward. Both expressions occur in the context of resuming sexual intercourse.

The cleanliness required for resuming intercourse does not mean that a woman is inherently impure or deprived of her connection with The God.

Nevertheless, proponents of juristic opinion and hadith have relied on reports and transmitted sayings to attribute further prohibitions concerning menstruating women’s siyam and salat to religion, although the Quran has not prescribed such prohibitions.

Regarding siyam (fasting), there is no Quranic command prohibiting a woman from practicing it solely because she is menstruating. When setting out the rules of siyam, the Quran itself takes people’s physical condition and capacity into account and places a path of ease before them.

In Al-Baqarah 2:184, practicing siyam on other days is stated for the sick. In the same verse, for those who bear siyam with severe hardship, the path of fidyah through feeding a person in need is given:

“وَعَلَى الَّذِينَ يُطِيقُونَهُ فِدْيَةٌ طَعَامُ مِسْكِينٍ”

“And for those who bear it with difficulty, there is a fidyah: food for one person in need.”

In Al-Baqarah 2:185, The God also says:

“يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ بِكُمُ الْيُسْرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ الْعُسْرَ”

“The God desires ease for you and does not desire hardship for you.”

Thus, if a woman is able to practice siyam during menstruation, the Quran has not forbidden her this worship. If pain and discomfort make her ill, she can practice siyam on other days. If she faces incapacity or severe and continuing hardship, the path of fidyah also comes into consideration according to Al-Baqarah 2:184.

The same verse says:

“وَأَنْ تَصُومُوا خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ”

“And that you practice siyam is better for you.”

A woman who is able can therefore practice siyam; and someone who uses a Quranic means of compensation because of illness or actual incapacity should not be blamed.

The God also says:

“فَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَهُ”

“So whoever voluntarily does more good, it is better for them.”

If someone willingly feeds a person in need in addition to practicing siyam on other days, they have done further good; but this voluntary act cannot be turned into a mandatory obligation.

The rulings on illness, human capacity, fidyah, and ease are examined in the article “Siyam in the Quran: Taqwa, Human Capacity, and the Place of Fidyah”.

Regarding salat (prayer), too, the Quran has not identified menstruation as a reason to prohibit worship.

In An-Nisa 4:43, The God speaks of not approaching salat while intoxicated until one knows what one is saying, and also gives the ruling on purification concerning janabah.

In Al-Maidah 5:6, ablution, cleansing from janabah, and the provision of tayammum are stated.

But menstruation is not janabah. Nowhere does the Quran say that menstruation prohibits a woman from establishing salat or performing ritual prayer.

During menstruation, a woman can perform her ritual prayer while observing the purification prescribed by the Quran. Establishing salat and giving zakat do not cease to be her religious responsibilities because of menstruation.

The reality of salat, however, is broader than the movements and ceremonies of prayer.

The God says in Ta-Ha 20:14:

“وَأَقِمِ الصَّلَاةَ لِذِكْرِي”

“And establish salat for My remembrance.”

Establishing salat means establishing and maintaining a continual relationship and an intellectual and moral orientation toward The God—a relationship also connected with the guidance of the prophets, the role of the angels, and bonds with His righteous servants.

In Al-Ahzab 33:56, the Quran speaks of the salat of The God and the angels upon the Prophet. In At-Tawbah 9:103, the Prophet’s salat is described as a source of tranquility for people. These usages show that the meaning of salat cannot be reduced to the movements of ritual prayer alone.

In the believer’s life, too, salat should have lasting effects: remembrance of The God, gratitude, patience, goodness, enjoining what is right, forbidding what is wrong, and care over one’s conduct.

Al-Ankabut 29:45 states:

“إِنَّ الصَّلَاةَ تَنْهَىٰ عَنِ الْفَحْشَاءِ وَالْمُنْكَرِ”

“Indeed, salat restrains from indecency and wrongdoing.”

In At-Tawbah 9:71, enjoining what is right, forbidding what is wrong, establishing salat, and giving zakat are also listed together among the qualities of believing men and women.

These responsibilities are not suspended by menstruation or even bodily janabah. Janabah has purification rules relating to ritual prayer, but it does not mean that a person should stop giving thanks, remembering, doing good, or avoiding wrongdoing.

In Maryam 19:31, Jesus also says:

“وَأَوْصَانِي بِالصَّلَاةِ وَالزَّكَاةِ مَا دُمْتُ حَيًّا”

“And He has enjoined upon me salat and zakat for as long as I live.”

Establishing salat is therefore a living and continuing duty, not a connection severed by a change in a person’s bodily condition.

This continuity becomes especially important during menstruation, because a woman suffering pain and discomfort may need patience, gratitude, tranquility, and remembrance of her Lord even more than on other days.

The God says in Al-Baqarah 2:153:

“اسْتَعِينُوا بِالصَّبْرِ وَالصَّلَاةِ”

“Seek help through patience and salat.”

How could a person, while suffering, set aside the very relationship that should provide help and steadfastness?

The principle of remembrance also makes this continuity clear. Al-Ahzab 33:41 states:

“اذْكُرُوا اللَّهَ ذِكْرًا كَثِيرًا”

“Remember The God abundantly.”

Aal Imran 3:191 also speaks of those who remember The God while standing, sitting, and lying down.

Remembrance of The God is not limited to one bodily condition. Whether active during menstruation or resting in bed because of severe pain, a woman can still remember her Lord, be grateful, and seek His help.

The principle of taqwa also holds in all these circumstances.

Al-Araf 7:201 states:

“إِنَّ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا إِذَا مَسَّهُمْ طَائِفٌ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ تَذَكَّرُوا”

“Indeed, those who practice taqwa remember when a prompting from Satan touches them.”

Taqwa is conscious care over one’s thoughts, conduct, and relationship with The God. A woman should maintain this care during menstruation as well: remain patient in suffering, not forget her blessings, avoid wrongdoing, and continue doing good within her ability.

Salat, remembrance, and taqwa are therefore neither suspended during menstruation nor postponed to another time. They are continuing foundations of a believer’s life, and in suffering the human need for them becomes more apparent.

Performing ritual prayer, however, also depends on physical ability.

A woman who cannot perform the usual movements of prayer because of severe pain, weakness, or illness should not be forced to do something beyond her capacity. The Quran provides three clear principles in this regard.

The principle of capacity: Al-Baqarah 2:286 states:

“لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا”

“The God does not burden any soul beyond its capacity.”

The principle of ease: Al-Baqarah 2:185 states that The God desires ease for people, not hardship. In Al-Maidah 5:6, when setting out the rules of purification, He also says:

“مَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيَجْعَلَ عَلَيْكُمْ مِنْ حَرَجٍ”

“The God does not intend to place you in hardship.”

Al-Hajj 22:78 also denies the imposition of hardship in religion.

The principle of no compulsion and hope in mercy: Al-Baqarah 2:256 states:

“لَا إِكْرَاهَ فِي الدِّينِ”

“There is no compulsion in religion.”

And in Az-Zumar 39:53, He says:

“لَا تَقْنَطُوا مِنْ رَحْمَةِ اللَّهِ”

“Do not despair of the mercy of The God.”

Therefore, if a woman needs rest because of pain and incapacity, she should not be forced or threatened into performing the movements of ritual prayer. The God does not ask of a person more than their capacity, and His mercy is not closed to His servants.

Nor has the Quran prescribed a separate ruling requiring prayers missed during menstruation to be made up.

The broader meaning of salat, the place of ritual prayer, and the continuity of its intellectual and moral responsibilities are examined in the article “Salat in the Quran: Meaning, Establishment, and the Practice of Prayer”.

The God ends the verse with these words:

“إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ التَّوَّابِينَ وَيُحِبُّ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ”

“Indeed, The God loves those who return and those who seek cleanliness.”

Menstruation is not a sin for which a woman needs to repent; nor should cleanliness become a pretext for considering her impure.

Should a woman set aside repentance and return to The God during menstruation? Should she postpone remembrance, gratitude, patience, and taqwa until the bleeding has ended?

If The God loves those who return and seek cleanliness, how can a woman be deprived of remembering and worshiping Him in His name?

Taqwa itself calls upon a person to remember their Lord and remain steadfast on the path of truth in every condition, especially during suffering and hardship. This continual remembrance and care are among the foundations of continually establishing salat.

The meaning of taqwa and its connection with conscious care are examined in the article “What Is Taqwa in the Quran?”.

The verse’s message is clear: menstruation is a natural and sometimes painful phenomenon. During this time, the Quran prohibits sexual intercourse, not siyam and salat. Physical incapacity can make ritual prayer difficult, but it suspends neither remembrance of The God, nor gratitude and patience, nor taqwa, nor the continual establishment of salat. No one has the right, in the name of religion, to deprive women of worship that The God Himself has not prohibited.
