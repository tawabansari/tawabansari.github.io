Al-Baqarah 2:270

ۗ

And whatever expenditure you make or whatever vow you vow, indeed The God knows it; and the wrongdoers have no helpers.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verses, the Quran spoke of the intention, wholesomeness, and value of giving. It now recalls that no expenditure or vow is hidden from God’s knowledge.

“وَمَا أَنْفَقْتُمْ مِنْ نَفَقَةٍ أَوْ نَذَرْتُمْ مِنْ نَذْرٍ”

“And whatever expenditure you make or whatever vow you vow.”

Here, for the first time in the order of Quranic verses, a vow, in the sense of a commitment, is mentioned alongside giving.

In this usage, a vow, from the root N DH R, is a voluntary commitment a person undertakes before God to perform or refrain from an act, binding themselves to fulfill it. For example, in Aal Imran 3:35, Mary’s mother vows her unborn child to service in God’s way; in Maryam 19:26, Mary speaks of a vow of fasting and refraining from speech; and in Al-Insan 76:7, fulfilling vows is described as a characteristic of the righteous. A vow can therefore concern giving wealth, performing a service, or refraining from an act. Its basis is accepting a commitment before God and fulfilling it. In common traditional understanding, a vow is often restricted to a promise conditional on a wish being fulfilled, such as saying: “If my wish is granted, I will give a certain amount in charity.” But limiting vows to such a conditional bargain does not encompass all their Quranic uses, since the examples mentioned in the Quran do not depend on the fulfillment of such a condition.

The Quran then says:

“فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُهُ”

“Then indeed, The God knows it.”

Whether a person gives openly or secretly, and whether they state a commitment before others or undertake it only before God, none of it is hidden from His knowledge.

The verse ends with a brief warning:

“وَمَا لِلظَّالِمِينَ مِنْ أَنْصَارٍ”

“And the wrongdoers have no helpers.”

“Ẓālimīn,” from the root Ẓ L M, refers to those who commit injustice, while “anṣār,” from the root N Ṣ R, denotes helpers.

In this verse’s context, a person should know that giving or making a vow does not provide cover for oppression and wrongdoing. Someone who violates others’ rights through their conduct cannot escape God’s judgment by relying on the outward appearance of good deeds or on others’ support.

The verse’s message is clear: every expenditure and commitment lies within God’s knowledge. Its value therefore depends on sincerity, right action, and respect for rights, not merely claiming to do good or making a vow.
