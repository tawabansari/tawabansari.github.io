Al-Baqarah 2:269

ۚ ۗ

He gives wisdom to whomever He wills; and whoever is given wisdom has indeed been given abundant good; and none take heed except people of understanding.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:268, the Quran set Satan’s promise of poverty against God’s promise of forgiveness and bounty. Now it speaks of one of the greatest blessings capable of transforming human life: wisdom.

“يُؤْتِي الْحِكْمَةَ مَنْ يَشَاءُ”

“He gives wisdom to whomever He wills.”

Wisdom, from the root H K M, is connected with firmness, restraint, and putting matters in their proper place. Wisdom is not merely possessing knowledge and information; it is the ability to discern correctly, weigh consequences, and apply knowledge and resources appropriately.

A person may possess considerable wealth or extensive knowledge, yet lack the wisdom to use them well. Despite great wealth, they may remain caught in anxiety, competition, greed, and dissatisfaction, deprived of peace of mind.

By contrast, a wise person can make more considered decisions with fewer resources, use what they have better, and live a calmer and more fruitful life.

In the preceding verses, we examined how giving does not benefit only others. In Al-Baqarah 2:265, the Quran spoke of “وَتَثْبِيتًا مِنْ أَنْفُسِهِمْ”: through giving for good, a person can also strengthen their will and character.

Conscious giving is one way of cultivating wisdom. Someone who overcomes fear of poverty and excessive attachment to wealth and puts their resources to good use learns that possessions have value not only in being kept and counted, but also in being used rightly.

In Luqman 31:12, God likewise connects His gift of wisdom to Luqman with gratitude and recalls that the benefit of gratitude returns to the person who gives thanks. This connection shows that wisdom is not merely recognizing a blessing; understanding one’s responsibility toward it and using it appropriately also matter.

A thought-provoking example of the importance of wisdom can be found in the story of Iblis.

Although Iblis was in the presence of the Lord, kept company with the angels, and had the opportunity and ability to advance and improve himself and his kind, he lacked sufficient wisdom to benefit rightly from these blessings. With Adam’s creation, he fell into jealousy, a sense of superiority, and arrogance. Instead of directing his abilities and efforts toward his own growth and elevation and that of his kind, he used them for his own downfall and to draw humanity into misguidance and descent.

The Quran then says:

“وَمَنْ يُؤْتَ الْحِكْمَةَ فَقَدْ أُوتِيَ خَيْرًا كَثِيرًا”

“And whoever is given wisdom has indeed been given abundant good.”

The Quran identifies wisdom itself as abundant good because a wise person can discern the right course in different situations and make better use of opportunities and resources.

This good is not limited to spiritual life and inner peace; it can also become apparent in economic and social life.

Sometimes creating substantial wealth does not require considerable initial capital. A simple but well-considered idea, together with recognizing people’s needs and using resources appropriately, can make possible an economic activity whose fruits reach later generations.

For example, companies such as Google, Amazon, and Microsoft began with ideas about meeting human needs and became large organizations with a broad impact on the economy and people’s lives.

These achievements were not the result of an initial idea alone: knowledge, effort, cooperation, and many decisions contributed to their expansion. Yet they illustrate the importance of recognizing an opportunity and turning an idea into useful work.

Wisdom can enable a person to recognize an opportunity for growth and value creation where others see only limited resources.

Of course, becoming wealthy is not in itself evidence of wisdom in its ethical and Quranic sense. How wealth is acquired and used also matters.

In giving, wisdom has another important aspect that may be overlooked at first: the good a person does for others can return to their own life and their children’s lives as collective welfare, ease, and security. Helping with education, treatment, employment, and reducing deprivation makes the society in which everyone lives more capable and flourishing.

The verse ends with a highly significant statement:

“وَمَا يَذَّكَّرُ إِلَّا أُولُوا الْأَلْبَابِ”

“And none take heed except people of understanding.”

“يَذَّكَّرُ,” from the root DH K R, refers to remembering, attending, and taking heed.

“أَلْبَاب” is the plural of “لُبّ,” meaning the kernel, core, and essential, pure part of something. In its human application, the word refers to understanding and the capacity to comprehend and discern.

But the Quran does not identify people of understanding solely by their ability to calculate or accumulate information. It describes them through their willingness to take heed, distinguish truth from falsehood, practice God-consciousness, and follow what is best.

These are some of the truths that people of understanding can grasp: wealth and knowledge without wisdom may become sources of pride and ruin rather than happiness; a considered idea can produce abundant good and wealth; and conscious giving can improve other people’s lives and society, as well as strengthen and develop the giver’s character.

Az-Zumar 39:18 states:

“الَّذِينَ يَسْتَمِعُونَ الْقَوْلَ فَيَتَّبِعُونَ أَحْسَنَهُ أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ هَدَاهُمُ اللَّهُ وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمْ أُولُو الْأَلْبَابِ” — Az-Zumar 39:18

“Those who listen to speech and follow the best of it; those are the ones whom God has guided, and those are the people of understanding.”

The issue, then, is not knowing alone: people of understanding hear the truth, reflect on it, and apply it in their conduct.

In the context of these verses about giving, they recognize that keeping wealth at any cost is not always the most beneficial choice, just as giving wealth is not merely an expense. Right giving can develop a person’s character, improve others’ lives, and create greater good and security in society.

For a fuller study of this expression, see the Quranic root L B B.

The message of this verse is that wisdom is itself a great blessing and a source of abundant good. A wise person does not look only at the amount of their wealth or information, but strives to use them rightly, learn from the consequences of their conduct, and consider individual and collective good in their decisions.
