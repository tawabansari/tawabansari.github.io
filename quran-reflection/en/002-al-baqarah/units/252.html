Al-Baqarah 2:252

ۚ

These are the verses of The God, which We recite to you in truth; and indeed, you are among the Messengers.

Our Reflection

This verse concludes the account of the Children of Israel, the selection of Talut, the testing of the troops’ commitment, the victory over Goliath, and David’s attainment of kingship. Through this account, the Quran teaches us about faith, fitness for leadership, commitment, patience, and the role of human beings in the realization of divine patterns.

«تِلْكَ آيَاتُ اللَّهِ نَتْلُوهَا عَلَيْكَ بِالْحَقِّ»

“These are the verses of The God, which We recite to you in truth.”

“نَتْلُوهَا” comes from the root T L W and denotes bringing in sequence and following. Recitation is not merely reading words; the verses are presented in connection with one another so that their truth and wisdom may be understood. For further explanation, see the study The Quranic Root T L W.

“بِالْحَقِّ” also emphasizes the truth of this recitation. The Quran has not recounted this story for entertainment or merely to record history; through it, the Quran reveals truths about human conduct and divine patterns so that readers may learn from it.

«وَإِنَّكَ لَمِنَ الْمُرْسَلِينَ»

“And indeed, you are among the Messengers.”

The address in “عَلَيْكَ” and “إِنَّكَ” is directed specifically to Muhammad. God emphatically declares his place among His Messengers. The word “mursalin” comes from the root R S L and means those who have been sent.

Just as, in the account of the Children of Israel, their Prophet announced God’s choice of Talut, Muhammad now conveys these verses to the people as a divine Messenger.

Thus, although the verse directly addresses Muhammad, its message and lessons are for everyone who reads the Quran and reflects on its verses.
