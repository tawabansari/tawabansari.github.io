Al-Baqarah 2:166

When those who were followed disown those who followed, and they see the punishment, and all their ties and means of support are severed.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, the Quran spoke of those who take “andad” for God and place their love and loyalty in a position that belongs only to The God. It now shows one consequence of such attachments on the Day of Reckoning: those who were followed disown their followers.

The two expressions “اتُّبِعُوا” and “اتَّبَعُوا” come from the same root, but distinguish the positions of two groups: first, those whom others followed, and second, those who followed. In earthly life, this relationship may rest on love, trust, authority, fear, or hope of protection; but upon seeing the punishment, those who had been others’ sources of support withdraw from their followers.

The phrase “وَتَقَطَّعَتْ بِهِمُ الْأَسْبَابُ” completes this picture of collapse. “Asbab” is the plural of “sabab” and here refers to the ties, means of support, and avenues of reliance that had connected them. Ties that appeared firm in earthly life, and in whose protection people had placed their hopes, can no longer save them on that day.

This verse also connects directly with the command in Al-Isra 17:36, where the Quran forbids following what one has no knowledge of and holds hearing, sight, and the heart accountable. People cannot relieve themselves of responsibility for their religious choices merely by saying that they followed a particular scholar, leader, narrator, or trusted figure. Al-Ahzab 33:67 also speaks of those who, on the Day of Reckoning, attribute their misguidance to obeying their great ones and leaders.

This does not absolve leaders who have led others astray. Both sides are responsible for their own conduct. In Al-Anam 6:164, the Quran also states that no one bears another’s burden of sin. Following others therefore does not remove a person’s responsibility to listen, think, and weigh the truth.

Someone who hands their reason and responsibility over to another in earthly life may discover on the Day of Reckoning that the very source of support disowns them. Faith and human choices must rest on understanding and evidence, not on the hope that someone else will answer before God for one’s decisions.
