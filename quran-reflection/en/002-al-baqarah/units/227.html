Al-Baqarah 2:227

And if they resolve upon divorce, indeed, The God is Hearing, Knowing.

Our Reflection

In Al-Baqarah 2:224, The God commanded us not to make an oath an obstacle to goodness and reconciliation. In Al-Baqarah 2:225, He distinguished idle speech from conscious human decisions. Then, in Al-Baqarah 2:226, He prescribed a four-month waiting period for men who swear to withdraw from their wives and left open the path of return and reconciliation.

The present verse now speaks of another path: deciding on divorce.

“وَإِنْ عَزَمُوا الطَّلَاقَ”

“And if they resolve upon divorce.”

“عَزَمُوا”, from the root ع ز م, indicates a serious and firm decision. This differs from words spoken at the height of anger and emotion. Someone may threaten their spouse with divorce during an argument, only to realize after calming down that they neither wanted separation nor had considered the consequences of their words.

Nevertheless, resolve alone does not mean that a decision is right or wise. A person can make a wrong decision with firm determination. The significance of this word is that the verse concerns a serious decision to end the marital bond, not merely threats and passing words.

In the preceding verse, The God set a four-month period for withdrawal arising from an oath—an opportunity to return, repair the relationship, and make a decision. It now becomes clear that if return and reconciliation do not occur and the man resolves upon divorce, the path of separation is also recognized in the Quran.

This contrasts with the common perception in many societies that divorce is a family disgrace and a social failure. Sometimes a woman and man remain for years in a relationship full of hostility, humiliation, and suffering solely out of fear of relatives’ judgments, people’s remarks, or concern for maintaining the family’s appearance.

But is continuing married life at any cost a virtue? Must a covenant made for tranquility, affection, and companionship be preserved even when it becomes a means of harm and hostility?

In Ar-Rum 30:21, The God presents tranquility, affection, and mercy as signs of the marital bond. If a wife and husband have worked to repair their relationship but found that continuing their shared life repeatedly brings greater suffering and injustice instead of peace and affection, a considered separation can be a way of preserving the dignity of both.

Imagine, for example, a wife and husband who, after years of disagreement and attempts at reconciliation, have concluded that they can no longer have a healthy and peaceful life together. Why should they force themselves and each other to continue such a relationship solely for fear of people’s taunts? A respectful separation may prevent further hostility and give both an opportunity to begin a healthier life.

In An-Nisa 4:35, the Quran sets out arbitration and efforts to resolve marital discord. But in An-Nisa 4:130, it also says that if the two separate, The God will provide for each out of His abundance.

Thus, the Quran neither encourages hasty separation nor regards the forced continuation of a harmful relationship as a virtue. Reconciliation and repair are valuable; but when the path of repair is closed, divorce too can be a responsible decision to end suffering and injustice.

Of course, ending the marital bond does not end the woman’s and man’s moral responsibility toward one another. In Al-Baqarah 2:229, The God sets out the principle of continuing life together honorably or separating with kindness.

This principle reminds us that even if marital affection has ended, respect for human dignity must not disappear. A woman and man can separate without then disparaging each other, exposing private secrets, or attacking each other’s character before friends, relatives, or strangers. Respecting dignity, however, differs from concealing injustice or remaining silent about violated rights. Seeking justice and telling the truth to defend a right must not be equated with revenge and defamation.

We will examine mutual rights and the manner of separation more extensively in the following verses. The purpose of marriage, spouses’ responsibilities, repairing the relationship, and the possibility of fair separation are also examined in detail in the article “Quranic Marriage”.

“فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ”

“Then indeed, The God is Hearing, Knowing.”

The ending of the verse with the two divine attributes, “Sami” and “Alim”, has particular significance.

The God hears people’s words and also knows their intentions and motives. He knows whether a decision to divorce has been made after efforts at reconciliation and with regard for the other person’s rights, or whether it is a means of threatening, taking revenge on, humiliating, or hurting a spouse.

Someone may claim before others that they have tried to preserve the family and now have no choice but divorce, when in fact their own injustice and mistreatment have caused the breakdown of married life. Another person may, after sincere efforts at reconciliation, have realized that continuing the relationship serves neither person’s well-being.

These two situations are not the same. People do not always know the full truth of others’ private relationships and should not judge their conduct and intentions solely because a divorce has occurred. But The God both hears what they say and knows the truth of their intentions and behavior.

The verse’s message is that divorce is not, in itself, a disgrace or a sign of moral failure. A woman and man can strive to preserve their marriage, but if continuing it has reached an impasse, a considered separation can also preserve their dignity, tranquility, and rights. What matters is not merely staying or separating, but the person’s honesty, justice, and responsibility in their decision and conduct.

The God is Hearing, Knowing; just as the bond of marriage should involve commitment and respect, its ending must not be separated from truth, justice, and kindness.
