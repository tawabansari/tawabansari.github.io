Al-Baqarah 2:150

ۚ

Wherever you set out from, turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque; and wherever you may be, turn your faces toward it, so that people may have no argument against you, except those among them who have done wrong. Do not stand in fear of them; stand in awe of Me, so that I may complete My favor upon you, and so that you may be guided.

Our Reflection

The command previously addressed to Muhammad now expands from “your face,” singular, to “your faces,” plural:

“Wherever you set out from, turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque; and wherever you may be, turn your faces toward it.”

First Muhammad takes his direction; then the community walking this path with him adopts the same direction.

This does not conflict with Al-Baqarah 2:148. There, each had a direction, and the shared field of competition was good deeds. Here, Muhammad’s community receives its direction. Accepting others’ directions does not require this community to have none, just as having a defined direction does not authorize imposing it on every prescribed way.

The verse now reveals the reason for this shared orientation:

“So that people may have no argument against you.”

The Qur’an does not say “the People of the Book.” It says “people.”

The issue is therefore broader than debate with one religious community. It concerns this community’s standing before people generally. A community identifying with Abraham’s way must be sufficiently clear and independent in its practical orientation that no contradiction in its position remains available as an argument against it.

An argument or proof (ḥujjah) belongs to the root Ḥ J J. The issue is more than objection, mockery, or opposition: it is something another party presents as evidence against you. The aim is therefore not to please everyone or silence every opposing voice, but to leave no basis for a justified, reasoned objection.

Here the Qur’an distinguishes between an argument and continued opposition:

“Except those among them who have done wrong.”

Removing grounds for an argument does not mean ending every objection. Some may continue opposing even after the matter has become clear. Continued objection does not necessarily establish that an argument remains.

“Among them” refers back to “people.” Again, the verse does not confine wrongful opposition to one religious label. Among people are those who respond fairly to evidence and those who persist from a position of injustice.

After drawing this distinction, the verse gives its central command:

“Do not stand in fear of them; stand in awe of Me.”

The two verbs belong to the root KH SH Y. This is distinct from KH SH ʿ, the root associated with humble submission. These two families must not be confused etymologically.

In Qur’anic usage, this fear or awe is often more than raw fear. Fatir 35:28 connects it with knowledge. Al-Ahzab 33:39 says that those conveying God’s messages stand in awe of Him and fear no one else. Al-Ma’idah 5:44 directly forbids fearing people and commands awe of God.

Here, “Do not stand in fear of them” means that people’s reactions, judgments, objections, and pressure must not determine your direction.

This does not mean disregarding people. Immediately before this, the verse says to act so that people have no argument against you. Together, the two statements establish a precise balance:

Be accountable to people,
but do not become captive to their judgment.

Leave no contradiction that can become an argument against you,
but do not abandon a truth made clear to you merely to escape people’s objections.

The preceding verses followed the same course. Al-Baqarah 2:120 warned against letting groups’ approval determine the prophet’s direction. Verse 145 condemned following desires after knowledge had arrived. Now the issue appears from another angle: neither people’s wishes nor fear of them should replace truth.

The connection between awe and the next statement also matters:

“And so that I may complete My favor upon you.”

Al-Ma’idah 5:3 has a closely related structure: it first forbids fearing opponents and commands awe of God, then speaks of religion being perfected and favor completed. This correspondence suggests that completing favor involves more than possessing a rule or ritual. The community must mature enough to take its direction from truth rather than fear of others’ reactions.

Religious independence means more than having a separate direction of prayer. It becomes meaningful when people recognize truth, have a defensible case before others, and do not fear public pressure so much that they change direction to secure others’ approval.

Then comes:

“So that you may be guided.”

This ending matters greatly. The Sacred Mosque itself is not the ultimate goal; direction is part of the path of guidance.

If the community preserves only the geographical direction while losing its meaning, the form remains but the verse’s purpose fades. A direction connected with Abraham, Ishmael, surrender to the one God, and breaking ethnic monopolies must not itself become the banner of a new monopoly.

Al-Baqarah 2:125 describes the House as a place of return for people. Al-Hajj 22:25 likewise identifies the Sacred Mosque as being for people, granting no inherent privilege to residents over those arriving from elsewhere. Al-Hajj 22:27 extends Abraham’s proclamation of pilgrimage to people. These connections help explain why this verse also states its reason in terms of people: the Abrahamic House must not become the spiritual property of one ethnicity or sect.

The connections between the direction of prayer, orientation, devotion to God alone, and Abraham’s way are examined in Qiblah, Wajh, and Ḥanīf in the Qur’an. The House as a center for people, its relationship to Abraham and Ishmael, and the different rites of communities are explored in Hajj in the Qur’an.

The change in direction thus establishes three things together: a clear orientation, accountability before people, and freedom from their psychological domination.

Muhammad’s community must live so that people have no argument against it, but it is not meant to live in pursuit of everyone’s approval.

Those who present reasons should receive answers. Those who persist in injustice after grounds for an argument have been removed must not become the community’s authority for decisions.

The heart of the verse lies in this balance:

“Do not stand in fear of them; stand in awe of Me.”

The intended result is more than an independent direction of prayer:

“So that you may be guided.”

The goal is guidance.
