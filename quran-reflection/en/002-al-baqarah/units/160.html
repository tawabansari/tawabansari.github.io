Al-Baqarah 2:160

ۚ

Except those who returned, reformed, and made the truth clear; it is to those that I turn with mercy, and I am the One who repeatedly accepts return, the Especially Merciful.

Our Reflection

The preceding verse concerned those who knowingly concealed divine clear signs and guidance after God had explained them for people in the Book. The Quran described them as subject to the curse of God and those who curse. Now the verse begins with “إِلَّا” and opens the way back. Even those who have covered clear truth and deprived others of knowing it can again receive God’s mercy if they turn away from that conduct and repair what they have damaged.

The first condition of this return is “تَابُوا,” from T W B, meaning to return and change direction. Repentance in the Quran is not merely feeling regret or asking forgiveness; it is a real return from a wrong path to the right one. In the context of these two verses, those who turned away from God’s clear explanation in the Book and concealed truth must return to that very truth. Their repentance here is a return from concealment to clarification, and from setting guidance aside to accepting and following what God has made clear in the Book.

But the Quran does not stop at “تَابُوا.” It immediately adds two further actions: “وَأَصْلَحُوا وَبَيَّنُوا”.

“أَصْلَحُوا,” from S L H, denotes reforming and setting right what has been damaged. Someone who has concealed truth and deprived people of knowing a divine ruling or guidance cannot merely regret it inwardly and disregard the effects of past conduct. As far as they can, they must repair what their actions have harmed. If they have led people onto a wrong path, they must work to bring them back to the right one; if concealing truth has violated someone’s rights, they must act to make amends.

“بَيَّنُوا” comes from B Y N, the same root as “الْبَيِّنَاتِ” and “بَيَّنَّاهُ” in the preceding verse. This linguistic connection is especially important. In Al-Baqarah 2:159, God says that He has made truth clear for people in the Book, but some conceal it. Now those same people are called to disclose truth as part of their return. What they concealed must be explained again. Repairing concealment therefore requires not only ending secrecy but also disclosing the very truth withheld from people.

Together, these three verbs show how to return from concealing God’s verses: a person first turns away from the wrong path, then repairs what their conduct damaged, and finally makes the concealed truth clear. Repentance without reform can remain a claim, while reform without disclosing truth may fail to repair the harm done to people’s understanding. This is why return, reform, and clarification appear together in this verse.

This criterion reveals a greater responsibility for holders of religious knowledge and authority. If a scholar concealed God’s clear verses to preserve a narration, fatwa, or religious ruling, or helped set those verses aside, expressing regret alone does not complete their return. They must correct what they wrongly taught people and make the hidden truth clear to them again. Someone whose words or rulings have affected others’ lives is also responsible for addressing their effects.

After these three actions, God says: “فَأُولَٰئِكَ أَتُوبُ عَلَيْهِمْ”; it is to those that I turn.

Here lies a subtle point about repentance itself. The Quran attributes the root T W B both to human beings and to God. People “tabu,” and God says “atubu alayhim.” But the two uses differ according to their subject. Human beings return from error and deviation; God is not a wrongdoer repenting of His own error. God’s turning toward a servant means opening the way of mercy, accepting their return, and providing the conditions for their guidance and reform.

The distinction between these directions also appears in the expressions themselves. When the Quran speaks of human repentance toward God, verses such as An-Nur 24:31 use “تُوبُوا إِلَى اللَّهِ”: return to God. When it attributes this turning to God, it uses “تَابَ عَلَيْهِمْ” or “أَتُوبُ عَلَيْهِمْ”: God turns toward them with mercy.

This two-way relationship should not be restricted to a rigid sequence in which people always return first and God only responds afterward. In At-Tawbah 9:118, the Quran says that God turned toward His servants so that they might repent. Divine mercy can therefore itself initiate and open the path of human return. People respond to this opening by changing direction and reforming their conduct. Repentance in this sense is a living relationship between human beings and their Lord: their return to God and His turning toward them with mercy and guidance.

The verse’s ending clarifies this relationship through two divine attributes: “وَأَنَا التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ”.

“Al-Tawwab” comes from the same root T W B and, of God, denotes the abundance and continuity of His acceptance of return. God does not open the way back only once; He repeatedly accepts return. “Al-Rahim” connects this acceptance with His special mercy. The emphasis of “wa-ana” at the beginning draws attention back to God Himself, from whom acceptance of return and mercy come.

This also clarifies the connection with the preceding verse. In Al-Baqarah 2:159, knowingly concealing the clear signs was associated with cursing and distance from mercy. In Al-Baqarah 2:160, return, reform, and disclosure of truth open the way for divine mercy to turn toward a person again. These two conditions are connected with divine patterns of guidance and misguidance. Someone who continually conceals truth distances themselves from guidance, but when they turn away from that course and reform their conduct, they no longer remain on the same path. For a broader examination, see “Cursing in the Quran: Exclusion from Divine Mercy”.

This verse shows that even after knowingly concealing truth, the way back is not closed. But a real return must involve reform and clarification. The same Book in which truth was made clear for people is also the criterion for return and reform. Those who turned away from it must return to its truth, and those who hid it from people must disclose it again.

God has opened the way of mercy for those who return. But someone who has concealed truth from people cannot shed the responsibility of reform and disclosure merely by claiming repentance.
