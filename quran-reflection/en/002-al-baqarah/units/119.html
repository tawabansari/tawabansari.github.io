Al-Baqarah 2:119

ۖ

Indeed, We have sent you with the truth, as a bearer of good news and a warner; you will not be called to account for the companions of the blazing Fire.

Our Reflection

The preceding verse spoke of people who did not consider clear signs sufficient. Before accepting truth, they demanded that God speak to them directly or bring a sign matching their own wishes. Faced with these responses of acceptance and rejection, the present verse makes clear how far the Messenger’s responsibility extends:

“We have indeed sent you with the truth.”

The Messenger does not own people. He has been sent to them with the truth, and his responsibility is to convey the very truth with which he came.

The verse then states his task toward those who hear him:

“As a bearer of good news and a warner.”

Both good news and warning have meaning when the listener can choose. Good news sets a good path and outcome before a person; warning makes them aware of the consequences of turning away. Neither means compulsion. A person must listen, weigh what they hear, and respond for themselves.

Immediately after this description, the verse says:

“You will not be called to account for the companions of the blazing Fire.”

It is not the Messenger’s responsibility to bring every person to faith. If he has conveyed the truth, brought good news, and given warning, whoever then accepts or turns away is responsible for their own choice. The Prophet cannot force faith into someone’s heart; nor does he bear responsibility for the outcome of a choice that another person has knowingly made.

Other Qur’anic verses clarify the same boundary:

“The Messenger’s duty is only to convey the message.” Al-Ma’idah 5:99.

And:

“Your duty is only to convey; the reckoning is Ours.” Ar-Ra‘d 13:40.

The success of the mission therefore cannot be measured solely by the number of its followers. The Messenger must convey the truth faithfully; people’s reckoning rests with God.

Al-Ghashiyah 88:21–22 states both this responsibility and its limit plainly:

“So remind; you are only a reminder. You are not a controller over them.”

And in Yunus 10:99 we read:

“Will you then compel people until they become believers?”

Read alongside Al-Baqarah 2:119, these verses make the boundary of the Messenger’s responsibility clearer. He must speak the truth, but faith cannot be produced by force. His duty is to warn, not to decide in another person’s place.

This also helps us understand the connection with “There is no compulsion in religion” in Al-Baqarah 2:256. Rejecting compulsion does not mean that human choices are unimportant. The Messenger’s very role as a bearer of good news and a warner shows that choices have consequences. Human freedom is not freedom from responsibility: people are free to respond, and precisely for that reason they are responsible for their response. This connection between freedom of faith, the duty to convey, and its boundary with coercion is examined in detail in There Is No Compulsion in Religion; the Messenger’s Duty Is Delivery.

We must therefore distinguish these three things: truth comes from God; conveying it and giving warning are the Messenger’s responsibility; and each person must accept or reject it themselves and bear responsibility for their choice.

The Prophet is not charged with saving people by force. He is charged with conveying the truth clearly and faithfully. After that, each person continues along the path they choose, and their ultimate reckoning is with God.
