Al-Imran 3:18

ۚ

The God bore witness that there is no deity except Him, as did the angels and those possessing knowledge; He upholds justice. There is no deity except Him; He is the Invincible, the Wise.

Our Reflection

This verse speaks of the oneness of The God, testimony to this truth, and the place of justice in His sovereignty. The God Himself bears witness to His oneness, and the angels and those possessing knowledge bear witness to the same truth.

“شَهِدَ اللَّهُ أَنَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ وَأُولُو الْعِلْمِ”

“The God bore witness that there is no deity except Him, as did the angels and those possessing knowledge.”

“شَهِدَ” comes from the root ش ه د, associated with conscious presence, observation, knowledge, and testimony to truth. Testimony is the expression of a truth of which the witness has knowledge.

The God bears witness to His oneness, and the angels and those possessing knowledge testify to the same truth. An-Nisa 4:166 also speaks of the testimony of The God and the angels concerning revelation, and declares the testimony of The God sufficient. Thus, the angels and those possessing knowledge join in testifying to the same truth that The God has declared.

Here, three manifestations of testimony can be recognized: The God’s testimony through creation, whose truth becomes evident in creation and the signs of existence and is also declared through revelation; the angels’ testimony through witnessing, as they have knowledge of divine commands and governance and the events they witness; and the rational testimony of those possessing knowledge, who recognize His oneness through observation, reflection, and understanding His signs.

“أُولُو الْعِلْمِ” means those possessing knowledge. Their mention alongside the angels makes clear the place of knowledge and understanding in testifying to truth. In Aal Imran 3:7, we also encountered those firmly grounded in knowledge, who believe in the whole Book and show humility and steadfastness before truth.

“قَائِمًا بِالْقِسْطِ”

“While upholding justice.”

“قَائِمًا” comes from the root ق و م, associated with standing, establishing, and keeping firm. In this verse, The God is described as “قَائِمًا بِالْقِسْطِ”: the One who establishes and sustains justice.

“قِسْط” comes from the root ق س ط and denotes respect for rights, fairness, and justice. Qist becomes evident in human relationships, judgment, testimony, assessing rights, and giving each person what is rightfully theirs.

The God upholds justice: His sovereignty and judgment rest on truth and justice. Al-Anbiya 21:47 also speaks of setting up the scales of justice on the Day of Reckoning, when no soul will be wronged and human deeds will be weighed correctly.

The Qur’an connects this same principle to human life. In An-Nisa 4:135, believers are commanded to uphold justice and bear witness for The God, even if their testimony is against themselves, their parents, or their relatives. Al-Maidah 5:8 also commands that hostility toward a group must not lead a person away from justice.

In Al-Hadid 57:25, The God identifies the purpose of sending the Messengers with the Book and the balance as enabling people to uphold justice. Thus, recognizing The God as the upholder of justice is directly connected with human responsibility to respect rights and act fairly.

Whoever testifies to the oneness of The God must also remain faithful to truth and justice in their conduct, for bearing witness to a God who upholds justice is incompatible with oppression and violating others’ rights.

Anyone who declares The God free from injustice and praises His justice should also manifest this understanding in their conduct. The connection between tasbih, justice, and human moral responsibility is explored in “Subhan and Tasbih in the Qur’an”.

“لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ”

“There is no deity except Him; He is the Invincible, the Wise.”

The verse concludes by declaring the oneness of The God once again. At the beginning, oneness was the subject of the testimony of The God, the angels, and those possessing knowledge; now the same truth is stated together with the two attributes “الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ.”

“عَزِيز” speaks of the power, authority, and invincibility of The God, while “حَكِيم” speaks of wisdom, firmness, and sound judgment in His commands and governance. These same two attributes also occur at the end of Aal Imran 3:6, alongside the declaration of His oneness.

These attributes have a clear meaning in connection with “قَائِمًا بِالْقِسْطِ”: The God is invincible in power, wise in governance, and the upholder of justice in judgment. No power is superior to Him, and no one can compel Him to oppress or judge unjustly.

Oneness, knowledge, power, wisdom, and justice in this verse all belong to The God. In recognizing and testifying to this truth, human beings are likewise responsible for remaining committed to truth and justice in their speech and conduct.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:6: Declaring the oneness of The God with the attributes the Invincible and the Wise.
  • Aal Imran 3:7: Those firmly grounded in knowledge and their belief in the whole Book.
  • An-Nisa 4:166: The testimony of The God and the angels concerning revelation, and the sufficiency of His testimony.
  • An-Nisa 4:135: Upholding justice and bearing witness for The God, even against oneself and one’s relatives.
  • Al-Maidah 5:8: Testimony based on justice and acting justly even in the face of hostility.
  • Al-Anbiya 21:47: The scales of justice on the Day of Reckoning and the exclusion of injustice toward any soul.
  • Al-Hadid 57:25: Sending the Messengers with the Book and the balance so that people uphold justice.
