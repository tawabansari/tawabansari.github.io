Al-Imran 3:52

ۖ

Then, when Jesus perceived their disbelief, he said: Who are my helpers in the way of The God? The disciples said: We are the helpers of The God; we have believed in The God, and bear witness that we are submitters.

Our Reflection

Jesus came to the Children of Israel with clear signs, confirmed the Torah, lifted some earlier prohibitions, and called people to serve the one Lord. Despite all those clear signs and proofs, Jesus perceived disbelief from some of his hearers.

As we explained in the study of kufr, disbelief differs from ignorance of the truth: a person may recognize the truth yet resist it because of prejudice, interests, or inherited beliefs. This resistance sometimes goes beyond verbal denial and becomes practical opposition to the truth. In Aal Imran 3:54, we will read that opposition to Jesus led to plotting and conspiracy.

When Jesus perceived their disbelief, he asked people for help:

«مَن أَنصارى إِلَى اللَّهِ»

Who are my helpers in the way of The God?

Despite receiving support from Spirit of The Holy and possessing the exceptional abilities described in the preceding verses, Jesus asks people to rise in his support. Divine support does not relieve human beings of responsibility for effort and participation in upholding truth. In Muhammad 47:4, The God explains that, had He willed, He could Himself have overcome the enemies, but He tests people through one another.

The disciples, Jesus’s close companions, replied:

«نَحنُ أَنصارُ اللَّهِ»

We are the helpers of The God.

In the preceding verse, Jesus identified The God as his Lord and the Lord of the people. Now he asks them to help him in the way of that same Lord. By standing beside Jesus, the disciples supported the divine mission, and for this reason called themselves helpers of The God. Their loyalty to Jesus arose from loyalty to the divine mission, not personality worship. In the next verse, they likewise speak together of believing in what The God has sent down and following the Messenger.

The God has no need of human help, but establishing justice, defending the oppressed, spreading awareness, and confronting corruption in society require human effort. Someone who knows that a statement is true and can defend its speaker, yet remains silent in the face of injustice against that person, has missed an opportunity to support truth.

In Al-Hajj 22:40, defending the oppressed and preventing the destruction of monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques are connected with helping The God. Defending people’s freedom of belief and worship, regardless of their religious affiliation, is itself an instance of helping The God.

The God says to those who believe:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنْ تَنْصُرُوا اللَّهَ يَنْصُرْكُمْ وَيُثَبِّتْ أَقْدَامَكُمْ

Muhammad 47:7

O you who have believed, if you help The God, He will help you and make your steps firm.

In Surah As-Saff, the disciples’ response is also presented as an example for all believers:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُونُوا أَنْصَارَ اللَّهِ كَمَا قَالَ عِيسَى ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ لِلْحَوَارِيِّينَ مَنْ أَنْصَارِي إِلَى اللَّهِ ۖ قَالَ الْحَوَارِيُّونَ نَحْنُ أَنْصَارُ اللَّهِ ۖ فَآمَنَتْ طَائِفَةٌ مِنْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ وَكَفَرَتْ طَائِفَةٌ ۖ فَأَيَّدْنَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا عَلَىٰ عَدُوِّهِمْ فَأَصْبَحُوا ظَاهِرِينَ

As-Saff 61:14

O you who have believed, be helpers of The God, as Jesus son of Mary said to the disciples: Who are my helpers in the way of The God? The disciples said: We are the helpers of The God. Then a group of the Children of Israel believed, while another group disbelieved. We then supported those who believed against their enemy, and they prevailed.

Being helpers of The God is therefore not a title exclusive to Jesus’s disciples or the Ansar in Muhammad’s time; it is a responsibility The God has placed upon believers.

The disciples continue:

«ءَامَنّا بِاللَّهِ وَاشهَد بِأَنّا مُسلِمونَ»

We have believed in The God; and bear witness that we are submitters.

Centuries before Muhammad’s mission and the revelation of the Qur’an, the disciples called themselves muslim. How can Islam be regarded as the proper name of the religion of Muhammad’s followers when Jesus’s companions were also muslim?

Islam is the verbal noun of the verb aslama, meaning to submit, and muslim is its active participle: someone who has performed the act of submission. Abraham, Jesus’s disciples, and all who submitted to The God were muslim in this sense.

The Qur’an even speaks of people who were muslim before receiving the Qur’an:

وَإِذَا يُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ قَالُوا آمَنَّا بِهِ إِنَّهُ الْحَقُّ مِنْ رَبِّنَا إِنَّا كُنَّا مِنْ قَبْلِهِ مُسْلِمِينَ

Al-Qasas 28:53

And when it is recited to them, they say: We believe in it; indeed, it is the truth from our Lord; we were submitters before it.

For these people, accepting the Qur’an continued their submission to The God; it did not mark the beginning of their Islam. As we explained in Din, Islam, and Shariah, inheriting the name Muslim from one’s parents is not the same as performing the act of submission to The God. Each person must themselves submit to the truth that reaches them from The God.

Al-Hujurat 49:14 distinguishes between submission and faith entering the heart. In the disciples’ response, faith, submission, and helping The God appear together. They declared their submission in circumstances where supporting truth could come at a cost. Their Islam was not merely spoken; it also appeared in their readiness to support truth.
