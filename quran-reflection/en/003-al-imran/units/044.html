Al-Imran 3:44

ۚ

That is among the reports of the unseen that We reveal to you. You were not with them when they were casting their pens to determine which of them would become Mary’s guardian; nor were you with them when they were disputing with one another.

Our Reflection

This verse shows that the concept of ghayb is not limited to the invisible world and the Hereafter. It also includes events that occurred in the past and remain hidden from human knowledge. In Hud 11:49 concerning Noah’s story, and in Yusuf 12:102 concerning Joseph’s story, The God likewise uses the expression “أنباء الغیب”.

Muhammad learned of this event through revelation. When recounting this event in the name of revelation, we too must confine ourselves to what revelation states and add nothing without knowledge (Al-Isra 17:36 and Al-Araf 7:33).

In explaining “يُلقونَ أَقلٰمَهُم”, commentators have mostly focused on the details of drawing lots. In later lexicographical sources, including Lisan al-Arab, qalam can also refer to arrows used in drawing lots. However, among the evidence we examined from Arabic before the Quran’s revelation, we found no independent attestation showing that casting pens was an expression for drawing lots.

Perhaps each person seeking guardianship of Mary wrote a petition with his pen, setting out his qualifications to care for her; perhaps they drew lots, or perhaps another custom existed for choosing a guardian. We accept none of these possibilities as the verse’s definitive meaning, because the Quran does not state these details.

We should not fill the Quran’s silence with our own wishes, suppositions, and conjectures and then pass them on to others as religious knowledge. We have examined this subject in detail in When Quranic Silence Is Filled with Reports: Isra’iliyyat, Hadith, and the Inability to Say “We Do Not Know”.

What we understand from the verse’s final expression, “يَختَصِمونَ”, is that there was disagreement and dispute among them over Mary’s guardianship.

Regardless of how they chose Mary’s guardian on that occasion, reason dictates that we should not leave a child’s guardianship—especially that of a young girl—to luck or chance. Rather, we should first establish the taqwa, trustworthiness, ability, and suitability of the person seeking to become the child’s guardian. Caring for a child is a trust, and in An-Nisa 4:58, The God commands that trusts be entrusted to those entitled to them.

In Aal Imran 3:37, we read that The God appointed Zechariah as Mary’s guardian, and in Al-Anam 6:85, Zechariah is counted among the righteous.
