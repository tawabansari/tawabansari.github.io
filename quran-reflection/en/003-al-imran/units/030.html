Al-Imran 3:30

ۗ ۗ

The day every person finds the good they have done present, and wishes, concerning the evil they have done, that there were a very great distance between themselves and it. The God warns you of Himself; and The God is deeply compassionate toward His servants.

Our Reflection

This verse continues the warnings of Aal Imran 3:28 and Aal Imran 3:29. In Aal Imran 3:28, The God warned believers against accepting the authority and protective allegiance of those who stand against truth; in Aal Imran 3:29, He made clear that He knows people's concealed and disclosed intentions. Now He speaks of a day when a person confronts the reality of their deeds. On that day, neither a claim of faith nor concealing one's motives can take the place of actual deeds.

“يَومَ تَجِدُ كُلُّ نَفسٍ ما عَمِلَت مِن خَيرٍ مُحضَرًا”: the day every person finds the good they have done present before them. “Every soul” excludes no one from this encounter, and “muḥḍaran” indicates that their good deeds are brought into their presence. What someone has done is not lost with The God, even if years have passed and they or others have forgotten it.

In Al-Baqarah 2:110, The God likewise says that whatever good people send ahead for themselves, they will find with Him. In Al-Baqarah 2:143, He also emphasizes that He does not let the believers' faith and effort go to waste. No good, however small or hidden from others, falls outside His knowledge and reckoning. In Al-Kahf 18:49, the Quran speaks of people finding what they have done present and realizing that no small or great deed has been left out of the account. Az-Zalzalah 99:7 and Az-Zalzalah 99:8 also describe seeing the outcome of good and evil, even to the weight of a particle.

“وَما عَمِلَت مِن سوءٍ تَوَدُّ لَو أَنَّ بَينَها وَبَينَهُ أَمَدًا بَعيدًا”: as for their evil deeds, the person wishes there were a very great distance between themselves and those deeds. “Amadan baʿīdan” evokes the vast separation they desire, as though wanting to be detached from their past and their wrong choices.

“وَيُحَذِّرُكُمُ اللَّهُ نَفسَهُ” repeats the warning in Aal Imran 3:28. This repetition matters. A person must not imagine that choosing the wrong protector, concealing an intention, or relying on others' power can release them from responsibility for their actions. Their return is to the same God who knows their outward and inward reality and leaves no deed outside His reckoning.

Yet the verse closes with a particularly significant description: “وَاللَّهُ رَءوفٌ بِالعِبادِ”: The God is deeply compassionate toward His servants.

Why, after warning of the Day of Reckoning and the wish to be far removed from evil deeds, does The God describe Himself as “raʾūf”?

The root R A F denotes compassion, kindness, and consideration for another's circumstances. This description occurs in varied Quranic settings and, in several cases, accompanies human hardship, weakness, lapses, or the need for divine kindness.

In Al-Baqarah 2:143, The God recalls the difficulty of changing the qiblah and assures believers that He will not let their faith go to waste; He then describes Himself as compassionate and merciful toward people. In At-Tawbah 9:117, the Quran speaks of the Prophet and those who followed him in the hour of hardship, when the hearts of some nearly swerved; The God turned toward them and recalled His compassion and mercy.

Al-Baqarah 2:207 likewise describes a person who relinquishes personal interests and desires in seeking His pleasure, and ends with this same expression: “وَاللَّهُ رَءوفٌ بِالعِبادِ”. The God has no need for His servants' suffering and treats their circumstances with the utmost kindness.

These uses help us understand the continuity of the three verses in Aal Imran. In Aal Imran 3:28, an exception is made for someone compelled to protect themselves in danger. In Aal Imran 3:29, The God is described as knowing a person's true intention and inward circumstances. Now, in Aal Imran 3:30, His compassion toward His servants is recalled alongside the warning about reckoning.

The God knows the difference between someone who freely betrays truth for personal gain and someone forced by threats and pressure to take precautions to preserve their life. In His judgment, the reality of a person's deeds, intentions, circumstances, and abilities is not hidden. This is consistent with the principle in Al-Baqarah 2:286 that each person's responsibility is within their capacity.

Divine compassion nevertheless does not remove human responsibility. In An-Nur 24:20, His compassion and mercy are recalled in the context of warning against slander and the spread of unfounded statements. In An-Nahl 16:47, the descriptions compassionate and merciful likewise accompany a warning about the consequences of wrongdoing. In Al-Hadid 57:9, The God connects sending clear signs to bring people out of darkness into light with His compassion and mercy.

Thus, divine warning and compassion do not contradict one another. The God makes people aware of the consequences of their actions before the day comes when they will wish to be far removed from their evil deeds. This warning offers an opportunity to reform their conduct in this life, abandon injustice and betrayal, and return to truth.

Aal Imran 3:135 likewise describes those who, after an indecent act or wronging themselves, remember The God, seek forgiveness, and do not knowingly persist in their error.

A person therefore need not wait until the day when seeing their evil deeds fills them with shame and the wish for a great distance from them. Today they have the opportunity to recognize their errors, reform their conduct, and return.

This verse reminds us that no good is lost with The God, and no evil disappears merely because a person forgets it. Yet the same God who warns of reckoning and responsibility is compassionate toward His servants: He knows their circumstances and abilities, places the path of reform before them, and makes them aware of their choices' consequences before the Day of Reckoning.
