Al-Imran 3:9

ۚ

Our Lord, You will surely gather the people for a day about which there is no doubt; indeed, The God does not fail His promise.

Our Reflection

This verse continues the prayer in the preceding verse. After asking for steadfastness in guidance and for mercy, those praying now express their faith in the Day of Reckoning and in The God’s certain fulfillment of His promise.

“رَبَّنَا إِنَّكَ جَامِعُ النَّاسِ لِيَوْمٍ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ”

“Our Lord, You will surely gather the people for a day about which there is no doubt.”

These words remind people that life is not without purpose and that their deeds, good or bad, have consequences. Death does not end human accountability; a day will come when everyone faces the reality of their actions.

In Aal Imran 3:30, God speaks of a day when every person finds their good and bad deeds present. In Aal Imran 3:25, He also makes clear that every soul will be repaid in full for what it has earned and that no one will be wronged.

“إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُخْلِفُ الْمِيعَادَ”

“Indeed, The God does not fail His promise.”

“Al-mi‘ad,” from W ʿ D, is connected with a promise and the time of its fulfillment. God remains faithful to His covenant, and His promise, unlike human promises, is not subject to falsehood, forgetfulness, inability, or breach.

This faithfulness is directly connected with The God’s justice and moral standards. Injustice is not only punishing someone beyond what they deserve; giving less than the reward to which the divine promise entitles them is also injustice. God neither exceeds justice in punishment nor lets anyone’s good reward go to waste.

In An-Nisa 4:40, God explicitly states that He does not wrong anyone even by an atom’s weight, and that when there is a good deed, He multiplies it. In Aal Imran 3:195, He also promises not to let the work of any worker, male or female, go to waste.

There is a further point in the connection between these three verses. In Aal Imran 3:7, those firmly grounded in knowledge believe in the whole Book, while the limits of human knowledge regarding the ultimate interpretation of ambiguous passages become clear. In Aal Imran 3:8, they ask their Lord for help in remaining steadfast in guidance and receiving mercy. Now, in Aal Imran 3:9, they affirm the fulfillment of the divine promise and the Day of Reckoning.

This connection shows that acknowledging the limits of human knowledge does not conflict with certainty in the Lord’s clear promise. People may not know how many unseen matters will unfold, yet they can believe in what God has clearly promised. For the same God who calls people to justice and faithfulness to covenants Himself wrongs no one and never fails His promise.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:7: Those firmly grounded in knowledge believe in the whole Book and recognize the limits of human knowledge.
  • Aal Imran 3:8: Asking for steadfastness in guidance and mercy; the beginning of the prayer continued here.
  • Aal Imran 3:25: Gathering for the Day of Reckoning, full recompense for deeds, and the denial of injustice.
  • Aal Imran 3:30: Finding good and bad deeds present on the Day of Reckoning.
  • Aal Imran 3:194: The repeated affirmation that God does not fail His promise.
  • Aal Imran 3:195: No good worker’s deeds, male or female, are allowed to go to waste.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:281: Returning to God, full recompense for deeds, and accounting without injustice.
  • An-Nisa 4:40: No injustice even by an atom’s weight, and multiplication of the reward for good.
