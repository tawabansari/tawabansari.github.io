Al-Imran 3:42

And when the angels said: O Mary, indeed The God has chosen you and purified you and chosen you above the women of all peoples.

Our Reflection

In Aal Imran 3:33 we read of the selection of Imran’s family, in Aal Imran 3:36 of Mary’s birth, and in Aal Imran 3:37 of her good acceptance and upbringing. Now the standing of the girl whose mother spoke of her being female at birth becomes clear.

The word “اصطَفىٰكِ” occurs twice in this verse. First, The God speaks of choosing Mary and then of purifying her. In the second mention of choosing, however, the preposition “عَلىٰ” is especially significant. The Quran does not say that He chose Mary “from among the women of all peoples,” but that He chose her “above the women of all peoples.” This expression indicates Mary’s distinguished standing in relation to other women.

Mary is not respected solely because she is Jesus’ mother; she is herself a chosen person. The God calls her a woman of truth in Al-Maidah 5:75 and presents her as an example for believers in At-Tahrim 66:12.

Just as we discussed false attributions to the Prophets in the reflection on the preceding verse, another error that has entered narrative sources is altering the standing of the Quran’s chosen figures in favor of figures preferred within a religious tradition.

In Shiʿi narrative sources, including Shaykh al-Saduq’s Maʿani al-Akhbar, page 107, report 1 of chapter 40, a report attributed to Jaʿfar al-Sadiq restricts Mary’s selection to the women of her own time and calls Fatimah the foremost woman among the first and the last generations.

Likewise, in Shaykh al-Saduq’s ʿIlal al-Sharaʾiʿ, volume 1, page 182, report 1 of chapter 146, a report attributed to Jaʿfar al-Sadiq states that the angels addressed Fatimah with the same words addressed to Mary in this verse and presented her standing as higher than Mary’s.

In al-Majlisi’s Bihar al-Anwar, volume 19, page 20, a report is also attributed to Muhammad claiming that The God married Mary daughter of Imran, Moses’ sister, and Pharaoh’s wife to him in Paradise. The same claim is also transmitted in Sunni sources, including al-Tabarani’s al-Muʿjam al-Kabir, report 8006, although Sunni hadith scholars have judged its chain weak. The Quran gives no such report about Mary’s and Muhammad’s lives in the Hereafter.

Respecting Muhammad, Ali, or Fatimah does not require restricting Mary’s standing. Respect for a religious figure should not lead to altering the Quran’s meaning to magnify that figure. In Al-Hujurat 49:13, The God identifies taqwa, rather than family relationship, as the criterion of honor before Him.

Our duty is not to make The God’s words conform to hadiths and reports, but to assess hadiths and reports against The God’s words.
