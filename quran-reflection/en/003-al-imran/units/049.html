Al-Imran 3:49

ۖ ۖ ۖ ۚ

And a Messenger to the Children of Israel: Indeed, I have brought you a sign from your Lord; I fashion for you from clay something in the shape of a bird, then breathe into it, and it becomes a bird by the permission of The God; and I heal the one born blind and the one afflicted with baras, and bring the dead to life by the permission of The God; and I inform you of what you eat and what you store in your homes. Indeed, in this is a sign for you, if you are believers.

Our Reflection

The signs of Jesus’s mission and divine permission

In the preceding verses, we discussed Jesus’s exceptional creation, his connection with Spirit of The Holy, and the source of his knowledge. The Qur’an now speaks of the signs he presented in his mission to the Children of Israel.

In the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:253, we examined how The God bestowed different distinctions and abilities upon His Messengers. Among Jesus’s particular distinctions were clear proofs and being strengthened with Spirit of The Holy. The present verse describes examples of those clear proofs.

Jesus fashioned something in the shape of a bird from clay, breathed into it, and it became a bird by divine permission. He healed those born blind and those afflicted with baras, and brought the dead to life by divine permission.

The use of the verb “أَخْلُقُ” concerning Jesus deserves attention. As we examined in the study of the root KH L Q, creation is not restricted to bringing something into existence from nothing; it also encompasses giving form to something and producing it from existing materials. In Al-Muminun 23:14, The God is also called the best of creators.

Here Jesus gives form to clay and breathes into it, but its becoming a bird takes place by divine permission.

In Divine Permission in the Qur’an, we concluded that divine permission is connected with the order and lawfulness governing creation. In Al-Araf 7:58, even the growth of plants from good soil occurs by the Lord’s permission. Jesus’s exceptional abilities likewise lie within the order of divine creation. Our lack of knowledge of how a phenomenon occurs does not mean that it occurs without order.

In the reflection on Aal Imran 3:46, we discussed the relationship between Jesus’s human existence and Spirit of The Holy, and in the reflection on Aal Imran 3:48, the emergence of his exceptional knowledge and awareness in the cradle. We now encounter further abilities manifested in his human existence.

Some of these same abilities are recalled in Al-Maidah 5:110 among the blessings bestowed upon Jesus by The God. In that verse, The God uses the expression “بِإِذْنِي” four times, emphasizing that these events depend upon His permission.

The present verse, however, mentions another ability not recalled in Al-Maidah 5:110: Jesus informed people of what they ate and what they stored in their homes.

The word “أُنَبِّئُكُم” belongs to the N B A word family and means informing or making someone aware. The subject here is not predicting the future, but informing people of matters hidden from the ordinary knowledge of others.

In the reflection on Aal Imran 3:44, we discussed Muhammad being informed of accounts of the unseen through revelation. In Al-Jinn 72:26 and Al-Jinn 72:27, the Qur’an also speaks of disclosing the unseen to chosen Messengers.

Jesus’s knowledge of hidden matters in people’s lives is further evidence for continuing our investigation into the source of his knowledge and his special relationship with Spirit of The Holy. The Qur’an does not explain how Jesus obtained this information, and we have no grounds for restricting this ability to ordinary human sensory perception.

Although the qualification “بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ” is not repeated after this ability, Jesus introduces all these abilities at the beginning of his speech as a sign from the Lord.

Jesus possessed these abilities not to display power or astonish people, but to present a sign through which the truth of his mission could be recognized. His speech ends with the condition “إِن كُنتُم مُؤمِنينَ”. The existence of a sign and drawing benefit from it through faith are two different matters. As we examined in the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:253, even receiving the clearest signs does not guarantee acceptance of the truth.

Jesus’s mission and the universal scope of divine guidance

One common reading of this verse is that Jesus’s mission was confined to the Children of Israel, whereas Muhammad’s mission was for all humanity.

In As-Saff 61:6, Jesus likewise introduces himself as the Messenger of The God to the Children of Israel, speaking of confirming the Torah and announcing a Messenger who would come after him. But does Jesus being sent to the Children of Israel mean that his guidance and message were confined to that people?

In Ibrahim 14:4, the Qur’an states that every Messenger was sent in the language of his own people to make the message clear to them. It is natural for the delivery of a mission to begin among the people with whom the Messenger lives and whose language he speaks. This does not mean, however, that the guiding value of his message is restricted to those people.

Concerning Muhammad, Al-Jumuah 62:2 also speaks of a Messenger being raised among the ummiyyin. In Ummi and Ummiyyin in the Qur’an, we discussed the meaning of ummiyyin and their position in relation to those possessing knowledge of divine scriptures. In Ash-Shura 42:7, the revelation of an Arabic Qur’an is also connected with warning the people of the Mother of Cities and those around it.

Yet none of these expressions means that Muhammad’s mission was confined to the people of Mecca, the ummiyyin, or the Arabs. In Al-Araf 7:158 and Saba 34:28, the Qur’an clearly states the universal scope of his mission.

Why, then, should identifying the Children of Israel as the recipients of Jesus’s mission mean that his guidance was exclusive to them?

At the beginning of this surah, in the reflection on Aal Imran 3:3 and Aal Imran 3:4, we examined how the Qur’an describes the Torah and Gospel as guidance for people, not only for the Children of Israel. In Al-Anam 6:91, the Book given to Moses is likewise called light and guidance for people.

We also see the universality of divine guidance in the very account of Jesus’s creation. Before Mary became pregnant, the spirit answered her astonishment:

قَالَ كَذَٰلِكِ قَالَ رَبُّكِ هُوَ عَلَيَّ هَيِّنٌ ۖ وَلِنَجْعَلَهُ آيَةً لِلنَّاسِ وَرَحْمَةً مِنَّا ۚ وَكَانَ أَمْرًا مَقْضِيًّا

Maryam 19:21

He said: So it is; your Lord has said: This is easy for Me; and so that We may make him a sign for people and a mercy from Us. And it was a matter determined.

This good news was given to Mary centuries before the revelation of the Qur’an. From the outset, Jesus was presented as a sign for people—“لِلنَّاسِ”—not only for the Children of Israel, and his creation was a mercy from The God.

The God also says of Mary and Jesus:

وَالَّتِي أَحْصَنَتْ فَرْجَهَا فَنَفَخْنَا فِيهَا مِنْ رُوحِنَا وَجَعَلْنَاهَا وَابْنَهَا آيَةً لِلْعَالَمِينَ

Al-Anbiya 21:91

And the woman who guarded her chastity: We breathed into her of Our spirit, and made her and her son a sign for all peoples.

The Qur’an also presents the universal guidance and value of divine missions predating Jesus.

In the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:124 and Imamah in the Qur’an, we examined how The God appointed Abraham an imam for people, not for a particular nation or ethnicity. Centuries later, in An-Nahl 16:123, Muhammad himself was commanded to follow the upright way of Abraham.

The universality of divine guidance was therefore not a feature that emerged for the first time with Muhammad’s mission.

In An-Nahl 16:36, the Qur’an speaks of raising a Messenger in every community and identifies the common foundation of their call as serving The God and avoiding taghut. Differences between the communities in which the Messengers were raised do not mean that the foundation of their message was different.

The Qur’an, too, is not an entirely new message disconnected from earlier missions. As we examined in the reflections on the opening verses of this surah, it confirms the earlier scriptures and serves as a criterion over them. In Al-Maidah 5:48, it is likewise described as confirming and exercising oversight over the earlier scriptures.

We also see this continuity clearly in Surah Ash-Shura:

شَرَعَ لَكُمْ مِنَ الدِّينِ مَا وَصَّىٰ بِهِ نُوحًا وَالَّذِي أَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْكَ وَمَا وَصَّيْنَا بِهِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ وَعِيسَىٰ ۖ أَنْ أَقِيمُوا الدِّينَ وَلَا تَتَفَرَّقُوا فِيهِ ۚ كَبُرَ عَلَى الْمُشْرِكِينَ مَا تَدْعُوهُمْ إِلَيْهِ ۚ اللَّهُ يَجْتَبِي إِلَيْهِ مَنْ يَشَاءُ وَيَهْدِي إِلَيْهِ مَنْ يُنِيبُ

Ash-Shura 42:13

He has prescribed for you of religion what He enjoined upon Noah, what We have revealed to you, and what We enjoined upon Abraham, Moses, and Jesus: establish religion and do not become divided in it. What you call the polytheists to is burdensome to them. The God chooses for Himself whomever He wills and guides to Himself whoever turns back to Him.

Some rulings and responsibilities of a Messenger may relate to the social and historical circumstances of his people, but the foundation of religion and the principles of divine guidance are exclusive to no people. We have examined this in Din, Islam, and Shariah in the Qur’an and The Unity of Religion and Diversity of Prescribed Ways.

A prominent example of the universality of earlier missions is Abraham, who lived centuries before Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad. In Al-Baqarah 2:124, The God appointed Abraham an imam for people, not for a particular nation or ethnicity. Abraham’s guidance was not confined to his own people or lifetime; centuries later, Muhammad himself was commanded in An-Nahl 16:123 to follow Abraham’s upright way. In Aal Imran 3:68, those closest to Abraham are identified as those who followed him, this Prophet, and those who believe. If Abraham’s guidance had been confined to his own people and time, why did The God command a Messenger raised for all people centuries later to follow his way? Abraham’s very standing is clear evidence that the universality of divine guidance did not begin with Muhammad’s mission.

In practice, too, centuries before the revelation of the Qur’an, Christianity had spread beyond the land of the Children of Israel into extensive parts of Europe, Asia, and Africa. Later, with Europeans’ arrival in the Americas, the Indigenous peoples of those lands also encountered the Gospel and Jesus’s teachings. In some parts of the world, the message of the Gospel reached people long before the message of the Qur’an.

This historical reality shows that a message reaching the world’s peoples does not require its Messenger’s presence among every people.

The God has not asked us to turn His Messengers into rivals. As we examined in the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:136 and the principle of believing in all Messengers without discrimination, faith in divine guidance involves accepting all His Messengers and scriptures.

Jesus was sent to the Children of Israel, just as other Messengers delivered the message of The God among particular peoples. But divine guidance is not the exclusive inheritance of any nation, ethnicity, or community.
