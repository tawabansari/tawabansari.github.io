Al-Imran 3:10

ۖ

Indeed, those who disbelieve—their wealth and children will avail them nothing against The God; and they are the fuel of the Fire.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, God spoke of the day when human beings will be gathered and of His certain fulfillment of the promise of accountability. He now warns that, on that day, no one can rely on wealth, children, or family standing to escape the consequences of denying truth.

“إِنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لَنْ تُغْنِيَ عَنْهُمْ أَمْوَالُهُمْ وَلَا أَوْلَادُهُمْ مِنَ اللَّهِ شَيْئًا”

“Indeed, those who disbelieve—their wealth and children will avail them nothing against The God.”

“Kafaru,” from K F R, is connected with covering and denial. In this context, the warning concerns those who recognize truth and knowingly deny it; they are responsible for this choice. We have examined this distinction in “Who is a kafir, and what is kufr?”.

“Tughniya,” from GH N Y, is connected with sufficiency and freedom from need. In this construction it means providing benefit or removing a need or predicament. This phrase is sometimes understood as though people in the hereafter were seeking to bribe God with their wealth or children to escape the consequences of their deeds.

The verse’s central message, however, is individual responsibility for one’s deeds: neither material advantages nor family relationships can replace a person’s own faith and conduct or exempt them from answering for their response to truth.

For example, someone may use their wealth to build a mosque, school, or hospital that benefits people. These services do not in themselves authorize knowingly denying truth or guarantee release from accountability. Their benefit to people is real, but judgment of a person’s actions concerns the entirety of their deeds, intentions, and responsibility. No one can rely on wealth and services to consider themselves exempt from answering to truth.

In Saba 34:37, the Quran likewise makes clear that wealth and children do not in themselves bring a person closer to God; faith and righteous action are the criteria.

A clear example appears in the account of Abraham and his father. Abraham was one of God’s great Prophets, but his position and faith could not replace his father’s own faith and responsibility. In At-Tawbah 9:114, the Quran states that Abraham sought forgiveness for his father, but when it became clear to him that his father was an enemy of The God, he dissociated himself from him.

Thus, even having a son like Abraham, with his prophethood and faith, cannot guarantee salvation for a father who knowingly denies truth.

The reverse example appears in the account of Noah and his son. In Hud 11:45, Noah appeals to his Lord concerning his son and recalls their family relationship. But in Hud 11:46, God’s answer makes clear that this relationship cannot compensate for the son’s wrongful conduct.

Neither a child’s faith and position can remove a father’s responsibility, nor a father’s position save a child from the consequences of the child’s own deeds.

This principle is stated explicitly in Luqman 31:33: no father can discharge anything on behalf of his child, nor can any child discharge anything on behalf of his father.

“وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمْ وَقُودُ النَّارِ”

“And those—they are the fuel of the Fire.”

“Waqud,” from W Q D, denotes the material by which a fire is kindled and kept burning. Through this expression, the Quran warns of the outcome for those who deny truth. Al-Baqarah 2:24 likewise speaks of a fire whose fuel is human beings and stones.

The verse’s message is clear: people must answer before their Lord through their own faith, recognition of truth, and conduct. Wealth cannot buy away their responsibility, nor can a righteous child, a Prophet, or an honored family benefit those who deny truth in this regard. Every person is responsible for what they have knowingly chosen and done.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:9: Gathering people for the Day of Reckoning and God’s fulfillment of His promise.
  • Aal Imran 3:11: The example of Pharaoh’s people and earlier peoples who denied the divine signs.
  • Aal Imran 3:116: The repeated warning that wealth and children cannot avert the consequences of disbelief.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:24: The fire described as having human beings and stones for fuel.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:48: Individual responsibility on the Day of Reckoning and others’ inability to compensate for it.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:124: The divine covenant does not extend to wrongdoers; family ties cannot replace personal worthiness and responsibility.
  • At-Tawbah 9:114: Abraham seeks forgiveness for his father, then dissociates himself when his father’s enmity toward God becomes clear.
  • Hud 11:45: Noah’s appeal concerning his son and his reference to their family relationship.
  • Hud 11:46: God’s answer to Noah and the inability of family ties to compensate for wrongful conduct.
  • Luqman 31:33: No father or child can discharge the other’s responsibility on the Day of Reckoning.
  • Saba 34:37: Wealth and children do not in themselves bring people nearer to God; faith and righteous action are the criteria.
