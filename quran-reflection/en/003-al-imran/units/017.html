Al-Imran 3:17

The patient, the truthful, the obedient, those who give, and those who seek forgiveness in the hours before dawn.

Our Reflection

This verse directly continues the preceding two verses, presenting five qualities of those promised gardens of Paradise and the approval of The God in Aal Imran 3:15. Aal Imran 3:16 expressed their prayer for forgiveness of their sins; the Qur’an now describes the conduct of that same group.

“الصَّابِرِينَ وَالصَّادِقِينَ وَالْقَانِتِينَ”

“The patient, the truthful, and the obedient.”

“الصَّابِرِينَ” comes from the root ص ب ر, associated with holding, restraining, and remaining steadfast under pressure. The patient are those who hold themselves steady in the face of difficulties, fear, personal desires, and pressure from others, without abandoning the path of truth. Patience is not merely enduring suffering; its value depends on the path a person strives to uphold.

“الصَّادِقِينَ” comes from the root ص د ق, which denotes truthfulness, correspondence with reality, and words or claims proving true. Honesty is not only speaking truthfully; it also includes harmony between a person’s faith, speech, and conduct. Someone who claims faith and justice must also uphold them in their behavior. In Al-Hujurat 49:15, the Qur’an connects sincerity of faith with believers’ actions and sacrifices.

“الْقَانِتِينَ” comes from the root ق ن ت, associated in Qur’anic usage with obedience and steadfast commitment to obeying. A qanit is someone who obeys the Lord with humility and commitment. In An-Nahl 16:120, Abraham is described as “قَانِتًا لِلَّهِ”: one who remained committed to serving and obeying The God.

“وَالْمُنْفِقِينَ وَالْمُسْتَغْفِرِينَ بِالْأَسْحَارِ”

“And those who give and those who seek forgiveness in the hours before dawn.”

“الْمُنْفِقِينَ” comes from the root ن ف ق and the verb “أَنْفَقَ,” which in this usage means giving out and putting to use what one possesses. Those who give use their wealth and resources for good and to help others. Human blessings are not limited to money; knowledge, abilities, time, and other resources can also serve what is good. We have examined this concept in the study “The Principle of Giving and the Law of Using Blessings”.

“الْمُسْتَغْفِرِينَ” comes from the root غ ف ر, associated with covering, forgiving, and protecting from the consequences of sin. Istighfar means asking for forgiveness: recognizing one’s faults and need for divine mercy and asking the Lord for forgiveness.

“بِالْأَسْحَارِ” comes from the root س ح ر and is the plural of “سَحَر”: the final hours of the night before daybreak. This group seeks forgiveness from the Lord at that time. Adh-Dhariyat 51:18 describes the same quality in those who practice taqwa.

This description has a clear connection with the preceding verse. In Aal Imran 3:16, they say: “فَاغْفِرْ لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَا”—so forgive our sins. We now see that seeking forgiveness is part of their conduct, not merely something they have said once.

Together, these five qualities show how taqwa becomes evident in human life: steadfastness in truth, honesty in speech and conduct, obedience to the Lord, giving, and seeking forgiveness. Despite their faith and good deeds, such people do not consider themselves without need of divine forgiveness.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:15: The promise of gardens of Paradise and the approval of The God to the people of taqwa, the same group described here.
  • Aal Imran 3:16: The prayer of this group for forgiveness of sins and deliverance from the punishment of the Fire.
  • Adh-Dhariyat 51:17: The people of taqwa are described as sleeping little at night.
  • Adh-Dhariyat 51:18: The people of taqwa seek forgiveness in the hours before dawn.
  • Al-Ahzab 33:35: Patience, truthfulness, obedience, and charity among the qualities of believing men and women.
  • An-Nahl 16:120: Abraham is described as obedient to The God.
  • Al-Hujurat 49:15: The connection between sincerity of faith, action, and sacrifice.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:177: Faith and righteousness joined with giving wealth, keeping covenants, and steadfastness in hardship.
  • Aal Imran 3:135: Seeking forgiveness after sin and refraining from knowingly persisting in it.
