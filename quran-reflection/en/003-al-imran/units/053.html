Al-Imran 3:53

Our Lord, we have believed in what You sent down and followed the Messenger; so write us among the witnesses.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, the disciples called themselves helpers of The God and testified to their faith and submission. Now, addressing their Lord, they make clear the basis of their faith and following: they have believed in what The God sent down and followed His Messenger.

Notice that the disciples refer to the one they follow by the title “Messenger.” The Qur’an’s choice of this title has particular significance, because a messenger is connected with the message and mission entrusted to him by the sender.

Prophethood is a particular standing The God grants to a servant, whereas Messenger is a broader title identifying someone in terms of being sent and the responsibility entrusted to them. In Yusuf 12:50, even a king’s envoy is called a messenger, and in Fatir 35:1 angels are also called messengers. By contrast, Jesus says while in the cradle:

قَالَ إِنِّي عَبْدُ اللَّهِ آتَانِيَ الْكِتَابَ وَجَعَلَنِي نَبِيًّا

Maryam 19:30

He said: Indeed, I am the servant of The God; He has given me the Book and made me a Prophet.

Jesus is a Prophet by virtue of being granted prophethood, and a Messenger by virtue of the mission The God entrusted to him. These same two titles are used of Moses in Maryam 19:51, Ishmael in Maryam 19:54, and Muhammad in Al-Ahzab 33:40.

This distinction does not mean that the duties of Prophets and Messengers are separate; in Al-Hajj 22:52, The God speaks of sending both. The difference lies in the relationship each title expresses. The Qur’an does not identify possession of a book or bringing a new shariah as the criterion distinguishing a Prophet from a Messenger.

Nowhere in the Qur’an does the command “أَطِيعُوا النَّبِيَّ,” or “Obey the Prophet,” occur. In commands that place obedience to Himself and His emissary together, The God uses the title “Messenger”:

وَمَا أَرْسَلْنَا مِنْ رَسُولٍ إِلَّا لِيُطَاعَ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ ۚ وَلَوْ أَنَّهُمْ إِذْ ظَلَمُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ جَاءُوكَ فَاسْتَغْفَرُوا اللَّهَ وَاسْتَغْفَرَ لَهُمُ الرَّسُولُ لَوَجَدُوا اللَّهَ تَوَّابًا رَحِيمًا

An-Nisa 4:64

We sent no Messenger except to be obeyed by the permission of The God. If, when they wronged themselves, they had come to you and sought forgiveness from The God, and the Messenger had sought forgiveness for them, they would have found The God accepting of repentance and merciful.

The authority of a Messenger’s command comes from his sender. If a king sends someone to convey a command or carry out a mission, the authority of that command and the authorization to perform that mission come from the king, not from the messenger’s personality.

During his lifetime, the Messenger teaches people, guides them, and judges between them. In An-Nisa 4:105, the sending down of the Book is connected with the responsibility of judging between people. In Yunus 10:15, the Messenger himself explicitly states that he has no authority to change the Qur’an on his own initiative and follows only what is revealed to him.

In Al-Mumtahanah 60:12, not disobeying the Prophet is also among the conditions of believing women’s pledge, but with the qualification “in what is right.” We have examined the concept of ma‘ruf and the criteria for recognizing it in a separate study.

In At-Tahrim 66:1, we see that even Muhammad’s personal decision to forbid something for himself is questioned by The God. Possessing the standing of prophethood does not turn a servant’s personal decisions into divine rulings. As we explained in the study of lordship, authority to judge and implement rulings differs from an independent right to legislate.

In the disciples’ words, faith in what The God has sent down and following the Messenger appear together. They follow a Messenger who bears divine revelation. In Al-Araf 7:157, faith in the Messenger and supporting and helping him are likewise connected with following the light sent down with him.

As we examined in the reflections on Aal Imran 3:31 and Aal Imran 3:32, following means pursuing a path and guidance, while obedience emphasizes accepting and carrying out a command. Both concepts are connected with the divine mission.

Following the way of the Messengers is not limited to their bodily presence. In An-Nahl 16:123, The God calls Muhammad to follow Abraham’s way, although centuries had passed since Abraham’s life. A Messenger’s death does not mean that his message loses its authority.

Just as human reports about Jesus’s life and sayings do not replace the Gospel sent down by The God, reports attributed to Muhammad cannot take the Qur’an’s place. Obeying the Messenger is a command of The God, but that command does not, by itself, establish the religious authority of reports attributed to the Messenger. We have examined this issue in detail in the separate study Confusing Obedience to the Messenger with Obedience to Hadith and Various Reports.

In Al-Furqan 25:1, the Qur’an identifies itself as furqan: a criterion for recognizing and distinguishing truth from falsehood. As we examined in the reflection on Aal Imran 3:4, in its role as furqan the Qur’an confirms divine truth within the religious heritage of earlier peoples and distinguishes what has been distorted from truth. In Al-Maidah 5:48, the Qur’an is also described as confirming and overseeing the earlier Scriptures. This same criterion must be observed when assessing Muslims’ religious heritage.

The Qur’an must therefore be the measure for assessing reports, sayings, interpretations, and rulings attributed to religion, whether in the past or the future; these sources must not assume the role of furqan over the Qur’an. When we declare a clear Qur’anic ruling abrogated on the basis of a report, impose a meaning contrary to a verse’s text and context, or add to religion a ruling The God has not prescribed, we effectively transfer the role of furqan from the Qur’an to a report. We then justify all this in the name of obeying the Messenger, although the Messenger himself followed revelation and had no authority to change it on his own initiative.

In Al-Baqarah 2:106 and An-Nahl 16:101, abrogating and replacing verses are attributed to The God. How can an authority The God did not give the Messenger himself be conferred on sayings that others have attributed to him? We have discussed this issue in detail in Naskh in the Qur’an: A Critique of Setting Verses Aside in the Name of Abrogation.

The disciples conclude their prayer with this request:

«فَاكتُبنا مَعَ الشّٰهِدينَ»

So write us among the witnesses.

In Surah Al-Maidah, the same request is also quoted from people who, on hearing the Qur’an, recognized the truth of the message sent down to Muhammad:

وَإِذَا سَمِعُوا مَا أُنْزِلَ إِلَى الرَّسُولِ تَرَىٰ أَعْيُنَهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ الدَّمْعِ مِمَّا عَرَفُوا مِنَ الْحَقِّ ۖ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا آمَنَّا فَاكْتُبْنَا مَعَ الشَّاهِدِينَ

Al-Maidah 5:83

And when they hear what has been sent down to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflowing with tears because of the truth they have recognized. They say: Our Lord, we have believed; so write us among the witnesses.

In two different periods, two groups of people recognize the truth of revelation and voice the same request. The Messengers differ, but the source of their missions is one.

In An-Nisa 4:69, The God also promises those who obey Him and His Messenger companionship with the Prophets, the deeply truthful, the witnesses, and the righteous.

As we explained in Who Is a Shahid in the Qur’an?, bearing witness to truth is an important responsibility of believers. The disciples do not stop at recognizing and accepting truth; they ask The God to place them among those who bear witness to it.
