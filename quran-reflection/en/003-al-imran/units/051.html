Al-Imran 3:51

ۗ

Indeed, The God is my Lord and your Lord; so serve Him. This is a straight path.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verses, we discussed Jesus’s exceptional creation, the realization of the word of The God and the command “Be, and it comes to be” in human existence, his special relationship with Spirit of The Holy, and his knowledge and abilities.

Despite all these distinctions, Jesus is neither the son of The God nor His equal, but His servant and Messenger. The first words the Qur’an quotes from Jesus in the cradle, in Maryam 19:30, identify him as a servant of The God.

Now, after describing his exceptional abilities—including bringing the dead to life and healing those born blind and those afflicted with baras in Aal Imran 3:49—Jesus plainly declares that his Lord is the same Lord as that of other human beings. In the expression “رَبّى وَرَبُّكُم”, Jesus does not exempt himself from divine lordship. None of his distinctions changes his standing from creature to Creator.

Consider the sequence of Jesus’s words in the preceding verse and this one. In Aal Imran 3:50, he calls people to mindfulness of The God and obedience to himself, then immediately invites them to serve their common Lord. The same sequence is repeated in Az-Zukhruf 43:63 and Az-Zukhruf 43:64.

As we examined in the reflection on Aal Imran 3:32, obedience to the Messenger concerns his capacity as Messenger. The source of the command is The God, and the Messenger bears responsibility for conveying and clarifying His message.

In the present verse, Jesus clearly identifies the straight path: The God is my Lord and your Lord; therefore serve Him.

For Jesus, recognizing The God as Lord and devoting service to Him is the straight path.

We, too, ask The God each day when reciting Al-Fatihah:

اهدِنَا الصِّرٰطَ المُستَقيمَ

Al-Fatihah 1:6

Guide us to the straight path.

This prayer is not only for finding the right path. As we explained in the reflection on Al-Fatihah 1:6, walking it, remaining steadfast upon it, and returning to the right course after a lapse are also aspects of guidance.

In Al-Fatihah itself, before asking for guidance to the straight path, we praise The God in these words:

الحَمدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ العٰلَمينَ

Al-Fatihah 1:2

Praise belongs to The God, Lord of all worlds.

And we devote our service to Him:

إِيّاكَ نَعبُدُ وَإِيّاكَ نَستَعينُ

Al-Fatihah 1:5

You alone we serve, and You alone we ask for help.

Thus, the same two foundations upon which Jesus places the straight path—acknowledging divine lordship and serving Him—are also expressed in Al-Fatihah before the request for guidance to that path.

In the final verse of Al-Fatihah, the straight path is described as:

صِرٰطَ الَّذينَ أَنعَمتَ عَلَيهِم غَيرِ المَغضوبِ عَلَيهِم وَلَا الضّالّينَ

Al-Fatihah 1:7

The path of those You have blessed, not those who have incurred anger, nor those who have gone astray.

But who are those whom The God has blessed?

In the reflection on Al-Fatihah 1:7, we examined how the Qur’an identifies this group. In Surah Maryam, after recounting the lives of a number of His Prophets and chosen servants—including Zechariah, John, Mary, Jesus, Abraham, Moses, Aaron, Ishmael, and Idris—The God says:

أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنَ النَّبِيِّينَ مِنْ ذُرِّيَّةِ آدَمَ وَمِمَّنْ حَمَلْنَا مَعَ نُوحٍ وَمِنْ ذُرِّيَّةِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْرَائِيلَ وَمِمَّنْ هَدَيْنَا وَاجْتَبَيْنَا ۚ إِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتُ الرَّحْمَٰنِ خَرُّوا سُجَّدًا وَبُكِيًّا

Maryam 19:58

Those were among the Prophets whom The God blessed, from the descendants of Adam, from those We carried with Noah, from the descendants of Abraham and Israel, and from those We guided and chose. When the verses of the Entirely Merciful were recited to them, they fell down in prostration and weeping.

Those who have been blessed are not limited to the Prophets. The Qur’an says in Surah An-Nisa:

وَمَنْ يُطِعِ اللَّهَ وَالرَّسُولَ فَأُولَٰئِكَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنَ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ وَالصَّالِحِينَ ۚ وَحَسُنَ أُولَٰئِكَ رَفِيقًا

An-Nisa 4:69

And whoever obeys The God and the Messenger will be with those whom The God has blessed: the Prophets, the truthful in conduct, the witnesses, and the righteous. What excellent companions they are!

In the preceding verse, An-Nisa 4:68, The God also speaks of guidance to the straight path. Walking the path of those who have been blessed is therefore possible for all human beings.

In Al-Maidah 5:23, the Qur’an recalls two blessed men who called their people to courage and trust in The God. In An-Nahl 16:121, Abraham is described as grateful for his Lord’s blessings; The God chose him and guided him to a straight path.

Jesus himself is also explicitly counted among those who have been blessed:

إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا عَبْدٌ أَنْعَمْنَا عَلَيْهِ وَجَعَلْنَاهُ مَثَلًا لِبَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ

Az-Zukhruf 43:59

He was only a servant whom We blessed and made an example for the Children of Israel.

When we ask in Al-Fatihah to be guided to the path of those whom The God has blessed, we are therefore asking for a path Jesus himself walked and introduces to us in the present verse.

The straight path in Jesus’s time was the same path to which Muhammad and the other Messengers of The God called people. In Al-Anam 6:90, Muhammad himself is commanded to follow the guidance of the earlier Prophets. In Al-Anam 6:161, the straight path to which Muhammad was guided is also connected with Abraham’s way.

This path is exclusive to no nation, religious school, or sect.

Walking the straight path is not accomplished merely by professing faith and performing religious rites. In Al-Anam 6:151, Al-Anam 6:152, and Al-Anam 6:153, avoiding shirk, treating parents well, protecting human life, respecting orphans’ rights, practicing justice, and fulfilling the divine covenant are connected with the straight path. Service to The God must also become manifest in human conduct and relationships with others.

What does the lordship of The God really mean?

The Arabic root R B B is connected with nurturing, maintaining, caring for, governing, and ordering affairs. Rabb belongs to this word family and denotes a lord and holder of authority responsible for nurturing and governing. Arbab is its plural.

When the Qur’an introduces The God as “رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ”, it speaks of an all-encompassing lordship: all worlds fall within His governance, sovereignty, and care.

Moses introduces the Lord in these words:

قَالَ رَبُّنَا الَّذِي أَعْطَىٰ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلْقَهُ ثُمَّ هَدَىٰ

Ta-Ha 20:50

He said: Our Lord is the One who gave each thing its creation, then guided it.

Within creation, the guidance of beings in accordance with their structures and capacities is a manifestation of divine lordship. A seed’s growth into a tree, an embryo’s development in its mother’s womb, and the body’s ability to use nutrients and repair its injuries all occur through the order and lawfulness the Lord has placed in existence.

In Ash-Shuara 26:78, Ash-Shuara 26:79, Ash-Shuara 26:80, Ash-Shuara 26:81, and Ash-Shuara 26:82, Abraham likewise describes different aspects of his relationship with the Lord: creation, guidance, provision, healing, death, renewed life, and forgiveness on the Day of Recompense.

Divine lordship also encompasses human intellectual and moral life. He has given human beings reason, the capacity to know, and choice, and through His Messengers has provided criteria for distinguishing truth from falsehood, moral growth, establishing justice, and ordering life.

One important aspect of accepting divine lordship is therefore recognizing Him as the sole authority for religious legislation. In Al-Araf 7:54, creation and command alike are attributed exclusively to The God.

Associating partners in lordship

As we explained in the principle of divine oneness, shirk is not confined to believing in a creator or object of worship besides The God; choosing a lord other than Him is also a form of shirk.

At the end of Surah Al-Anam, after describing the straight path and devoting human service, life, and death to the Lord of all worlds, The God commands Muhammad:

قُلْ أَغَيْرَ اللَّهِ أَبْغِي رَبًّا وَهُوَ رَبُّ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ ۚ وَلَا تَكْسِبُ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ إِلَّا عَلَيْهَا ۚ وَلَا تَزِرُ وَازِرَةٌ وِزْرَ أُخْرَىٰ ۚ ثُمَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُمْ مَرْجِعُكُمْ فَيُنَبِّئُكُمْ بِمَا كُنْتُمْ فِيهِ تَخْتَلِفُونَ

Al-Anam 6:164

Say: Shall I seek a lord other than The God, when He is the Lord of everything? Each soul acquires only to its own account, and no bearer of burdens bears another’s burden. Then to your Lord is your return, and He will inform you of that over which you differed.

In the Qur’an, we encounter examples of taking human beings as lords in the spheres of political power and religious authority.

Pharaoh said to his people:

فَقَالَ أَنَا رَبُّكُمُ الْأَعْلَىٰ

An-Naziat 79:24

Then he said: I am your highest lord.

Pharaoh claimed authority beyond political rule. When the magicians believed in the Lord of Moses and Aaron, he rebuked them in Ta-Ha 20:71 for believing before he had given them permission. He considered even people’s faith and convictions to fall within his own authority.

In the sphere of religious authority, too, the Qur’an speaks of people who had taken their religious scholars and monks as lords instead of The God:

اتَّخَذُوا أَحْبَارَهُمْ وَرُهْبَانَهُمْ أَرْبَابًا مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ وَالْمَسِيحَ ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ وَمَا أُمِرُوا إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا إِلَٰهًا وَاحِدًا ۖ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ۚ سُبْحَانَهُ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

At-Tawbah 9:31

They took their religious scholars and monks, and the Messiah, son of Mary, as lords instead of The God, although they were commanded only to serve one God. There is no god but Him. Exalted is He above what they associate with Him.

Taking a religious leader as a lord does not require believing that he created the universe, calling him an object of worship, or prostrating before him. If we authorize him to determine what is lawful and forbidden without divine warrant and to impose rulings as religious obligations, we have handed him a right belonging to divine lordship.

The Qur’an clearly describes this kind of associating partners in Surah Ash-Shura:

أَمْ لَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ شَرَعُوا لَهُمْ مِنَ الدِّينِ مَا لَمْ يَأْذَنْ بِهِ اللَّهُ ۚ وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةُ الْفَصْلِ لَقُضِيَ بَيْنَهُمْ ۗ وَإِنَّ الظَّالِمِينَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

Ash-Shura 42:21

Or do they have partners who have prescribed for them in religion what The God has not permitted? Were it not for the word of decision, judgment would have been passed between them. Indeed, the wrongdoers will have a painful punishment.

In the reflection on Aal Imran 3:50, we examined examples of human intervention in religious legislation and the creation of religious prohibitions. We have also explained the Qur’anic foundations of this issue in detail in Halal and Haram in the Qur’an: Divine Legislative Authority and the Danger of Setting Up Partners in Religion.

The Qur’an’s warning about taking Pharaoh, scholars, and monks as lords is not merely for recognizing the errors of earlier peoples. We, too, should not choose shaykhs, muftis, mawlawi scholars, religious authorities followed in imitation, sultans, emirs, or even a consensus of religious scholars as our lords.

If a mufti declares something forbidden without divine warrant, or a sultan forces people to accept a religious belief, they claim an authority The God has not given them. The agreement of thousands of religious scholars upon a ruling does not turn that ruling into divine revelation.

Later in this surah, Aal Imran 3:64 calls the People of the Book not to take one another as lords; and Aal Imran 3:79 and Aal Imran 3:80 reject even serving the Prophets and taking Prophets and angels as lords.

Among the fruits of accepting the lordship of The God are freedom from servitude to other human beings and acceptance of responsibility for one’s own thought, conduct, and choices. As we explained in the reflection on Al-Fatihah 1:5, the Messengers of The God show people the way to serve Him, rather than taking the place of their god and lord.

For the scope and different aspects of lordship, see Lordship in the Qur’an: Nurturing, Servitude, and the Right to Command.
