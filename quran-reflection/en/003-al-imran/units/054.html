Al-Imran 3:54

ۖ

And they devised a plan, and The God also devised a plan; and The God is the best of planners.

Our Reflection

After presenting clear signs and calling people to serve the One Lord, Jesus perceived disbelief on the part of some of his audience and asked for help. The disciples came to his aid by declaring their faith in revelation and following the Messenger. The Qur’an now speaks of the plans of Jesus’ opponents and God’s planning in response to them.

In modern Persian, makr commonly means trickery, deception, and cunning, but in Qur’anic Arabic its range of meaning is broader. Makr is a kind of concealed or indirect planning intended to redirect another person’s actions or prevent them from achieving their purpose, without that person being aware of it.

Tadabbur is reflection on the dimensions and outcomes of matters; tadbir is arranging matters with their outcome in view; and makr is planning that remains hidden from the other party’s awareness. Every instance of makr is a kind of planning, but not every plan is makr.

If an oppressor devises a plan to capture an innocent person, and someone else secretly devises a plan to rescue that person without the oppressor’s knowledge, both have engaged in makr; yet their actions do not have the same moral value. In Fatir 35:43, too, the Qur’an distinguishes makr itself from its moral value through the expression “evil planning,” and connects its return upon those who devise it with God’s unchanging way.

Some opponents of the Qur’an transfer the negative connotations of makr to this verse and accuse God of deception, whereas the Qur’an itself clearly explains this expression in the account of Muhammad:

وَإِذْ يَمْكُرُ بِكَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لِيُثْبِتُوكَ أَوْ يَقْتُلُوكَ أَوْ يُخْرِجُوكَ ۚ وَيَمْكُرُونَ وَيَمْكُرُ اللَّهُ ۖ وَاللَّهُ خَيْرُ الْمَاكِرِينَ

Al-Anfal 8:30

And when those who disbelieved were devising a plan against you to detain you, kill you, or expel you; and they devise plans, and The God devises plans; and The God is the best of planners.

Can planning to kill an innocent person and planning to rescue that person be regarded as morally equivalent?

Notice that God uses the word “خَيْرُ” in both verses. In Yunus 10:21, where the speed of planning is at issue, the word “أَسْرَعُ” is used, but here the superiority of God’s planning is expressed through the concept of goodness. The superiority of divine planning lies not only in its power and effectiveness, but also in its direction, justice, and outcome. As we read in Aal Imran 3:26, God is the source of goodness.

Human beings plan with limited knowledge and resources, without encompassing all the factors and consequences of their actions. God knows human plans, while human beings do not know every dimension of God’s planning.

But who devised plans against Jesus?

The context shows that the planning mentioned here belongs to the opposition to Jesus: those from whom he perceived disbelief. In As-Saff 61:14, too, the Qur’an speaks of the emergence of two groups, of faith and disbelief, among the Children of Israel. The Qur’an identifies the disciples as God’s helpers in this event and does not attribute the hostile planning to them.

In Al-Maidah 5:110, God speaks of restraining the Children of Israel from harming Jesus. Just as in An-Naml 27:50 Salih’s opponents were unaware of God’s planning, in the account of Jesus, An-Nisa 4:157 reports the failure of those who claimed to have killed the Messiah.

Reading these verses together, we understand that Jesus’ opponents devised plans to eliminate him and halt his mission, but the fate of Jesus and his mission was not in their hands. In the next verse, we will read how God speaks of taking Jesus, purifying him from those who disbelieved, and placing his followers above them.
