Al-Imran 3:13

ۖ ۚ ۗ

There was a sign for you in two groups that met: one group was fighting in the way of The God and the other was disbelieving; they saw them with their own eyes as twice their number. And The God strengthens whomever He wills with His help; indeed, in this is a lesson for those possessed of insight.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, God spoke of the defeat of those who stand against truth. Now, by recalling the encounter between two groups, He shows that victory does not depend solely on numbers and apparent strength.

“قَدْ كَانَ لَكُمْ آيَةٌ فِي فِئَتَيْنِ الْتَقَتَا”

“There was a sign for you in two groups that met.”

Many commentators and translators have identified this encounter with the Battle of Badr, citing Aal Imran 3:123 and Al-Anfal 8:41. Yet the verse itself does not name Badr. Moreover, Al-Anfal 8:44 speaks of the two groups appearing few in one another’s eyes, whereas this verse speaks of seeing twice the number. These references are therefore insufficient to establish the specific encounter with certainty.

Some commentators also favor the Badr reading on the basis of historical reports about the time of revelation. The fundamental problem with this method is making understanding of the verse dependent on historical reports. The Quran itself does not present the chronological order of revelation as a necessary criterion for interpretation. Rather, Aal Imran 3:7 emphasizes the authority of the clear foundational verses, while Al-Furqan 25:32 and Al-Isra 17:106 speak of the Quran’s gradual revelation.

On the other hand, the preceding verses concern Pharaoh and those around him. Yet in the account of the Children of Israel’s departure from Egypt, the Quran describes their pursuit by Pharaoh’s army and their deliverance, rather than two groups fighting one another. This identification therefore also presents a difficulty.

In Aal Imran 3:7, concerning clear and ambiguous verses, we learned not to impose our reading upon the verses or claim certainty about a matter whose ultimate reality is unclear to us. Understanding this verse’s message likewise does not require us to determine the name and date of the encounter.

“فِئَةٌ تُقَاتِلُ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَأُخْرَىٰ كَافِرَةٌ يَرَوْنَهُمْ مِثْلَيْهِمْ رَأْيَ الْعَيْنِ”

“One group was fighting in the way of The God and the other was disbelieving; they saw them with their own eyes as twice their number.”

“Fi’ah” denotes a group of people. The expression “yarawnahum mithlayhim” speaks of seeing twice the number. But because the pronouns’ referents are disputed, this phrase alone cannot establish which group saw the other as twice its number, or which group was actually more numerous.

The more important point, however, is that numerical superiority does not guarantee victory. A group may be superior in numbers, equipment, and resources, yet still be defeated.

In Al-Baqarah 2:249, this same principle is stated clearly in the story of Saul and Goliath: how often a small group has overcome a large group by God’s permission.

In At-Tawbah 9:25, God also recalls that, on the day of Hunayn, the believers marveled at their large numbers, but those numbers availed them nothing and they retreated. Even for believers, therefore, greater numbers do not in themselves guarantee victory.

“وَاللَّهُ يُؤَيِّدُ بِنَصْرِهِ مَنْ يَشَاءُ”

“And The God strengthens whomever He wills with His help.”

“Yu’ayyidu,” from A Y D, refers to strengthening and making firm. This expression shows that the outcome of an encounter is not confined to human calculations based on appearances.

In Aal Imran 3:126, the Quran likewise emphasizes that help and victory come from The God.

The God’s will must not be understood separately from the effective laws and patterns that govern creation. Victory and defeat likewise occur within this order, with a role for human choice, effort, planning, and steadfastness—not merely according to the number of forces. We have examined this connection in “Divine Permission in the Quran: The Order of Creation, Guidance, and Human Choice”.

“إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَعِبْرَةً لِأُولِي الْأَبْصَارِ”

“Indeed, in this is a lesson for those possessed of insight.”

“‘Ibrah,” from ʿ B R, is connected with passing through something and reaching the other side. Taking a lesson means going beyond observing an event to understand its message for one’s own life.

Within this verse, “ra’y al-‘ayn” refers to seeing with the eye, whereas “ulu al-absar” refers to those who draw lessons from what they see. Seeing an event is not the same as understanding its message. This connection also appears in Al-Hashr 59:2, where God calls upon people of insight to take heed.

Continuing the preceding verse, this verse’s message is that those who disbelieve may be defeated in this very world, even if they are superior in numbers and resources. Power and numerous forces do not guarantee victory, and people must not mistake apparent power for being in the right.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:7: Clear and ambiguous verses and recognition of the limits of human knowledge in interpretation.
  • Aal Imran 3:11: The example of Pharaoh and those around him knowingly denying the signs.
  • Aal Imran 3:12: A warning of the defeat of those who disbelieve and accountability in the hereafter.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:249: A small group overcoming a large group by God’s permission.
  • At-Tawbah 9:25: The believers’ numerical strength availing them nothing on the day of Hunayn.
  • Aal Imran 3:123: Recalling divine help at Badr.
  • Aal Imran 3:126: Emphasizing that victory and help come from God.
  • Al-Anfal 8:41: Reference to the day two groups met.
  • Al-Anfal 8:44: The two groups appearing few in one another’s eyes during their encounter.
  • Ash-Shuara 26:61: Pharaoh’s forces and Moses’ companions coming within sight of each other during the pursuit.
  • Al-Hashr 59:2: Calling people of insight to learn from the fate of those who held power.
  • An-Nisa 4:75: Fighting in God’s way connected with defending men, women, and children who have been rendered powerless.
