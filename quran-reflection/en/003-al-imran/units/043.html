Al-Imran 3:43

O Mary, be constant in devotion to your Lord, and prostrate, and bow with those who bow.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, The God chose Mary above the women of all peoples; now He calls her to qunut, prostration, and bowing.

The word “اقنُتى” comes from the root q n t and means perseverance in worshipful service with humility. Qunut appearing alongside prostration and bowing shows that this verse concerns elements of practical worship and Salat. In Al-Baqarah 2:238, qunut also appears alongside standing during Salat, and Az-Zumar 39:9 speaks of qunut while prostrating and standing.

In this verse, The God states important elements of prayer, rather than its details. The Quran does not tell us whether Mary performed Salat in all its details in the same way as Muhammad. What we understand from this verse is that establishing Salat was customary among the People of the Book before Muhammad; perhaps even Muhammad’s practical manner of Salat drew inspiration from their worship tradition. Historical evidence shows that standing, bowing, and prostration also existed in the worship of Jews and some Christians. For further study, see Salat in the Quran: Meaning, Establishment, and the Manner of Prayer. The God knows best.

Another important point is the phrase “مَعَ الرّٰكِعينَ”. The same command given to the Children of Israel in Al-Baqarah 2:43 is now addressed directly to a woman, Mary. In Aal Imran 3:37, we also read that Zechariah visited Mary in the sanctuary. In At-Tahrim 66:12, Mary is counted among “القانتین”. Thus, the use of the masculine plural in expressions such as “القانتین” and “الراکعین” does not mean that they refer exclusively to men.

Above all, The God commands Mary to prostrate and bow with those who bow. Those who, in the name of religion and religious law, prevent women from attending mosques or regard their presence there as reprehensible because they are women stand in conflict with the message of this Quranic verse. In Al-Araf 7:31, when speaking of mosques, The God addresses all the children of Adam; in Al-Baqarah 2:114, He condemns those who prevent His name from being remembered in mosques.

Mosques belong to The God (Al-Jinn 72:18); they are not the exclusive domain of men.
