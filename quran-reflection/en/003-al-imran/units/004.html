Al-Imran 3:4

ۗ ۗ

Previously, as guidance for people; and He sent down the Furqan. Indeed, those who disbelieved in the signs of The God have a severe punishment; and The God is invincible, possessing the power of retribution.

Our Reflection

Continuing the preceding verse, this verse presents the Torah and Gospel as Books sent down before the Quran to guide people. The expression “هُدًى لِلنَّاسِ” matters: divine guidance was not confined to the followers of one Messenger or one religious community.

It then says: “وَأَنْزَلَ الْفُرْقَانَ”—and He sent down the Furqan.

“Furqan,” from the root F R Q, concerns separating and distinguishing. By making the boundary between truth and falsehood clear, the Quran serves as Furqan. As we examined in the preceding verse, the Quran confirms the Books present with the People of the Book; this does not mean accepting everything added to them or distorted over time. As Furqan, the Quran affirms the truth remaining in those Books and exposes falsehood. Al-Maidah 5:48 also expresses this by describing the Quran as confirming and “مُهَيْمِنًا عَلَيْهِ”—a guardian over it.

Furqan was not exclusive to the Quran, however. Al-Baqarah 2:53 states that God gave Moses the Book and the Furqan, and Al-Anbiya 21:48 speaks of granting the Furqan to Moses and Aaron. That Furqan was the divine guidance revealed to them, which could distinguish truth from falsehood when conveyed and needed no other Furqan placed above it. Now, however, the Quran encounters Books that have undergone distortion and alteration over time. Its role as Furqan therefore does not invalidate the original earlier revelations; it identifies and affirms divine truth against distorted material.

It then warns:

“إِنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا بِآيَاتِ اللَّهِ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ شَدِيدٌ”

“Indeed, those who disbelieved in the signs of The God have a severe punishment.”

This warning is not directed only at followers of other religions. Followers of Muhammad are not exempt if they recognize the Quran’s clear signs and knowingly act against them.

Some rely on reports and sources outside the Quran to confine the confirmation of earlier Books to their original, vanished copies. By claiming abrogation and setting aside the Torah and Gospel, they seek to make divine guidance the monopoly of the followers of one Book and one community.

When someone understands these verses yet denies the validity of all earlier Books by claiming their absolute abrogation to establish their own religion’s superiority and exclusivity, that conduct is an instance of knowingly concealing and denying truth.

In Al-Baqarah 2:75, the Quran speaks of a group who heard God’s words and knowingly distorted them after understanding them. This warning is not merely a reproach to earlier people: anyone who distorts God’s signs to defend religious prejudice and interests has fallen into the same conduct.

In Al-Maidah 5:47, God calls on the People of the Gospel to judge by what He revealed in their Book. Al-Maidah 5:48 also speaks of different prescribed ways for communities and invites them to compete in good deeds. How can one read these commands and then declare that all earlier Books and ways have been wholly abrogated and invalidated, and that guidance and salvation belong exclusively to our community?

This subject is examined in detail in “Abrogation in the Quran” and “Mu’min or Muslim?”.

Nor should the severe suffering arising from denial of truth be deferred entirely to the Hereafter. People also experience the consequences of their deeds in this world. When we declare earlier Books wholly abrogated, monopolize guidance for our community, marginalize others, and replace truth and justice with religious superiority, we create conditions for division, humiliation, hostility, and conflict, fanning the flames of strife.

In Al-Anam 6:65, the Quran counts being divided into hostile groups and experiencing one another’s violence and harshness among forms of punishment. Ar-Rum 30:41 also attributes corruption on land and sea to what human beings themselves have earned.

Consider, for example, what has been happening in Israel and Palestine. Generations have experienced hostility, war, killing, displacement, and destruction. This war and the suffering arising from it are an example of worldly suffering that people bring about through injustice, prejudice, aggression, and hostility, and that affects everyone, whether Jewish, followers of Muhammad, or anyone else. The oppressor’s and victim’s responsibilities are not equal, of course, but innocent people are not spared the destructive consequences of others’ deeds.

When followers of Messengers who all called people to worship The God, uphold justice, and do good become enemies over religious names and the superiority of their people or community, are not this division, bloodshed, and destruction among the bitterest sufferings people experience in this world?

This is the same danger recalled in Aal Imran 3:105 concerning those who divided and disagreed after receiving clear proofs, for whom there is a great punishment.

The verse ends with “وَاللَّهُ عَزِيزٌ ذُو انْتِقَامٍ.” “Aziz” expresses God’s power and invincibility, and “Dhu Intiqam” His power to exact punishment. No community can secure immunity from divine justice and accountability by relying on the name of its Messenger, Book, or religious school. Nor does this warning authorize people to seek vengeance in God’s name; it recalls that final judgment and punishment belong to The God.
