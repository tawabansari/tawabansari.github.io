Al-Imran 3:37

ۖ ۖ ۖ ۖ

So her Lord accepted her with a good acceptance and nurtured her with a good growth, and appointed Zechariah as her guardian. Whenever Zechariah entered the sanctuary to visit her, he found provision with her. He said: Mary, where has this come from for you? She said: It is from The God; surely The God provides for whomever He wills, without reckoning.

Our Reflection

In Aal Imran 3:35, Imran’s wife vowed the child in her womb to worship and serve The God and asked her Lord to accept the vow. In Aal Imran 3:36, after her daughter’s birth, she named her Mary and placed her and her descendants in The God’s protection. Now the divine response to the mother’s vow and prayer becomes clear:

“فَتَقَبَّلَها رَبُّها بِقَبولٍ حَسَنٍ وَأَنبَتَها نَباتًا حَسَنًا,” meaning “So her Lord accepted her with a good acceptance and nurtured her with a good growth.”

The God did not merely accept the mother’s vow; He accepted Mary herself with a good acceptance and nurtured her well. The repetition of “حَسَن” in “بِقَبُولٍ حَسَنٍ” and “نَبَاتًا حَسَنًا” matters: first the quality of acceptance is stated, and then the quality of upbringing. The child whose acceptance the mother may have worried about because she was a girl was not only accepted, but also received good divine nurturing.

The word “أَنْبَتَهَا” comes from N B T, a root connected with sprouting and growing. In Nuh 71:17, the Quran also speaks of causing human beings to grow from the earth. Here, Mary’s good growth is not confined to her physical development; her faith, knowledge, character, and worthiness also become evident as the story continues. This nurturing did not remove Mary’s will: in At-Tahrim 66:12, Mary herself is praised for her faith, affirmation, and obedience. The mother’s pure intention, The God’s good acceptance and nurturing, and Mary’s own righteous conduct stand alongside one another.

The Quran then says:

“وَكَفَّلَها زَكَرِيّا,” meaning “and He appointed Zechariah as her guardian.”

Mary’s good upbringing was accompanied by the care of a particular person. Zechariah is named among the righteous in Al-Anam 6:85, and Aal Imran 3:44 also discusses the appointment of Mary’s guardian.

The verse continues:

“كُلَّما دَخَلَ عَلَيها زَكَرِيَّا المِحرابَ وَجَدَ عِندَها رِزقًا,” meaning “Whenever Zechariah entered the sanctuary to visit her, he found provision with her.”

The word “كُلَّما” indicates that Zechariah’s visits were repeated. He would find provision with Mary whose origin prompted him to ask:

“قالَ يٰمَريَمُ أَنّىٰ لَكِ هٰذا,” meaning “He said: Mary, where has this come from for you?”

The concept of provision in the Quran extends beyond food and possessions. In our reflections on Al-Baqarah 2:3 and Al-Baqarah 2:254, we also considered how provision can include not only material blessings but also knowledge, talent, intelligence, perceptiveness, wisdom, ability, health, security, peace, and opportunities in life. All of these can be gifts that a person draws upon to live, grow, and do worthy deeds.

Mary replies:

“هُوَ مِن عِندِ اللَّهِ,” meaning “It is from The God.”

Mary identifies The God as the source of her provision. Zechariah was her guardian, but Mary does not attribute her provision to her guardian’s independent power or ownership. Even when blessings reach us through people and natural means, their ultimate source is The God.

She then says:

“إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَرزُقُ مَن يَشاءُ بِغَيرِ حِسابٍ,” meaning “Surely The God provides for whomever He wills, without reckoning.”

This expression connects directly with Aal Imran 3:27. As we examined in our reflection on that verse, “بِغَيرِ حِسابٍ” does not mean that The God’s provision lacks wisdom, order, or governing laws. Rather, divine giving is not dependent on or confined to the limited calculations and means that human beings know. A person cannot calculate in advance every route through which blessings, opportunities, and relief may arrive.

Thus, the story that began with the mother’s vow now moves from Mary’s good acceptance and upbringing to Zechariah’s care and recognition of the source of provision. Mary was not merely a recipient of blessings; by recognizing and attributing provision to The God, she voiced a truth that also prepared the way for her guardian’s own prayer.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:35 — Imran’s wife’s vow and request for acceptance, setting the context for the divine response here.
  • Aal Imran 3:36 — Mary’s birth and naming, and her mother’s prayer for the protection of her daughter and descendants.
  • Aal Imran 3:27 — Provision “without reckoning”: divine giving beyond limited human calculations.
  • Aal Imran 3:38 — Zechariah’s prayer for good offspring after seeing Mary’s provision and hearing her reply.
  • Aal Imran 3:44 — The appointment of Mary’s guardian and the dispute concerning her care.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:3 — Giving from divine provision, connected with the breadth of the blessings placed at humanity’s disposal.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:254 — Using provision and blessings, including knowledge, talents, and abilities, before life’s opportunity ends.
  • Al-Anam 6:85 — Zechariah among the righteous, connected with his care for Mary.
  • Nuh 71:17 — The imagery of causing human beings to grow, connected with Mary’s good growth.
  • At-Tahrim 66:12 — Mary’s own faith, affirmation, and obedience, showing her worthiness and choice within good divine nurturing.
