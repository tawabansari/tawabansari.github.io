Al-Imran 3:48

And He will teach him the Book and wisdom and the Torah and the Gospel.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verses, we discussed the nature of Jesus’s creation, his connection with Spirit of The Holy, and the emergence of awareness in him from the very beginning of his human life. In the reflection on Aal Imran 3:46, we compared the lives of John and Jesus, examining the difference between the judgment granted to John in childhood and Jesus’s awareness in the cradle. In the reflection on Aal Imran 3:47, we also understood Jesus as a manifestation of the command “Be, and it comes to be” and the realization of the word of The God in the material world.

The present verse sheds further light on the aspect of knowledge in this exceptional creation: the source and content of Jesus’s knowledge.

Jesus’s instruction and the source of his awareness in the cradle

When Mary brought her child to her people and faced their accusation, Jesus spoke from the cradle and said:

قَالَ إِنِّي عَبْدُ اللَّهِ آتَانِيَ الْكِتَابَ وَجَعَلَنِي نَبِيًّا

Maryam 19:30

He said: I am the servant of The God; He has given me the Book and made me a Prophet.

وَجَعَلَنِي مُبَارَكًا أَيْنَ مَا كُنْتُ وَأَوْصَانِي بِالصَّلَاةِ وَالزَّكَاةِ مَا دُمْتُ حَيًّا

Maryam 19:31

And He has made me blessed wherever I may be, and has enjoined Salat and Zakat upon me for as long as I live.

The wonder of this event lies not only in an infant’s ability to speak from the cradle, but also in the content of his words. At the very beginning of his human life, Jesus was aware of servitude to The God, the Book, prophethood, Salat, Zakat, and—as we read in the following two verses—his moral responsibility toward his mother, his death, and his resurrection.

Notice also the choice of verb tenses in his speech. Jesus does not say that The God will give him the Book and make him a Prophet in the future. Rather, he expresses the granting of the Book, his appointment to prophethood, and his reception of divine instructions through past-tense verbs.

وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَيَّ يَوْمَ وُلِدْتُ وَيَوْمَ أَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ أُبْعَثُ حَيًّا

Maryam 19:33

And peace is upon me on the day I was born, the day I die, and the day I am raised alive.

In this same speech, birth is expressed with a past-tense verb, while death and resurrection are expressed with imperfect verbs. Although the Qur’an sometimes uses the past tense to express the certainty of future events, even if we understand the granting of the Book and prophethood in this way, our fundamental question remains: how was Jesus aware of these matters in the cradle?

As we concluded in our studies Salat in the Qur’an and Zakat in the Qur’an, the meaning of Salat is not confined to ritual prayer, nor that of Zakat to financial giving. Jesus’s own speech in the cradle is among the Qur’anic evidence considered in both studies. He speaks of divine instructions for his entire life; what matters here is his awareness of their content at the very beginning of his human life.

In our reflections on the preceding verses, we studied the spirit’s appearance to Mary before her pregnancy in Maryam 19:17, the breathing of spirit into her in Al-Anbiya 21:91, and the description of Jesus himself as a spirit from The God in An-Nisa 4:171. This led us to consider the possibility that Jesus was the manifestation of that conscious spirit in human existence.

Now this verse, alongside Jesus’s words in the cradle, brings us to the question: had his instruction begun before his human birth?

In Surah Al-Maidah, when recalling His blessings upon Jesus and his mother, The God says:

إِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ اذْكُرْ نِعْمَتِي عَلَيْكَ وَعَلَىٰ وَالِدَتِكَ ۖ إِذْ أَيَّدْتُكَ بِرُوحِ الْقُدُسِ تُكَلِّمُ النَّاسَ فِي الْمَهْدِ وَكَهْلًا ۖ وَإِذْ عَلَّمْتُكَ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ وَالتَّوْرَاةَ وَالْإِنْجِيلَ ۖ وَإِذْ تَخْلُقُ مِنَ الطِّينِ كَهَيْئَةِ الطَّيْرِ بِإِذْنِي فَتَنْفُخُ فِيهَا فَتَكُونُ طَيْرًا بِإِذْنِي ۖ وَتُبْرِئُ الْأَكْمَهَ وَالْأَبْرَصَ بِإِذْنِي ۖ وَإِذْ تُخْرِجُ الْمَوْتَىٰ بِإِذْنِي ۖ وَإِذْ كَفَفْتُ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ عَنْكَ إِذْ جِئْتَهُمْ بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ فَقَالَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْهُمْ إِنْ هَٰذَا إِلَّا سِحْرٌ مُبِينٌ

Al-Maidah 5:110

When The God said: O Jesus, son of Mary, remember My blessing upon you and your mother; when I strengthened you with Spirit of The Holy, so that you spoke to people in the cradle and in maturity; and when I taught you the Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel; and when, by My permission, you fashioned from clay something in the shape of a bird, then breathed into it and it became a bird by My permission; and you healed the one born blind and the one afflicted with leprosy by My permission; and when you brought forth the dead by My permission; and when I restrained the Children of Israel from you, when you brought them clear proofs and those among them who denied said: This is nothing but manifest magic.

Much of the good news conveyed to Mary before Jesus’s birth is recalled in this verse as blessings realized in Jesus’s life. Here his speech in the cradle is connected with his being strengthened by Spirit of The Holy, while instruction in the Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel is also counted among the blessings bestowed upon him by The God.

Strengthening, from the A Y D word family, is connected with imparting strength and support. A human being may be strengthened through another being’s assistance or through knowledge and abilities placed within them. The concept of strengthening alone therefore does not determine whether Spirit of The Holy was external to Jesus’s human existence or whether its abilities manifested within him.

What is particularly significant in Jesus’s case is that, in addition to speaking of his being strengthened with Spirit of The Holy, the Qur’an also describes Jesus himself as a spirit from The God.

In our study Gabriel and the Spirit in the Qur’an, we saw that the Qur’an presents Spirit of The Holy and the Trustworthy Spirit as conveying revelation to Muhammad. Ash-Shura 42:52 also speaks of a time when Muhammad did not yet know what the Book and faith were. Jesus, however, speaks of having been given the Book and prophethood while still in the cradle.

Is this difference connected with the nature of Jesus’s creation? Was the awareness expressed through his words in the cradle a continuation of the awareness of the spirit that spoke to Mary before her pregnancy?

In our view, this evidence taken together provides substantial grounds for investigating the hypothesis that Jesus’s instruction began before his human birth. What we clearly know is that his knowledge in the cradle was not the product of the ordinary course of human learning. The precise timing of his instruction in each of the four matters, and the nature of his existential relationship with Spirit of The Holy, remain subjects of our research.

The spirit, as stated in Al-Isra 17:85, is of the Lord’s command, not His essence. Jesus, too, is a creature and servant of The God, neither His son nor a manifestation of His essence. We have examined the foundations of this distinction in Subhan and Tasbih in the Qur’an.

The Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel

The present verse speaks of Jesus being taught the Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel. To understand the scope of this instruction, we must first consider the Qur’anic meanings of the Book and wisdom.

When people today hear the word “book,” they usually imagine written sheets bound between two covers. But the Qur’anic meaning of kitab is not confined to this form. Besides writing, the K T B word family is connected with recording, establishing, and prescribing. In Al-Baqarah 2:183, for example, the same root is used to express the prescription of fasting.

Teaching people to write is also part of divine instruction, addressed in Al-Baqarah 2:282 and Al-Alaq 96:4. Yet when Jesus speaks from the cradle of having been given the Book, his words are accompanied by a body of divine teachings and knowledge. We should therefore not restrict the granting of the Book to possessing writing skills or written pages. Here, the Book can encompass the content of divine teachings, rulings, and guidance.

In the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:269, we discussed wisdom and its role in understanding, discernment, decision-making, and the proper use of knowledge and resources.

Learning, knowledge, experience, thought, and wisdom are interconnected and contribute to one another in human intellectual growth. People acquire knowledge through learning and experience, reach deeper understanding through reflection, and through wisdom can distinguish between different matters, weigh the consequences of their decisions, and apply their knowledge appropriately.

A physician may know a great deal about diseases and medicines, yet fail to discern which treatment is appropriate for which patient and under what circumstances. In such a case, what matters is not simply the amount of knowledge, but also wisdom in applying it.

Wisdom should not be restricted to applying knowledge in practical matters. Understanding truth, reasoning, sound judgment, and teachings themselves that rest upon sound understanding are also connected with wisdom.

Jesus says of his mission:

وَلَمَّا جَاءَ عِيسَىٰ بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ قَالَ قَدْ جِئْتُكُمْ بِالْحِكْمَةِ وَلِأُبَيِّنَ لَكُمْ بَعْضَ الَّذِي تَخْتَلِفُونَ فِيهِ ۖ فَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُونِ

Az-Zukhruf 43:63

And when Jesus came with clear proofs, he said: I have indeed brought you wisdom, and to clarify for you some of that over which you differ; so be mindful of The God and obey me.

In this verse, bringing wisdom is connected with clarifying matters over which people disagree. Jesus possessed wisdom through which he could make truths clear to people and help them understand and discern correctly.

We can observe this knowledge and wisdom even in Jesus’s first words in the cradle. In response to the accusation against his mother, he explained his own standing, his servitude to The God, and his responsibility toward his mother through measured words.

The Torah and the Gospel were also part of the instruction bestowed upon Jesus by The God. In Al-Maidah 5:44, the Qur’an describes the Torah as containing guidance and light, according to which Prophets and learned people judged. In Al-Maidah 5:46, it likewise describes the Gospel as containing guidance and light, confirming the Torah, and offering admonition to the mindful.

In the following verse, it says:

قَالَ إِنِّي عَبْدُ اللَّهِ آتَانِيَ الْكِتَابَ وَجَعَلَنِي نَبِيًّا

Al-Maidah 5:47

And let the people of the Gospel judge by what The God has sent down in it; and those who do not judge by what The God has sent down—it is they who are defiantly disobedient.

We therefore cannot regard the Torah as merely a collection of laws and regulations, or the Gospel as merely a collection of moral teachings. Both books contained guidance, light, wisdom, and rulings for human life. In Aal Imran 3:50, we will also see Jesus speak of making lawful some of what had been forbidden to the Children of Israel.

In religious interpretations, the discussion has been diverted toward the secondary question of whether the Book and wisdom are separate from one another. We examined this previously in the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:151. In Al-Isra 17:39, the Qur’an itself calls revealed teachings wisdom. In our view, the Qur’an, the Torah, and the Gospel are each both Book and wisdom. The Book and wisdom are each complete in their own place and complement one another. Their separate mention is not evidence that they are disconnected or that there are two independent sources of religious legislation.

What matters in this verse is the breadth of the teachings and knowledge that The God bestowed upon Jesus. The source of this knowledge is the same God who created and taught him.

In the next verse, we will encounter another aspect of Jesus’s special distinction: his exceptional abilities within human existence and by the permission of The God.
