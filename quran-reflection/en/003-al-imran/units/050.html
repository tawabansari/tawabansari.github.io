Al-Imran 3:50

ۚ

And confirming what preceded me of the Torah, and so that I may make lawful for you some of what has been forbidden to you; and I have come to you with a sign from your Lord. So be mindful of The God and obey me.

Our Reflection

Confirming the Torah and correcting religious distortions

Among the Children of Israel, Jesus presents himself as confirming the Torah while also speaking of his mission to make certain earlier prohibitions lawful. To understand these two responsibilities, we must distinguish between revealed scripture and what had become prevalent in society in the name of religion over generations.

In earlier reflections on Al-Baqarah, especially Al-Baqarah 2:59, Al-Baqarah 2:75, and Al-Baqarah 2:79, we discussed replacing the word of The God, distorting His speech after understanding it, and writing things with one’s own hands and attributing them to Him.

Jesus was sent to people among whom such distortions had arisen. Confirming the Torah did not mean endorsing every belief, ruling, and tradition that people had attributed to it throughout history. Faithfulness to the truth of the Torah required distinguishing that truth from human religious inventions.

The God says concerning Jesus and the Gospel:

وَقَفَّيْنَا عَلَىٰ آثَارِهِمْ بِعِيسَى ابْنِ مَرْيَمَ مُصَدِّقًا لِمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ مِنَ التَّوْرَاةِ ۖ وَآتَيْنَاهُ الْإِنْجِيلَ فِيهِ هُدًى وَنُورٌ وَمُصَدِّقًا لِمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ مِنَ التَّوْرَاةِ وَهُدًى وَمَوْعِظَةً لِلْمُتَّقِينَ

Al-Maidah 5:46

And We sent Jesus, son of Mary, following in their footsteps, confirming what preceded him of the Torah; and We gave him the Gospel, containing guidance and light, confirming what preceded it of the Torah, and as guidance and admonition for the mindful.

Both Jesus and the Gospel are described in this verse as confirming the Torah. Alongside confirming its truth, the Gospel also provided a means of recognizing and distinguishing that truth from distortions that had arisen among the Children of Israel. From this perspective, the Gospel served as a criterion in relation to the religious heritage before it, just as the Qur’an confirms and exercises oversight over earlier scriptures.

As we examined in the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:53, furqan is not a concept exclusive to the Qur’an. That verse itself speaks of Moses being given the Book and the criterion.

Loosening the bonds of religious law

Jesus says he has come to make lawful some of what had been forbidden to the Children of Israel. The word “بَعْضَ” shows that this is not the removal of every prohibition, but the lifting of some of them.

Another important point is that “حُرِّمَ” is a passive verb. Jesus does not say he has come to make lawful some of what The God had forbidden to them, but some of what had been forbidden to them. The agent imposing the prohibition is not named in this expression.

The Qur’an provides evidence of different origins for these prohibitions.

Aal Imran 3:93 states that, before the Torah was sent down, all food was lawful for the Children of Israel except what Israel had forbidden to himself. This is a clear example of a restriction originating in a human choice.

On the other hand, An-Nisa 4:160 and Al-Anam 6:146 speak of prohibitions that The God Himself prescribed for the Children of Israel because of their wrongdoing and rebellion.

Aal Imran 3:78 also speaks of a group among the People of the Book who presented material so that people would think it belonged to the Book of The God, although it did not. At-Tawbah 9:31 speaks of taking scholars and monks as lords instead of The God.

The religious restrictions prevalent among the Children of Israel therefore did not all share the same origin. People had imposed some upon themselves; The God had imposed others under particular circumstances; and still others arose through religious leaders’ intervention in determining what was lawful and forbidden. This verse does not list the prohibitions Jesus lifted, but it identifies releasing earlier restrictions as part of his mission.

Alongside these matters, a culture of excessive questioning and attention to unnecessary details also existed among the Children of Israel.

In Al-Baqarah 2:67, Al-Baqarah 2:68, Al-Baqarah 2:69, Al-Baqarah 2:70, and Al-Baqarah 2:71, we saw that The God gave them a simple command to slaughter a cow. Instead of carrying out the command, they repeatedly asked about its age, color, and other characteristics, narrowing their options with every question. Something initially easy became difficult through their own insistence on further details.

This conduct is not merely a historical event; it exemplifies a religious mentality that assumes a divine command becomes more sacred the more complicated and difficult it is.

In Al-Maidah 5:101 and Al-Maidah 5:102, The God also warns believers against repeating such conduct: asking questions whose disclosed answers cause difficulty, and through which earlier peoples became entangled.

Alongside confirming the Torah, Jesus came to loosen some of the bonds that had made people’s lives difficult. Even a prohibition prescribed by The God under particular circumstances can be lifted by His command. Restrictions invented by people without divine permission should likewise not be imposed in the name of faithfulness to Him.

Yet the same danger arose again among Jesus’s followers. In Al-Hadid 57:27, the Qur’an speaks of monasticism that they themselves introduced, although The God had not prescribed it for them.

A person may choose a difficult path for themselves to seek divine approval, but has no right to turn that personal choice into a divine obligation for others.

Have we bound people again with bonds that had been loosened?

What this verse tells us about Jesus’s mission is also expressed clearly in the description of Muhammad’s mission:

الَّذِينَ يَتَّبِعُونَ الرَّسُولَ النَّبِيَّ الْأُمِّيَّ الَّذِي يَجِدُونَهُ مَكْتُوبًا عِنْدَهُمْ فِي التَّوْرَاةِ وَالْإِنْجِيلِ يَأْمُرُهُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَاهُمْ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ وَيُحِلُّ لَهُمُ الطَّيِّبَاتِ وَيُحَرِّمُ عَلَيْهِمُ الْخَبَائِثَ وَيَضَعُ عَنْهُمْ إِصْرَهُمْ وَالْأَغْلَالَ الَّتِي كَانَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ ۚ فَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِهِ وَعَزَّرُوهُ وَنَصَرُوهُ وَاتَّبَعُوا النُّورَ الَّذِي أُنْزِلَ مَعَهُ ۙ أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ

Al-Araf 7:157

Those who follow the Messenger, the ummi Prophet, whom they find written about in the Torah and Gospel with them: he commands them to what is right and forbids them from what is wrong, makes wholesome things lawful for them and impure things forbidden, and removes their burden and the bonds that were upon them. Those who believe in him, honor and support him, and follow the light sent down with him—it is they who prosper.

What a clear picture of the missions of the Messengers of The God! Jesus came to make certain earlier prohibitions lawful, and Muhammad likewise to remove the heavy burdens and bonds upon people.

In the principle of ease in religion and removal of hardship, with regard for human capacity, we saw that ease in religion is a fundamental Qur’anic principle. In Al-Baqarah 2:185, The God desires ease for people, not hardship; in An-Nisa 4:28, He speaks of lightening their burden; and in Al-Hajj 22:78, He explicitly states that He has placed no hardship upon people in religion.

Unfortunately, after Muhammad’s death, as the culture of hadith and transmitted reports expanded and legal systems developed, some of the rulings and restrictions found in earlier religious law and culture were imposed again upon followers of the Qur’an. Isra’iliyyat entered Muslim religious culture not only as stories and information about earlier Prophets; traces of this heritage can also be observed in some legal rulings. We have examined this in When Qur’anic Silence Is Filled with Reports: Isra’iliyyat, Hadith, and the Inability to Say “We Do Not Know”.

These are only examples of rulings and restrictions that people have imposed upon themselves and others in the name of religion. Unfortunately, by adding so many permissions, prohibitions, and obligations to religion, we have not only made people’s lives difficult but have also left many weary of religion and turning away from it.

Yet one of Jesus’s responsibilities was to make some earlier prohibitions lawful, and one of Muhammad’s responsibilities was to remove the heavy burdens and bonds upon people. Can we claim to follow their missions while restoring those bonds?

Mawlawi scholars, muftis, and other religious scholars may teach, present evidence, and help people understand the Book of The God. But they have no right to turn their own opinions, without divine permission, into binding religious law. In At-Tawbah 9:31, the Qur’an condemns taking scholars and monks as lords. Why should we imagine that this warning concerns only followers of earlier religions and does not also apply to us?

The God says:

وَلَا تَقُولُوا لِمَا تَصِفُ أَلْسِنَتُكُمُ الْكَذِبَ هَٰذَا حَلَالٌ وَهَٰذَا حَرَامٌ لِتَفْتَرُوا عَلَى اللَّهِ الْكَذِبَ ۚ إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَفْتَرُونَ عَلَى اللَّهِ الْكَذِبَ لَا يُفْلِحُونَ

An-Nahl 16:116

And do not say, because of the falsehood your tongues describe, “This is lawful and this is forbidden,” thereby fabricating lies against The God. Indeed, those who fabricate lies against The God do not prosper.

Mindfulness and obedience to the Messenger

Jesus’s making certain earlier prohibitions lawful did not mean that he possessed independent authority to legislate. In this same verse, Jesus reminds people that he has come with a sign from their Lord. In the reflection on Aal Imran 3:49, we also saw that the signs of Jesus’s mission were presented with an emphasis on divine permission.

Making certain prohibitions lawful was therefore part of Jesus’s divine mission.

At the end of the verse, Jesus first commands people to be mindful of The God, then to obey him. As we examined in the reflection on Aal Imran 3:31 and Aal Imran 3:32, obedience to the Messenger of The God is obedience to him in his capacity as Messenger and conveyor of the divine command. This principle is not exclusive to Muhammad, nor does it mean granting independent legislative authority to the Messengers of The God.

Just as some Muslims have turned obedience to Muhammad into obedience to collections of reports and juristic opinions, were Jesus’s followers also obliged after him to obey every saying transmitted in his name as a command of The God?

We examined the foundations of this distinction in Confusing Obedience to the Messenger with Obedience to Hadith and Miscellaneous Reports.

Jesus confirmed the Torah but lifted some earlier prohibitions. Muhammad came in continuity with the same guidance to remove people’s heavy burdens and bonds. Faithfulness to Muhammad’s mission requires us to understand religion through the Qur’an itself, rather than binding people again, in the name of obedience to him, with the bonds he came to loosen.
