Al-Imran 3:32

ۖ

Say: Obey The God and the Messenger; then if they turn away, surely The God does not love the disbelievers.

Our Reflection

In Aal Imran 3:31, The God promised His love and forgiveness to those who follow His Messenger. In Aal Imran 3:32, He continues that subject with a command to obey, also clarifying the consequence of turning away.

The preceding verse spoke of following, meaning pursuing a path and guidance; this verse emphasizes accepting and acting on a command through obedience. The two are connected: a person recognizes and follows guidance, but the reality of that following appears in carrying out divine commands.

The God commands Muhammad: “قُل أَطيعُوا اللَّهَ وَالرَّسولَ”: say, obey The God and the Messenger.

Here obedience to The God and the Messenger appears together, not because there are two independent authorities for religious legislation, but because sender and messenger are connected. The God is the source of revelation and command; the Messenger is commissioned by Him to deliver the message and call people to act on it.

An-Nisa 4:80 identifies obedience to the Messenger as obedience to The God. This connection arises from the mission itself: when a messenger conveys the sender's message and command, obeying him in that capacity means obeying the command that came from the sender.

The choice of “the Messenger” is also important. In commands placing obedience to The God and His envoy together, the Quran uses “Messenger,” rather than “Prophet” or personal names such as Muhammad or Abraham. No Quranic command uses the expression “obey the Prophet” or “obey Muhammad.”

This distinction matters. “Prophet” and “Messenger” are two Quranic titles whose meanings must not be confused even when applied to one person. “Messenger,” from the root R S L, denotes someone dispatched by a sender to convey a message or carry out a mission. The title used in the command to obey therefore identifies the person through the mission entrusted to him.

In Aal Imran 3:32, the Messenger is Muhammad, present among people when the verse was revealed; the principle of obeying Messengers, however, is not confined to him.

An-Nisa 4:64 states that no Messenger was sent except to be obeyed by The God's permission. We also see this in Noah's story. In Ash-Shuara 26:107, Noah introduces himself as a trustworthy Messenger to his people; in Ash-Shuara 26:108, he asks them to be mindful of The God and obey him. Obedience to Noah relates to the mission entrusted to him by The God.

Muhammad himself was commanded to follow the way of earlier Messengers. An-Nahl 16:123 reveals to him that he should follow Abraham's upright religious way. Aal Imran 3:95 likewise commands following the way of Abraham, who was not among those associating partners with The God.

Al-Baqarah 2:285 presents faith in all Messengers and rejects discrimination between them. The divine mission must therefore not become partisan attachment to one person, people, or religious community. The issue is accepting the guidance The God delivered through His Messengers. This is examined in the principle of believing in all Messengers without discrimination.

Other Quranic verses clearly explain the relationship between obedience to the Messenger and the divine message.

An-Nur 24:54 commands people to obey The God and the Messenger and clarifies each side's responsibility. If people obey the Messenger, they will be guided; his task is clear delivery of the message.

Al-Maidah 5:92 likewise follows the command to obey The God and the Messenger with a warning: if people turn away, they should know that His Messenger's responsibility is clear delivery.

At-Taghabun 64:12 repeats this connection: people are commanded to obey The God and the Messenger, and if they turn away, the Messenger's responsibility remains clear communication of the message.

These three verses connect obedience to the Messenger with the mission of clear delivery. An-Nahl 16:35 states the same responsibility for Messengers in the plural; conveying clearly is not exclusive to Muhammad.

The Quran itself is also described in many verses as a clear Book and a clear Quran. An-Nahl 16:89 calls the revealed Book clarification of everything, guidance, and mercy.

Obedience to the Messenger in his mission therefore cannot be separated from the revelation and message he is commissioned to deliver. He conveys and clarifies the divine message; he is not an independent source of legislation alongside The God. This connection is examined in Al-Mubin and the Messenger's Clear Delivery and the principle of the Quran's clarity and illuminating character.

Muhammad himself followed revelation. In Yunus 10:15, when asked to bring a different Quran or change it, he is commanded to declare that he has no authority to change revelation on his own initiative and follows only what is revealed to him. Al-Araf 7:203 states the same principle.

The command to obey Muhammad therefore cannot be converted into obedience to rulings prescribed in religion's name independently of revelation, without divine permission, or contrary to clear Quranic principles.

The same issue matters for earlier Messengers. The Quran affirms the missions of Moses and Jesus and the revelation sent to them. Yet Al-Maidah 5:48 describes the Quran as confirming earlier scriptures and as muhaymin over them: their guardian, witness, and criterion of assessment. That verse commands judgment by divine revelation and not following others' desires against truth.

Accepting the missions of Moses and Jesus therefore does not mean accepting without examination every ruling or report later attributed to them. By confirming earlier revelation and holding its position as muhaymin, the Quran remains the criterion for assessing religious rulings and reports.

During Muhammad's life, people could hear him directly, question him, and obey his guidance and judgment as Messenger. His life, however, ended like those of earlier Messengers.

Aal Imran 3:144 recalls that Muhammad is only a Messenger, preceded by other Messengers, and asks whether people would turn back if he died or was killed.

This question shows that the end of Muhammad's life must not mean people's retreat from the guidance he delivered.

In Al-Anam 6:19, Muhammad is commanded to declare that the Quran was revealed to him to warn those present and everyone the message reaches. His revealed message was therefore not sent only for people who had seen him and heard him speak; later generations are also addressed.

Thus, for generations without access to Muhammad in person, the noble Quran is the revealed message remaining from his mission and the clear criterion for following divine guidance. Obedience to his mission does not end with his death; following the revelation he delivered continues that same path.

We now return to the verse's second part: “فَإِن تَوَلَّوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ لا يُحِبُّ الكٰفِرينَ”: if they turn away, The God does not love the disbelievers.

“Tawallaw” comes from W L Y, a root relating to nearness, connection, succession, and orientation. Depending on form and context, this family gives rise to meanings such as forming a bond, support and responsibility, turning toward, or turning away.

“Tawallā” therefore does not always mean turning away. Sometimes it concerns accepting a bond and responsibility; sometimes it means turning one's back on an invitation or withdrawing from it.

In Aal Imran 3:32, because the command to obey precedes it, “if they turn away” refers to rejecting this invitation and refusing the command. Al-Qiyamah 75:32 likewise places denial of truth alongside turning away. The verb's meaning must therefore be understood from its form and situation in the particular verse, rather than imposing one fixed meaning on every derivative of the root.

In Aal Imran 3:31, The God promised love and forgiveness to those following the Messenger's guidance. In Aal Imran 3:32, He states the other side of that choice: if they turn away from divine command, He does not love the disbelievers.

This contrast clarifies the connection between divine love and accepting truth in practice. His love is not compatible with consciously denying truth and turning one's back on His guidance.

But who are “the disbelievers” in this context?

To understand the word, we must consider kufr in the Quran. K F R relates to covering and concealing. In Quranic usage, that covering may concern truth, blessings, or divine signs. In the context of denying a Messenger's mission, kufr involves denial and turning away from truth whose evidence has become clear to a person.

Aal Imran 3:19 speaks of those who disagreed through transgression after knowledge had arrived. Aal Imran 3:21 describes people denying divine signs and opposing Prophets and those calling for justice. In Aal Imran 3:23, some scripture-holders turn away when summoned to The God's Book for judgment.

These verses show that kufr in the present context involves denying truth, opposing clear guidance, and turning away from divine judgment, rather than simply the name of a religion or community.

The warning in Aal Imran 3:32 likewise concerns turning away from The God's command and the mission His Messenger was entrusted to convey.

But denying the mission is not the same as refusing every statement that others attribute to the Messenger.

This distinction is crucial. Commands to obey the Messenger have sometimes been used to accuse those who do not accept hadith and reports attributed to Muhammad. They are told that refusing to obey these reports means turning away from the Messenger and therefore falling under the judgment concerning disbelievers.

Has the Quran established such a criterion?

Suppose someone attributes a report to Muhammad and uses it to declare something obligatory or forbidden. Another person assesses it against the Quran and finds its attribution unproven, its content contrary to clear Quranic principles, or its ruling an imposition in The God's name that the Quran has not prescribed. Does rejecting that report deny Muhammad's mission?

Al-Isra 17:36 prohibits following what one has no knowledge of and recalls the responsibility of hearing, sight, and the heart. Al-Hujurat 49:6 likewise commands examining news before acting upon it so that people are not harmed through ignorance.

An-Nahl 16:116 condemns attributing permissions and prohibitions to The God through false claims. Merely attributing a statement to the Messenger cannot therefore make it his certain command or justify imposing a ruling in The God's name without sound evidence.

Someone who asks for evidence that Muhammad actually said something, or rejects an unreliable report inconsistent with the Quran, is not identified as a disbeliever merely for doing so.

Rejecting a report attributed to the Messenger differs from rejecting the Messenger himself and his divine message.

The command to obey the Messenger must not become an instrument obliging people to obey every hadith scholar, jurist, religious leader, or collection of reports attributed to Muhammad. The Messenger was commissioned to convey The God's message; attributing statements to him without establishing their truth does not replace the certain divine message.

This issue is examined with Quranic and historical evidence in Confusing Obedience to the Messenger with Obedience to Hadith and Miscellaneous Reports, Is the Quran Incomplete Without Hadith?, and the principle of the Quran's completeness and sufficiency for religious guidance.

The warning about turning away and disbelief, moreover, does not authorize compelling others to believe and obey.

In Aal Imran 3:20, when the possibility of people turning away is raised, the Messenger's task is identified as delivering the message. An-Nur 24:54 likewise specifies his responsibility for clear delivery after commanding obedience. Al-Baqarah 2:256 also rejects compulsion in religion.

People are responsible for accepting or rejecting guidance, but no one may use this warning to claim authority over others' faith or compel them, in religion's name, to accept human rulings and reports.

Continuing the preceding verse, Aal Imran 3:32 clarifies the connection between divine love, following, and practical obedience. Obedience to the Messenger in his mission is inseparable from obedience to The God and the guidance he was commissioned to deliver. Knowingly turning away from this truth is incompatible with divine love; assessing statements attributed to the Messenger against the Quran and rejecting unsupported attributions, however, does not deny his mission.
