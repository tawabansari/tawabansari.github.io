Al-Imran 3:31

ۗ

Say: If you love The God, follow me, so that The God may love you and forgive your sins; and The God is forgiving and merciful.

Our Reflection

In Aal Imran 3:30, The God spoke of the day when people confront their good and evil deeds, recalling His deep compassion toward His servants. In Aal Imran 3:31, He now explains the way to receive His love and forgiveness: those who love Him must show that love by following His guidance. Muhammad is therefore commanded to tell people that if they love The God, they should follow him so that The God may love them and forgive their sins.

Love for The God is not merely a verbal claim or an inward feeling; it must also appear in one's chosen path and conduct. Someone who professes love for Him yet knowingly sets aside His command and follows desires opposed to His guidance creates a contradiction between that claim and their behavior.

In human relationships, too, genuine love is not recognized solely by saying “I love you.” Conduct, loyalty, and respect for another's rights show how far love affects one's life. Love for The God likewise becomes visible when a person follows His guidance to seek His pleasure.

This is directly connected with andad, or rivals set up alongside The God. Al-Baqarah 2:165 describes people taking rivals alongside Him and loving them as they love The God; believers, by contrast, have a stronger love for Him.

This does not prohibit love for family, a spouse, children, friends, or life's other blessings. Aal Imran 3:14 also speaks of people's love for life's desirable things. The problem begins when preserving these attachments leads someone to abandon truth and justice or place another's wishes above His command.

For example, someone may speak extensively of loving The God, yet choose a religious leader's command when it conflicts with a clear Quranic ruling, through partisanship or fear of losing social standing. Their conduct then reveals where they have placed their practical loyalty.

Al-Baqarah 2:166 describes the day when those who were followed see the punishment and disown their followers. In Al-Baqarah 2:167, the followers wish for an opportunity to return so they could disown those leaders as the leaders disowned them. This warning shows that love and dependence must not prevent people from assessing others' paths against truth.

In Aal Imran 3:31, The God also promises His love to those following His guidance: “يُحبِبكُمُ اللَّهُ وَيَغفِر لَكُم ذُنوبَكُم”: The God may love you and forgive your sins.

Love here is not one-sided. A person loves The God and, by following His guidance, receives divine love. Al-Maidah 5:54 likewise speaks of people whom He loves and who love Him, showing humility toward believers, firmness toward disbelievers, striving in His way, and freedom from fear of others' blame.

The Quran does not restrict divine love to one people or religious community. In Aal Imran 3:134, The God loves those who do good; in Aal Imran 3:76, the mindful; and in Aal Imran 3:146, the patient. What matters is therefore the reality of faith and conduct, not merely one's chosen label.

Alongside divine love comes the promise of forgiveness. People may err, but the path of return to The God is not closed. Aal Imran 3:135 describes those who, after indecent conduct or wronging themselves, remember Him, seek forgiveness for their sins, and do not knowingly persist in their error.

Following divine guidance is thus connected with reforming conduct and turning back from error. The verse closes with “ghafūr, raḥīm”: The God is abundantly forgiving and merciful. Someone who has erred should not despair of His mercy; the path of reform and forgiveness remains open.

There is another important distinction here. Aal Imran 3:31 connects His love for His servants with following Muhammad, rather than professing personal affection for him.

“Ittibāʿ,” from T B ʿ, means following a path and guidance. The next verse, Aal Imran 3:32, uses “obedience,” emphasizing compliance with a command. These concepts are related but not identical. In Ta-Ha 20:90, Aaron likewise asks his people both to follow him and to obey his command.

At-Tawbah 9:24 places love for The God and His Messenger against attachment to family, possessions, and comfortable living. There too, however, the title “Messenger” is used, rather than love for Muhammad as an individual personality. A Messenger is one sent to convey a divine message, and this title is not confined to Muhammad: Gabriel, the Trustworthy Spirit, is also identified as a Messenger in conveying revelation (At-Takwir 81:19, Ash-Shuara 26:193, and Al-Baqarah 2:97). The Quran speaks of many Messengers and prohibits making distinctions between them (Al-Baqarah 2:285). This is examined in the principle of believing in all Messengers without discrimination.

We must now consider how following Muhammad in his capacity as Messenger is fulfilled.

Muhammad was a Messenger chosen by The God to convey the message and guide people. His contemporaries could hear him, question him, and follow his guidance and judgment in that capacity. “Fa-ttabiʿūnī,” meaning “then follow me,” was therefore a real, practical instruction for them, not merely an expression of respect or personal affection for Muhammad.

Muhammad himself, however, was commanded to follow revelation. In Al-Anam 6:50, he declares that he neither possesses divine treasures, has independent knowledge of the unseen, nor is an angel; he follows only what is revealed to him.

In Yunus 10:15, when asked to bring a different Quran or change the existing one, he is commanded to say that he cannot change it on his own initiative and follows only what is revealed to him. Al-Araf 7:203 states the same principle. In Al-Ahqaf 46:9, Muhammad declares that he is not unprecedented among Messengers and follows nothing but revelation.

Thus, following Muhammad as Messenger means following the path and guidance he was commissioned to convey and practice. Al-Araf 7:157 also places following the Messenger alongside following the light sent down with him.

How, then, is this command fulfilled by generations born after Muhammad's death?

Muhammad was present among people during his mission, but his life, like those of other Messengers, came to an end. Aal Imran 3:144 recalls that Muhammad is only a Messenger, preceded by other Messengers, and asks whether people would turn back if he died or was killed.

The end of Muhammad's life must not mean people's retreat from the guidance he conveyed. Following his mission therefore does not end with his death; what remains for later generations is the divine message he delivered.

In Al-Anam 6:19, Muhammad is commanded to declare that this Quran was revealed to him to warn his immediate audience and everyone it reaches. Its message was therefore not only for his contemporaries; later generations are also addressed.

Al-Araf 7:3 commands people to follow what their Lord has revealed. Al-Anam 6:155 describes the revealed Book as blessed and commands following it.

An-Nur 24:54 also commands obedience to The God and the Messenger, while identifying the Messenger's task in the same verse as “al-balāgh al-mubīn”: clear delivery of the message. This connection recurs in Al-Maidah 5:92 and At-Taghabun 64:12.

For generations without access to Muhammad in person, the clear way to follow his mission is therefore to follow the noble Quran: the revelation he delivered and was himself commanded to follow.

In subsequent history, however, extensive collections of hadith and reports were attributed to Muhammad. Transmitted through various narrators to later generations, they also came to include disagreements, conflicting accounts, and Israʾiliyyat.

An important confusion about obedience to the Messenger arose here: The God's certain command to follow and obey the Messenger was extended to accepting miscellaneous reports that others attributed to him. Sometimes rulings and beliefs not prescribed by the Quran were also presented through those reports as binding religious rules.

But does merely attributing a statement to Muhammad establish that he actually said it? Can a report whose truth is unclear justify declaring something obligatory or forbidden in The God's name?

Al-Isra 17:36 prohibits following what one has no knowledge of, recalling the responsibility of hearing, sight, and the heart. Al-Hujurat 49:6 likewise commands examining news before acting upon it, so as not to harm people through ignorance.

This responsibility matters still more for statements attributed to The God's Messenger: an unreliable report may lead someone to accept a ruling or belief in religion's name and impose it on others.

Al-Anam 6:114 describes the revealed Book as set out in detail. An-Nahl 16:89 describes it as clarification of everything, guidance, and mercy. Al-Ankabut 29:51 asks whether the Book revealed and recited to people is not sufficient for them.

The Quran's authority therefore cannot be made dependent on reports collected after Muhammad, with people then required to treat those reports as the final criterion for identifying divine rulings.

Forqan examines this with documentation and Quranic references, particularly in Al-Mubin and the Messenger's Clear Delivery, Confusing Obedience to the Messenger with Obedience to Hadith and Miscellaneous Reports, Is the Quran Incomplete Without Hadith?, and the principle of the Quran's completeness and sufficiency for religious guidance.

An-Nisa 4:80 identifies obedience to the Messenger as obedience to The God, and Al-Ahzab 33:21 presents His Messenger as a good example for believers. Yet these verses do not by themselves establish that every later report attributed to Muhammad is his certain command. The Messenger's actual position must be understood from the Quran, distinguishing the delivered message from human reports about him.

Someone today who follows the Quran, the revealed message delivered by Muhammad, has not turned away from his mission; rather, they seek to follow the same guidance that he himself followed.

Love for The God is recognized through conduct: following His guidance, remaining faithful to truth, correcting errors, and not setting aside divine commands to please others. He promises His love and forgiveness to those following this path, and He is abundantly forgiving and merciful.
