Al-Imran 3:2

The God; there is no deity except Him; the Living, the Self-Subsisting Sustainer.

Our Reflection

This verse repeats the opening words of Ayat al-Kursi, Al-Baqarah 2:255, in whose reflection we examined God’s oneness, life, and sustaining authority in detail.

“اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ” expresses the oneness of The God; no other being is His equal in divinity and worship.

“الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ” are two fundamental attributes of His. God is living, but His life, unlike that of created beings, does not depend on food, energy, an environment, or other conditions. He is Al-Qayyum: self-subsisting and sustaining all existence. His existence depends on no other, while all created beings need Him both to come into being and to continue existing.

These two attributes also appear together in Ta-Ha 20:111. The preceding verses describe intercession only by divine permission and the limits of created beings’ encompassing knowledge—the same subjects connected with God’s sustaining authority in Ayat al-Kursi.

In Al-Furqan 25:58, God commands reliance upon the Living One who does not die. Likewise, in Ghafir 40:65, after declaring His life and oneness, the command is to call upon God directly, sincerely devoting religion to Him. Recognizing the living, self-subsisting God is therefore not merely a theoretical belief; it also concerns worship, prayer, and a person’s ultimate reliance.

In the following verses of this surah, Aal Imran 3:3, Aal Imran 3:4, Aal Imran 3:5, and Aal Imran 3:6, the Quran speaks of the sending down of divine Books, God’s encompassing knowledge, and His shaping of human beings in the womb. These are expressions of the knowledge, governance, and sovereignty of The God.

For a fuller examination of “الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ,” see the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:255.

Cross-references
  • Al-Baqarah 2:255: Ayat al-Kursi begins with these same words; a full discussion of divine oneness, life, sustaining authority, knowledge, and sovereignty.
  • Aal Imran 3:3; Aal Imran 3:4: The sending down of the Book, Torah, Gospel, and Furqan by The God.
  • Aal Imran 3:5; Aal Imran 3:6: God’s encompassing knowledge, shaping people in the womb, and the repeated declaration of divine oneness.
  • Aal Imran 3:18: Testimony to God’s oneness and upholding justice; “qa’iman bil-qist” shares the root Q W M, with a meaning appropriate to its own context.
  • Al-Anam 6:102: God’s oneness in divinity, creation, and governance.
  • Ta-Ha 20:109; Ta-Ha 20:110; Ta-Ha 20:111: Intercession by divine permission, the limits of created beings’ knowledge, and the attributes “Al-Hayy Al-Qayyum.”
  • Al-Furqan 25:58: Reliance upon the Living One who does not die.
  • Fatir 35:15: Human need and God’s self-sufficiency.
  • Fatir 35:41: God’s sustaining of the heavens and the earth.
  • Ghafir 40:65: God’s oneness and life connected with prayer and sincere devotion.
  • Al-Hadid 57:3: God is the First and the Last.
  • Ash-Shura 42:11: The denial of any likeness between God and created beings.
  • Al-Ikhlas 112:2: God’s self-sufficiency and others’ need of Him.
