Al-Imran 3:22

Those are the ones whose deeds have become fruitless in this world and the Hereafter; and they have no helpers.

Our Reflection

This verse directly continues Aal Imran 3:21, where the Qur’an spoke of those who disbelieve in the signs of The God, kill the Prophets without right, and eliminate people who call others to fairness and justice. It now states the outcome of such conduct: their deeds become fruitless in this world and the Hereafter, and they will have no helpers.

“حَبِطَتْ” comes from the root ح ب ط and means becoming fruitless and losing the yield and value of an action. This loss may become evident in the reward for an action, its outcome, or the consequences it leaves in a person’s life. Someone may have done good deeds during their life, yet their disbelief and oppression may prevent those deeds from bringing them reward and salvation. An action may also appear beneficial and successful at first, only to produce an outcome contrary to its doer’s expectations over time. Thus, the nullification of deeds should neither be confined to the loss of wealth and power in this world nor understood solely as losing reward in the Hereafter. The Qur’an names both worlds.

An example can be seen in the lives of Pharaoh and Moses. Moses was raised in Pharaoh’s household, and Pharaoh later regarded that upbringing as a favor he had bestowed upon Moses (Ash-Shuara 26:18). Yet that same Moses stood against Pharaoh’s oppression and was commissioned to liberate the Children of Israel. Raising Moses could not justify Pharaoh’s oppression, guarantee his reward and salvation, or protect his power from the consequences of his conduct. What Pharaoh once treated as grounds for boasting and reminding Moses of his favor ultimately did not benefit his rule.

People usually judge the results of their actions by what they see at the time. If their power grows, they acquire wealth, or they manage to silence opponents, they may consider themselves victorious. Yet an action’s consequences do not always become apparent immediately. A decision that seems beneficial today may set in motion a chain of outcomes that emerge years later—outcomes the person who acted could not have fully foreseen. This connects with the discussion of “سَرِيعُ الْحِسَابِ” in Aal Imran 3:19: a delay in the appearance of a deed’s consequences does not mean that it lies outside divine reckoning. A person sees only part of the course their actions take, while The God knows all deeds and their consequences.

The verse’s conclusion, “وَمَا لَهُمْ مِنْ نَاصِرِينَ,” completes this point. Those who may rely on their power and supporters to suppress others and evade accountability in this world will have no helpers before the reckoning and judgment of The God. Neither their power nor their wealth can turn their oppression into right, and the good deeds of which they boast will not automatically make amends for their disbelief and aggression.

The value and ultimate outcome of an action cannot be measured solely by its apparent success and immediate result. Human conduct has consequences that may become apparent in this world over time, and its reckoning is not confined to worldly life. Someone who denies truth to preserve their power and interests and takes the lives of those calling for justice may see themselves as victorious for a while. But that apparent victory does not prove that their deeds are accepted by The God.
