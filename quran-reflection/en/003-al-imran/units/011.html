Al-Imran 3:11

ۚ ۗ

Like the way of Pharaoh’s people and those before them; they denied Our signs, so The God seized them for their sins; and The God is severe in punishment.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, God spoke of the consequences of disbelief. Now, through the example of Pharaoh and those around him, He shows how disbelief can be knowingly repeated, even when truth is recognized.

“كَدَأْبِ آلِ فِرْعَوْنَ وَالَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِهِمْ”

“Like the way of Pharaoh’s people and those before them.”

“Da’b,” from D A B, refers to an enduring, repeated manner of conduct. Here the Quran presents the behavior of Pharaoh and those before him as an example of a pattern of denial.

In Ta-Ha 20:56, God states that He showed Pharaoh all His signs, yet Pharaoh denied them and refused. In An-Naml 27:14, He also makes clear that they denied the signs while inwardly certain of them.

A practical example of this conduct appears in Al-Araf 7:134 and Al-Araf 7:135: when punishment afflicted Pharaoh and those around him, they asked Moses to pray for its removal and promised to believe and release the Children of Israel. Yet as soon as the punishment was lifted, they broke their covenant.

We have examined this issue in “Who is a kafir, and what is kufr?”.

Their problem, then, was not ignorance of truth. They recognized it and promised to accept it when in danger, but denied it again once the danger had passed.

“كَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا فَأَخَذَهُمُ اللَّهُ بِذُنُوبِهِمْ”

“They denied Our signs, so The God seized them for their sins.”

“Kadhdhabu,” from K DH B, means declaring something false. In Al-Anfal 8:52, the Quran describes this same conduct of Pharaoh’s people in terms of disbelief in God’s signs, rather than declaring the signs false. In presenting this practical example, the Quran thus connects disbelief with denial of the signs.

Here it concerns denying God’s signs, rather than disagreeing with human beliefs and statements.

This distinction matters today as well. Some religious scholars, invoking what they call “scholarly consensus,” have regarded wholesale denial of the authority of sunnah and hadith as disbelief. But that claim is not the same as refusing to place reports attributed to the Messenger on an equal footing with the Quran. Indeed, placing the Quran’s clear truth on an equal footing with disparate reports and quotations is itself a form of concealing and denying truth. In “Refuting the claim that transmitters of the Quran and transmitters of hadith have equal standing”, in the “Quran’s completeness” section, we have also explained that human reports cannot hold the same standing as divine revelation. Disbelief is not a label that holders of religious power can invoke their own consensus to attach to ideological and political opponents. This verse concerns knowingly denying divine signs after recognizing truth, rather than disagreeing with the beliefs, reports, or rulings issued by a religious sect.

The expression “for their sins” also shows that they were seized because of their own sins and deeds—the same principle that connects human responsibility with one’s own choices and actions.

“وَاللَّهُ شَدِيدُ الْعِقَابِ”

“And The God is severe in punishment.”

“Al-‘iqab,” from ʿ Q B, is connected with consequence and outcome. This expression warns of the grave consequences of knowingly denying the divine signs.

The verse’s message is that disbelief must not be equated with a difference of belief or lack of knowledge. Pharaoh’s example shows that a person may recognize truth yet repeatedly and knowingly refuse to accept it.
