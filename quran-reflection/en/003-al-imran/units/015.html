Al-Imran 3:15

ۚ ۗ

Say: Shall I tell you of something better than these? For those who have been mindful, with their Lord are gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein, and purified spouses and approval from The God; and The God sees His servants.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, God spoke of the desires and blessings of worldly life, recalling that these are passing enjoyments. He now announces something better: lasting fulfillment in the hereafter.

“قُلْ أَؤُنَبِّئُكُم بِخَيْرٍ مِنْ ذَٰلِكُمْ”

“Say: Shall I tell you of something better than these?”

The Quran has not declared worldly blessings worthless or forbidden. Rather, it wants people to understand that life is not confined to these limited benefits. However valuable wealth, family, and worldly amenities may be, a person’s enjoyment of them will one day end.

“لِلَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا عِنْدَ رَبِّهِمْ”

“For those who have been mindful, with their Lord…”

This promise is for those who practice taqwa: they consciously watch their conduct and choices so as not to stray from truth, justice, and The God’s command.

People can enjoy worldly blessings while also striving for their hereafter. In Al-Qasas 28:77, the Quran recalls this same connection between enjoying this world and seeking the hereafter.

“جَنَّاتٌ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا وَأَزْوَاجٌ مُطَهَّرَةٌ”

“Gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein, and purified spouses.”

Gardens, rivers, and purified spouses are among the rewards the Quran promises to those who practice taqwa. In contrast to this world’s passing blessings, this verse speaks of abiding in that life.

But we do not know how paradise is created, the nature of the human body there, or the character of the senses and life in it. The Quran does not explain such details in this verse. What matters for understanding its message is the promise of lasting fulfillment and enjoyment of the Lord’s blessings.

“وَرِضْوَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ”

“And approval from The God.”

This part makes the verse’s most important message clear.

Gardens, rivers, and purified spouses are rewards, but what has greater value is The God’s approval of a person.

In At-Tawbah 9:72, after promising gardens and good dwellings, God explicitly states that His approval is greater.

In Al-Bayyinah 98:8, mutual satisfaction is also expressed: God is pleased with His worthy servants, and they are pleased with Him.

Thus, humanity’s greatest fulfillment is not merely attaining the blessings of the hereafter, but reaching a position in which one’s Lord is pleased with oneself.

“وَاللَّهُ بَصِيرٌ بِالْعِبَادِ”

“And The God sees His servants.”

God knows His servants’ deeds. Wealth, rank, fame, or claims of religiosity cannot replace taqwa and worthy conduct. He knows which path people have chosen in life and how they have used His blessings.

The verse’s message is that worldly blessings are valuable but passing. For a person who practices taqwa, everlasting life and good rewards are promised; yet the greater fulfillment is gaining The God’s approval.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:14: The desirable things and passing blessings of worldly life.
  • At-Tawbah 9:72: The God’s approval is greater than the blessings of paradise.
  • Al-Bayyinah 98:8: Everlasting reward, God’s pleasure with His worthy servants, and their pleasure with Him.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:25: The promise of gardens, rivers, and purified spouses for those of faith and righteous conduct.
  • Al-Qasas 28:77: Enjoying this world while seeking the home of the hereafter.
  • Al-Kahf 18:46: The superiority of enduring righteous deeds over worldly adornment.
