Al-Imran 3:46

And he speaks to people in the cradle and in middle age, and is among the righteous.

Our Reflection

In the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:253, we discussed the differing favors The God bestowed on His Messengers. One particular favor granted to Jesus is his support through Spirit of The Holy. There, we noted the connection between this favor, Jesus’ coming into being, and the emergence of his awareness in the cradle. We now reflect further on this subject by examining verses from Aal Imran and Maryam.

Acquired worthiness and Jesus’ exceptional standing

In our view, prophethood and messengership are based on worthiness and competence. Although a person’s worthiness or lack of it is known in The God’s eternal knowledge, under the patterns of creation and the laws governing this world, human worthiness is acquired in the course of life.

Knowledge, wisdom, and thought are acquired, not conferred by appointment. Someone can be appointed to a position, but an appointment cannot make them possess knowledge, wisdom, and thought.

In An-Nahl 16:78, The God says:

وَاللَّهُ أَخرَجَكُم مِن بُطونِ أُمَّهٰتِكُم لا تَعلَمونَ شَيـًٔا وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمعَ وَالأَبصٰرَ وَالأَفـِٔدَةَ لَعَلَّكُم تَشكُرونَ

An-Nahl 16:78 — And The God brought you out of your mothers’ wombs knowing nothing, and gave you hearing, sight, and hearts, so that you might be grateful.

Using the resources and abilities The God has bestowed upon them, human beings learn, gain experience, think, and develop their character.

In introducing Abraham, whom The God made an imam for people, the Quran does not speak only of the outcome of his thinking; it also portrays how his approach to thought began, grew, and developed. Al-Anam 6:75 speaks of showing Abraham the dominion of the heavens and the earth so that he might attain certainty. In the following verses, we see Abraham’s thought and reasoning about the star, moon, and sun. Finally, in Al-Anam 6:79, he turns his face toward the Creator of the heavens and the earth and describes himself as hanif.

In Al-Baqarah 2:124, too, Abraham is granted the position of imamah after being tested and completing those tests. We have examined this subject in detail in Imamah in the Quran: Abraham’s Standing and the Question of Leadership.

We also read about Moses in Al-Qasas 28:14:

وَلَمّا بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُ وَاستَوىٰ ءاتَينٰهُ حُكمًا وَعِلمًا ۚ وَكَذٰلِكَ نَجزِى المُحسِنينَ

Al-Qasas 28:14 — And when he reached his full strength and maturity, We gave him judgment and knowledge; and thus We reward those who do good.

The same theme appears concerning Joseph in Yusuf 12:22. The granting of judgment and knowledge is linked to their reaching a stage of growth and development of character. Muhammad, too, had spent years of his life among people before the Quran’s revelation (Yunus 10:16).

Yet among the Prophets introduced to us in the Quran, Jesus has an exceptional standing in his possession of knowledge and awareness at the beginning of his human life. Before he had the opportunity to gain experience with the passage of time or acquire knowledge through instruction, discussion, and debate, he spoke from the cradle about serving The God, the Book, prophethood, Salat, Zakat, death, and resurrection.

To better understand this particular favor granted to Jesus, we should study the Quranic accounts of Zechariah and Mary, and then of John and Jesus, side by side.

Zechariah and Mary: two accounts mirroring one another

In Maryam and Aal Imran, The God recounts the stories of Zechariah and Mary using very similar structures.

Glad tidings and naming the child before birth:

يٰزَكَرِيّا إِنّا نُبَشِّرُكَ بِغُلٰمٍ اسمُهُ يَحيىٰ لَم نَجعَل لَهُ مِن قَبلُ سَمِيًّا

Maryam 19:7 — O Zechariah, We give you glad tidings of a boy named John; We have not previously given anyone his name.

إِذ قالَتِ المَلٰئِكَةُ يٰمَريَمُ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُبَشِّرُكِ بِكَلِمَةٍ مِنهُ اسمُهُ المَسيحُ عيسَى ابنُ مَريَمَ وَجيهًا فِى الدُّنيا وَالـٔاخِرَةِ وَمِنَ المُقَرَّبينَ

Aal Imran 3:45 — When the angels said: O Mary, indeed The God gives you glad tidings of a word from Him whose name is the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, honored in this world and the Hereafter and among those brought near.

In both announcements, the child is named before birth. Yet the announcement to Zechariah speaks of a “boy”, while the angels’ announcement to Mary speaks of “a word from The God”.

Zechariah’s and Mary’s wonder at how the child would be born:

قالَ رَبِّ أَنّىٰ يَكونُ لى غُلٰمٌ وَكانَتِ امرَأَتى عاقِرًا وَقَد بَلَغتُ مِنَ الكِبَرِ عِتِيًّا

Maryam 19:8 — He said: My Lord, how shall I have a boy when my wife is barren and I have reached extreme old age?

قالَت أَنّىٰ يَكونُ لى غُلٰمٌ وَلَم يَمسَسنى بَشَرٌ وَلَم أَكُ بَغِيًّا

Maryam 19:20 — She said: How shall I have a boy when no human has touched me and I have not been unchaste?

The responses to their wonder:

قالَ كَذٰلِكَ قالَ رَبُّكَ هُوَ عَلَىَّ هَيِّنٌ وَقَد خَلَقتُكَ مِن قَبلُ وَلَم تَكُ شَيـًٔا

Maryam 19:9 — He said: Thus it is; your Lord said: This is easy for Me. I created you before, when you were nothing.

قالَ كَذٰلِكِ قالَ رَبُّكِ هُوَ عَلَىَّ هَيِّنٌ ۖ وَلِنَجعَلَهُ ءايَةً لِلنّاسِ وَرَحمَةً مِنّا ۚ وَكانَ أَمرًا مَقضِيًّا

Maryam 19:21 — He said: Thus it is; your Lord said: This is easy for Me; and so that We may make him a sign for people and a mercy from Us. It was a matter already decided.

In Aal Imran, too, the responses to Zechariah’s and Mary’s wonder follow similar structures, but with an important difference in wording. The response to Zechariah is “كَذٰلِكَ اللَّهُ يَفعَلُ ما يَشاءُ” (Aal Imran 3:40), while the response to Mary is “كَذٰلِكِ اللَّهُ يَخلُقُ ما يَشاءُ” (Aal Imran 3:47).

Zechariah’s and Mary’s silence:

قالَ رَبِّ اجعَل لى ءايَةً ۚ قالَ ءايَتُكَ أَلّا تُكَلِّمَ النّاسَ ثَلٰثَ لَيالٍ سَوِيًّا

Maryam 19:10 — He said: My Lord, appoint a sign for me. He said: Your sign is that you will not speak to people for three nights while sound in health.

فَكُلى وَاشرَبى وَقَرّى عَينًا ۖ فَإِمّا تَرَيِنَّ مِنَ البَشَرِ أَحَدًا فَقولى إِنّى نَذَرتُ لِلرَّحمٰنِ صَومًا فَلَن أُكَلِّمَ اليَومَ إِنسِيًّا

Maryam 19:26 — So eat and drink and be comforted; and if you see any human being, say: I have vowed a fast to the Entirely Merciful, so today I will not speak to any human being.

Just as Zechariah conveyed his message to his people without speaking (Maryam 19:11), Mary pointed to her child without speaking when her people questioned her (Maryam 19:29).

The births of John and Jesus were both extraordinary. One was born to a barren mother and an elderly father, and the other without a man having touched his mother. Yet in Al-Anbiya 21:90, The God speaks of setting right the condition of Zechariah’s wife, while in the next verse, Al-Anbiya 21:91, He speaks of breathing His Spirit into Mary.

Thus, while presenting the similarities between these two events, the Quran also carefully shows us their differences.

John and Jesus: two accounts mirroring one another

Later in Surah Maryam, the stories of John and Jesus also mirror one another. There are striking similarities across four consecutive verses about each of them, yet a fundamental difference in how they are expressed.

First: the Book, judgment, and prophethood:

يٰيَحيىٰ خُذِ الكِتٰبَ بِقُوَّةٍ ۖ وَءاتَينٰهُ الحُكمَ صَبِيًّا

Maryam 19:12 — O John, take hold of the Book firmly; and We gave him judgment as a child.

قالَ إِنّى عَبدُ اللَّهِ ءاتىٰنِىَ الكِتٰبَ وَجَعَلَنى نَبِيًّا

Maryam 19:30 — He said: I am the servant of The God; He has given me the Book and made me a Prophet.

For John, the text speaks of judgment being granted in childhood; for Jesus, it speaks of his awareness in the cradle itself. Sabi conveys childhood generally, whereas mahd in this context refers to the earliest stage of a child’s life. The Quran itself places both words together in Maryam 19:29: “فِى المَهدِ صَبِيًّا”.

John receives an external command to take hold of the Book firmly, and the Quran’s speaker describes judgment being granted to him in childhood. Jesus, however, speaks in his own voice and declares that The God has given him the Book and made him a Prophet. It is as though the speaking spirit is he himself.

Second: purity, blessing, and Zakat:

وَحَنانًا مِن لَدُنّا وَزَكوٰةً ۖ وَكانَ تَقِيًّا

Maryam 19:13 — And tenderness from Us, and purity; and he was mindful of The God.

وَجَعَلَنى مُبارَكًا أَينَ ما كُنتُ وَأَوصٰنى بِالصَّلوٰةِ وَالزَّكوٰةِ ما دُمتُ حَيًّا

Maryam 19:31 — And He has made me blessed wherever I may be, and enjoined upon me Salat and Zakat as long as I live.

Again, for John, the Quran’s speaker describes the qualities and blessings granted to him, while Jesus himself is the speaker. He knows of the blessing and of The God’s instructions to him, and expresses them.

Third: kindness to parents and not being domineering:

وَبَرًّا بِوٰلِدَيهِ وَلَم يَكُن جَبّارًا عَصِيًّا

Maryam 19:14 — And he was kind to his parents and was not a disobedient tyrant.

وَبَرًّا بِوٰلِدَتى وَلَم يَجعَلنى جَبّارًا شَقِيًّا

Maryam 19:32 — And He has made me kind to my mother, and has not made me a wretched tyrant.

The same difference in expression continues. John’s kindness to his parents is described, whereas Jesus himself speaks of kindness to his mother. Another difference is that John is described in relation to both father and mother, while Jesus is described in relation to his mother alone.

Fourth: peace on the day of birth, death, and resurrection:

وَسَلٰمٌ عَلَيهِ يَومَ وُلِدَ وَيَومَ يَموتُ وَيَومَ يُبعَثُ حَيًّا

Maryam 19:15 — And peace be upon him on the day he was born, the day he dies, and the day he is raised alive.

وَالسَّلٰمُ عَلَىَّ يَومَ وُلِدتُ وَيَومَ أَموتُ وَيَومَ أُبعَثُ حَيًّا

Maryam 19:33 — And peace be upon me on the day I was born, the day I die, and the day I am raised alive.

Peace is pronounced upon John from outside, by the Quran’s speaker, whereas Jesus pronounces peace upon himself. It is as though the speaking spirit is he himself.

This difference recurs across all four consecutive verses. While making the structures of the two accounts similar, The God distinguishes the speaker from the person being described in one, while in the other the person being described is himself the speaker.

The significance of this difference in expression becomes clearer when we place these mirrored accounts alongside verses that speak of the Spirit’s special connection with Jesus’ coming into being and his support through Spirit of The Holy.

Jesus’ particular favor and his connection with Spirit of The Holy

In Gabriel and the Spirit in the Quran, we discussed Gabriel’s role in conveying revelation and the different Quranic uses of spirit.

Concerning revelation to Muhammad, the Quran speaks of Gabriel bringing the message down upon his heart by The God’s permission (Al-Baqarah 2:97). Concerning Jesus, however, no Quranic verse says that Gabriel conveyed revelation to him. What is stated particularly and repeatedly about Jesus is his support through Spirit of The Holy.

In Surah Maryam, it is the Spirit himself who is sent to Mary and appears before her as a fully formed human being (Maryam 19:17). It is the Spirit himself who identifies himself as her Lord’s Messenger before her pregnancy (Maryam 19:19). Mary’s pregnancy is then mentioned (Maryam 19:22).

In Al-Anbiya 21:91, The God speaks of breathing His Spirit into Mary, and in An-Nisa 4:171 He describes Jesus himself as a Messenger, a word He conveyed to Mary, and a spirit from Him.

Is this the same Spirit who appeared to Mary in human form before her pregnancy, identified himself as a Messenger, then developed in Mary’s womb and was born as Jesus son of Mary and a servant of The God?

If so, Jesus’ awareness from the cradle and the difference between his manner of speaking and John’s take on another meaning. It is as though, from the very beginning of his human life, Jesus possessed an awareness that was not the product of time and human life experience.

In Al-Maidah 5:110, while recalling His blessings upon Jesus and his mother, The God says:

«إِذ أَيَّدتُكَ بِروحِ القُدُسِ تُكَلِّمُ النّاسَ فِى المَهدِ وَكَهلًا»

Al-Maidah 5:110, part of the verse — When I strengthened you with Spirit of The Holy; you spoke to people in the cradle and in middle age.

The phrase about Jesus speaking in the cradle and in middle age, which appears in the present verse, stands directly alongside his support through Spirit of The Holy in Al-Maidah 5:110.

Jesus’ distinction therefore does not lie solely in his extraordinary birth, since John’s birth was extraordinary too. Nor does it lie solely in judgment being granted during childhood, since John also received judgment as a child. Jesus’ particular favor should be sought in the combined evidence concerning the Spirit’s connection with his coming into being, his support through Spirit of The Holy, and the emergence of his awareness from the cradle.

In the reflection on Al-Baqarah 2:253 and in the principle of believing in all Prophets without discrimination or division, we explained that making no distinction among Prophets and Messengers does not mean denying their differing favors. The God determines these favors. Our duty is to recognize them through Quranic evidence, rather than invent ranks for Prophets on the basis of our religious affiliations.

God willing, in reflections on the following verses we will examine further how Jesus was created and his connection with the Spirit and The God’s command.
