Al-Imran 3:25

How, then, will it be when We gather them for a Day about which there is no doubt, and every soul is given in full what it has earned, and they are not wronged?

Our Reflection

In the two preceding verses, The God spoke of people who, despite having a portion of the Book, turned away from its judgment because they had deceived themselves with the belief that the Fire would touch them for only a few numbered days. Now, through a question, He directs their attention to a Day whose arrival is beyond doubt: what will they do when they are all gathered for the reckoning? On that Day, invented claims about salvation and brief punishment will no longer take the place of truth.

The expression “وَوُفِّيَتْ كُلُّ نَفْسٍ مَا كَسَبَتْ” makes the principle of reckoning clear: every soul receives in full what it has earned.

With “وَهُمْ لَا يُظْلَمُونَ”, the verse states another important truth: The God does not wrong even those who have themselves wronged others. God does not commit injustice in order to condemn injustice.

In final judgment, both truths are weighed in their proper place. God knows all human actions, circumstances, intentions, and abilities. In An-Nisa 4:40, He states that He does not wrong anyone even by the weight of a particle. In Al-Anbiya 21:47, He also speaks of setting up the scales of justice on the Day of Resurrection, so that no soul is wronged in anything, even if a deed weighs as little as a mustard seed.

The same principle appears in Al-Baqarah 2:281. There, after the commands concerning riba, financial rights, and granting time to a debtor, The God recalls humanity’s return and the full receipt of the outcome of their deeds. In both contexts, reckoning does not depend on a person’s name, position, wealth, or religious affiliation; each person is responsible for what they themselves have earned.

Through this statement, Aal Imran 3:25 both dispels fabricated assurance of salvation and rules out any notion of injustice in divine judgment. No one can escape responsibility for their conduct by inventing a promise in God’s name; and no one, even the most oppressive person, will be wronged before the justice of The God.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:23: Turning away from the judgment of the Book of The God; the context of the question about final reckoning.
  • Aal Imran 3:24: The claim of punishment for a few days and deception by invented beliefs about salvation.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:281: Returning to God and receiving in full what one has earned, without injustice.
  • Aal Imran 3:161: Full receipt of each soul’s deeds and the denial of injustice, in the context of betrayal and responsibility.
  • An-Nisa 4:40: God does not wrong anyone even by the weight of a particle, nor does He disregard goodness.
  • Al-Anbiya 21:47: The scales of justice on the Day of Resurrection, excluding injustice even in the smallest amount.
  • Al-Kahf 18:49: Deeds brought forth in the reckoning and the assurance that the Lord wrongs no one.
  • Al-Anam 6:164: Each soul’s responsibility for what it earns, without bearing another’s burden of sin.
  • Az-Zalzalah 99:7: Seeing the outcome of the smallest good deed.
  • Az-Zalzalah 99:8: Seeing the outcome of the smallest evil deed.
