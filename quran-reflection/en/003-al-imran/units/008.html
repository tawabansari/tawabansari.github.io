Al-Imran 3:8

ۚ

Our Lord, do not cause our hearts to deviate after You have guided us, and grant us mercy from Yourself; indeed, You are the Abundant Giver.

Our Reflection

This verse directly continues the words of those firmly grounded in knowledge in Aal Imran 3:7. In the preceding verse, God first spoke of those whose hearts contain deviation and who follow ambiguous passages to seek discord and their preferred interpretation. He then introduced those firmly grounded in knowledge, who say that all the clear and ambiguous verses come from our Lord. This prayer now further clarifies their conduct and position, while teaching everyone humility and the continuing need for divine guidance.

“رَبَّنَا لَا تُزِغْ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَيْتَنَا”

“Our Lord, do not cause our hearts to deviate after You have guided us.”

“Tuzigh” comes from Z Y GH, denoting inclination and deviation from a path. The same root appeared in the preceding verse as “zaygh,” describing those whose hearts have deviated from truth. In contrast to that group, we now hear the prayer of those who have been guided, asking their Lord to preserve their hearts from deviation.

This connection is fundamental. Being firmly grounded in knowledge does not mean knowing the true and ultimate interpretation of all ambiguous passages, nor does it mean immunity from error, stumbling, and deviation. If people were no longer susceptible to deviation after acquiring knowledge and faith, asking for steadfastness after guidance would lose its meaning.

In the preceding verse, God reserved knowledge of the true interpretation of ambiguous passages to Himself. If an ambiguity is completely resolved and its ultimate interpretation becomes apparent, it is no longer an unresolved mystery in that respect. Those firmly grounded in knowledge should therefore not be understood as encompassing every reality and ultimate interpretation of the Quran. Their firmness in knowledge accompanies recognition of its limits and faith in the whole Book.

The growth of human knowledge also demonstrates this limitation in practice. Today, the empirical sciences offer explanations of heredity, human genetic structure, embryonic development, the motion of celestial bodies, and the structure of the universe that were not known and expressed in this form at the time of the Quran’s revelation. Even Muhammad himself, the Messenger of God, did not explain these phenomena to people through today’s scientific accounts; nor do we possess such explanations from appointed imams or other self-proclaimed religious leaders.

In Al-Anam 6:50, the Quran states through Muhammad that he does not claim knowledge of the unseen, and in Ta-Ha 20:114, he is commanded to ask his Lord for increased knowledge. In Fussilat 41:53, God also speaks of showing His signs in the horizons and within human beings until the truth becomes clear to them.

How, then, can one go even beyond the Prophet and Messenger and regard self-proclaimed imams and appointed leaders as possessing limitless knowledge and knowing every interpretation of the Quran? Such a claim confuses limited human knowledge with The God’s encompassing knowledge. Advances in empirical science may illuminate aspects of the verses concerning creation, but this understanding still does not amount to encompassing the ultimate interpretation of all ambiguous passages.

Furthermore, the plea “Do not cause our hearts to deviate” recalls that no one should imagine that attaining understanding and faith makes them invulnerable to pride, prejudice, error, or deviation.

In Al-Isra 17:74, God reminds Muhammad himself that, had He not made him firm, he would have nearly inclined a little toward others’ wishes. In At-Tawbah 9:117, He also speaks of a group of Muhammad’s companions whose hearts nearly deviated.

In As-Saff 61:5, the Quran states that a group first deviated themselves, after which God caused their hearts to deviate. This verse shows that human orientation and choice play a role in deviation, just as preserving guidance concerns both human responsibility and the Lord’s assistance.

This prayer therefore does not ask for release from responsibility for one’s choices. It asks the Lord for help in remaining steadfast upon truth. Even someone who has acquired knowledge and followed guidance for years must guard against allowing learning and standing to turn into pride and misplaced self-assurance.

“Qulubana,” our hearts, is also plural: this prayer is not for one individual alone. The speakers ask for steadfastness for their own hearts and one another’s. In Al-Fatihah 1:6, people likewise ask the Lord for guidance to the straight path. Guidance is not a need that one act of understanding and faith removes forever.

“وَهَبْ لَنَا مِنْ لَدُنْكَ رَحْمَةً”

“And grant us mercy from Yourself.”

After asking for steadfastness in guidance comes the request for mercy.

“Wahaba,” from W H B, means giving and granting. Unlike an exchange in which something is given for a return, a gift does not leave its recipient owing a payment in exchange.

The expression “min ladunka,” from Yourself, also matters. Those praying ask for mercy from the Lord Himself. Despite their knowledge, faith, and efforts, they do not regard divine mercy as a debt they can compel God to pay on account of their deeds.

This manner of prayer appears elsewhere in the Quran. In Aal Imran 3:38, Zechariah asks his Lord to grant him good offspring from Himself. In Al-Kahf 18:10, the young people who have sought refuge to preserve their faith ask their Lord for mercy from Himself and a favorable way forward in their affair.

These prayers show that in different circumstances—from family needs to steadfastness in faith and facing hardship—people can turn directly to their Lord and ask for His mercy.

In Al-Kahf 18:65, the Quran also speaks of a servant whom God granted mercy from Himself and taught knowledge from His presence. Knowledge too is therefore a blessing that people should not regard solely as their own independent achievement. This awareness should make them humbler, rather than lead them to claim absolute superiority or freedom from the need for guidance.

“إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْوَهَّابُ”

“Indeed, You are the Abundant Giver.”

“Al-Wahhab” comes from the same root, W H B, in an intensive form: one who gives abundantly and generously. The pronoun “anta,” You, further emphasizes that the source of the generosity being sought is The God Himself.

In Sad 38:9, God is described, in connection with the treasuries of His mercy, as “the Invincible, the Abundant Giver.” No human being independently owns those treasuries—not a wealthy person, a scholar, a Prophet, or a religious leader.

Those firmly grounded in knowledge ask their Lord with this understanding. They know that, despite their knowledge and faith, they still need The God’s mercy and generosity. Divine guidance, knowledge, and mercy should not become tools for boasting and asserting superiority; they should lead to gratitude and greater responsibility.

This verse teaches that no human being, however knowledgeable and faithful, is immune to error and deviation or encompasses all truth. Firmness in knowledge should lead to humility, recognition of one’s limitations, readiness to correct mistakes, and requests for guidance and mercy from The God—not to claims of knowing everything or the construction of religious positions that place themselves beyond questions and criticism.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:7: Clear and ambiguous verses, the warning about deviation of the heart, and the words of those firmly grounded in knowledge.
  • Aal Imran 3:9: The collective prayer continues by recalling the day people are gathered and God’s fulfillment of His promise.
  • Al-Fatihah 1:6: Asking for guidance to the straight path and the continuing need for the Lord’s guidance.
  • As-Saff 61:5: People first deviate from the path, after which their hearts are made to deviate.
  • Al-Isra 17:74: Muhammad’s own need for divine firmness against the danger of inclining away from the path.
  • At-Tawbah 9:117: The hearts of some of Muhammad’s companions nearly deviating in difficult circumstances.
  • Al-Anam 6:50: Muhammad does not claim possession of God’s treasuries or independent knowledge of the unseen.
  • Ta-Ha 20:114: The command to Muhammad to ask his Lord for increased knowledge.
  • Fussilat 41:53: Divine signs becoming apparent in the horizons and within human beings.
  • Aal Imran 3:38: Zechariah’s request for good offspring from the Lord.
  • Al-Kahf 18:10: The young people seeking refuge ask for mercy and a favorable way forward from the Lord.
  • Al-Kahf 18:65: God grants mercy and knowledge to one of His servants.
  • Sad 38:9: The treasuries of divine mercy connected with “the Invincible, the Abundant Giver.”
