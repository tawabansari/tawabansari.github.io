Al-Imran 3:33

Surely The God chose Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham, and the family of Imran above all peoples.

Our Reflection

After the commands to follow and obey The God and the Messenger in the preceding verses, the Quran now names those He chose: Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham, and the family of Imran.

“Iṣṭafā,” from Ṣ F W, relates to clarity, purity, and choosing. Here it means “chose.” The word itself does not necessarily mean being purified of every error or having undergone particular tests; it speaks of divine selection.

Adam and Noah are named individually, whereas Abraham and Imran appear with “āl,” which in this context refers to their families and connections through descent.

However, a family's being chosen does not mean that worthiness and virtue automatically pass to every descendant. In Al-Baqarah 2:124, when Abraham asks about his descendants, The God replies that His covenant does not reach wrongdoers. In Hud 11:46, Noah's son's family relationship does not bring him within the promise of deliverance because of his unrighteous conduct. Family descent alone therefore does not guarantee individual worthiness.

Some have inferred from Adam's being chosen here the possibility that other humans existed before him and that he was selected from among them. Yet the verse says “ʿalā al-ʿālamīn,” meaning “above all peoples,” rather than “mina al-ʿālamīn,” meaning “from among all peoples.” The existence of humans before Adam therefore cannot be established from this expression alone, although raising and considering such a question can itself provide an opportunity for reflection and research.

The next verse, Aal Imran 3:34, speaks of the continuity of the generations mentioned. Then Aal Imran 3:35 begins the story of Imran's family with his wife's vow, and the positions of Mary and Jesus become clearer as the account unfolds. This verse thus introduces the divinely chosen people and the story of Imran's family in the remainder of the surah.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:34 — The continuity of generations in the account of those chosen by The God.
  • Aal Imran 3:35 — The story of Imran’s family beginning with his wife’s vow.
  • Aal Imran 3:42 — Mary’s selection and purification, with distinct verbs for choosing and purifying.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:124 — Abraham’s appointment to leadership and the divine covenant not reaching wrongdoers among his descendants.
  • Hud 11:46 — Noah’s son excluded from the promise of deliverance because of unrighteous conduct; family ties alone are insufficient.
  • Maryam 19:58 — Groups of Prophets and chosen people from different generations and communities.
  • An-Nisa 4:54 — Scripture, wisdom, and great dominion granted to Abraham’s family.
  • Al-Hajj 22:75 — Choosing Messengers from angels and human beings.
  • Fatir 35:32 — People chosen to inherit the Book and differences in their conduct.
