Al-Imran 3:24

ۖ

That is because they said, “The Fire will touch us for only a few numbered days”; and what they fabricated deceived them in their religion.

Our Reflection

In the preceding verse, The God spoke of people who had received a portion of the Book, yet when they were called to the judgment of the Book of The God, a group of them turned away. He now makes the reason for this conduct clear: they had convinced themselves that the Fire would touch them for only a few numbered days. The same claim appears in Al-Baqarah 2:80. There, The God asks whether they have a covenant with Him guaranteeing this, or whether they attribute to Him something they do not know. In the present verse, however, the effect of this belief on human conduct and religious life becomes apparent.

The human being is hasty. People tend to pay greater attention to what is near and readily available, sometimes overlooking future consequences for the sake of a passing pleasure or benefit. In Al-Isra 17:11, the Qur’an describes the human being as hasty. In Al-Qiyamah 75:20 and Al-Qiyamah 75:21, it speaks of loving the immediate life and neglecting the Hereafter. Al-Insan 76:27 recalls the same disposition: some love the fleeting life and leave behind them a weighty Day that lies ahead.

Al-Araf 7:169 is one of the clearest Qur’anic examples of this conduct. The God speaks of people who inherited the Book but take the goods of this immediate life and say, “We will be forgiven.” He then adds that if similar goods come their way again, they take them again. They have not merely slipped once; they repeat their conduct and soothe their conscience each time with the hope of forgiveness. The God asks whether the covenant of the Book had not been taken from them that they should say nothing about Him except the truth, and reminds them that the abode of the Hereafter is better for those who guard themselves.

Here the relationship between immediate desires, religious belief, and human conduct becomes clear. A person may violate someone else’s rights to obtain wealth, power, or a passing advantage. When their conscience reproaches them, instead of restoring those rights and correcting their conduct, they reassure themselves that God is forgiving and will eventually forgive them. They may even assure themselves that because they belong to the correct denomination, any punishment they suffer will be temporary. Such a belief no longer motivates a return and a change in conduct; it has become a means of continuing the same conduct.

The expression “وَغَرَّهُمْ فِي دِينِهِمْ مَا كَانُوا يَفْتَرُونَ” exposes this danger: what they fabricated had deceived them too. A person may accept an unsupported claim to justify their desires and become so attached to it that they are no longer willing to hear a truth that contradicts it. The danger arises when this deception takes root within a person’s religion and religious life, and religious belief becomes a means of escaping responsibility instead of making them answerable to the truth.

This warning is not limited to the followers of one scripture or denomination. Anyone who imagines guaranteed salvation for themselves under the name Muslim, Jewish, Christian, Sunni, Shia, or any other designation must show where The God has given such a guarantee. An-Nisa 4:123 likewise makes clear that the reckoning is based neither on the wishes of those addressed by the Qur’an nor on the wishes of the People of the Book. Hope in God’s mercy and forgiveness differs from claiming a special privilege to disobey and wrong others. This issue is examined in detail in “Blind to Ourselves, Quick to Judge Others”.

The reason for turning away from the judgment of the Book in the preceding verse can now be understood more clearly. Someone who has already reassured themselves that they are safe from the serious consequences of disobedience may be unwilling to give up an immediate advantage to accept God’s judgment. Instead of correcting their beliefs and conduct in accordance with the Book of The God, they construct a belief about God that allows them to live as they please. This is the deception against which Aal Imran 3:24 warns.

Cross-references
  • Aal Imran 3:23: A group given a portion of the Book turns away when called to its judgment.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:80: The same claim of punishment for a few days, and the demand for a divine covenant supporting it.
  • Al-Baqarah 2:81: Rejecting unfounded assurance about punishment and describing one encompassed by sin.
  • Al-Araf 7:169: Taking worldly goods, expecting forgiveness, and repeating the conduct; the covenant of the Book and the superiority of the Hereafter.
  • Al-Isra 17:11: Human hastiness.
  • Al-Qiyamah 75:20: Love of the immediate, fleeting life.
  • Al-Qiyamah 75:21: Neglecting the Hereafter through attachment to the present life.
  • Al-Insan 76:27: Preferring the fleeting life and leaving behind a weighty Day.
  • Al-Hadid 57:14: Being deceived by hopes and imaginings.
  • An-Nisa 4:123: Group wishes and claims are not the criterion for salvation and reckoning.
  • Aal Imran 3:25: Gathering on the Day of Reckoning and receiving the full outcome of deeds without injustice.
