Surah Al-Fatihah

(The Opening)

7 Verses

This page presents the full surah in one reading flow. Each verse has a stable reference link for direct citation.

Verse 1

Reference link

بِسمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحمٰنِ الرَّحيمِ

English Translation

By Name of The God The Merciful The Compassionate.

Qur’anic Reflection

This opening begins by orienting speech, thought, and action toward The God. Mercy and compassion are placed before the reader as the first doorway into the Qur’an, so the journey begins with dependence, gratitude, and humility rather than ownership or self-claim.

Verse 2

Reference link

الحَمدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ العٰلَمينَ

English Translation

All praise belongs to The God, Lord of the worlds.

Qur’anic Reflection

Praise is not presented as flattery, but as recognition of reality. The One being praised is not a tribal deity or a local power, but the Lord of all worlds. This expands the heart beyond identity, tribe, language, and nation.

Verse 3

Reference link

الرَّحمٰنِ الرَّحيمِ

English Translation

The Merciful, The Compassionate.

Qur’anic Reflection

The repetition of mercy and compassion deepens the opening. Before judgment is mentioned, mercy is established. This prevents the soul from reading divine authority as cruelty, and reminds the reader that God’s rule is not separated from His mercy.

Verse 4

Reference link

مٰلِكِ يَومِ الدّينِ

English Translation

Master of the Day of Judgment.

Qur’anic Reflection

The Day of Judgment places human life inside moral accountability. No worldly rank, inherited authority, sectarian claim, or social power owns the final verdict. Judgment belongs to The God alone.

Verse 5

Reference link

إِيّاكَ نَعبُدُ وَإِيّاكَ نَستَعينُ

English Translation

You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.

Qur’anic Reflection

This verse is the covenantal center of the surah. Worship and dependence are directed to The God alone. It rejects both arrogance and spiritual dependency on created intermediaries.

Verse 6

Reference link

اهدِنَا الصِّرٰطَ المُستَقيمَ

English Translation

Guide us to the straight path.

Qur’anic Reflection

Guidance is asked for directly from The God. The seeker admits that knowing the path is not the same as walking it, and that the straight path requires divine help, sincerity, and correction.

Verse 7

Reference link

صِرٰطَ الَّذينَ أَنعَمتَ عَلَيهِم غَيرِ المَغضوبِ عَلَيهِم وَلَا الضّالّينَ

English Translation

The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor, not of those who have drawn anger, nor of those who are lost.

Qur’anic Reflection

The straight path is described through moral direction, not sectarian labels. The seeker asks for the path of divine favor, while seeking protection from paths shaped by rejection, corruption, anger, or loss.
