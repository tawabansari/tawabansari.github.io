Article and Note

Why is Human Corruption Restricted to Earth?

A linguistic and philosophical inquiry into the limits of human agency and the cosmic order of the Seven Heavens

In the name of The-God, The-Merciful, The-Compassionate

In the Qur’an, the heavens and the earth are frequently mentioned together. When the discourse is about creation, divine ownership, glorification (tasbīḥ), cosmic order, or the truth of existence, the Qur’an usually brings them together—as if the entire universe, from the highest spiritual planes of creation to the immediate field of human life, exists under the ownership and governance of the unique Deity.

يُسَبِّحُ لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ (yusabbiḥu lillāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍi) (62:001)
وَلِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ (wa-lillāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍi) (2:284)
خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بِالْحَقِّ (khalaqa al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍa bi-al-ḥaqqi) (6:073)

In these verses, the heavens and the earth are seen together. Creation, glorification, ownership, and the truth of existence demonstrate a unified system; a system in which the earth is not separate from the heavens, and the heavens are not defined apart from the earth. Both exist in a larger network of divine command, measure, order, and wisdom.

However, when the Qur’an speaks of human corruption (fasād), the expression changes. "Heavens and earth" are no longer paired. Human corruption, in the Qur’an, is attributed solely to the Earth:

وَلَا تُفْسِدُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ بَعْدَ إِصْلَاحِهَا (wa-lā tufsidū fī al-arḍi baʿda iṣlāḥihā) (7:056)
وَيَسْعَوْنَ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَسَادًا (wa-yasʿawna fī al-arḍi fasādan) (5:033)
وَإِذَا تَوَلَّىٰ سَعَىٰ فِي الْأَرْضِ لِيُفْسِدَ فِيهَا (wa-idhā tawallā saʿā fī al-arḍi liyufsida fīhā) (2:205)
وَلَا تَبْغِ الْفَسَادَ فِي الْأَرْضِ (wa-lā tabghi al-fasāda fī al-arḍi) (28:077)

Even when the Qur’an shows the scope of corruption to be vast, it still does not take it out of the jurisdiction of the Earth:

ظَهَرَ الْفَسَادُ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِي النَّاسِ (ẓahara al-fasādu fī al-barri wa-al-baḥri bimā kasabat aydī al-nāsi) (30:041)

Land and sea are both fields of human earthly life. Therefore, this linguistic difference is not accidental. When the Qur’an speaks of the entire order of creation, it pairs the heavens and the earth; but when it speaks of human corruption, it identifies the field of corruption as the earth. This opens a fundamental question: Why is human corruption only on earth?

1. The Earthen Jurisdiction of Choice

The Qur’an states regarding the status of man: "Indeed, I am placing a vicegerent (khalifah) on Earth." (2:030). Man is the vicegerent *on earth*, not the vicegerent of the heavens. The field of choice, trial, cultivation, destruction, justice, oppression, growth, tyranny, mercy, and corruption is the earth. Therefore, it is natural that the Qur’an records his corruption in the same realm: fī al-Arḍ.

Wherever man has the power of disposal, he has responsibility; and wherever he has responsibility, he can either reform or corrupt. This question has become more serious today. Man no longer just walks on the soil. He has gone into space, touched the moon, placed satellites in orbit, and even polluted near-earth orbits with space debris, military competition, and political greed. So, has the scope of human corruption gone beyond the earth? Or should we ask more accurately: are the atmosphere, Earth's orbit, and the immediate vicinity of this planet still within the realm of al-Arḍ, rather than the stable and grand al-Samāwāt of which the Qur’an speaks?

2. The Universal Law of Corruption

Some argue that "corruption on earth" should not be extended to modern air pollution, environmental destruction, the atmospheric crisis, plastics, factories, or industrial waste, because the people at the time of revelation did not have these tools. However, this view imprisons the Qur’an within the tools of the 7th century. The Qur’an does not just name the tools; it defines the law of corruption.

The Qur’an did not say corruption only occurs with swords, tribes, or primitive bloodshed. It said corruption appears on land and sea because of what the hands of people have earned. This expression is not historically limited to past tools. Tools change, but the sunnah (tradition) of corruption remains the same. Yesterday, man corrupted through oppression and the destruction of crops; today, the same spirit of corruption manifests through industrial poisons, the destruction of forests, and the pollution of the earth's orbit. The question is: do these actions turn a reformed system into ruin? If they do, they fall under the divine warning: "Do not corrupt on earth after its reformation." (7:056).

3. The Error of Reducing "Heavens" to Atmosphere

The word Sama (heaven) in the Qur’an is a tiered term. Sometimes it means the upward direction. Sometimes it refers to the place where rain and provision descend. And sometimes, especially in the term al-Samāwāt, it refers to the grand, stable, tiered, and transcendent structure of creation. We cannot, under the pretext of its breadth, limit it to the atmosphere wherever we wish. Meaning must harmonize with context and the Qur’an itself.

This is where the simplistic view of "Seven Heavens = Atmospheric Layers" faces a serious problem. To bring the Qur’an closer to modern science, some have claimed that the Seven Heavens are the troposphere, stratosphere, etc. If examined closely, this creates textual contradictions. The Qur’an speaks of the "nearest heaven" adorned with stars:

إِنَّا زَيَّنَّا السَّمَاءَ الدُّنْيَا بِزِينَةٍ الْكَوَاكِبِ (innā zayyannā al-samāʾa al-dunyā bi-zīnatin al-kawākibi) (37:006)
وَلَقَدْ زَيَّنَّا السَّمَاءَ الدُّنْيَا بِمَصَابِيحَ (wa-laqad zayyannā al-samāʾa al-dunyā bi-maṣābīḥa) (67:005)

If the nearest heaven were merely the first layer of the atmosphere, it would imply that all stars and galaxies exist within Earth's gas shell. This is a scientific and textual absurdity. Reducing the Heavens to the atmosphere makes the text vulnerable to unnecessary doubt. Atmosphere is part of the Earth's shield; it is not the grand Heavens of the Qur’an.

Furthermore, the atmosphere is not expanding cosmically. The verse: "And the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed, We are its expanders" (51:047) refers to the large-scale structure of the universe where galaxies move apart. Imagine an elastic surface, like a stretchable carpet, being pulled from all sides. Points on it move away not because they grow, but because the fabric itself expands. This cosmic image is far closer to the Qur’anic Heaven than a few layers of gas around Earth.

4. The Celestial Origin of Water and Provision

The Qur’an says: "And He sent down water from the sky." The desert dweller saw rain, and that was sufficient. But this is not the full horizon. Today we know that clouds and the atmosphere are not the primary producers of water; they cycle existing water through evaporation and precipitation. The original source of Earth's water is a deep question in planetary science. Studies into the deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio and samples from asteroids like Bennu and Ryugu suggest that Earth's water and life-essential molecules were delivered from space during the planet's formation.

This shows the depth of Qur’anic language. It speaks to the desert dweller without being imprisoned by his understanding. He understood rain; the modern human understands the cosmic delivery of life's building blocks. Both stand before the same expression: "sending water from heaven." Heaven is the source of giving; Earth is the vessel of acceptance and responsibility. If man pollutes the water once it settles on earth, the corruption remains fī al-Arḍ. It occurs where the hand of man has disposal, not at the source of the blessing.

5. The Concept of "Taskheer" and Human Flight

The-God speaks of the heavens and earth being subjected (taskhīr) to man, but this subjection is not on the same level. Earth is for disposal, cultivation, and physical modification. The heavens are subjected through light, time, order, and signs. Man benefits from the sun and moon but does not own their orbits. When man goes into space, he has not conquered the heavens; he is forced to follow their laws with absolute precision. The smallest error destroys him. In space, he lives inside a capsule and a suit—carrying a piece of Earth's conditions with him. If he litters the orbit with debris, it is the continuation of his earthly corruption into his expanded jurisdiction.

6. Why was such a being chosen as Khalifah?

If humans cause such corruption, why were they chosen as vicegerents? The angels identified the risk: "Will You place therein one who corrupts and sheds blood?" (2:030). But the Divine response—"I know what you do not know"—did not deny the risk. It pointed toward human potential. Man can be compared to a form of **Biological Intelligence**. Like an AI without data, human intelligence is raw; but it grows through history, trial, and collective learning. Man possesses agency and conscience. History is not just war; it is a record of Tawbah (reform), learning, and discovery.

Conclusion

The distinction between Earth and Heaven is profound. Earth is the field of responsibility; the Heavens are the mirror of Divine majesty beyond our reach. Corruption is limited to the Earth because it is the field of our choice. We may pollute our surroundings, but we cannot disrupt the grand cosmic order. Man will corrupt as long as he treats Earth as property; once he treats it as a trust (amanah), reform begins. The secret of khalifah is not biological power, but the capacity for recognition, learning, and turning awareness into accountability.
