Language as a fingerprint of origin

In modern textual study, words are not merely tools for carrying meaning. They are signs that disclose the origin, setting, and period of a text. The language of a text functions like a fingerprint. Just as an archaeologist can recognize the age of a civilization through layers of soil, a linguist can often recognize the mental world and historical layer of a text through vocabulary, metaphors, sentence structure, and patterns of expression.

If this standard is applied to religious texts, the result is illuminating. The difference between the language of the Qur’an, the language of the Gospels, and the language of Hadith reveals the difference between a revelatory text and a historical text.

The historical trace in the Bible and Gospels

In the study of the Bible, many researchers have concluded that we are not dealing with one single and internally uniform text, but with a collection of different traditions. In some passages, God is called “Elohim”; in others, “YHWH.” This difference is not merely lexical. It points to textual layers, communities, and inherited traditions that came from different settings.

In some narratives, God is described as utterly transcendent and far removed from embodiment. In others, the text speaks of God walking in a garden or wrestling with a human being. These differences are part of what led scholars to the documentary hypothesis.

The New Testament also shows this phenomenon. The Gospel of John begins with language strongly colored by Greek philosophical thought: “In the beginning was the Word.” The term Logos carries a philosophical background. Other Gospels, such as Mark and Matthew, read more like historical narratives. This difference indicates that these writings are not the product of one time, one mind, and one linguistic horizon.

The Qur’an’s unified linguistic signature

When we turn to the Qur’an, we meet a different phenomenon. From beginning to end, the Qur’an carries one linguistic fingerprint. From Al-Fatihah to An-Nas, despite the variety of subjects, a stable rhetorical structure remains visible. Sometimes it speaks of law, sometimes of earlier messengers, sometimes of nature, sometimes of moral life. Yet its language does not leave its own horizon.

One of the most important features of this language is absolute divine transcendence. The Qur’an introduces The God in a way that negates every material likeness:

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ

Nothing is like Him, and He is The Hearing, The Seeing.

Ash-Shura 42:11

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ۝ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ

Say: He is The God, One; The God, The Self-Sufficient; He neither begets nor is begotten; and there is none comparable to Him.

Al-Ikhlas 112:1-4

Even when the Qur’an speaks of “the hand of The God,” it places the expression within the field of power and authority, not material embodiment:

يَدُ اللَّهِ فَوْقَ أَيْدِيهِمْ

The hand of The God is above their hands.

Al-Fath 48:10

When the Qur’an speaks of divine greatness, even its metaphors remain in the realm of meaning:

اللَّهُ نُورُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ

The God is the light of the heavens and the earth.

An-Nur 24:35

Hadith language and its distance from Qur’anic transcendence

Now apply the same standard to Hadith. In many reports, the language suddenly moves away from the Qur’an’s level of transcendence and becomes strikingly material.

تَقُولُ جَهَنَّمُ هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ حَتَّى يَضَعَ رَبُّ الْعِزَّةِ فِيهَا قَدَمَهُ

Hell says, “Is there more?” until the Lord of Majesty places His foot in it.

Bukhari, Muslim

وَمَا تَرَدَّدْتُ عَنْ شَيْءٍ أَنَا فَاعِلُهُ تَرَدُّدِي عَنْ نَفْسِ الْمُؤْمِنِ

I have not hesitated over anything I do as I hesitate over taking the soul of the believer.

Bukhari

يُؤْذِينِي ابْنُ آدَمَ يَسُبُّ الدَّهْرَ وَأَنَا الدَّهْرُ

The son of Adam harms Me; he curses time, and I am time.

Bukhari, Muslim

When these statements are compared with Qur’anic language, the difference becomes immediate. The Qur’an purifies The God from any imagined likeness with created things. These reports, however, attribute expressions such as placing a foot, hesitating, or being harmed to The God.

This is not merely a theological disagreement. It is a linguistic sign. Qur’anic language remains at the height of transcendence, while many reports bring divine speech down into the field of human experience.

الْكِبْرِيَاءُ رِدَائِي وَالْعَظَمَةُ إِزَارِي

Majesty is My cloak and greatness is My lower garment.

Sahih Muslim

This kind of imagery resembles the figurative language of preachers and storytellers who explain abstract concepts through clothing, tools, and bodily images. The Qur’an, by contrast, has a remarkable economy of language. No word feels excessive. Each word is placed with precision. Many reports contain long repetitions and explanatory additions, the natural style of oral narration and public preaching.

If Hadith and reports were revelation in the same sense as the Qur’an, we would expect to see the same linguistic fingerprint: the same rhetorical structure, the same kind of metaphor, and the same level of divine transcendence. But the visible difference shows that we are dealing with two kinds of text: one with a stable and unified signature, the Qur’an; and another made of reports that formed within history and carry the mental world and culture of their time.

A comparison: Ayat al-Kursi and a Hadith Qudsi

A clear example of the Qur’an’s linguistic structure can be seen in Ayat al-Kursi. It is a long verse, yet it possesses precise syntactic and rhetorical order:

اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ ۚ لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۗ مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِنْدَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ ۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِّنْ عِلْمِهِ إِلَّا بِمَا شَاءَ ۚ وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ ۖ وَلَا يَئُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْعَظِيمُ

The God, there is no god except Him, The Living, The Self-Subsisting. Neither drowsiness nor sleep takes Him. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth. Who can intercede with Him except by His permission? He knows what is before them and what is behind them. They encompass nothing of His knowledge except what He wills. His kursi extends over the heavens and the earth, and preserving them does not burden Him. He is The High, The Magnificent.

Al-Baqarah 2:255

Beside it, a long Hadith Qudsi in Sahih Bukhari 6502 moves in a different register: step-by-step narration, bodily imagery, and the language of hesitation.

مَنْ عَادَى لِي وَلِيًّا فَقَدْ آذَنْتُهُ بِالْحَرْبِ، وَمَا تَقَرَّبَ إِلَيَّ عَبْدِي بِشَيْءٍ أَحَبَّ إِلَيَّ مِمَّا افْتَرَضْتُهُ عَلَيْهِ، وَلَا يَزَالُ عَبْدِي يَتَقَرَّبُ إِلَيَّ بِالنَّوَافِلِ حَتَّى أُحِبَّهُ، فَإِذَا أَحْبَبْتُهُ كُنْتُ سَمْعَهُ الَّذِي يَسْمَعُ بِهِ، وَبَصَرَهُ الَّذِي يُبْصِرُ بِهِ، وَيَدَهُ الَّتِي يَبْطِشُ بِهَا، وَرِجْلَهُ الَّتِي يَمْشِي بِهَا، وَإِنْ سَأَلَنِي لَأُعْطِيَنَّهُ، وَلَئِنِ اسْتَعَاذَنِي لَأُعِيذَنَّهُ، وَمَا تَرَدَّدْتُ عَنْ شَيْءٍ أَنَا فَاعِلُهُ تَرَدُّدِي عَنْ نَفْسِ الْمُؤْمِنِ، يَكْرَهُ الْمَوْتَ، وَأَنَا أَكْرَهُ مَسَاءَتَهُ.

Whoever shows hostility to a friend of Mine, I have declared war against him... When I love him, I become his hearing with which he hears, his sight with which he sees, his hand with which he grasps, and his foot with which he walks... I have not hesitated over anything I do as I hesitate over taking the soul of the believer.

Sahih Bukhari 6502

Ayat al-Kursi never leaves its central path: transcendence, sovereignty, knowledge, and divine independence. Even when it speaks through imagery, such as kursi, the image remains at the level of meaning. The Hadith, however, carries the style of report, sermon, and earthly imagery. The difference is not only semantic; it is a difference in kind of language.

Al-Baqarah 2:23-24 and the challenge of producing an equal

The Qur’an gives its own criterion:

وَإِن كُنتُمْ فِي رَيْبٍ مِّمَّا نَزَّلْنَا عَلَىٰ عَبْدِنَا فَأْتُوا بِسُورَةٍ مِّن مِّثْلِهِ وَادْعُوا شُهَدَاءَكُم مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ إِن كُنتُمْ صَادِقِينَ

If you are in doubt about what We have sent down upon Our servant, then bring a surah like it, and call your witnesses besides The God, if you are truthful.

Al-Baqarah 2:23

This verse is not merely a literary invitation. It is a structural criterion. The Qur’an says: if this speech is not from The God, then produce something equal to it; a text that can stand at the same level in language, rhetoric, coherence, and depth of meaning.

فَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا فَاتَّقُوا النَّارَ الَّتِي وَقُودُهَا النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَةُ ۖ أُعِدَّتْ لِلْكَافِرِينَ

So if you do not do it, and you will never do it, then guard yourselves against the Fire whose fuel is people and stones, prepared for those who cover the truth.

Al-Baqarah 2:24

The expression “if you do not do it, and you will never do it” moves the matter from hypothetical possibility to structural inability. The issue is not that humanity has not yet had enough time to compose an equal text. The issue is that revelation carries an order not produced by ordinary human speech.

This is where the position of reports becomes clear. Human beings have produced systems of narration and report, and at times have given them a weight close to revelation, even in the form of statements attributed to The God. Yet when these texts are examined linguistically, structurally, semantically, and theologically, their difference from the Qur’an becomes visible.

The Qur’anic challenge is not to produce merely religious speech or spiritual writing. It is to produce a structure equal to the speech of The God. A Hadith Qudsi, sermon, philosophical text, or moral report may be beautiful or influential, but when placed before the criterion of Al-Baqarah 2:23-24, the trace of human language becomes visible. This is where the Qur’an is not only revelation; it is also the measure by which other texts are weighed.

Conclusion

The result of this study is simple but important. The Qur’an is a text with one unified linguistic signature. It was revealed over twenty-three years, yet its style did not fracture. Hadith, by contrast, is a collection of historical reports gathered in later centuries, and its language reflects the culture, assumptions, and narrative habits of those periods.

For this reason, the difference between the transcendent Arabic of the Qur’an and the narrative language of Hadith is not merely literary. It is a sign of different origins. Anyone who looks carefully at this difference will find it difficult to believe that expressions such as “cloak and lower garment” or “placing a foot in Hell” are the same kind of speech as the text whose Lord is described by: Nothing is like Him.

The discussion therefore ends where the Qur’an itself places it: if someone doubts the divine origin of the Qur’an, the test is clear. Let him bring a surah like it. As long as this has not been done, the Qur’an remains a text with the unified signature of revelation; a text that is itself the criterion, and before which other texts are measured.

The Qur’an is the measure, not because later reports validate it, but because it carries in its own language, structure, meaning, and transcendence the independent sign of revelation.
