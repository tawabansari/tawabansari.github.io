The Qur’an’s Authority

Qur’an Completeness

Articles on the Qur’an as complete, preserved, sufficient, and the final criterion.

This section examines a foundational question: is the Qur’an sufficient and final as the criterion for religious guidance, truth and falsehood, tawhid, worship, justice, taqwa, the Hereafter, and the boundaries of faith and wrongdoing; or should the Qur’an’s authority be placed under later reports, sectarian legal systems, inherited religious customs, and religious power?

The articles here study Qur’an completeness from within the Qur’an itself: divine preservation, the difference between Qur’an and Hadith, the danger of following probability as religion, the public transmission of the Qur’an, the Prophet’s role as deliverer of the message, the relation between Qur’an and later religious traditions, and why no statement can be placed before the signs of The God.

What Is This Section About?

The Qur’an as the preserved, clear, and final criterion of religion, not merely one source beside equal sources.

What Will Be Examined Here?

Claims that the Qur’an is incomplete, Qur’an and Hadith, probable reports, Qur’anic tawatur, and the authority of The God’s signs.

What Is the Method?

The Qur’an is read through the Qur’an; reports, history, law, and religious customs are measured before the Qur’an.
