**In the name of The-God, The-Merciful, The-Compassionate** The Qur'an, in [Ar-Rum 30:35](/quran-reflection/en/030-ar-rum/035/), challenges the intellectual structure of those who fracture religion into sects and inherited claims: > **أَمْ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَـٰنًا فَهُوَ يَتَكَلَّمُ بِمَا كَانُوا۟ بِهِۦ يُشْرِكُونَ** > > Or have We sent down to them a sultan that speaks concerning what they associate with Him? To understand the force of this question, the word **sultan** cannot be flattened into a dead external object. The verse itself gives us markers: something sent down, something authoritative, and most strikingly, something that **speaks**.

How Earlier Commentators Understood Sultan

Classical and later commentators often read **sultan** as an external proof: a book, a written document, a miracle, an angelic authorization, legal dominance, or kingship. Al-Tabari reads it mainly as textual evidence, narrative proof, or a miracle from God. Ibn Kathir continues this line and treats it as a speaking book that would legitimize what they associate with God. Ibn Taymiyyah restricts **sultan** across the Qur'an to revealed book, transmitted proof, or prophetic miracle. Later reformist and literary commentators also often remain in the same general frame: **sultan** as an external document, power, or warrant. That reading captures part of the field, but it does not fully account for the living structure of [Ar-Rum 30:35](/quran-reflection/en/030-ar-rum/035/): **a sultan that speaks**.

Four Structural Attributes of Sultan

When the verse is read carefully, four structural attributes appear.

1. It Is Sent Down

**Sultan** is not manufactured from below by tribal custom, social pressure, inherited authority, or sectarian habit. It is connected to something sent down from above: a trans-human criterion within creation.

2. It Appears Indefinite

The word appears as **سُلْطَـٰنًا**, indefinite. We are not dealing with the absolute essence of God's command, but with a mechanism, a law, or an embedded criterion through which truth can be measured.

3. It Carries Dominance

The root field of sultan carries force, authority, dominance, and penetration. When true sultan enters the arena, it cuts through superstition, inherited fear, false authority, and unsupported claims.

4. It Speaks

This is the most important feature in [Ar-Rum 30:35](/quran-reflection/en/030-ar-rum/035/). The Qur'an does not ask whether they possess a silent object. It asks whether they possess a **sultan that speaks**. That means articulate authority: a criterion capable of exposing contradiction, clarifying claims, and stating truth coherently.

Four Qur'anic Dimensions of Sultan

Across the Qur'an, the uses of **sultan** can be grouped into four broad dimensions.

1. Sultan Between the Human Being, Satan, and Truth

[Al-Isra 17:65](/quran-reflection/en/017-al-isra/065/) says: > **إِنَّ عِبَادِي لَيْسَ لَكَ عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَانٌ وَكَفَى بِرَبِّكَ وَكِيلًا** > > Indeed, over My servants you have no sultan, and sufficient is your Lord as disposer of affairs. Other verses in this field include [An-Nahl 16:99](/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/099/), [An-Nahl 16:100](/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/100/), [Al-Hijr 15:42](/quran-reflection/en/015-al-hijr/042/), [Saba 34:21](/quran-reflection/en/034-saba/021/), [Ibrahim 14:22](/quran-reflection/en/014-ibrahim/022/), and [As-Saffat 37:30](/quran-reflection/en/037-as-saffat/030/). In these verses, Satan has invitation, whisper, illusion, and manipulation, but he does not possess true **sultan** over God's servants. Sultan marks the boundary between objective authority and empty suggestion.

2. Sultan Against Arbitrary Naming

[Al-A'raf 7:71](/quran-reflection/en/007-al-araf/071/) says: > **أَتُجَادِلُونَنِي فِي أَسْمَاءٍ سَمَّيْتُمُوهَا أَنْتُمْ وَآبَاؤُكُمْ مَا نَزَّلَ اللَّهُ بِهَا مِنْ سُلْطَانٍ** > > Do you dispute with me concerning names you have named, you and your forefathers, for which The-God has sent down no sultan? Other related verses include [An-Najm 53:23](/quran-reflection/en/053-an-najm/023/), [Yusuf 12:40](/quran-reflection/en/012-yusuf/040/), [Yunus 10:68](/quran-reflection/en/010-yunus/068/), [Al-A'raf 7:33](/quran-reflection/en/007-al-araf/033/), and [Al-Kahf 18:15](/quran-reflection/en/018-al-kahf/015/). Here, **sultan** stands against invented names, inherited labels, sectarian constructions, and claims attributed to God without objective grounding.

3. Sultan as Articulate Exposition Against Shirk

[Ar-Rum 30:35](/quran-reflection/en/030-ar-rum/035/) is central: > **أَمْ أَنْزَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَانًا فَهُوَ يَتَكَلَّمُ بِمَا كَانُوا بِهِ يُشْرِكُونَ** > > Or have We sent down to them a sultan that speaks concerning what they associate with Him? Other verses in this field include [Ghafir 40:56](/quran-reflection/en/040-ghafir/056/), [Ghafir 40:35](/quran-reflection/en/040-ghafir/035/), [Aal-Imran 3:151](/quran-reflection/en/003-aal-imran/151/), [Al-Hajj 22:71](/quran-reflection/en/022-al-hajj/071/), and [Al-An'am 6:81](/quran-reflection/en/006-al-anam/081/). This is where the Qur'an reveals the unique attribute: **sultan speaks**. It is not mere volume, rank, tradition, or emotional certainty. It is articulate authority that can withstand contradiction.

4. Sultan as Dominance, Immunity, and Breakthrough

[Al-Mu'minun 23:45](/quran-reflection/en/023-al-muminun/045/) says: > **ثُمَّ أَرْسَلْنَا مُوسَى وَأَخَاهُ هَارُونَ بِآيَاتِنَا وَسُلْطَانٍ مُبِينٍ** > > Then We sent Musa and his brother Harun with Our signs and a manifest sultan. This wider network includes verses such as [Hud 11:96](/quran-reflection/en/011-hud/096/), [Al-Isra 17:80](/quran-reflection/en/017-al-isra/080/), [Al-Qasas 28:35](/quran-reflection/en/028-al-qasas/035/), [Ad-Dukhan 44:19](/quran-reflection/en/044-ad-dukhan/019/), [Ghafir 40:23](/quran-reflection/en/040-ghafir/023/), [Al-Isra 17:33](/quran-reflection/en/017-al-isra/033/), [An-Naml 27:21](/quran-reflection/en/027-an-naml/021/), and [Ar-Rahman 55:33](/quran-reflection/en/055-ar-rahman/033/). In this field, sultan is authority that breaks through, grants proof, protects from baseless attack, and establishes a decisive position.

Sultan as Logic

When these verses are placed beside each other, **sultan** emerges as more than power and more than document. It is articulate authority. It is proof with force. It is a criterion that speaks. This is why the connection with logic is powerful. The Arabic word **mantiq** is connected with **n-t-q**, speech and articulation. Logic is not merely private thought; it is ordered articulation that can expose contradiction. So when the Qur'an asks whether they possess a **sultan that speaks**, it is asking whether their claim has an articulate, contradiction-resistant authority behind it.

The Historical Problem of Language

Part of the difficulty is historical. Formal logic as a named discipline entered the Islamic intellectual world through translation and philosophical vocabulary. The word **mantiq** became the Arabic carrier for what Greek philosophy had called logos or logic. But the Qur'an did not need to borrow the later technical term in order to point to the function. It had already placed the function inside its own vocabulary: **sultan** as decisive, articulate, truth-bearing authority.

Logic as the Sultan of the Human Being

In this sense, logic is one expression of the human being's sultan. Through it, contradictions are exposed, false names are challenged, superstition is pierced, and creation becomes intelligible. This is why blocking knowledge, reason, and education is not a small social crime. It is a blockage of access to sultan: the capacity to test claims, uncover patterns, measure reality, and refuse inherited falsehood.

Final Reflection

The Qur'an does not merely ask whether they possess a document. It asks whether they possess a **sultan that speaks**. A living authority. A criterion. A logic. A force that exposes contradiction. A light that pierces illusion. So the question remains: do we follow inherited names and sectarian shadows, or do we seek the sultan that speaks with truth?
