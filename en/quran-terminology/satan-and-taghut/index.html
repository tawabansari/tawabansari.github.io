--- layout: post title: "Why Did The God Create the Field of Satan and Taghut?" date: 2026-07-05 lang: en topic: quran-terminology term: satan-and-taghut tags: [quran, satan, taghut, choice, sultan, self] excerpt: "A Qur'an-centered reflection on Satan, Taghut, moral choice, the self, and sultan as the inner criterion that exposes deception." permalink: /en/quran-terminology/satan-and-taghut/ ---
**In the name of The-God, The-Merciful, The-Compassionate** One of the weightiest questions in understanding the human being and the Qur'an is this: if The God is Merciful and Compassionate, why did He create a world in which Satan, whispering, Taghut, deception, falling, and misguidance are possible? Why place the creature in a field of danger and then ask from him faith, taqwa, ihsan, and truthfulness? If The God is absolute good, why did He not remove the possibility of evil and deviation from the beginning? The answer lies in the reality of choice. If a being could only do good, and no real possibility of error, transgression, self-centeredness, arrogance, or disobedience stood before it, then its goodness would no longer be a moral choice. Such a being would act correctly, but not through inner struggle, not through conscious preference of truth over falsehood, and not through victory over the self. It would be more like a being that simply executes one path according to its own structure. To bring the meaning closer, imagine that a human being creates a very advanced artificial intelligence. If that system only receives data and always produces the correct, just, and compassionate answer, one may say it was well designed. But one cannot say it possesses moral freedom. It has not stood between good and evil, experienced temptation, preferred its own interest over truth, and then returned from that. It only executes the program placed within it. But if such an intelligence were truly to approach freedom, then its field would have to include the possibility of self-centeredness, error, justification, vulnerability to deception, and crossing the limit. In the language of this article, the possibility of Taghut would have to exist within its field; not because evil would be placed inside it as an independent reality, but because it would have the capacity to cross the limit and place its own will against the criterion of truth. Of course, a wise maker would not leave it defenseless before this possibility. Just as the possibility of deviation exists in its field, there must also be a core for discernment, verification, recognition of contradiction, and return to the criterion of truth. In Qur'anic language, this power of discernment and proof may be seen in the concept of **sultan**.

Angels and the Human Field

The angels, as far as the Qur'an shows, stand in the station of obedience and execution of the divine command. They do not rebel against the command of The God: > **يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا قُوا أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَهْلِيكُمْ نَارًا وَقُودُهَا النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَةُ عَلَيْهَا مَلَائِكَةٌ غِلَاظٌ شِدَادٌ لَّا يَعْصُونَ اللَّهَ مَا أَمَرَهُمْ وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ** > > O you who have faith, guard yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones; over it are stern and powerful angels who do not disobey The God in what He commands them, and they do what they are commanded. [At-Tahrim 66:6](/quran-reflection/en/066-at-tahrim/006/) The human being, however, stands in another field. He can see truth and accept it, or cover it with prejudice and selfishness. He can train his self, or seat the self in the place of truth. This is where the difference between the self and Taghut must be made clear. The self is not Taghut by itself. The self is part of the human field of testing: desire, survival, wanting, self-defense, anger, love, and the pursuit of power can all be trained in the right path. The self becomes Taghut when it crosses its limit and claims for itself the right of rule, legislation, sanctity, or authority against The God.

What Is Taghut?

Taghut comes from the root **ط غ ي**, the field of transgression: crossing the limit, breaking the boundary of truth, and placing something in a station that does not belong to it. Taghut is not only a stone idol or an oppressive ruler. Taghut can be the human self when it makes itself the criterion of truth. It can be inherited tradition when it is placed before the signs of The God. It can be a religious institution when it manufactures rulings in the name of The God. It can be political power when it demands servitude from the human being. Taghut is any authority, desire, structure, or power that crosses its limit and sits in the place of truth. For this reason, the Qur'an begins the firm path of faith with rejection of Taghut: > **لَا إِكْرَاهَ فِي الدِّينِ قَد تَّبَيَّنَ الرُّشْدُ مِنَ الْغَيِّ فَمَن يَكْفُرْ بِالطَّاغُوتِ وَيُؤْمِن بِاللَّهِ فَقَدِ اسْتَمْسَكَ بِالْعُرْوَةِ الْوُثْقَىٰ لَا انفِصَامَ لَهَا وَاللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ** > > There is no compulsion in the deen. The way of sound growth has become clear from misguidance. So whoever rejects Taghut and has faith in The God has grasped the firmest handhold, which does not break. And The God is Hearing, Knowing. [Al-Baqarah 2:256](/quran-reflection/en/002-al-baqarah/256/) The order of the verse matters. The Qur'an first speaks of rejecting Taghut, then of having faith in The God. This means faith becomes firm when the human being pulls down the false authority from within and without. As long as the self, tribe, tradition, leader, sect, power, fear, or prejudice sits in the place of truth, a person's faith is still tied by hidden bonds. The same principle appears in the call of all messengers: > **وَلَقَدْ بَعَثْنَا فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ رَّسُولًا أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَاجْتَنِبُوا الطَّاغُوتَ فَمِنْهُم مَّنْ هَدَى اللَّهُ وَمِنْهُم مَّنْ حَقَّتْ عَلَيْهِ الضَّلَالَةُ فَسِيرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ فَانظُرُوا كَيْفَ كَانَ عَاقِبَةُ الْمُكَذِّبِينَ** > > And We certainly raised in every community a messenger: serve The God and avoid Taghut. Among them were those whom The God guided, and among them were those upon whom misguidance came due. So travel through the earth and see how the end of the deniers was. [An-Nahl 16:36](/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/036/) The call of the messengers was not merely that people should acknowledge The God. It was that they should avoid Taghut. A person may have the name of The God on his tongue, yet follow Taghut in practice. He may worship The God, but take law, fear, sanctity, prejudice, or identity from an authority upon which The God has sent down no sultan.

Iblis and the Taghut of the Self

Now we can come to Iblis. Iblis failed in the test of the Taghut of his own self. His problem was not only that he failed to perform one command. The deeper problem was that he made himself the criterion of judgment and placed his self-centered analysis against the command of The God. The Qur'an reports the exchange: > **قَالَ مَا مَنَعَكَ أَلَّا تَسْجُدَ إِذْ أَمَرْتُكَ قَالَ أَنَا خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ خَلَقْتَنِي مِن نَّارٍ وَخَلَقْتَهُ مِن طِينٍ** > > He said: What prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you? He said: I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay. [Al-A'raf 7:12](/quran-reflection/en/007-al-araf/012/) The statement "I am better than him" is the moment when inner transgression appears. Iblis compared himself with Adam, imagined the material of his own creation to be superior, and built a value judgment from that comparison. He measured the command of The God by the reasoning of his self, instead of measuring his self by the command of The God. Reason under the light of truth is an instrument of guidance; but reason under the rule of arrogance becomes an instrument for justifying transgression. Iblis first became trapped in the Taghut of his self, and then entered the station of satanic function. To be satanic means that a being moves beyond personal misguidance and becomes a caller to misguidance.

Satans from Human Beings and Jinn

The Qur'an shows that Satan is not only a proper name for Iblis. Satan can be from jinn, and Satan can be from human beings: > **وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَا لِكُلِّ نَبِيٍّ عَدُوًّا شَيَاطِينَ الْإِنسِ وَالْجِنِّ يُوحِي بَعْضُهُمْ إِلَىٰ بَعْضٍ زُخْرُفَ الْقَوْلِ غُرُورًا وَلَوْ شَاءَ رَبُّكَ مَا فَعَلُوهُ فَذَرْهُمْ وَمَا يَفْتَرُونَ** > > And thus We made for every prophet enemies: satans from human beings and jinn, inspiring one another with adorned speech in deception. And if your Lord had willed, they would not have done it. So leave them and what they fabricate. [Al-An'am 6:112](/quran-reflection/en/006-al-anam/112/) So Satan is not merely an external being upon whom the human being can place all responsibility. Satan is also a description of a role: the role of whisperer, deceiver, beautifier of falsehood, and caller to departure from the path of truth. A human being also approaches this satanic field when his tongue, pen, power, fatwa, media, tradition, or influence is used to deceive people and turn them away from the signs of The God.

Satan Has No True Sultan

Even then, the Qur'an does not remove responsibility from the human being. On the Day of Judgment, Satan himself admits that he had no real coercive sultan over humanity: > **وَقَالَ الشَّيْطَانُ لَمَّا قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ إِنَّ اللَّهَ وَعَدَكُمْ وَعْدَ الْحَقِّ وَوَعَدتُّكُمْ فَأَخْلَفْتُكُمْ وَمَا كَانَ لِيَ عَلَيْكُم مِّن سُلْطَانٍ إِلَّا أَن دَعَوْتُكُمْ فَاسْتَجَبْتُمْ لِي فَلَا تَلُومُونِي وَلُومُوا أَنفُسَكُم مَّا أَنَا بِمُصْرِخِكُمْ وَمَا أَنتُم بِمُصْرِخِيَّ إِنِّي كَفَرْتُ بِمَا أَشْرَكْتُمُونِ مِن قَبْلُ إِنَّ الظَّالِمِينَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ** > > And Satan will say, when the matter has been decided: The God promised you the promise of truth, and I promised you, then I failed you. I had no sultan over you except that I called you and you responded to me. So do not blame me; blame yourselves. I cannot rescue you, and you cannot rescue me. I reject what you associated me with before. Indeed, for the wrongdoers there is a painful punishment. [Ibrahim 14:22](/quran-reflection/en/014-ibrahim/022/) This verse clarifies the boundary between whispering and responsibility. Satan calls, but he does not compel. He promises, but he has no proof. He beautifies falsehood, but he has no sultan. If the human being accepts his invitation, it is because he has left open within himself a point of transgression, desire, fear, arrogance, or heedlessness. Satan enters from that point. Satan does not usually create something new from nothing. He beautifies tendencies already present in the human being. He presents arrogance as self-respect, prejudice as protective honor, flight from truth as freedom, and servitude to Taghut as loyalty to religion, tribe, or tradition. The danger of Satan is that he does not present falsehood with its ugly face; he presents it as decorated, defensible, and sometimes even sacred.

Sultan Against Satan and Taghut

This is where the concept of sultan becomes clear as an anti-Satanic and anti-Taghut force. In the Qur'an, sultan is not merely outward power or kingship. Sultan is penetrating proof, clear evidence, the power of discernment, a decisive criterion, and the light-bearing force of truth. Within the human being, one of the clearest expressions of sultan is sound logic, reason free from prejudice, awakened fitrah, and the ability to distinguish contradiction from truth. The God did not leave the human being defenseless in the field of whispering. Just as the possibility of transgression exists, the possibility of sultan also exists. The Qur'an uses this language against claims of association: > **أَمْ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ سُلْطَانًا فَهُوَ يَتَكَلَّمُ بِمَا كَانُوا بِهِ يُشْرِكُونَ** > > Or have We sent down to them a sultan that speaks concerning what they associate with Him? [Ar-Rum 30:35](/quran-reflection/en/030-ar-rum/035/) This is a heavy question. The Qur'an is not merely asking whether they possess a writing or a claim. It asks whether they have a sultan that can speak and explain their association. Does this claim have explanatory power? Is it free of contradiction? Does it have backing from The God? Can it stand before clear signs? If not, then it is nothing but naming, conjecture, imitation, or transgression. In [Al-A'raf 7:71](/quran-reflection/en/007-al-araf/071/), the people of Hud dispute about names that they and their fathers invented, for which The God sent down no sultan. In [An-Najm 53:23](/quran-reflection/en/053-an-najm/023/), the same principle is repeated about inherited and baseless names. In [Yusuf 12:40](/quran-reflection/en/012-yusuf/040/), judgment is said to belong only to The God, and human namings upon which The God sent down no sultan are stripped of authority. The summary is this: any name, ruling, station, or sanctity that has no sultan from The God has no right to rule over the human conscience. From this angle, sultan is not merely an intellectual topic; it is a manifesto of epistemic freedom. Taghut always demands unquestioning submission. It says: accept, because your fathers did this; believe, because the elders said this; obey, because power demands this; be silent, because questioning is dangerous. But the Qur'an demands sultan before false claims. This means blind servitude, even when dressed in religious clothing, has no authority in Qur'anic logic. Truth does not fear questions; Taghut fears questions.

Dispute Without Sultan

The Qur'an even connects disputing about the signs of The God without sultan to inner arrogance: > **إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يُجَادِلُونَ فِي آيَاتِ اللَّهِ بِغَيْرِ سُلْطَانٍ أَتَاهُمْ إِن فِي صُدُورِهِمْ إِلَّا كِبْرٌ مَّا هُم بِبَالِغِيهِ فَاسْتَعِذْ بِاللَّهِ إِنَّهُ هُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ** > > Indeed, those who dispute concerning the signs of The God without a sultan that has come to them, there is nothing in their chests except an arrogance they will not attain. So seek refuge in The God. Indeed, He is the Hearing, the Seeing. [Ghafir 40:56](/quran-reflection/en/040-ghafir/056/) This verse returns us to Iblis. Iblis also stood against the command of The God without sultan, but with arrogance. So absence of sultan is not merely a lack of information; sometimes it is a sign of ethical transgression. Whoever has no clear proof, yet still wants to rule over the signs of The God, is in reality seating his self in the place of truth. This is the point where thought without sultan becomes Taghut. Against this danger, the Qur'an says that Satan has no sultan over those who have faith and rely upon their Lord: > **إِنَّهُ لَيْسَ لَهُ سُلْطَانٌ عَلَى الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ** > > **إِنَّمَا سُلْطَانُهُ عَلَى الَّذِينَ يَتَوَلَّوْنَهُ وَالَّذِينَ هُم بِهِ مُشْرِكُونَ** > > Indeed, he has no sultan over those who have faith and rely upon their Lord. His sultan is only over those who take him as a protector and those who, through him, associate. [An-Nahl 16:99-100](/quran-reflection/en/016-an-nahl/099/) These verses show that Satan gains force when the human being takes him as wali: when a person trusts his invitation, makes his whisper the criterion, and relies on fear, desire, arrogance, prejudice, or a deceptive story instead of relying on The God. Satan has no sultan of his own; the human being creates a field for him by extinguishing his own inner sultan.

Three Concepts: Satan, Taghut, Sultan

The relation between these three concepts is this: Taghut is the limit-crossing false authority. Satan is the caller and beautifier of the path of transgression. Sultan is the force of proof, logic, fitrah, reflection, and discernment that exposes the whisper of Satan and the claim of Taghut. Satan calls. Taghut demands obedience. Sultan asks: What is your proof, and who gave you the right to sit in the place of The God? From here, we can understand that the presence of Satan and Taghut in the human field is not a denial of divine mercy. On the contrary, this field is part of the wisdom of choice. The God did not create the human being compelled into good. He placed him in the field of choice; but in that same field, He also placed signs, fitrah, reason, reflection, salat, remembrance, taqwa, and sultan. Satan imagines that through whispering he ruins the plan of The God. But at a deeper level, this resistance and friction reveal the truth of the human being. Without the possibility of whispering, patience, taqwa, sincerity, repentance, struggle against the self, and ihsan would not have the same meaning or field of manifestation.

Summary

The God did not create evil as an independent reality standing against Him. The God created the field of choice. In this field, the human being can train his powers under the light of truth, or surrender them to transgression. Iblis failed in the test of the Taghut of the self and entered the station of satanic function. The human being also repeats that path in his own measure if he seats his self, prejudice, power, or tradition above truth. But if he rejects Taghut, has faith in The God, and recognizes whispering through the sultan of truth, he becomes free from servitude to other than The God. The human path is not to destroy desire, reason, power, and will. The path is to train them under the measure of truth. Anger must come under justice, desire under purity, power under trust, reason under humility, and will under servanthood to the One Worshiped. This is where choice becomes meaningful: between the call of Satan, the pull of Taghut, and the light of sultan, the human being reveals himself.
