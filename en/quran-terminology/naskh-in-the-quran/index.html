Naskh in the Qur’an: Challenging the Claim That Its Verses Can Be Set Aside

Introduction

The legal doctrine of naskh, usually called abrogation in English, holds that the ruling of one Qur’anic verse may have been brought to an end by another, even though its wording remains in the written Qur’an and continues to be recited. Some accounts go further: a ruling is said to remain binding and enforceable even though the verse that supposedly established it is no longer in the Qur’an.

The Qur’an does not state that technical definition in those terms. A problem arises when a definition developed outside the Qur’an is read into every occurrence of the Arabic root n-s-kh, and the presence of the word is then offered as proof of the doctrine. The interpretation has been assumed before the text has been examined.

To understand naskh, we need to reverse that procedure. We should first ask how the Qur’an uses the root, what each occurrence refers to, and how its context determines its meaning. We can then assess legal definitions against that evidence.

More than vocabulary is at stake. A doctrine of abrogated verses can leave the Qur’an honored in recitation while displacing its authority in judgment. The verse remains on the page; it is recited and even displayed on walls. Yet when it conflicts with an inherited ruling, the answer is that its words remain but its ruling no longer applies.

Where lives, freedom, property, human rights, and the attribution of lawful and unlawful to The God are concerned, “This verse is abrogated” cannot be the end of the inquiry.

The question is not whether The God has the authority to change a command. The command is His. Across the course of prophetic guidance, He can issue a new instruction, lighten an obligation, establish an endpoint for a ruling, or replace one āyah with another.

The question is how that divine authority to change a command is supposed to have passed to human beings.

An objection may follow: “We do not abrogate anything. The God did so during the Prophet’s lifetime; we merely report what happened.”

The question then shifts from authority to evidence:

How do you know that The God ended the ruling of this particular verse?

A report carrying so consequential a claim cannot establish its own reliability merely by repeating the claim.

Several situations must therefore be distinguished: a ruling whose endpoint is specified from the outset; a relaxation expressly announced by the Qur’an; the disappearance of the circumstances to which a command applies; two verses that seem to conflict because their different subjects have been overlooked; a change announced by The God Himself; and, finally, a claim that a verse’s ruling has ended on the authority of a report or theory outside the Qur’an.

Our criterion is straightforward:

Where the Qur’an itself announces a change, a relaxation, a condition that ends a requirement, or a replacement, we accept it. But the ending of a ruling cannot be attributed to The God merely because someone alleges a contradiction or cites a report from outside the Qur’an.

In each case, we must establish whether the two verses address the same subject, audience, and circumstances. What relationship does the text itself establish between them? If an earlier ruling is said to have ended, who announced its end, and where?

A verse’s continuing authority does not mean that it must be applied without qualification to every person at every time. Its conditions, addressees, circumstances, exceptions, and specified endpoint belong to the command itself. Fidelity to the Qur’an means reading the whole verse, rather than retaining one part while discarding another.

1. Naskh in the Qur’an’s Own Usage

Before constructing a theory, we must examine the Qur’anic occurrences of the root n-s-kh.

The root occurs four times, and those occurrences do not all concern the same thing. In Arabic, it also follows more than one semantic path: one concerns removing or replacing something; another, associated with writing, concerns transferring or copying it into a written record. That range alone prevents us from treating the technical legal definition—ending a verse’s ruling while retaining its wording—as the root’s inherent meaning. The word āyah, likewise, may denote a sign or a revealed passage; its scope must be considered in context.

Of Moses’ tablets, we read:

وَلَمَّا سَكَتَ عَن مُّوسَى الْغَضَبُ أَخَذَ الْأَلْوَاحَ وَفِي نُسْخَتِهَا هُدًى وَرَحْمَةٌ لِّلَّذِينَ هُمْ لِرَبِّهِمْ يَرْهَبُونَ

Al-A‘raf 7:154

When Moses’ anger subsided, he took up the tablets. In their writing were guidance and mercy for those who stood in awe of their Lord.

Nuskhatihā here concerns what is written on the tablets. The writing is not being invalidated; it is described as containing guidance and mercy.

In Al-Jathiyah, we read:

هَٰذَا كِتَابُنَا يَنطِقُ عَلَيْكُم بِالْحَقِّ إِنَّا كُنَّا نَسْتَنسِخُ مَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ

Al-Jathiyah 45:29

This is Our record, speaking truthfully against you. We were recording what you did.

Nastansikh here concerns recording and writing, not stripping something of its authority.

In Al-Hajj 22:52, The God removes what Satan casts, then makes His own verses firm.

In Al-Baqarah 2:106, setting aside an āyah is accompanied by bringing something better or like it.

Even before the legal argument begins, one point is clear: naskh is not a Qur’anic technical term with the single fixed meaning “cancellation of a verse’s ruling.” Each use must be understood within its own sentence and context.

One passage concerns writing, another the recording of deeds, another the removal of what Satan casts. Al-Baqarah speaks of superseding or leaving an āyah and bringing something better or similar.

The definition must emerge from the Qur’an rather than be imposed upon it.

2. Establish the Conflict Before Declaring Abrogation

One foundation of the doctrine of abrogating and abrogated verses is the existence of two incompatible rulings. That incompatibility must itself be demonstrated before a conclusion is drawn.

The Qur’an calls its reader to reflect:

أَفَلَا يَتَدَبَّرُونَ الْقُرْآنَ وَلَوْ كَانَ مِنْ عِندِ غَيْرِ اللَّهِ لَوَجَدُوا فِيهِ اخْتِلَافًا كَثِيرًا

An-Nisa 4:82

Do they not reflect on the Qur’an? Had it been from anyone other than The God, they would have found much inconsistency in it.

Two different statements do not yet constitute a contradiction.

An instruction for someone who is ill may differ from one for a healthy person. War and peace are different circumstances. A widow’s waiting period is not the same subject as her right to accommodation. Describing the harm a substance causes does not contradict a command to avoid it.

Time also belongs to the subject of a ruling. If a verse says that a requirement continues until a specified situation arises, reaching that situation does not invalidate the verse. It fulfills the condition the verse laid down from the beginning.

Before declaring abrogation, then, we must ask whether we really have two rulings about the same subject, audience, and circumstances.

Al ‘Imran also distinguishes between muḥkamāt and mutashābihāt—firmly established verses and those susceptible to resemblance or ambiguity:

هُوَ الَّذِي أَنزَلَ عَلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ مِنْهُ آيَاتٌ مُّحْكَمَاتٌ هُنَّ أُمُّ الْكِتَابِ وَأُخَرُ مُتَشَابِهَاتٌ فَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ زَيْغٌ فَيَتَّبِعُونَ مَا تَشَابَهَ مِنْهُ ابْتِغَاءَ الْفِتْنَةِ وَابْتِغَاءَ تَأْوِيلِهِ وَمَا يَعْلَمُ تَأْوِيلَهُ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَالرَّاسِخُونَ فِي الْعِلْمِ يَقُولُونَ آمَنَّا بِهِ كُلٌّ مِّنْ عِندِ رَبِّنَا وَمَا يَذَّكَّرُ إِلَّا أُولُو الْأَلْبَابِ

Al ‘Imran 3:7

He is the One who sent down the Book to you. Some of its verses are firmly established; they are the mother of the Book. Others are susceptible to ambiguity. Those whose hearts incline away from the truth pursue what is ambiguous in it, seeking discord and seeking its interpretation. None knows its interpretation except The God. Those firmly grounded in knowledge say, “We believe in it; all of it is from our Lord.” Only people of understanding take heed.

If a verse’s meaning is still unclear to us, on what basis can we confidently declare that it has lost its authority?

But this principle must not become an excuse for denying changes that the Qur’an expressly announces. When it says:

«الآن خفف الله عنكم»

Al-Anfal 8:66

Now The God has lightened your burden,

we have no right to deny that a relaxation has occurred. The question is how the verse explains it and whether that explanation warrants saying that the earlier verse has lost its authority.

Where a ruling includes an endpoint from the outset, we must honor that condition.

The standard applies in both directions: alleging a conflict requires evidence, and so does reconciling two passages. Claiming a change requires evidence, and so does denying one.

3. Al-Baqarah 2:106: Does It Identify Existing Qur’anic Verses as Abrogated?

Al-Baqarah 2:106 is the principal verse invoked in support of legal abrogation. The argument runs as follows: The God says that whenever He abrogates an āyah, He brings something better or similar; therefore, during revelation, some Qur’anic verses lost their legal force when others were revealed. Lists of abrogating and abrogated verses are then constructed on that reading. In some cases, the wording is said to remain while its ruling is no longer enforceable.

Before accepting that definition, we must first consider what the verse says and the discussion in which it appears:

مَا نَنسَخْ مِنْ آيَةٍ أَوْ نُنسِهَا نَأْتِ بِخَيْرٍ مِّنْهَا أَوْ مِثْلِهَا أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ أَنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

Al-Baqarah 2:106

Whatever āyah We supersede or cause to be forgotten, We bring one better than it or like it. Do you not know that The God has power over all things?

Detached from its context, this sentence can be made the foundation of several definitions. But the Qur’an does not introduce it as an isolated legal rule. It appears within an extended discussion of the Children of Israel, the People of the Book, earlier scriptures, the arrival of a new Messenger, and responses to newly revealed guidance.

A few verses earlier, we read:

وَلَمَّا جَاءَهُمْ رَسُولٌ مِّنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ مُصَدِّقٌ لِّمَا مَعَهُمْ نَبَذَ فَرِيقٌ مِّنَ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ وَرَاءَ ظُهُورِهِمْ كَأَنَّهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

Al-Baqarah 2:101

When a Messenger from The God came to them, confirming what they had, a group among those given the Book cast The God’s Book behind their backs, as though they did not know.

The arrival of a new Messenger and the response of some earlier recipients of scripture are already the subject.

After the discussion of the devils, Solomon, Harut, and Marut in verses 102–103, one verse addresses the believers:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَقُولُوا رَاعِنَا وَقُولُوا انظُرْنَا وَاسْمَعُوا وَلِلْكَافِرِينَ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

Al-Baqarah 2:104

You who have believed, do not say “rāʿinā,” but say “unẓurnā,” and listen. For those who reject the truth there is a painful punishment.

This is not itself an instance of abrogating an earlier command: the Qur’an had not previously commanded believers to say rāʿinā. Elsewhere, it supplies the background to the expression:

مِّنَ الَّذِينَ هَادُوا يُحَرِّفُونَ الْكَلِمَ عَن مَّوَاضِعِهِ وَيَقُولُونَ سَمِعْنَا وَعَصَيْنَا وَاسْمَعْ غَيْرَ مُسْمَعٍ وَرَاعِنَا لَيًّا بِأَلْسِنَتِهِمْ وَطَعْنًا فِي الدِّينِ وَلَوْ أَنَّهُمْ قَالُوا سَمِعْنَا وَأَطَعْنَا وَاسْمَعْ وَانظُرْنَا لَكَانَ خَيْرًا لَّهُمْ وَأَقْوَمَ وَلَٰكِن لَّعَنَهُمُ اللَّهُ بِكُفْرِهِمْ فَلَا يُؤْمِنُونَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

An-Nisa 4:46

Among those who became Jews are people who shift words from their places and say, “We hear and disobey,” and “Listen, without being made to hear,” and “rāʿinā,” twisting their tongues and disparaging the religion. Had they said, “We hear and obey,” and “Listen,” and “Give us consideration,” it would have been better and more upright for them. But The God has excluded them from mercy because of their rejection, so they believe only a little.

Even Al-Baqarah 2:104 therefore does not break the connection with the People of the Book. The contrasting expressions rāʿinā and unẓurnā receive explanation elsewhere in that same setting.

Immediately before “Whatever We supersede,” the People of the Book and the descent of new good are mentioned again:

مَّا يَوَدُّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ وَلَا الْمُشْرِكِينَ أَن يُنَزَّلَ عَلَيْكُم مِّنْ خَيْرٍ مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ وَاللَّهُ يَخْتَصُّ بِرَحْمَتِهِ مَن يَشَاءُ وَاللَّهُ ذُو الْفَضْلِ الْعَظِيمِ

Al-Baqarah 2:105

Those among the People of the Book who reject the truth, and those who associate partners with The God, do not wish any good from your Lord to be sent down to you. The God singles out for His mercy whom He wills, and The God possesses immense bounty.

The issue is explicit: some people resent good being sent down from the Lord to these recipients. The Qur’an answers that no people or community owns divine mercy:

«وَاللَّهُ يَخْتَصُّ بِرَحْمَتِهِ مَن يَشَاءُ»

Al-Baqarah 2:105

Immediately afterward comes:

مَا نَنسَخْ مِنْ آيَةٍ أَوْ نُنسِهَا نَأْتِ بِخَيْرٍ مِّنْهَا أَوْ مِثْلِهَا أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ أَنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

Al-Baqarah 2:106

Whatever āyah We supersede or cause to be forgotten, We bring one better than it or like it. Do you not know that The God has power over all things?

The following verse continues the theme of divine authority:

أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ أَنَّ اللَّهَ لَهُ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَمَا لَكُم مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ مِن وَلِيٍّ وَلَا نَصِيرٍ

Al-Baqarah 2:107

Do you not know that dominion over the heavens and the earth belongs to The God, and that apart from Him you have neither protector nor helper?

Moses—and thus the history of the Children of Israel—then returns to the discussion:

أَمْ تُرِيدُونَ أَن تَسْأَلُوا رَسُولَكُمْ كَمَا سُئِلَ مُوسَىٰ مِن قَبْلُ وَمَن يَتَبَدَّلِ الْكُفْرَ بِالْإِيمَانِ فَقَدْ ضَلَّ سَوَاءَ السَّبِيلِ

Al-Baqarah 2:108

Do you wish to question your Messenger as Moses was questioned before? Whoever exchanges faith for rejection has strayed from the even path.

The next verse once again names the People of the Book:

وَدَّ كَثِيرٌ مِّنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ لَوْ يَرُدُّونَكُم مِّن بَعْدِ إِيمَانِكُمْ كُفَّارًا حَسَدًا مِّنْ عِندِ أَنفُسِهِم مِّن بَعْدِ مَا تَبَيَّنَ لَهُمُ الْحَقُّ فَاعْفُوا وَاصْفَحُوا حَتَّىٰ يَأْتِيَ اللَّهُ بِأَمْرِهِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

Al-Baqarah 2:109

Many of the People of the Book wish to turn you back from faith to rejection, out of envy within themselves after the truth has become clear to them. So pardon and overlook until The God brings His command. The God has power over all things.

Al-Baqarah 2:106 does not stand between two unrelated discussions. Immediately before it, the People of the Book and the associators resent the descent of new good. Immediately after it comes divine dominion and authority, followed by Moses and the People of the Book again.

The argument unfolds coherently: some earlier recipients of scripture resent the descent of new good; The God declares that mercy is His to bestow; if He supersedes or leaves an āyah, He brings something better or similar, for dominion over the heavens and earth belongs to Him. The new audience’s conduct is then compared with that of people who had earlier encountered Moses.

In this context, the verse first addresses divine authority to continue and change the course of guidance, rather than presenting a legal theory of cancellation within the Qur’an.

No community or recipient of scripture can claim that what was given to it marked the final limit of divine authority, beyond which The God could bring no new sign or command.

The Qur’an makes the same principle clear in its account of Jesus:

وَرَسُولًا إِلَىٰ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ أَنِّي قَدْ جِئْتُكُم بِآيَةٍ مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ أَنِّي أَخْلُقُ لَكُم مِّنَ الطِّينِ كَهَيْئَةِ الطَّيْرِ فَأَنفُخُ فِيهِ فَيَكُونُ طَيْرًا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ وَأُبْرِئُ الْأَكْمَهَ وَالْأَبْرَصَ وَأُحْيِي الْمَوْتَىٰ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ وَأُنَبِّئُكُم بِمَا تَأْكُلُونَ وَمَا تَدَّخِرُونَ فِي بُيُوتِكُمْ إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لَّكُمْ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ ۝ وَمُصَدِّقًا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيَّ مِنَ التَّوْرَاةِ وَلِأُحِلَّ لَكُم بَعْضَ الَّذِي حُرِّمَ عَلَيْكُمْ وَجِئْتُكُم بِآيَةٍ مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ فَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُونِ

Al ‘Imran 3:49، Al ‘Imran 3:50

And as a Messenger to the Children of Israel, [he says]: “I have come to you with a sign from your Lord. I fashion for you out of clay something in the shape of a bird, then breathe into it, and it becomes a bird by The God’s permission. I heal the person blind from birth and the person with leprosy, and bring the dead to life by The God’s permission. I tell you what you eat and what you store in your homes. In this is a sign for you, if you believe. I confirm the Torah before me, and I have come to make lawful for you some of what was forbidden to you. I have brought you a sign from your Lord; so be mindful of The God and obey me.”

Jesus confirms the Torah while making lawful some things previously forbidden to his audience. Within the Qur’an’s account, confirming earlier guidance is compatible with bringing a new command.

Reading Al-Baqarah 2:106 within the continuing guidance of successive messengers, and changes between its stages, thus arises from the surrounding verses themselves.

Yet the argument does not depend on establishing that reading as the only possible one.

Suppose someone argues that āyah in 2:106 can refer to a verse of this Qur’an. A decisive question still remains:

Which verse?

Al-Baqarah 2:106 identifies no particular Qur’anic verse as abrogated, declares no specific ruling ended, and installs no outside report in its place.

The God Himself is the subject of the act: nansakh, “We supersede.”

He is also the One who brings the replacement: “We bring one better than it or like it.”

Even if this verse includes changes made during the Qur’an’s revelation, it does not follow that later generations may draw up lists of abrogating and abrogated verses and suspend an existing verse’s ruling on the strength of reports outside the Qur’an.

Several other passages are often raised here. Al-A‘la says:

سَنُقْرِئُكَ فَلَا تَنسَىٰ ۝ إِلَّا مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ إِنَّهُ يَعْلَمُ الْجَهْرَ وَمَا يَخْفَىٰ

Al-A‘la 87:6، Al-A‘la 87:7

We shall have you recite, and you will not forget, except what The God wills. He knows what is manifest and what is hidden.

The verse leaves a possibility under divine will, but names no particular verse that was forgotten. Nor does it say that a verse’s wording disappeared from the Qur’an while its ruling survived in another report.

Al-Isra says:

وَلَئِن شِئْنَا لَنَذْهَبَنَّ بِالَّذِي أَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْكَ ثُمَّ لَا تَجِدُ لَكَ بِهِ عَلَيْنَا وَكِيلًا

Al-Isra 17:86

If We willed, We could take away what We have revealed to you. You would then find no advocate against Us to recover it for you.

This too concerns divine power and will. It identifies no verse to which such an event happened and yields no list of abrogated passages.

Ar-Ra‘d says:

وَلَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا رُسُلًا مِّن قَبْلِكَ وَجَعَلْنَا لَهُمْ أَزْوَاجًا وَذُرِّيَّةً وَمَا كَانَ لِرَسُولٍ أَن يَأْتِيَ بِآيَةٍ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ لِكُلِّ أَجَلٍ كِتَابٌ ۝ يَمْحُو اللَّهُ مَا يَشَاءُ وَيُثْبِتُ وَعِندَهُ أُمُّ الْكِتَابِ

Ar-Ra‘d 13:38، Ar-Ra‘d 13:39

We sent messengers before you and gave them spouses and descendants. No messenger could bring an āyah except by The God’s permission. For every appointed term there is a decree. The God erases what He wills and establishes what He wills; with Him is the mother of the Book.

These verses likewise affirm divine authority to erase and establish. The question remains: which existing Qur’anic verse has ceased to be binding, and on what Qur’anic evidence?

The reading nunsihā in Al-Baqarah 2:106 concerns causing to be forgotten; the reading nansaʾhā concerns deferral. Neither, by itself, says that a verse was removed from the Qur’an while its ruling survived in a report.

Divine authority to change guidance does not validate every later claim made in the name of that authority.

4. An-Nahl 16:101 and Al-Hajj 22:52: Divine Replacement and the Removal of What Satan Casts

An-Nahl 16:101 is another principal passage invoked to establish internal Qur’anic abrogation. The God expressly says that He puts one āyah in place of another, the argument goes; therefore some Qur’anic verses were replaced during revelation and the earlier ruling ceased to apply.

The verse reads:

وَإِذَا بَدَّلْنَا آيَةً مَّكَانَ آيَةٍ وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا يُنَزِّلُ قَالُوا إِنَّمَا أَنتَ مُفْتَرٍ بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

An-Nahl 16:101

When We put one āyah in place of another—and The God knows best what He sends down—they say, “You are only a fabricator.” Rather, most of them do not know.

“Put one āyah in place of another” must not be denied or explained away. The Qur’an speaks of replacement. Two features must be retained: The God performs the replacement, and it occurs within divine revelation.

A few verses earlier, the discussion turns to reading the Qur’an and Satan:

فَإِذَا قَرَأْتَ الْقُرْآنَ فَاسْتَعِذْ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ

An-Nahl 16:98

When you read the Qur’an, seek refuge with The God from Satan the outcast.

إِنَّهُ لَيْسَ لَهُ سُلْطَانٌ عَلَى الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ

An-Nahl 16:99

He has no authority over those who believe and place their trust in their Lord.

إِنَّمَا سُلْطَانُهُ عَلَى الَّذِينَ يَتَوَلَّوْنَهُ وَالَّذِينَ هُم بِهِ مُشْرِكُونَ

An-Nahl 16:100

His authority extends only over those who take him as their patron and those who associate partners through him.

The replacement verse follows directly. The answer to the charge of fabrication then comes immediately:

قُلْ نَزَّلَهُ رُوحُ الْقُدُسِ مِن رَّبِّكَ بِالْحَقِّ لِيُثَبِّتَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَهُدًى وَبُشْرَىٰ لِلْمُسْلِمِينَ

An-Nahl 16:102

Say: The Holy Spirit brought it down from your Lord in truth, to strengthen those who believe, and as guidance and good news for those who submit.

It then records another accusation:

وَلَقَدْ نَعْلَمُ أَنَّهُمْ يَقُولُونَ إِنَّمَا يُعَلِّمُهُ بَشَرٌ لِّسَانُ الَّذِي يُلْحِدُونَ إِلَيْهِ أَعْجَمِيٌّ وَهَٰذَا لِسَانٌ عَرَبِيٌّ مُّبِينٌ

An-Nahl 16:103

We certainly know that they say, “A human being is teaching him.” The tongue of the person they point to is non-Arabic, while this is a clarifying Arabic tongue.

An-Nahl 16:101 therefore grants human beings no independent authority to set aside a verse later and put another statement in its place. The God Himself performs the replacement—“We replaced”—and the new revelation is also attributed to that same Lord.

Al-Hajj 22:52 uses the root n-s-kh itself, and explicitly names the object of the act:

وَمَا أَرْسَلْنَا مِن قَبْلِكَ مِن رَّسُولٍ وَلَا نَبِيٍّ إِلَّا إِذَا تَمَنَّىٰ أَلْقَى الشَّيْطَانُ فِي أُمْنِيَّتِهِ فَيَنسَخُ اللَّهُ مَا يُلْقِي الشَّيْطَانُ ثُمَّ يُحْكِمُ اللَّهُ آيَاتِهِ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ

Al-Hajj 22:52

We sent no messenger or prophet before you but that, whenever he tamannā, Satan cast something into his umniyyah. The God then removes what Satan casts, and afterward makes His verses firm. The God is knowing and wise.

The words tamannā and umniyyah have deliberately been left untranslated here. Their precise range requires a separate lexical inquiry; their relationship to wishing and recitation has been debated in Arabic. This section’s argument does not depend on resolving that question, because the end of the verse expressly identifies what is removed:

«فَيَنسَخُ اللَّهُ مَا يُلْقِي الشَّيْطَانُ»

Al-Hajj 22:52

What The God removes is what Satan casts.

The verse then says:

«ثُمَّ يُحْكِمُ اللَّهُ آيَاتِهِ»

Al-Hajj 22:52

Afterward, The God makes His own verses firm.

In this verse, divine verses are not the object of naskh. “What Satan casts” is the object; the verses are subsequently made firm.

The following verses sustain this contrast:

لِّيَجْعَلَ مَا يُلْقِي الشَّيْطَانُ فِتْنَةً لِّلَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ وَالْقَاسِيَةِ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَإِنَّ الظَّالِمِينَ لَفِي شِقَاقٍ بَعِيدٍ

Al-Hajj 22:53

So that He may make what Satan casts a trial for those whose hearts are diseased and those whose hearts are hardened. The wrongdoers are in profound opposition.

وَلِيَعْلَمَ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْعِلْمَ أَنَّهُ الْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّكَ فَيُؤْمِنُوا بِهِ فَتُخْبِتَ لَهُ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ لَهَادِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِلَىٰ صِرَاطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ

Al-Hajj 22:54

And so that those given knowledge may know that it is the truth from your Lord, believe in it, and let their hearts submit humbly to it. The God guides those who believe to a straight path.

On one side stands “what Satan casts”; on the other, “the truth from your Lord.”

Elsewhere, the Qur’an also speaks of devils among human beings and jinn:

وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَا لِكُلِّ نَبِيٍّ عَدُوًّا شَيَاطِينَ الْإِنسِ وَالْجِنِّ يُوحِي بَعْضُهُمْ إِلَىٰ بَعْضٍ زُخْرُفَ الْقَوْلِ غُرُورًا وَلَوْ شَاءَ رَبُّكَ مَا فَعَلُوهُ فَذَرْهُمْ وَمَا يَفْتَرُونَ

Al-An‘am 6:112

Thus We appointed for every prophet enemies—devils among human beings and jinn—some inspiring others with embellished speech to deceive. Had your Lord willed, they would not have done so. Leave them and what they fabricate.

Reading these passages together offers a criterion for assessment, but its limits must be kept clear. Al-Hajj 22:52 does not directly discuss hadith, jurisprudence, or later books. Applying it to inherited religious material is our application. The principle is that claims made in the name of religion must be measured against divine verses, rather than silencing a clear Qur’anic verse to preserve an outside statement.

Not every report or tradition is satanic. But neither a tradition’s name nor its age exempts it from scrutiny. If preserving a claim requires setting aside a clear divine command, the claim must produce its evidence.

Rajm, stoning, provides a practical example. An-Nur 24:2 prescribes one hundred lashes for zinā. If another ruling is to restrict or replace that express command, the burden of proof belongs to that claim. The historical and hadith questions, including the alleged “verse of stoning,” are examined separately in Stoning: A Qur’anic Ruling or a Rule Derived from Reports?.

If a clear Qur’anic verse is declared abrogated or restricted simply to preserve a reported ruling, the direction of scrutiny has been reversed. Instead of holding the outside statement accountable to the Qur’an, the Qur’an is made to yield to that statement.

5. “None Can Alter His Words”: Divine Words as the Standard of Judgment

The preceding section showed that An-Nahl 16:101 attributes replacement to The God Himself. In a different use of n-s-kh, Al-Hajj 22:52 identifies what Satan casts as the object of removal and divine verses as what is made firm.

Against that background, passages concerning the unalterability of divine words acquire particular importance.

The Qur’an says:

وَتَمَّتْ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ صِدْقًا وَعَدْلًا لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ

Al-An‘am 6:115

Your Lord’s word has been fulfilled in truth and justice. None can alter His words. He is the Hearing, the Knowing.

In Al-Kahf, the statement appears directly alongside the revealed Book:

وَاتْلُ مَا أُوحِيَ إِلَيْكَ مِن كِتَابِ رَبِّكَ لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ وَلَن تَجِدَ مِن دُونِهِ مُلْتَحَدًا

Al-Kahf 18:27

Recite what has been revealed to you of your Lord’s Book. None can alter His words, and you will find no refuge apart from Him.

The connection within this verse deserves attention. It first commands recitation of what has been revealed of the Lord’s Book, then declares that none can alter His words. Human beings do not own those words or determine their authority at will.

In Yunus, we also read:

لَهُمُ الْبُشْرَىٰ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ لَا تَبْدِيلَ لِكَلِمَاتِ اللَّهِ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ

Yunus 10:64

For them is good news in the life of this world and in the Hereafter. There is no alteration of The God’s words. That is the great triumph.

The context in Yunus concerns divine promise and good news; it should not be extended to every legal command without evidence. Al-Kahf 18:27, however, directly connects the revealed Book with “None can alter His words.”

The point is not that The God cannot bring a new command. As already discussed, when He announces a change, the command is His. The question concerns human beings and later sources: an existing Qur’anic verse cannot be declared ineffective to preserve something outside the Qur’an without clear evidence establishing that attribution to The God.

It may be objected that no jurist claims to alter divine words personally. The claim is that The God ended the ruling during the Prophet’s lifetime, and that the jurist or transmitter merely reports the event.

That makes the issue more precise. The question is no longer whether The God has the power to change a command. It is this:

What establishes that The God ended this particular ruling?

Calling it naskh does not answer the question. If the ruling ended by divine authority, the evidence for attributing its end to The God must be clear.

The governing principle is simple: divine words are the standard of judgment. What has developed around them must be assessed against them, rather than setting divine words aside to preserve that surrounding material.

6. Cases Presented as Abrogation Within the Qur’an

With the question and criterion now established, we can examine the examples offered as evidence of abrogation within the Qur’an itself.

The aim is not to deny every change or relaxation. These examples differ. Sometimes the command specifies a condition for its own conclusion; sometimes the Qur’an expressly announces relief. Elsewhere it reports an earlier command or situation whose original instruction is absent from the written Qur’an. Sometimes two verses concern different subjects; sometimes removing a verse from its context creates a conflict absent from the full passage.

The same question applies throughout: has one existing Qur’anic verse really invalidated and rendered another ineffective, or does some other relationship connect them?

6.1. Fāḥishah and Zinā: An Endpoint Built into the Command

The relationship between An-Nisa 4:15 and the ruling on zinā in An-Nur is one proposed example of internal abrogation.

An-Nisa says:

وَاللَّاتِي يَأْتِينَ الْفَاحِشَةَ مِن نِّسَائِكُمْ فَاسْتَشْهِدُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةً مِّنكُمْ فَإِن شَهِدُوا فَأَمْسِكُوهُنَّ فِي الْبُيُوتِ حَتَّىٰ يَتَوَفَّاهُنَّ الْمَوْتُ أَوْ يَجْعَلَ اللَّهُ لَهُنَّ سَبِيلًا

An-Nisa 4:15

For those of your women who commit fāḥishah, call four witnesses from among you against them. If they testify, keep the women in their homes until death takes them or The God provides a way for them.

The following verse says:

وَاللَّذَانِ يَأْتِيَانِهَا مِنكُمْ فَآذُوهُمَا فَإِن تَابَا وَأَصْلَحَا فَأَعْرِضُوا عَنْهُمَا إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ تَوَّابًا رَّحِيمًا

An-Nisa 4:16

As for the two among you who commit it, subject them to hurt. If they repent and make amends, leave them alone. The God is ever accepting of repentance and merciful.

Even before identifying the “way” or determining the precise scope of fāḥishah, one feature is explicit: An-Nisa 4:15 did not establish an arrangement without an endpoint. It says that the situation continues until death takes them or The God provides a way for them.

If He subsequently provides that way, following it does not oppose An-Nisa 4:15. It fulfills the possibility the verse itself anticipated.

Fāḥishah is broader than zinā, but the Qur’an expressly identifies zinā as an instance of it:

وَلَا تَقْرَبُوا الزِّنَىٰ إِنَّهُ كَانَ فَاحِشَةً وَسَاءَ سَبِيلًا

Al-Isra 17:32

Do not approach zinā. It is an indecency and an evil way.

An-Nisa 4:25 also connects fāḥishah with a punishment that, for one group, is half that imposed on free women. An-Nur then gives the ruling on zinā explicitly:

الزَّانِيَةُ وَالزَّانِي فَاجْلِدُوا كُلَّ وَاحِدٍ مِّنْهُمَا مِائَةَ جَلْدَةٍ وَلَا تَأْخُذْكُم بِهِمَا رَأْفَةٌ فِي دِينِ اللَّهِ إِن كُنتُمْ تُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَلْيَشْهَدْ عَذَابَهُمَا طَائِفَةٌ مِّنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ

An-Nur 24:2

The woman and the man who commit zinā: give each one a hundred lashes. If you believe in The God and the Last Day, do not let pity for them prevent you from applying The God’s religion. Let a group of believers witness their punishment.

On our reading, these passages clarify the way that An-Nisa 4:15 left open. Even if the scope of fāḥishah is disputed, or whether the “way” refers specifically to An-Nur 24:2, the central argument remains: the original command itself stated the condition under which it could end.

The Qur’an does not say here, “This verse has been abrogated.” It says:

«أَوْ يَجْعَلَ اللَّهُ لَهُنَّ سَبِيلًا»

An-Nisa 4:15

Or The God provides a way for them.

Stoning is examined independently in Stoning: A Qur’anic Ruling or a Rule Derived from Reports?. That discussion need not be repeated here.

6.2. The Change of Qiblah: An Earlier Divine Command, but No Abrogated Verse in the Written Qur’an

The change of qiblah, the direction faced in worship, is often offered as evidence of abrogation within the Qur’an. Muslims first faced another direction; the Qur’an then prescribed the direction of the Sacred Mosque. This is consequently described as one Qur’anic ruling abrogating another.

Examining the example from the Qur’an itself, however, clarifies the distinction between changing a divine command and abrogating an existing Qur’anic verse.

The Qur’an says:

وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا لِّتَكُونُوا شُهَدَاءَ عَلَى النَّاسِ وَيَكُونَ الرَّسُولُ عَلَيْكُمْ شَهِيدًا وَمَا جَعَلْنَا الْقِبْلَةَ الَّتِي كُنتَ عَلَيْهَا إِلَّا لِنَعْلَمَ مَن يَتَّبِعُ الرَّسُولَ مِمَّن يَنقَلِبُ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيْهِ وَإِن كَانَتْ لَكَبِيرَةً إِلَّا عَلَى الَّذِينَ هَدَى اللَّهُ وَمَا كَانَ اللَّهُ لِيُضِيعَ إِيمَانَكُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ بِالنَّاسِ لَرَءُوفٌ رَّحِيمٌ

Al-Baqarah 2:143

Thus We made you a middle community, so that you might be witnesses over humanity and the Messenger a witness over you. We appointed the qiblah you formerly faced only to distinguish those who follow the Messenger from those who turn back on their heels. It was indeed difficult, except for those The God guided. The God would not let your faith go to waste. He is compassionate and merciful toward people.

The verse plainly acknowledges an earlier qiblah and attributes its appointment to The God: “the qiblah you formerly faced” and “We appointed.” The existence of an earlier divine command is not in dispute.

The new instruction then follows:

قَدْ نَرَىٰ تَقَلُّبَ وَجْهِكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ فَلَنُوَلِّيَنَّكَ قِبْلَةً تَرْضَاهَا فَوَلِّ وَجْهَكَ شَطْرَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ وَحَيْثُ مَا كُنتُمْ فَوَلُّوا وُجُوهَكُمْ شَطْرَهُ وَإِنَّ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ لَيَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّهُ الْحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّهِمْ وَمَا اللَّهُ بِغَافِلٍ عَمَّا يَعْمَلُونَ

Al-Baqarah 2:144

We see your face turning toward the sky. We shall turn you toward a qiblah that pleases you. Turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque; wherever you are, turn your faces toward it. Those given the Book know that it is the truth from their Lord. The God is not unaware of what they do.

The change is explicit. But if this is to demonstrate one Qur’anic verse abrogating another, where is the first verse?

No earlier verse in the Qur’an commands the Messenger and believers to face the previous qiblah. The Qur’an reports that direction’s existence and divine appointment but does not reproduce the original command in the written text.

This distinction matters. The Qur’an can attest an earlier divine command whose wording is not contained in the written Qur’an. We accept that command’s existence because the Qur’an reports it. But that fact does not automatically authorize every later account of an “unwritten command,” or establish its power to end an existing Qur’anic ruling.

The continuation of the passage also matters:

وَلَئِنْ أَتَيْتَ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ بِكُلِّ آيَةٍ مَّا تَبِعُوا قِبْلَتَكَ وَمَا أَنتَ بِتَابِعٍ قِبْلَتَهُمْ وَمَا بَعْضُهُم بِتَابِعٍ قِبْلَةَ بَعْضٍ وَلَئِنِ اتَّبَعْتَ أَهْوَاءَهُم مِّن بَعْدِ مَا جَاءَكَ مِنَ الْعِلْمِ إِنَّكَ إِذًا لَّمِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

Al-Baqarah 2:145

Even if you brought those given the Book every sign, they would not follow your qiblah. Nor are you a follower of theirs, nor do they follow one another’s qiblah. If you followed their desires after the knowledge that has come to you, you would then be among the wrongdoers.

The discussion expressly concerns the People of the Book and their directions of worship. In our contextual reading, this fits the theme of Al-Baqarah 2:106: divine authority to bring new guidance and commands across successive messengerships. But the Qur’an does not explicitly locate the earlier qiblah command in the Torah or another scripture. That connection must remain a contextual interpretation, not a statement attributed directly to the verse.

In any event, the qiblah example does not establish the claim it is offered to prove:

Which existing Qur’anic verse was abrogated?

In this example, there is no such earlier verse in the written Qur’an.

6.3. Al-Anfal 8:65–66: Human Weakness and the Claim of Abrogation

Two consecutive verses of Al-Anfal are among the less persuasive examples offered for internal abrogation. The argument is that verse 65 initially required Muslims to withstand a force ten times their number, before verse 66 reduced the ratio to one against two, thereby abrogating the first ruling. An account that treats these verses as evidence of cancellation needs to demonstrate what the text actually requires before drawing that conclusion.

Before applying the label naskh, we must examine the verses themselves.

The first says:

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ حَرِّضِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ عَلَى الْقِتَالِ إِن يَكُن مِّنكُمْ عِشْرُونَ صَابِرُونَ يَغْلِبُوا مِائَتَيْنِ وَإِن يَكُن مِّنكُم مِّائَةٌ يَغْلِبُوا أَلْفًا مِّنَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا بِأَنَّهُمْ قَوْمٌ لَّا يَفْقَهُونَ

Al-Anfal 8:65

Prophet, urge the believers to fight. If there are twenty steadfast people among you, they will overcome two hundred; if there are a hundred, they will overcome a thousand of those who reject the truth, because they are people who do not understand.

The verse does not say, in those words, “Every twenty Muslims are obliged to fight two hundred.” Its sentence is conditional:

«إِن يَكُن مِّنكُمْ عِشْرُونَ صَابِرُونَ»

Al-Anfal 8:65

If there are twenty steadfast people among you…

From the outset, the stated ratio of victory is connected with their steadfastness.

The next verse says:

الْآنَ خَفَّفَ اللَّهُ عَنكُمْ وَعَلِمَ أَنَّ فِيكُمْ ضَعْفًا فَإِن يَكُن مِّنكُم مِّائَةٌ صَابِرَةٌ يَغْلِبُوا مِائَتَيْنِ وَإِن يَكُن مِّنكُمْ أَلْفٌ يَغْلِبُوا أَلْفَيْنِ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ وَاللَّهُ مَعَ الصَّابِرِينَ

Al-Anfal 8:66

Now The God has lightened your burden, knowing that there is weakness among you. If there are a hundred steadfast people among you, they will overcome two hundred; if there are a thousand, they will overcome two thousand by The God’s permission. The God is with the steadfast.

We must not deny that a change has occurred. The Qur’an itself calls it a lightening of the burden:

«الْآنَ خَفَّفَ اللَّهُ عَنكُمْ»

Al-Anfal 8:66

It also gives the reason in the same sentence:

«وَعَلِمَ أَنَّ فِيكُمْ ضَعْفًا»

Al-Anfal 8:66

He knew that there was weakness among you.

The change is not presented without explanation: the audience’s condition and capacity affect the practical ratio. The first verse speaks of a steadfast group able to overcome larger numbers; the second expressly brings the audience’s weakness into the account.

This requires precision about the ruling being claimed. If verse 65 imposed a binding duty on every Muslim to withstand ten opponents, that obligation must first be demonstrated from the passage. A conditional statement does not, by itself, settle whether there is an obligation, and both verses refer to steadfastness. The inquiry must therefore include the command to encourage fighting, the stated weakness, and the expressly announced relief.

Ending an earlier obligation is not the same as turning an earlier statement into a falsehood. Conversely, a verse’s continuing truth or instructive value does not, by itself, prove that every earlier obligation remains in force. The secure ground here is what the text states: a burden has been lightened in relation to the audience’s weakness. Any further account of what duty ended must establish its case from that relationship.

Al-Anfal thus illustrates another principle: accept the relaxation the Qur’an announces, while specifying what changed and what evidence establishes it. Naming that relationship “abrogation” does not supply a general authority to suspend other verses on the strength of outside reports.

The difficulty begins when two statements are first turned into conflicting legal rules and one is then labeled abrogated to resolve the alleged conflict. The burden is to establish those rules and their relationship from the text. Neither asserting a contradiction nor denying a change can replace the Qur’an’s own explanation of weakness and relief.

6.4. Charity Before Private Consultation: Access to the Messenger and the Claim of Abrogation

Al-Mujadilah 58:12–13 is another frequently cited example. Verse 12 requires a charitable offering before private consultation with the Messenger, the argument runs; verse 13 then removes that requirement, making verse 12 abrogated.

The discussion must begin by reading verses 12–13 within their context of private consultation, najwā.

That discussion begins several verses earlier:

أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى الَّذِينَ نُهُوا عَنِ النَّجْوَىٰ ثُمَّ يَعُودُونَ لِمَا نُهُوا عَنْهُ وَيَتَنَاجَوْنَ بِالْإِثْمِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ وَمَعْصِيَتِ الرَّسُولِ وَإِذَا جَاءُوكَ حَيَّوْكَ بِمَا لَمْ يُحَيِّكَ بِهِ اللَّهُ وَيَقُولُونَ فِي أَنفُسِهِمْ لَوْلَا يُعَذِّبُنَا اللَّهُ بِمَا نَقُولُ حَسْبُهُمْ جَهَنَّمُ يَصْلَوْنَهَا فَبِئْسَ الْمَصِيرُ

Al-Mujadilah 58:8

Have you not seen those forbidden to hold secret consultations, who return to what they were forbidden and confer about sin, aggression, and disobedience to the Messenger? When they come to you, they greet you in a way The God has not greeted you, and say within themselves, “Why does The God not punish us for what we say?” Hell is enough for them; they will enter it. What an evil destination.

The believers are then addressed:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا تَنَاجَيْتُمْ فَلَا تَتَنَاجَوْا بِالْإِثْمِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ وَمَعْصِيَتِ الرَّسُولِ وَتَنَاجَوْا بِالْبِرِّ وَالتَّقْوَىٰ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ الَّذِي إِلَيْهِ تُحْشَرُونَ

Al-Mujadilah 58:9

You who have believed, when you confer privately, do not confer about sin, aggression, and disobedience to the Messenger. Confer about goodness and mindfulness of The God, and be mindful of The God to whom you will be gathered.

The passage continues:

إِنَّمَا النَّجْوَىٰ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ لِيَحْزُنَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَلَيْسَ بِضَارِّهِمْ شَيْئًا إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ وَعَلَى اللَّهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ

Al-Mujadilah 58:10

Such secret consultation—the blameworthy kind described in the preceding verses—is from Satan, to grieve those who believe. Yet it cannot harm them at all except by The God’s permission. Let the believers place their trust in The God.

This cannot be made an unqualified statement about every private conversation. Verse 9 has just approved consultation in goodness and mindfulness of The God. The context distinguishes sinful, distressing secrecy from legitimate private discussion.

Within that setting comes the instruction:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا نَاجَيْتُمُ الرَّسُولَ فَقَدِّمُوا بَيْنَ يَدَيْ نَجْوَاكُمْ صَدَقَةً ذَٰلِكَ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ وَأَطْهَرُ فَإِن لَّمْ تَجِدُوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ

Al-Mujadilah 58:12

You who have believed, when you consult the Messenger privately, offer something in charity before your consultation. That is better and purer for you. If you find nothing to give, The God is forgiving and merciful.

In this context, charity may function as a practical threshold: protecting direct access to the Messenger from pointless interruptions, trivial demands, or deliberate misuse, while requiring someone with a serious concern and the means to give to contribute to a good purpose.

But even verse 12 does not impose an unqualified requirement. It already provides for the person who lacks the means:

«فَإِن لَّمْ تَجِدُوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ»

Al-Mujadilah 58:12

If you find nothing to give, The God is forgiving and merciful.

Forgiveness and mercy thus belong to the command itself. Access to the Messenger is not to become a privilege of wealth.

The next verse says:

أَأَشْفَقْتُمْ أَن تُقَدِّمُوا بَيْنَ يَدَيْ نَجْوَاكُمْ صَدَقَاتٍ فَإِذْ لَمْ تَفْعَلُوا وَتَابَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْكُمْ فَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَأَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ وَاللَّهُ خَبِيرٌ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ

Al-Mujadilah 58:13

Were you afraid to offer charitable gifts before your private consultations? Since you did not do so, and The God has turned toward you, establish Salat, give Zakat, and obey The God and His Messenger. The God is fully aware of what you do.

If this is understood as lifting the general requirement of charity before consultation, the reason and direction of that relief are supplied by the text: they did not act, and The God “turned toward you.” We need not discover this relief centuries later through an outside report.

We must nevertheless distinguish the lifting of an obligation from the continuing authority of the verse as guidance. Verse 12 still explains the arrangement, its benefit, and its provision for people without means. That continuing value does not, by itself, prove that the original payment requirement remains. To determine whether it ended, we must examine verse 13’s language and its relationship to verse 12.

The command also has a specified subject from the outset:

«إِذَا نَاجَيْتُمُ الرَّسُولَ»

Al-Mujadilah 58:12

When you consult the Messenger privately.

Once the Messenger is no longer present, that particular situation no longer exists. The Qur’an appoints no successor to this position who could claim the same entitlement as a caliph, jurist, religious teacher, or later ruler.

Calling this relationship naskh does not relieve us of explaining it. Private consultation, financial capacity, forgiveness, and mercy belong to the passage itself. An account of the change must address all of them, rather than merely label the earlier verse ineffective.

The substantive distinction is therefore between a change supported by the text and a claim that asks us to suspend a verse on outside authority. If the term naskh is used for the former, the term alone does not prove the latter. The account of consultation illustrates why each case needs its own evidence.

6.5. The Nights of Fasting: A Changed Situation Without an Earlier Verse in the Written Qur’an

Al-Baqarah 2:187 announces relief from an earlier situation. A few verses before it, the discussion of fasting begins by linking it to an earlier tradition:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ

Al-Baqarah 2:183

You who have believed, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may become mindful of The God.

Verse 187 then says:

أُحِلَّ لَكُمْ لَيْلَةَ الصِّيَامِ الرَّفَثُ إِلَىٰ نِسَائِكُمْ هُنَّ لِبَاسٌ لَّكُمْ وَأَنتُمْ لِبَاسٌ لَّهُنَّ عَلِمَ اللَّهُ أَنَّكُمْ كُنتُمْ تَخْتَانُونَ أَنفُسَكُمْ فَتَابَ عَلَيْكُمْ وَعَفَا عَنكُمْ فَالْآنَ بَاشِرُوهُنَّ وَابْتَغُوا مَا كَتَبَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ وَكُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَكُمُ الْخَيْطُ الْأَبْيَضُ مِنَ الْخَيْطِ الْأَسْوَدِ مِنَ الْفَجْرِ ثُمَّ أَتِمُّوا الصِّيَامَ إِلَى اللَّيْلِ وَلَا تُبَاشِرُوهُنَّ وَأَنتُمْ عَاكِفُونَ فِي الْمَسَاجِدِ تِلْكَ حُدُودُ اللَّهِ فَلَا تَقْرَبُوهَا كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ آيَاتِهِ لِلنَّاسِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ

Al-Baqarah 2:187

Intimacy with your wives has been made lawful for you on the nights of fasting. They are a garment for you, and you are a garment for them. The God knew that you were betraying yourselves, so He turned toward you and pardoned you. Now have relations with them and seek what The God has prescribed for you. Eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread, then complete the fast until night. Do not have relations with them while you are in retreat in the mosques. These are The God’s limits, so do not approach them. Thus The God makes His verses clear to people, so that they may become mindful of Him.

“Has been made lawful for you,” “He turned toward you,” and “Now have relations with them” indicate relief from a prior situation. That must not be denied.

But if this is offered as evidence that a Qur’anic verse was abrogated, the first verse must also be produced: where, before Al-Baqarah 2:187, does a verse prohibit intercourse throughout the entire night of fasting?

No such verse exists in the written Qur’an.

Verse 2:183 connects fasting with earlier practice, but the Qur’an does not identify the precise source of the nighttime restriction. We should not claim more than it tells us.

The principle is the same as in the qiblah discussion. The Qur’an can report an earlier situation or divine command whose original wording is absent from the written text. We accept what it reports. That does not give later reports a general license to silence an existing Qur’anic verse.

6.6. Al-Muzzammil: An Individual Command, Companions Who Joined, and Collective Relief

The beginning and end of Al-Muzzammil are also offered as evidence of internal abrogation. The claim is that the opening verses required Muslims to stand for much of the night, and verse 20 then lightened that requirement, abrogating the original command.

The inquiry must begin with the text: to whom was the opening command addressed?

The surah begins:

يَا أَيُّهَا الْمُزَّمِّلُ ۝ قُمِ اللَّيْلَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا ۝ نِّصْفَهُ أَوِ انقُصْ مِنْهُ قَلِيلًا ۝ أَوْ زِدْ عَلَيْهِ وَرَتِّلِ الْقُرْآنَ تَرْتِيلًا

Al-Muzzammil 73:1، Al-Muzzammil 73:2، Al-Muzzammil 73:3، Al-Muzzammil 73:4

You who are wrapped up, stand through the night, except a little: half of it, or a little less, or add to it; and recite the Qur’an with measured deliberation.

Every command is singular: “stand,” “lessen,” “add,” and “recite.” The addressee is al-muzzammil, the one wrapped up. The verse does not say, “You who have believed, all of you must do this.”

The final verse continues addressing the same individual, then mentions a group accompanying him:

إِنَّ رَبَّكَ يَعْلَمُ أَنَّكَ تَقُومُ أَدْنَىٰ مِن ثُلُثَيِ اللَّيْلِ وَنِصْفَهُ وَثُلُثَهُ وَطَائِفَةٌ مِّنَ الَّذِينَ مَعَكَ وَاللَّهُ يُقَدِّرُ اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ عَلِمَ أَن لَّن تُحْصُوهُ فَتَابَ عَلَيْكُمْ فَاقْرَءُوا مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنَ الْقُرْآنِ عَلِمَ أَن سَيَكُونُ مِنكُم مَّرْضَىٰ وَآخَرُونَ يَضْرِبُونَ فِي الْأَرْضِ يَبْتَغُونَ مِن فَضْلِ اللَّهِ وَآخَرُونَ يُقَاتِلُونَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ فَاقْرَءُوا مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنْهُ وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَأَقْرِضُوا اللَّهَ قَرْضًا حَسَنًا وَمَا تُقَدِّمُوا لِأَنفُسِكُم مِّنْ خَيْرٍ تَجِدُوهُ عِندَ اللَّهِ هُوَ خَيْرًا وَأَعْظَمَ أَجْرًا وَاسْتَغْفِرُوا اللَّهَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ

Al-Muzzammil 73:20

Your Lord knows that you stand for nearly two-thirds of the night, and half of it, and a third of it, as does a group of those with you. The God measures night and day. He knew that you would not measure it precisely, so He turned toward you. Read, then, as much of the Qur’an as is manageable for you. He knew that some of you would be ill, others traveling through the land seeking The God’s bounty, and others fighting in His cause. Read as much of it as is manageable, establish Salat, give Zakat, and lend The God a good loan. Whatever good you send ahead for yourselves, you will find it with The God, better and greater in reward. Seek The God’s forgiveness. The God is forgiving and merciful.

The verse begins:

«إِنَّ رَبَّكَ يَعْلَمُ أَنَّكَ تَقُومُ»

Al-Muzzammil 73:20

Your Lord knows that you stand.

It then adds:

«وَطَائِفَةٌ مِّنَ الَّذِينَ مَعَكَ»

Al-Muzzammil 73:20

And a group of those with you.

The text does not explain why this group joined him. We should not invent an answer, whether voluntary participation or a separate command. What it tells us is that the opening instruction addresses one person, a group joined him, and the ending announces relief to the group:

«فَتَابَ عَلَيْكُمْ فَاقْرَءُوا مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنَ الْقُرْآنِ»

Al-Muzzammil 73:20

He turned toward you; so read what is manageable.

This is real relief, and the Qur’an explains it: the difficulty of measuring the night, illness, travel, seeking divine bounty, and fighting.

Elsewhere, a nighttime instruction again addresses that same recipient in the singular:

وَمِنَ اللَّيْلِ فَتَهَجَّدْ بِهِ نَافِلَةً لَّكَ عَسَىٰ أَن يَبْعَثَكَ رَبُّكَ مَقَامًا مَّحْمُودًا

Al-Isra 17:79

During part of the night, keep vigil with it, as an additional observance for you; perhaps your Lord will raise you to a praised station.

This verse does not resolve every question about Al-Muzzammil. It does show, however, that the Qur’an also addresses nighttime worship specifically and individually to the Messenger.

Nor does Al-Muzzammil itself give us reason to imagine its addressee as an unknown person from a remote age. “Your Lord,” “you stand,” and “those with you” in verse 20 continue the opening address within the same setting of messengership. We need not insert the name Muhammad into the wording; the surah’s continuity is sufficient to identify whom it addresses.

The account is therefore more complex than “Verses 1–4 issued a general obligation for all Muslims, and verse 20 abrogated it.” The opening command is singular; a group’s participation is subsequently reported; and collective relief is announced by the Qur’an itself.

If someone calls this relationship naskh within their own terminology, they must still acknowledge that the Qur’an itself explains the relationship. This is not an outside report arriving to deprive an existing verse of its authority.

6.7. Widows: Two Periods Governing Two Different Matters

Al-Baqarah 2:234 and 2:240 are sometimes set against each other, with one declared the abrogator of the other. The apparent reason is straightforward: one verse mentions four months and ten days, the other a year. If both periods govern the same requirement, they appear to conflict.

But that is not what the verses say.

وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا يَتَرَبَّصْنَ بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا فَإِذَا بَلَغْنَ أَجَلَهُنَّ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِيمَا فَعَلْنَ فِي أَنفُسِهِنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَاللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ خَبِيرٌ

Al-Baqarah 2:234

Those of you who die leaving wives: the women shall wait for four months and ten days. When they reach the end of their term, there is no blame upon you for what they properly do concerning themselves. The God is fully aware of what you do.

This concerns a woman’s waiting period after her husband’s death: the interval before she makes a new decision about her life.

A few verses later, we read:

وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا وَصِيَّةً لِّأَزْوَاجِهِم مَّتَاعًا إِلَى الْحَوْلِ غَيْرَ إِخْرَاجٍ فَإِنْ خَرَجْنَ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِي مَا فَعَلْنَ فِي أَنفُسِهِنَّ مِن مَّعْرُوفٍ وَاللَّهُ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ

Al-Baqarah 2:240

Those of you who die leaving wives: let there be a bequest for their wives, providing maintenance for a year without expelling them. If they leave, there is no blame upon you for what they properly do concerning themselves. The God is mighty and wise.

The subject is no longer the waiting period. It is a bequest, maintenance for a year, and protection from being expelled.

Even the phrase:

«فَإِنْ خَرَجْنَ»

Al-Baqarah 2:240

If they leave,

shows that a woman is not required to remain in the house for a year. The year is neither confinement nor a waiting period; it is a right to provision and protection from eviction.

Four months and ten days and one year are thus not competing answers to the same question.

One verse specifies how long the woman waits.

The other specifies how long she is entitled to provision and protection from being turned out.

Abrogation resolves no difficulty here and explains nothing further in the Qur’an. Two different subjects are first collapsed into one; a conflict is then created; one verse is finally declared abrogated to resolve that manufactured conflict.

6.8. Bequests and Inheritance: The Inheritance Verses Themselves Preserve Prior Bequests

Another familiar claim holds that the command to make a bequest in Al-Baqarah 2:180 was abrogated by the inheritance verses in An-Nisa. The reasoning is that, once the Qur’an assigned fixed shares to heirs, there was no longer room for a bequest to parents or relatives who were themselves heirs.

That reading, however, conflicts with the inheritance verses themselves.

Al-Baqarah says:

كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ إِن تَرَكَ خَيْرًا الْوَصِيَّةُ لِلْوَالِدَيْنِ وَالْأَقْرَبِينَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ حَقًّا عَلَى الْمُتَّقِينَ

Al-Baqarah 2:180

It is prescribed for you, when death approaches one of you and they leave wealth, to make a proper bequest for parents and near relatives: a duty upon those mindful of The God.

If the inheritance verses were intended to abolish this instruction, we would expect them to set bequests aside. Instead, the inheritance verse says:

يُوصِيكُمُ اللَّهُ فِي أَوْلَادِكُمْ لِلذَّكَرِ مِثْلُ حَظِّ الْأُنثَيَيْنِ فَإِن كُنَّ نِسَاءً فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ فَلَهُنَّ ثُلُثَا مَا تَرَكَ وَإِن كَانَتْ وَاحِدَةً فَلَهَا النِّصْفُ وَلِأَبَوَيْهِ لِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِّنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ مِمَّا تَرَكَ إِن كَانَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ فَإِن لَّمْ يَكُن لَّهُ وَلَدٌ وَوَرِثَهُ أَبَوَاهُ فَلِأُمِّهِ الثُّلُثُ فَإِن كَانَ لَهُ إِخْوَةٌ فَلِأُمِّهِ السُّدُسُ مِن بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِي بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ آبَاؤُكُمْ وَأَبْنَاؤُكُمْ لَا تَدْرُونَ أَيُّهُمْ أَقْرَبُ لَكُمْ نَفْعًا فَرِيضَةً مِّنَ اللَّهِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيمًا حَكِيمًا

An-Nisa 4:11

The God instructs you concerning your children: a male receives the share of two females. If there are more than two females, theirs is two-thirds of what is left; if there is one, hers is half. Each parent receives one-sixth of what is left if the deceased has a child. If there is no child and the parents inherit, the mother receives one-third. If there are siblings, the mother receives one-sixth, after any bequest made or debt. You do not know whether your parents or your children are nearer to you in benefit. This is an obligation from The God. The God is knowing and wise.

The very verse assigning shares to parents and children specifies that distribution takes place after a bequest or debt:

«مِن بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِي بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ»

An-Nisa 4:12

The following verse repeats the sequence several times and adds an important qualification:

وَلَكُمْ نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ أَزْوَاجُكُمْ إِن لَّمْ يَكُن لَّهُنَّ وَلَدٌ فَإِن كَانَ لَهُنَّ وَلَدٌ فَلَكُمُ الرُّبُعُ مِمَّا تَرَكْنَ مِن بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِينَ بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ وَلَهُنَّ الرُّبُعُ مِمَّا تَرَكْتُمْ إِن لَّمْ يَكُن لَّكُمْ وَلَدٌ فَإِن كَانَ لَكُمْ وَلَدٌ فَلَهُنَّ الثُّمُنُ مِمَّا تَرَكْتُم مِّن بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ تُوصُونَ بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ وَإِن كَانَ رَجُلٌ يُورَثُ كَلَالَةً أَوِ امْرَأَةٌ وَلَهُ أَخٌ أَوْ أُخْتٌ فَلِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِّنْهُمَا السُّدُسُ فَإِن كَانُوا أَكْثَرَ مِن ذَٰلِكَ فَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ فِي الثُّلُثِ مِن بَعْدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصَىٰ بِهَا أَوْ دَيْنٍ غَيْرَ مُضَارٍّ وَصِيَّةً مِّنَ اللَّهِ وَاللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَلِيمٌ

An-Nisa 4:12

You receive half of what your wives leave if they have no child; if they have a child, you receive a quarter, after any bequest they make or debt. They receive a quarter of what you leave if you have no child; if you have a child, they receive an eighth, after any bequest you make or debt. If a man or woman leaves an estate in the circumstance described as kalālah and has a brother or sister, each receives one-sixth; if there are more, they share one-third, after any bequest made or debt, without causing harm. This is an instruction from The God. The God is knowing and forbearing.

“Without causing harm” shows that the Qur’an has not ignored possible misuse: a bequest must not become a means of injuring others.

On what basis, then, are the inheritance verses said to abrogate bequests?

Nor do these verses establish that an heir can no longer benefit from a bequest. Al-Baqarah expressly names parents and near relatives as beneficiaries, while An-Nisa calculates inheritance shares after the bequest.

The two entitlements need not cancel each other out.

Consider a contemporary illustration. A family may have a child with a severe disability who will need lifelong support. In recognition of that need, the parents could make additional provision through a bequest, within the requirements of propriety and without harming others. After the bequest and debts are settled, that child would still receive the inheritance share specified by the verses. This example is not evidence for the verse’s meaning; it illustrates how bequest and inheritance can serve different purposes.

No Qur’anic statement says that being an heir invalidates a bequest made to that person, or that receiving a bequest cancels their right to inherit.

The sequence in the verses is clear: bequest and debt, without causing harm; then distribution of the inheritance.

If someone concludes from these verses that bequests are abrogated, the difficulty is not in the text. The text preserves both. The interpreter has removed one and called that removal naskh.

6.9. Khamr and Maysir: Complementary Verses Rather Than Mutually Abrogating Ones

The passages on khamr and maysir—intoxicants and gambling—are among the best-known examples offered for gradual legislation followed by abrogation of earlier stages. The familiar account says that the Qur’an first permitted khamr, then restricted it only around Salat, and finally prohibited it, each stage abrogating the one before.

We must return to the verses themselves.

Before turning to Al-Baqarah 2:219, consider An-Nahl:

وَمِن ثَمَرَاتِ النَّخِيلِ وَالْأَعْنَابِ تَتَّخِذُونَ مِنْهُ سَكَرًا وَرِزْقًا حَسَنًا إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لِّقَوْمٍ يَعْقِلُونَ

An-Nahl 16:67

From the fruits of date palms and vines you obtain intoxicating drink and good provision. In this is a sign for people who reason.

The verse reports that people obtain “intoxicating drink” and “good provision” from these fruits. It issues no ruling that intoxicating drink is lawful. Even the adjective “good” modifies “provision,” not “intoxicating drink.” Reporting an existing practice does not in itself authorize it.

Al-Baqarah then says:

يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْخَمْرِ وَالْمَيْسِرِ قُلْ فِيهِمَا إِثْمٌ كَبِيرٌ وَمَنَافِعُ لِلنَّاسِ وَإِثْمُهُمَا أَكْبَرُ مِن نَّفْعِهِمَا وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ مَاذَا يُنفِقُونَ قُلِ الْعَفْوَ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ اللَّهُ لَكُمُ الْآيَاتِ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَفَكَّرُونَ

Al-Baqarah 2:219

They ask you about khamr and maysir. Say: In them is great sin and benefit for people, but their sin is greater than their benefit. They ask what they should spend. Say: What is surplus. Thus The God makes the verses clear to you, so that you may reflect.

This verse likewise issues no permission. It assesses khamr and maysir: they offer benefits, but the sin associated with them is greater.

An-Nisa says:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَقْرَبُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَأَنتُمْ سُكَارَىٰ حَتَّىٰ تَعْلَمُوا مَا تَقُولُونَ وَلَا جُنُبًا إِلَّا عَابِرِي سَبِيلٍ حَتَّىٰ تَغْتَسِلُوا وَإِن كُنتُم مَّرْضَىٰ أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ أَوْ جَاءَ أَحَدٌ مِّنكُم مِّنَ الْغَائِطِ أَوْ لَامَسْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَلَمْ تَجِدُوا مَاءً فَتَيَمَّمُوا صَعِيدًا طَيِّبًا فَامْسَحُوا بِوُجُوهِكُمْ وَأَيْدِيكُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَفُوًّا غَفُورًا

An-Nisa 4:43

You who have believed, do not approach Salat while intoxicated until you know what you are saying, nor in a state of janābah, except while passing through, until you have washed. If you are ill or traveling, or one of you has relieved themselves, or you have had contact with women, and you find no water, seek clean earth and wipe your faces and hands with it. The God is pardoning and forgiving.

This specifically restricts intoxication in relation to Salat. A reader may infer that intoxication outside Salat had not, at that stage, been prohibited in equally explicit terms. Accepting that inference does not yet establish abrogation.

Verse 4:43 never says, “Intoxication outside Salat is lawful.” It gives a specific prohibition: do not approach Salat while intoxicated until you understand what you say. A verse’s limited subject does not create a positive ruling for every situation outside it.

Al-Ma’idah then says:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنَّمَا الْخَمْرُ وَالْمَيْسِرُ وَالْأَنصَابُ وَالْأَزْلَامُ رِجْسٌ مِّنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَانِ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ

Al-Ma’idah 5:90

You who have believed, khamr, maysir, cultic stones, and divining arrows are an impurity of Satan’s work. Avoid it, so that you may prosper.

The next verse expands on the reason:

إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ الشَّيْطَانُ أَن يُوقِعَ بَيْنَكُمُ الْعَدَاوَةَ وَالْبَغْضَاءَ فِي الْخَمْرِ وَالْمَيْسِرِ وَيَصُدَّكُمْ عَن ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَعَنِ الصَّلَاةِ فَهَلْ أَنتُم مُّنتَهُونَ

Al-Ma’idah 5:91

Satan seeks to sow hostility and hatred among you through khamr and maysir, and to turn you away from remembrance of The God and from Salat. Will you desist?

Notably, this final passage returns to the same concern with Salat:

«وَيَصُدَّكُمْ عَن ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَعَنِ الصَّلَاةِ»

Al-Ma’idah 5:91

Al-Ma’idah has not discarded the reasoning of An-Nisa; it places it within a wider account. Intoxication obstructs understanding of what is said in Salat, while khamr more broadly turns people from remembrance of The God and Salat.

Nor does Al-Ma’idah invalidate Al-Baqarah’s statement. The assessment that khamr and maysir involve great sin outweighing their benefit remains true after the command to avoid them.

We may recognize a progressive clarification and strengthening of the practical requirement without treating that progression as invalidation of earlier statements. In this reading, the passages complement one another:

Al-Baqarah weighs sin against benefit; An-Nisa addresses intoxication’s effect on awareness during Salat; Al-Ma’idah identifies the satanic source, the social and spiritual consequences, and the requirement to abstain.

To construct abrogation here, one must turn An-Nahl’s report into legal permission, infer a general permission outside Salat from An-Nisa’s specific prohibition, then make Al-Ma’idah abrogate those inferred permissions. That procedure manufactures the rulings needed for an abrogation scheme rather than reading what the verses actually state.

6.10. At-Tawbah 9:5: Removing Context and Importing Religious Coercion

One of the most dangerous uses of abrogation theory concerns At-Tawbah 9:5. It has been detached from its setting of war, treaties, and a period of notice, then treated as a general command about all associators or even all non-Muslims. The resulting conflict with other verses is resolved by declaring the passages on peace, justice, and “no compulsion in religion” abrogated.

This is more than a technical mistake about naskh. It changes the verse’s subject and introduces religious compulsion that the passage itself does not prescribe.

To understand verse 5, we must read what precedes and follows it.

The preceding verse says:

إِلَّا الَّذِينَ عَاهَدتُّم مِّنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ ثُمَّ لَمْ يَنقُصُوكُمْ شَيْئًا وَلَمْ يُظَاهِرُوا عَلَيْكُمْ أَحَدًا فَأَتِمُّوا إِلَيْهِمْ عَهْدَهُمْ إِلَىٰ مُدَّتِهِمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُتَّقِينَ

At-Tawbah 9:4

Except those associators with whom you made a treaty, who have not failed you in anything or supported anyone against you: fulfill your treaty with them until its term ends. The God loves those mindful of Him.

Before the fighting verse, then, the Qur’an expressly exempts a group of associators: those who have not broken their treaty.

It then says:

فَإِذَا انسَلَخَ الْأَشْهُرُ الْحُرُمُ فَاقْتُلُوا الْمُشْرِكِينَ حَيْثُ وَجَدتُّمُوهُمْ وَخُذُوهُمْ وَاحْصُرُوهُمْ وَاقْعُدُوا لَهُمْ كُلَّ مَرْصَدٍ فَإِن تَابُوا وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ فَخَلُّوا سَبِيلَهُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ

At-Tawbah 9:5

When the sacred months have passed, kill the associators wherever you find them, capture them, besiege them, and lie in wait for them at every lookout. If they repent, establish Salat, and give Zakat, leave their way open. The God is forgiving and merciful.

Read alone, “If they repent…” might suggest that conversion is the only means of release. The next verse prevents that generalization:

وَإِنْ أَحَدٌ مِّنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ اسْتَجَارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ حَتَّىٰ يَسْمَعَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ أَبْلِغْهُ مَأْمَنَهُ ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ قَوْمٌ لَّا يَعْلَمُونَ

At-Tawbah 9:6

If any of the associators seeks your protection, grant it so that he may hear The God’s words, then deliver him to a place of safety. That is because they are people who do not know.

Safe passage is not made conditional upon accepting the faith. At-Tawbah 9:5 cannot therefore be reduced to “Islam or death.”

The following verse returns to treaties:

كَيْفَ يَكُونُ لِلْمُشْرِكِينَ عَهْدٌ عِندَ اللَّهِ وَعِندَ رَسُولِهِ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ عَاهَدتُّمْ عِندَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ فَمَا اسْتَقَامُوا لَكُمْ فَاسْتَقِيمُوا لَهُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُتَّقِينَ

At-Tawbah 9:7

How can the associators have a treaty with The God and His Messenger, except those with whom you made a treaty at the Sacred Mosque? As long as they remain faithful to you, remain faithful to them. The God loves those mindful of Him.

A few verses later, the grounds for fighting are stated more explicitly:

وَإِن نَّكَثُوا أَيْمَانَهُم مِّن بَعْدِ عَهْدِهِمْ وَطَعَنُوا فِي دِينِكُمْ فَقَاتِلُوا أَئِمَّةَ الْكُفْرِ إِنَّهُمْ لَا أَيْمَانَ لَهُمْ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَنتَهُونَ

At-Tawbah 9:12

If they break their oaths after their treaty and attack your religion, fight the leaders of rejection, for they have no binding oaths, so that they may desist.

أَلَا تُقَاتِلُونَ قَوْمًا نَّكَثُوا أَيْمَانَهُمْ وَهَمُّوا بِإِخْرَاجِ الرَّسُولِ وَهُم بَدَءُوكُمْ أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍ أَتَخْشَوْنَهُمْ فَاللَّهُ أَحَقُّ أَن تَخْشَوْهُ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ

At-Tawbah 9:13

Will you not fight people who broke their oaths, sought to expel the Messenger, and initiated hostilities against you? Do you fear them? The God has a greater claim to your reverence, if you are believers.

In this context, association of partners is not, by itself, the criterion for war. Treaties, treaty-breaking, expulsion, and the initiation of hostilities belong to the passage’s own account.

Al-Mumtahanah draws the same distinction:

لَا يَنْهَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنِ الَّذِينَ لَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَلَمْ يُخْرِجُوكُم مِّن دِيَارِكُمْ أَن تَبَرُّوهُمْ وَتُقْسِطُوا إِلَيْهِمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُقْسِطِينَ

Al-Mumtahanah 60:8

The God does not forbid you to act kindly and justly toward those who have not fought you on account of religion or expelled you from your homes. The God loves those who act justly.

إِنَّمَا يَنْهَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنِ الَّذِينَ قَاتَلُوكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَأَخْرَجُوكُم مِّن دِيَارِكُمْ وَظَاهَرُوا عَلَىٰ إِخْرَاجِكُمْ أَن تَوَلَّوْهُمْ وَمَن يَتَوَلَّهُمْ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الظَّالِمُونَ

Al-Mumtahanah 60:9

The God only forbids you to take as allies those who fought you on account of religion, expelled you from your homes, and supported your expulsion. Those who take them as allies are the wrongdoers.

On belief itself, the Qur’an states expressly:

لَا إِكْرَاهَ فِي الدِّينِ قَد تَّبَيَّنَ الرُّشْدُ مِنَ الْغَيِّ فَمَن يَكْفُرْ بِالطَّاغُوتِ وَيُؤْمِن بِاللَّهِ فَقَدِ اسْتَمْسَكَ بِالْعُرْوَةِ الْوُثْقَىٰ لَا انفِصَامَ لَهَا وَاللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ

Al-Baqarah 2:256

There is no compulsion in religion. Sound guidance has become distinct from error. Whoever rejects taghut and believes in The God has grasped the firmest handhold, which never breaks. The God is hearing and knowing.

At-Tawbah 9:29 is sometimes introduced to extend the argument:

قَاتِلُوا الَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَلَا بِالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَلَا يُحَرِّمُونَ مَا حَرَّمَ اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ وَلَا يَدِينُونَ دِينَ الْحَقِّ مِنَ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ حَتَّىٰ يُعْطُوا الْجِزْيَةَ عَن يَدٍ وَهُمْ صَاغِرُونَ

At-Tawbah 9:29

Fight those among the People of the Book who do not believe in The God or the Last Day, do not treat as forbidden what The God and His Messenger have forbidden, and do not adhere to the religion of truth, until they pay the jizyah by hand while in a subordinate position.

That verse requires its own contextual examination; this article does not attempt to resolve all its details. But even here, the endpoint is not “until they believe.” The specified condition is payment of jizyah. It does not establish a rule of changing belief by the sword.

Making At-Tawbah 9:5 a warrant for religious coercion requires a series of omissions: separate it from treaty and war; ignore verse 4’s exception; remove verse 6’s protection; set aside verse 7’s reciprocal fidelity; forget treaty-breaking and the initiation of hostilities in verses 12–13; then declare other passages, including “no compulsion in religion,” abrogated.

This goes beyond mislabeling a relationship as naskh. The verse’s subject is changed, and a ruling is imported into religion that a reading of the complete passage does not yield.

Here the application of Al-Hajj 22:52 to inherited religious material becomes relevant. This is explicitly an application, not a claim that Al-Hajj 22:52 literally discusses At-Tawbah 9:5 or later schools of law. An interpretation that survives only by silencing verses on treaties, protection, justice, and freedom from compulsion must itself be reassessed against the Qur’an.

What needs removing is the interference and distortion that imports coercion into religion, rather than the clear verses defining the limits of war, treaty obligations, protection, and freedom of belief.

7. Gradual Revelation: The Qur’an Explains Its Purpose

After examples such as khamr, Al-Anfal, Al-Mujadilah, and Al-Muzzammil, an objection may remain: surely gradual revelation itself shows that earlier rulings applied temporarily before later verses abrogated them.

Those who rejected the message themselves asked why the Qur’an was not revealed all at once, and the Qur’an explained its gradual delivery:

وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لَوْلَا نُزِّلَ عَلَيْهِ الْقُرْآنُ جُمْلَةً وَاحِدَةً كَذَٰلِكَ لِنُثَبِّتَ بِهِ فُؤَادَكَ وَرَتَّلْنَاهُ تَرْتِيلًا

Al-Furqan 25:32

Those who rejected the truth said, “Why was the Qur’an not sent down to him all at once?” Thus it was, so that We might strengthen your heart through it; and We arranged it in measured sequence.

The next verse continues:

وَلَا يَأْتُونَكَ بِمَثَلٍ إِلَّا جِئْنَاكَ بِالْحَقِّ وَأَحْسَنَ تَفْسِيرًا

Al-Furqan 25:33

They bring you no example without Our bringing you the truth and the best clarification.

The stated purpose is to strengthen the Prophet’s heart and bring truth and clarification in response to what his opponents raise. Revelation unfolds amid life, questions, pressure, decisions, and encounters with actual circumstances.

Gradual delivery alone does not establish that earlier verses were revealed in order to be invalidated.

In some cases the Qur’an expressly announces relief or a new arrangement: Al-Anfal speaks of a lightened burden and the audience’s weakness; Al-Mujadilah of The God turning toward them; Al-Muzzammil of reading what is manageable; Al-Baqarah 2:187 of “Now have relations with them.” Each must be read according to its own subject and context.

These examples establish that, where the Qur’an explains a change for its audience, the explanation is present in the text. Gradual revelation does not by itself authorize a list of verses stripped of authority.

This matters today too. We now have the Qur’an as a complete collection, but human understanding and formation are not completed all at once. Someone may read the entire Book quickly without yet undergoing the change in thought and conduct called for by even one of its verses.

The Book is complete; our reception of it still unfolds in stages.

When a verse conflicts with an inherited belief or ruling, that belief must first be reassessed against the Qur’an. Difficulty of understanding, or conflict with inherited tradition, is not itself evidence that a verse’s ruling has ended.

8. Can the Messenger’s Clarification or Another Revelation Set Aside a Qur’anic Ruling?

One major defense of abrogation through reports remains. Even if the Qur’an itself does not declare a verse abrogated, the argument says, the Prophet was charged with clarification and was to be obeyed. He may therefore have announced the end of its ruling through knowledge or revelation outside the Qur’anic text.

This argument contains several separate steps. They must not be taken for granted as a single proposition.

First, was the Messenger charged with clarification? Yes.

The Qur’an says:

بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ وَالزُّبُرِ وَأَنزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ الذِّكْرَ لِتُبَيِّنَ لِلنَّاسِ مَا نُزِّلَ إِلَيْهِمْ وَلَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ

An-Nahl 16:44

With clear proofs and writings; and We sent down the Reminder to you so that you might clarify for people what was sent down to them, and so that they might reflect.

The sentence identifies the subject of that clarification:

«مَا نُزِّلَ إِلَيْهِمْ»

An-Nahl 16:44

What was sent down to them.

The same surah says:

وَمَا أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ إِلَّا لِتُبَيِّنَ لَهُمُ الَّذِي اخْتَلَفُوا فِيهِ وَهُدًى وَرَحْمَةً لِّقَوْمٍ يُؤْمِنُونَ

An-Nahl 16:64

We sent down the Book to you only so that you might clarify for them what they differed about, and as guidance and mercy for people who believe.

The Messenger’s clarification belongs to his mission. It does not follow, without a further argument, that clarification means authority to disable the ruling of the revealed text. That is a separate claim requiring its own evidence.

The Qur’an also expressly commands obedience to the Messenger:

مَن يُطِعِ الرَّسُولَ فَقَدْ أَطَاعَ اللَّهَ وَمَن تَوَلَّىٰ فَمَا أَرْسَلْنَاكَ عَلَيْهِمْ حَفِيظًا

An-Nisa 4:80

Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed The God. As for those who turn away, We have not sent you as their keeper.

This article does not deny obedience to the Messenger. It asks about a further step: how do we know that a particular report attributed to him today really records his words? And if it does, how do we know that those words were intended to end a Qur’anic verse’s ruling?

Yunus 10:15 defines the limits of changing the Qur’an even for the Messenger himself:

وَإِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتُنَا بَيِّنَاتٍ قَالَ الَّذِينَ لَا يَرْجُونَ لِقَاءَنَا ائْتِ بِقُرْآنٍ غَيْرِ هَٰذَا أَوْ بَدِّلْهُ قُلْ مَا يَكُونُ لِي أَنْ أُبَدِّلَهُ مِن تِلْقَاءِ نَفْسِي إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰ إِلَيَّ إِنِّي أَخَافُ إِنْ عَصَيْتُ رَبِّي عَذَابَ يَوْمٍ عَظِيمٍ

Yunus 10:15

When Our clear verses are recited to them, those who do not expect to meet Us say, “Bring a Qur’an other than this, or change it.” Say: It is not for me to change it on my own initiative. I follow only what is revealed to me. If I disobey my Lord, I fear the punishment of a tremendous Day.

Even the Messenger has no authority to change the Qur’an “on my own initiative.”

The Qur’an does, however, show that The God could inform the Prophet of matters whose disclosure is not reproduced as an independent command in the written Qur’an. For example:

وَإِذْ أَسَرَّ النَّبِيُّ إِلَىٰ بَعْضِ أَزْوَاجِهِ حَدِيثًا فَلَمَّا نَبَّأَتْ بِهِ وَأَظْهَرَهُ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ عَرَّفَ بَعْضَهُ وَأَعْرَضَ عَن بَعْضٍ فَلَمَّا نَبَّأَهَا بِهِ قَالَتْ مَنْ أَنبَأَكَ هَٰذَا قَالَ نَبَّأَنِيَ الْعَلِيمُ الْخَبِيرُ

At-Tahrim 66:3

When the Prophet confided something to one of his wives, and she disclosed it, The God made him aware of it. He made part of it known and passed over part. When he told her of it, she asked, “Who informed you of this?” He said, “The Knowing, the Aware informed me.”

We cannot therefore deny in principle that the Prophet received information beyond the text of Qur’anic commands. The qiblah example likewise attests an earlier divine instruction whose wording is absent from the written Qur’an. Al-Baqarah 2:187 reports an earlier situation without reproducing its original instruction.

These three examples also establish a point about our knowledge: we know of these matters because the Qur’an itself reports them.

It does not automatically follow that a parallel body of legislation existed whose authority any later report could invoke to end an existing Qur’anic ruling.

At least two propositions must be established separately:

First, does this particular report genuinely come from the Messenger?

Second, if its attribution is established, was the statement intended to end the ruling of this particular verse?

Calling a report “unrecited revelation” proves neither proposition.

The Qur’an says of its own role:

قُلْ أَيُّ شَيْءٍ أَكْبَرُ شَهَادَةً قُلِ اللَّهُ شَهِيدٌ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَكُمْ وَأُوحِيَ إِلَيَّ هَٰذَا الْقُرْآنُ لِأُنذِرَكُم بِهِ وَمَن بَلَغَ أَئِنَّكُمْ لَتَشْهَدُونَ أَنَّ مَعَ اللَّهِ آلِهَةً أُخْرَىٰ قُل لَّا أَشْهَدُ قُلْ إِنَّمَا هُوَ إِلَٰهٌ وَاحِدٌ وَإِنَّنِي بَرِيءٌ مِّمَّا تُشْرِكُونَ

Al-An‘am 6:19

Say: What is greatest in testimony? Say: The God is witness between me and you. This Qur’an has been revealed to me so that I may warn you through it, and whoever it reaches. Do you testify that there are other gods alongside The God? Say: I do not testify to that. Say: He is only One God, and I am free of what you associate with Him.

The same issue arises with “the Book and wisdom.” Two different words do not by themselves establish that “wisdom” names an independent collection of reports.

After a series of its own commands, the Qur’an says:

ذَٰلِكَ مِمَّا أَوْحَىٰ إِلَيْكَ رَبُّكَ مِنَ الْحِكْمَةِ وَلَا تَجْعَلْ مَعَ اللَّهِ إِلَٰهًا آخَرَ فَتُلْقَىٰ فِي جَهَنَّمَ مَلُومًا مَّدْحُورًا

Al-Isra 17:39

This is of the wisdom your Lord has revealed to you. Do not set up another god alongside The God, lest you be cast into Hell, blameworthy and banished.

This establishes that at least some Qur’anic content is called “of the wisdom.” We should infer neither that all wisdom is confined to those verses nor that every occurrence of “wisdom” names a separate hadith collection.

Another verse says:

وَاذْكُرْنَ مَا يُتْلَىٰ فِي بُيُوتِكُنَّ مِنْ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ وَالْحِكْمَةِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ لَطِيفًا خَبِيرًا

Al-Ahzab 33:34

Remember what is recited in your homes of The God’s verses and wisdom. The God is subtle and fully aware.

This too speaks of divine verses and wisdom within what is recited in the homes, without identifying wisdom as a particular external collection. Merely coordinating “Book and wisdom” or “verses and wisdom” does not establish the authority of a later body of reports.

The familiar verse in Al-Hashr also requires its full context:

مَّا أَفَاءَ اللَّهُ عَلَىٰ رَسُولِهِ مِنْ أَهْلِ الْقُرَىٰ فَلِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ وَلِذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْيَتَامَىٰ وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ كَيْ لَا يَكُونَ دُولَةً بَيْنَ الْأَغْنِيَاءِ مِنكُمْ وَمَا آتَاكُمُ الرَّسُولُ فَخُذُوهُ وَمَا نَهَاكُمْ عَنْهُ فَانتَهُوا وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ شَدِيدُ الْعِقَابِ

Al-Hashr 59:7

What The God restored to His Messenger from the people of the settlements belongs to The God, the Messenger, near relatives, orphans, those in need, and the stranded traveler, so that it does not circulate only among the wealthy among you. Take what the Messenger gives you and refrain from what he forbids you. Be mindful of The God; The God is severe in punishment.

The context concerns the distribution of property. Even if “Take what the Messenger gives you” yields a broader principle of obedience, the same two questions remain: does the particular report genuinely come from the Messenger, and, if so, was it intended to end a Qur’anic ruling?

The principle of obedience does not replace the evidence needed for those two steps.

Al-Mubin—“The Clarifier”—and the Messenger’s delivery and clarification are examined more fully in The Distortion of “Al-Mubin”—“The Clarifier”—and the Separation of the Messenger’s Delivery from the Clarifying Qur’an.

9. Attributing a Ruling to The God: The Label Naskh Is Not Evidence

The issue ultimately leads to a more fundamental question. When we say that a ruling belongs to religion, or that The God no longer requires a verse’s ruling, what supports that attribution to Him?

On legislating in religion, the Qur’an says:

أَمْ لَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ شَرَعُوا لَهُم مِّنَ الدِّينِ مَا لَمْ يَأْذَن بِهِ اللَّهُ وَلَوْلَا كَلِمَةُ الْفَصْلِ لَقُضِيَ بَيْنَهُمْ وَإِنَّ الظَّالِمِينَ لَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

Ash-Shura 42:21

Or do they have partners who have prescribed for them in religion what The God has not permitted? Were it not for the decisive word, judgment would already have been passed between them. The wrongdoers will have a painful punishment.

On declaring things lawful and unlawful, it says:

وَلَا تَقُولُوا لِمَا تَصِفُ أَلْسِنَتُكُمُ الْكَذِبَ هَٰذَا حَلَالٌ وَهَٰذَا حَرَامٌ لِّتَفْتَرُوا عَلَى اللَّهِ الْكَذِبَ إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَفْتَرُونَ عَلَى اللَّهِ الْكَذِبَ لَا يُفْلِحُونَ

An-Nahl 16:116

Do not say, through the falsehood your tongues describe, “This is lawful and this is unlawful,” inventing a lie against The God. Those who invent lies against The God will not prosper.

Declaring abrogation is ultimately an attribution to The God. To say “This verse’s ruling has ended” is to claim that He no longer required that command to be carried out.

That attribution needs evidence too.

Popularity and majority opinion cannot replace it:

وَإِن تُطِعْ أَكْثَرَ مَن فِي الْأَرْضِ يُضِلُّوكَ عَن سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ إِن يَتَّبِعُونَ إِلَّا الظَّنَّ وَإِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَخْرُصُونَ

Al-An‘am 6:116

If you obey most of those on earth, they will lead you away from The God’s path. They follow only conjecture and do nothing but guess.

This does not mean that every majority is mistaken. The criticism concerns following conjecture. It nevertheless shows that the number of people who hold a view cannot turn it into revelation.

Few adherents do not prove a statement true; many adherents do not replace proof either.

Terms such as naskh, specification, clarification, consensus, or Sunnah must not conceal the underlying question.

If a theory’s practical consequence is to set aside a clear verse’s ruling, the question remains:

Where is the evidence that The God ended this ruling?

Conclusion: Remove the Interference, Not the Divine Words

This study does not deny change in the course of divine guidance. The God can bring a new command, lighten a duty in relation to an audience’s circumstances, set an endpoint for a ruling, or prescribe fresh guidance across successive messengerships. The objection is to making naskh a device for disabling existing Qur’anic verses without clear evidence from revelation itself.

Al-Baqarah 2:106 belongs to a discussion of the People of the Book, newly revealed good, divine mercy, and The God’s authority to continue guidance. Even if āyah there includes verses of this Qur’an, the passage identifies no particular Qur’anic verse as abrogated.

Other passages, including Al-A‘la 87:6–7, Al-Isra 17:86, and Ar-Ra‘d 13:38–39, affirm divine power and will over forgetting, taking away, erasing, and establishing. They likewise supply no list of existing Qur’anic verses whose rulings have ended.

An-Nahl 16:101 attributes replacement to The God Himself. In a different use of n-s-kh, Al-Hajj 22:52 makes “what Satan casts” the object of removal and “His verses” what is made firm. Applying this criterion to later inheritance is our application. But the direction of scrutiny remains clear: statements surrounding the Qur’an must be tested against it, rather than silencing divine verses to preserve those statements.

“None can alter His words” reinforces that boundary. The issue is not whether The God can bring a new command. It is that human beings and later writings do not own His words.

The proposed examples of internal abrogation do not follow one pattern. An-Nisa 4:15 builds the possibility of a future endpoint into the command itself. For the qiblah and the nights of fasting, the Qur’an reports an earlier situation or command without preserving the wording of the initial instruction. Al-Anfal explains relief through the audience’s weakness. Al-Mujadilah places mercy and relief within the discussion of private consultation. Al-Muzzammil begins with a singular command, reports a group’s participation, and announces collective relief within the same surah. For widows, bequests, and inheritance, much of the alleged conflict results from conflating different subjects. The khamr and maysir passages complement one another; progression does not invalidate the truth of earlier statements. And detaching At-Tawbah 9:5 from treaties, protection, treaty-breaking, and the initiation of hostilities can import religious coercion that reading the complete passage does not support.

The Qur’an also shows that The God could inform the Messenger of matters or issue an earlier command whose wording is not recorded in the written Qur’an. We do not deny that. But we know these particular cases because the Qur’an attests them. They do not, without further evidence, establish a parallel body of reported law capable of terminating an existing Qur’anic ruling.

The central question remains straightforward:

How do we know that The God ended the ruling of this particular verse?

If the Qur’an itself establishes that ending, we accept it. But if silencing an existing verse requires a report, consensus, a legal term, or an inherited ruling, the outside source must establish two things: that it genuinely comes from the Messenger or The God, and that it was intended to end this particular verse’s ruling.

The name naskh does not replace that evidence.

The Qur’an must not become a book whose words survive while its authority to judge is handed over to later writings. When inherited religious teaching conflicts with a clear verse, it is that inheritance which must first answer to the Qur’an.

فَيَنسَخُ اللَّهُ مَا يُلْقِي الشَّيْطَانُ ثُمَّ يُحْكِمُ اللَّهُ آيَاتِهِ

Al-Hajj 22:52

The God removes what Satan casts, then makes His own verses firm.

What must be removed is interference with divine words, not the divine words themselves.
