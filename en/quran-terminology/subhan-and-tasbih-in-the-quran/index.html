Introduction

“Subhan Allah” (The God is exalted beyond every unworthy attribution) is a familiar expression in Muslim life. People say it during prayer, when something astonishes them, when they contemplate the grandeur of creation, or when they wish to glorify God.

But if tasbih (acknowledging and declaring God’s transcendence) consists only of speaking a phrase, how are we to understand the tasbih of the heavens, the earth, mountains, and birds?

The God says:

تُسَبِّحُ لَهُ السَّمَاوَاتُ السَّبْعُ وَالْأَرْضُ وَمَنْ فِيهِنَّ ۚ وَإِنْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ إِلَّا يُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِهِ وَلَٰكِنْ لَا تَفْقَهُونَ تَسْبِيحَهُمْ ۗ إِنَّهُ كَانَ حَلِيمًا غَفُورًا

Al-Isra 17:44

The seven heavens, the earth, and everyone in them glorify Him. There is nothing that does not glorify Him with praise, but you do not understand their glorification. He is indeed forbearing and forgiving.

The Qur’an attributes this glorification not only to human beings and angels, but to the whole of existence.

This study begins with the meaning of subhan and its relationship to a theology of divine transcendence and the philosophy of God’s absolute oneness. It then considers how tasbih finds expression in the order of creation, human understanding, and the way we live.

1. What do subhan and tasbih mean?

The Arabic word family built on s-b-h has two established branches of meaning: swimming and fluid movement, and glorification or declaring God free of unworthy attributes.

In Arabic, sabaha (to swim or move fluidly) describes swimming in water or moving smoothly through a course or expanse. The Qur’an uses yasbahun (they swim or move along)—a plural form of this verb—for the movement of the sun and moon in an orbit.

Sabbaha (to glorify or declare God transcendent), pronounced with a doubled “b,” has this devotional meaning. The difference in pronunciation marks a different verb form.

The table gives several members of this word family. Some are shown in their basic form; the Qur’an uses these forms or their grammatical variations.

Subhan is an Arabic verbal-noun construction used to express tanzih (affirming that God is free of defects and unworthy attributes). Subhan Allah declares The God transcendent; subhanaka means “You are transcendent,” addressing Him directly; subhanahu means “He is transcendent,” speaking about Him.

The shared root of fluid movement and glorification invites us to explore possible conceptual connections. Those connections, however, must be understood through the contexts and different uses of this word family in the Qur’an.

2. Divine transcendence: beyond the limits of human imagination

The God says:

سُبْحَانَ رَبِّكَ رَبِّ الْعِزَّةِ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ

As-Saffat 37:180

Your Lord, the Lord of might, is exalted beyond what they attribute to Him.

The same chapter speaks of people who attributed offspring to God or invented a kinship between Him and the jinn (unseen beings discussed in the Qur’an): As-Saffat 37:152 and As-Saffat 37:158. The words “beyond what they attribute” turn the discussion back to their claims. Yet affirming God’s transcendence does not end with rejecting these particular claims. Even when we use an attribute that the Qur’an itself applies to Him, can we contain its reality within our own conception of it?

We observe the world around us, learn about its phenomena through our senses and thought, and give things names and descriptions on the basis of that experience. When we speak about God, we necessarily use a language whose words and concepts have developed within the world of created beings.

Even in attributing good qualities to God, we may therefore unintentionally confine Him within our limited understanding of those qualities.

When we speak of God’s knowledge, we must not treat it as the limited, acquired knowledge of a human being. When we speak of His power, we must not measure it against power obtained through resources, tools, wealth, or influence. When we speak of His kindness, we must not imagine it as dependent on the emotions, needs, and attachments that shape human kindness.

The Qur’an says:

فَاطِرُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۚ جَعَلَ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا وَمِنَ الْأَنْعَامِ أَزْوَاجًا ۖ يَذْرَؤُكُمْ فِيهِ ۚ لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ

Ash-Shura 42:11

The Originator of the heavens and the earth: He has made mates for you from among yourselves, and pairs among livestock; through this He multiplies you. Nothing is like Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing.

The sequence of ideas has particular philosophical significance. The verse first describes pairing and reproduction among created beings, then denies any likeness to their Creator, and immediately affirms that He hears and sees.

We too hear and see. Our hearing, however, depends on ears, sound waves, and environmental conditions; our sight depends on eyes, light, and other biological capacities.

God hears and sees, but His hearing and sight cannot be defined within the limits of our experience of these capacities in created beings.

The Qur’an also says:

فَلَا تَضْرِبُوا الْأَمْثَالَ ۚ إِنَّ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنْتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

An-Nahl 16:74

So do not set up likenesses for The God. The God knows, and you do not know.

And in Ta-Ha:

يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِهِ عِلْمًا

Ta-Ha 20:110

He knows what is before them and what is behind them, and they cannot encompass Him in knowledge.

The God exceeds every image of divinity that the human mind can construct. Our study of divine names and the language of transcendence explains why descriptions of His forgiveness and mercy must convey that they surpass human limits. Our minds learn what forgiveness and kindness mean through the lives of created beings; that experience cannot set a boundary to God’s mercy.

Even the foundational declaration la ilaha illa Allah (there is no deity except The God) begins with a negation.

Alongside rejecting other objects of worship, that negation calls us to free ourselves from mental idols and restrictive images of divinity that we have fashioned.

The God alone is worthy of worship. Whenever we try to understand Him as even the most perfect being we can imagine, we have still imposed the boundary of our imagination upon Him. The reality of His being exceeds even our conception of an ilah (a deity or object of worship). This is what absolute transcendence means.

3. Family relationships, offspring, and divine self-sufficiency

Projecting family relationships onto the divine

In many religious and mythological cultures, people have extended their own family relationships into the world of the gods. They have imagined divine spouses, children, fathers, mothers, and genealogies, and have even explained divine rule through family relationships and inheritance.

In the Nicene–Constantinopolitan Creed, in the form adopted at the Council of Constantinople in 381 CE, Jesus Christ is called God’s only Son, begotten of the Father before all ages and of one essence with Him.

The Qur’an approaches this question through God’s self-sufficiency and absolute transcendence:

قَالُوا اتَّخَذَ وَلَدًا ۗ سُبْحَانَهُ ۖ هُوَ الْغَنِيُّ ۖ لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۚ إِنْ عِنْدَكُمْ مِنْ سُلْطَانٍ بِهَٰذَا ۚ أَتَقُولُونَ عَلَى مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

Yunus 10:68

They say, “The God has taken a child.” Exalted is He! He is free of all need. Everything in the heavens and everything on earth belongs to Him. You have no proof for this. Do you attribute to The God what you do not know?

Why does “He is free of all need” follow immediately after subhanahu (exalted is He)? What is the relationship between having a child and being in need?

How parenthood creates new needs

Even leaving aside the question of a spouse, having a child creates new relationships and needs in human life. The infant needs milk and care, while the parents become concerned for the child’s welfare. Their emotional bond can be so strong that separation from the child affects a parent’s health.

In the account of Moses’s mother, we read:

وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَىٰ أُمِّ مُوسَىٰ أَنْ أَرْضِعِيهِ ۖ فَإِذَا خِفْتِ عَلَيْهِ فَأَلْقِيهِ فِي الْيَمِّ وَلَا تَخَافِي وَلَا تَحْزَنِي ۖ إِنَّا رَادُّوهُ إِلَيْكِ وَجَاعِلُوهُ مِنَ الْمُرْسَلِينَ

Al-Qasas 28:7

We revealed to Moses’s mother: “Nurse him. Then, when you fear for him, cast him into the water, and neither fear nor grieve. We will return him to you and make him one of the Messengers.”

The verse brings together the infant’s need for nourishment and protection and the mother’s emotional attachment to her child.

In the account of Jacob and Joseph, we also read:

وَتَوَلَّىٰ عَنْهُمْ وَقَالَ يَا أَسَفَىٰ عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ وَابْيَضَّتْ عَيْنَاهُ مِنَ الْحُزْنِ فَهُوَ كَظِيمٌ

Yusuf 12:84

He turned away from them and said, “Alas for Joseph!” His eyes turned white from grief, while he held his sorrow within.

Jacob’s attachment to his son was so deep that the grief of separation from Joseph affected his physical health.

The Qur’an also addresses the material and social responsibilities that parenthood creates:

وَالْوَالِدَاتُ يُرْضِعْنَ أَوْلَادَهُنَّ حَوْلَيْنِ كَامِلَيْنِ ۖ لِمَنْ أَرَادَ أَنْ يُتِمَّ الرَّضَاعَةَ ۚ وَعَلَى الْمَوْلُودِ لَهُ رِزْقُهُنَّ وَكِسْوَتُهُنَّ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۚ لَا تُكَلَّفُ نَفْسٌ إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا ۚ لَا تُضَارَّ وَالِدَةٌ بِوَلَدِهَا وَلَا مَوْلُودٌ لَهُ بِوَلَدِهِ ۚ وَعَلَى الْوَارِثِ مِثْلُ ذَٰلِكَ ۗ فَإِنْ أَرَادَا فِصَالًا عَنْ تَرَاضٍ مِنْهُمَا وَتَشَاوُرٍ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْهِمَا ۗ وَإِنْ أَرَدْتُمْ أَنْ تَسْتَرْضِعُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا سَلَّمْتُمْ مَا آتَيْتُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۗ وَاتَّقُوا وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ

Al-Baqarah 2:233

Mothers shall nurse their children for two complete years, for those who wish to complete the nursing period. The child’s father is responsible for their food and clothing in a fair and recognized manner. No soul is burdened beyond its capacity. No mother shall be harmed because of her child, nor any father because of his child; the heir has a corresponding responsibility. If the two parents wish to wean the child by mutual consent and consultation, there is no blame upon them. If you wish to engage a wet nurse for your children, there is no blame upon you, provided you pay what is due in a fair and recognized manner. Be mindful of The God, and know that The God sees what you do.

With a child’s birth, the mother’s food and clothing, the parents’ cooperation in making decisions, and the heir’s responsibility all become matters of obligation to which the Qur’an gives attention.

These relationships and attachments are not faults in human life. Loving a child, caring for one, and making sacrifices for a child’s upbringing are among the finest human qualities. But a virtue in human life is not necessarily an attribute appropriate to God’s being.

Human beings also need children to continue their lineage because their own lives are finite. They may need heirs, support in old age, or someone to carry their name and work forward after their death.

The God, however, is not mortal and does not need a child to continue His existence. He is not powerless and does not need a child’s support to accomplish His purposes. Nor does He suffer from loneliness that must be relieved through companionship and emotional attachment.

We must distinguish here between meeting another’s needs and becoming dependent on another.

The God shows mercy to His creatures, provides for them, and meets their needs. He does not depend on them for His own mercy, power, or perfection.

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ أَنْتُمُ الْفُقَرَاءُ إِلَى ۖ هُوَ الْغَنِيُّ الْحَمِيدُ

Fatir 35:15

Human beings, you are the ones in need of The God; The God is free of all need and worthy of all praise.

Fatir 35:15 makes the direction of this dependence clear. Created beings need Him for their life and continued existence, and He provides for them all. Anyone who needs a child, heir, or supporter is themselves among those in need.

The fundamental distinction between Creator and creation

The Qur’an also approaches the question of offspring from another direction:

بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ أَنَّىٰ يَكُونُ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَلَمْ تَكُنْ لَهُ صَاحِبَةٌ ۖ وَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ ۖ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

Al-An'am 6:101

The unprecedented Originator of the heavens and the earth: how could He have a child when He has no consort? He created everything, and He knows everything.

The verse moves from spouse and child to the creation of “everything.” God’s relationship to every being is that of Creator to creation. In the account of Jesus himself, the Qur’an explains his coming into existence through God’s creative command.

مَا كَانَ أَن يَتَّخِذَ مِن وَلَدٍ سُبْحَانَهُ إِذَا قَضَىٰ أَمْرًا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُ كُن فَيَكُونُ

Maryam 19:35

It is not fitting for The God to take a child. Exalted is He! When He determines a matter, He only says to it, “Be,” and it comes to be.

In Al-Mu’minun, having offspring and the existence of multiple deities are considered together:

مَا اتَّخَذَ مِنْ وَلَدٍ وَمَا كَانَ مَعَهُ مِنْ إِلَٰهٍ ۚ إِذًا لَذَهَبَ كُلُّ إِلَٰهٍ بِمَا خَلَقَ وَلَعَلَا بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ ۚ سُبْحَانَ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ

Al-Mu'minun 23:91

The God has taken no child, and there has been no deity alongside Him. Otherwise, each deity would have taken away what it had created, and some would have risen above others. The God is exalted beyond what they attribute to Him.

At the end of Al-Isra, the Qur’an also says:

وَقُلِ الْحَمْدُ الَّذِي لَمْ يَتَّخِذْ وَلَدًا وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ شَرِيكٌ فِي الْمُلْكِ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ وَلِيٌّ مِنَ الذُّلِّ ۖ وَكَبِّرْهُ تَكْبِيرًا

Al-Isra 17:111

Say: praise belongs to The God, who has taken no child, has no partner in sovereignty, and needs no protector out of weakness or humiliation. Proclaim His greatness.

Three kinds of relationship are denied together: having a child, having a partner in sovereignty, and needing a protective supporter because of weakness.

All three should be examined in the light of God’s freedom from need, His self-subsistence and sustaining power, and His absolute transcendence.

4. Absolute oneness: can there be more than one absolute deity?

Projecting human systems of rule onto divinity

Ancient narratives sometimes place struggles for sovereignty among the gods themselves.

In Greek mythology, Zeus gains sovereignty by overcoming Cronus and defeating the Titans. In Egyptian mythology, Horus and Seth compete over succession to Osiris. Old Norse narratives also recount war between two groups of gods, the Aesir and the Vanir.

In Hindu tradition, the avatar (a divine manifestation or descent into the world) offers another conception of the relationship between divinity and human life. In Bhagavad Gita 4:7 and 4:8, Krishna speaks of manifesting in different ages to protect the righteous, destroy evildoers, and re-establish dharma (the sustaining moral and religious order). This passage concerns manifestation to restore order; struggles over succession and sovereignty appear in the preceding examples.

For a theology of divine transcendence, the question is whether we can understand God’s being through criteria drawn from birth, rivalry, sovereignty, and other experiences of our own world.

Examining the argument from conflicting divine wills

The God says:

لَوْ كَانَ فِيهِمَا آلِهَةٌ إِلَّا لَفَسَدَتَا ۚ فَسُبْحَانَ رَبِّ الْعَرْشِ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ

Al-Anbiya 21:22

Had there been deities in the two of them other than The God, both would have fallen into disorder. The God, Lord of the Throne, is exalted beyond what they attribute to Him.

Abd al-Malik al-Juwayni presents the argument from mutual obstruction in Al-Irshad (Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyyah, Beirut, p. 27). Suppose two gods exist: one wills a body to move, and the other wills the same body to remain still at the same instant. Both wishes cannot be realized. If neither is realized, both are unable to accomplish their will; if only one is realized, the other is unable. Al-Juwayni also considers agreement between the two wills and takes the possibility of disagreement as sufficient for his argument. Our question begins with that premise. If both supposed gods possess absolute knowledge and wisdom, why should we accept from the outset that either could will the opposite of the other?

Assuming an impossible situation to expose its contradiction is a familiar form of reasoning. The difficulty arises when we turn the incompatibility of two wills into a picture resembling a struggle between two rulers. If one wish is wise and the other mistaken, we have attributed error to one of the supposed gods. If both seek to impose their will on the other, we have imagined both as power-seeking. Why should our understanding of God begin with such a picture?

The end of the verse opens another path. God declares Himself exalted beyond what people attribute to Him. The pursuit of power, ignorance, and the need to prevail all arise from the limitations of created beings. Why introduce these qualities into our conception of the divine in order to establish His oneness?

Even if two supposed gods never disagreed or struggled with one another, would multiple absolute deities be possible?

Incomparability, self-subsistence, and absolute perfection

The God says:

لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ

Al-Imran 3:2

The God—there is no deity except Him—is the Ever-Living, self-subsisting and sustaining all.

Al-Ikhlas sets out the foundations of absolute oneness in four short verses:

قُلْ هُوَ أَحَدٌ

Al-Ikhlas 112:1

Say: He, The God, is One.

الصَّمَدُ

Al-Ikhlas 112:2

The God is free of need, and all depend on Him.

لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ

Al-Ikhlas 112:3

He neither begets nor was begotten.

وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ

Al-Ikhlas 112:4

And no one has been His equal.

The chapter brings together oneness, freedom from need, the denial of begetting or being begotten, and the denial of every equal.

Ash-Shura 42:11 likewise rules out any likeness to God with the declaration “Nothing is like Him.”

These attributes must not be studied in isolation. God’s oneness is bound to His absolute freedom from need, and His incomparability is fundamentally related to the absoluteness of His being and attributes.

Suppose two independent gods agree in everything and neither ever wills anything contrary to the other. A question still remains: what distinguishes them?

If one possesses a perfection that the other lacks, the other is not absolutely perfect.

If one knows something the other does not, the other’s knowledge is not absolute.

If one’s power ends where the other’s domain begins, neither is all-powerful.

If one rules only part of existence and the other rules another part, neither possesses absolute sovereignty.

If they need each other’s cooperation to accomplish anything, neither is absolutely self-sufficient.

If the existence and continuance of one depend on the other, the dependent one is not absolutely self-subsisting.

If both possess every perfection equally, we must still ask what distinguishes them.

Within creation, we can conceive of two things with the same qualities that differ in location, origin, time of emergence, or position in existence.

We cannot apply those same criteria to absolute reality. We cannot assign God a location, a time, an origin, or a bounded domain and then use it to distinguish Him from another absolute reality.

To speak of two independent realities, there must be a real distinction between them. If one has a place, time, origin, or rank that the other does not, we have identified them through their limits. If none of these limits exists and no difference can be shown in their being, knowledge, power, or self-subsistence, what remains beyond two names in our minds?

For this reason, “Nothing is like Him” and “No one has been His equal” are not merely two descriptions among other divine attributes. They are foundations for a philosophical understanding of absolute oneness.

If we imagine two realities that are both absolutely perfect, with no boundary distinguishing one from the other, we are in fact speaking of the same single reality.

Al-Mu’minun 23:91, examined above, speaks of dividing the domain of creation and of some deities rising above others. If each deity has a separate creative domain, its sovereignty is limited. If one is superior to another, the other lacks absolute perfection and power.

God’s oneness does not depend on His having rivals and defeating them. He alone is the reality worthy of absolute divinity.

This understanding is closely connected to the principle of God’s oneness and the study of andad (rivals or equals set up alongside God).

“As they say”: examining human conceptions of divinity

To understand the argument in Al-Anbiya 21:22 more fully, we should read it alongside two verses from Al-Isra.

قُلْ لَوْ كَانَ مَعَهُ آلِهَةٌ كَمَا يَقُولُونَ إِذًا لَابْتَغَوْا إِلَىٰ ذِي الْعَرْشِ سَبِيلًا

Al-Isra 17:42

Say: if there were deities alongside Him, as they say, they would then seek a way to the Possessor of the Throne.

سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَىٰ عَمَّا يَقُولُونَ عُلُوًّا كَبِيرًا

Al-Isra 17:43

He is exalted and far above what they say.

The key expressions are “as they say” in the first verse and “above what they say” in the second.

“As they say” locates the verse’s supposition within the claim of those who associate other deities with God. The wording itself says that these deities would seek a way to the Possessor of the Throne. In Ma‘alim al-Tanzil, commenting on Al-Isra 17:42, al-Baghawi interprets this search as an attempt to overcome Him and overthrow His sovereignty. He compares it to what earthly kings do to one another. Of the two interpretations he reports, he selects this first reading, writing that “the first is more correct.”

But can the conduct of earthly kings provide a measure for understanding the Possessor of the Throne? An earthly ruler has a limited territory that another can invade. He needs armies, allies, and supporters to retain his power. Applied to the supposed gods of those who worship multiple deities, this picture exposes their limitations. Applied to the Possessor of the Throne as well, it makes God resemble a ruler whose throne is vulnerable to seizure.

Seeking power itself indicates that the sought power is not yet possessed. Someone seeking a higher position does not already occupy it. The God, moreover, does not need to defend the Throne against rivals in order to preserve His sovereignty. After presenting the claim about other deities, the Qur’an returns to “what they say” and declares God exalted far above it.

Even in attempting to establish God’s oneness, we may imagine Him through the very limitations from which we declare Him free. Subhan (the declaration of His transcendence), at the end of this argument, is also a measure by which to examine our own way of thinking.

5. Human images of divinity within Muslim religious beliefs

Muslims too have written and taught about God’s attributes, His will, and His relationship to created beings. The same principle of transcendence by which we examine other people’s beliefs must also guide our examination of these beliefs.

Examples from Sunni religious sources

Attributing hesitation to God

Sahih al-Bukhari, a major Sunni hadith collection (reports attributed to Muhammad), contains the following statement attributed to God in report 6502:

«وَمَا تَرَدَّدْتُ عَنْ شَيْءٍ أَنَا فَاعِلُهُ تَرَدُّدِي عَنْ نَفْسِ الْمُؤْمِنِ، يَكْرَهُ الْمَوْتَ وَأَنَا أَكْرَهُ مَسَاءَتَهُ»

“I have not hesitated over anything I am going to do as I hesitate over taking the believer’s soul. He dislikes death, and I dislike distressing him.”

The report says the believer’s soul will be taken, yet also attributes hesitation to God. In our human experience, we know what it is to hesitate between carrying out an action and disliking its consequences. Which verse authorizes us to attribute such a condition to God?

Descending to the lowest heaven in the final third of the night

Sahih al-Bukhari, report 1145, states:

«يَنْزِلُ رَبُّنَا تَبَارَكَ وَتَعَالَى كُلَّ لَيْلَةٍ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ الدُّنْيَا حِينَ يَبْقَى ثُلُثُ اللَّيْلِ الْآخِرُ»

“Our Lord, blessed and exalted, descends every night to the lowest heaven when the final third of the night remains.”

The final third of the night arrives in one part of the world when it may be daytime elsewhere. If we understand “descending” as God moving from one location to another, we measure His location by the earth’s rotation and the hours of the night. In the Qur’an, the servant who calls upon Him finds Him near, wherever and whenever that call is made.

وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي عَنِّي فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌ ۖ أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ الدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ ۖ فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا لِي وَلْيُؤْمِنُوا بِي لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْشُدُونَ

Al-Baqarah 2:186

When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the caller’s prayer when they call upon Me. So let them respond to Me and believe in Me, so that they may find their way.

The expression “He places His foot” in a description of Hell

Sahih al-Bukhari, report 4848, says of Hell:

«حَتَّى يَضَعَ قَدَمَهُ فَتَقُولُ قَطِ قَطِ»

“Until He places His foot, and it says, ‘Enough, enough.’”

The Qur’an itself, when speaking of God’s greatness, also uses expressions that human beings know through bodily experience. In Az-Zumar 39:67, one such expression appears alongside subhanahu (exalted is He).

وَمَا قَدَرُوا حَقَّ قَدْرِهِ وَالْأَرْضُ جَمِيعًا قَبْضَتُهُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ وَالسَّمَاوَاتُ مَطْوِيَّاتٌ بِيَمِينِهِ ۚ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَىٰ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

Az-Zumar 39:67

They have not recognized The God’s true greatness. On the Day of Resurrection, the whole earth will be in His grasp, and the heavens will be folded in His right hand. He is exalted and above what they associate with Him.

The very verse that speaks of “His grasp” and “His right hand” begins with God’s greatness and moves to “He is exalted and above.” Earth and heaven are wholly under His command. For understanding such expressions, whose meaning calls for careful interpretation, the Qur’an provides a firm criterion in Ash-Shura 42:11. If we take “His right hand” to mean an organ like the hand of a created being, how can that be reconciled with “Nothing is like Him”? The report’s expression “He places His foot” must be assessed by the same criterion.

Reducing the prayers from fifty to five after repeated requests

Sahih al-Bukhari, report 349, recounts the mi‘raj (Muhammad’s ascent to heaven). According to the account, fifty prayers were initially prescribed for Muhammad’s community. Moses told Muhammad that his community could not bear this and advised him to ask his Lord for a reduction. After Muhammad returned repeatedly, the number was reduced to five.

In this report, Moses warns Muhammad about people’s capacity, and the requests for a reduction follow. If the Creator knows His servants’ capacity from the beginning, what warrants saying that He adjusted the number of prayers to that capacity after these repeated exchanges?

أَلَا يَعْلَمُ مَنْ خَلَقَ وَهُوَ اللَّطِيفُ الْخَبِيرُ

Al-Mulk 67:14

Does the One who created not know, when He is subtle in His care and fully aware?

Examples from Shi‘i religious sources

Making the earth’s continued existence depend on an Imam

Al-Kulayni’s Al-Kafi, volume 1, in the chapter “The earth is never without a divine proof,” report 10, p. 179, states:

«لَوْ بَقِيَتِ الْأَرْضُ بِغَيْرِ إِمَامٍ لَسَاخَتْ»

“If the earth were to remain without an Imam, it would sink.”

An Imam here is a divinely appointed religious guide in Shi‘i belief. The Qur’an describes the sustaining of heaven and earth as follows:

إِنَّ يُمْسِكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ أَنْ تَزُولَا ۚ وَلَئِنْ زَالَتَا إِنْ أَمْسَكَهُمَا مِنْ أَحَدٍ مِنْ بَعْدِهِ ۚ إِنَّهُ كَانَ حَلِيمًا غَفُورًا

Fatir 35:41

The God holds the heavens and the earth so that they do not pass away. Were they to pass away, no one after Him could hold them. He is indeed forbearing and forgiving.

Making the Imams intermediaries in sending rain and holding up the sky

Al-Shaykh al-Saduq includes the Ziyarat al-Jami‘ah al-Kabirah (a devotional visitation address to the Imams) in Man La Yahduruhu al-Faqih, volume 2, report 3213. This address also appears in the prayer collection Mafatih al-Jinan. It says to the Imams:

«بِكُمْ فَتَحَ اللَّهُ وَبِكُمْ يَخْتِمُ وَبِكُمْ يُنَزِّلُ الْغَيْثَ وَبِكُمْ يُمْسِكُ السَّمَاءَ أَنْ تَقَعَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ»

“Through you God began, and through you He brings to an end. Through you He sends down rain, and through you He holds the sky from falling upon the earth except by His permission.”

In Al-Hajj, we read:

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ سَخَّرَ لَكُمْ مَا فِي الْأَرْضِ وَالْفُلْكَ تَجْرِي فِي الْبَحْرِ بِأَمْرِهِ وَيُمْسِكُ السَّمَاءَ أَنْ تَقَعَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ ۗ إِنَّ بِالنَّاسِ لَرَءُوفٌ رَحِيمٌ

Al-Hajj 22:65

Have you not seen that The God has made available to you what is on earth and the ships that sail through the sea by His command, and that He holds the sky from falling upon the earth except by His permission? The God is indeed deeply compassionate and merciful towards people.

In both passages, God holds the sky. By adding “through you,” the devotional address makes the Imams intermediaries in this action. What evidence in the Qur’an establishes that God holds the sky through the Imams?

The return and reckoning of creation in the same devotional address

The same address says to the Imams:

«إِيَابُ الْخَلْقِ إِلَيْكُمْ وَحِسَابُهُمْ عَلَيْكُمْ»

“The return of creation is to you, and its reckoning rests with you.”

In Al-Ghashiyah, we read:

إِنَّ إِلَيْنَا إِيَابَهُمْ

Al-Ghashiyah 88:25

Indeed, to Us is their return.

ثُمَّ إِنَّ عَلَيْنَا حِسَابَهُمْ

Al-Ghashiyah 88:26

Then, indeed, their reckoning rests with Us.

The same two ideas—return and reckoning—appear in both texts. The devotional address speaks to the Imams and says “to you” and “with you.” In the Qur’an, God says “to Us” and “with Us.”

Authority over every particle of existence

In Wilayat al-Faqih (Islamic Government), in the section on wilayat takwini (authority over the created order), Ruhollah Khomeini discusses the Imam’s spiritual station, distinguishing it from the duty of government. Of that station, he writes:

“It is a vicegerency over the created order by virtue of which all particles are subject to the ‘holder of authority.’”

The Qur’an says of the obedience of created beings:

وَلَهُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ كُلٌّ لَهُ قَانِتُونَ

Ar-Rum 30:26

Everyone in the heavens and the earth belongs to Him; all are obedient to Him.

The verse states the obedience of all to God. Attributing the submission of every particle to an Imam requires a separate justification.

Making religious figures the reason for the universe’s creation

Mulla Ali al-Qari al-Harawi records the following well-known saying in Al-Asrar al-Marfu‘ah fi al-Akhbar al-Mawdu‘ah (a work on fabricated reports), entry 385, p. 288:

«لَوْلَاكَ لَمَا خَلَقْتُ الْأَفْلَاكَ»

“Were it not for you, I would not have created the heavens.”

Al-Qari reports al-Saghani’s judgment that the saying is fabricated. Al-Qari himself considers its meaning sound.

A longer version, cited from Al-Jannah al-‘Asimah, appears in the supplement to ‘Awalim al-‘Ulum wa al-Ma‘arif, volume 11, part 1, published by the Imam Mahdi Institute in Qom, pp. 43–44:

«يَا أَحْمَدُ، لَوْلَاكَ لَمَا خَلَقْتُ الْأَفْلَاكَ، وَلَوْلَا عَلِيٌّ لَمَا خَلَقْتُكَ، وَلَوْلَا فَاطِمَةُ لَمَا خَلَقْتُكُمَا.»

“Ahmad, were it not for you, I would not have created the heavens. Were it not for Ali, I would not have created you; and were it not for Fatimah, I would not have created the two of you.”

Ahmad is a name used for Muhammad; Ali was his cousin and son-in-law, and Fatimah his daughter. What justification is there for making The God’s will to create the universe conditional on the existence of Muhammad, Ali, or Fatimah?

In At-Talaq, the creation of heaven and earth is connected to human recognition of God’s power and knowledge:

الَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ وَمِنَ الْأَرْضِ مِثْلَهُنَّ يَتَنَزَّلُ الْأَمْرُ بَيْنَهُنَّ لِتَعْلَمُوا أَنَّ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ وَأَنَّ قَدْ أَحَاطَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عِلْمًا

At-Talaq 65:12

The God is the One who created seven heavens, and of the earth their like. The command descends among them so that you may know that The God has power over everything and that The God encompasses everything in knowledge.

Of the creation of jinn and human beings, He says:

وَمَا خَلَقْتُ الْجِنَّ وَالْإِنْسَ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُونِ

Adh-Dhariyat 51:56

I created jinn and human beings only that they might worship Me.

مَا أُرِيدُ مِنْهُمْ مِنْ رِزْقٍ وَمَا أُرِيدُ أَنْ يُطْعِمُونِ

Adh-Dhariyat 51:57

I seek no provision from them, nor do I ask them to feed Me.

إِنَّ هُوَ الرَّزَّاقُ ذُو الْقُوَّةِ الْمَتِينُ

Adh-Dhariyat 51:58

The God Himself is the abundant Provider, the possessor of power, the Firm.

Worship is the purpose stated here for creating jinn and human beings. God Himself also provides for them. These verses do not make the creation of the world conditional upon the existence of any person.

Imagining a system of intermediaries before God

In Egyptian Dar al-Ifta fatwa 6976, dated 3 September 2006, Ali Gomaa rules that istighathah (appealing for rescue or aid) to Prophets, holy figures, and righteous people is legitimate, whether they are living or deceased, present or absent. Within Twelver Shi‘i devotional tradition, Shaykh Abbas Qummi’s Al-Baqiyat al-Salihat, an appendix to Mafatih al-Jinan, includes a section entitled “The Prayer for Aid through al-Batul,” a title of Fatimah. It instructs a person in need to say while prostrating, “My lady, Fatimah, come to my aid.” The wording appears on p. 789 of the Arabic edition.

In Yunus, the Qur’an records the claim of people who regarded the objects of their worship as intercessors with God:

وَيَعْبُدُونَ مِنْ دُونِ مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُمْ وَلَا يَنْفَعُهُمْ وَيَقُولُونَ هَٰؤُلَاءِ شُفَعَاؤُنَا عِنْدَ ۚ قُلْ أَتُنَبِّئُونَ بِمَا لَا يَعْلَمُ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَلَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۚ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَىٰ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

Yunus 10:18

They worship, apart from The God, things that can neither harm nor benefit them, and say, “These are our intercessors with The God.” Say: are you informing The God of something He does not know in the heavens or on earth? He is exalted and above what they associate with Him.

Fatir 35:13 speaks of those whom people invoke instead of God. The next verse explains the result of invoking them:

إِنْ تَدْعُوهُمْ لَا يَسْمَعُوا دُعَاءَكُمْ وَلَوْ سَمِعُوا مَا اسْتَجَابُوا لَكُمْ ۖ وَيَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ يَكْفُرُونَ بِشِرْكِكُمْ ۚ وَلَا يُنَبِّئُكَ مِثْلُ خَبِيرٍ

Fatir 35:14

If you call upon them, they do not hear your call; and even if they heard, they would not answer you. On the Day of Resurrection they will disown your associating them with God. No one can inform you like the One who is fully aware.

When we are in need, God Himself has invited us to call upon Him. He does not need anyone to tell Him about our condition, and we do not need to approach another being in order to convey our request to Him.

This question is examined in detail in the study of intercession in the Qur’an.

Someone who glorifies God applies the same Qur’anic criterion to their own religious beliefs that they apply to the beliefs of others.

6. Glorification, praise, and sanctification

At the beginning of the account of Adam’s creation, the angels address The God:

وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ إِنِّي جَاعِلٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً ۖ قَالُوا أَتَجْعَلُ فِيهَا مَنْ يُفْسِدُ فِيهَا وَيَسْفِكُ الدِّمَاءَ وَنَحْنُ نُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِكَ وَنُقَدِّسُ لَكَ ۖ قَالَ إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

Al-Baqarah 2:30

When your Lord said to the angels, “I am placing a successor on earth,” they said, “Will You place there someone who will spread corruption and shed blood, while we glorify You with praise and sanctify for You?” He said, “I know what you do not know.”

Three concepts appear together in this verse: tasbih (declaring God transcendent), hamd (praise), and taqdis (sanctification or affirming holiness).

Tasbih emphasizes declaring God free of unworthy attributes and recognizing His transcendence.

Hamd (praise) expresses the recognition and praise of God’s perfections and worthiness.

Taqdis (sanctification) is associated with purity, sacredness, and honoring what is holy.

When the Qur’an speaks of glorification “with praise,” knowing God is not confined to denying defects. It also involves recognizing and praising His perfections.

For example, when we say that God does not wrong anyone, we declare Him free of injustice. When we praise His justice, we offer hamd.

The wording “we sanctify for You” also deserves attention. The angels do not say nuqaddisuka (we sanctify You), but nuqaddisu laka (we sanctify for You).

Alongside honoring God’s holiness, this wording can also carry the sense of purifying oneself for Him.

Someone who declares God free of injustice and praises His justice should also strive to cleanse their own thinking and conduct of injustice.

In this way, declaring God transcendent, praising Him, and sanctifying can complement one another in human understanding and life.

7. Creation’s glorification: order, movement, and the laws of existence

From divine transcendence to the grandeur of creation

In the final two verses of Al-Hashr, the Qur’an itself connects divine transcendence with the glorification of all creation:

هُوَ الَّذِي لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْمَلِكُ الْقُدُّوسُ السَّلَامُ الْمُؤْمِنُ الْمُهَيْمِنُ الْعَزِيزُ الْجَبَّارُ الْمُتَكَبِّرُ ۚ سُبْحَانَ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

Al-Hashr 59:23

He is The God; there is no deity except Him: the Sovereign, the All-Holy, the Source of Peace and Wholeness, the Giver of Security, the Guardian, the Invincible, the Possessor of Compelling Power, the Possessor of Greatness. The God is exalted beyond what they associate with Him.

هُوَ الْخَالِقُ الْبَارِئُ الْمُصَوِّرُ ۖ لَهُ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ ۚ يُسَبِّحُ لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ

Al-Hashr 59:24

He is The God, the Creator, the Originator, the Giver of Form. To Him belong the most beautiful names. Everything in the heavens and the earth glorifies Him, and He is the Invincible, the Wise.

The first verse states divine attributes and then declares God exalted beyond the partners people assign to Him. The next speaks of creation, the giving of form, the most beautiful names, and the glorification of all existence.

Once freed from mistaken images of God, a person can also recognize expressions of His greatness, power, and wisdom in creation.

In Al-Furqan, we read:

الَّذِي لَهُ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَلَمْ يَتَّخِذْ وَلَدًا وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ شَرِيكٌ فِي الْمُلْكِ وَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ فَقَدَّرَهُ تَقْدِيرًا

Al-Furqan 25:2

The One to whom belongs the sovereignty of the heavens and the earth, who has taken no child and has no partner in sovereignty, and who created everything and determined its measure.

This verse brings together the oneness of God’s sovereignty, the denial of offspring and partners, and the establishing of measure within creation.

Movement and lawfulness in creation

To consider tasbih across existence, let us return to the other branch of the Arabic word family s-b-h:

وَهُوَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ وَالشَّمْسَ وَالْقَمَرَ ۖ كُلٌّ فِي فَلَكٍ يَسْبَحُونَ

Al-Anbiya 21:33

He is the One who created the night and the day, the sun and the moon; all swim along in an orbit.

The verb here is yasbahun (they swim or move fluidly), which conveys an image of smooth movement and floating.

The Qur’an also says:

لَا الشَّمْسُ يَنْبَغِي لَهَا أَنْ تُدْرِكَ الْقَمَرَ وَلَا اللَّيْلُ سَابِقُ النَّهَارِ ۚ وَكُلٌّ فِي فَلَكٍ يَسْبَحُونَ

Ya-Sin 36:40

It is not for the sun to overtake the moon, nor does the night outstrip the day; all swim along in an orbit.

These verses present a picture of order, movement, boundaries, and coordination in creation.

Yasbahun (they swim or move along) describes the movement of celestial bodies; yusabbihun (they glorify) expresses glorification. The connection between these discussions lies in what a person comes to recognize through observing the ordered movement of the heavens.

الشَّمْسُ وَالْقَمَرُ بِحُسْبَانٍ

Ar-Rahman 55:5

The sun and moon follow a precise reckoning.

And in Al-Mulk:

الَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا ۖ مَا تَرَىٰ فِي خَلْقِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ مِنْ تَفَاوُتٍ ۖ فَارْجِعِ الْبَصَرَ هَلْ تَرَىٰ مِنْ فُطُورٍ

Al-Mulk 67:3

The One who created seven heavens in layers: you see no inconsistency in the creation of the Entirely Merciful. Look again: do you see any fissure?

God asks human beings to look at creation, study it, and reflect on the order that governs it.

Our studies of divine permission and intercession discuss the lawfulness of creation and the enduring patterns that operate throughout existence.

Here we approach that same order from another direction. Every phenomenon that comes into being, moves, changes, and affects other phenomena according to the laws of creation can reveal something of its Creator’s greatness, knowledge, and wisdom.

Even change, decay, death, and renewed emergence occur within these laws. Lawfulness does not mean that phenomena remain unchanged. It means that there is an order within which those changes take place.

Different forms of glorification among created beings

In An-Nur, The God says:

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ يُسَبِّحُ لَهُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَالطَّيْرُ صَافَّاتٍ ۖ كُلٌّ قَدْ عَلِمَ صَلَاتَهُ وَتَسْبِيحَهُ ۗ عَلِيمٌ بِمَا يَفْعَلُونَ

An-Nur 24:41

Have you not seen that everyone in the heavens and the earth glorifies The God, as do birds with wings outspread? Each knows its own salat (prayer or devotional orientation) and glorification. The God knows what they do.

The book Salat in the Qur’an: Meaning, Practice, and the Life of Prayer draws on this verse to explore the wider dimensions of salat (the Qur’anic term commonly rendered “prayer”).

When describing the birds’ glorification, the Qur’an depicts them with outspread wings. The verse closes by affirming God’s knowledge of what they do.

Picture a bird lifting from the ground into flight. The shape of its wings, its body weight, bone structure, muscle activity, breathing, balance, and the laws governing the movement of air all contribute to its ability to fly.

A person could spend years studying just one of these phenomena and still discover more.

A bird’s flight, a plant’s growth, the circulation of blood, the movement of celestial bodies, and even the relationships among the smallest constituents of matter all display the extraordinary order of creation.

The Qur’an also attributes glorification to phenomena that people do not ordinarily think of as possessing language or speech:

وَيُسَبِّحُ الرَّعْدُ بِحَمْدِهِ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ مِنْ خِيفَتِهِ وَيُرْسِلُ الصَّوَاعِقَ فَيُصِيبُ بِهَا مَنْ يَشَاءُ وَهُمْ يُجَادِلُونَ فِي وَهُوَ شَدِيدُ الْمِحَالِ

Ar-Ra'd 13:13

Thunder glorifies Him with praise, as do the angels in awe of Him. He sends thunderbolts and strikes with them whom He wills, while they dispute about The God; He is mighty in power and severe in requital.

Of David, the Qur’an also says:

فَفَهَّمْنَاهَا سُلَيْمَانَ ۚ وَكُلًّا آتَيْنَا حُكْمًا وَعِلْمًا ۚ وَسَخَّرْنَا مَعَ دَاوُودَ الْجِبَالَ يُسَبِّحْنَ وَالطَّيْرَ ۚ وَكُنَّا فَاعِلِينَ

Al-Anbiya 21:79

We gave Solomon understanding of it, and to each We gave judgment and knowledge. We subjected the mountains and birds to glorify with David; We brought this about.

إِنَّا سَخَّرْنَا الْجِبَالَ مَعَهُ يُسَبِّحْنَ بِالْعَشِيِّ وَالْإِشْرَاقِ

Sad 38:18

We subjected the mountains to glorify with him in the evening and at sunrise.

“With David” and “with him” matter in this account. The mountains glorify together with him, and the Qur’an even identifies the times of this companionship: evening and sunrise. A mountain follows the laws of creation at other hours too. Reducing this glorification entirely to the mountain’s natural order leaves both its companionship with David and these particular times unexplained.

Existence is not silent. In its order and movement we see signs of the Creator’s greatness. Yet in Al-Isra 17:44, after affirming that everything glorifies Him, the Qur’an says, “But you do not understand their glorification.” We do not comprehend how they glorify.

8. Glorification and knowledge: understanding creation and the limits of understanding

Subhan as the outcome of reflection

The Qur’an gives a vivid account of the relationship between observing creation, reflecting, and glorifying God:

إِنَّ فِي خَلْقِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَاخْتِلَافِ اللَّيْلِ وَالنَّهَارِ لَآيَاتٍ لِأُولِي الْأَلْبَابِ

Al-Imran 3:190

In the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the alternation of night and day, there are signs for people of understanding.

الَّذِينَ يَذْكُرُونَ قِيَامًا وَقُعُودًا وَعَلَىٰ جُنُوبِهِمْ وَيَتَفَكَّرُونَ فِي خَلْقِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ رَبَّنَا مَا خَلَقْتَ هَٰذَا بَاطِلًا سُبْحَانَكَ فَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ

Al-Imran 3:191

Those who remember The God standing, sitting, and lying on their sides, and reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth: “Our Lord, You have not created this in vain. Exalted are You! Protect us from the punishment of the Fire.”

The sequence of ideas in these two verses deserves close attention.

A person observes creation, remembers God, reflects on what has been created, recognizes that it is not purposeless, and then says subhanaka (exalted are You).

This declaration of transcendence arises from reflection and understanding.

The God Himself also uses this expression when describing an aspect of creation:

سُبْحَانَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ الْأَزْوَاجَ كُلَّهَا مِمَّا تُنْبِتُ الْأَرْضُ وَمِنْ أَنْفُسِهِمْ وَمِمَّا لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

Ya-Sin 36:36

Exalted is the One who created all pairs—from what the earth grows, from themselves, and from what they do not know.

By studying this order and understanding relationships among beings, a person can recognize both the greatness of the Creator and the fundamental distinction between Him and the order He has created.

A scientist studying the structure of living organisms, the motion of celestial bodies, or the laws governing matter can discover, with each new insight, another expression of The God’s greatness.

A farmer who plants a seed, watches it grow, and comes to understand the relationships among soil, water, light, warmth, and life can also recognize and glorify God through that experience.

One need not be a natural scientist to glorify God. What matters is not passing indifferently by the signs of creation, but reflecting on them within one’s knowledge and capacity.

Subhan and acknowledging the limits of knowledge

In the account of Adam, when the angels encountered something they did not know, they said:

قَالُوا سُبْحَانَكَ لَا عِلْمَ لَنَا إِلَّا مَا عَلَّمْتَنَا ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْعَلِيمُ الْحَكِيمُ

Al-Baqarah 2:32

They said, “Exalted are You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. You are indeed the All-Knowing, the Wise.”

Here subhanaka (exalted are You) accompanies an acknowledgment of limited knowledge.

The angels did not treat their own ignorance as a deficiency in God’s knowledge or wisdom. They distinguished what they knew from what The God knew.

This lesson matters greatly for human beings too.

When we do not understand how something happens, we should not treat our ignorance as proof that creation lacks wisdom. When a verse’s meaning is not fully clear to us, we should not attribute our personal interpretation to God.

Just as understanding a phenomenon in creation can lead a person to proclaim God’s transcendence, recognizing the limits of one’s knowledge can also become a reason to glorify Him.

One arises from knowing; the other from knowing what we do not know.

9. Glorification through existence and through choice: from natural order to moral responsibility

Living within creation’s necessities while choosing how to act

If nothing exists without glorifying God, does an unjust person also glorify Him?

An unjust person’s heart beats according to the laws of creation just as a good person’s heart does. Their cells function, their respiratory system works, and their body follows physical and biological laws.

No one can choose to step outside the reach of these laws.

Yet The God has granted human beings freedom in thought, choice, and conduct. A person can choose justice or injustice, accept truth or deny it, honor a trust or betray it.

The Qur’an also distinguishes willing from unwilling submission:

أَفَغَيْرَ دِينِ يَبْغُونَ وَلَهُ أَسْلَمَ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ طَوْعًا وَكَرْهًا وَإِلَيْهِ يُرْجَعُونَ

Al-Imran 3:83

Do they seek a religion other than that of The God, when everyone in the heavens and the earth has submitted to Him, willingly or unwillingly, and to Him they will be returned?

This verse concerns submission. Its distinction can help us understand the difference between a person’s place within creation and their responsibility as someone who makes choices. For that purpose, this study uses two expressions:

Glorification through existence: what is disclosed by a person’s very existence and their subjection to the order of creation.

Glorification through choice: what a person consciously does to recognize, express, and bear witness to The God’s transcendence and greatness.

An unjust person’s body has not left the order of creation. By choosing injustice, however, that person has disobeyed God’s moral command.

The value of consciously chosen glorification lies precisely in that awareness and freedom.

Balance and justice: the practical consequence of conscious glorification

In Ar-Rahman, after describing the measured movement of the sun and moon, the Qur’an speaks of balance:

وَالسَّمَاءَ رَفَعَهَا وَوَضَعَ الْمِيزَانَ

Ar-Rahman 55:7

He raised the heaven and established the balance.

أَلَّا تَطْغَوْا فِي الْمِيزَانِ

Ar-Rahman 55:8

So that you do not transgress in the balance.

وَأَقِيمُوا الْوَزْنَ بِالْقِسْطِ وَلَا تُخْسِرُوا الْمِيزَانَ

Ar-Rahman 55:9

Establish weighing with justice, and do not give short measure.

A person living in a world of order and balance is also commanded to uphold balance and justice.

If we declare God free of injustice, how can we justify our own wrongdoing in His name? For a seller, this question arises when weighing a customer’s goods; for a judge, when one of the parties is a relative. Such situations reveal what effect our recognition of God’s justice has had on our conduct.

Declaring God transcendent and taking responsibility for our own wrongs

In the account of the garden owners, the Qur’an itself connects glorification with accepting responsibility for injustice. The owners set out to harvest their crop, whispering to one another:

أَن لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا الْيَوْمَ عَلَيْكُم مِّسْكِينٌ

Al-Qalam 68:24

“Let no needy person enter the garden upon you today.”

When they arrived and found their garden ruined, the most moderate among them reminded them of what he had said before:

قَالَ أَوْسَطُهُمْ أَلَمْ أَقُل لَّكُمْ لَوْلَا تُسَبِّحُونَ

Al-Qalam 68:28

The most moderate among them said, “Did I not tell you: why do you not glorify God?”

قَالُوا سُبْحَانَ رَبِّنَا إِنَّا كُنَّا ظَالِمِينَ

Al-Qalam 68:29

They said, “Our Lord is exalted! We were indeed wrongdoers.”

Those who had set out intending to deprive the needy now called themselves wrongdoers. In one sentence, they both declared their Lord transcendent and accepted responsibility for their decision.

Jonah, too, called upon his Lord from within darkness with the same acknowledgment:

وَذَا النُّونِ إِذْ ذَهَبَ مُغَاضِبًا فَظَنَّ أَنْ لَنْ نَقْدِرَ عَلَيْهِ فَنَادَىٰ فِي الظُّلُمَاتِ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنْتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

Al-Anbiya 21:87

And remember the man of the fish, when he went away in anger and thought We would not constrain him. Then he called out in the darkness: “There is no deity except You. Exalted are You! I was indeed among the wrongdoers.”

Jonah affirmed God’s oneness with “There is no deity except You,” declared His transcendence with “Exalted are You,” and attributed wrongdoing to himself with “I was indeed among the wrongdoers.”

In another account of Jonah’s story, The God says:

فَلَوْلَا أَنَّهُ كَانَ مِنَ الْمُسَبِّحِينَ

As-Saffat 37:143

Had he not been among those who glorify,

لَلَبِثَ فِي بَطْنِهِ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ

As-Saffat 37:144

He would have remained in its belly until the day they are raised.

These verses place Jonah among those who glorify God.

This description turns our attention from saying a phrase in a moment of distress to the place of glorification in a person’s character and life.

In An-Nur, while discussing false accusation and slander, The God also says:

وَلَوْلَا إِذْ سَمِعْتُمُوهُ قُلْتُمْ مَا يَكُونُ لَنَا أَنْ نَتَكَلَّمَ بِهَٰذَا سُبْحَانَكَ هَٰذَا بُهْتَانٌ عَظِيمٌ

An-Nur 24:16

Why, when you heard it, did you not say, “It is not for us to speak of this. Exalted are You! This is a terrible slander.”

Here the declaration of God’s transcendence accompanies a refusal to repeat slander and a defense of people’s good name.

10. Glorification in daily life: remembrance, reflection, and conduct

Giving direction to a person’s life

In the account of Zechariah, we read:

قَالَ رَبِّ اجْعَلْ لِي آيَةً ۖ قَالَ آيَتُكَ أَلَّا تُكَلِّمَ النَّاسَ ثَلَاثَةَ أَيَّامٍ إِلَّا رَمْزًا ۗ وَاذْكُرْ رَبَّكَ كَثِيرًا وَسَبِّحْ بِالْعَشِيِّ وَالْإِبْكَارِ

Al-Imran 3:41

He said, “My Lord, give me a sign.” He said, “Your sign is that you will not speak to people for three days except by gesture. Remember your Lord often, and glorify Him in the evening and the morning.”

The reflection on Al ‘Imran 3:41 discusses the importance of frequent remembrance and glorification in human life.

In another account of the same event, Zechariah calls his people to glorify God as well:

فَخَرَجَ عَلَىٰ قَوْمِهِ مِنَ الْمِحْرَابِ فَأَوْحَىٰ إِلَيْهِمْ أَنْ سَبِّحُوا بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا

Maryam 19:11

He came out to his people from the sanctuary and signaled to them to glorify God in the morning and evening.

This command is not exclusive to Zechariah and his people. In Al-Ahzab, all believers are addressed:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اذْكُرُوا ذِكْرًا كَثِيرًا

Al-Ahzab 33:41

You who believe, remember The God abundantly.

وَسَبِّحُوهُ بُكْرَةً وَأَصِيلًا

Al-Ahzab 33:42

And glorify Him morning and evening.

Morning begins a person’s daily activities; evening offers an opportunity to look back on what they have done.

We can begin the day by remembering God, recognizing His greatness and justice, and resolving to live within His guidance. At the day’s end, we can examine our thinking and conduct in the light of that recognition.

Did I respect other people’s rights today? Did I overstep any limits in business, work, family life, or my responsibilities? Did I attribute something to God that I did not know?

Glorification is not confined to morning and evening. The God says:

فَاصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا يَقُولُونَ وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ وَقَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا ۖ وَمِنْ آنَاءِ اللَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْ وَأَطْرَافَ النَّهَارِ لَعَلَّكَ تَرْضَىٰ

Ta-Ha 20:130

Be patient with what they say, and glorify your Lord with praise before sunrise and before sunset. Glorify Him also during parts of the night and at the ends of the day, so that you may be content.

Through its repeated invitation to remembrance and glorification, the Qur’an gives us opportunities to keep our fundamental direction in sight amid life’s demands.

Spoken glorification as an expression of understanding

Saying “Subhan Allah” (The God is exalted beyond every unworthy attribution) is a valuable expression of human glorification. The Qur’an itself repeatedly teaches us this declaration and its different forms.

In Az-Zukhruf, when speaking of the means of travel that The God has made available to human beings, we read:

لِتَسْتَوُوا عَلَىٰ ظُهُورِهِ ثُمَّ تَذْكُرُوا نِعْمَةَ رَبِّكُمْ إِذَا اسْتَوَيْتُمْ عَلَيْهِ وَتَقُولُوا سُبْحَانَ الَّذِي سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَٰذَا وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُ مُقْرِنِينَ

Az-Zukhruf 43:13

So that you may settle upon their backs, then remember your Lord’s favor when you have settled upon them, and say: “Exalted is the One who has made this subject to us, for we ourselves could not have brought it under control.”

Here a person first remembers the Lord’s favor, recognizes the limits of their own ability, and then voices the declaration of transcendence.

Spoken glorification is, in this instance, an expression of understanding.

As the study of shukr (gratitude expressed in recognition and action) explains, spoken gratitude should also become visible in how we use what we have been given. In the same way, spoken glorification should be accompanied by a sound understanding of God and conduct consistent with that understanding.

In An-Nasr, we read:

فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَاسْتَغْفِرْهُ ۚ إِنَّهُ كَانَ تَوَّابًا

An-Nasr 110:3

Glorify your Lord with praise and seek His forgiveness. He is ever accepting of repentance.

In this verse, after the announcement of victory, God calls His Messenger to glorification, praise, and seeking forgiveness.

Victory should become an occasion for recognizing God’s greatness and examining our own conduct, rather than a source of self-importance and superiority.

Conclusion

When we say “Subhan Allah,” we declare The God free of every limit our minds impose upon divinity. He hears and sees, and nothing is like Him. This recognition restrains us from attributing human relationships, needs, and rivalries to God—both when judging religious beliefs and when constructing an argument for His oneness.

The Qur’an describes all creation as glorifying God. We live within that same world and can consciously glorify our Lord. Sometimes this arises from reflecting on creation; sometimes from the courage to say, “We were wrongdoers.” In both, a person comes to recognize what can rightly be attributed to the Lord and what remains their own responsibility.

God knows best.
